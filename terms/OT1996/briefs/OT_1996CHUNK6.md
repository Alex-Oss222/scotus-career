## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.
- **Citation or Docket:** 83 F.3d 118 (5th Cir. 1996); court-of-appeals No. 95-30510
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Procedural Category:** Simulation-assigned appellate merits
- **Lower-Court Event Date:** 1996-05-20; this is a source date, not an assigned simulation event.

- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment dismissing Oncale's Title VII action because its prior Garcia panel held same-sex harassment categorically outside the statute. The ruling did not adjudicate the alleged episodes on their truth.
- **Relief Requested:** Oncale seeks reversal of the categorical exclusion and consideration of his claims; Sundowner and the individual defendants seek affirmance. The separate finding that coworkers Pippen and Johnson were not statutory employers must not be erased without a presented challenge.
- **Material Record:** Oncale worked on a Sundowner offshore rig from August to November 1991. He alleged repeated forced sexual contact, rape threats, and a coercive shower assault by supervisor John Lyons and coworkers Danny Pippen and Brandon Johnson. He left the job soon after the shower incident. His suit described both hostile-environment and quid-pro-quo theories. The employer disputes statutory liability; the opinion's assumed account is not a factual verdict.
- **Vehicle Limits:** The presented legal bar is separable from proof that the conduct occurred, that it was because of sex, that it altered conditions, and that an actionable basis exists to charge Sundowner. No discrimination finding, constructive-discharge ruling or damages amount is established.

### Questions Presented

1. Whether workplace sexual harassment violates Title VII's prohibition against discrimination because of sex when the harasser and the employee subjected to the harassment are members of the same sex.
2. Whether actionable discrimination because of sex requires evidence of sexual desire or attraction, or may be established through other evidence that the harassment imposed discriminatory working conditions because of the employee's sex.
3. Whether severe or pervasive sexually abusive workplace conduct is actionable under Title VII when it occurs in an all-male or all-female workplace, and how courts should distinguish discrimination because of sex from offensive conduct not motivated by sex.

The enacted text, 42 U.S.C. §2000e-2(a)(1), forbids discrimination against an individual in employment terms and conditions because of that individual's sex. Meritor recognizes sex-based hostile conditions and separates unwanted advances from apparent acquiescence, while leaving important employer-attribution questions unresolved.

Simulated Harris v. Forklift (November 9, 1993) unanimously requires severe or pervasive sex-based conduct creating an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Frequency, severity, physical threat, humiliation and interference are relevant but not individually mandatory. No medical diagnosis, psychological injury or reduced productivity is required. The reported threats and physical conduct here are therefore materially different from isolated crude language, if proved, but their sexual form alone does not eliminate the because-of-sex element. The all-male setting is contextual evidence, not a substitute for that causal inquiry.

Simulated St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks preserves the plaintiff's ultimate burden of proving intentional discrimination; a rejected defense permits but does not invariably compel the ultimate inference. Its burden-allocation holding does not install McDonnell Douglas as the exclusive method for a harassment claim with direct evidence. The Court must distinguish legal sufficiency of sex-based inferences from a merits finding based on this untried record. Simulated Harris does not establish a special reasonable-woman test, strict employer liability or individual coworker liability. The proposed Kinman and other OT1996 briefs supply no completed same-term rule.

## C. LITIGATION POSITIONS, LAWFUL PATHS, AND UNCERTAINTY

Oncale and the EEOC emphasize gender-neutral statutory language and discrimination because of the victim's sex, regardless of the harasser's sex. Defendants rely on the circuit's Garcia rule and the distinction between gender discrimination and conduct with sexual overtones. The record allows consideration of desire, hostility toward a sex, differential targeting, sex-specific threats, and social context; the parties dispute what is necessary and what can be inferred.

The Court may reverse the categorical bar and remand for the remaining elements, or affirm if it construes the statute to exclude these claims. A narrower record-specific decision cannot silently decide all-male workplaces never experience sex discrimination. Conversely, eliminating the categorical exclusion cannot establish that all humiliating or violent conduct is Title VII discrimination. The Court should retain any independently dispositive individual-defendant ruling outside the granted scope and identify which preserved quid-pro-quo allegations need further analysis.

## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 1 — coverage.** Stone would hold that the shared sex of offender and employee does not exclude Title VII coverage. The statute asks why working conditions were imposed on the claimant, not whether offender and claimant occupy different sex categories. Meritor already recognizes that abusive conditions can constitute discrimination; the new application follows the enacted individual protection. Stone's broader equality commitments support taking the alleged injury seriously but do not create statutory liability independently of the text.

**Question 2 — proof and sexual desire.** Desire is one possible factual explanation, not a required statutory element. Sex-based hostility, selective conduct and other competent circumstances can prove that sex caused the discriminatory conditions. Stone would neither require evidence that a harasser identifies as gay nor treat a sexual act as conclusively establishing discriminatory causation. The trier must evaluate the actual inference and alternatives. Hicks preserves the ultimate burden; it does not demand a fictitious comparator or bar circumstantial proof.

**Question 3 — severity and workplace context.** An all-male workforce does not make sex-based treatment legally impossible. Stone would apply Harris's objective and subjective inquiry in that workplace's actual circumstances, without excusing forced contact and rape threats simply as rough camaraderie. Evidence of severe physical humiliation can meet the threshold without psychiatric injury, but it must still be unwelcome, because of sex, and attributable to an employer under the applicable rule. Ordinary offensive banter and interpersonal hostility unconnected to sex remain outside this statutory cause.

Stone would not announce universal employer responsibility, treat coworkers as employers by fiat, or award relief under a free-standing dignity theory. He would preserve a distinct inquiry into any quid-pro-quo claim: the alleged demand, supervisor's use of employment power, and actual employment consequence must be identified. His proposed interpretation is not a finding that any allegation is true or that all defendants owe relief.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Reverse the judgment insofar as it rests on a categorical same-sex exclusion and remand for adjudication under the correct statutory and Harris standards. Retain independently unchallenged grounds concerning individual coworker employer status. The lower courts should address employer attribution, preserved quid-pro-quo matters, proof of causation and available relief in their ordinary order. No final harassment finding, reinstatement, backpay, punitive award or constructive-discharge determination follows from this mandate.

# 61. Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District
- **Citation or Docket:** 88 F.3d 274 (5th Cir. 1996); panel January 10, rehearing denied May 31; court-of-appeals No. 94-60631
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Procedural Category:** Simulation-assigned appellate merits
- **Lower-Court Event Date:** 1996-05-31; this is a source date, not an assigned simulation event.

Mississippi's 1994 statute required permission for student-initiated nonsectarian, nonproselytizing prayer at school events, including compulsory assemblies, while disclaiming governmental endorsement and including severability language. Parents and students challenged it before implementation. The district court heard evidence and preliminarily enjoined operation except at high-school graduations, relying on circuit graduation precedent.

The State appealed; challengers disputed the graduation exception. Students seeking intervention through religious-liberty counsel asserted independent expression interests. The district court allowed amicus participation and denied intervention without foreclosing renewed consideration. Review includes standing, likely constitutional validity, scope of interim relief, and Rule 24—not a completed trial record or a final declaration that all student prayer is unconstitutional.

### Questions Presented

1. Whether a state statute permitting student-initiated, nonsectarian, nonproselytizing prayer at compulsory and noncompulsory public school events violates the Establishment Clause when the statute expressly disclaims governmental sponsorship or endorsement of religious expression.
2. Whether student-initiated religious expression at school-sponsored assemblies, athletic events, and graduation ceremonies constitutes private speech protected by the Free Speech and Free Exercise Clauses, or governmental religious activity subject to the Establishment Clause.
3. Whether permitting student-initiated prayer at school events creates unconstitutional governmental coercion or endorsement when school officials control the event, attendance may be compulsory, or students face institutional pressure to participate.
4. Whether parents and students have Article III standing to challenge a school-prayer statute before its implementation when they allege that unconstitutional governmental participation and coercion are inevitable.
5. Whether a federal court may facially enjoin an entire statute authorizing religious expression in public schools when some applications may be constitutionally permissible and the statute contains a severability provision.
6. Whether students asserting independent free speech and religious-exercise interests must be permitted to intervene under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 24 when the state attorney general is already defending the challenged statute.

**Actual simulated religion doctrine.** *Lee v. Weisman* contains two independently sufficient majority rationales. The official-sponsorship majority, joined by Stone, addressed school organization, selection and direction of clergy, and official religious ceremony without requiring quantified coercion. The distinct pressure/coercion majority did not include Stone. Neither permits attributing every student religious statement to the government. Here official event control and a statutory content filter support scrutiny of state participation; the words voluntary and nonsectarian do not alone cure school-sponsored religious exercise.

*Capitol Square Review Board v. Pinette* protects the private religious speaker's equal access in its forum setting. The Court did not settle one comprehensive Establishment Clause formula or automatically approve every disclaimer. Simulated *Chabad of Southern Ohio* requires attention to contextual attribution; forum designation alone is insufficient. *Rosenberger* concerned neutral support of independently edited and attributed private expression, not a general permission for school-directed worship. Those holdings require examining who selects, controls, delivers, and is reasonably understood to speak in each school setting. Lemon has not been globally displaced.

**Standing and relief.** Simulated *Lujan* requires a concrete personal interest and an actual or imminent threatened injury. A child's impending attendance at an identified official event can support prospective injury; an abstract objection to a law cannot. Pre-enforcement standing therefore depends on implementation, event, and attendance facts, not the mere assertion that unlawful coercion is inevitable. The Court must use the controlling portions of Lujan, not Stone's separate position or a nonmajority redressability analysis.

Interim relief must be supported by likely success, irreparable injury, and the applicable equitable considerations. A facial claim does not eliminate standing or severability. The operative statutory requirements should be examined separately, preserving independently lawful applications where state severability law permits. In Rule 24 analysis, shared opposition to invalidation supports an adequacy inference but does not conclusively establish that the Attorney General represents the students' distinct private-expression interest. Timeliness, protectable interest, practical impairment, and adequacy all remain relevant.

## C. LITIGATION POSITIONS, LAWFUL PATHS, AND UNCERTAINTY

The challengers contend that the statute institutionalizes religious exercise at official, sometimes compulsory events and that official screening for nonsectarian content itself entails state religious supervision. The State invokes accommodation, private initiation, and the statutory disclaimer. Proposed intervenors argue that an injunction could suppress constitutionally protected private speech and that the State's institutional defense may not fully represent them.

Permissible paths include affirming supported interim restraints, narrowing them to governmental sponsorship, reconsidering the graduation exception under actual Lee, or remanding insufficient imminence or attribution findings. Private expression in a genuine neutral forum remains a separate application. The Court may revisit intervention on a distinct-interest record without automatically allowing everyone who supports a statute to intervene. It must not convert preliminary review into a final universal school-prayer code.

## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **The disclaimer does not save governmental religious sponsorship.** Stone would assess the actual mechanism, including official events and required religious-content screening. Under Lee's sponsorship majority, a school cannot avoid responsibility merely by assigning delivery to a student or announcing voluntariness. An independently attributable private forum use is a different application and must remain protected.

2. **Student initiation is relevant but not decisive.** Selection, control, audience, and attribution determine whether the speech remains private. Stone would apply Pinette and Chabad to genuine private access, while applying Lee where the school incorporates prayer into its ceremony. Rosenberger does not convert school-directed religious observance into private expression merely because the selected speaker is a student.

3. **Official sponsorship can suffice without a separate quantification of coercion.** Compulsory attendance and institutional pressure strengthen the constitutional concern, but Stone would rely on the actual sponsorship holding he joined. He would not retroactively claim the separate Lee coercion rationale as his own holding. Nor would he presume that hearing a fellow student's private religious viewpoint constitutes unconstitutional coercion.

4. **These enrolled students have standing to challenge the imminent official-event mechanism, with parents asserting their children's interests through the proper representative route.** Stone would draw the concrete-threat inference from their impending attendance at public-school events, the statute's operative requirement that prayer be permitted, and the school's necessary control of event access and the statutory religious-content limits. The challenge arose immediately before the school year, so exposure was not a hypothetical remote contingency. He would acknowledge the contrary argument that districts had not yet implemented a policy; the mandatory mechanism and imminent event participation supply the credible threat despite that uncertainty. He would not grant standing to an abstract objector elsewhere or infer final unconstitutional implementation merely from this jurisdictional conclusion.

5. **Do not enjoin lawful private expression merely because other applications fail.** Stone would require provision-specific and event-specific severability analysis. He would remove the categorical graduation safe harbor insofar as it authorizes the very official sponsorship Lee forbids, while preserving genuinely private, independently attributable access. The interim order should restrain the unconstitutional statutory mechanism, not prohibit every religious statement by a student.

6. **Intervention is not automatic, but the shared desired outcome is not conclusive adequacy.** Stone would require a Rule 24 determination addressing whether the students' private-speech interest is actually protected by the State's litigation position and the contemplated injunction. If that distinct interest may be impaired and inadequately represented, intervention should be allowed on appropriate terms. Amicus participation alone does not answer the rule's entitlement inquiry.

Find Article III standing for the enrolled-student challenge on the concrete impending-exposure basis above. Affirm preliminary protection against school-sponsored religious exercises supported by that record; vacate the categorical graduation exception and any blanket reach into protected private speech; remand for an appropriately severed interim order and a specific Rule 24 adequacy determination. Resolve no final damages claim and impose no general ban on student religious speech. This follows Stone's documented distinction between institutional religious action and equal treatment of genuinely private expression.

### Issue 1: Best-interests bypass for parental notification

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Planned Parenthood, Sioux Falls Clinic v. Miller (1996) applies simulated Casey’s complete six-step standard, as applied in Fargo, to compulsory minor notice. Challengers first establish interference caused by the duty; demonstrated parental practical control or substantial obstruction cannot be justified by a general welfare interest. The decision enjoined the indivisible compulsory condition because the enacted law supplied no usable independent maturity or best-interests route. It did not equate every notice law with consent or forbid every forty-eight-hour period.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Casey’s five-Justice six-step rule requires identification of the actual duty and protected baseline; the duty’s function and purpose; its causal added interference and independently defined affected population; ordered substantive review; clinical and truthful-communication safeguards; and violation-specific relief. A baseline violation, objectively established obstructive purpose, or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring; remaining meaningful interference requires a substantiated permissible justification and fit, including an answer to a concrete practicable substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference, that heightened access inquiry ends, without foreclosing other constitutional claims. Usable exceptions and bypasses matter; absence of an obstacle alone does not validate every burden. Fargo applies the complete rule to facial and cumulative claims, and Miller applies it to compulsory minor notice.

- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 52 and the decree's text. Substantial compliance means performance that satisfies the decree's essential purposes despite only immaterial deviations. Because the decree specifies fair, reasonable, and timely decisionmaking and durable procedures for permanent placement, the court must address each essential objective, explain the significance of deviations from enumerated criteria, state the methodology used to weigh system data and case evidence, and make enough subsidiary findings for review. - **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal court may dissolve this institutional consent decree only on reviewable findings that the Department maintained substantial and continuous compliance with the decree's essential purposes for twelve consecutive months. Conclusory findings that do not explain material deviations, methodology, or subsidiary facts do not satisfy Rule 52(a).
