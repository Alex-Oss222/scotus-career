## Event

**Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District**, court-of-appeals No. 94-60631, 88 F.3d 274. Merits decision, March 19, 1997. On review of the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of a preliminary injunction against Mississippi's school-prayer statute, its graduation exception and denials of student intervention. Merits hearing and decision: March 19, 1997 under the established Court calendar; no separate argument date is supplied.

The questions concern preimplementation standing; official versus private student prayer, including school pressure and a statutory disclaimer; lawful reach and severability of interim relief; the graduation exception; and intervention under Rule 24.

## Holdings

### I. Imminent personal exposure permits this challenge

**Rule and authority:** A student facing a concrete, imminent application of an official school-event mechanism may seek prospective relief before the threatened exposure occurs. Part I has all nine joins and controls; standing does not establish the threatened conduct's illegality.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires personal injury, a causal connection and redressability, rather than abstract disagreement with a statute. These enrolled students face impending events under a mandatory permission mechanism administered by school officials, and the hearing record supports that concrete prospect. Their parents may represent their children's interests through the proper representative route. The absence of a completed local policy does not make the impending exposure remote, while the availability of lawful applications remains relevant to the merits and remedy. This holding establishes neither general taxpayer standing nor a right for an abstract objector elsewhere.

### II. Official school worship cannot be insulated by student delivery or a disclaimer

**Rule and authority:** A school violates the Establishment Clause when it organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event; student initiation or delivery and a statutory disclaimer do not alone make that exercise private. Part II controls through Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, independently of a separate quantified-pressure showing.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee supplies the prohibition on official sponsorship of school worship. The mandatory event permission and religious-content eligibility terms give officials an operational role that must be examined, rather than disregarded because the speaker is a student. Section 1(2) permits student-initiated, nonsectarian, nonproselytizing prayer at compulsory and noncompulsory events; the State may not administer those conditions through official devotional organization or religious supervision. Section 1(4)'s disclaimer does not erase actual official conduct. Section 2, by contrast, denies schools authority to prescribe prayer's form or content. That is a statutory command, not a factual finding that it is always obeyed or always violated. Pinette and Chabad require attention to attribution, while Rosenberger protects neutral support for independently edited private speech; none makes every school religious statement official. The supported prospective mechanism warrants bounded interim protection, not a final holding against every permissible application.

### III. School-directed worship in a pressured setting is independently forbidden

**Rule and authority:** School direction of religious exercise in compulsory or comparably pressured school settings violates the Establishment Clause when the official setting places actual pressure on students to participate. Part III independently controls through Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; mere offense at another student's private viewpoint does not suffice.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee's distinct school-pressure holding supplies this ground. Official control, students' attendance and participation expectations, and the setting's practical demands matter even without a legal punishment for declining prayer. The State cannot dispose of that inquiry merely by describing participation as voluntary or substituting a student speaker. These circumstances support interim protection against the official mechanism. The Court does not presume coercion from hearing private religious speech, require a quantified social-pressure measurement, or hold every voluntary gathering coercive. This is an alternative ground with its own six-Justice coalition, not an aggregation with Part II.

### IV. Correct the graduation exception and preserve lawful expression

**Rule and authority:** Preliminary relief must restrain supported unlawful official worship while preserving independently lawful statutory provisions and genuinely private expression. Part IV's legal limit has all nine joins; its continued restraint, removal of graduation's categorical exemption and remand have Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee does not exempt graduation merely because students initiate and deliver prayer; the same official attribution and pressure inquiries apply. A genuinely independent student's religious viewpoint remains protected. The lower court must therefore remove its automatic graduation safe harbor and frame the interim order around the actual official mechanism. Section 1(3)'s protection of other voluntary expression, Section 2's separate voluntary-prayer provision and no-prescription command, and Section 1(5)'s severability direction must each receive effect. The supported threat of official worship establishes the relevant interim constitutional injury; narrowing protects the countervailing private-speech interests. The Court neither compels a new forum nor deletes the religious filters to create a broader statutory access entitlement. State-law construction and the precise severed decree remain below. Scalia and Thomas agree with the private-expression limit but would remove the retained restraint too.

### V-A. Actual adequate representation defeats intervention as of right here

**Rule and authority:** Under Rule 24(a)(2), a timely applicant with a protectable interest potentially impaired by litigation is entitled to intervene if existing representation may be inadequate; a shared desired outcome or the State's official identity does not conclusively establish adequacy. All eight Associates join Part V-A and hold the actual representation adequate here, affirming denial without deciding unnecessary alternative elements.

**Controlling explanation:** Trbovich supplies the minimal burden for showing possible inadequacy and recognizes that governmental and private interests may differ. The Attorney General here actually advanced the proposed intervenors' private-expression interests, and they identify no relevant interest omitted from this statutory-validity controversy. The required narrowed decree protects those independent interests. Their private status alone therefore does not establish a representational gap on this record. Amicus participation could not replace an otherwise established right to intervene. Materially changed interests may be considered on a proper later application; this judgment orders neither a renewed hearing nor automatic future intervention.

### V-B. A common question permits but does not compel permissive intervention

**Rule and authority:** Rule 24(b) permits intervention where its common-question condition is met, subject to the court's discretion and consideration of undue delay or prejudice to the original parties; it does not require admission merely because participation would be relevant. All eight Associates join Part V-B and affirm the denial as within discretion on this record.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court's duplication and delay assessment is permissible in this narrow statutory proceeding. The opportunity to make amicus submissions helps explain why excluding duplicative party participation caused no demonstrated prejudice, but does not defeat intervention as of right. The Court adopts neither an absolute rule against applicants without novel legal arguments nor an unreviewable discretion or universal plain-error requirement. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without adding a ground.

## Event

**Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.**, Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510, 83 F.3d 118. Merits decision, March 19, 1997. On review of the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of dismissal on a categorical same-sex-harassment ground. Merits hearing and decision: March 19, 1997 under the established Court calendar; no separate argument date is supplied.

The questions are whether Title VII excludes harassment when offender and employee share a sex, whether sexual desire is necessary, and whether an all-male workplace precludes actionable sex discrimination.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment insofar as it rests on the categorical same-sex exclusion and remands the affected claims.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse in relevant part and remand, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The categorical ground is rejected for the affected claims against Sundowner and Lyons. The independently grounded dismissal of Pippen and Johnson as not statutory employers remains undisturbed outside the grant; the Court adopts no new rule of individual immunity. Lyons's statutory status remains unresolved. No final liability, trial entitlement or award follows.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous reversal in relevant part and remand | All holdings and limitations |

No separate writing is published.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

### Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII

**Rule:** Title VII does not exclude discriminatory harassment merely because the offender and employee share a sex. The claimant must plead and ultimately prove that the challenged employment conditions were imposed because of sex and must satisfy the remaining requirements of the asserted claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor establishes that abusive working conditions can constitute statutory discrimination. The enacted protection concerns the individual employee's treatment, not an unstated requirement that employee and offender belong to different sex categories. A categorical exclusion therefore cannot support this dismissal. Rejecting that exclusion does not prove any allegation, create liability for all objectionable conduct, or adopt a free-standing dignity cause of action. The independent statutory-employer ruling outside the grant remains untouched.

### Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element

**Rule:** Discrimination because of sex may be established through competent direct or circumstantial evidence without proving sexual desire, a particular sexual orientation, or the presence of an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish discriminatory causation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hicks preserves the claimant's ultimate persuasion burden without making one evidentiary route compulsory. Sex-directed hostility, selective conduct, discriminatory statements, threats and the circumstances can support a causal inference; this illustrative list is neither exclusive nor a checklist. Desire can be relevant, but making it indispensable would exclude sex-based mistreatment for reasons the statute does not recognize. The trier must evaluate the actual inference and competing explanations. Ordinary interpersonal hostility unconnected to sex remains outside this cause, and no orientation identity is found here.

### Apply Harris in the actual workplace context

**Rule:** A hostile-environment claim requires sex-based conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively hostile or abusive environment in context, which the claimant actually perceived as abusive. An all-male workforce supplies context, not categorical immunity; neither psychiatric injury nor measurable productivity loss is indispensable.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires an objective and subjective inquiry into the circumstances and supplies the severe-or-pervasive threshold. Context helps distinguish actionable abuse from ordinary offensive banter; it cannot excuse forced physical humiliation or rape threats simply by labeling them rough camaraderie. The alleged conduct may satisfy the threshold, but allegations are not findings and gravity does not substitute for unwelcome treatment, discriminatory causation or employer attribution. No single factor, diagnosis, treatment, universal reasonable-woman standard or medical proof is made compulsory.

### Attribution, other claims and relief remain distinct on remand

**Rule:** If employer attribution is reached, Andrade requires examination whether actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the hostile environment; job title alone is insufficient, and absence of higher-management notice alone does not defeat that agency route. Negligence and any preserved quid-pro-quo claim remain distinct inquiries, and this reversal decides neither their factual predicates nor relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Andrade supplies the governing agency rule, including that employment action or economic loss is not invariably necessary for its authority-aided-environment route. A quid-pro-quo theory separately requires the alleged demand, use of employment power and employment-condition consequence to be identified; coverage does not collapse the two theories. The lower courts must apply the correct rules to the affected claims and any properly presented independent defenses in ordinary course. The Court does not reinstate an unchallenged coworker-employer theory, decide Lyons's status, find constructive discharge, order discovery or trial, or award reinstatement, backpay or punitive damages. Remedy questions for the 1991 conduct remain open.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied the recognition of discriminatory abusive working conditions, extending its statutory application to employees and offenders who share a sex without eliminating any element.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Applied its contextual severe-or-pervasive, objective and subjective inquiry; no psychiatric injury or measurable economic harm is added.
- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks:** Preserved ultimate proof of discrimination and the availability of circumstantial inference; no compulsory comparator or desire showing is created.
- **Andrade v. Mayfair Management:** Applied the actual-authority/material-aid employer-attribution rule, keeping negligence and employment consequences distinct.
- **Brown v. Polk County and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Their separate statutory and constitutional grounds do not replace Title VII's requirements or decide a sexual-orientation coverage question here.
- **Garcia's categorical same-sex exclusion:** The lower-court rule cannot govern Title VII after this decision.

## Law After Decision

Title VII reaches discriminatory harassment without an opposite-sex requirement. Desire and workforce composition create no categorical gates or immunity. The employee still must establish sex causation and every element of the applicable harassment theory; Harris and Andrade retain their independent force. No sexual-orientation coverage holding or automatic liability rule is adopted.

Pippen and Johnson's independently based dismissal stands outside review. No finding of liability or automatic entitlement to further trial proceedings is made.

## Event

**Lambert v.  Wicklund**, No. 96-858. Summary decision, March 31, 1997; submitted without oral argument. On review of the Ninth Circuit's judgment sustaining an injunction against Montana's parental-notification law. The question is whether the judicial-bypass ground requiring waiver when notification is not in the petitioner's best interests is deficient because it does not use the proposed abortion-without-notice wording.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the resolved best-interests-wording ground and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse in relevant part and remand, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The injunction is vacated insofar as it rests on the asserted defect in this best-interests formulation. Any preserved independent ground remains for its own adjudication; this judgment does not direct enforcement notwithstanding another surviving ground.

## Opinion Topology

A per curiam opinion states the common rationale of all nine participating Justices. No separate writing is published.

## Holdings

### An independent notice-best-interests finding is sufficient

**Rule and authority:** A parental-notification bypass is not deficient merely because it requires authorization without notice when notification is not in the petitioner's best interests. A qualifying finding on that statutory ground itself requires authorization; no separate finding that abortion is in her best interests is an additional prerequisite. All nine Justices join the per curiam opinion at this limited scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The unanimous minor-bypass rule in Casey and Miller's application require a usable independent route when parental involvement would give practical control over protected care. Ohio v. Akron Center supplies a prior accepted notice-focused formulation. Montana permits consideration of notification's consequences for the minor, including obtaining the abortion; it does not direct the decisionmaker to ignore those consequences or require parental approval after waiver. Its independent maturity, evidence-of-abuse and notice-best-interests grounds are alternatives, not cumulative hurdles. The challengers' wording distinction therefore establishes no additional obstacle or meaningful interference by itself. A possible restrictive construction cannot simply be presumed, but reliable prospective evidence or an authoritative restrictive construction may support a different claim. The Court does not declare notice and abortion interests universally identical, impose a new proof burden or preapprove every procedure.

### The judgment removes only the identified wording defect

**Rule and authority:** The limited textual ruling leaves the complete governing Casey/Fargo/Miller standard applicable to any preserved independent interference claim and confines relief to the question actually resolved. All nine join this limit; recognizing the framework's binding force does not attribute its entire original rationale to Justices who did not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Fargo requires application of the actual framework to supported access burdens; Miller rejected compulsory notice without a usable independent route. Neither requires rebuilding a factual record merely to decide this verbal distinction, nor makes a bypass label a cure for other burdens. The Court leaves clear-and-convincing proof, emergencies, actual usability, appeal, investigation, adult incapacity and penalties unresolved. It creates no general all-applications barrier to facial relief and orders no new trial, compulsory visit, bypass or appellate timetable.

The statutory procedures remain distinct from constitutional approval: proceedings and records are confidential; counsel is provided on request; guardian appointment is permitted; specific written findings and prompt consideration are required. The forty-eight-hour decision period may be extended at the petitioner's request, and failure to rule automatically grants waiver only when time has not been extended. Evidence of a pattern of physical, sexual or emotional abuse is an independent authorization ground; alleged abuse also activates a separate reporting duty. The state supreme court may adopt expedited confidential-appeal rules, but no particular timetable is established here. Authorization is unappealable. A pregnant minor may not be charged a fee for a waiver petition or appeal. These provisions are neither erased nor held universally adequate.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey:** Applied the unanimous independent-bypass protection and preserved the actual five-Justice complete framework. The narrow wording claim establishes no added interference; distinct supported burdens retain the governing justification, fit, clinical-protection and remedial inquiries.
- **Fargo Women's Health Organization:** Preserved its complete-framework and concrete-burden inquiry without requiring a new evidentiary proceeding on every textual objection.
- **Planned Parenthood v. Miller:** Distinguished compulsory notice lacking a usable independent route from Montana's enacted alternative; practical access remains required.
- **Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health:** Applied its acceptance of a notice-focused independent best-interests route; its broader historical facial-review language does not replace later governing Casey/Fargo law.
- **Bellotti v. Baird:** Preserved independent maturity and best-interests protection without requiring identical statutory wording.

## Law After Decision

Montana's notice-best-interests formulation is sufficient against the specific wording objection. It is an independently sufficient route, not a double-proof requirement. The Court approves neither every application nor every feature of the notification law; actual Casey, Fargo and Miller continue to govern separate preserved burdens.

None.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must remove the wording-based ground for the injunction and decide any preserved independent issue under governing law. No automatic enforcement order or new procedural obligation follows beyond conforming proceedings to this limited judgment.

## Event

**Robinson v. City of Edmond**, lower-court Nos. 94-6237 and 95-6008, 68 F.3d 1226. Argued December 2, 1996; decided March 31, 1997. On granted review of the Tenth Circuit's Establishment Clause judgment following a two-day bench trial concerning Edmond's official seal.

The questions are whether the plaintiffs have a personal basis for prospective relief, whether the City's pervasive use of a Christian cross in its official seal impermissibly identifies civil government with a particular religion, and what relief follows. The separate fee appeal does not enlarge the granted merits questions.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the granted Establishment Clause merits judgment and returns the matter for appropriate prospective relief.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** None.

The City must cease using the cross-bearing seal as its current official imprimatur within a reasonable implementation period set by the district court. Accurate contextual historical records and displays may be preserved. The Court orders no replacement artwork, destruction of historical materials, removal of private religious speech, fixed deadline, personal damages or new fee award. Existing lower proceedings for appropriate relief and fees continue within their proper scope.

## Opinion Topology

| Same opinion, partial join | Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Supports the entire judgment; agrees only with Part I's disposition | II–V; no added ground on I |
| Same opinion, partial joins | Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Supports the entire judgment on the narrower merits ground | I, II, IV and V; no III |

## Holdings

### I. A recurring personal municipal communication supplies standing here

**Rule and authority:** A person who actually and repeatedly receives the City's own allegedly sectarian civic identification may seek prospective relief against that continuing use when the asserted personal injury is attributable to the City and redressable by the requested relief. All eight Associate Justices join Part I; Stone-Zsela supports reaching the merits without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires concrete personal injury, causation, redressability and proof appropriate to the stage of litigation; Valley Forge excludes an abstract demand for constitutional compliance. Feldman's supported recurring receipt of the cross-bearing City water bill, together with his direct encounter and personal objection, provides the required individual relationship to the continuing use. His 1988 encounter alone would not establish present prospective injury. Residence, work, taxation, general awareness and the City's decision not to contest standing are not substitutes for that relationship. The filed account of monthly receipt is accepted as an accurate account of this litigated record, considered with the actual posttrial findings and established continuing municipal use; it is not a new sworn exhibit. The Court assigns no unnamed lower finding of changed behavior to Feldman and imposes no universal avoidance-expense requirement. Because his claim supports the shared prospective relief, the Court need not separately decide the other three merits plaintiffs' standing for that same relief or any distinct personal remedy. Orza's dismissed merits claim is not before the Court.

### II. The adopted municipal seal is the City's own expression

**Rule and authority:** A symbol selected and adopted as the municipality's official seal and used to authenticate its own communications, personnel and property is attributable to the municipality, even if a private person originally designed it. Part II has all nine joins; attribution alone does not establish that every governmental religious acknowledgment is unconstitutional.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinette and Chabad distinguish official adoption from private religious expression admitted through neutral access; Rosenberger protects neutral support for independently edited private speech. Those protections do not convert the City's own identifier into a private message. Edmond selected the seal through its authorized design process, registered it for municipal services and uses it in ordinary official business. No neutral forum, independent speaker or disclaimer separates that continuing civic identification from the City. The actual adopted function, rather than the designer's private status or beliefs, resolves attribution. The constitutional character of that expression requires the further inquiries below.

### III. Context establishes an impermissible official religious affiliation

**Rule and authority:** Government violates the Establishment Clause when the whole context of its own continuing civic expression identifies it with a particular faith; a secular historical purpose and surrounding secular images do not by themselves cure that effect. Part III controls through Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon supplies the independent inquiry into religious effect, and Allegheny requires consideration of the expression's actual context. The unmistakable Christian cross occupies its own identifying quadrant in the seal carried throughout the City's official operations. The Court considers the composite design, its history, municipal uses and absence of church control. Assuming the district court's historical-purpose finding, the other three quadrants do not make this religious identifier neutral, and age is neither a constitutional safe harbor nor proof of affiliation by itself. Favorable witnesses' reactions do not displace that contextual inquiry. The rule requires neither a majority-offense poll nor coercion; it also creates no automatic rule against an isolated graphic regardless of setting. Purpose and entanglement need not be independently decided. No new credibility finding or universal rule for reviewing constitutional facts supplies the result.

### IV. Making a faith's badge the City's permanent civil identifier is independently forbidden

**Rule and authority:** A city may not deliberately and pervasively use a recognizable symbol of a particular faith as a permanent identifying part of its own badge of present civil authority. Part IV has all nine direct joins and independently supports the judgment on the combined adoption, institutional-identification and continuing-use facts established here.

**Controlling explanation:** Kennedy's separate writing in Allegheny identifies a boundary between permissible acknowledgment and permanent official religious identification. The Court now directly adopts that bounded distinction on the institutional-use facts here. Edmond crosses that boundary by placing this freestanding Christian cross in its selected civic seal and carrying the seal through its ordinary official business. This is the government's own institutional identity, rather than another speaker's religious message or an exhibit about a particular historical artifact. The conclusion does not require a proved evangelical motive, church control, an added proselytizing inscription, a compulsory profession, denial of a benefit or visual dominance over every secular quadrant. It does require the actual combination present here; government ownership or religious content alone is insufficient. Religious place names, specific historical buildings, inherited coats of arms, holiday acknowledgments, historical exhibits and private symbols retain their own contexts. The Court announces no categorical approval or invalidation of them. This ground has nine direct votes; its authority does not depend on combining incompatible rationales.

### V. Relief must end the present official use and preserve the distinction from history

**Rule and authority:** Prospective relief must end the unconstitutional use of the cross-bearing seal as the City's current official identifier, with a reasonable implementation period fixed below; accurate contextual preservation of historical records and displays remains permissible. All nine join PartV.

**Controlling explanation:** The violation concerns the City's continuing institutional identification. An order directed to that use remedies it without rewriting history or suppressing private expression. The district court may set the practical implementation period, but longevity does not authorize continued official adoption without end. The Court supplies no replacement design and commands no destruction of artifacts. Personal liability, immunity, nominal damages, unappealed Free Exercise and state-law claims, the production dispute and independent fees or costs remain outside this merits judgment. Existing proceedings for appropriate relief and fees may continue without a new fee award from this Court.

## Event

**Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission**, No. 95-992. Merits decision, March 31, 1997. Argued October 7, 1996. On direct appeal from the three-judge District Court for the District of Columbia, 910 F. Supp. 734, after the 1994 remand. The granted question asks whether the developed record establishes advancement and fit under the intermediate scrutiny prescribed for the general full-power must-carry core.

**Statutory setting relevant to fit:** Under 47 U.S.C.§534, systems with 12 or fewer usable activated channels must carry at least three local commercial signals; systems also having 300 or fewer subscribers are exempt from that section only while they delete no broadcast signal. Systems with more than 12 channels carry eligible local commercial signals up to one-third of usable activated capacity. This is not a combined commercial-and-educational ceiling. Substantially duplicative commercial signals already represented in carriage and additional same-network affiliates need not be carried; voluntary duplicates and multiple affiliates count toward the obligation. When eligible stations exceed the required number, the operator selects among them, but cannot substitute low power for full power and must choose the elected network's affiliate with the closest city-of-license reference point to the principal headend.

Section535 adds separate educational duties. Systems with 12 or fewer channels carry one qualified local educational signal and may carry more. Systems with 13–36 carry at least one and need not carry more than three, subject to continued carriage; they may carry more. If no qualified local station is present, the former category must import one qualified educational signal and the latter at least one. For that importation, the smallest systems need not remove programming actually provided on March 29, 1990 but must use the first available channel. All qualified local educational stations carried on that date retain carriage unless operator and station give written waiver consent. Systems above36 channels, including those expanding beyond36, carry requesting qualified local educational stations; a system required to carry three need not add stations substantially duplicating another qualified local educational station requesting carriage. This is not a general three-station cap. A13–36-channel system already carrying a State-network educational affiliate need not add another same-network affiliate whose programming substantially duplicates the carried affiliate.

Commercial distant signals require indemnification of increased copyright liability. A commercial station below the specified headend levels (−45dBm UHF; −49dBm VHF) is excluded if it does not agree to bear the costs of delivering a good-quality or baseband signal. Educational carriage is not required without delivery of a good-quality or baseband signal; its distant-signal indemnity exception concerns adding a signal and expressly does not erase the continued-carriage rule. These are actual programming obligations, including primary video, audio and captions. Commercial carriage includes technically feasible program-related blanking-interval or subcarrier material; educational carriage includes technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education or language purposes. Other ancillary material remains discretionary. Positioning and availability duties also constrain operation; the law does not merely reserve empty channels. No new dispute over ancillary material, positioning, payment, notice or enforcement is decided.

The reserved low-power duty depends on an insufficient number of full-power local commercial stations: systems with35 or fewer channels carry one qualified low-power station, and larger systems two. Its disputed programming criterion concerns local needs inadequately served because of full-power stations' geographic distance. The separate market-modification factor asks whether another eligible station supplies the community news, sports or events; it is not that requirement imposed on the requesting station. These provisions' independent classifications remain outside the granted question.

## Event

**United States v.  Lanier**, No. 95-1717. Merits decision, March 31, 1997. Argued January 7, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, which vacated convictions under 18 U.S.C. §242 using a rule requiring a Supreme Court precedent involving fundamentally similar facts. The question is the fair-warning standard governing the charged official deprivations, not the ultimate validity of every count.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the en banc Sixth Circuit judgment and remands for application of the proper standard.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacate and remand, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court does not automatically reinstate the convictions or sentence. Conduct-time notice and any remaining properly presented issues require count-specific appellate review under the governing law.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous vacatur and remand | All controlling propositions and limits |

No separate writing is published.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

### Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted

**Rule:** Section 242, alone or as authoritatively construed, must make the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; a novel or unforeseeable criminal application is forbidden. Willful bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws supplies the statute's willfulness and definite-right limitations. Vagueness, lenity and the prohibition on unexpected retrospective judicial enlargement protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. The courts interpret Congress's criminal enactment rather than create common-law crimes. The reprehensibility or state criminality of the alleged conduct does not substitute for identification of a federal right and adequate notice of its application. Current decisions govern the reviewing court's legal method at their actual scopes; a decision issued after the conduct cannot itself supply earlier notice.

### Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions

**Rule:** Fair warning under §242 may be supplied by the relevant pre-existing legal landscape, including lower-court authority; it does not categorically require a Supreme Court decision on the particular application. Neither one lower-court decision nor a fixed numerical consensus is automatically sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws did not prescribe the Sixth Circuit's Supreme-Court-only restriction. Elder supplies the examination of all relevant authority in assessing clear constitutional law; actual conflicts, source authority and their relation to the charged acts matter. Excluding a whole relevant class of decisions can wrongly convert established law into unknowable law. Conversely, source breadth does not eliminate the demand for sufficient clarity in the particular context.

### Obvious applications need no factual twin

**Rule:** Existing constitutional law can give fair warning of an obviously covered application without an earlier case involving fundamentally similar facts. A broad invocation of liberty or bodily security is insufficient where the particular application remains unsettled, including where an earlier decision expressly leaves it open.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson supplies a particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness, not a demand for repetition of every factual detail. Officials cannot insist upon a decision matching their office, victim and precise method when established law applies with obvious clarity. At the same time, Elder and the actual Pinder and Uhlrig decisions preserve attention to unsettled applications and personal responsibility. Those distinctions prevent an abstract right from automatically resolving each charged assault, official nexus or notice question. The Court rejects the categorical similarity prerequisite while leaving those count-specific determinations below.

### The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact

**Rule:** Section 242 fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: constitutional unlawfulness must be apparent in light of pre-existing law. That common protection supplies neither automatic criminal liability nor a substitute for constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the Government's criminal burden of proof and every other statutory element.

**Controlling explanation:** Due process does not demand a categorically clearer legal rule in the criminal case than the clarity protection furnished in the civil inquiry. But a conclusion about civil damages immunity cannot itself convict anyone. Screws's willfulness requirement performs separate work, and judicial title alone does not establish that each act occurred under color of state law. The Sixth Circuit must keep legal notice and proof of each criminal predicate distinct, rather than use either to erase the other.

### Review the counts without the rejected legal shortcuts

**Rule:** The appellate court must reconsider the case without a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite and without categorical exclusion of due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody from §242. The judgment resolves no count's ultimate constitutional basis, fair warning, official nexus, proof or sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws and United States v. Price foreclose excluding rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process wholesale. DeShaney's limits on affirmative protection against private violence do not immunize an official's own assault merely because the victim was outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendment. Actual Collins, Pinder, Uhlrig and Nabozny preserve the distinction among personal official wrongdoing, omitted protection and supervisory attribution. These are legal directions for review, not new findings of assault, automatic restoration of the verdicts, a universal constitutional-assault formula or an order for a new evidentiary hearing.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Screws v. United States:** Applied definite-right and willfulness protections; rejected a supposed Supreme-Court-only source restriction and categorical exclusion of due-process rights.
- **United States v. Price:** Preserved §242's reach to protected due-process rights without deciding the elements of each charged count.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied particularized apparent unlawfulness; no factual-twin requirement follows.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Applied consideration of relevant existing authority while preserving temporal limits.
- **Pinder v. Johnson and Uhlrig v. Harder:** Preserved their particularized notice, unsettled-application and personal-attribution boundaries; later holdings cannot create earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Kept constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation and supervisory theories distinct; their later dates cannot establish notice for earlier conduct.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserved the difference between a constitutional deprivation and every injurious official act; no broad federal tort rule is created.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Distinguished limits on protection from private violence from the official's own asserted assault.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied the specific-provision rule when a particular Amendment governs, without treating the Fourth and Eighth Amendments as exhaustive of every abusive official act.

Pre-existing law must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness apparent, using the same legal-clarity protection as clearly-established-law analysis while preserving every distinct criminal element and burden. The Court creates no new substantive right or count-specific liability holding.

## Event

**Young v. Fordice**, No. 95-2031. Merits decision, March 31, 1997. Argued January 6, 1997. On direct appeal from a three-judge district court that granted summary judgment for Mississippi in litigation over National Voter Registration Act implementation and Voting Rights Act §5 preclearance. The questions concern the operative registration baseline, discretionary changes in the New System and the reach of earlier approval.

## Participation

## Public Action

The Court reverses the district court judgment and remands for proceedings enforcing the preclearance requirement at its proper scope.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reverse and remand, 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Mississippi may not administer the covered discretionary changes without §5 preclearance. The district court must identify the components and submission status and enter prospective relief consistent with lawful federal registration duties and its remedial authority. No merged rolls, individual registration cancellation, discrimination finding or particular interim system is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Unanimous reversal and remand | All holdings and qualifications |

No separate writing is published.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins; every following proposition controls at its stated scope.

### Federal implementation does not exempt discretionary voting changes

**Rule:** A covered jurisdiction must preclear new voter-registration practices embodying state policy choices even when adopted to comply with federal legislation. The NVRA preserves Voting Rights Act obligations; this decision does not decide an act compelled in every detail by federal law and leaving no state discretion.

**Controlling explanation:** Voting Rights Coalition preserves the valid federal-registration duties and distinct state-election questions; it supplies no automatic preclearance. Presley locates this case within practices directly governing registration and voting. Lopez prevents state-law compulsion from excusing a covered jurisdiction's administration of voting changes, but the Court need not mechanically extend that ruling to the different wholly federal hypothetical. Mississippi retained choices about instructions, forms, agencies, assistance, reporting and how applicants understand federal-only registration. Federal compliance does not exempt those choices from the statutory sequence.

### This Provisional Plan never became the §5 comparison baseline

**Rule:** The Provisional Plan did not become a practice in force or effect for §5 comparison on the combined circumstances of forty-one days of registration use, adoption by only about one-third of registration officials, no election held or imminent, and prompt abandonment when anticipated state legislation would not be enacted. The Old System supplies the benchmark for this New System challenge; no single listed circumstance creates a general threshold.

**Controlling explanation:** Perkins and Lockhart preserve the principle that a practice may be operative despite a state-law defect. State illegality alone therefore cannot exclude a baseline. Here the limited use and uptake, nonelectoral setting and prompt abandonment together establish the tentative character of the arrangement, rather than a settled registration practice displaced later. Actual registrations are not erased from the facts: the Court finds neither that nobody registered nor that nobody relied on the plan. It determines the system-level §5 comparison, not whether every individual registration was legally valid, and adopts no forty-one-day, one-third-adoption, intent or election-use bright line.

### The New System still changes the Old System

**Rule:** Retention of old state-election qualifications does not remove §5 coverage where the New System changes the Old System's registration instructions, agency designations, manuals, reporting, assistance or communication of federal-only and combined registration status. Both minor and major covered changes require preclearance without prior proof that they harm minority voters.

**Controlling explanation:** Allen and Hampton County require submission of changes in voting practices, not just changes deemed major or discriminatory in the enforcement suit. Mississippi's New System introduces discretionary procedures absent from the Old System; calling it a return to old state qualifications does not restore the former machinery. The changes can affect who registers, the information received and the administration of forms and voter categories. This supplies an independent coverage ground even though the Provisional Plan itself never became the benchmark. Substantive purpose and effect belong to the designated preclearance process, not this coverage decision.

### Approval covers the identified submission, not every later contingency

**Rule:** Preclearance of a submitted registration arrangement does not approve materially different later choices that the submission did not identify. The submitting jurisdiction must describe its changes sufficiently; ambiguity does not require the Attorney General to infer and approve every possible later arrangement.

**Controlling explanation:** McCain and Lopez require accurate identification of what was submitted and approved. The earlier submission proceeded on an arrangement allowing NVRA registration to serve federal and state elections; the later dual arrangement's new communications and administration were not submitted as such. Neither federal enactment nor the Attorney General's knowledge of legislative uncertainty supplies blanket approval of unpresented choices. Particular genuine submission questions remain for the district court on the actual terms. The Court does not rescind the earlier approval or make it subject to judicial review merely because it fails to cover a later change.

### Enforce nonuse through lawful, fitted relief

**Rule:** The enforcement court determines coverage and submission status and enforces nonuse of covered unprecleared practices; only the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia supplies substantive preclearance. Relief must preserve lawful NVRA duties and remain within independent remedial authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Clark, Morse, Shaw and Lopez preserve that division of responsibility. Calling a State-selected plan court ordered does not exempt it. Under Connor and McDaniel, a necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by a court may operate without separate §5 approval only when independent lawful authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement. No such plan or emergency exception is selected here. The district court must identify the covered changes, enforce nonuse and permit expeditious use of the designated channels without blocking compliance with a federal command. The Court orders no identical rolls, racial-purpose or retrogression finding, damages, cancellation of registrations or completed-election consequence.

## Event

Opuku-Boateng v. California. Merits decision, April 7, 1997, on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 95 F.3d 1461, No. 94-16542. The Ninth Circuit reversed the District Court's trial judgment for California and remanded for appropriate relief. The questions are whether Title VII protects a public-employment applicant's known religious conflict on the same terms as an employee's, and whether California established undue hardship from the supported scheduling alternatives under the governing accommodation standard. ## Participation

## Public Action

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |

## Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification stated below.

**1. Applicants receive the same statutory protection.** Title VII's prohibition on refusing to hire because of religion and its definition of religion require reasonable accommodation of an applicant's known sincere religious practice on the same terms that protect an employee. Public employment creates neither an exemption from that duty nor an enhanced duty; prior temporary service is not a prerequisite.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 2000e-2(a)(1) and 2000e(j) expressly connect refusal to hire with religious observance and the employer's accommodation defense. Opuku-Boateng disclosed the Sabbath conflict, and the conceded prima facie case brings California's refusal to complete his appointment within that inquiry. The State's operational concerns therefore go to accommodation and hardship, not to whether an applicant is protected. The Court decides no separate constitutional claim.

**2. Supported predictions may establish hardship, but good-faith discussions are not themselves an accommodation.** An employer must offer an effective reasonable accommodation or show that accommodation would impose more than a de minimis hardship under the governing Hardison rule. When no effective accommodation has been supplied, that showing must address supported materially distinct proposals in the actual operation; it may rest on concrete noncash costs and reasonable, evidence-supported operational predictions without a trial arrangement, realized accident, complaint, preferred accommodation or exhaustion of every conceivable alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Hardison preserves protected seniority rights and legitimate costs or transferred burdens; Beadle and Brown require a practice-specific connection between the proposed arrangement and those burdens while allowing supported professional predictions. Ansonia ends the inquiry when an effective reasonable accommodation has already been offered. California discussed the conflict in good faith for a week, but those discussions produced no effective accommodation. Good faith is relevant conduct, not a substitute for either statutory answer. Nor does Title VII demand an experiment or make negotiation a freestanding prerequisite to an otherwise sufficient hardship defense. Noncontractual coworker burdens, necessary coverage, efficiency and security can count; hostility to religious practice and abstract future requests do not by themselves carry the burden.

**3. California's completed proof does not sustain its hardship defense.** On this record, neutral Saturday rotation and the asserted morale and operational concerns do not establish the required hardship from supported combinations of other undesirable shifts and voluntary trades. This conclusion neither declares every proposed arrangement feasible nor treats all shifts as equally burdensome to every employee.

**Controlling explanation:** Opuku-Boateng offered nights, Sundays, holidays and other undesirable work. The station had no established collective-bargaining or contractual seniority restriction, but that absence alone does not make all coworker interests immaterial. The State failed to identify the material burden transferred by the offered combinations. The poll's occurrence was admitted; its reported results were expressly not admitted for their truth and cannot establish collective unwillingness. Lozano addressed a mistakenly understood three-day absence, while Whitacre could not recall the question or number surveyed. Those limitations do not prove that every employee misunderstood, but leave the cited evidence insufficient to establish that adequate voluntary combinations were unavailable. The testimony favoring varied schedules to prevent collusion did not connect this limited Sabbath absence, with variation on other days, to a concrete security loss. Existing flexibility for courses and a minister's Sunday mornings reinforces the need for that specific comparison without creating an unlimited exemption. Rejecting one permanent substitute did not answer the supported combinations. The absence of an implemented experiment is not independently decisive.

**4. Clear-error review preserves permissible findings, not the use of excluded evidence or an incorrect legal rule.** Rule 52 protects supported trial findings and reasonable operational inferences. It does not require sustaining a hardship conclusion that depends on using evidence for a purpose expressly excluded, a materially mistaken description of the requested absence, or equating neutral rotation with adequate accommodation; the remaining completed proof here cannot carry the defense under the correct predictive standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The strongest argument for restoring the District Court's judgment is its experience with the station's needs and Hardison's low hardship threshold. Those considerations would support an evidence-based finding about scheduling, coverage, security or transferred burdens. They do not remedy the particular evidentiary and legal defects identified here. Beadle permits remand where materially distinct proposals remain for proper assessment; Brown permits preserving resolved liability when a completed record cannot sustain the existing defense. California had its trial opportunity under that defense, and no new burden or exclusion preventing its proof is established. The Court therefore affirms the Ninth Circuit's judgment without endorsing a compulsory experiment, exhaustive negotiation or realized-disruption rule, and does not reopen 1982 liability for a new hardship case.

**5. Relief remains source-authorized and dependent on the facts relevant to remedy.** The remand determines lawful employment and equitable relief, backpay and mitigation, any present basis for prospective relief, and fees and costs under their ordinary predicates. Liability does not guarantee appointment, a transfer vacancy, a particular schedule, a fixed award or a continuing injunction; the employer may choose an effective accommodation, and a permanent schedule may be prescribed only if lawful and necessary after that opportunity.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII relief should place Opuku-Boateng as nearly as lawful and practicable in the position he would have occupied absent discrimination. Current staffing or hiring conditions may affect placement and prospective relief without reopening the failed defense to the original refusal to hire. The 1991 Act's new compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury entitlement do not apply retrospectively to the 1982 conduct, as Brown's temporal remedial distinction confirms. Ordinary mitigation, equitable discretion and fee predicates remain. No Free Exercise, individual constitutional-liability or mixed-motive question is decided, and no general right to a preferred shift is created.

## Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification below.

**1. Candidacy does not waive bodily privacy.** Government-required urine collection and chemical analysis are Fourth Amendment searches. Choosing to seek public office does not waive that protection; candidacy's public setting may inform reasonableness but does not itself authorize a compelled bodily search.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner establishes the search character of compelled urinalysis, and Vernonia preserves that conclusion even where participation in an activity is voluntary. A candidate's exposure to public scrutiny of qualifications is different from consent to bodily collection and medical analysis. Georgia's use of a personal physician and initial disclosure to the candidate reduce the intrusion, but do not eliminate the protected interest. The Court evaluates those actual safeguards and attributes no unsupported public disclosure, observed collection, police use or unrelated medical testing to the program.

**2. Georgia has not supplied a special need sufficient to justify this candidate-testing program.** A suspicionless search justified by a need beyond ordinary law enforcement must be reasonable in light of the actual governmental need, the program's operation, and the affected privacy interests. General desires for drug-free officials and public confidence do not supply the substantial, concrete need required for this broad advance-scheduled candidate program; its reduced intrusion cannot make up that deficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner and Von Raab permit special-needs searches when the protective or integrity interests and the program's operation justify the departure from individualized suspicion. Vernonia sustains its particular school-athletic program through custodial responsibility, contextual privacy and protective need. Political candidates do not share that setting. Georgia's covered offices have no common duty supplying a comparable protective risk, and the record identifies neither a concrete drug problem nor another substantial operational need connecting this broad class to the search. Sound judgment, integrity and deterrence remain legitimate interests, but do not explain why a negative certificate from one predictable test, scheduled within thirty days before qualification, adequately answers the asserted concern about future service. Advance scheduling limits what the certificate establishes; it does not make every scheduled test useless or unconstitutional. Nor must a State wait for a catastrophe, quantify efficacy, exhaust every alternative, or show safety-sensitive duties in every conceivable valid program. The holding rests on this program's combined deficiencies, not a mandatory checklist of missing facts. Georgia's strongest argument—the importance of trustworthy officials and the modest intrusion—therefore fails because a modest bodily search still needs an adequate governmental justification.

**3. The live prospective challenge requires relief against enforcement, without altering past elections.** Chandler's stated and uncontested intention to run again preserves the controversy despite completion of the 1994 election. The judgment sustaining the challenged requirement is reversed, and the case is remanded for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief barring its enforcement.

**Controlling explanation:** The continuing qualification condition affects Chandler's future candidacy, so resolving that live Fourth Amendment dispute does not require setting aside a completed election. The Court prescribes no replacement testing regime or new candidate qualification, and decides no damages, drug use, test result or individualized disqualification. The holding leaves materially different programs to their own supported special-needs justification. Independent First Amendment or state-law qualification claims, medical-fitness examinations, financial disclosure, particularized-suspicion testing, security screening tied to an actual safety risk, and private-sector testing are not decided.

Public prominence and reduced intrusion alone do not provide it; neither a proven catastrophe nor exhaustion of all alternatives becomes a universal condition.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | For the unanimous judgment | Entire opinion; every holding below |

## Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each following proposition has nine direct votes. Scalia and Kennedy also state a separate, noncontrolling reservation about the original meaning of §1983; it changes none of these joins or the mandate.

**1. A §1983 statutory claim must identify an individual right.** A claimant must identify the particular statutory provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding rather than merely precatory obligation, and defining the asserted individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than leaving it vague or amorphous. The text, statutory setting and actual entitlement asserted govern; violation of a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish the right.

**Controlling explanation:** Wright and Wilder recognize defined individual entitlements within funding statutes; they do not make an entire program an indivisible promise. Livadas separately identifies the federal right, the §1983 channel, congressional foreclosure, defendant and remedy. Suter likewise preserves provision-specific adjudication rather than one answer for all state-plan provisions. The families must therefore identify what each particular command entitles them to demand. Program importance, mandatory wording and remedial usefulness cannot take the place of that inquiry; qualitative language is not universally unenforceable merely because applying it requires judgment.

**2. Title IV-D supplies no individual right to general administrative adequacy.** The federal substantial-compliance measure does not give each beneficiary a personal right to compel systemwide compliance. The same general theory fails when restated as an entitlement to sufficient agency staffing or satisfactory computerized administration as such; rejection of those theories does not decide whether a distinct, actually asserted provision creates a particular individual right.

**Controlling explanation:** The substantial-compliance measure evaluates aggregate performance for federal oversight and funding consequences. A State may meet that benchmark while failing one family; aggregate success cannot extinguish an independently existing personal right. Section 654(3) directs a single, separate organizational unit meeting prescribed staffing requirements, but identifies no beneficiary's right to a particular staff complement, caseload or assigned worker. Former and amended §654(16), their associated requirements and §654a's management and performance functions similarly organize program administration. Their technical detail does not make the system itself an individual entitlement. Mandatory duties important to effective collections therefore do not sustain the general claim recognized below. Alleged shortages, backlogs and accounting defects may still bear on a proved violation or fitted remedy, but create no right by themselves. No separately pleaded claim to each standardized computer requirement, confidentiality protection or individual transmission deadline is established here. The Court adjudicates no individual clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 and announces no categorical rule against regulatory elaboration of an established statutory right. A genuinely distinct claim must receive its own provision-specific inquiry, rather than automatic approval or rejection because it appears in an administrative section.

**3. General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983.** Once a provision creates an individual federal right, that right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes the channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Title IV-D's federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions do not alone foreclose every otherwise existing individual claim; any specific incompatible remedy must be assessed for the actual provision and claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Livadas separates enforceability from whether a right exists. Funding sanctions regulate the federal-state relationship and offer no general administrative forum for a family to obtain case-specific relief. Their detail and practical importance do not create the incompatibility found in the different remedial arrangements of Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson. Wright and Wilder likewise reject automatic foreclosure from federal administrative enforcement. The District Court's chapterwide preclusion ground cannot support dismissal of every possible concrete right. This holding does not establish a particular right, excuse ordinary defendant or relief requirements, or resolve a hypothetical provision-specific remedy not identified here.

**4. State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 prohibit rejecting enforceability merely because a Social Security Act provision appears among requirements for a state plan or its contents. They do not dispense with the individual-right inquiry or alter the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not enforceable in a private action.

**Controlling explanation:** These provisions apply to actions pending on their respective October 20 and October 31, 1994 enactment dates and actions brought thereafter, including this action. Their text leaves other established private-action grounds intact except for overturning any state-plan ground applied in Suter but not in earlier enforcement decisions, and expressly saves the §671(a)(15) result. The Court applies Suter's actual provision-specific rights and separate implied-action holdings, including its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) case-plan claim. No broader state-plan immunity is imported through the statutory reference, and no every-provision entitlement follows from removing that categorical obstacle.

**5. Madrid's concrete distribution allegation and the operative law require separate examination.** A broad prayer for programwide relief does not erase Madrid's allegation that collected support was not distributed under former §657(b)(1). The District Court must identify each asserted provision, conduct, remedy, governing version and effective date before deciding right, violation and relief; neither a superseded duty nor a duty not yet effective may silently support an injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** Former §657(b)(1) is materially more specific than an aggregate administrative benchmark, but specificity does not decide Madrid's unadjudicated eligibility, payment facts, legal right, defendant or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not promise a fresh $50 for each installment regardless of timing. Subsection (b) covers collections under an approved plan in fiscal years beginning after September 30, 1976, subject to subsection (d)'s distinct foster-care rules. Those rules first reimburse current foster-care maintenance, direct qualifying excess up to ordered support to the placement agency for the child's interests, then reimburse unrepaid past foster-care or family-assistance payments from the remainder, with the federal financing share reimbursed appropriately and any balance going to that agency for the child's interests. The agency may reserve its payments for future needs or make them available to the person meeting the child's daily needs. No relevant exception or payment fact is established for Madrid. The 1996 changes likewise have separate operative dates and transitions; compilation together does not make all duties presently applicable. The court below must apply them to identified claims and dates, without inferring Arizona implementation or default.

**Statutory timing limits on the remand:**

- Pub. L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.
- Under §395(b), the applicable date is the later of the otherwise specified date or the effective date of implementing state legislation, but no later than the first day of the first calendar quarter after the close of the first regular legislative session beginning after August 22, 1996. Each year of a two-year session counts separately. Under §395(c), a State unable to comply without amending its constitution is protected from a noncompliance finding until the earlier of one year after the necessary amendment takes effect or five years after August 22, 1996. Neither rule establishes an Arizona event or compliance fact here.

No Arizona qualification for it is found, and these future duties are not April 1997 mandates merely because they appear in the compilation.

**6. Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation.** On remand, the District Court must identify and assess the actual individual claims, then any §1983 foreclosure, proper defendant, breach, causation and lawful relief. Neither general administrative failure nor rejection of the programwide theory authorizes an undifferentiated substantial-compliance injunction, blanket dismissal, specified payment, damages award or prescribed staffing or computer redesign.

**Controlling explanation:** General administrative shortcomings can be evidence relevant to an established right and a remedy fitted to its violation. They are not a general commission to supervise Arizona's program. The Court decides no directly implied Title IV-D action through the §1983 inquiry. The Eleventh Amendment argument and request to overrule Maine v. Thiboutot were neither presented below nor included in the certiorari questions and are not reached; that preservation limit does not resolve every future defendant-specific question. The Ninth Circuit's undifferentiated right cannot stand, but the State's requested dismissal of every possible particular claim does not follow.

## Law After Decision

Title IV-D's aggregate compliance measure and general administrative machinery create no individual entitlement to systemwide adequacy. Specific actual claims remain subject to the individual-right test and a separate congressional-foreclosure inquiry. General federal oversight does not alone displace §1983, and state-plan location does not alone defeat enforcement. Remedy must fit an established right and violation under the applicable statutory version; no individual distribution right or statewide structural remedy is newly established.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy, concurring.** Both join the Court's application of existing precedent and its full mandate. They reserve whether a beneficiary of a federal-state funding agreement holds a right secured by law within §1983's original meaning, rather than standing only as a third-party beneficiary of a contract between governments. Their concern is that such a beneficiary's claim historically belonged to the contracting governments, not necessarily to the beneficiary through this statutory action. They acknowledge that Wright and Wilder permitted beneficiary suits and that this foundational question was not raised there. They leave it for further consideration; the concurrence creates no categorical bar to spending-statute claims, identifies no additional reason to dismiss these plaintiffs' concrete allegations, and changes no holding of the Court.

## Event

New Mexico Department of Human Services v. Joseph A. and Josephine A. Argued December 3, 1996; decided April 21, 1997. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, 69 F.3d 1081, No. 94-2160. The September 23, 1983 foster-care decree permits termination after twelve consecutive months of substantial and continuous compliance. The District Court terminated it on June 30, 1994, adopting reports from June 1989 and April 30, 1993; the Tenth Circuit vacated and remanded on November 9, 1995 and denied rehearing December 14, 1995. Review concerns the decree's release standard, the adequacy of Rule 52 findings and methodology, and the distinction between performance-based termination and Rufo modification. The separate 1994 monitoring-fee judgment is outside the grant.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Tenth Circuit is affirmed. Its vacatur of termination and remand for adequate findings remain in effect.

## Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The decree remains operative while the District Court makes adequate findings on the existing record or conducts lawful additional proceedings. Termination may follow supported findings of the agreed twelve consecutive months of substantial and continuous compliance; the Court decides neither actual compliance nor actual noncompliance and supplies no new clock or fixed release date.

## Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification below.

**1. The agreed termination condition governs.** The Department may obtain termination by establishing twelve consecutive months of substantial and continuous compliance with the decree's essential purposes and specified obligations. Immaterial departures from particular criteria need not defeat release, but general improvement cannot excuse a material failure to establish fair, reasonable and timely adoption decisionmaking and fair, reasonable and adequate procedures and practices securing access to permanent homes.

**Controlling explanation:** ITT Continental Baking directs attention to the consent decree's agreed obligations, interpreted in light of their purposes rather than replaced by a new decree. The 1983 judgment protects both the children's right to those obligations and the Department's right to termination when its own condition is met. Substantial compliance is not perfect compliance, nor is it whatever an unexplained overall impression approves. The court must relate material deviations to the essential objectives and the obligations chosen to accomplish them. No universal percentage creates a safe harbor. The required institutional procedures must genuinely be in place; that is an existing obligation, not a new indefinite guarantee against every future lapse or an extension of the bargained twelve-month period.

**2. Rule 52 requires findings that disclose why the condition is or is not satisfied.** The District Court must supply enough subsidiary findings and explanation of material data, case evidence and methodology to permit review of its ultimate compliance conclusion. Rule 52(a) applies to a master's findings to the extent adopted; a lengthy evidence summary or repetition of the conclusion cannot replace the material findings, though every item and irrelevant detail need not be recited.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley v. Everglades Drainage District requires findings sufficient to reveal the factual basis of the ultimate conclusion. Supported findings retain clear-error protection. That protection does not allow an appellate court to supply an unexpressed methodology, resolve credibility disputes or weigh conflicting evidence in place of the trial tribunal. The State's strongest contrary point is that an experienced judge adopted extensive reports and substantial compliance tolerates minor defects. Those considerations do not answer omissions about essential obligations and material conflicting evidence. Meaningful review requires an intelligible account, not a transcript of every evidentiary choice or a newly prescribed supervision system.

**3. The adopted reports omit material connections needed to review termination.** The reports do not adequately explain how their methodology and competing quantitative and qualitative evidence support the finding of substantial and continuous compliance. The Court therefore sustains vacatur of termination and remand for adequate findings, without deciding whether the Department has actually complied.

**Controlling explanation:** The report describes conference measures ranging from 6.2 to 63 percent and other content measures from 31 to 97 percent, but does not explain their relationship to its compliance conclusions. It likewise fails to account adequately for timely attorney notification of 26 percent and no notification for 8 percent; discounts adverse individual cases as selected instances without explaining why they are unrepresentative rather than evidence of broader defects; and does not adequately resolve the timely ADAPT-data challenge or explain or discredit testimony that custody duration increased from 1.5 to 2.1 years. These are evidence and explanation problems, not this Court's acceptance of every adverse measure or finding of systemic failure. A reasoned account may show why a shortfall is immaterial or an opposing measure more reliable, but it must come from the factfinding tribunal. The appeal is from the final termination order adopting both reports, and is not confined to the 1993 report; the 1989 report had already been found inadequate standing alone. The later State Auditor report and proposed supplemental study, not before the Special Master, supply no adverse finding. Timely quality-assurance evidence actually considered below is distinct.

**4. Proof of performance and modification of obligations are different inquiries.** The Department need not prove a significant change under Rufo to establish performance of this decree's existing termination condition. A request instead to reduce still-binding obligations because law or facts have changed must satisfy Rufo's changed-circumstances and tailoring requirements; ordinary compliance review cannot rewrite the decree to a constitutional minimum.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo governs relief from duties whose continued enforcement is said to have become inequitable. Its threshold serves a different question from whether agreed duties have been performed for the required period. Freeman and Missouri v. Jenkins reinforce the need to connect release findings to the actual remedy in their school-desegregation settings; neither substitutes the full school-release inquiry for this foster-care decree's language. No prison-conditions predicate for applying the PLRA is established. The Court imposes neither a new constitutional-violation prerequisite for enforcing the existing judgment nor an independent changed-circumstances hurdle for proving compliance.

**5. Remand preserves the decree and its lawful route to termination.** The existing decree remains effective unless lawfully terminated or modified. The District Court must address the material deviations, explain relevant methodology and resolve the material factual conflicts necessary for review, then enter the result warranted by supported findings under the existing twelve-month condition, including termination if that condition is proved.

**Controlling explanation:** Retaining the decree during that determination enforces the judgment already entered without predicting the result. The Court starts no new twelve-month clock, fixes no release date and requires no plaintiff consent, consensus, neutral-reviewer endorsement, new monitor or guarantee against every future lapse. It does not order automatic retrial of settled facts or automatic admission of new submissions; any additional proceedings remain governed by ordinary authority. No §1988 fee issue is before the Court: the separate monitoring-fee appeal at 28 F.3d 1056 is outside this grant, including prevailing-party eligibility, necessity of monitoring work and findings supporting a fee amount.

- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail: Preserves its significant-change and suitable-tailoring rule for modification; it creates no additional hurdle to proving performance under an existing release clause and permits no automatic reduction to the constitutional minimum.
