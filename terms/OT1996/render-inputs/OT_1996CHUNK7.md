## Event

**Case:** Kadic v. Karadzic; Doe v. Karadzic. Consolidated review of Second Circuit Nos. 94-9035 and 94-9069; 70 F.3d 232 and 74 F.3d 377.
**Event and date:** Merits decision, April 21, 1997; October Term 1996, chunk 7, inventory 73.
**Route and chronology:** On writs of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Second Circuit; granted October 7, 1996 and submitted on the merits. The judgments below reversed the district court's blanket jurisdictional dismissal and remanded.
**Questions:** Whether §1350 reaches the alleged private or nonstate genocide and war crimes; which asserted norms require official action; and whether political-question or related foreign-affairs grounds require dismissal. The included domestic-source question is distinct from jurisdiction. Ultimate liability and amount of relief are not presented.
**Posture:** Karadzic seeks restoration of dismissal. The two plaintiff groups seek continuation on allegations of killing, rape, forced prostitution and impregnation, torture, and a personally planned or directed campaign against protected ethnic or religious groups. Those allegations are not findings. The consolidated review does not merge their distinct service circumstances.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participate at submission and decision. Nine participate; the six-member quorum is satisfied.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Second Circuit judgment reversing dismissal and remands both actions for further proceedings consistent with its opinion.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

All nine also support renewed consideration of supplemental claims, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only in that component's disposition and in the particular service disposition. ## Holdings

### I. Jurisdiction and the domestic action are separate questions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350 gives the district court jurisdiction over these substantial allegations by aliens of torts violating the law of nations; it imposes no universal official-action requirement on norms that bind private individuals. A federal forum, an applicable international norm, a domestic civil action, this defendant's responsibility, and authority for the requested remedy remain separate requirements.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls. Unless a different coalition is specified below, Parts IV–VI have that same nine-Justice support; Parts II–III have the seven-Justice coalition identified in the opinion table.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Abebe-Jira's separation of jurisdiction from liability. The complaints allege international wrongs, rather than merely an ordinary domestic assault involving foreign persons. Their asserted source of civil liability presents a substantive question within that jurisdiction. A norm's personal scope must be determined from that norm: an official-action requirement attached to one prohibition cannot be transferred to another that expressly binds individuals. Suter supplies the distinction between an enforceable obligation and an action; neither that distinction nor an eventual failure on the merits defeats jurisdiction over this pleaded category. The allegations do not establish that a plaintiff has proved every required element.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's forum/action distinction is applied; Suter's source-specific distinction between obligation and action remains effective.

### II. A confined civil action reaches personally committed or directed genocide

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350's reception of law-of-nations tort adjudication supports a domestic civil action against an individual for personally perpetrating, planning, or directing genocide, whether or not that individual acted under state authority. The plaintiff must establish intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group as such, through killing members, causing serious bodily or mental harm, deliberately imposing conditions calculated to bring about the group's physical destruction in whole or in part, imposing measures intended to prevent births, or forcibly transferring children to another group, together with the defendant's qualifying responsibility, causation, and a lawful basis for relief.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas accept the norm's private coverage but reject this additional domestic action.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira recognizes the particular statutory reception of a determinate personal tort while reserving private violence. The Court now resolves that reservation for genocide. Article IV of the Genocide Convention expressly subjects private individuals to its prohibition, and Article II supplies the defined destructive acts and group-destruction intent. These provisions identify the obligatory norm; the distinct domestic source is the received-action construction of §1350. The Convention and the criminal implementation chapter are not themselves treated as civil causes. Section 1092's disclaimer of an enforceable substantive or procedural right created by that criminal chapter remains fully effective. It does not repeal this independently received action, and the criminal statute's territorial and nationality limits govern prosecution rather than silently amending §1350. The contrary argument correctly demands a domestic source but gives insufficient effect to the particular reception function already recognized in Abebe-Jira. The alleged personal planning and direction of a campaign against protected groups suffice to defeat categorical dismissal. Ethnic cleansing, rape, forced pregnancy, and murder must still satisfy the defined act and specific intent; their labels prove neither. No universal action for every international norm or liability from command title follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira is extended to this separately defined private wrong, while its official-torture holding remains intact. Atherton's rejection of common law based merely on a general federal interest is preserved. Central Bank's confined received-action method supplies an analogy, not an international aiding-and-abetting standard.

### III. The pleaded core war crimes do not require a recognized state actor

**Holding and operative rule:** The same confined received domestic action reaches personally perpetrated, planned, or directed deliberate killing, torture, and grave sexual violence against protected persons as core war crimes, without requiring that the defendant act for a diplomatically recognized state. Each claim requires the relevant armed conflict, protected victim, prohibited act, connection between the act and the conflict, personal responsibility, causation, and independently supported relief; the Court recognizes no civil action for every breach of a treaty provision.

**Authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by the same seven Justices as Part II. Scalia and Thomas accept the individual international obligations but reject the additional received civil action.

**Operative norm and limits:** Common Article 3 binds each party to a noninternational armed conflict occurring in the territory of a High Contracting Party. It protects persons taking no active part in hostilities, including members of armed forces who have laid down their arms and persons out of combat through sickness, wounds, detention, or any other cause. It requires humane treatment without adverse distinction based on race, colour, religion or faith, sex, birth, wealth, or similar criteria. At any time and place it prohibits, against those persons, violence to life and person, especially murder, mutilation, cruel treatment and torture; hostage-taking; outrages upon personal dignity, especially humiliating and degrading treatment; and sentences or executions without a previous judgment by a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees. Its further commands and permissions remain distinct: the wounded and sick must be collected and cared for; an impartial humanitarian body may offer services; the parties should endeavour to put other Convention provisions into force through special agreements; application does not affect the parties' legal status. The civil holding concerns the pleaded core wrongs, not automatic enforcement of every one of these clauses.

**Controlling explanation:** Common Article 3 applies to each party to the defined conflict. A categorical recognized-state requirement would defeat that individual protection. Abebe-Jira's particular reception rationale supplies the domestic source; neither the existence of a treaty obligation nor the seriousness of an alleged injury alone creates a civil action. Yamashita confirms individual duties in war, but its military adjudication does not supply this domestic civil action or decide an absent commander's liability. The complaints' allegations of personal planning and direction permit proceedings on the defined claims. They do not establish protected status, the conflict connection, any defendant's responsibility, or damages. The Court does not determine Protocol II's customary status, decide alternative international-conflict theories, adopt a comprehensive command-responsibility rule, or transplant Central Bank's securities-assistance standard. These reservations preserve questions unnecessary to the pleaded deliberate-conduct ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's received-action rationale is extended at this confined scope. Yamashita is applied only for individual international obligations, preserving the difference between military prosecution and civil liability. Central Bank remains confined to its securities setting.

### IV. Official torture and the enacted TVPA route retain their separate conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Abebe-Jira's action remains available for deliberate personal participation in or supervision of severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment, or intimidation; ordinary private torture or killing outside a proved genocide or war-crime setting requires an independently authorized route. The Torture Victim Protection Act separately authorizes damages against an individual who, under actual or apparent authority or color of law of a foreign nation, subjects an individual to the statute's defined torture or extrajudicial killing, subject to its beneficiaries, exhaustion requirement, ten-year limit, definitions, and exclusions stated here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion has nine direct joins. Scalia and Thomas apply the controlling official-torture holding without withdrawing their disagreement with its source; they independently accept the TVPA's enacted action.

**Statutory conditions:** Torture damages run to the injured individual; killing damages run to the individual's legal representative or a person entitled to claim in a wrongful-death action. The court shall decline to hear a claim when adequate and available remedies in the place where the conduct occurred have not been exhausted. Suit must commence within ten years after the cause arose. Extrajudicial killing means a deliberated killing not authorized by a previous judgment of a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees; it excludes a killing lawfully carried out under foreign national authority under international law. Torture requires intentionally inflicted severe physical or mental pain or suffering against a person in the offender's custody or physical control, excluding pain arising only from, inherent in, or incidental to lawful sanctions. Its purposes include obtaining information or a confession from the victim or a third person; punishing the victim for an act the victim or a third person committed or is suspected of committing; intimidating or coercing the victim or a third person; and discrimination of any kind. Mental pain means prolonged mental harm caused by intentional or threatened severe physical pain; administration, application, or threatened use of mind-altering substances or procedures calculated to disrupt profoundly the senses or personality; threatened imminent death; or a threat that another person imminently will suffer death, severe physical pain or suffering, or those substances or procedures.

**Controlling explanation:** An enacted cause remains distinct from a received action. The TVPA permits these claims to be considered under its actual words; neither a generalized allegation of cruelty nor inability to identify a better forum establishes its statutory conditions. Its definitions do not automatically rewrite every element of Abebe-Jira's separate action. Where torture or killing also satisfies the distinct genocide or war-crime norm, Parts II–III govern that additional route; ordinary private violence acquires no federal cause merely from its gravity. Section 1331 supplies federal-question jurisdiction for the enacted TVPA claim, but the Court does not decide whether it supplies a universal customary-law action. Nor is a further independent ATS action for ordinary official execution necessary to the disposition. The absence of a finding about exhaustion, timeliness, or a defined element requires claim-specific adjudication, not assumed compliance or forfeiture.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's official-torture action and limits are applied without alteration. Suter's separation of statutory obligation, action, and relief is preserved.

### V. Nonrecognition alone does not defeat an adequately alleged official connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Diplomatic nonrecognition does not categorically foreclose proof of governmental attributes or concerted action with an existing foreign state where the applicable norm requires official action. The TVPA still requires actual or apparent authority or color of law attributable to a foreign nation; a claimed title or mere semblance of authority is insufficient by itself.

**Authority:** Part V of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court's blanket approach failed to address the alternative allegations of concerted action with Yugoslavia and Serbia. The plaintiffs may also attempt to establish the alleged government's population, territory, governmental organization, and capacity for relations. Allowing proof does not diplomatically recognize a government or establish its attributes. Lugar supplies a useful analogy concerning joint action with government, but does not transfer every §1983 rule into international law or the TVPA. The operative source must establish the necessary official connection in each claim. Neither an alleged presidency nor the Court's rejection of categorical dismissal supplies that proof.

**Precedent treatment:** Lugar's concerted-action analysis supplies a bounded analogy; its domestic §1983 framework is not adopted wholesale. Abebe-Jira's official-authority requirement remains effective for its own norm.

### VI. The asserted foreign-affairs objection does not require dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** These defined claims against an individual are not categorically nonjusticiable merely because they arise during a foreign civil war or concern a political leader. Dismissal as a political question requires an identified constitutional commitment, absence of manageable judicial standards, or other concrete obstacle under Baker; none is established by this record.

**Authority:** Part VI of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires examination of the issue the court must actually decide. The Court can apply defined personal-tort elements without selecting Bosnia's government, fixing borders, or directing negotiations. Abebe-Jira applies that distinction to foreign individual misconduct, while Nixon concerns a particular Senate impeachment procedure and supplies no categorical foreign-affairs bar. The published United States position expressly disclaims a political-question obstacle; it reinforces, rather than replaces, the independent judicial inquiry. No present executive recognition or immunity determination in this defendant's favor is established. Act of state was not asserted below and is not decided. The absence of an established suitable alternative forum on this record neither creates a worldwide forum duty nor resolves any properly supported later forum contention.

**Precedent treatment:** Baker and Abebe-Jira are applied. Nixon's issue-specific nonjusticiability holding remains confined to its actual constitutional and procedural setting.

### VII. Invitee status supplies no general immunity; particular service questions remain

**Holding and operative rule:** The United Nations Headquarters Agreement gives these invitees no general immunity from suit or from the reported service outside the headquarters district: §9's consent requirement for service inside that district, §15's privileges for designated member-state representatives, and §11's transit protections do not establish that asserted immunity. Kadic's court-authorized delivery through a security officer and admitted receipt permit continuation; Doe's intercepted hotel-lobby delivery, disputed distance, and remaining service issues require their own adjudication.

**Authority:** Part VII's rejection of general invitee immunity is joined by all nine. Its plaintiff-specific service application is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition without joining this additional service rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Agreement differentiates the headquarters district, designated representatives, and travel to the United Nations. It does not convert every invitee into a diplomat immune from civil proceedings. The State Department letter reports no invitee immunity. A narrowly presented question about direct airport-to-headquarters transit remains open because the reported service circumstances do not present it. The two plaintiffs' delivery records cannot be collapsed: acknowledged receipt under an alternate-service order in Kadic differs from the contested delivery in Doe. Rejection of a general immunity defense does not adjudicate every ordinary service or personal-jurisdiction objection. No general exception to sovereign or diplomatic immunity for international torts is announced.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's preservation of independently supported immunity questions remains intact; neither its justiciability holding nor today's decision displaces immunity rules not presented here.

### VIII. Affirmance restores proceedings, not liability or an award

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's reversal of the blanket dismissal is affirmed, and both actions return for claim-specific service, factual, liability, and remedial adjudication under the surviving federal routes. Supplemental claims may receive renewed consideration because federal claims survive, but neither an exercise of supplemental jurisdiction nor any state-law judgment follows automatically.

**Authority:** The affirmance and full scope of surviving actions have seven judgment votes, with Scalia and Thomas dissenting only as to the new actions in Parts II–III. All nine agree on the ordinary proof and remedial limits; all eight Associates join the supplemental-claim rationale, while Stone-Zsela joins that disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The pleaded conduct suffices to defeat the categorical grounds on which dismissal rested. Compensation still requires an authorized action, personal responsibility, causation, and proved loss. Abebe-Jira's punitive rule retains its established scope for proved official torture and is not automatically extended to every additional category. Franklin's appropriate-relief principle presupposes an action and left punitive authority open. Any additional relief needs its own legal source and predicate facts. The Court awards no damages, punishment, fees, class certification, or injunction, and authorizes no prospective supervision of a foreign government. Supplemental jurisdiction is reconsidered under its ordinary limits; survival of a federal claim is not an instruction to enter judgment on a state claim. The remand preserves proof and defenses without reviving categorical grounds rejected here.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's proved-torture remedy rules remain within their original scope. Franklin preserves the action/remedy distinction and supplies no independent punitive authorization.

## Precedent Treatment

- Abebe-Jira v. Negewo (January 14, 1997): its forum/action distinction, official-torture action, source-specific reception rationale, and concrete political-question inquiry remain controlling. The received action is extended only to the defined personal genocide and core-war-crime wrongs; its existing remedy rules are not extended automatically.
- Suter v. Artist M.: obligation, action, and relief remain separate; its administrative-plan holding supplies no categorical prohibition of this received tort action.
- Atherton v. FDIC and O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: general federal interests alone remain insufficient to make federal common law; §1350's particular reception function is the identified source here.
- Central Bank of Denver v. First Interstate Bank: bounded development of a received action supplies an analogy, while its securities-assistance standard is not imported.
- In re Yamashita: individual obligations in war support the norm inquiry, without creating this civil action or deciding absent-superior responsibility.
- Lugar v. Edmondson Oil: joint action with government supplies a limited analogy, without wholesale adoption of §1983's attribution rules.
- Baker v. Carr and Nixon v. United States: the issue-specific political-question inquiry remains; the latter's Senate-procedure holding creates no general foreign-affairs exemption.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools: appropriate relief presupposes an action; its reservation of punitive authority remains effective.

## Law After Decision

From April 21, 1997, §1350's confined received action includes personally perpetrated, planned, or directed genocide and the pleaded core war crimes at the elements and limits stated above. International condemnation alone remains insufficient. Ordinary official torture retains Abebe-Jira's conditions; the TVPA retains its own enacted elements, beneficiaries, exhaustion command, ten-year period, and exclusions. No universal customary-law action, title-based liability, comprehensive command rule, generic treaty-enforcement action, or automatic punitive remedy is established. The Court rejects the presented categorical state-action, political-question, and invitee-immunity barriers while preserving particular proof, service, defenses, and relief questions.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurs in the jurisdictional, justiciability, general-immunity, and preserved-route rulings but dissents from recognition of the two additional received civil actions. They accept that genocide and core war crimes can impose duties on private persons. Their objection concerns domestic authority to enforce those duties through the new damages actions. Abebe-Jira's official-torture holding controls its own reserved scope; it did not decide these extensions. Its jurisdictional grant and a clear international prohibition do not, in their view, supply the missing authorization. Section 1092's failure to repeal a received action cannot demonstrate that such an action existed in the first place. Atherton reinforces the need for a specific source rather than a general federal interest, and Central Bank's established securities action supplies no independent international action. They would exclude the added claims while retaining the enacted TVPA route under every statutory condition, the controlling official-torture route, and ordinary remand proceedings. They neither seek restoration of blanket jurisdictional dismissal nor deny international obligations, and they announce no categorical rule against relief in a properly authorized action.

## Procedure After Action

Both actions return through the Second Circuit for ordinary proceedings. The lower courts must resolve properly presented service and personal-jurisdiction issues, the elements of each surviving claim, defenses, personal responsibility, causation, and any authorized relief. Doe's disputed service is not resolved by the general-immunity ruling. Supplemental claims may be reconsidered under ordinary rules. No liability finding or damages award is directed.

## Holdings

### I. A reasonable multiyear inquiry governs the contacts record

**Holding and operative rule:** Whether a corporation's forum business is continuous and systematic may be assessed over a reasonable period preceding commencement, rather than exclusively during the filing year; a ceased earlier activity is relevant to that course but does not by itself establish present amenability. The 1987–1993 discovery period was reasonable on this record, and the discovery limit remains undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion has all nine Justices' support for the reasonable-period rule. Its separate discovery-limit rationale is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supplying eight direct joins including the author. Stone-Zsela supplies a disposition-only vote on that subcomponent; all nine support leaving the discovery order undisturbed.

**Controlling explanation:** International Shoe makes the quality and nature of the actual forum relationship material. Perkins and Helicopteros distinguish a substantial continuing operation from insufficient recurrent contacts; neither requires a filing-year snapshot. The plaintiff was entitled to show a reasonably recent course, while the defendant was entitled to resist unlimited retrospective discovery. Discovery covering 1987–1993 permitted examination of repeated sales, company support, visits, solicitation, and the earlier employee office. The end of that office does not erase the rest of the course, and its past existence alone does not decide current jurisdiction. The Court approves the actual reasonable discovery limit, without prescribing seven years in every case or ordering a new evidentiary hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** International Shoe's quality-and-nature inquiry and Perkins/Helicopteros's general-jurisdiction distinction are applied without a new mandatory temporal formula.

### II. The credited aggregate contacts satisfy the existing general-jurisdiction standard

**Holding and operative rule:** At the prima-facie stage presented here, Robertson's sustained selling, deliberate solicitation, supported distribution relationships, repeated company visits, and earlier staffed office, considered together, constitute continuous and systematic forum business sufficient for general jurisdiction. This conclusion credits the plaintiff's factual averments after discovery; it neither converts them into final findings nor makes sales, registration, an independent dealer, a former office, or any percentage independently sufficient.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter: seven direct joins. Ginsburg and Breyer disagree with the sufficiency conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** General jurisdiction permits adjudication of unrelated claims only upon substantially greater affiliation than purposeful contact supporting a particular related claim. Perkins supplies the continuous-operation principle; Helicopteros shows why regular purchases and associated training do not necessarily satisfy it. This defendant's own sustained Vermont sales and support course is qualitatively different. The credited record includes approximately $4 million in sales over the discovered multiyear course, $226,319 in 1993, five Star dealers and four Ceco authorized builders, product and trademark support, national and targeted solicitation reaching Vermont architectural firms, more than 150 employee or engineer visits, and an employee office during 1989–1990. The independent outlets, lack of current property or office, and small 1993 share of total sales are considered with those facts. None controls alone. Quill explains purposeful nonphysical affiliation for a related tax duty; it does not turn such affiliation into all-purpose jurisdiction. The Court independently applies the more demanding general standard to the full course. Glater's different selling operation is informative but does not establish a categorical rule that this combination can never suffice. No rule limiting general jurisdiction to incorporation or headquarters, automatic dealer-attribution rule, registration-consent holding, or numerical threshold is adopted.

**Precedent treatment:** Perkins and Helicopteros are applied to the aggregate business course. Quill's due-process holding remains confined to its related collection-duty setting; its distinct Commerce Clause rule is unaffected.

### III-A. General jurisdiction retains an independent constitutional fairness inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Sufficient continuous and systematic contacts do not end the due-process inquiry: a defendant may still establish that exercising general jurisdiction would be constitutionally unreasonable by making a compelling showing grounded in the actual burden and relevant plaintiff, forum, and judicial-system interests. The absence of a connection between the claim and forum may inform that inquiry, but ordinary unrelatedness cannot alone do decisive work because general jurisdiction exists to reach some unrelated claims.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: six direct joins. Scalia reserves whether a freestanding multifactored inquiry must invariably follow adequate general contacts; Ginsburg and Breyer do not reach this issue after finding those contacts insufficient. The six directly adopt this rule without a fractured-opinion synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** International Shoe protects fair play as well as a sufficient relationship. Burger King requires a compelling case of unreasonableness once purposeful contacts exist, and Asahi's independent fairness holding gives real weight to the defendant's burden and the other relevant interests. That holding is not erased because this case concerns general jurisdiction or domestic parties. Nor does it make the lack of claim-specific forum interests a substitute for demonstrated constitutional unfairness. Sustained voluntary forum business supplies notice and substantially weakens an ordinary objection that suit there is unexpected or inconvenient. The inquiry remains demanding and concrete; serious domestic unfairness is not categorically excluded, and the factors are not restricted to foreign defendants. Litigation convenience and available venue remedies remain distinct from the constitutional limit.

**Precedent treatment:** International Shoe and Burger King's fairness requirements are applied to general jurisdiction. Asahi's eight-Justice independent-fairness holding remains effective; its separate stream-of-commerce plurality is not made controlling.

### III-B. The offered fairness objection is insufficient on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** The foreign location of the claim-generating conduct, proof and witnesses, the parties' nonresidence, and the slight domestic defense burden shown here, considered together, do not make jurisdiction over this deliberately maintained commercial relationship constitutionally unfair. The Vermont dismissal cannot rest on that reasonableness determination; preserved venue and transfer questions remain for their own rules.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter: seven direct joins. Scalia joins this application assuming the complete separate inquiry applies; the other six also join Part III-A's requirement of that inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The construction dispute concerns a Miami building and conduct in Missouri, Texas, and Florida, rather than Vermont. Met Life and Robertson are both domestic corporations headquartered outside Vermont. Those circumstances favor another trial location, but Burger King's compelling-case requirement demands more than ordinary inconvenience. The lower court described the domestic defense burden as slight. Its conclusion that the forum and plaintiff had little claim-specific interest gave ordinary unrelatedness disproportionate constitutional force. Asahi involved a severe international burden and competing national legal systems not shown here. The Court does not require those international features in every successful fairness objection; it distinguishes their actual contribution to that decision. Keeton also prevents an asserted favorable limitations period from distorting personal jurisdiction. Selection of a forum with such a period is not itself unconstitutional, and the Court decides neither the applicable period nor its expiration. Even taking all relevant interests into account, this record does not establish the required unfairness.

**Precedent treatment:** Burger King's compelling-case rule is applied. Asahi is distinguished in application, not confined to foreign defendants. Keeton preserves the separation of personal jurisdiction from choice of law and limitations.

### IV. Reversal removes the constitutional dismissal and leaves ordinary adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment affirming the Rule 12(b)(2) dismissal is reversed and the case remanded because the credited contacts are sufficient and the offered fairness ground fails. Remaining properly presented factual, service, limitations, venue, transfer, and merits questions must be decided under their ordinary requirements; the Court directs neither damages, transfer, nor a new evidentiary proceeding on questions the existing record resolves.

**Authority:** Part IV's formal reversal and remand are adopted by O'Connor, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: six Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees with removing the constitutional dismissal and remanding under Parts II–III's limits but would vacate rather than reverse, and joins only the stated proof, defenses, and remand limits within Part IV. Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm for insufficient general contacts.

**Controlling explanation:** Extensive discovery followed by a prima-facie assessment does not decide disputed ultimate facts. The errors identified here remove the present constitutional ground for dismissal; they do not adjudicate construction liability, waive a defense, resolve a limitations question, or make jurisdictional averments binding trial findings. The other defendant's separate summary judgment is outside this appeal. No entitlement to a particular transfer or new evidentiary hearing follows. Preserving ordinary procedure gives effect to the jurisdictional holding without turning it into a merits judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** The mandate applies the jurisdiction, fairness, and choice-of-law distinctions stated above; it creates no independent liability, limitations, or transfer rule.

## Event

Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno; review of Third Circuit No. 95-7137, 76 F.3d 1294 (February 13, 1996). Argued December 4, 1996; decided April 28, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Pic-A-State seeks declaratory and prospective injunctive relief against the Interstate Wagering Amendment to 18 U.S.C. §1301. The questions are whether the stopped procurement business presents a concrete pre-enforcement controversy, whether Congress may regulate its interstate procurement transmissions although the tickets remain in another State, and what effect the express interstate-agreement exception has on the dormant Commerce Clause objection.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The denial and dismissal of the requested federal declaratory and prospective injunctive relief stand on the merits. No injunction, prosecution, penalty, damages or new hearing is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirmance | Entire opinion; all three holdings and their qualifications |

## Holdings

### I. The stopped business presents a concrete and ripe challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A business presents a concrete, ripe pre-enforcement challenge when a criminal statute directly reaches its existing activity, it has stopped that activity but intends to resume, serious penalties create a sufficiently real enforcement risk, and the requested relief would remove that present federal constraint. Those conditions are met here; neither an actual prosecution nor resolution of every independent state-law restriction is required.

**Authority:** Kennedy's unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies all three holdings at their stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories supplies the inquiry into fitness for judicial decision and hardship from withholding review. Pic-A-State stopped the very interstate procurement operation the amendment targeted; its reported intent to resume and exposure to substantial criminal sanctions establish a present injury. Bennett supplies personal injury, traceability and likely redress at the litigation stage, not automatic standing for every economically affected business. The Government's statement that prosecution is unlikely bears on risk but does not repudiate enforcement. Relief would remove an actual federal barrier even if Pennsylvania law separately remained relevant. The predominantly legal commerce question needs no prosecution to sharpen it, while withholding review would require continued cessation or exposure to punishment. The absence of a disavowal is not sufficient by itself in every case. Younger supplies no abstention ground where no state prosecution is pending. The prospective constitutional challenge against the enforcing Attorney General is a proper channel; standing does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

### II. Congress may prohibit the actual interstate procurement transmission

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit a procurement business's knowing interstate or foreign transmission of information to obtain, for a person in one State, a ticket, chance, share or interest in a lottery, gift enterprise or similar scheme conducted by another State. The challenged commercial transmission is interstate commerce itself even when the physical ticket remains in the conducting State; the prohibition remains subject to the agreement exception and separate §1307 exemptions stated below.

**Controlling explanation:** Champion v. Ames establishes congressional power to prohibit interstate commerce in lottery interests; Gibbons recognizes interstate commercial intercourse beyond the movement of tangible paper. Pic-A-State's computer communications accomplish the economic procurement transaction across state lines. The amendment closes that commercial route around the existing physical-ticket prohibition. Robertson confirms that an actual interstate business operation needs no independently substantial effect from each transaction. Lopez rejects a remote chain from local possession through social costs; it does not displace direct business regulation. Harris, Hale and American Life League likewise preserve genuine economic-class and direct-market grounds. Here the enacted knowing-transmission and procurement-business elements themselves supply the connection, without a judicially added element or an aggregation holding. Hodel permits rational means within that established power: closing the procurement gap and accommodating different state lottery choices meet that requirement. Revenue preservation alone would not establish federal power over local conduct. Congress need not prohibit every lottery, prove a particular revenue loss, or make transaction-specific findings to regulate this interstate business.

### III. The agreement exception supplies bounded congressional authorization

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1301 permits the otherwise covered procurement business when an agreement between the States in question or appropriate authorities of those States permits it. That express federal choice removes dormant Commerce Clause restraints on the particular state choices it authorizes, while leaving independent constitutional restraints intact.

**Controlling explanation:** Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin recognizes congressional power to authorize state burdens that the dormant Commerce Clause would otherwise forbid, and Northeast Bancorp requires the authorization to be clear. The agreement condition states the permitted category expressly. It accommodates state choices within Congress's independently valid regulation of interstate procurement; state permission for the underlying lottery does not displace the federal condition. Tracy separated ordinary economic rationality from discrimination analysis and reserved congressional authorization, so it does not supply a broader permission here. No qualifying agreement is established for Pic-A-State. The Court neither validates every Pennsylvania lottery restriction nor transfers unlimited federal legislative power to the States. The holding removes only the dormant implication and decides no independent First Amendment, equal-protection or nondelegation claim.

### Coverage, distinct exceptions, and questions not reached

The procurement clause requires a person engaged in that business, a customer in one State, a covered scheme conducted by another State, and knowing interstate or foreign transmission for procurement. It does not prohibit every lottery communication or wholly local purchase. The separate physical-carriage and receipt clauses remain distinct. The penalty is a fine under title 18, imprisonment for no more than two years, or both.

Section 1307(a)(1) separately exempts advertisements, prize lists or other information about a state-authorized, state-conducted lottery when contained in a publication published in that State or another lottery State, or broadcast by a station licensed in such a State. Section 1307(a)(2) exempts such information about other schemes authorized or not prohibited in the conducting State when conducted by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or as a clearly occasional and ancillary commercial promotional activity. Section 1307(b) separately exempts transportation or mailing of equipment, tickets or materials concerning a state-authorized lottery to addresses within its conducting State, and materials designed for an authorized foreign lottery to addressees within that foreign country. State includes the States, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and United States territories or possessions; foreign country includes an empire, country, dominion, colony, protectorate or subdivision other than the United States and its territories or possessions. The subsection-(b) lottery definition concerns pooled ticket-or-chance proceeds allocated by chance and excludes sporting-event bets or wagers. A nonprofit is an organization that would qualify as tax exempt under Internal Revenue Code §501. No fact places this business within an exemption; these provisions supply no blanket exemption for every lawful state lottery. No rule governs hypothetical wholly local transmissions or validates an interstate element unrelated to the commercial transaction.

## Event

Richards v. Wisconsin, No. 96-5955. Argued March 24, 1997; decided April 28, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Wisconsin Supreme Court, 201 Wis. 2d 839, 549 N.W.2d 218 (1996). That court affirmed the conviction after denial of suppression and no-contest pleas. The questions are whether the Fourth Amendment permits its categorical announcement exception for warrants seeking evidence of felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver, and whether the actual entry is sustainable on a narrower ground. Its interwoven federal and state reasoning supplies no independently sufficient state ground barring review; genuinely independent state protection remains distinct.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed on the particular circumstances of the entry. Wisconsin's categorical exception is rejected.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The conviction and denial of suppression remain in force because the particular entry was reasonable. No new hearing, suppression order, plea vacatur or sentence is directed.

## Opinion Topology

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; Stone-Zsela and Scalia except Part III | Affirmance | All nine join I, II and IV; seven Associates join III |

## Holdings

### I. The felony-drug category cannot replace the required execution inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not permit officers to dispense with announcement solely because a warrant seeks evidence of felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver. A valid search warrant authorizes the search but does not itself establish that an unannounced forcible entry is reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine in Parts I, II and IV; Part III has the distinct seven-Justice coalition specified below.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson v. Arkansas makes announcement of identity and authority part of reasonable dwelling entry while preserving supported exceptions. Wisconsin's category assumes danger and threatened destruction in every included case despite differences in occupants, evidence and execution. General associations among drugs, guns and easy disposal cannot do the work of a particular justification. Announcement protects voluntary admission, property, privacy and occupant safety even when officers hold a warrant. Buie rejects an automatic offense premise for a broader protective sweep; its permission to inspect immediately adjoining attack spaces rests on a different immediate risk. Maryland v. Wilson likewise permits a limited passenger-exit direction through its specific safety and incremental-intrusion comparison, not a general license for surprise dwelling entry. The Court therefore rejects this category without declaring every categorical Fourth Amendment rule invalid. Chandler's attention to the actual justification in its different testing context is consistent with, but does not enlarge, this holding.

### II. Reasonable suspicion governs a supported execution exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers must identify particular circumstances and rational inferences supporting reasonable suspicion that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including by allowing destruction of evidence. The threshold is below probable cause but requires more than the offense label or an unsupported hunch, and the relevant facts must be known when the challenged force begins.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson preserves announcement exceptions but leaves their precise quantum open. Buie's use of articulable suspicion for a limited protective step supports a practical threshold by analogy: execution of an independently authorized search may require a prompt response without awaiting probable cause of imminent destruction. This rule supplies no authority to search without a warrant or another lawful basis and does not transfer every protective-sweep rule to evidence searches. For the destruction branch, suspicion must connect delay for announcement and voluntary admission to a threat to preservation of the evidence. Officers need not hear destruction or wait until evidence is lost. A drug label or unsupported general weapons claim remains insufficient, and later discovery or flight cannot retroactively justify earlier force. The standard applies to the identified execution exceptions; the Court fixes no universal waiting time and does not equate seeing a uniform with knowing the officers' purpose. The independent useless-gesture contention need not be decided here.

### III. Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles

**Holding and operative rule:** In reviewing this execution under the reasonable-suspicion standard, the historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, with grounded experience given real weight; the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of those facts is independently assessed. That allocation permits review of the supported record without manufacturing new facts.

**Authority:** Breyer, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven direct joins. Scalia withholds this method; Stone-Zsela joins the substantive holdings and application without an additional position on it.

**Controlling explanation:** Ornelas separates historical factfinding from ultimate reasonable suspicion in its stop and warrantless-search setting. The same functional distinction applies here: the trial court decides what officers observed and whose testimony to credit, while the constitutional execution threshold remains a legal question. Ornelas did not previously decide every warrant-execution question; this is its reasoned application to the present one. Robinette demonstrates that correcting a general rule does not invariably decide its application or require remand. Here the original hearing already supplies the material observations and credibility determination. The Court respects those findings while testing their constitutional significance, and need not supplement them with assumed purpose knowledge or later events.

### IV. The credited contemporaneous facts justified this entry

**Holding and operative rule:** The credited look toward a nearby uniformed officer and surprised reaction, combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, supplied particularized reasonable suspicion of destruction risk before the first attempt to enter; the subsequent rapid closure and locking strengthened that justification for the later force. The entry was therefore reasonable despite the invalid categorical rule and the judge's advance refusal of no-knock authorization.

**Controlling explanation:** Pharo's maintenance ruse brought Richards to the door. The trial judge rejected the material portion of Richards's contrary account and inferred awareness of the uniformed officer from the credited observations. Recognition is an inference on disputed evidence, not a concession. The look toward the uniform and surprise preceded the first attempted entry and supported the inference that the ruse had exposed the police; rapid closure and locking strengthened it for later force. Combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, these facts supplied more than a general drug assumption. Closing against a supposed late-night maintenance intruder is a possible innocent account, but it does not eliminate reasonable suspicion from the credited police-recognition response. Neither intended destruction nor certainty of recognition had to be proved. Identity was shouted two or three seconds after closure, possibly while force was already underway; purpose was recalled as announced when the door opened. A belated announcement cannot cure prior unlawful force, but a destruction exception already justified this execution. Later window flight, drugs found above ceiling tiles, unproved destruction noises and an unproved weapon play no part in supplying pre-force justification. The original hearing suffices without deciding the objection to supplemental testimony. Advance refusal required attention to new facts, not disregard of the judge's ruling on the same general premise; the doorway developments supplied those new facts. The ruse was not consent to forced entry.

The Court does not decide exclusion after a violation, an independently sufficient futility ground, the cross-petition objection concerning the alternative remedy argument, or a general doctrine for every police-created exigency. A valid warrant alone is not declared an independent evidentiary source or proof of inevitable lawful discovery.

## Precedent Treatment

- Wilson v. Arkansas is applied to reject the offense category and given a reasonable-suspicion execution threshold; its constitutional exclusion question remains open.
- Maryland v. Buie supplies a practicable articulable-suspicion analogy and distinguishes a justified limited category from an automatic offense premise for a greater intrusion.
- Ornelas is applied by functional analogy to the review allocation in Part III, with the existing factual protections intact.
- Ohio v. Robinette supports review of interwoven federal/state reasoning and separates correction of a rule from application and remedy; it imposes no obligatory remand on an adequate record.

Wisconsin may no longer rely on this felony-drug category alone. Unannounced execution requires reviewable reasonable suspicion grounded in the particular circumstances, and the time of the challenged force controls the justification. The Court applies a distinct factual/legal appellate allocation by seven direct joins. The existing conviction stands on the destruction-risk ground; no new constitutional exclusion rule, automatic valid-warrant immunity or universal waiting period follows.

The supported execution facts permit his affirmance without deciding that methodological disagreement anew.

## Holdings

### I. This authorized public highway falls within Montana's nonmember framework

**Holding and operative rule:** For ordinary public motorists using this lawfully open, state-maintained federal right-of-way, the Tribe lacks the exclusion power that would otherwise govern access; Montana's nonmember framework therefore applies to this road-use tort. That consequence lasts within the authorized public use and neither conveys fee title nor removes every compatible tribal power.

**Authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins in Parts I, III and IV. Parts II and V are joined by the eight Associates.

**Controlling explanation:** Montana distinguishes retained internal and land-based authority from authority over nonmembers on land opened to outside use, preserving two exceptions and superior federal authorization. Bourland applies an exclusion consequence to particular acquired lands while requiring a separate residual-authority inquiry. The authorized public highway has the pertinent characteristic here: ordinary motorists do not obtain the Tribe's discretionary permission to pass. The federal instrument is a conditional easement, not a fee conveyance or an express surrender of every governmental power. Its continuing trust status and compatible crossings remain effective but do not establish a present gatekeeping right over authorized motorists. Sac and Fox's classification of qualifying rights-of-way as Indian country remains intact; territorial classification does not resolve every nonmember governing power. No exact collision-parcel owner, invalid issuance or termination event is found. The Court decides this highway use, not every reservation road, easement or source of tribal authority.

**Grant qualifications:** The May 8, 1970 grant applies to trust lands only, excludes non-trust property, and acknowledges $5,546.50 plus other good consideration. Tenure is unlimited only while used for the specified highway purpose and remains subject to valid existing rights or adverse claims. The United States grantor may terminate all or part after thirty days' written notice and failure to correct a breach of grant terms or applicable regulations, two consecutive years of nonuse for the purpose, abandonment, or failure to file the completion affidavit when construction ends. Indian owners, lessees, successors and assigns retain reasonably necessary crossings, constructed and maintained at their risk with responsibility for resulting right-of-way damage. The stated conditions bind and benefit the grantee's successors. No termination trigger is established on this record. The header's zero tribal acres and 87.48 individually owned acres do not identify the collision parcel; missing incorporated maps and a separate consent instrument establish no grant defect.

### II. A tribal forum requires an adequate source of governing power

**Holding and operative rule:** Subject to controlling treaties or statutes and Montana's two exceptions, tribal adjudicatory authority over nonmembers does not exceed the corresponding substantive governing authority. A tribal court cannot compel these defendants to litigate this highway claim solely by proposing to apply another sovereign's law; completed exhaustion supplies no independent jurisdictional grant.

**Authority:** Scalia, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins the narrower case-specific grounds, not this general ceiling.

**Controlling explanation:** A binding tort judgment exercises sovereign authority over the defendants' conduct even though forum and choice of law are analytically distinct. National Farmers Union supplies federal review after tribal examination; Iowa Mutual protects that initial institutional opportunity, not ultimate power over every reservation occurrence. Williams involved a trader's claim against resident members about their transaction, a concrete internal relationship missing here. Merrion rejects equating sovereign taxing power with a private owner's right to exclude and rejects surrender by contractual silence. The Court infers no general waiver here: taxation of mineral lessees' reservation business and governmental-services relationship differs from the ordinary highway claim. Montana's external limit, the authorized public use and separately tested exceptions supply the rule. Congress remains able to enlarge tribal-court jurisdiction; no criminal-jurisdiction analogy displaces the distinct civil inquiry.

### III. The subcontract does not bring this ordinary highway claim within the consensual exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Montana's first exception requires a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements with the Tribe or its members. Even assuming Stockert's trip served A-1's tribal community-center subcontract and Fredericks resided on the reservation, this non-Indian motorist's ordinary highway-negligence claim lacks that relation; contracting with a tribal enterprise does not submit a contractor to every reservation claim.

**Controlling explanation:** A-1 voluntarily made the $12,490 subcontract with the tribally owned LCM corporation. Its existence deserves real consideration; neither express forum consent nor contractual privity is a universal prerequisite to every use of the exception. But Fredericks asserts the duty one public-road driver owes another non-Indian user, not a tribal contract entitlement, an obligation to a participant in the work, or control of project performance. The truck uses a public right of passage rather than permission available only through the bargain. The contract can explain the trip without making this claim part of governing that relationship. The work purpose and residence are assumed favorably, not resolved against petitioners, so further factfinding on those disputes cannot change this result. No weight is given to a purported Utah forum or law clause outside the appellate record, and reported building or employment obligations establish no blanket consent. A different injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a participant in the consensual activity presents a different question.

### IV. The supported interests do not satisfy the self-government exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Montana's second exception preserves authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. The serious individual injury, family relationships, assumed residence and ordinary road-safety interests shown here do not establish the threat or direct tribal effect necessary to bring this nonmember highway claim within that exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Montana protects necessary tribal self-government, not general territorial jurisdiction over every local civil wrong. Fredericks's twenty-four-day hospitalization, ties to her tribal-member sons, and the community's safety interests are real; the Court does not find an absence of consequences. Their ordinary individual, familial and community character nevertheless does not supply the distinct governmental threat or direct effect required here. Treating those effects as sufficient for every road accident would undo the external limit the exception qualifies. No unproved fiscal or service burden supplies a missing premise. Her sons' membership does not change her status or bring their separate claims into this appeal. The protective state action indicates another possible forum, but availability of a state forum is not the independent reason tribal power is absent. No negligence or limitations result in that forum is decided.

### V. The jurisdictional judgment does not itself establish every predicate for an injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmance requires federal relief consistent with the absence of tribal jurisdiction over the reviewed claim, but does not itself issue an injunction or establish entitlement to every coercive remedy. On remand the federal court must respect the actual claims and lawful parties, immunity limits and ordinary equitable requirements.

**Authority:** The same eight-Associate coalition as Part II directly supports this remedial holding; Stone-Zsela favors affirmative prospective relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The amended answer consents to defending declaratory relief on tribal civil jurisdiction while withholding consent to other claims, issues, relief and liability. Resolution 92-025-JJR is likewise confined to protecting and asserting jurisdiction in this case. Descriptions of the defense in the lower opinions do not enlarge those instruments into an unrestricted waiver. The outer federal jurisdictional ruling permits appropriate conforming declaratory relief; any injunction still requires its actual lawful basis and equitable predicates. Neither the sons' unresolved claims nor the dismissed insurer can be disposed of through this appeal. No additional exhaustion or factual inquiry into the assumed trip and residence is necessary to decide the reviewed jurisdictional question.

The Court does not decide tribal or closed internal roads, member conduct, explicit forum consent, qualifying superior federal authorization, separate criminal authority, police patrols or detention for handoff, or every third-party tort. The contested late treaty theory and unratified agreement supply no independent ground; no holding of inevitable forfeiture, ratification or exclusive state jurisdiction follows.

## Law After Decision

This public-highway claim lies outside tribal adjudicatory power even on the favorable work-trip and residence assumptions. The general adjudicatory ceiling has eight direct joins, with superior federal law and the Montana exceptions preserved. The decision neither extinguishes all tribal authority over the land nor creates a universal third-party-tort or privity bar. Appropriate federal relief must still fit the claims, parties, immunity and equitable requirements.

## Separate Writings

## Event

Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party, No. 95-1608. Argued December 4, 1996; decided April 28, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, Twin Cities Area New Party v. McKenna, 73 F.3d 196 (January 5, 1996). The court below reversed summary judgment for state officials and held Minnesota's old prohibition unconstitutional as applied to the New Party's nomination of Andy Dawkins, a consenting, supported candidate already seeking the Democratic-Farmer-Labor nomination. The DFL did not object. The grant concerns that minor/major-party combination under the First and Fourteenth Amendments, not the separate two-major-party prohibition. Minnesota Laws 1996, chapter 419, conditionally changed the statute and expressly makes reversal consequential.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Eighth Circuit judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.** Supporting: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine reach the live controversy. The old prohibition is sustained in this application; the remand must account for chapter 419's express expiration and prior-law revival upon reversal. The completed 1994 election is not reopened, and no particular intervening filing or election is adjudicated.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Breyer; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg in I and III | Reversal and remand | Five join merits Part II; all eight Associates join live-controversy Part I and statutory-transition Part III; Stone-Zsela agrees to reach the merits without an additional statutory rationale |

## Holdings

### I. The conditional enactment leaves a live controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge to the old prohibition remains live where the officials seek reversal of its invalidation and an enacted provision expressly revives that prohibition upon reversal. Chapter 419 supplies that concrete consequence; neither its replacement scheme nor completion of the 1994 election makes this dispute advisory.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined in Parts I and III by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela also votes to reach the merits without an additional chapter-specific ground.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Mesquite distinguishes a genuine end to a challenged restriction from repeal that leaves its recurrence consequential, while keeping mootness separate from entitlement to an injunction. The Minnesota officials continue to contest the same party's asserted right, and chapter 419 conditions revival of the old law on the judgment they seek. That express condition makes adjudication affect legal rights even without finding a present stay or mandate recall. Norman preserves a continuing election challenge despite completion of the immediate election. The new scheme's existence is therefore relevant but not conclusive. Dawkins's earlier consent and the DFL's lack of objection do not establish compliance with every later written-chair-consent requirement. The Court does not decide the independent constitutionality of that replacement scheme or determine which law governed an unexamined intermediate filing.

### II. This limitation is justified within the complete nomination system

**Holding and operative rule:** Minnesota may prevent this otherwise qualified minor party from placing its consenting nominee on the ballot under its name when he also seeks or holds the other major party's nomination, where the complete system leaves the candidate available and the party able to nominate other candidates, endorse and campaign. In this application the burden is less than severe and proportionate interests in ballot integrity, defined nomination choices and a stable electoral system justify the restriction under Anderson and Burdick.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer: five direct joins in Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson and Burdick require attention to the actual burden, precise interests and their connection; severe restrictions require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest, while reasonable nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily may rest on important regulatory interests. The Party's nomination choice is real association, not an arbitrary protest message, and endorsement is not literally the same legal act. But the rule excludes neither this actual candidate nor the Party itself from meaningful electoral participation. It limits an additional nomination designation within the State's selection machinery. Norman barred unrelated obstacles to authorized party expansion and independently qualified candidacies; it did not require every overlapping nomination of an available candidate. Colorado Republican protects independent campaign expression, a different mechanism from official ballot selection. The State may use general preventive nomination rules to maintain coherent candidacies and stable election administration without proving a past disruption in this particular alliance. Storer supports orderly candidate-selection rules; Jenness and Munro preserve reasonable screening and prevention without a demand for elaborate empirical proof. Candidate consent and qualifying signatures lessen some concerns but do not compel the State to redesign its nomination structure whenever an alliance proposes a workable alternative. This holding does not depend on voter confusion, an asserted incapacity of voters or proof of past fraud. Nor does it approve suppression of competition merely to protect incumbents, pure rational-basis immunity for ballot laws, or every anti-fusion restriction in a more restrictive system.

### III. Reversal has the enacted transition consequence, without a retrospective election ruling

**Holding and operative rule:** Chapter 419 §10 makes its amendments effective for the 1996 state primary and thereafter, suspends them while the McKenna judgment is stayed or its mandate recalled, and provides that reversal causes the amendments to expire and prior law to revive. This reversal engages that expiration-and-revival condition; the remand must respect it without assuming an unproved stay, recall or continuous intervening statutory regime.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted condition links the state-law consequence to reversal, not to a finding about an earlier filing. Recognizing that consequence does not adjudicate the replacement provisions' independent validity or establish that their requirements were satisfied in 1994. The existing case asks whether the old ban may be applied to this nomination, so sustaining that application permits a conforming remand. It supplies no basis to reopen the completed election or allocate rights in an unexamined intervening candidacy. Any such distinct question would require its actual filing facts and relevant stay or mandate history.

### Statutory scope and limits

The challenged 1994 §204B.06 subdivision 1(b) barred an affidavit when another affidavit was on file for any office at the same primary or next general election. Section 204B.04 subdivision 2 barred petition nomination for the same office by a primary candidate, subject to two distinct exceptions. Under §204D.10 subdivision 2, if any major-party primary candidate received ten percent of the average vote for the party's state officers at the last state general election in the relevant district, its highest-vote candidates became nominees; if none met that threshold, none were nominated and all could seek petition nomination under §§204B.07–.09. The relevant state officers were governor, lieutenant governor, secretary of state, state auditor, state treasurer and attorney general. The §204B.13 subdivision 4 nonpartisan-vacancy exception allowed a petition within one week of the vacancy and no later than four calendar days before election; an eligible voter could sign despite voting in the primary or signing another petition. Neither exception applied to Dawkins. The distinct ban on nomination by two major parties is not decided.

The conditional replacement permitted a major-party and one-or-more-minor-party nomination for the same partisan office with written consent of the parties' state chairs; petition nomination also required the candidate's written consent. Losing the major-party primary forfeited the simultaneous minor-party nominations, and a party candidate could not also file as an independent. It retained the nonpartisan-vacancy exception, barred a defeated major-party primary candidate except to fill a §204B.13 vacancy, and repealed the old primary-turnout provision. Its affidavit rule referred to any other office, required petition acceptance and the consent forms, and contained a distinct dual-office exception for a soil-and-water district supervisor outside Anoka, Hennepin, Ramsey and Washington counties and a mayor/council or town-supervisor office in a city or town of no more than 2,500 people wholly or partly within the district. That different-office exception is not fusion. A multiply nominated candidate's name appeared once with party names; major/minor nomination used the major-party position and multiple-minor nomination the first filing party's position. Votes for multiply nominated candidates were excluded from the specified party-status qualification or retention counts. These features neither establish compliance in this case nor receive independent constitutional approval.

The Court recognizes no right to duplicate votes, separate party vote totals or a particular ballot design and adjudicates no independent challenge to consent, counting, format, support or deadline rules.

## Law After Decision

The old anti-fusion restriction is constitutional in this minor/major-party application. Reversal engages chapter 419's expiration of its amendments and revival of prior law. The replacement scheme's independent constitutional questions remain undecided.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents, joined by Ginsburg throughout and Souter in Parts I–II. A willing, sufficiently supported standard bearer is a central associational choice, and endorsing him or choosing someone else does not replace the Party's legally operative nomination. Even without declaring every such burden severe, they find inadequate fit: no defeated candidate seeks reentry, the candidate consents, the other party does not object, support requirements have been met, and another party designation adds no new individual to the race. Norman, Tashjian and Eu require attention to that actual association; Burdick permits reasonable prevention but does not make every general interest sufficient. Stevens and Ginsburg further reject preserving established parties' competitive position as a reason to exclude this consensual alliance. They would affirm at the as-applied scope, without mandating duplicate votes, separate party totals or a court-designed ballot.

Souter also dissents separately. Less-than-severe burden does not end the inquiry into fit. He agrees that the present interests are inadequate, but does not join a categorical rejection of every stability justification. A demonstrated threat to effective governing and a connection between fusion and that threat might justify a restriction; he leaves that question open. His Norman and Burdick positions preserve support requirements and primary finality, neither of which explains this supported nomination of the primary winner.

Stone-Zsela dissents. The burden is substantial but not severe, and the Eighth Circuit's strict-scrutiny reasoning should not control. Losing the qualified party's legally operative nomination and distinct identity affects effective electoral choice even though endorsement and campaigning remain possible. Applying Anderson-Burdick, he finds the absolute ban insufficiently fitted to this consenting, supported nomination: neutral labels, single counting and access requirements address the identified concerns, while the record supplies no demonstrated confusion, manipulation or splintering sufficient for this burden. Norman protects authorized party identity, and Colorado Republican confirms that shared candidate support does not erase a smaller party's independence without itself deciding fusion. Political stability cannot simply mean protecting established competitors. He reserves a properly supported stability limit and whether the other party's consent may be required. His proposed relief is prospective and leaves lawful design, consent, counting, fraud-prevention, deadlines and support requirements available. He recognizes no right to a separate line, multiple vote credit, general ballot expression or a particular format, and would not reopen 1994.

## Holdings

### This confined benefits-administration use does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding a constitutional interest against unjustified governmental acquisition and internal use of intimate medical information, this record does not establish its violation through unsolicited receipt followed by the limited, benefits-related administrator and physician inquiry and cessation of identified reporting. That conclusion accepts the supported deliberate follow-up, unnecessary identifiers and deficient safeguards; it does not require every datum to be indispensable, and it authorizes no general publication or use detached from the benefits function.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer: five direct joins. Scalia and Thomas's separate source objection neither supplies nor limits the majority's expressly assumed-right application.

**Controlling explanation:** Whalen sustains safeguarded identified-drug administration without making indispensability a constitutional requirement, while reserving unwarranted disclosure and inadequately protected systems. Sheets protects against unjustified official facilitation of public dissemination of intimate material entrusted for a limited purpose; it does not settle this internal-use question or impose universal least-intrusive means. Under Sheets's favorable-evidence rule, the Court accepts that names were unnecessary, safeguards deficient, and Pierce's subsequent examination and HIV inquiry deliberate. Initial receipt alone was unrequested. Those concessions do not end independent assessment of constitutional sufficiency. SEPTA administered a self-insured plan and sought utilization, possible misuse, generic-substitution and expensive-medication information. The actual follow-up stayed within the responsible benefits and medical group and included examination of whether the report could support an audit. Pierce and Aufschauer newly learned the condition; the two consulted physicians already knew it, although renewed communication remained a use of the information. No general publication, treatment veto, employment penalty or use detached from benefits review is established. When names' necessity was questioned, Pierce destroyed the report and required unnamed reports. The HIV-identification question and unnecessary names are serious objections, but the limited related inquiry and corrective response do not make imperfect administration a deprivation of the assumed constitutional interest. This is not a finding that every identified use was necessary, a rule that accepting benefits waives confidentiality, or a requirement of economic injury. The Court neither adopts the lower court's seven Westinghouse considerations as constitutional law nor recognizes or rejects a general medical-information right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public dissemination is not declared indispensable to every possible future claim; broader or unrelated internal use remains distinct. Hodge and Uhlrig do not supply an immunity judgment here, and Chandler's bodily-search standard is not an informational due-process test. The Court decides no independent damages sufficiency, preserved Rule 59 issue, private-employer duty, municipal-attribution issue, or unappealed failure-to-train claim. Confidential retention, lawful disclosure and different administrative uses retain their own questions.

## Precedent Treatment

- Whalen v. Roe supplies the distinction between legitimate identified-drug administration and reserved abuses; it establishes neither universal indispensability nor the general right assumed here.
- Sheets v. Salt Lake County remains binding at its intentional public-dissemination scope and for favorable-evidence review. Its intimate-information protection is neither overruled nor automatically extended to this internal prescription use.
- Hodge v. Jones retains its conduct-time immunity disposition and reserved constitutional merits; it decides no immunity or general medical-record rule here.
- Uhlrig retains separation of favorable facts, legal attribution and the distinct immunity inquiry; no such defense is adjudicated in this appeal.
- Chandler v. Miller remains a bodily-search decision and supplies no medical-information due-process formula.

The directed defendants' judgment stands on a confined no-violation holding that expressly assumes the asserted interest. The general constitutional coverage of identified medical acquisition and internal use remains unresolved; the Court adopts no universal balancing formula, least-intrusive-method requirement, employment waiver or permission for unrelated diagnostic use. Sheets's public-dissemination protection remains intact.

## Separate Writings

Scalia concurs, joined by Thomas. Both fully join the Court's assumed-right application but would also reject the proposed extension for lack of a demonstrated constitutional source. Whalen reserved the issue, and decisional-autonomy decisions do not establish general control over identified data held by public benefits administrators. Sheets now binds at its public-dissemination scope; extending it requires additional justification. They accept the intimacy and supported intentional conduct, and rely neither on absence of economic loss nor on state remedies as a cure for a federal violation.

Stevens dissents, joined by Souter and Ginsburg. Whalen distinguished rational safeguarded collection from unresolved abuses, and Sheets protects intimate entrustment and requires favorable treatment of supported trial evidence. They would extend that protection to deliberate identification or internal use of a serious medical condition without an adequate governmental connection. The jury-favorable evidence includes highlighted HIV/AIDS drugs, the question seeking HIV identities, the conceded lack of need for names, missing notice and safeguards, and Doe's limited confidential entrustment. A general cost-control interest cannot conclusively answer that distinct inquiry. Their rule requires adequate connection rather than indispensable necessity of every datum, and does not adopt Westinghouse's seven factors or turn emotional distress alone into a violation. They would vacate and remand for application of the bounded rule and consideration of properly remaining trial and damages questions. The record does not establish that every condition of the new rule was necessarily found, so they would not automatically restore $125,000.

Stone-Zsela dissents. Identified prescription records, particularly HIV information, fall within a limited protection that he would extend beyond Sheets's decided public-disclosure setting. Personally identifying access and internal use must be genuinely and proportionately connected to the actual public function, considering sensitivity, the need for identifiers instead of aggregate or coded information, recipients and existing safeguards. Legitimate utilization monitoring did not establish a right to continued identified review as a matter of law: the jury could find unnecessary names, deliberate research after sensitivity became apparent, and no safeguard against the excess. He would reverse the appellate Rule 50 direction, leave the District Court's denial of judgment as a matter of law in place, and remand unresolved preserved Rule 59 and damages matters. His test creates no automatic violation from lack of consent or universal least-intrusive-method duty. It preserves aggregate or coded audits, identified review tied to concrete fraud or eligibility concerns, legally required disclosure and confidential retention alone. He decides neither municipal liability, personal immunity, damages nor private-employer duties.

## Procedure After Action

The Third Circuit judgment directing judgment for SEPTA and Pierce is affirmed. The Court orders no new trial, factual hearing or appellate remand and does not reach the alternative damages-sufficiency question. The unappealed failure-to-train disposition remains undisturbed.

## Event

Harbor Tug & Barge Co. v. Papai, No. 95-1621. Argued January 13, 1997; decided May 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, September 25, 1995, 67 F.3d 203. The questions concern Jones Act seaman status and whether a final LHWCA award forecloses the seaman claims. Papai injured his knee during a one-day dockside painting assignment aboard Pt. Barrow; the parties dispute the legal significance of his staffing role and prior work. The appeal below also challenged the later vessel-negligence trial judgment, which the Ninth Circuit did not reach.

## Participation

## Public Action

## Holdings

### I. A shared hiring hall does not itself identify a fleet

**Holding and operative rule:** A worker may aggregate service on an identifiable fleet for Jones Act seaman status only when the vessels share ownership or operational control. Obtaining assignments through one union hiring hall does not itself supply that connection, although a hiring-hall worker may establish the required substantial connection to an actual vessel or commonly owned or controlled fleet.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a common source of labor does not turn independently directed vessels into one fleet. Chandris requires both contribution to the vessel's work and a connection substantial in nature and duration to a vessel or identifiable fleet. Its actual-assignment inquiry protects an employment relationship, rather than every maritime task a worker performs. Wilander rejects a requirement of traditional navigation duties, but does not eliminate the required vessel connection.

The contrary view treats the operators' use of the same crew market as enough to combine otherwise unrelated engagements. That would make the relevant fleet depend on which vessels happened to employ workers from that hall, without a shared operational relationship. Common ownership or control supplies the necessary objective boundary and connects the inquiry to the employment undertaken. This decision clarifies the fleet alternative; it does not attribute that previously disputed answer to Chandris. No wider common operational arrangement is established here. Daily hiring is not itself disqualifying, and neither permanent employment nor a voyage on the injury day is an absolute condition. Rejecting hall-wide aggregation leaves Papai's distinct same-employer theory for its own examination.

### II. The supported employment relationship cannot establish substantial connection in nature

**Holding and operative rule:** Considering the entire accepted engagement and the supported prior Harbor Tug work, the record permits no reasonable finding that Papai had a vessel connection substantial in nature. Summary judgment therefore must be reinstated against his Jones Act and other seaman-dependent claims; neither the deckhand designation nor a staffing declaration supplies sea-service obligations absent from the actual undertaking.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by the same six Justices as Part I. This is the sufficient status ground for their judgment, applying the fleet boundary and independently assessing the same-employer alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Chandris directs attention to the actual assignment and preserves factfinding when the properly considered evidence permits competing reasonable conclusions. Here Papai accepted one day of dockside chipping and painting, with no planned sailing or commitment to return. His three or four specifically recalled earlier Pt. Barrow jobs and the immediately preceding Sea Fox job were maintenance. The twelve earlier Harbor Tug engagements do not establish a different continuing sea-service obligation; the Court does not find that every unidentified job was identical.

Secchitano's declaration supplies real contrary staffing evidence: Papai replaced a more regularly assigned deckhand, filled the two-deckhand complement and was dispatched into the operational crew rather than a shoreside maintenance gang. Taking those assertions into account does not change the accepted limits of this engagement. Ordinary maintenance can be part of established crew service; that proposition does not make this discrete job such a continuing relationship. The contract describes qualifications and possible duties, not performance of each duty. General testimony that most earlier hiring-hall work was deckhand work on moving boats does not establish the nature of the Harbor Tug relationship.

The Court decides no withdrawal from navigation merely because the tug was docked with idle engines, and calculates no unsupported percentage. Chandris's existing guide for ordinary arrangements is about thirty percent: a rule of thumb, not an inflexible cutoff or jurisdictional condition, permitting justified departures and genuinely changed essential assignments. Meeting it cannot replace nature; falling below it is not invariably fatal. A brief assignment involving actual sea service, a genuine continuing crew obligation or different supported prior work remains outside this holding. Gizoni's protection of genuine disputes remains intact; it does not require trial when the correctly assessed relationship permits only one legal conclusion.

### III. Reversing the status ruling does not decide the separate vessel-negligence appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** Reversal of the Ninth Circuit's reopening of the seaman claims reinstates status summary judgment only. The case returns for ordinary appellate consideration of the separately challenged post-trial vessel-negligence judgment, which the Ninth Circuit did not reach; the Court neither affirms that judgment on its merits nor automatically orders a new negligence trial.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Souter's opinion is joined by all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees in this component's disposition without an additional ground; the eight direct rationale joins control.

**Controlling explanation:** The January 20, 1993 appeal challenged both status and the judgment after the vessel-negligence trial. The court of appeals reopened status and did not resolve the latter challenge. Its expectation that a Jones Act case would use a more favorable standard did not adjudicate the independent vessel claim. Removing that expectation cannot substitute for appellate consideration of the actual judgment.

Section 905(b) permits its specified vessel-negligence action subject to its own limits. It excludes seaworthiness as a basis of liability, bars employer indemnity to the vessel and makes its remedy exclusive against the vessel except for LHWCA remedies. Its stevedoring restriction applies where the vessel employed the worker for that service and the injury arose from negligence of those providing it. Its separate shipbuilding, repairing or breaking restriction applies when the employer is the vessel owner, owner pro hac vice, agent, operator or charterer, and bars the specified action against that employer in any capacity or its employees. The Court decides neither whether this painting is repairing service within that provision nor whether such a contention is properly available here. Section 905(c)'s Outer Continental Shelf reciprocal-indemnity qualification has no established predicate.

The trial court's findings about the ladder, Papai's movement and Low's credibility belong to the unresolved negligence appeal; they are not adverse concessions on the earlier status motion. No finding of negligence, causation, consortium entitlement, jury waiver or damages follows. This remand preserves the lawful appellate task without guaranteeing any independent remedy.

**Questions not reached:** The final compensation order's effect under §905(a) and ordinary administrative issue preclusion are separate questions, both unnecessary to the status holding. The Court neither adopts the lower court's nonforeclosure reasoning nor orders a new award-effects inquiry. The award remains operative in its own system. No refund, credit amount, fee adjustment, modification deadline or factual correction of that award is decided.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, dissenting in part, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would affirm reopening of status and the rejection of foreclosure by this award, while joining Part III's preservation of the independent vessel appeal. ## Event

Inter-Modal Rail Employees Association v. Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co., No. 96-491. Argued March 17, 1997; decided May 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, March 27, 1996, 80 F.3d 348. The question is whether ERISA §510 excludes interference with welfare-benefit attainment because those benefits do not vest. The claim arises from a transfer of cargo-handling work and alleged discharges intended to avoid benefit obligations; the Court reviews a categorical pleading-stage dismissal, not proved intent or liability.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the welfare-interference dismissal and remands. All nine Justices agree that lack of statutory vesting does not categorically exclude welfare rights from §510.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 8–1 as to the formal disposition.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing that disposition:** Stone-Zsela, who would reverse and remand with a direction to reinstate the welfare claims for the remaining statutory inquiries. He does not favor affirmance of the categorical exclusion.

The Court sets aside the reviewed categorical welfare ground only. It finds no §510 violation, orders no employment reinstatement or damages, and does not independently reopen the other lower-court components.

## Holdings

### I. Section 510 includes prospective welfare-plan rights

**Holding and operative rule:** ERISA §510 protects against a covered discharge or other prohibited action undertaken for the purpose of interfering with attainment of a right a participant may become entitled to under an employee welfare benefit plan, even though the benefit is not subject to ERISA's statutory vesting requirements. Lawful authority to amend or terminate a welfare plan does not itself authorize interference through conduct forbidden by §510.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this statutory coverage rule and its limits, notwithstanding their different formal dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that welfare benefits are within the attainment clause's protection. ERISA defines employee benefit plans to include welfare and pension plans. Section 510 addresses future attainment, while Congress elsewhere used narrower pension and nonforfeitable-benefit terms. Section 1051(1) exempts welfare plans from the participation and vesting part; it does not create an additional exemption from §510. The separate exercise-of-right and prospective-attainment clauses must retain their distinct work.

Respondents' strongest objection is that protection of amendable benefits would vest them indirectly and obstruct ordinary cost-saving restructuring. But a lawful revision of plan terms differs from a covered employment action undertaken for the prohibited purpose. Curtiss-Wright preserves amendment authority while requiring its lawful exercise. Lockheed separates sponsor design from implementation and independent statutory commands. Those decisions do not authorize evasion of plan procedures through a prohibited discharge. Enforcing §510's own conduct and purpose conditions answers the objection without erasing an entire defined category of plans.

The employees allege that the transfer of cargo-handling work and their discharge were designed to avoid contributions and minimize benefit payments. Those allegations must not be treated as findings. The absence of statutory vesting cannot alone sustain dismissal of their welfare theory. This holding does not promise continued employment, freeze plan terms, establish intent from cost reduction or settle every meaning of attainment. In particular, the separate argument that employees already eligible for benefits have already attained the relevant right remains open. Actual eligibility, entitlement, action, purpose, causation and authorized relief require their own consideration.

### II. The categorical dismissal is vacated for the remaining statutory inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's welfare-interference dismissal is vacated because its categorical nonvesting ground is erroneous, and the case is remanded for consideration of the properly remaining grounds. Plaintiffs must establish the applicable participant or beneficiary status, relevant plan right, prohibited action, interference purpose, causation and remedy authorized through §510's reference to §502; this decision establishes no violation or automatic recovery.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct joins support this vacatur and its scope. Stone-Zsela does not join Part II and would reverse the welfare dismissal with the reinstatement direction stated in his separate opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals dismissed the welfare theory because the benefits did not vest. Rejection of that ground does not decide every alternative argument the lower courts have not addressed. Whether a participant already eligible for the particular welfare benefit can invoke interference with attainment requires first consideration below. So do properly preserved questions concerning specific intent, eligibility, causation, mixed motives or business justification, proper defendants and relief. The Court adopts no new business-necessity test or presumption that a benefits-reducing transfer is unlawful.

Varity's function-specific fiduciary holdings and qualified individual-equity rule do not resolve this independent §510 theory. Mertens preserves the limits of the remedy actually authorized by §502; statutory coverage does not itself authorize compensatory damages. No employer or administrator status automatically establishes liability, and the circuit's nonemployer-conspiracy footnote is not adopted as a new holding of this Court.

The pension claim's reinstatement, Railroad Retirement Act exclusion, association-standing ruling, conditional request for individual class representatives, FELA and Rule 23 rulings, and fee-sanction remand are not independently reopened. The limitations contention declined below because it was first raised at appellate argument is not revived. These boundaries follow the granted welfare question; they do not affirm or reverse unrelated lower rulings by implication.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen:** Preserves lawful amendment authority and its actual procedural exercise; that power supplies no immunity for independently prohibited discharge.
- **Lockheed Corp. v. Spink:** Applies the distinction between sponsor design and independent statutory obligations without classifying every business decision as fiduciary administration.
- **Varity Corp. v. Howe:** Its fiduciary and qualified individual-equity holdings remain separate; neither those holdings nor the disagreement about them eliminates §510's express route.
- **Mertens v. Hewitt Associates:** Preserves the equitable-category limit where §502(a)(3) relief is sought; coverage creates no compensatory-damages authorization.
- **Ingersoll-Rand Co. v. McClendon:** Its pension-interference setting remains intact; it did not already decide the disputed welfare-plan coverage question.

## Law After Decision

Section 510 now expressly reaches prospective welfare-plan attainment without a vesting prerequisite. The distinction between lawful changes to plan terms and prohibited conduct directed at participants governs; nonvesting benefits remain amendable. Purpose, actual protected rights, proper defendants, causation and authorized remedies retain independent force. The meaning of attainment for already eligible participants remains unresolved, and no general unfair-dismissal or permanent-benefits rule is created.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the formal disposition.** He agrees that the enacted definition and future-attainment language include welfare plans, and that lawful amendment power does not authorize evasion through a prohibited discharge. Congress knew how to refer to pensions and nonforfeitable benefits and did not impose a vesting element in §510. He would reverse the welfare-claim dismissal and direct reinstatement of those claims for consideration of specific intent, eligibility, causation, any preserved mixed-motive or business-justification issue, proper defendants and remedy. He makes no finding of a violation. Whether these employees had already attained the particular benefits remains open, and neither nonvesting benefits nor a defendant's nonemployer or nonadministrator role is converted into automatic liability. His disposition does not restore every dismissed claim, guarantee recovery or create permanent benefits.

## Event

Johnson v. United States, No. 96-203. Argued February 25, 1997; decided May 12, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, whose judgment affirming the §1623 perjury conviction is reported at 82 F.3d 429 (1996) (table). The question is whether the unobjected judicial determination of materiality requires correction under Rule 52(b). Gaudin intervened before direct review ended; Johnson seeks a new trial.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the conviction-sustaining judgment on Rule 52(b)'s independent discretionary ground, while recognizing the jury-element error and reserving its substantial-rights classification.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

No new trial or acquittal is ordered, and no new sentence calculation or jury finding is entered. Unanimity on affirmance does not extend Scalia's opinion joins to Parts II–III.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer throughout; Scalia in Parts I and IV | Affirms through the independent fourth condition | I: error/forfeiture; II: intervening-law plainness; III: assumption and classification reservation; IV: discretionary refusal to correct. |

Scalia assumes plainness and substantial-rights impact to reach Part IV but joins neither Part II's plainness holding nor Part III's explanatory discussion. He files no separate writing; no opposing rule or reason for the withheld joins is stated.

## Holdings

### I. The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the §1623 perjury offense and must be determined by the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; the judge's determination instead was constitutional error. Johnson's failure to object presents forfeiture under Rule 52(b), not an established intentional waiver extinguishing that error.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by all eight Associates; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin requires the jury to apply the law to an actual materiality element. Griffith makes that controlling constitutional rule applicable while this case remains on direct review. Wells construed a different statute, §1014; it supplies no reason to remove §1623's genuine element. The District Judge followed then-governing circuit law, but that does not make the instruction correct under the law governing this appeal.

Olano distinguishes forfeiture, a failure timely to assert a right, from valid intentional relinquishment of a known right. Johnson did not object and earlier opposed materiality evidence because circuit law assigned that issue to the judge. Those circumstances do not establish an intentional surrender of the jury right later recognized in Gaudin. The claim therefore remains reviewable under Rule 52(b)'s distinct requirements: error, plainness, effect on substantial rights and the independent discretionary condition. A constitutional violation does not itself resolve the remedy after forfeiture, and collateral-review burdens are not imported into this direct criminal appeal.

### II. Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting

**Holding and operative rule:** When the law at trial settled an issue against the defendant but controlling law changes before direct review ends, the error is plain if it is clear at appellate consideration. This resolves that intervening-law setting without deciding every case in which the law was unsettled at trial or dispensing with Rule 52(b)'s other requirements.

**Authority:** Part II of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Scalia assumes plainness without joining this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The court exercises Rule 52(b)'s correction authority on the appeal before it. Gaudin now makes the allocation error unambiguous. A defendant need not have made a futile objection under settled contrary law to satisfy this particular plainness inquiry after that controlling change.

Olano required clear or obvious error under current law but expressly left error first clarified on appeal unresolved. The Court answers that reserved question at the stated scope rather than treating Olano as having already decided it. The objection that trial judges must be judged by the law then available confuses blame for the earlier instruction with the legal authority to correct a conviction on pending review. Recognizing plainness now does not condemn the judge's adherence to circuit precedent, make nonobjection irrelevant or guarantee relief. Substantial rights and remedial discretion remain separate conditions. The Court does not adopt a rule for every initially uncertain question.

### III. Substantial-rights impact is assumed; classification remains open

**Limiting premise:** The Court assumes in Johnson's favor that the error affected substantial rights and decides the independent discretionary condition. It does not adopt the court of appeals' third-step disposition, classify this omission as structural or nonstructural, or hold that every withheld element does or does not require an outcome-prejudice showing.

**Authority:** Part III of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by the same eight Justices as Part II. Scalia makes the assumption necessary to reach Part IV but does not join this explanatory discussion. This reservation supplies no affirmative holding about the unresolved classification.

**Explanation of scope:** Gaudin left error classification open. Sullivan held that a reasonable-doubt instruction vitiating the standard governing all findings leaves no valid reasonable-doubt verdict for harmlessness review; it did not classify every omitted element. Roy rejects an automatic structural rule for every omission while preserving its actual-verdict inquiry and the State's burden for the preserved personal-intent habeas error it addressed. Neither that burden nor its particular classification determines this forfeited materiality claim. Because the fourth condition independently resolves this record even on the favorable assumption, the Court need not decide ordinary third-step prejudice, a proposed presumption or this defect's classification. The Court creates no universal structural-error exception to Rule 52(b).

### IV. The independent discretionary condition does not warrant correction here

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming an effect on substantial rights, this forfeited materiality error does not warrant correction under Rule 52(b) because, in the established circumstances, it did not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of the proceedings. That conclusion rests on the clear materiality of the source-of-funds account in the particular investigation and the intervening change from settled trial law; it is neither a general overwhelming-evidence exception nor a substitute jury verdict.

**Authority:** Part IV of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by all eight Associates; nine direct joins support this independently sufficient ground for affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Olano gives the fourth condition work even when the first three are satisfied. The jury's constitutional role matters to that inquiry; the defendant need not identify a second, independent institutional wrong. Nor must she prove innocence. The question is whether this forfeited violation calls for extraordinary appellate correction on this record.

The grand jury was investigating Fields's drug proceeds. Johnson attributed $80,000–$120,000 used for home improvements to a box of cash Talcott gave her mother in 1985 or 1986. An alternative explanation of those funds directly bore on the investigation and could divert it. Talcott's 1982 death concerns falsity; materiality instead follows from the account's capacity to influence the particular inquiry, without requiring actual reliance. The Court assesses that relationship as overwhelming and essentially uncontroverted in the material before it, rather than inventing a party concession or a jury finding. The record supplies no plausible materiality dispute that would make a new trial serve the protected interest in these circumstances.

The strongest objection is that confidence about a missing element can merely rename appellate factfinding. The Court does not deem that element found, declare the violation harmless under a preserved-error standard or infer materiality from falsity. It assumes the injury and applies the separate forfeiture inquiry, considering the actual omission, the direct account/investigation relationship and the trial court's adherence to settled law before Gaudin. On that confined basis, ordering a new trial would not protect the fairness or reputation of adjudication. Cases with disputed materiality, less decisive evidence or an effect on trial strategy remain open. Trial courts remain obliged to submit genuine elements to juries; likely guilt alone is not a universal reason to deny correction.

## Precedent Treatment

## Holdings

### I. The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Procedural default and Teague nonretroactivity are distinct, nonjurisdictional limits, with no inflexible rule requiring one always to precede the other. In this case the Court decides the accepted Teague issue while reserving the disputed state-default predicates; that choice neither excuses default nor decides the constitutional merits.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support from Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stone-Zsela and O'Connor disagree with reaching Teague before the default inquiry in this case.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court ordinarily respects the independent state ground before granting federal relief, but these two defenses do not establish jurisdictional priority. Stringer rejected a Teague ground and remanded an unresolved default question without creating a universal order. Schiro confirms that nonretroactivity is nonjurisdictional; Caspari distinguishes proper presentation of a preserved subsidiary defense from other omitted questions. Here Teague is expressly accepted for review.

The State repeatedly asserted default, so the Court does not proceed on a supposed wholesale waiver. The present dispute concerns the adequacy and operation of the actual state rule and any legally sufficient excuse, questions not resolved by the lower appellate disposition. Addressing the accepted finality issue now does not decide those unsettled predicates or establish an advisory constitutional rule: it resolves a distinct restriction on this pending claim. The strongest alternative is to return immediately for default because a sufficient, preserved bar could end the case. That route is lawful, but is not obligatory before this accepted nonjurisdictional question. No established jurisdictional defect is bypassed, and no mandatory Teague-first rule is announced.

### II. This claim invokes an old guidance rule at November 24, 1986 finality

**Holding and operative rule:** For Lambrix's judgment final on November 24, 1986, the claim that inadequate aggravator guidance in Florida's legally consequential capital jury recommendation can infect the final death sentence invokes an already dictated rule. Teague therefore does not bar this claim as new merely because the judge separately must exercise independent judgment; the ruling establishes no actual instructional defect, harmful influence or failed cure on this record.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support from Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices. O'Connor affirmatively joins this holding once Teague is reached, while maintaining her objection to that sequence. Stone-Zsela takes no position on the new-rule question. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject the holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Godfrey already required meaningful guidance of capital aggravation. Teague and Caspari require comparison of the precise application with law at finality, not merely citation of a broad principle. Stringer applies Godfrey's existing protection to consequential weighing; Gray likewise distinguishes preserving an established protection's real function from creating a new procedural entitlement. Those current decisions govern the inquiry without being recast as cases available in 1986.

Prefinality Florida law establishes the function at issue. LeDuc gave a death recommendation operative weight if all relevant data were considered, without making it binding. Messer treated an informed jury contribution as a distinct statutory safeguard: later judicial consideration did not replace wrongly excluded accomplice-sentence and proposed psychiatric mitigation before the jury. Godfrey supplies the federal duty; the state safeguard alone does not.

Ross supplies the strongest contrary argument. It requires the judge's independent reasoned judgment and recognizes judicial experience as protection against lay notions of heinousness. Spaziano and Baldwin likewise preserve judicial sentencing authority. But Ross simultaneously retains the recommendation's weight and LeDuc's qualification. Its remand corrected excessive judicial deference, not defective jury guidance. Eutzy preserves weight and independence together, and rejected the instruction challenge only because the jury recommended life; it did not decide the instructions' merits.

The independent judge therefore adds a protection without making the consequential jury contribution disappear. Applying meaningful guidance to that contribution preserves Godfrey's existing function rather than creating a right to jury-imposed sentencing. Neither identical power nor equal weight for life and death recommendations is required. The later date of Espinosa, a reserved question or reasonable disagreement asserted without a substantive distinction cannot alone establish novelty. Actual cure remains a separate inquiry. LeDuc's affirmance, Messer's particular new-jury remedy, Ross's judicial remand and Eutzy's favorable-life reasoning do not mechanically select a remedy here.

### III-A. Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support at this exact scope from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. The further binding old-rule instruction rests separately on Part III-B's five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals disposed of the claim without resolving the State's asserted procedural bar. The actual operation of the rule and the State's litigation conduct bear on independence, adequacy, preservation and an applicable excuse. Those genuine unfinished inquiries require a bounded remand; neither an unestablished procedural premise nor a generalized waiver assertion supplies their answer.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial. Florida's identified ground concerns his failure to pursue that issue on direct appeal, not failure to object at trial. Its separate successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and alternative merits rejection do not establish a federal cause determination. The lower court must assess the actual state materials and any properly available cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice contention. It may not treat a proposed exception as already established or a present reservation as a finding that the bar is inadequate.

The common direction sets aside the reviewed appellate disposition and requires default to be addressed before any available constitutional merits and relief. It does not establish a universal order for nonjurisdictional defenses. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, declares no waiver or exception and creates no present right to a new penalty proceeding. The Justices' different positions on Teague do not erase their agreement on this operative remand task; those differences are preserved in Parts I–II, Part III-B and the separate writings.

### III-B. The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by the same five Justices as Part II. They directly adopt this additional binding direction. Stone-Zsela joins Part III-A's common vacatur and default remand, but not the instruction deciding Teague now or foreclosing its consideration if default fails; his distinct mandate is stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on a Teague conclusion the Court rejects. Vacatur therefore removes that judgment and binds further proceedings to the old-rule determination; it is not an invitation to repeat the rejected ground. The accepted claim includes the heinous, atrocious or cruel and cold, calculated and premeditated instruction issues. The court of appeals assumed inadequate guidance for nonretroactivity analysis, which establishes neither a violation nor its effect.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial; Florida relied on failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. That is not an unpreserved trial objection. Coleman and Ford require attention to the actual independent state ground, its adequacy when compliance was required, preservation and an applicable excuse. The separate state treatment of successive appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and its alternative merits rejection are not federal findings of cause. Ordinary cause and prejudice remain distinct from Sawyer's clear-and-convincing requirement that no reasonable juror would find death eligibility absent constitutional error; a selection defect alone does not meet that gateway.

If an available violation is established, Espinosa and Stringer require genuine authorized reweighing or Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt addressing the recommendation's transmitted influence. Remaining valid aggravators, bare proportionality review or an independent-judge label alone supplies neither cure. If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required. None is presently ordered.

Gray preserves any genuinely applicable, unresolved statutory condition before an operative writ. This decision settles neither chapter 153's temporal reach, amended §2254(d)'s application, chapter 154 qualification nor an exhaustion waiver or statutory exception. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, conditional writ or revived deadline, and no guilt retrial, life sentence, release, stay or execution authorization. Unrelated assistance, double-jeopardy, testimony and guilt rulings remain untouched.

## Separate Writings

**Justice O'Connor, concurring in Parts II–III and dissenting from Part I's sequencing.** She would first vacate and remand for the unresolved procedural bar. Coleman-based comity and avoidance favor resolving an adequate state ground before addressing another federal habeas limit. Exceptions are possible, including an unusually complicated default issue paired with an easily resolved Teague question, but she finds no supported reason to make an exception here. Repeated assertion of default and the lack of a lower adequacy decision warrant the bounded remand; an incomplete account of a state rule does not itself prove that the judicial question is exceptionally difficult.

Once the Court reaches Teague, she affirmatively agrees that the claim invokes old law and joins the binding remand subject to that holding. Godfrey's guidance duty follows the real contribution established by LeDuc's qualified death-recommendation weight and Messer's distinct informed-jury safeguard. Ross's independent judge and Eutzy's life-recommendation limit preserve separate roles and actual cure, rather than eliminating that contribution. Her Stringer and Espinosa joins, and her agreement with Gray's old-rule holding while reserving its final effect and remedy, keep classification distinct from present relief. She does not find default excused, an instruction actually unconstitutional, harmful transmission or a failed cure. Her alternative join is neither assent to reaching Teague now nor a formal reversal vote.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part III-A and dissenting from the present Teague determination and binding old-rule instruction.** He would vacate the Eleventh Circuit judgment and remand for the actual state bar and any applicable exception. Both defenses are nonjurisdictional, and no universal default-first ladder governs. A court may lawfully decide an easier dispositive threshold first; doing so is not inherently advisory. Here the unresolved state-rule and litigation questions make default-first review the useful bounded course because a sufficient, preserved and unexcused bar could dispose of the claim.

If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, he would deny relief on that ground. If it is unavailable or excused, he would then require the finality-specific Teague comparison under Espinosa, Stringer and Caspari, followed only through a legally available habeas channel by any merits and harmless-error inquiry. A label of novelty would not substitute for that comparison. He takes no present position on the Florida bar's ultimate validity, whether this claim is new, or entitlement to sentencing relief. His remand orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, establishes no waiver or exception and creates no right to a new penalty proceeding. His agreement to set aside and remand does not join the Court's instruction conclusively resolving Teague now.

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They would affirm on the ground that the requested application was new at November 24, 1986 finality and satisfies neither contemporary exception. The state decisions establish genuine recommendation weight and a distinct jury safeguard; the dissent does not deny those features or rely on the advisory label alone. But Godfrey's guidance requirement for the constitutional sentencer did not, in their view, compel the precise federal transmission rule despite the judge's independently reasoned sentence. Ross specifically interposes experienced judicial narrowing; Spaziano and Baldwin preserve a substantively distinct judicial function. Eutzy confirms that independence can coexist with great weight, without supplying a death-recommendation harmlessness holding.

Stringer's direct-weighing holding and current Espinosa remain binding. Their force does not establish that every later institutional application was dictated before finality. The contrary argument combines Messer's nonreplaceable jury contribution and Stringer's refusal to disregard consequential weighing. The dissent answers that a state statutory safeguard does not itself decide which defects in its input require a federal constitutional correction after an independent judicial decision. This is a finality distinction, not a finding of actual cure or an inference of novelty from disagreement alone. Kennedy and Thomas retain their Espinosa merits positions; Scalia's prior broader weighing objection is not adopted as the dissent's common ground.

The rule regulates selection of an otherwise permitted death sentence, rather than excluding conduct, persons or punishment from the State's substantive power. Its guidance protection, within a system also requiring independent judicial findings, does not satisfy the exceptional watershed category essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Both exceptions remain legally available; neither is abolished. The dissent decides no actual default, unconstitutional instruction, cure, statutory transition or execution event.
