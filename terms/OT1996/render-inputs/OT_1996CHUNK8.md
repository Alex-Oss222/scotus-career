## Event

Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board, No. 95-1455, and Price v. Bossier Parish School Board, No. 95-1508. Consolidated direct appeals from the three-judge United States District Court for the District of Columbia. Argued December 9, 1996; decided May 12, 1997. The January 8, 1996 judgment granted preclearance to the Board's twelve-district plan. The questions are whether §5 incorporates §2's results standard, whether dilution evidence bears on discriminatory purpose, and whether prohibited purpose is confined to intended retrogression. The Board adopted the Police Jury plan, containing no majority-Black district, and rejected an alternative containing two. The parties stipulated to nonretrogressive effects; §2 liability and the Board's actual purpose were not adjudicated by this Court.

## Participation

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the preclearance judgment in both consolidated appeals and remands for the purpose inquiry under the rules stated below.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded in both appeals, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All support vacatur of the ultimate clearance judgment; the scope of the remand rationale and instructions has the different coalitions identified in the opinion table. The three-judge court must consider relevant evidence, keep §2 liability distinct from §5 effect, and apply the five-Justice rule that unconstitutional purposeful racial vote dilution need not aim at retrogression. It must determine actual purpose on the existing record and any lawful supplementation. No §2 violation, unlawful purpose, replacement plan, election injunction or additional majority-Black district is ordered or found.

## Holdings

### I. Distinct statutory inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5's effect inquiry compares the proposed voting change with the operative benchmark for retrogression; a violation of §2 does not itself establish prohibited §5 effect. A proved §2 violation is also not an independent ground for withholding §5 preclearance, but clearance leaves a separate §2 challenge available.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I. All nine join the effect-benchmark and independent-action propositions. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply six direct votes for the complete nonincorporation rule; Stone-Zsela does not join that additional proposition, and Stevens and Souter oppose it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court keeps the two statutory tasks distinct. *Beer* supplies the retrogression inquiry, and *Lockhart* preserves comparison with the operative voting practice. Section 2 separately asks whether minority voters have equal electoral opportunity. *De Grandy* requires the statutory predicates and totality inquiry and rejects a command to maximize majority-minority districts. The objection that Congress cannot permit clearance of an unlawful plan mistakes clearance for a determination that the plan is lawful for every purpose. Section 2 enforcement remains available, with its own standards and burdens. Neither the stipulated lack of retrogression nor the possibility of two additional majority-Black districts decides §2 liability. The Court makes no original §2 finding and requires no proportional representation.

### II. Relevant evidence remains relevant to purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** Evidence bearing on racial dilution may also bear on the purpose of a voting change and may not be categorically excluded from §5 purpose review merely because it supports a §2 theory. When such an exclusion may have affected the purpose determination, the ultimate preclearance judgment must be vacated for consideration of the relevant evidence without treating it as conclusive proof or altering the jurisdiction's existing burden to establish absence of prohibited purpose.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by all nine Justices, controls directly.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that evidentiary relevance and substantive liability are different questions. *Arlington Heights* makes impact, historical background, the sequence of events, departures from ordinary procedure or substantive criteria, contemporaneous explanations and possible pretext relevant to intent. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* preserve purpose as an inquiry distinct from nonretrogressive effects. The Board's selection of the Police Jury plan, rejection of the alternative, parish history and disputed explanations therefore require consideration for what they show about its actual purpose. They are not presumptive proof. The contrary argument—that evidence of a permissible result cannot show an impermissible purpose—does not justify excluding evidence capable of supporting the required inference. The district court's apparent categorical separation leaves uncertainty whether it applied the correct inquiry. The Court remands rather than finds purpose itself and creates neither a same-decision defense nor a new causation rule or burden shift.

### III. Unconstitutional purposeful racial dilution is not confined to intended retrogression

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5 prohibits preclearance of a covered voting change adopted with an unconstitutional purpose to dilute voting opportunity on account of race, even when that purpose does not include worsening the existing benchmark. Nonretrogressive effect therefore does not resolve that purpose inquiry; the court must determine actual purpose, preserving the existing preclearance burden and without treating every consideration of race or rejection of a feasible alternative as unlawful.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, has five direct votes and controls the remand. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not decide this larger scope question.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the independent purpose prohibition its own work. The statutory bar addresses a purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race, not merely a purpose to worsen the existing arrangement. *Beer* defines effect through retrogression; it does not replace the separate purpose language. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* confirm that benign comparative effects do not dispose of unlawful intent. Intentional unconstitutional dilution consequently cannot obtain clearance simply because the resulting plan does not make an already deficient arrangement worse. This holding does not incorporate every §2 results violation. *Shaw* preserves the distinction between purposeful racial dilution and racial consideration in territorial design alone; *De Grandy* rejects automatic liability from the feasibility of additional districts. The Board's actual purpose remains for the factfinder. No same-decision defense, altered allocation of proof, maximization duty or proportional-representation guarantee follows.

## Precedent Treatment

- *Beer v. United States*: Retains the retrogression benchmark for §5 effect; the separate purpose rule does not overrule it.
- *City of Lockhart v. United States*: Applies its comparison with the operative voting practice, without merging §2 liability into §5.
- *Johnson v. De Grandy*: Preserves separate statutory predicates, totality proof and rejection of maximization; feasible alternative districts are evidence, not liability by themselves.
- *Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp.*: Applies its contextual intent evidence; no new same-decision or burden rule is adopted here.
- *City of Pleasant Grove v. United States* and *City of Richmond v. United States*: Apply their separation of purpose and effect to require an independent purpose inquiry.
- *Shaw v. Reno*: Preserves the distinction between purposeful deprivation and racial territorial design alone.
- *Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia* and *Young v. Fordice*: Preserve the designated preclearance process, independent enforcement and distinct coverage, benchmark, purpose and effect tasks; neither previously resolved this purpose-scope question.

## Law After Decision

Effective May 12, 1997, §5 clearance does not depend on separate §2 compliance, but cannot be granted for a change adopted with unconstitutional purposeful racial vote dilution merely because retrogression was neither intended nor achieved. Dilution evidence must receive its proper evidentiary consideration. The effect benchmark, independent §2 standards, existing allocation of the preclearance burden and designated review channels remain unchanged. Actual liability and purpose in this case remain unresolved.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** They agree with the effect benchmark, evidentiary remand and broader unconstitutional-purpose rule, but would also deny clearance when the Attorney General or a challenger proves a §2 violation. Their approach does not require the jurisdiction routinely to prove §2 compliance as an additional condition, and a feasible additional district does not establish a violation. In their view, a limited benchmark inquiry does not require approval of a racial abridgment independently established under the Act. *De Grandy*'s actual-opportunity inquiry and *Morse*'s effective statutory protection support that distinct enforcement conclusion. They find no §2 violation themselves and prescribe no map.

**Thomas, concurring:** Thomas maintains his objection to judicial statutory vote-dilution adjudication, reflected in his *De Grandy* position, while accepting the bounded remand under existing dilution precedent. He also withholds any general rule that the district court owes normal deference to the Attorney General's own preclearance standards. The Court adopts no such deference rule. His concurrence does not decide that purpose is limited to retrogression or endorse every dilution theory; he joins only Parts I–II.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring:** The Chief Justice reads the enacted purpose language to forbid a proved purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race even without an intent to worsen the benchmark. He joins the Court's unconstitutional-purposeful-dilution holding while preserving that text-based formulation. A nonretrogressive result rules out neither an unsuccessful intent to retrogress nor a different purpose to deny equal voting opportunity. The same record evidence may bear on the actual purpose without proving it presumptively. His reasoning preserves *Beer* for effect, independent §2 enforcement and the existing burden; it supplies no same-decision defense, maximization rule or finding against the Board. His concurrence does not adopt the Court's additional categorical refusal to treat proved §2 illegality as an independent clearance bar.

## Procedure After Action

Both appeals return to the three-judge District Court for a purpose determination under the evidentiary rule and the controlling nonretrogression-independent purpose rule. The ultimate clearance judgment is vacated. The Court directs neither a particular purpose finding nor a replacement district plan; lawful supplementation of the existing record remains available within ordinary procedure.

## Event

Reyes-Hernandez v. INS, on review of the Seventh Circuit judgment in No. 95-3677, 89 F.3d 490, decided July 17, 1996. Merits hearing/submission and decision: May 12, 1997. The questions concern the temporal application of AEDPA §§440(a) and 440(d) to a preenactment concession of deportability surrendering a colorable defense; the significance of that completed choice under the presumption against retroactivity; the limits of the colorable-defense premise; and the legality of the actual waiver denial and treatment of a supplemental brief. The intervening commencement of IIRIRA §242(g) presents a separate threshold issue. The October 1995 BIA order affirmed denial of §212(c) relief. Reyes, the party losing the petition for review, seeks relief from that adverse judgment; the Government's jurisdictional arguments defend that judgment through a different disposition.

## Participation

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—participated at the merits hearing/submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment denying the petition for review. It neither awards discretionary relief nor directs removal, reopening or a stay.

## Holdings

### I. Section 242(g) does not withdraw review of these completed-adjudication claims

**Holding and operative rule:** After its April 1, 1997 commencement, §242(g) reaches past, pending and future proceedings but withdraws review of the identified executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders, rather than every legal challenge arising from completed adjudication. These challenges to temporal eligibility, the legality of the completed waiver denial and treatment of a supplemental brief remain outside that withdrawal and may use the otherwise saved former §106 review route.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, has eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the threshold result.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Yang*'s April 1 commencement holding and construes the scope it reserved. Corrected §306(c)(1) applies the general amendments through §309 while extending §242(g) across old and new proceedings. Corrected §309(c)(1) preserves earlier proceedings and review subject to the stated transition rules: paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only before the evidentiary hearing has commenced, with notice at least thirty days before that hearing; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. Neither predicate exists after this completed 1995 adjudication. The October 1995 final administrative order does not meet §309(c)(4)'s requirement of an order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; no agency election or later final order is established. Section 306(c)(2) protects existing §106 judgments from invalidation or required reconsideration by paragraph (1), but creates no jurisdiction.

The contrary construction stresses the broad reference to any claim notwithstanding other law and the exception for review provided in §242. But making the three enumerated executive choices encompass every adjudicated legal issue would erase the detailed saving scheme beyond Congress's stated exceptions. Review of a completed legal result differs from challenging the executive action to adjudicate. Former §106 review is not declared to be review textually provided in §242; it survives through §309 only for claims outside the specified withdrawal. This construction does not preserve every discretionary or constitutional claim. *Felker* requires identification of the particular channel withdrawn and retained; neither a hypothetical §2241 remedy nor §1651 supplies the jurisdiction here.

### II. The two AEDPA restrictions do not attach these new consequences to the completed concession

**Holding and operative rule:** On the uncontested premise that Reyes surrendered a colorable defense to deportability before enactment in order to pursue the then-available waiver-and-review path, §440(d) does not remove eligibility for that original waiver claim, and §440(a) does not eliminate ordinary review of its lawful denial, because neither clearly prescribes those new consequences for that completed choice. A genuine defense is necessary to this lost-option rationale, not a universal element of retroactivity analysis or an exemption for every preenactment conviction, pending case or later reopening request.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Landgraf* separately to each provision: first examine enacted temporal direction, then the new legal consequence attached to the completed act. Section 440(f) directs the reach of subsection (e), not either subsection at issue. Section 440(d) would remove eligibility that formed part of Reyes's completed surrender. Section 440(a) would separately remove the then-available opportunity to correct an unlawful denial through ordinary review after that surrender. Discretionary relief was never guaranteed, but the absence of an entitlement to mercy does not answer whether a real legal option has acquired a new disability.

The strongest objection treats §440(a) solely as regulation of present judicial power. *Landgraf* does not let the jurisdictional label replace examination of the actual consequence. This record supplies a completed concession, more than mere pendency or a general hope that a forum will persist. *Felker* applied its review restriction to authorization proceedings begun after enactment, a different temporal event. The Government did not dispute the colorable-defense predicate; the Court invents no particular defense and orders no factfinding to reopen that accepted premise. An utterly insubstantial defense would not establish this surrendered-option theory. Other temporal predicates remain open, and no universal subjective-reliance requirement, constitutional invalidation, habeas holding or promise of favorable discretion is adopted. The IIRIRA dual-offense technical correction supplies no additional conviction finding.

### III. Eligibility does not compel a favorable waiver balance

**Holding and operative rule:** An eligible §212(c) applicant remains subject to a reasoned individualized exercise of lawful discretion, and the BIA may adopt an immigration judge's adequate reasons without independently repeating the entire balance. Here consideration of rehabilitation and family hardship against serious, recent and repeated drug offenses lawfully supports denial; the reviewing court may not replace that balance merely because the favorable equities are substantial.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes. The jurisdictional dissenters do not reach this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Yang*'s distinction between eligibility, permissible factors and reasoned administration. Permission to exercise discretion does not permit pretext, disregard of binding rules or an unexplained departure from genuinely settled policy. Those defects are not established here. The immigration judge addressed family hardship and rehabilitation, yet gave greater weight to repeated serious drug violations; the BIA adopted those actual reasons. Adoption of an adequate explanation is not absence of analysis. Reyes's stronger-equities argument asks for a fresh judicial weighing, not correction of a demonstrated legal error. The Court sustains this denial without creating a presumption against every eligible drug offender or foreclosing review of an actual policy departure.

### IV. A supplemental appellate brief did not require original factfinding or automatic reopening

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the BIA could require the established motion to supplement, remand or reopen before considering new post-hearing evidence offered in an appellate brief, rather than treating that brief as a different motion or conducting original appellate factfinding. The decision does not permit disregard of material evidence already admitted or decide the eligibility, timeliness, merits or disposition of any later motion.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes refusing to receive new evidence through an improper vehicle from ignoring evidence already in the record. The supplemental brief described later employment, sobriety and volunteer work, but the agency had not admitted that material. It required the established procedural route and expressly identified reopening as remaining possible. *Abudu* supplies the governing reopening framework; *Doherty*'s fractured result supplies no controlling general rationale. The argument that several years of developments could affect the equities shows why an evidentiary route matters, not why the Board had to convert a merits brief into a new proceeding. On the actual record that requirement was lawful. The Court neither guarantees a successful motion nor decides a later proceeding.

## Precedent Treatment

- *INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang*: Applies its April 1 commencement holding, decides the previously reserved §242(g) scope at the stated boundary, and applies its separation of eligibility from reasoned discretion.
- *Landgraf v. USI Film Products*: Applies its provision-specific temporal inquiry to the completed surrender of an actual legal option; neither pendency nor a jurisdictional label controls by itself.
- *Felker v. Turpin*: Preserves channel-specific review and distinguishes its post-enactment authorization proceedings; no presumed alternative remedy replaces the actual preserved route.
- *INS v. Abudu*: Preserves the existing reopening framework and distinction between record review and presentation of new material.
- *INS v. Doherty*: Its fractured disposition still supplies no general controlling reopening rationale; no separate writing is elevated into law.

## Law After Decision

Effective May 12, 1997, §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not eliminate these legal challenges to a completed adjudication within the saved former review scheme. The separate AEDPA review and eligibility restrictions do not attach the specified retrospective consequences to this uncontested surrender of a colorable defense. That limited temporal protection supplies review and eligibility, not a waiver, automatic reopening or a guarantee that agency discretion will favor the applicant. The completed waiver denial and ordinary new-evidence procedure stand. Other convictions, temporal predicates, later motions, actual habeas scope and constitutional review requirements remain unresolved.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** They join the bounded §242(g) construction but conclude that §440(a) withdraws present direct-review jurisdiction. Their relevant-activity approach, maintained in *Landgraf* and *Felker*, identifies the judicial review now sought as the activity that provision governs. Surrender of a defense may bear on the separate substantive eligibility question without assuring a perpetual reviewing forum. They therefore would vacate the Seventh Circuit's merits judgment and direct dismissal for want of jurisdiction, not deny the waiver on its merits. They leave §440(d)'s separate temporal question, actual habeas review and constitutional limits unresolved. Their conditional assessment that *Yang* would support the agency's actual reasons if jurisdiction existed is not an operative merits join.

## Procedure After Action

The Seventh Circuit's judgment denying the petition remains in force. No new agency balance, hearing or reopening is ordered. Any later motion must proceed under its own applicable requirements; none is deemed filed or decided. This judgment supplies no separate removal-execution or stay order.

## Event

United States v. Ortiz and United States v. Hurtado, consolidated review of Seventh Circuit Nos. 95-1386 and 95-1869, 84 F.3d 977. Argued January 14, 1997; decided May 12, 1997. The Seventh Circuit's May 24, 1996 judgment affirmed Ortiz's convictions against his pager-suppression challenge and separately vacated Hurtado's 37-month consecutive contempt sentence with directions to use §2J1.5. The questions are whether immediate retrieval of stored numerical messages from a pager taken from Ortiz's person was a permissible search incident to lawful arrest, and how the actual conduct, supported purpose and effect of Hurtado's immunized refusal to testify govern the most-analogous-guideline choice and resentencing. The two defendants' judgments are distinct. Ortiz's vehicle, firearm and next-day wristwatch issues are outside review; Hurtado's contempt conviction is unchallenged.

## Holdings

### I. Immediate retrieval from this on-person numerical pager falls within the arrest exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Activating the seized pager to disclose stored numerical messages is a Fourth Amendment search distinct from physical seizure. A lawful custodial arrest nevertheless permits this contemporaneous examination of a numerical pager taken from the arrestee's person under the established personal-effects search-incident authority, without a separate showing that this particular pager faced imminent overwrite; the holding does not authorize delayed retrieval, remotely stored information or searches of electronic devices generally.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I. All nine join the search/seizure distinction; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply eight direct votes for the stated incident-search rule and affirmance. Stone-Zsela rejects that categorical ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court recognizes protected information and then asks whether a lawful exception reaches it. *Pinson* rejects the claim that technology or external reception eliminates a protected search; *Wellons* separates possession of an object from authority to examine its closed contents. Neither abolishes established exceptions. *Robinson* permits a contemporaneous search of the person and immediately associated personal effects following lawful custodial arrest without a new danger finding for each object. This pager was taken from Ortiz and read immediately at the parking lot. *Chadwick* concerned property not immediately associated with the person and a search remote from arrest; securing this on-person item does not alone place it outside *Robinson*.

The contrary argument—that secured electronic information cannot injure an officer and has no proved destruction risk—identifies why a generic exigency rationale would fail, but does not defeat the established incident category applied here. The record establishes neither this device's capacity or overwrite behavior nor an imminent incoming page or inability to preserve data without reading. The Court therefore adopts no independent particularized-exigency finding or rule that every pager must be searched immediately. *Richards* rejects an unsupported warrant-execution category while preserving justified categories in other settings; *Chandler*'s different special-needs inquiry likewise does not displace *Robinson*. The ruling concerns this immediately searched numerical pager, not every form or time of electronic inquiry. The pager-admission ruling and Ortiz's reviewed judgment stand.

### II. Contempt requires a supported most-analogous-guideline choice

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 2J1.1 and 2X5.1 require the most analogous guideline for the actual contempt, assessed through its nature and circumstances, effect on administration of justice and need to vindicate judicial authority; neither obstruction nor material-witness failure applies automatically to every immunized refusal to testify. Hurtado's existing sentence remains vacated, but the mandatory §2J1.5 direction must also be vacated because a properly supported obstruction analogy does not require successful obstruction, an acquittal-seeking motive in every case or proof of every statutory accessory element; resentencing must compare the lawful analogies without preselecting a guideline or term.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, has five direct votes. All nine accept the textual most-analogous command and sentence vacatur; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg also accept the common commentary principles but disagree with leaving the guideline choice open. Stone-Zsela adopts no additional general commentary doctrine.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Stinson*: explanatory commentary is authoritative unless unconstitutional, contrary to federal statute, inconsistent with the guideline or plainly erroneous, and cannot amend contrary text. Section 2J1.1's consistent commentary requires contextual comparison and identifies obstruction as analogous in some cases. *Koon* separately preserves correction of legal errors and unsupported grounds, protection of supported findings and proper deference to sentencing judgments; it does not insulate a mistaken analogy or authorize new appellate sentencing reasons.

Hurtado refused further testimony after immunity and a direct order, although his limited testimony helped identify Ortiz and his own transaction. The district court's additional characterization that he sought to help Ortiz escape punishment lacks record support and cannot sustain 37 months. Yet a refusal can obstruct a prosecution despite eventual conviction or a wish to avoid informing. Absence of the elements of the statutory accessory offense does not by itself defeat operation of a lawfully selected guideline cross-reference. The appellate court's comparison with more serious crimes likewise cannot replace the required analogy with the gentler range. Earlier cooperation and the absence of additional assistance to Ortiz are material facts favoring the competing material-witness comparison, but do not compel that choice as a matter of the legal tests used below. Resentencing must rest on the actual supported conduct and commentary, not an automatic accessory label, presumed acquittal purpose or a fixed replacement sentence.

## Precedent Treatment

- *United States v. Robinson*: Applies the established contemporaneous on-person personal-effects exception to this numerical pager; no independent device-specific danger finding is required for that ground.
- *Chimel v. California* and *United States v. Chadwick*: Preserve the search/seizure and temporal limits of arrest-related authority; *Chadwick*'s remote search of property not immediately associated with the person is distinguished.
- *United States v. Pinson* and *United States v. Wellons*: Preserve protected concealed-information and closed-content interests while leaving recognized exceptions available; neither is overruled or converted into a universal electronic-memory rule.
- *Richards v. Wisconsin* and *Chandler v. Miller*: Preserve the actual limits of their warrant-execution and special-needs holdings; neither forbids all categorical Fourth Amendment rules.
- *Stinson v. United States*: Applies the complete consistency-limited authority of interpretive commentary; commentary cannot amend contrary guideline text.
- *Koon v. United States; Powell v. United States*: Applies independent correction of legal and unsupported-ground error within its task-sensitive review framework; it supplies no automatic guideline selection, fixed sentence or substitute appellate reasons.

## Law After Decision

Effective May 12, 1997, contemporaneous retrieval of the stored numerical messages from this pager taken from the person is a protected search justified by the established incident-to-arrest exception. Lawful possession of hardware alone remains insufficient, and no general device, delayed-search or categorical erasure-exigency rule is created. Contempt sentencing must make the actual most-analogous-guideline comparison; refusal after immunity neither automatically becomes accessory conduct nor categorically excludes an obstruction analogy. Hurtado must be resentenced on supported grounds, with lawful adjustments and the resulting term left for that proceeding.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** They join Ortiz's affirmance and the common sentencing principles but would affirm the mandatory §2J1.5 direction. *Stinson* and *Koon* require attention to what the record supports. Hurtado's refusal, earlier cooperation and limited helpful testimony, without supported additional conduct assisting Ortiz's escape from punishment, make the material-witness analogy the closer one here. They do not require obstruction to succeed, make comparative sentence harshness an independent legal rule or exempt all other refusals from obstruction treatment. They prescribe no replacement term and leave the contempt conviction intact.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** On Ortiz, the Chief Justice agrees that retrieval is a search distinct from seizure, but would not extend the categorical physical-container treatment of *Robinson* and *Belton* to secured electronic memory beyond its preservation justification. Immediate retrieval limited to volatile data requires known operation of the particular device and circumstances showing a concrete, imminent overwrite risk before a warrant can reasonably be obtained, with no way to preserve the information without viewing it. This record establishes neither capacity, overwrite behavior, an imminent incoming page nor absence of a nonsearch preservation method. He would reverse and remand for other preserved admission grounds and harmlessness under *Pinson* and *Wellons*, preserving the lawful arrest and seizure. His rule permits no exploratory review, unrelated or nonvolatile-data search, delayed inspection after the risk ends or reliance on a generic alteration possibility; if information can be preserved without inspection, a warrant is required.

On Hurtado, he would affirm vacatur and the §2J1.5 direction because the enacted guideline chain requires the actual best analogy. The limited testimony assisted identification, no purpose to aid acquittal was established, and aversion to informing does not itself establish accessory-after-the-fact conduct. Actual conduct, supported purpose and effect make the material-witness analogy better on this record; actual obstruction on different supported facts may warrant another guideline. He fixes no replacement sentence and disturbs no contempt conviction.

The contempt conviction remains untouched, and no adjustment, range or new term is selected here.

## Event

Camps Newfound/Owatonna, Inc. v. Town of Harrison, No. 94-1988. On review of the Supreme Judicial Court of Maine, 655 A.2d 876, which on April 21, 1995 reversed summary judgment for the camp and sustained the challenged property tax. Argued October 9, 1996; decided May 19, 1997. The question is whether the nonresident-beneficiary condition in Maine's charitable-property exemption discriminates against interstate commerce and whether a subsidy, market-participant or resident-benefit justification sustains it. The Maine nonprofit's 180-acre Christian Science camp recruits nationally; about 95 percent of campers come from outside Maine. Its reported charge is roughly $400 per week and annual property taxes exceed $20,000. It timely pursued state refund and prospective-exemption procedures.

Section 652(1)(A) generally covers real and personal property owned and occupied or used solely for the purposes of benevolent and charitable institutions incorporated by Maine, without loss of exemption merely because of the source of funds or limitations in beneficiary classes, subject to the express nonresident subparagraph. An institution operated principally for nonresidents may exempt no more than $50,000 of current just property value only if the statutory average weekly charge per person does not exceed $30; above that rate it receives no exemption under that subparagraph. The computation divides all tax-year stipends or charges for services, benefits or advantages by all persons receiving them during that year, then divides by the weeks during that tax year when the institution is actually operated principally for nonresidents; §502 defines the tax year. The reported weekly price is not substituted for that statutory computation.

The nonresident subparagraph does not apply to a nonprofit corporation incorporated solely to conduct medical research; that exception does not waive the general exemption's other requirements and is not found applicable to this camp. The paragraph also includes nonprofit nursing, boarding and boarding-care facilities licensed under Title 22, chapter 1665 or its successor; nonprofit community mental-health facilities licensed under Title 34-B, chapter 3; and nonprofit child-care centers incorporated by Maine as benevolent and charitable institutions. Nonprofit means exempt under Internal Revenue Code §501(c)(3). No independent challenge to Maine incorporation, neutral qualification, religious liberty, equal protection or travel rights is decided.

## Participation

## Holdings

### I. Paid interstate camp services remain protected against discriminatory taxation

**Holding and operative rule:** A charitable camp that recruits nationally and sells services to visitors crossing state lines participates in interstate commerce, although its property and performance are local and it is nonprofit. Where the same otherwise qualifying private camp offering receives worse tax treatment because its principal beneficiaries are nonresidents, that express geographic condition discriminates; a claimed resident-service distinction must rest on the actual relationship or offering, not beneficiary residence alone.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, controls by six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court examines the transaction and the comparison. *Heart of Atlanta* establishes that interstate travel to obtain locally consumed services is commerce. *Fulton* establishes that a local taxpayer and local property do not remove a tax condition keyed to an interstate relationship. Nonprofit organization does not eliminate the paid camp service and national clientele, though the Court decides no rule for every charitable act or public benefit.

*Tracy* requires attention to actual service obligations. Its regulated rate, reliability and service duties materially distinguished the offering there; it created no categorical public-utility or welfare exception. Here the asserted difference is that resident-serving charity benefits Maine's own community. That is a legitimate objective, but the present condition identifies recipients rather than a materially different private offering or supported public-service obligation. No actual service-segment uncertainty comparable to *Tracy* requires a new evidentiary hearing. The Court neither makes utility-style regulation indispensable in every case nor presumes all charitable services identical. It rejects this particular attempt to make the challenged residence criterion establish its own immunity. The same camp's tax treatment depends on changing the geographic mix of its clients. The low-charge property-value cap and medical-research exception do not make that applied condition neutral; the Court decides no hypothetical claimant's qualification under either provision.

### II. Resident benefit, subsidy and public-capacity labels do not justify this tax condition

**Holding and operative rule:** This discriminatory private-clientele tax condition can survive only upon a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; support for resident charity does not establish that showing here. Forgone taxes do not make Maine a purchaser of the camp's services or remove the discriminatory tax condition from review, and a compensatory-tax defense requires an identified intrastate burden, a roughly equivalent nonexcessive charge and substantially equivalent taxable events, none established by general resident contributions or asserted charitable savings.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, has six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Oregon Waste*'s discrimination-and-alternatives inquiry and *Fulton*'s refusal to let a deduction label cleanse geographic favoritism. Maine may support local welfare through direct resident programs, ordinary general-revenue assistance or neutral charitable-property criteria; it has not shown that resident support requires worsening a private provider's tax treatment because outsiders buy its services. The contrary argument treats the exemption itself as resident-directed aid and stresses that direct payments can also select residents. But permissible public assistance does not confer immunity on taxation controlling independent private clientele. This decision neither invalidates direct resident benefits nor requires charitable support everywhere.

*SSC* protects actual purchased performance, while no purchase, contract for camp services or government operation is established here. *West Lynn* examines practical fiscal operation and preserves ordinary assistance; Maine need not replicate its dedicated commercial assessment for a different discriminatory tax condition to fail. The tax/subsidy comparison is therefore not merely an economic equivalence inquiry. Compensation is independently governed by *Fulton* and *Oregon Waste*: no matching burden, equivalence or equivalent taxable event follows from generalized contribution or expenditure-saving claims. The Court finds no invented numerical disproportion, no litigant default and no duty to disprove every imaginable alternative. It applies the existing rule to the actual grounds supporting this condition.

### III. Reversal requires lawful state relief, not an automatic exemption or fixed refund

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining this discriminatory condition must be reversed and the case remanded for state-law severability, remaining neutral exemption conditions and constitutionally adequate relief. If payment was compelled before meaningful review, adequate backward-looking correction is required; independently, a clearly held-out postpayment remedy may not be withdrawn after payment and replaced by an unpursued prepayment route, even where payment was voluntary, subject to reasonable preannounced conditions and fair prospective changes.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, has five direct votes. Stone-Zsela would require the exemption under the presented scheme and does not join this complete remedial choice; Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg would affirm.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Fulton*'s separation of constitutional invalidity from the State's lawful remedial choices. *McKesson* supplies the conditional backward-looking duty when payment precedes meaningful review; *Reich* separately prevents withdrawal of the promised postpayment route. Where correction is required, lawful refunds, constitutionally permissible retrospective equalization or a lawful combination may suffice. The camp's timely pursuit of state procedures preserves its claim but does not establish the amount due or every neutral exemption qualification. Reversal therefore does not automatically reinstate every feature of the trial-level judgment. The state courts must address their law and the actual procedural predicates without enforcing the invalid residence preference. The Court orders no particular refund, interest, assessment against absent charities, unconditional enlarged exemption or substitute subsidy, and calculates no statutory weekly average.

## Event

Edmond v. United States, No. 96-262, including under Rule 12.4 the petitions of Lazenby, Leaver, Leonard, Nichols and Venable. Argued February 24, 1997; decided May 19, 1997. The Court reviews the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces judgments reported at 45 M.J. 19, 44 M.J. 272 and 44 M.J. 273. The questions are whether §323(a) authorized the Secretary of Transportation to appoint civilian judges of the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals and whether these judges are inferior officers under the Appointments Clause. Two civilian judges previously assigned by the Department's General Counsel were formally appointed by the Secretary. The petitioners seek new lawful military appellate review; the Government seeks affirmance.

## Participation

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court sustains the Secretary's actual appointments and affirms each of the six military appellate judgments within the granted appointment questions.

## Judgment & Remedy

**All six judgments affirmed, 9–0.** Supporting for each of Edmond, Lazenby, Leaver, Leonard, Nichols and Venable: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The civilian judges were lawfully appointed inferior officers. No appointment defect requiring fresh Article 66 review is established; no acquittal, trial-conviction reversal or new hearing is ordered.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Affirms all six judgments | Parts I–II in full |

The statutory holding is unanimous. The combined-controls classification rationale has eight direct votes; Souter's distinct sufficient rationale is not an additional join in it.

## Holdings

### I. Congress authorized these appointments by the Secretary

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 323(a), read with Article 66's provision for civilian military appellate judges, authorizes the Secretary of Transportation as department head to appoint the civilian officers serving on the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals. Article 66 assignment is distinct from appointment, but no additional statute repeating the office's name in an appointment clause is required for these existing statutory offices.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, joined by all eight Associates, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the two enactments together. Section 323(a) expressly permits the Secretary to appoint departmental officers and employees and prescribe their duties and powers. Article 66 establishes the military appellate structure and permits both commissioned officers and qualified civilians to serve as appellate judges. The former supplies appointment authority; the latter supplies the office, assignment and adjudicative responsibilities. The argument that general personnel administration cannot create judicial offices therefore misses both the genuine appointment language and the already established office. The Court infers no power to create unrelated offices or appoint principal officers.

*Weiss* upheld germane assignments of officers already commissioned by the President with Senate consent and expressly left civilian appointments open. It cannot validate these civilians by itself. *Freytag* preserves the need for a constitutionally proper appointment, and *Ryder* prevents a prior defective assignment from becoming lawful merely through continued service. The Secretary's actual formal appointment, rather than the General Counsel's earlier assignment, supplies the lawful statutory act. The Court does not hold that every general personnel statute is sufficient or that an official other than a constitutionally recognized department head may make these appointments. Constitutional rank remains an independent question.

### II. The combined statutory hierarchy establishes inferior status here

**Holding and operative rule:** These civilian military appellate judges are inferior officers because the combined structure subjects their assignments and administration to the Judge Advocate General, permits removal from judicial assignments and prescription of rules, and places their decisions under superior review by presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed judges; they lack final authority free of that system of direction and review. Their significant duties and protected adjudicative independence do not make them principal officers, and neither appellate review nor removal alone is held sufficient for every office.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight direct votes. Souter agrees with inferior status on a distinct fuller rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court evaluates the actual relationship of this office to its superiors. The Judge Advocate General organizes the appellate court, prescribes procedural rules and controls judicial assignments and removal from those assignments. The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces provides superior judicial review within the executive structure. Article 67 review is required for affirmed death sentences and cases the Judge Advocate General sends, and otherwise requires the accused's petition and good cause. Under Article 67(c), it may act only on the findings and sentence approved by the convening authority and affirmed or set aside as incorrect in law by the Court of Criminal Appeals, and takes action only on matters of law. In a Judge Advocate General referral, it need take action only on the issues he raises; on the accused's petition, it need take action only on issues specified in the grant. Those minimum issue obligations do not convert legal review into guaranteed plenary factual rehearing.

The petitioners correctly emphasize Article 66's real power to weigh evidence, assess credibility, decide disputed facts and determine the findings and sentence that should be approved. But those important adjudicative tasks operate within the combined hierarchy rather than create unreviewable executive authority. *Morrison* and *Freytag* support attention to the actual office; *Weiss* preserves judicial safeguards without exempting military offices from the Appointments Clause. Neither one supervisory feature nor a general measure of importance decides the case. Removal authority is not permission for unlawful decisional interference.

*Ryder* requires fresh lawful Article 66 review for a qualifying timely substantial appointment defect and does not treat Article 67 as a substitute for the lost fact-and-sentence review. That rule remains intact. Here the actual appointment is both authorized and constitutionally permissible, so its defect premise is absent and the six judgments stand. The Court decides neither Article III judges' status nor an office with unreviewable final authority and no removable or supervisory superior, and establishes no minimum supervision formula for every different institutional design.

## Precedent Treatment

- *Weiss v. United States*: Preserves its commissioned-officer/germane-duty holding and independent judicial safeguards; the civilian question it reserved is now answered through separate statutory authority and classification.
- *Ryder v. United States*: Preserves meaningful fresh Article 66 adjudication for a timely direct challenge establishing a substantial appointment defect; no remedy is triggered by these lawful appointments and no de facto validation is used.
- *Buckley v. Valeo*: Preserves the officer character of significant governmental adjudicative duties and the requirement of constitutional appointment.
- *Morrison v. Olson*: Informs examination of the actual office; no universal one-factor test or categorical military exception replaces that inquiry.
- *Freytag v. Commissioner*: Preserves constitutional appointment requirements and attention to office functions; the presence of adjudicative discretion does not alone establish principal status.

## Law After Decision

Effective May 19, 1997, the Secretary's §323(a) authority together with Article 66 supports appointment of these civilian appellate judges, and their combined subordinate structure establishes inferior status. Appointment, assignment, classification and defect remedies remain separate inquiries. The law does not validate every earlier General Counsel assignment, enlarge §323(a) to principal offices, erase Article 66 factual review or require new review after a lawful appointment.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment:** Souter joins the statutory authority holding and affirms every petitioner's judgment. He accepts a supervisory superior as necessary here but does not regard administration, removal and higher review alone as a sufficient classification inquiry. Under *Morrison*, the court must also examine the office's duties, jurisdiction, tenure and removal, with appropriate respect for the political branches' judgment in this close classification. The judges perform substantial, independent Article 66 review, but their jurisdiction and assignment-based tenure, the available removal and actual superior relationship, taken together, establish inferior status. His *Weiss* and *Ryder* positions preserve both constitutional appointment and meaningful adjudication; neither makes Article 67 a full factual rehearing or creates a blanket military exception. This fuller ground supports the same result without joining the Court's sufficient combined-controls rationale.

## Procedure After Action

The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces judgments for Edmond, Lazenby, Leaver, Leonard, Nichols and Venable remain intact within the granted appointment issues. No remand for renewed Article 66 review follows, and the Court resolves no independent trial, evidence or sentence-merits question.

## Event

Edwards v. Balisok, No. 95-1352. On review of the Ninth Circuit's October 5, 1995 judgment. Argued November 13, 1996; decided May 19, 1997. The question is whether §1983 claims alleging procedural defects in prison discipline may proceed when success necessarily invalidates an outstanding loss of good-time credits. Balisok lost thirty days of earned credits and alleges concealment of exculpatory statements, refusal of requested questions, failure to state supporting facts, deceit and bias. ## Participation

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's categorical procedural-claim rule and remands. The invalidity-dependent deceit-and-bias claims require dismissal without prejudice absent qualifying invalidation; genuinely independent procedural claims and prospective relief require separate adjudication.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Damages and retrospective declaratory claims whose success necessarily invalidates the outstanding credit adjudication are dismissed without prejudice unless qualifying invalidation has occurred. The lower court must separately evaluate any genuinely independent statement-of-reasons, questioning or other procedural theory and the prospective date-stamping request. There is no award of credits, release, damages or an injunction; no claim is declared meritorious and no misconduct is found.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Reverses the categorical exemption and remands for the specified claim-specific dispositions | Entire opinion, including invalidity prerequisite, independent injuries, dismissal/accrual and prospective-relief limits |

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices, supplies nine direct votes for every following rule and qualification.

### I. Procedural labels do not avoid necessary invalidity

**Holding and operative rule:** In the in-custody review setting presented, damages and retrospective declaratory claims that a disciplinary adjudicator deliberately concealed exculpatory evidence and acted dishonestly or with disabling bias are not presently cognizable under §1983 when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of the outstanding deprivation of earned good-time credits, absent qualifying invalidation. The inquiry concerns the legal consequence of the actual claim, not its procedural label or omission of a request to restore credits; the reserved position of former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity is not decided.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Heck*'s controlling necessary-implication rule at its recorded scope. Deliberate suppression and disabling bias would vitiate the existing adjudication supporting the credit loss. The possibility that a later fair hearing could reach the same misconduct result does not make the present adjudication valid. *Preiser* separately places restoration of credits in habeas; omitting that requested relief does not make an invalidity-dependent declaration or damages theory independent. *Wolff* preserves procedural civil relief compatible with an outstanding custody judgment, and *Carey* recognizes process injuries even where the ultimate result could have been the same. Those principles require comparison of the actual claim with the adjudication, not categorical permission for every procedural challenge. *Sandin* and *Young v. Harper* preserve significant liberty and adequate process; neither eliminates the governing remedial channel. The Court applies binding operative precedent without requiring adoption of a new universal common-law or statutory-coordination rationale. It does not make present habeas availability a universal element or decide the claimant classes that *Heck* reserved.

### II. Genuinely independent procedural injuries require their own analysis

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim falls outside Heck's invalidation prerequisite when its success does not necessarily establish that the credit adjudication was invalid, even if it arises from the same disciplinary hearing. A genuinely separable failure to state supporting facts can meet that boundary, and questioning or other procedural allegations must be classified individually; separability establishes neither a due-process violation nor entitlement to damages.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Heck*'s independent-injury rule and *Wolff*'s compatible civil-remedy category. The State cannot obtain dismissal of every alleged omission merely because the same complaint also alleges disabling bias. A separately deficient statement of supporting facts may be a process injury compatible with the underlying credit determination; the precise pleaded theory controls. *Carey* permits an appropriate nominal remedy for an established procedural denial without presuming compensable injury, but a nominal-damages label does not save a necessarily invalidating theory. Conversely, refusal of particular questions does not automatically state a constitutional violation or necessarily undermine the credit decision. *Wolff* preserves the ability to refuse witnesses for irrelevance, lack of necessity or safety or correctional hazards; this case creates no entitlement to every requested question. The lower court must examine the applicable process and each claim's necessary consequence, preserving ordinary pleading, personal responsibility, immunity, liability and remedial requirements. No official misconduct or harmlessness is found here.

### III. Qualifying invalidation precedes accrual; an exhaustion stay is not the remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** For an invalidity-dependent claim within Heck's decided category, qualifying invalidation means reversal on direct review, executive expungement, invalidation by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling the custody judgment into question, and the claim accrues only then. Without that event the claim is dismissed without prejudice rather than stayed to impose state-remedy exhaustion; failed review, exhaustion or expiration or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Heck*'s deferred-accrual and dismissal consequences. The limitations period for the barred, unaccrued claim is not consumed while the prerequisite remains unmet; no preservation stay or immediate-accrual tolling device is necessary. *Patsy* forbids imposing a general state-remedy exhaustion requirement on §1983, and favorable invalidation is a cognizability prerequisite rather than exhaustion by another name. Keeping this unavailable claim pending while directing pursuit of state relief would confuse those distinct rules. The decision does not convert the complaint to habeas, establish a right to release or decide whether independently applicable statutory administrative exhaustion is satisfied. A stay for an independently authorized reason remains a different question. Loss of custody-review opportunity does not itself establish invalidation or decide the reserved claimant categories.

### IV. Prospective date stamping remains for ordinary equitable adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuinely prospective request governing future date stamping of received witness statements ordinarily need not invalidate a completed credit adjudication and is not automatically barred by Heck. Because this request was not adjudicated below, remand requires examination of live standing, ordinary equitable requirements and the actual claim; no prospective label saves relief whose substance necessarily invalidates the past judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** A future receipt practice can operate without restoring past credits or declaring the completed adjudication invalid. *Heck*'s claim-specific comparison therefore does not support categorical dismissal merely because the requested practice concerns prison discipline. But a compatible subject does not establish a continuing personal stake, likely future injury or entitlement to an injunction. Those actual predicates remain for the lower court on the unadjudicated request. The Court awards no injunction and does not prescribe the details of a prison recording system.

## Precedent Treatment

- *Heck v. Humphrey*: Applies the bounded in-custody necessary-implication prerequisite, four invalidation routes, deferred accrual, independent-injury boundary and dismissal without prejudice to credit-affecting discipline. Its reserved claimant categories and distinct rationale joins remain unchanged.
- *Preiser v. Rodriguez*: Preserves habeas as the route for restoring credits without converting this damages complaint into habeas.
- *Wolff v. McDonnell*: Preserves compatible procedural civil relief and its context-sensitive process qualifications; not every alleged procedural defect invalidates the discipline or establishes a violation.

- *Patsy v. Board of Regents*: Remains the rule against a general judicial §1983 state-exhaustion requirement; an invalidation prerequisite is distinct.
- *Sandin v. Conner* and *Young v. Harper*: Preserve meaningful liberty and required process without deciding a general exemption from Heck's remedy-channel limits.

Genuinely independent procedural injuries and compatible prospective relief remain subject to ordinary adjudication. No categorical all-procedure permission or all-procedure dismissal survives. No extension to former prisoners or unavailable-review claimants, automatic release, conversion to habeas or general exhaustion stay is adopted.

##### I. The offense-date statute covers these transmitted images

**Holding and operative rule:** Offense-date §1465 reaches obscene visual image files knowingly transported through interstate computer-and-telephone facilities for sale or distribution. Applying its picture, image and electrical-transcription terms to this means of conveying the charged images does not depend on a later computer-specific amendment; the statutory distribution purpose and applicable culpability requirements remain necessary.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this statutory proposition directly, although Stevens dissents on the constitutional question.

**Controlling explanation:** The objects transmitted were the pictures made available to the approved member, not merely an abstract sequence of numbers divorced from those pictures. The statutory media terms encompass their actual conveyance. United States v. Alpers supports applying comprehensive media language to a method beyond the familiar physical forms; legislative purpose alone would not create an offense. Carlin's treatment of recorded telephone messages does not remove visual images from the enacted terms. United States v. Lanier requires fair warning in the law at the time of the conduct, so neither a later amendment nor a later judicial decision supplies an earlier prohibition. The ordinary visual-media language and the knowing distribution presented here answer the statutory objection. This holding establishes no rule that every new technology necessarily falls within every old criminal statute.

##### II. Receiving-community standards apply to this controlled distribution

**Holding and operative rule:** When operators screen and approve a subscriber, know his receiving community and knowingly authorize delivery there, Miller's geographic community standards may govern prurient appeal and patent offensiveness. Serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value remains Pope's reasonable-person inquiry, independent of local approval; no national cyberspace standard is required on this record.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by the eight Justices identified as its complete joiners. Stevens does not join this proposition. Eight direct votes supply controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller v. California and Hamling v. United States permit the receiving community's standards in the distribution of obscenity; Pope v. Illinois separately protects serious value from local majoritarian judgment. Sable Communications applies the obscenity rule to commercial electronic transmission while preserving the distinction between obscenity and protected indecency. Here membership review, address information, telephone contact and individual passwords gave the operators actual control over this distribution. They knew that they were serving Memphis and could decline the subscription or delivery. The concern that an unknown viewer could expose an uncontrollable global publication to an unexpected locality therefore does not describe these transactions. A recipient's willingness does not remove otherwise unprotected obscenity from the governing distribution doctrine. The Court does not announce a general rule for open websites, anonymous networks, unavoidable routing or publishers unable to identify or control recipients, and does not require knowledge of obscenity law or of the jury's eventual legal classification.

##### III. Venue lies where the authorized interstate transportation ends

**Holding and operative rule:** Article III, the Sixth Amendment and 18 U.S.C. §3237(a) permit trial in the district where the charged continuing interstate transportation reaches the recipient whom the distributor authorized to receive it. The approved Memphis downloads completed that transportation there; known destination supports this application without becoming a universal additional statutory element.

**Authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this bounded venue proposition directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Venue follows the charged conduct's actual beginning and completion. The operators authorized and provided the interstate transfer to this screened subscriber. The inspector's act of downloading was the completion of that offered distribution, not an independent event with no connection to the defendants' conduct. Locating the server in California does not remove the receiving district from a continuing offense under §3237(a). Mere remote effects or an unknown viewer would not, by themselves, establish this holding's premises. The Court decides neither venue in every district traversed by electronic signals nor venue for a passive page addressed to an unknown audience.

The judgment affirms only the three granted questions. It does not decide entrapment, selective prosecution, separate evidentiary or counsel objections, sentencing, forfeiture, a new child-pornography theory or the legal effect of uncharged communications. Robert Thomas's Count 11 acquittal remains undisturbed.

##### I. Religious advocacy is a viewpoint within this admitted program

**Controlling explanation:** Lamb's Chapel supplies the distinction between a religious perspective on an admitted subject and a subject genuinely outside a forum. Good News/Good Sports applies that distinction where prayer and Bible instruction are the means of addressing an admitted subject. Here the City's broad private movie and class program includes nondevotional treatments of the Bible and religion. ##### II. This private access does not establish religion

**Holding and operative rule:** Allowing the requested privately sponsored presentation on the actual voluntary, nonresidential adult-program terms does not violate the Establishment Clause. Public ownership and devotional content do not alone convert the speaker's expression into governmental worship; actual official selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured sponsorship could produce a different case.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices for this concrete conclusion. The complete contextual rationale in Part III has the separate six-Justice authority stated there.

Equal access therefore admits private speech rather than creating a municipal religious program. Lamb's Chapel and Good News preserve that distinction in actual private-use arrangements. Lee v. Weisman and Ingebretsen concern official organization and pressure, and Robinson concerns the government's own institutional religious identification; voluntary attendance alone would not cure such official action. The combined private sponsorship, adult setting and absence of governmental adoption support this result. ##### III. Context satisfies the retained purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Lemon, equal private access here serves the secular purpose of the City's broad activity forum, does not primarily advance religion as governmental expression, and creates no excessive entanglement. Actual private attribution and the program's operation, considered together, establish that conclusion without a universal disclaimer, monitoring duty or devotional-financing entitlement.

**Authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six direct joins control; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this complete methodology. No Marks combination is used.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon retains its purpose, primary-effect and entanglement inquiries. Good News and Chabad require real examination of the private-use arrangements, and Pinette supplies no comprehensive majority exemption for every public forum. ### I. Immediate review reaches this integrated immunity and trial-protection order

**Holding and operative rule.** An appeal from the conclusive rejection of the President's asserted immunity from present suit permits review of this same order's categorical trial protection, including its alternative docket-control basis, because those rulings determine the scope and remedy of the same asserted temporal immunity. It does not permit review of independent liability claims or defenses, and creates no automatic appeal from a later scheduling order.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the asserted protection cannot be restored by review after the litigation from which immunity is claimed. Fitzgerald supplies the immediate-review rule for that immunity question. The District Court denied dismissal, allowed discovery and withheld trial for the Presidency. Those rulings define one asserted temporal protection; deciding its categorical trial limit is necessary to meaningful review of the order. Swint forbids adding an independent nonappealable liability question merely because another party properly appeals immunity. It reserved genuinely necessary or inextricable connected review, and this integrated order presents that narrower case. An alternative reference to docket control does not place the same relief beyond review. Common facts, efficiency and presidential importance would not suffice. Removing Ferguson's exclusively derivative trial protection does not adjudicate his independent defenses. The undeveloped official-defamation defense, a general pendent-party doctrine and future process orders remain outside this ruling. The challenge to discovery protection pending the completed lower appeal remains moot.

**Precedent treatment.** Fitzgerald is applied for immediate review of an asserted immunity from present suit. Swint's limit on unrelated liability review remains intact; its connected-review reservation is resolved only for this legally integrated protection order.

### II. No temporary immunity for the presented pre-presidential conduct

**Holding and operative rule.** A sitting President has no constitutional temporary immunity from a properly brought federal civil action seeking damages for alleged unofficial conduct before taking office. The Presidency alone neither requires dismissal without prejudice nor suspends that action until the term ends; concrete demands upon official duties require their own accommodation analysis.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the constitutional protection of the Presidency concerns the exercise of its assigned powers. Fitzgerald protects official presidential decisionmaking from distortion by personal damages liability. The 1991 conduct alleged here preceded Clinton's Presidency and was no exercise of presidential responsibility. Adjudicating that conduct does not give the Judiciary authority to perform an executive function. Forrester requires attention to the function involved rather than the actor's title and recognizes the President's distinct official-function protection; Antoine likewise insists on a justified connection between immunity and the protected function, without deciding presidential immunity itself.

The President's present responsibilities remain substantial. Time and energy consumed by litigation may affect those responsibilities, and the Court does not predict that every demand will be harmless. But that concern does not justify immunity from this whole action merely because the defendant now holds office. The response must address the actual demand and the constitutional duty it threatens. An assessment of political motives behind the complaint or the abstract importance of the plaintiff's claim supplies no constitutional immunity rule.

United States v. Nixon rejects an absolute premise that Article III process cannot reach a President. Its criminal-evidence setting does not decide the balance for every civil demand, which that decision expressly left open. The actual Nixon v. United States decision concerns Senate impeachment procedure assigned by the Constitution; it does not exempt private presidential conduct from adjudication. Nor do Plaut's protection of completed private judgments or Reich's confined officer review establish a general exemption from interbranch effects. The holding is confined to this federal civil action concerning alleged unofficial pre-office conduct. Liability, state-court jurisdiction, criminal proceedings and compulsory attendance at a particular time or place remain undecided.

**Precedent treatment.** Fitzgerald's official-responsibility immunity is preserved and distinguished from temporary status-based immunity. Forrester supplies the function/identity distinction; Antoine supplies a limited functional analogy. United States v. Nixon supplies the possibility of presidential exposure to judicial process while preserving its civil-evidence reservation. Nixon v. United States, Plaut and Reich retain their confined structural and remedial boundaries; none creates a general civil exemption.

### III. Official-act immunity turns on function, not presidential status

**Holding and operative rule.** Fitzgerald's absolute personal civil-damages immunity continues to protect conduct within the outer perimeter of presidential official responsibility; title alone cannot convert pre-presidential conduct into an official presidential act, and alleged illegality or improper motive alone does not strip an otherwise official act of that protection. The possible official-function defense concerning post-inauguration press-secretary statements remains for initial District Court consideration on a developed record.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that identifying the function and deciding its legality are different inquiries. Forrester supplies the functional distinction: immunity follows the responsibility performed, rather than the identity of the official. Fitzgerald deliberately protects the outer perimeter of presidential responsibility, so an allegation that an official act was wrongful cannot itself defeat the protection. Conversely, present office cannot supply a presidential function for conduct occurring before the office was assumed. The allegations' references to Clinton's governorship and state personnel do not transform Arkansas conduct into presidential conduct; whether state action or another defense is established remains separate.

The complaint also concerns statements attributed to the presidential press secretary after inauguration. The circumstances of those statements, Clinton's legal responsibility and the elements of defamation have not been sufficiently developed or decided below. The Court neither classifies those statements nor declares every allegation in the complaint unofficial. Rejection of the broad temporary-immunity claim leaves the actual official-act defense available for its own adjudication. The Court announces no universal classification sequence, reasonable-officeholder test, motive exception or exception for allegations of grave wrongdoing. Fitzgerald is not overruled or replaced by qualified immunity. The merits of the federal and state claims, state-office defenses and any particular relief for a proven unlawful official act remain open.

**Precedent treatment.** Fitzgerald retains its outer-perimeter rule and its refusal to make alleged improper purpose alone decisive. Forrester is applied to distinguish function from identity without diluting the President's recognized official-function immunity.

### IV. This categorical trial stay is an abuse of discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court abused its discretion by ordering in advance that trial await the end of the Presidency without a developed showing connecting that duration to particular presidential duties and without sufficiently accounting for the plaintiff's timely-adjudication and evidence interests. Ordinary stay authority survives the rejection of constitutional immunity, but generalized burdens and the duration of office do not justify this order.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that immunity and docket management are distinct. Discovery continued, so the trial stay was not literally the same protection as exemption from the action. The Court therefore affirms the Eighth Circuit's judgment without adopting the proposition that every discretionary trial stay is equivalent to immunity. Landis recognizes ordinary authority to control a docket, but requires a clear showing of hardship or inequity when even a fair possibility exists that a stay will harm the other party. Extraordinary public interests can justify a moderate, nonoppressive delay; relief must be bounded when entered. The presidential term's endpoint supplies a calendar limit, not a justification for that whole period. No particular demand or official conflict establishes the present duration, while delay may impair access to witnesses and evidence. Allowing discovery does not establish that all trial-delay prejudice disappears. No actual loss of evidence or disputed survival-law conclusion is necessary. A later, supported request remains possible.

**Linked Ferguson remedy.** Ferguson's trial protection was expressly derivative of the presidential protection just rejected. That stay loses its stated basis and is removed; no independent Ferguson immunity or personal defense is adjudicated. Discovery against both defendants remains subject to lawful management and privilege.

**Precedent treatment.** Landis is applied for the triggered applicant's burden, consideration of competing interests and bounded, nonoppressive duration. It creates no special presumption of presidential postponement and is not displaced by the denial of immunity.

### V. Concrete presidential duties receive case-specific accommodation

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court must give substantial respect to the President's constitutional duties throughout this action; a party seeking protection must identify the particular litigation demand and the conflict or hardship, and any relief must respond to its supported need, duration and alternatives while accounting for the plaintiff's legitimate interests. The court may adjust timing, sequence or manner and may grant a justified, bounded stay, but its discretion cannot authorize significant constitutionally forbidden impairment of another branch's assigned functions.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the proper inquiry concerns concrete duties and litigation demands, not a general privilege of office. Articles II and III preserve each branch's assigned work. The Judiciary may adjudicate a private controversy without acquiring authority to obstruct the Executive's constitutional performance. At the same time, the President's title does not establish a particular conflict or shift to the plaintiff the burden to disprove it. The applicant must identify the need for protection. Landis places the clear-showing burden on the applicant when even a fair possibility of harm from a requested stay exists; that conditional burden is retained in its own terms.

Ordinary procedural tools permit practical accommodation. Timing, sequence, the manner of testimony, protective measures and limited delay can respond to an identified conflict where authorized. The court must examine how long protection is needed, whether alternatives address the problem, and what delay would do to timely adjudication and preservation of evidence. Relief cannot extend beyond its justified scope and remains subject to reconsideration as circumstances change. No party has an absolute entitlement to a particular date, place or method of testimony.

The Court rejects the proposed status-based presumption of postponement, including a shift requiring Jones first to prove exceptional or irreparable prejudice. It also rejects treating presidential constitutional work as merely an ordinary convenience. These conclusions do not resolve a concrete compulsion, privilege, sanction or irreconcilable scheduling dispute: none is before the Court. No universal hearing, special proof quantum, fixed discovery limit or new interlocutory appeal is established. The Court's judgment neither finds that this litigation can never seriously interfere with official duties nor decides the precise constitutional remedy if an actual future demand does so. Breyer's further restriction on second-guessing a reasonable presidential scheduling explanation is stated in his separate opinion, not adopted as part of this holding.

**Precedent treatment.** Landis continues to govern a supported stay request at its stated harm trigger. Fitzgerald's institutional protection explains the significance of presidential duties without supplying immunity for private conduct. United States v. Nixon does not settle every civil-process conflict; Reich's presidential-coercion reservation remains intact.

### VI. The standard governing proceedings after this decision

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court must proceed with the federal action under ordinary law, separately determine any properly presented official-act or other defense, and consider a specifically supported request for accommodation under Part V; neither the Presidency nor rejection of temporary immunity predetermines a later scheduling dispute. Parts I–V supply controlling rules with the coalitions stated above, while the two concurrences add no controlling classification test, constitutional exception, presumption or standard for compelled attendance.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the mandate requires continued adjudication, not liability or immediate trial on a specified date. The existing categorical stays fall for the reasons given in Part IV, including the derivative Ferguson consequence. The court retains its ordinary procedural authority within the constitutional and Landis limits already explained. The official-defamation issue must receive initial district-court consideration on a developed record. No present judicial finding establishes the truth of the allegations, satisfaction of a federal claim, privilege, an actual conflict with presidential work or the adequacy of any future accommodation. The detailed guidance proposed by Stone-Zsela and Breyer's further scheduling restriction are separate positions; they cannot enlarge the mandate or become a universal test through their proximity to majority reasoning.

**Precedent treatment.** The mandate applies the preserved Fitzgerald and Landis rules and the confined Swint connected-review application without altering independent defenses or existing requirements for later appellate review.

## Separate Writings

### Stone-Zsela, concurring; no joins

Stone-Zsela joins the Court and its entire judgment. His concurrence proposes a more explicit objective method for distinguishing presidential functions from private conduct. A court should first identify the conduct specifically and the presidential authority claimed; then determine whether objectively established circumstances connect the conduct to performing, directing or supervising a responsibility entrusted by the Constitution or federal law, including Fitzgerald's outer perimeter; then check whether a reasonably informed officeholder would understand it as presidential responsibility rather than a private act. Legal classification remains the judge's task. An ordinary observer's assessment can expose a self-serving label but cannot create or eliminate constitutional power, and subjective good faith is not dispositive. Forrester supports the functional starting point, not this particular proposed check.

Title, location, staff, security, public resources or an official label alone does not establish presidential function. Nor does alleged illegality, retaliation or personal motivation automatically remove an otherwise official act from Fitzgerald's protection. Directing military operations and supervising a department ordinarily perform presidential functions even when their legality is disputed. A private encounter does not become presidential government merely because an official event or staff is involved. The present pre-presidential allegations require no decision of a contested presidential-act classification; the proposed method is guidance in his separate opinion.

He agrees that the actual categorical stay fails and that Landis preserves tailored relief on demonstrated need. He separates official-action review from personal damages immunity: courts may assess executive legality even when Fitzgerald forecloses a particular damages remedy. He would be prepared in a properly presented case to reconsider or limit Fitzgerald concerning proved treason or grave, deliberate constitutional wrongdoing. He adopts no such exception here, and an allegation of unlawfulness alone supplies none.

His broader view is that purely private conduct does not become a presidential function because it occurs during the term, including conduct that may violate criminal law. Criminal liability nonetheless requires an applicable offense, jurisdiction, proof and resolution of any immunity or supremacy issue. He reserves prosecution timing, federal or state criminal jurisdiction, criminally charged official acts, comparable state-court civil proceedings, the post-inauguration defamation defense and all merits questions. His concurrence changes no controlling law on those subjects.

### Breyer, concurring in the judgment; no joins

Breyer agrees that no automatic immunity protects the presented private conduct and that the existing stay is premature because the President has not supplied a specific reasoned explanation of need. He agrees with preservation of Fitzgerald's official-function immunity and the undeveloped press-secretary defense. His disagreement concerns the constitutional protection of presidential time, not the judgment or a finding that this trial already interferes with official duties.

Article II places the entire executive responsibility in one nationally elected individual. The President can delegate tasks but cannot delegate ultimate responsibility; Congress and courts can act through other members or substitutes. Significant interference with presidential performance therefore affects the Executive as an institution. Fitzgerald protects against diversion of time and energy as well as distortion of official decisions by feared liability. Harlow, Imbler, Tenney and Mitchell likewise recognize litigation burdens as relevant to effective public service. These authorities do not establish present immunity for private acts, but they support constitutional protection when a particular judicial demand threatens official performance.

The historical writings of Story, Adams, Ellsworth and Jefferson support functional protection of ongoing duties without establishing their broadest immunity assertions. Voluntary presidential testimony proves no unlimited power to compel civil-litigation demands. United States v. Nixon involved criminal evidence and individual liberty and expressly left civil-evidence balancing open. Youngstown's review helps determine executive authority itself; its burdens do not automatically settle the distraction caused by a private damages action. Breyer will not rest constitutional protection on predictions that such suits will be few, sanctions will eliminate every burden or ordinary discretion necessarily will suffice.

Once the President gives a reasoned explanation identifying a conflict between a particular judicial demand and public duties, Article II constrains scheduling. In the ordinary civil case where postponement would cause no overwhelming or enormous harm to the plaintiff, the court must avoid significant interference with those duties. Judicial power to second-guess a reasonable presidential explanation of scheduling needs is very limited. This is a constitutional requirement, not merely optional courtesy. Bare reliance on office remains insufficient, and the President retains the initial obligation to explain the need. Breyer does not decide the result if delay would cause the extraordinary plaintiff harm he identifies, or prescribe a fixed hearing, new proof standard, automatic stay or new appeal. The precise contours, possible future presumptions and consequences of an actual irreconcilable conflict remain open. His further formulation is noncontrolling.

## Holdings

##### The final land-use decision does not depend on selling transferable rights

Here the permit denial and completed governing-board appeal settle that institutional question: Suitum may not develop this parcel. A sale of rights usable elsewhere cannot revise the answer. ## Event

United States v. Hyde, No. 96-667. Merits decision, May 27, 1997. Argued April 15, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 92 F.3d 779. ## Holdings

##### An accepted plea requires a fair and just reason for presentence withdrawal

**Holding and operative rule:** When a district court has accepted a guilty plea following the required Rule 11 inquiry but has deferred acceptance of the associated plea agreement, former Rule 32(e) requires a fair and just reason to withdraw the plea before sentencing. Acceptance of the plea and acceptance of the agreement are distinct acts; deferral of the latter does not create an unconditional right to undo the former.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices directly. The protection and remedial qualifications below form part of that unanimous rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The plea is the defendant's admission and relinquishment of trial rights; the agreement proposes the disposition and undertakings associated with it. Former Rule 11 permits deferred consideration of this agreement and expressly identifies what follows if it is rejected. Former Rule 32(e) governs withdrawal of the already accepted plea. Treating deferral as nonacceptance of the plea would erase that separation. The strongest contrary argument is that the plea depends on the bargain while the court remains free to reject it. The rules answer that concern with a withdrawal right upon qualifying rejection, as well as the continuing fair-and-just route; pending approval is not rejection. Kercheval supplies the background of the fair-and-just standard but does not exclude accepted pleas subject to later rejection protection. Santobello and Mabry preserve the significance of actual promises and valid assent; neither makes the two acceptance acts legally inseparable. Advisory comments concerning presentence-report disclosure cannot displace the enacted withdrawal rules. This result applies the rule itself, rather than inferring a contractual waiver of its protection.

**Retained protections in their terms:** This is a former Rule 11(e)(1)(A) agreement under which the Government moves to dismiss charges. For an A agreement or an (e)(1)(C) agreement specifying a sentence, rejection triggers Rule 11(e)(4): the court must disclose the rejection, personally advise that it is not bound, afford the defendant an opportunity to withdraw without a fair-and-just-reason showing, and warn that persisting may produce a less favorable disposition. A type B sentencing recommendation is different: the defendant must be warned that failure to follow the recommendation does not itself give a withdrawal right. Deferral is not rejection under either category. Actual acceptance, the agreement's lawful terms, knowing and voluntary assent, and supported coercion or other fair-and-just claims remain independently relevant. Former Rule 11(f)'s factual-basis prerequisite attaches to entry of judgment on the accepted plea; this record's earlier inquiry is not a universal new acceptance sequence.

**Limits and remedy:** The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's legal rule and remands. It does not redetermine duress, voluntariness or factual basis, independently affirm every finding, require a renewed hearing, fix a sentence or order a new trial. The Government's obligation before court acceptance of the agreement is not decided; rejecting the asymmetry argument does not authorize prosecutorial retraction. Any properly preserved remaining challenge is considered under the correct rules.

## Precedent Treatment

- Kercheval v. United States: preserves the fair-and-just withdrawal standard's background; a pending agreement does not take an accepted plea outside the enacted rule.
- Santobello v. New York: preserves the importance of the actual inducing promise and lawful plea remedies; does not merge plea acceptance with agreement acceptance.
- Mabry v. Johnson: preserves actual plea assent and the legal significance of the bargain without creating free withdrawal during deferral.
- Godinez v. Moran: retains the distinction between competence and an actual knowing, intelligent and voluntary relinquishment; capacity alone does not validate a plea.
- United States v. Mezzanatto: preserves its limited consent holding and individual voluntariness review; this decision applies a withdrawal rule rather than inferring an advance waiver of it.
- Libretti v. United States: preserves distinctions among guilty plea, sentencing matters, informed consent and statutory limits; no agreement enlarges lawful punishment or removes the protections of former Rules 11 and 32.

Presentence withdrawal remains available for a fair and just reason, and actual rejection of a qualifying agreement triggers former Rule 11(e)(4) separately. No presumption of voluntariness or rule of absolute irrevocability is added.

## Holdings

##### I. An applicable enhanced maximum supplies the statutory benchmark

**Holding and operative rule:** For an offender covered by §994(h) and subject to a lawfully invoked statutory recidivist enhancement, the maximum term authorized is the enhanced statutory maximum. Amendment 506 is invalid insofar as its career-offender benchmark excludes that applicable increase because it rests on the defendant's prior criminal record; the Commission may not replace the legally operative maximum with the unenhanced maximum.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. These six directly adopt every controlling proposition and remedy stated here; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent on the competing statutory construction. No Marks synthesis combines those incompatible readings.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 994(h) directs the Commission to assure guidelines specifying imprisonment at or near the maximum authorized for its defined categories. The statutory maximum is the highest term the governing penalty law authorizes for the actual offender, including an enhancement that legally applies. The categories language identifies covered offenders; it does not authorize erasing a penalty distinction Congress created. Section 841(b)(1)(C)'s ordinary twenty-year ceiling and applicable thirty-year recidivist ceiling illustrate the distinction. Section 851 notice matters: without the required information filed before trial or acceptance of the plea, that enhanced ceiling is unavailable. These respondents received the required notices. The Commission's uniformity and charging-disparity concerns do not make noticed and unnotified offenders legally identical. Stinson gives commentary authority within statutory limits, not power to contradict this command. Neal preserves distinct statutory and guideline measures where lawful; it does not authorize replacement of the statutory benchmark Congress expressly selected. MCI likewise requires attention to the actual delegation and duty, rather than treating consequential policy change as automatically forbidden. Here the text resolves the benchmark, so the Court need not decide the Commission's general entitlement to Chevron deference.

**Coverage and limits:** Section 994(h) concerns a defendant at least eighteen years old with a current qualifying felony and two or more prior qualifying felonies. The current and each prior felony must be a crime of violence or an offense described in 21 U.S.C. §841, §§952(a), 955 or 959, or the Maritime Drug Law Enforcement Act. Eligibility is not disputed here. Potential enhancement eligibility alone does not make an uninvoked maximum applicable. The Court requires no prosecutor to seek an enhancement, approves no improper charging basis, and decides no unpresented constitutional challenge or defect in notice or predicates. The holding concerns Amendment 506's prior-record exclusion, not every conceivable statutory or guideline enhancement.

##### II. The maximum benchmark and proximity remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Identifying the enhanced maximum does not require imposing that maximum in every covered case. Section 994(h)'s at-or-near language leaves lawful implementation, adjustments and departures within the governing statutes and guidelines; the Court sets no numerical proximity threshold and rejects Amendment 506 on the benchmark defect without a separate quantitative near holding.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Opinion of the Court directly adopts this qualification and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress separately specified the reference point and allowed latitude in the guideline sentence's relation to it. Flexibility in proximity does not authorize a different reference point. Conversely, invalidating the lower benchmark does not abolish otherwise lawful sentencing choices. A guideline maximum cannot test the meaning of the statutory instruction that governs writing that guideline. Arranging separate terms under §3584 is a different question from calculating the maximum for this offense; no rule aggregates all possible punishment into one benchmark. The Court neither fixes a percentage nor adjudicates an unpresented multiple-count or probation issue.

##### III. Invalid commentary cannot authorize these sentence reductions

**Holding and operative rule:** Amendment 506's retroactive designation cannot supply lawful authority under §3582(c)(2) for a reduction based on its invalid benchmark. The reviewed sentencing components must be reversed and remanded for proceedings consistent with that invalidity, preserving the separate statutory gates, discretionary choices, unreviewed claims and respondent-specific posture.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Opinion of the Court directly joins the rule and all three remedial components.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 3582(c) generally forbids modification of an imposed prison term except under an applicable statutory exception. Under §3582(c)(2), the term must have been based on a range subsequently lowered by the Commission pursuant to §994(o); the defendant, the Bureau of Prisons Director or the court itself may initiate consideration; the court must consider applicable §3553(a) factors; a reduction must accord with applicable Commission policy statements; and the ultimate choice to reduce remains discretionary. Retroactive designation neither validates an unlawful amendment nor guarantees relief. LaBonte's existing reduction rests on the invalid legal premise, even though the Government did not develop a separate challenge to the discretionary choice of 151 months. Hunnewell and Dyer had possible-reduction remands, not awarded reductions; those remands cannot continue on the invalid premise. Dyer's independently affirmed non-sentencing §2255 dismissal and Piper's separate appeal remain outside this mandate. The Court fixes no prison term and does not reopen every final career-offender judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- Stinson v. United States: applies the statutory-consistency limit on authoritative guideline commentary; lawful interpretation remains authoritative within that limit.
- Neal v. United States: preserves distinct lawful statutory and guideline calculations; the LSD holding does not itself decide this career-offender benchmark.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T: preserves contextual examination of the actual delegation and operative duty; no universal prohibition on consequential agency adjustments is adopted.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC: the Court resolves the statutory text without deciding the Commission's general entitlement to deference; no broader replacement of Chevron follows.
- Auer v. Robbins: preserves bounded interpretive authority and does not independently settle §994(h).
- United States v. Gonzales: preserves separately authorized sentencing choices at its own statutory scope; it does not decide this maximum benchmark or force every sentence to the maximum.

## Law After Decision

The career-offender guideline must use the enhanced statutory maximum when the recidivist increase lawfully applies. Amendment 506 cannot exclude that operative increase, and its retroactive designation supplies no reduction authority for these requests. Lawful adjustments, departures and independent sentencing-modification requirements survive. No general numerical definition of near or universal Commission-deference rule is announced.

## Separate Writings

Breyer, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, dissents from all three reviewed sentencing dispositions. The dissent reads maximum term authorized in the context of a command to the Commission to construct categories, distinguishing the offense-based benchmark from offender-specific recidivist increases. It finds genuine interpretive room in that scheme and considers Amendment 506 a permissible implementation serving uniformity, proportionality and administrability, not a policy power to disregard text already found unambiguous. Enhanced statutory ceilings and floors still bind; an enhanced ceiling can authorize otherwise lawful sentences above the ordinary ceiling, including appropriate departures or other guideline calculations. Thus the amendment need not render Congress's recidivist provisions meaningless.

The dissent separates the benchmark from proximity. Adjusted final sentences alone do not establish that the career-offender scheme fails the at-or-near direction, but it prescribes no universal percentage or permission for every remote sentence. It would affirm LaBonte's existing 151-month reduction at the reviewed scope and the possible-reduction remands for Hunnewell and Dyer, preserving all §3582(c)(2) conditions and discretion. It would neither guarantee another reduction, reopen Dyer's independent collateral claims nor add Piper to the mandate. This statutory construction is noncontrolling.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed First Circuit sentencing components are reversed and the case remanded for lawful proceedings without Amendment 506's invalid prior-record exclusion. No sentence is automatically restored or fixed. Dyer's independently affirmed non-sentencing §2255 disposition remains undisturbed, and Piper is outside this mandate.
