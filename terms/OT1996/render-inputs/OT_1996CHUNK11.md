## Event

Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey; Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-16248 and 94-16313, 95 F.3d 1422; district court No. CV-86-05042-CAL. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Certiorari granted January 13, 1997. Submitted and decided June 16, 1997.

Following repeated proposals to develop 37.6 oceanfront acres and the City's final denial, the owners obtained separate takings and equal-protection liability verdicts supporting one $1,450,000 award. The Ninth Circuit's September 13, 1996 judgment sustained the taking claim and the rejection of post-trial relief; it did not reach the City's preserved equal-protection objections. Further appellate and rehearing proceedings were held in abeyance pending this review by a separate appellate order. The grant itself did not remove appellate jurisdiction. The adverse substantive-due-process ruling was not appealed. The owners waived their additional-damages cross-appeal only if the judgment was affirmed.

The questions are whether this §1983 damages action carries a Seventh Amendment jury right on the disputed taking facts; whether the repeated denials can constitute a taking through complete loss of economically viable use or the then-governing substantial-advancement formulation; and whether damages are available through §1983 rather than only prospective or administrative relief. No separate equal-protection merits question is presented.

## Participation

At submission and decision, all nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice is disqualified or otherwise absent. The Court has a quorum.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment on the three questions presented and preserves the existing compensatory award.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The $1,450,000 award and denial of the City's new-trial motion stand within the questions reviewed. Affirmance activates the owners' conditional waiver of additional damages. No new award, permit, equal-protection merits judgment or reinstatement of the unappealed substantive-due-process claim is ordered. The opposing Justices would vacate and remand for independent judicial taking findings, with lawful use of the existing record and further consideration of preserved equal-protection objections if necessary to sustain the common award.

## Holdings

### I. The final decision and the scope of review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definitive municipal decision fixing the permitted use of the parcel satisfies the final-decision requirement; later valuation does not make that decision provisional. This claim may proceed on the established absence of an adequate state compensation route for the challenged period, without eliminating any compensation prerequisite in other cases.

**Authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, I–III, is joined by all nine Justices; that common coalition controls Holdings I–III.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson County requires a final application of land-use rules and an adequate-compensation inquiry. Suitum separates a definitive use decision from valuation and ultimate liability. The City's fixed position and the earlier finality proceedings establish the former here; the case proceeded on the stated compensation-route predicate. Neither finality nor the futility of another proposal proves a taking. The grant presents the taking, jury and damages questions, while the City's preserved equal-protection objections and the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling retain their distinct status.

### II. A permit denial is not subject to Dolan's dedication burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The government must establish nexus and individualized rough proportionality when it demands conveyance of identified property interests as a permit condition. That special burden does not govern this ordinary permit-denial claim; the Ninth Circuit's contrary proportionality discussion supplies no independent ground to disturb the judgment because it was not part of the jury instructions and was unnecessary to verdict review.

**Controlling explanation:** Dolan protects the owner's interest against compelled dedication; it does not impose a government justification burden upon every unsuccessful development proposal. Prior dedication discussions do not change the action actually challenged. The jury instead received the Agins-based reasonable-relationship instruction and the complete-economic-deprivation alternative. Lucas requires proof of complete loss of economically beneficial use, after which the government must substantiate a background property or nuisance limitation that independently excluded the use; a newly declared environmental harm is insufficient by itself. Penn Central remains applicable to partial deprivation. Legitimate environmental aims, repeated rejection and a later sale price are relevant without conclusively proving or defeating either submitted taking theory. The Court does not replace Agins's received formulation with a new general rule.

### III. Compensation remains available through the established municipal action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 permits compensatory damages for a proved taking attributable to a municipality's own qualifying policy or final decision, subject to the action's constitutional and procedural predicates. Recovery is limited to the legally attributable extent and duration of the deprivation, accounting for sale proceeds or other recovery to prevent duplication; prospective relief alone does not extinguish a completed compensable injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell requires the municipality's own policy or final decision and causation, rather than liability solely for employing an official. McMillian preserves the function-specific inquiry into the institution represented by a final policymaker; it supplies no new finding about an unidentified Monterey official. First English permits compensation for the actual period of a temporary taking. Franklin distinguishes available relief in an established action from creation of a new action or proof of loss. Those principles defeat a categorical limit to prospective or administrative relief without guaranteeing anticipated profits, treating ordinary permit processing as compensable, or dispensing with proof. The questions reviewed establish no basis for a second damages amount or a construction order.

### IV. The statutory wording alone does not resolve jury entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983's provision for legal, equitable and other proper redress does not by itself confer a statutory jury right. Any jury entitlement in the present action must therefore be established under the Seventh Amendment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this statutory ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lorillard's inference from another statute depended on incorporated enforcement procedures as well as the availability of legal relief. Section 1983's remedial wording alone does not provide that answer. This conclusion leaves the damages action available and requires the separate constitutional inquiry; it neither decides that inquiry against the owner nor converts legal compensation into equitable relief.

### V. This legal §1983 action carries the constitutional jury right

**Holding and operative rule:** A §1983 action against a municipality seeking compensatory legal damages for violation of federally protected rights sounds in tort and falls within the legal actions protected by the Seventh Amendment. Its legal character does not disappear because the particular federal right concerns property or because an inverse-condemnation remedy could otherwise exist; equitable-only claims, sovereign immunity and allocation of subsidiary tasks remain separate questions.

**Authority:** Parts V–VII of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supply five direct joins for Holdings V–VII. No vote from a separate rationale is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Imbler, Carey and Memphis Community School District identify the tort character and compensatory purpose of §1983 relief. Curtis protects the jury in statutory tort actions seeking legal relief; Tull and Terry direct attention to the actual action and remedy. The present action seeks compensation for the municipality's alleged completed federal violation and proceeds on the absence of an adequate state compensation route. Calling the underlying injury a taking does not remove the statutory action's legal character. The contrary comparison to ordinary condemnation does not overcome that character here. The holding does not settle every inverse-condemnation proceeding, authorize damages against an immune sovereign, dispense with an underlying violation, or assign every subsidiary legal issue to a jury.

### VI. The disputed tasks submitted here belong to the jury

**Holding and operative rule:** Under legal standards stated by the judge, the jury may decide whether this parcel retained economically viable use and whether this particular final denial, in its development history, bore the required relationship to the City's specified legitimate purposes. The judge determines governing law, the relevant property interest, legal legitimacy of the purposes and evidentiary sufficiency; the jury may not choose a constitutional standard or revise general land-use policy.

**Controlling explanation:** Markman separately examines the action's jury status and allocation of its tasks. Its assignment of patent meaning, including embedded technical and credibility issues, to the judge remains intact. This permit denial's practical effects and highly particularized application of the stated purposes present different work. The actual instructions use the received Agins reasonable-relationship formulation, not Dolan's proportionality burden. The absence of objection to that wording does not waive the City's allocation objection, which the Court rejects on its merits. The decision does not comprehensively decide the full validity or elements of every substantial-advancement formulation, general zoning challenges, all as-applied regulations or all condemnation proceedings.

### VII. The supported taking verdict sustains the single award

**Holding and operative rule:** The City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law or a new trial on the reviewed grounds because sufficient evidence supports both undifferentiated taking alternatives under the instructions actually given. The taking verdict therefore sustains the existing single $1,450,000 compensatory award without decision of the separate equal-protection theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The owners offered evidence that the combined beach/access, viewshed and habitat requirements left no feasible development area and that further proposals could not satisfy the City's fixed position. Their evidence also addressed compliance with the fifteen conditions, prior grading/restoration approval, favorable habitat evidence and the City's role in the access difficulty. Crediting that evidence and reasonable favorable inferences supports the complete-deprivation and reasonable-relationship findings without reweighing credibility. No substantiated antecedent title limitation independently requires the entire deprivation found. The sale to California for $800,000 above the purchase price is relevant to value and compensation, but does not conclusively establish a productive use during the earlier period or payment for its loss. Neither denial of the preferred housing project alone nor disagreement among environmental experts would suffice. Support for both taking alternatives avoids any need to decide the preserved equal-protection objections; the award is not sustained by assuming those objections were abandoned. Affirmance gives effect to the owners' conditional additional-damages waiver and leaves the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling untouched.

## Law After Decision

Municipal §1983 compensation claims of the kind presented carry a constitutional jury right, with law and legally legitimate purposes supplied by the judge and these concrete factual applications entrusted to the jury. The existing verdict and award stand under the actual instructions. The statutory language alone establishes no jury right. Ordinary exactions, complete deprivation, partial deprivation, finality, municipal attribution and compensation remain distinct. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

## Event

*Fletcher v. Kalina*; October Term 1996, chunk 11, inventory No. 119. Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129, 93 F.3d 653, decided below August 22, 1996; no Supreme Court docket number is assigned. Plenary merits review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Submitted and decided June 16, 1997; no separate oral-argument date is established.

Fletcher's personal-capacity §1983 action challenges Kalina's sworn factual certification supporting an arrest warrant. The district court denied absolute immunity and left qualified immunity unresolved on disputed facts; the Ninth Circuit upheld the absolute-immunity denial. Kalina seeks reversal of that ruling. The questions concern whether the personal oath is protected advocacy or the act of a complaining witness, whether preparation and presentation must be separated from factual attestation, and whether allegedly material false statements or omissions contributing to an unconstitutional arrest permit personal liability. The fourth question is reached only to explain the unresolved elements and defenses at this interlocutory stage.

## Participation

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. No Justice is recused or otherwise excluded.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the denial of absolute immunity for Kalina's personal factual certification and remands for the unresolved proceedings, while preserving absolute immunity for protected advocacy.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** The Ninth Circuit's denial of absolute immunity is affirmed insofar as it concerns Kalina's personally executed factual certification, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the functional distinction. **Vote: 9–0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** No Justice.

The affirmance does not authorize liability for protected preparation, filing or legal presentation. The constitutional violation, required culpability, materiality, causation, damages, qualified immunity and other personal defenses remain unresolved. No damages judgment is entered or reversed, and no mandatory trial is ordered.

## Holdings

### I. Protected advocacy and a distinct evidentiary act

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor has absolute immunity under §1983 for preparing and filing an information or warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case when performing an advocate's function. An allegation of bad purpose or false evidence does not remove that protection from the advocacy function, but the protection does not extend to a distinct personal evidentiary certification merely because the same prosecutor performs both acts.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the protection of legitimate charging and advocacy and the need to distinguish the personal evidentiary act, but not Part I's refusal to recognize an additional exception for knowing fabrication or deliberately false material assertions causing a constitutional deprivation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the act performed determines the immunity. Imbler protects initiating a prosecution and presenting the State's case; Burns preserves the advocate's presentation to a judge while requiring a separate inquiry into other functions. Buckley preserves genuine evaluation and preparation for advocacy while withholding absolute protection from ordinary investigative evidence gathering. Antoine requires historical and functional justification rather than reliance on an act's importance to judicial proceedings. Those decisions require protection for Kalina's legal preparation and presentation without treating every act in the charging transaction as advocacy. A prosecutor may present another person's evidence without personally guaranteeing its truth. Separating those functions therefore does not expose the protected legal work merely by relabeling a claim. The line also does not turn on whether advocacy occurred before or after probable cause, or on whether an allegation of dishonesty ultimately succeeds. The Court does not adopt a general dishonesty exception to absolute immunity for otherwise protected advocacy.

### II. Personal factual certification supporting an arrest warrant

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions offered to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness and has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for that certification. This classification depends on the personal evidentiary undertaking, not on proof of falsity, and does not withdraw immunity from ordinary courtroom witness testimony.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Kalina's personal oath supplies evidence rather than the advocate's legal presentation of another person's evidence. Malley denies absolute immunity to the officer who supplies a warrant application and provides the relevant comparison; prosecutorial title does not change the function of furnishing the factual assurance needed to procure process. Imbler and Burns protect advocacy, but their protection does not absorb a separately assumed witness function. Buckley and Antoine require that distinction even when the acts contribute to one prosecution. Kalina's claimed protection therefore fails as to her personally executed certification. The question is not whether someone else actually would have supplied a truthful certification, nor whether Kalina knowingly lied. Those are not premises necessary to classify the act. Nor does this holding make every sworn statement actionable: it concerns the factual certification used to obtain this arrest warrant, preserves ordinary testimony immunity, and leaves constitutional injury and the remaining defenses for further proceedings.

### III. The immunity ruling permits further proceedings, not a liability judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath leaves Fletcher to establish an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; Kalina retains qualified immunity and other available personal defenses. The existing qualified-immunity framework governs the remand, using the law and information relevant to the conduct at issue, without a new defense-burden rule or a finding that any element or defense has been established.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins the unresolved-liability and bounded-remand propositions and accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework as governing because his proposed reforms are not adopted; he adds no separate ground on the source-specific causation and notice explanations below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a function-specific denial of immunity is not a decision on damages liability. Hafer permits a personal-capacity §1983 claim while preserving personal immunities and the plaintiff's obligation to establish the officer's own constitutional violation. A judge's issuance of the warrant does not categorically break causation when material deception procured it; a truthful, corrected account may nevertheless establish probable cause. Neither culpability nor the content and effect of that corrected account is decided here. Harlow and Anderson preserve the existing objective inquiry into clearly established law in the particular context. Elder requires independent consideration of relevant precedent on the preserved immunity issue, and Lanier rejects a categorical demand for a factually identical prior case. Neither later decision supplies retrospective notice of unlawfulness. Disputed historical facts remain subject to the ordinary procedural rules, and the legal immunity determination belongs to the judge. The broader fourth question presented cannot turn this interlocutory immunity appeal into a determination of constitutional liability. The lower court must separate any claims resting only on protected advocacy from those resting on the personal oath; no damages award, mandatory trial or automatic finding against either party follows.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409:** Preserved for prosecutorial advocacy; distinguished from personally supplying the warrant-supporting factual oath. The Court adopts no general dishonesty exception to otherwise protected advocacy.
- **Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478:** Applied to protect legal presentation to a judge while requiring historical and functional justification for protection of a different act.
- **Malley v. Briggs, 475 U.S. 335:** Applied to the personal warrant-affiant function; a prosecutor does not gain absolute protection for the same evidentiary undertaking through official title. The warrant's issuance does not by itself resolve responsibility for material deception.
- **Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259:** Applied and extended to distinguish personal factual certification from genuine advocacy preparation; its investigation, press-activity and qualified-immunity boundaries remain intact. No categorical pre-charge/post-charge line is adopted.
- **Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., 508 U.S. 429:** Applied its historically justified functional inquiry; importance to adjudication does not itself establish absolute immunity.
- **Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21:** Applied the separation between personal-capacity liability, an actionable constitutional violation and personal defenses; official authority alone does not bar the suit.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800:** Preserved the existing objective clearly-established-law protection; no replacement qualified-immunity test is adopted.
- **Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635:** Preserved the particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness without requiring a prior case with identical facts.
- **Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510:** Applied independent consideration of all relevant precedent on the preserved immunity issue, with notice fixed to the conduct date and claim/evidence preservation limits intact.
- **United States v. Lanier, decided March 31, 1997:** Applied its rejection of categorical source and factual-twin restrictions on fair warning; it supplies no retrospective notice and does not decide Kalina's immunity.

## Law After Decision

From June 16, 1997, absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect a prosecutor's personal factual oath used to procure an arrest warrant. Protected preparation and legal presentation remain distinct and retain absolute immunity; the charging transaction cannot be classified as a single undifferentiated act. The holding adds that function-specific certification rule without changing ordinary testimony immunity, the elements of §1983 liability or the existing qualified-immunity framework. Stone-Zsela's proposed dishonesty exception, replacement test and defense-burden allocation are noncontrolling. No actual constitutional violation, falsity, materiality, causation, damages or ultimate personal defense is determined.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment; no other Justice joins.** He joins the distinction between legitimate charging or advocacy and personally swearing to factual assertions as a complaining witness. He also joins affirmance and the bounded remand: the constitutional violation, materiality, causation, damages and remaining personal defenses must be determined below, with no present finding of liability. He withholds the Court's refusal to recognize a narrow exception to absolute immunity for knowingly fabricated evidence or deliberately false material assertions causing a constitutional deprivation. Honest mistakes and disputed factual judgments would not satisfy that proposed exception.

He would also replace the existing qualified-immunity formulation with a Contextual Fair-Notice and Reasonable-Conduct Test. The proposed test protects genuinely reasonable mistakes assessed in light of the official's responsibilities, available information and governing law, excludes knowing constitutional violations and objectively indefensible conduct, and places the burden of establishing the defense on the official. These proposals receive no additional joins and are not holdings of the Court. Because they are not adopted, he accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework as governing the remand. His opinion neither finds deliberate falsehood nor imposes liability.

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by Justice Thomas.** Both join the Court's opinion in full. Scalia explains that the modern functional categories do not reproduce every feature of the common law: the privilege for testimony concerned defamation, while a complaining witness could face malicious-prosecution liability for procuring proceedings even without personally testifying. Following Imbler under stare decisis and Malley's treatment of the warrant affiant supports the present result without creating a general exception for all sworn statements. Their agreement concerns procuring arrest through the personal factual certification and does not make ordinary defamation a constitutional tort or dispense with constitutional injury, causation or the remaining defenses.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for the unresolved personal-capacity proceedings in the district court. The lower courts must distinguish protected advocacy from the personal oath and apply the existing liability and qualified-immunity rules to the properly supported record. The Supreme Court merits proceeding is concluded; further trial-level procedure depends on the remaining issues and ordinary procedural rules, not on an automatic trial direction.

## Event

Piscataway Township Board of Education v. Taxman; Third Circuit Nos. 94-5090 and 94-5112, 91 F.3d 1547 (en banc). On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Submitted and decided June 16, 1997.

A necessary reduction eliminated one business-department teaching position. Sharon Taxman and Debra Williams had equivalent qualifications and seniority. To retain Williams, the department's only Black teacher, the Board used race instead of its previously used lottery. The Board expressly relied on departmental diversity rather than a demonstrated remedial imbalance or its own past discrimination. The en banc judgment of August 8, 1996, amended August 21, sustained liability and relief under Title VII and the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination.

The questions are whether Title VII permits this diversity-based racial choice between equally qualified and senior employees; whether voluntary affirmative-action permission requires the remedial connection described in Weber and Johnson; whether the discharge unnecessarily burdens the employee's protected interests; and whether educational constitutional diversity principles supply statutory employment permission. No constitutional claim was before the lower court. Taxman had been rehired before the damages trial, so reinstatement is not at issue.

At submission and decision, all nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No nonparticipation is established; a quorum is present.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the reviewed judgment on two independently sufficient statutory grounds and decides no equal-protection question.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Both the absence of a permissible remedial premise and the unnecessary burden of this layoff independently sustain federal liability. The $134,014.62 federal award for backpay, benefits and prejudgment interest, the $10,000 state-law emotional-suffering award and restored seniority remain in place. There is no new reinstatement order. The denials of a broader prospective injunction and punitive damages are undisturbed. The opposing Justices would vacate federal liability and dependent relief and remand for application of a statutory rule allowing consideration of a genuinely supported educational-integration purpose. They would not decide that this layoff was lawful. The independent NJLAD ground would remain for identification of the relief it supports; neither automatic cancellation of all relief nor unconditional preservation of relief dependent solely on the vacated federal determination would follow.

## Holdings

### I. Statutory permission and constitutional permission remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Selecting which equally qualified and equally senior employee loses an existing position because of race requires permission under Title VII; the employment tie does not remove the discrimination question. Constitutional recognition of a diversity interest does not itself create an exception to that employment statute, and this statutory case decides no equal-protection question.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Court opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII protects individuals against covered employment decisions because of race. Weber and Johnson recognize limited voluntary affirmative action without requiring an adjudication that the employer itself violated the statute. That accommodation must be examined on its own terms. Hopwood now affirmatively holds, by six direct votes, that concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse public-university student body can supply a compelling interest on a supported record. It requires substantiation of the educational objective and narrow fit of the actual means; a diversity label, racial balance or assumed racial viewpoint is insufficient. Its unanimous invalidation of the separate admissions machinery illustrates that an available interest does not establish lawful operation.

Hopwood expressly reserved race-conscious employment and other educational levels. Its university holding neither amends Title VII nor establishes the educational benefit or statutory justification for this faculty layoff. Nor is that reservation a constitutional holding against faculty diversity. Hopwood preserved Bakke's actual five-Justice competitive-consideration proposition and adopted its own more specific affirmative rule; the Court does not reduce the current rule to unresolved Bakke discussion. Adarand's distinct federal constitutional standard supplies no local employment exemption. Hopwood's remedial-duty discussion supplies no unasserted continuing violation or imbalance here; its Title VI intent and admissions same-decision/remedy rules do not replace the distinct employment inquiries. No constitutional claim is decided and no admissions proof burden is imported.

### II. This nonremedial layoff lies outside the recognized accommodation

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VII's voluntary-affirmative-action accommodation does not authorize choosing an incumbent for discharge because of race solely to preserve departmental diversity, without the remedial connection to a manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category described in Weber and Johnson. A qualifying remedial plan need not rest on proof that this employer committed an actionable past violation; diversity alone does not supply the missing remedial premise here.

**Authority:** Parts II–III of O'Connor's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supply five direct joins for this holding and the independent holding below.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber permits a bounded effort to open traditionally segregated employment, and Johnson permits voluntary action addressing manifest imbalance while preserving individual interests. Neither makes every beneficial racial objective sufficient to justify a race-selected discharge. The Board claims no remedial imbalance under those standards. Its strongest argument combines the equal employment tie with a continuing educational benefit and Hopwood's recognition that a supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available. Hopwood's employment reservation does not decide this statutory question against the Board, but its affirmative university rule does not enact the requested employment exception. Equal competence does not supply the statutory connection that distinguishes the recognized accommodation from a general desirable-purpose exception. Preserving one department's existing racial composition cannot alone carry that burden. The Court preserves Weber and Johnson and does not decide that only judicially proved discrimination can justify voluntary action or that every remedial layoff arrangement is forbidden.

### III. This discharge independently imposes an unnecessary burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming a diversity purpose could qualify under Title VII, this nonremedial policy unnecessarily trammels the displaced employee's protected interests by assigning her the loss of an existing tenured job because of race, without a remedial limiting goal and despite an available nonracial tie procedure. This record-specific burden independently establishes the violation; it does not invalidate every race-conscious arrangement involving some burden or every remedial layoff plan.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber and Johnson require attention to the interests of employees who bear a voluntary plan's costs. Losing an existing job is a concentrated injury, not merely exclusion from one competitive opportunity. The staff reduction makes one loss necessary, but it does not make selecting the person by race necessary. The Board had previously used a lottery to resolve an equal-seniority tie and could use a nonracial procedure here. Equal qualifications and seniority do not erase Taxman's interest in that equal chance. Hopwood likewise distinguishes a protected opportunity interest from entitlement to ultimate success; that distinction informs the comparison without itself deciding the statutory burden here. Unlike a remedial plan with a limiting objective tied to employment exclusion, the stated departmental-diversity policy supplies no such limiting goal. The Court does not hold that all hiring preferences are permissible or that every layoff arrangement is impermissible; the necessity and justification of this burden are assessed in this actual policy and record.

### IV. The actual relief follows the distinct federal and state judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmance preserves the reviewed federal liability and $134,014.62 backpay, benefits and prejudgment-interest award, the independent NJLAD liability and $10,000 emotional-suffering award, and restored seniority. Taxman's earlier rehiring makes a new reinstatement order unnecessary; the denials of a broader injunction and punitive damages remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** The five judgment supporters sustain this disposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join its complete rationale; Stone-Zsela joins the federal rationale and concurs only in the disposition of the independent state-law component. All eight Associates independently join the principle that federal and state liability and remedial sources must remain distinct; no new NJLAD merits rule is announced.

**Controlling explanation:** The record identifies separate statutory sources and separate damage components. Neither the federal violation nor its review turns the state emotional-suffering award into federal damages. Because the federal judgment is affirmed, no reassessment based on loss of that federal premise is required. The Court grants no additional recovery and decides no unpresented interest-rate, punitive-damages or state-law construction question. Treating the already rehired employee as requiring a new reinstatement command would misstate both the judgment and the available relief.

## Precedent Treatment

- United Steelworkers v. Weber remains binding and supplies the remedial accommodation and protection of affected employees; the Court declines to extend it to this nonremedial discharge.
- Johnson v. Transportation Agency remains binding, including manifest-imbalance permission without proof of the employer's own actionable violation and the no-unnecessary-trammeling limit. Neither independent holding overrules it.
- Hopwood v. Texas, November 25, 1996, retains its affirmative six-Justice rule permitting a concretely supported university educational-diversity interest, its strict scrutiny of actual means and burdens, and its unanimous invalidation of the separate admissions machinery. Its express employment reservation is respected without treating it as a prohibition. Its Fordice, Title VI, admissions burden and remedy components supply no substitute statutory predicate, burden or remedy here.
- Bakke retains the actual five-Justice Part V-C competitive-consideration proposition recognized in Hopwood; neither all of Powell's reasoning nor Hopwood's later affirmative rule is attributed to an earlier majority.
- Adarand's rule for the supported federal constitutional arrangement supplies no local employment exemption or amendment to Title VII. Its actual federal holding remains intact.
- Wygant's attention to concrete burdens informs the competing positions, while its constitutional questions and separate writings are not treated as a controlling Title VII disposition.

## Law After Decision

A public employer cannot invoke the voluntary-affirmative-action accommodation to select an equally qualified incumbent for discharge solely to preserve departmental diversity on this nonremedial record. The absence of the recognized remedial connection and the unnecessary burden independently sustain the judgment. Manifest-imbalance remediation remains available under Weber and Johnson, without a requirement to confess or prove the employer's own violation. The Court adopts neither a universal prohibition on remedial layoff plans nor a constitutional rule against diversity. Hopwood's affirmative public-university educational-interest holding and actual-means requirements remain intact; this employment decision does not extend its constitutional rule into Title VII or import its admissions burdens and remedies. Federal and state relief retain their separate sources.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring.** Title VII's individual prohibition on discharge because of race is sufficient to reject the proposed diversity extension. His Johnson dissent explains why desirable representational objectives cannot replace the enacted text. He recognizes Hopwood's binding affirmative university rule while preserving the contrary position stated in Section A of his separate writing there; that opposition is not Court law. Hopwood expressly reserved employment, and its equal-opportunity holding also confirms that an exact tie does not erase the individual's protected interest. Scalia would reconsider Weber's textual departure in an appropriate case, but joins both present grounds as sufficient applications of existing law. No overruling, constitutional decision or punitive-culpability finding is needed.

**Thomas, concurring.** The statutory protection belongs to the employee facing discharge. The asserted departmental benefit does not itself answer why this employee may bear a racial job loss, and the absence even of Weber and Johnson's recognized premise suffices. His join in Hopwood's bounded remedial-duty holding distinguishes him from Scalia on that separate issue: he does not reject every supported remedial measure or an established dismantlement duty. **Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** Weber and Johnson identify sufficient remedial purposes without excluding every forward-looking educational justification. Its employment reservation leaves the Title VII question open; it does not answer it for either party.

The dissent would reject both categorical exclusion of the proposed nonremedial purpose and a rule treating this layoff burden as dispositive without the full statutory application. The lower courts would identify which monetary and seniority relief that ground independently sustains, neither cancelling all relief automatically nor preserving relief dependent solely on a vacated federal determination unconditionally. No new reinstatement, punitive award, broader injunction or constitutional claim would follow.

## Holdings

### I. A later predicate does not renew a stale injury

**Holding and operative rule:** A predicate act within four years of suit does not restart civil RICO's limitation period for an earlier actionable injury or permit stale damages merely because the act belongs to the same pattern. This rule applies to continuation of the pattern and to acts injuring someone else; the Court does not choose a universal injury, injury-discovery or injury-and-pattern-discovery trigger.

**Authority:** Parts I–II of Breyer's opinion, joined by the six Justices identified in the topology. Their direct majority adopts this limited rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Agency Holding supplies the borrowed four-year period and the close Clayton Act analogy. A pattern can continue indefinitely, with years between predicates. Restarting recovery of already actionable harm at the latest act would destroy repose and permit a knowledgeable claimant to accumulate treble damages. The criminal last-act analogy cannot displace the civil limitation adopted in Agency Holding. Holmes supplies the direct relation between violation and injury, and NOW preserves RICO's injury and causation requirements; neither previously chose a general accrual trigger. Petitioners distinguish their last-act theory from the Third Circuit's last-act-or-injury/discovery formula, but their theory still fails to the extent it revives old loss. Grimmett's unexplained dismissal supplies no accrual holding. The Court decides the revival defect without resolving the broader trigger dispute.

### II. New injury and previously unprovable damages are different from continuing loss

**Holding and operative rule:** A later wrongful act directly causing a genuinely new injury may support timely recovery for that injury without reopening older damages. Separately, damages genuinely too speculative or incapable of proof to recover earlier may accrue when they occur under Zenith; continued accumulation of calculable loss, inadequate investigation and a later calculation of old harm do not satisfy that qualification.

**Authority:** Parts I–II, with the same six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Zenith and Hanover Shoe preserve recovery from a new injurious transaction while leaving old damages barred, and Zenith separately protects losses that could not earlier be proved. Common product, damage type, defendant or pattern does not conclusively establish a single injury. Nor do later advertisements or repairs establish a new one without the required causal relation. The Eighth Circuit recognized independent-injury accrual and treated the asserted late losses as continuing damage from the original failed storage system. The reviewed allegations concern continuing dairy losses; they do not identify additional harm caused by a late act distinct from that ongoing loss. The farm losses are concrete and calculable, unlike Zenith's previously unprovable future damages. Affirmance rests on this limited application, not a categorical same-source bar. The Court finds no damages amount and does not decide that a fall-1990 repair was a fraudulent predicate. It does not determine the contents of lodging cited but not reproduced in the published excerpts, or foreclose every conceivable later injury from the product.

### III. Concealment does not eliminate reasonable diligence

**Holding and operative rule:** A civil-RICO plaintiff invoking fraudulent-concealment tolling must show that concealment prevented timely discovery despite reasonable diligence. Misleading conduct alone does not toll the period when reasonable inquiry would disclose the claim; extraordinary investigation defeating otherwise successful concealment is not required.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Bailey and Holmberg supply the equitable principle that a wrongdoer should not benefit from concealment that frustrates diligent discovery. That protection does not erase the plaintiff's responsibility to investigate facts reasonably signaling injury and its source. The antitrust borrowing context and civil RICO's encouragement of private investigation reinforce the requirement. The Klehrs' legal argument that affirmative concealment eliminates diligence therefore fails. Their separate fact-specific challenge is outside the review undertaken here, and the existing discovery determination is not retried. This decision concerns civil RICO concealment, not every equitable-estoppel doctrine. Brockamp's tax-specific construction supplies no general ban on federal equitable tolling.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agency Holding Corp. v. Malley-Duff: applied for the four-year period and Clayton Act analogy; no general civil-RICO trigger is attributed to it or selected here.
- Zenith Radio Corp. v. Hazeltine Research: applied to distinct new injury and genuinely later-provable damages; neither doctrine renews previously recoverable loss.
- Hanover Shoe v. United Shoe Machinery: applied to new injury from a continuing violation without recovery of stale damages.
- Holmes v. SIPC: preserved as the direct-injury and causation requirement; not extended into a universal accrual rule.
- NOW v. Scheidler: preserved; rejecting an economic-motive element did not eliminate injury and causation.
- Bailey v. Glover and Holmberg v. Armbrecht: applied to concealment that frustrates reasonable diligence; no diligence-free tolling rule is adopted.
- United States v. Brockamp: distinguished as a tax-specific limitations construction; no universal anti-tolling rule follows.
- Grimmett v. Brown: its unexplained certiorari dismissal supplies no substantive accrual rule.

## Law After Decision

The last-predicate-act theory cannot revive stale civil-RICO damages. Timely new injury and genuinely later-provable damages remain distinct paths with their own causal and temporal predicates. The general accrual trigger remains open, and fraudulent-concealment tolling requires reasonable diligence. The Court adopts no categorical rule that one product or pattern can cause only one injury.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They join the reasonable-diligence ruling but would resolve accrual by borrowing the Clayton Act injury-accrual rule together with its associated limitations doctrines. Agency Holding's borrowing, read with Zenith and the borrowing principles in Johnson v. Railway Express, Hardin and Lampf, does not in their view justify taking the period while withholding its ordinary trigger. Their approach retains Zenith's qualification for genuinely later-provable loss but supplies no timely claim here. They therefore affirm without joining the Court's reservation of a general trigger.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He agrees that a later predicate cannot revive an older time-barred injury, that genuinely new injury may accrue separately, and that concealment tolling requires reasonable diligence. He would vacate and remand because he regards the record's treatment as insufficiently specific about each alleged injury, its causal act, discoverability and tolling evidence. Lower courts should perform that inquiry injury by injury and select no universal trigger unless necessary. His position preserves timely new injury and damages previously incapable of proof, does not require knowledge of the legal label RICO, and does not demand extraordinary investigation when reasonable inquiry would be defeated by concealment.

## Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit judgment stands. No further Supreme Court-directed proceeding is required by this decision; ordinary mandate issuance gives effect to the affirmance at the reviewed scope.

## Event

Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo, No. 96-272. Argued March 17, 1997; decided June 19, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 81 F.3d 840, decided April 10, 1996, after this Court's earlier review in Rambo I. The questions are whether the Longshore and Harbor Workers' Compensation Act permits nominal compensation for a significant potential of future injury-related earning-capacity loss despite no current loss, and whether the court of appeals could direct an award without the administrative law judge's necessary finding.

Rambo injured his back and leg in 1980 and received an award based on a stipulated 22.5 percent permanent partial disability. After crane-operator training, he earned about three times his preinjury wages. Metropolitan sought modification on October 30, 1989. The administrative law judge terminated benefits on findings about training, sustained market work, physical demands and earning capacity. The Ninth Circuit directed a nominal award. The stipulated award was not a §8(i) settlement.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument on March 17, 1997 and at decision on June 19, 1997. Nine participated; a quorum was present and five votes suffice for a judgment component. No nonparticipation is recorded.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Ninth Circuit judgment and remands for administrative application of the significant-future-risk standard.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse the nominal-award direction and sustain termination on the reviewed issue.

The Ninth Circuit shall remand to the Benefits Review Board with instructions to return the case to the administrative law judge. That judge must determine whether Rambo has proved a significant, nonconjectural potential that his injury will reduce future earning capacity. No nominal award, amount or unconditional new evidentiary hearing is directed. Existing lawful findings and substantial-evidence review remain available.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I-A, II and III | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer | Vacatur and remand | Statutory availability of nominal compensation; significant-risk proof; preserved employer burden; administrative application and limits |
| Same opinion, Part I-B | Souter | Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer | Vacatur and remand | Appropriate nominal compensation is due once qualifying disability and all ordinary statutory predicates are proved |
| Dissent | O'Connor | Scalia and Thomas | Would reverse and sustain termination on this issue | Present assessment of complete earning capacity; rejection of significant-risk preservation payments; sufficiency of existing administrative findings |

Parts I-A, II and III have six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part I-B has five direct joins: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins statutory availability and its necessary conditions, but not the additional affirmative-entitlement formulation; he states no contrary general entitlement rule. ## Holdings

### I. Nominal compensation requires a significant injury-related future risk

**Holding and operative rule:** For an unscheduled work injury causing no current earning-capacity loss, the Act permits nominal compensation only if the claimant proves a significant, nonconjectural potential that the injury, interacting with reasonably foreseeable economic conditions, will reduce future earning capacity below preinjury earnings. Permanent physical impairment or a merely conceivable possibility alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I-A of Souter's Opinion of the Court, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This six-Justice coalition also adopts Holdings II and III.

**Further controlling entitlement rule:** Once the claimant establishes that qualifying disability and all ordinary statutory predicates, appropriate nominal compensation is due. No finding that Rambo has established those predicates, and no current award or amount, follows from this legal rule.

**Authority for entitlement:** Part I-B, directly adopted by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices. Stone-Zsela's statutory-availability join does not supply a sixth vote for this further formulation.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 2(10) and 8(c)(21) make disability an economic loss of capacity, rather than physical impairment alone. Section 8(h) also commands attention to disability's natural future effects. Those provisions recognize a proved present vulnerability to future economic loss even where current wages fairly represent undiminished current capacity. Sections 13(a) and 22 limit later claims and modification: telling a worker with that vulnerability simply to wait can close the route before the anticipated loss occurs. The deadline does not itself create benefits; the injury-related significant risk supplies the statutory predicate. The five Justices joining Part I-B further conclude that a claimant who proves this compensable economic disability and all other statutory conditions is entitled to the corresponding nominal payment, just as the Act requires compensation on its ordinary proved predicates.

The contrary reading would require a present dollar valuation of every future effect and treat nominal compensation as evasion of finality. The Act ordinarily allows adjustment as economic conditions develop, avoiding speculative forecasts, while §8(h)'s future-effects command must operate when present wages are undiminished. A nominal award reflects the supported vulnerability without compensating bodily impairment or presuming unemployment. Potomac Electric preserves the separate scheduled-injury branch. Rambo I expressly left nominal compensation open and did not promise an ongoing award; today's construction resolves that question without changing §22's grounds or deadlines or reopening true §8(i) settlements.

### II. Persuasion concerns proof of significant risk, not a more-likely-than-not future loss

**Holding and operative rule:** The employer seeking modification must prove the qualifying change in earning capacity, and increased wages alone create no automatic recovery presumption or persuasion shift. Once current earnings fairly represent current capacity, the claimant requesting a nominal order must prove by a preponderance that the work injury creates the significant future potential; this does not require proof that future loss itself is more probable than not.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion directly adopts this allocation and risk distinction.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenwich Collieries assigns persuasion to the proponent of the order, subject to an applicable statutory exception, operative presumptions and lawful defense burdens. Rambo I applies that rule to the employer's modification request. Actual earnings establish capacity only insofar as they fairly and reasonably represent it; otherwise §8(h) requires consideration of injury, impairment, usual employment, other capacity-affecting circumstances and natural future effects. Greater wages cannot by themselves transfer persuasion to the worker.

Rambo's distinct nominal request requires affirmative proof of a significant risk, not an inference from permanent impairment. Confidence, under the preponderance standard, that such a risk exists differs from requiring that the future event itself exceed a one-half probability. The Court selects no numerical risk threshold or actuarial formula. Section 20(a)'s presumption that a claim comes within the Act applies in the absence of substantial evidence to the contrary; it is not a permanent-improvement or future-loss presumption and does not relieve the factfinder of ultimate persuasion after rebuttal. No presumption is deemed triggered, unrebutted or dispositive here. Administrative convenience cannot supply the nominal-order predicate.

### III. The statutory factfinder must apply the distinct risk inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A court of appeals may not direct nominal compensation by making the significant-future-risk finding itself or by presuming it from permanent impairment. The administrative law judge must apply the governing inquiry, subject to ordinary substantial-evidence review; a remand does not unconditionally require new evidence where lawful findings on the existing record suffice.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion adopts the rule and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The demonstrated crane training, sustained market employment, wages and physical demands support the end of current economic loss. That conclusion assesses an actual capacity change; it is not an automatic consequence of a larger paycheck. The existing discussion of steady employment and relative job-loss risk nevertheless did not decide the distinct significant-potential question under today's standard. Sections 21 and 22 assign factfinding and review their separate jobs. Rambo I requires lawful review of preserved questions without presuming either termination or a new hearing, and Greenwich Collieries requires complete weighing under the proper persuasion allocation. The Ninth Circuit could not supply the missing application from its own appraisal. The administrative law judge must determine Rambo's entitlement; the Court sets no amount and directs no award.

## Precedent Treatment

- Metropolitan Stevedore Co. v. Rambo (1995): preserved and applied. Demonstrated economic change may justify timely modification; increased wages do not automatically shift persuasion. The previously reserved nominal-compensation question is resolved here.
- Director, OWCP v. Greenwich Collieries: applied to each requested order's proponent, with preponderance, operative statutory presumptions and genuine defense burdens retained.
- Potomac Electric Power Co. v. Director, OWCP: distinguished as preserving the scheduled branch; today's capacity-based nominal category concerns an unscheduled injury.
- Bath Iron Works Corp. v. Director, OWCP: its mandatory scheduled-loss operation remains unchanged; no economic-loss condition is added to it.
- O'Keeffe v. Aerojet-General Shipyards and Banks v. Chicago Grain Trimmers: their recognition of modification authority remains bounded by §22's enacted grounds and time limits; nominal compensation creates no unrestricted reopening power.

## Law After Decision

Nominal compensation is available for a proved significant future earning-capacity risk caused by an unscheduled work injury, even when no present wage loss exists. The employer's modification burden and the claimant's nominal-order burden remain distinct. The rule does not guarantee awards for permanent impairment, presume unemployment, change true-settlement finality or substitute appellate probability judgments for administrative findings.

## Separate Writings

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** Section 8(h), read with §8(c)(21), requires a present assessment of complete earning capacity, including predictable future economic effects. High wages therefore need not preclude a compensable disability, but a significant possibility of later loss is not itself the presently reduced capacity that the compensation formula requires. A nominal payment whose function is to keep modification available evades §22's one-year limit. Greenwich Collieries requires proof of the actual statutory predicate, rather than a useful administrative mechanism. They also regard the administrative findings about crane training, steady work, available employment and job-loss prospects as a substantially supported rejection of the asserted future loss. They would reverse the directed nominal award and sustain termination on this issue, subject to any genuinely preserved independent question under Rambo I. They do not make current wages conclusive or shift the employer's persuasion burden merely because wages increased.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit must return the matter through the Benefits Review Board to the administrative law judge for the significant-future-risk determination and lawful review. An award may issue only on the required findings and statutory predicates. Later modification remains subject to §22, and no payment amount or new deadline is fixed by this decision.

Governing provisions include 33 U.S.C. §§902(10), 908(c)(21), 908(h), 913(a), 920(a), 921 and 922, and 5 U.S.C. §556(d). ## Event

O'Dell v. Netherland, No. 96-6867. Argued March 18, 1997; decided June 19, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, which sitting en banc on September 5, 1996 reversed the District Court's Simmons ruling under Teague, 95 F.3d 1214. O'Dell's judgment became final on October 3, 1988, after the Virginia Supreme Court's January 15, 1988 judgment. The granted question is whether Simmons's parole-ineligibility protection is available on habeas review of that final capital judgment.

Virginia argued future dangerousness, and the trial court refused the requested parole-ineligibility instruction and an attempted inquiry whether life meant life without parole. The jury found both future dangerousness and vileness and selected death. It also heard O'Dell describe sixteen years on an earlier parole violation and state that, at his age, he would never get out. The District Court's sentencing relief was expressly conditioned on proof of actual legal parole ineligibility; that predicate was not conclusively established by the order.

## Holdings

### I. The opportunity to answer this future-danger case was dictated at finality

**Holding and operative rule:** When the State relies on future dangerousness to seek death and the only lawful noncapital sentence actually excludes ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to give the jury accurate information about that legal ineligibility. Gardner and Skipper dictated that limited protection before O'Dell's October 3, 1988 finality; Teague therefore does not bar the claim, although eligibility, violation and relief still require their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt the old-rule holding; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner protects the opportunity to deny or explain material information supporting a death sentence. Skipper expressly protects a response to the State's future-danger argument, independently of its additional mitigation rationale. Fixed legal ineligibility answers that argument by identifying the actual alternative to death. This concrete protected function, not a general appeal to fairness, supplies the dictated application.

The strongest objection distinguishes historical evidence about the defendant from legal information about postsentencing confinement. The distinction does not justify withholding the accurate response when the State makes future danger material. Ramos permits truthful information about commutation; it does not authorize exclusion of the ordinary-parole answer. Caldwell forbids misleading diminution of the sentencing jury's responsibility, not accurate explanation of its lawful choices.

Simmons states the present protection and its limits: accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction may suffice, no fixed script or immunity from clemency is required, and the State may prove danger within prison. Graham, Gray and Lambrix require functional comparison without inserting their later decisions into the law existing in 1988. Gilmore and Caspari still permit genuinely new procedural extensions after that comparison. No rule that every capital protection is old, or that every defendant is entitled to release predictions, follows.

### II. Independently, the confined protection satisfies the watershed exception if treated as new

**Independent alternative holding and operative rule:** Even if the parole-information protection were classified as new, Teague's watershed exception permits its application where future dangerousness is an operative basis for choosing death, the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and the defendant is denied an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule establishes retroactive availability in that confined category, not that O'Dell has proved those predicates or is presently entitled to sentencing relief.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Five adopt this independently sufficient alternative. The four Associates also join Part I; Stone-Zsela actually regards the rule as new. O'Connor takes no position on this alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Teague's exception, as applied in Sawyer, Gilmore and Caspari, requires a basic safeguard of accuracy and fundamental fairness, not merely an important procedural improvement. The confined conjunction here meets that demanding requirement. Death is sought on a prediction of future danger while the defendant lacks an effective means to explain the legal confinement alternative that directly answers the release premise. That loss corrupts the adversarial choice between the two lawful sentences. Gideon supplies the model of a bedrock safeguard for a fair adversarial proceeding; Crane and Gardner identify the basic importance of an effective chance to answer the case against the defendant. The analogy concerns that function, not equivalence between every evidentiary rule and the right to counsel.

The objection that this is only a helpful instruction understates the holding. A refused instruction alone is insufficient: an accurate alternative defense presentation may satisfy Simmons. Nor does the protection's limited incidence diminish its basic role where the conjunction exists. The exception covers no general class of capital-sentencing rules, no case without a future-danger ground, no legally available ordinary parole or practical release prediction, and no case with an effective corrective opportunity. It does not make the error structural, displace a separate effect inquiry, or abolish the substantive/new-procedure distinction.

### III. Retroactivity does not by itself establish a present sentencing writ

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's Teague disposition must be vacated with that bar unavailable under the preceding holdings, but an operative sentencing writ requires actual legal parole ineligibility, denial of Simmons's effective opportunity, the applicable error consequence, and resolution of genuinely applicable unresolved habeas conditions. The District Court's conditional order is not automatically reinstated, and the conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This five-Justice coalition supplies the formal vacatur and bounded-remand mandate; Stone-Zsela seeks the different conditional-restoration direction stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** Simmons permits accurate defense presentation as well as an adequate instruction. The refused instruction therefore does not establish every element of violation. O'Dell's remarks about sixteen years on a prior parole violation and never leaving prison differ from accurate legal information about this conviction's sole alternative, but remain part of the complete presentation. The verdict's vileness finding and evidence of an earlier prison murder do not automatically cure an influence on sentence selection. Prison dangerousness remains a lawful State argument, without creating a categorical exception to the parole-information protection.

Brecht applies Chapman to preserved Doyle error, and O'Neal extends that treatment to its identified personal-intent claim. Gray found the capital preparation injury harmful under both competing approaches and selected no universal collateral standard. The necessary standard here must be resolved under those holdings, or a supported outcome reached under both; no general prisoner-prejudice rule is assumed. The Court makes no present harm finding.

Gray also preserves genuinely applicable unadjudicated statutory conditions, each with its own temporal and substantive predicates; the Court invents no condition, waiver or satisfaction. If an available violation ultimately warrants relief, Virginia may provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital disposition. No new factual hearing, reinstated or expired deadline, release or execution order follows from this remand.

## Law After Decision

The confined Simmons opportunity-to-answer claim is available against the Teague defense through two independently majority-supported grounds: it was dictated at finality, and, even if new, it satisfies the watershed exception on Part II's exact conjunction. The alternative does not label Simmons new as Court law, extend watershed treatment to sentencing procedures generally or make relief automatic. Eligibility, effective opportunity, error effect and actually applicable habeas conditions remain separate; the universal collateral harmless-error question remains unresolved.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the confined watershed holding in Part II and the rejection of the Teague bar, but concludes that Simmons was not dictated in 1988 and therefore does not join Part I. The new procedure qualifies, in his view, only when the State's future-danger case, actual legal parole ineligibility and loss of an effective means to correct the release premise combine to distort the sentencing choice. He would reverse and restore the District Court's conditional sentencing-relief framework. Restoration would still require proof of legal ineligibility, denial of an effective opportunity through accurate presentation or an adequate instruction, and the applicable error analysis. Neither instructional refusal alone nor the retroactivity holding proves entitlement. He would leave guilt undisturbed and permit Virginia a compliant new penalty proceeding or lawful noncapital sentence if the conditions warrant relief.

**Justice Thomas, joined by Justices Scalia and Kennedy, dissenting.** They would affirm under Teague. Gardner concerned undisclosed adverse information and Skipper excluded historical evidence; neither compelled in 1988 a legal-information obligation concerning the postsentencing regime. Ramos's state latitude and Caldwell's distinct responsibility rule support that substantive distinction. The rule may validly govern cases where it is available without having been dictated at finality. It regulates selection of an otherwise authorized penalty, so it is not substantive; its defined rebuttal protection also lacks the systemic character required for a watershed procedure. The dissenters preserve both exceptions but find neither satisfied. They decide no actual parole status, violation, harm, statutory condition or prison-danger exception.

## Event

United States v. Alaska, No. 84, Original. Argued February 24, 1997; decided June 19, 1997. Original action between the United States and Alaska on exceptions to the Special Master's report, not review of a lower-court judgment. The United States filed in 1979 over Beaufort Sea mineral leasing; Alaska counterclaimed to quiet title within the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Wildlife Range. ## Holdings

### I. The normal baseline governs this coast

**Holding and operative rule:** Alaska's statutory three-mile submerged-land grant is measured from the Convention's normal low-water baseline, including applicable river and bay closing rules and separate belts of qualifying islands. The evidence establishes no firm and continuing general federal rule converting all waters behind fringing islands with entrances under ten miles into inland waters; the earlier Mississippi Sound judgment does not preclude that conclusion.

**Authority:** Parts I–II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by all nine Justices. This coalition also directly adopts the Dinkum holding.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. California supplies the Convention framework. Article 4 permits straight baselines in an appropriate island-fringe setting, but does not require their selection; the United States made no such choice here. The international twelve-mile sea does not enlarge the statutory three-mile State grant.

Alaska claims a general federal practice, not specific historic title to Stefansson Sound, so its claim must be tested on that basis. The 1930 assimilation proposal used four-mile closures, its high-seas straits proposal used six-mile entrances, and its inland-sea proposal used ten-mile bay principles. The Chapman Line, Webb letter, Gulf submissions, 1949 materials and 1961 letter do not establish the asserted uniform ten-mile rule. Leasing convenience and avoidance of federal enclaves cannot supply the missing practice.

The 1985 Alabama/Mississippi decision established Mississippi Sound's particular historic status through specific assertions and foreign acquiescence, not the universal formulation now asserted. Preclusion therefore fails even assuming it is available; Mendoza's general rule against nonmutual estoppel against the United States requires no expansion or new original-action exception. The later Alabama/Mississippi and Massachusetts decrees retain their geographic force. The Nome permit decision addresses an artificial extension's added entitlement, not these natural features. No general authority to contract existing state territory or foreclose a different proved inland-water claim is decided.

### II. Dinkum Sands does not satisfy the island definition

**Holding and operative rule:** A naturally formed feature must ordinarily remain above the agreed mean-high-water datum to qualify as an island under Convention Article 10(1). Dinkum Sands' frequent seasonal submergence below that datum defeats island status and a separate three-mile grant; isolated emergence does not create alternating island entitlement, and abnormal temporary inundation of an otherwise enduring island remains distinct.

**Authority:** Parts I–II, with all nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Article 10's high-tide requirement describes an ordinary characteristic of an island. Its drafting history distinguishes exceptional inundation from an ordinarily shifting gravel-and-ice formation. Borax and the California decree support the mean-high-water datum, averaged over approximately nineteen years, rather than an absolute permanence condition.

The joint monitoring study and subsequent measurements support the Master's finding that Dinkum Sands frequently falls below that datum. The survey found no reliably above-datum elevation; limited readings within the error band and later observed emergence do not establish an ordinarily above-water feature. The 1971 chart and 1979 map relied on an earlier survey, and visual appearance does not displace calibrated elevations. Alaska's argument that Article 10 contains no permanence adjective therefore does not warrant a legal coastline appearing and disappearing with ordinary seasonal changes in this formation. Natural accretion or erosion of an established island presents a different issue. No percentage-of-days test is adopted. Article 11's low-tide-elevation rule supplies no separate entitlement at the reported distances exceeding three miles from the mainland and nearby islands.

### III. The Petroleum Reserve instruments clearly retain the disputed submerged estate

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal retention requires clear inclusion of the submerged lands and clear intent to defeat future state title for an appropriate public purpose; geography or general purpose alone does not suffice. The 1923 Petroleum Reserve order's boundary and petroleum function, together with Congress's specific Statehood Act treatment of that reserve, satisfy both requirements and retain the disputed submerged estate, under the same demanding inquiry for equal-footing and statutory territorial-sea lands.

**Authority:** Parts III–IV of O'Connor's opinion, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These seven also directly adopt the Range holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Shively recognizes Congress's territorial power for appropriate public purposes. Utah Division and Montana require separate clear inclusion and intent to defeat later state title. The order's ocean-side barrier boundary, closures across small lagoons and express three-mile qualification identify the submerged estate. Excluding submerged portions of the same petroleum deposits would defeat the reserve's national-defense purpose. Unlike Montana's unneeded riverbed or Utah Division's reservoir-control rationale, ownership of these deposits performs the reservation's central work.

Section 11(b) preserves exclusive federal legislative authority over federally owned military lands at admission and specifically names the naval petroleum reserve. Read with its defined estate and petroleum purpose, Congress confirmed continued ownership and ratified submerged inclusion. Legislative jurisdiction alone would not establish title, and §11(b) is not rewritten as a generic submerged-title clause. The specific recognition and operative estate answer the express-retention objection. Section 1313(a)'s requirement is not diluted by a presumption favoring federal ownership merely because the Act is a federal grant. Congress's confirmation makes it unnecessary to resolve the disputed independent reach of the Pickett Act; no broad Executive power to defeat future state title is announced.

### IV. The prestatehood Range segregation comes within the wildlife exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** The 1957 application and legally operative temporary-segregation regulation set the specifically described submerged lands apart for the proposed wildlife refuge before statehood. Statehood Act §6(e)'s exclusion of lands withdrawn or otherwise set apart as wildlife refuges therefore retained those lands, although the application was not a completed final reservation; lands outside its boundaries or lacking comparable legal effect are not decided.

**Authority:** Parts III–IV, with the same seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas neither join the timing/legal-effect rule nor assent to the separate geographic determination.

**Controlling explanation:** Utah Division and Montana require clear inclusion separately from title retention. The changed extreme-low-water boundary, coastal submerged features and stated habitat needs establish inclusion. The application sought protection for polar bears, foxes, seals and whales, including the identified aquatic habitat.

The regulation immediately segregated the land from settlement, location, sale, selection, entry, lease and other public-land disposals, including mining and mineral leasing, only to the extent the proposed reservation, if effected, would prevent them. It left administrative jurisdiction unchanged. Those limits do not remove the presently effective, purpose-specific segregation while the Secretary considers the application. Section 6(e)'s broader alternative reaches land legally set apart in that manner; requiring a completed reservation underreads that alternative. The later choice of all or part of the area does not erase the current segregation of the described land.

Congress's wildlife exclusion supplies clear title-defeating intent; neither general federal ownership nor wildlife purpose alone suffices. Section 6(m)'s incorporation of the Submerged Lands Act performs different work. The 1960 order is not given retroactive effect, and the 1980 enlargement does not fix the original statehood boundary. Alaska's defensive inclusion argument overlaps the application's legal effect and is addressed despite its lack of a separate exception to the Master's geographic discussion. The resulting decree must implement these identified classifications, not assumed new boundaries.

## Event

## Holdings

### I. The showing does not justify modifying this continuing injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A party seeking relief from the prospective operation of an injunction under Rule 60(b)(5) must show a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable; any modification must respond suitably to that change. New expense, administrative difficulty, the availability of a cheaper constitutional arrangement or anticipated changes in the Court's membership or views do not by themselves satisfy that burden; the showing here establishes no qualifying change, so denial of relief was not an abuse of discretion.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by the other members of the five-Justice Court coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All five adopt this rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo permits adjustment to significant changed circumstances without demanding an unforeseeable catastrophe. Mere foreseeability does not bar relief. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a particular risk impose a heavy burden, and a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation is not erased merely because the Constitution would allow less; this injunction, however, implements an adjudicated constitutional rule rather than a negotiated promise. The City’s costs and reduced practical access deserve consideration. They show serious burdens of compliance, but do not establish a material change in the program, its relationship with the schools or the facts underlying Aguilar sufficient to make enforcement inequitable. The Court does not discount them simply because they were foreseeable.

The asserted legal change is also absent. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding. Rosenberger permitted controlled vendor financing of a private publication; Chabad and Pinette addressed private expression in public forums. Those distinctions do not remove the direct rule governing public instruction here. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to follow that rule until this Court changed it. A prediction that this Court might reconsider a precedent is not an intervening holding. The Court separately considers and rejects the present request to overrule Aguilar; it does not make the decree immune to a later qualifying motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo is applied to this continuing constitutional injunction with its actual conditions preserved. Rodriguez de Quijas is applied to lower-court obedience. Kiryas Joel, Zobrest, Rosenberger, Chabad and Pinette are distinguished at their actual scopes; none previously displaced Aguilar.

### II. Aguilar remains controlling for this public instructional program

**Holding and operative rule:** Aguilar and the pertinent public-instruction principles of Ball remain in force. The described Title I program is not removed from Aguilar's rule merely because eligible pupils are selected neutrally and public employees, supervision and secular materials are used inside religious schools; no sufficient legal basis for overruling that rule has been shown here.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by the same five-Justice Court coalition. This is a direct majority decision retaining precedent, not a synthesis of the separate concurrences.

**Controlling explanation:** The safeguards now emphasized were central to the original dispute. The record identifies no actual indoctrination, transfer of public money to the schools or religious control of public teachers. Aguilar nevertheless addressed the institutional relationship created by public instruction inside religious schools and the continuing monitoring required to keep it secular. The absence of reported proselytizing does not disprove that structural judgment. Public control is material, but it does not itself establish a new fact or legal reason requiring repudiation of the directly applicable decision.

The contrary argument presses the real costs and diminished access caused by off-site delivery and the difficulty of treating safeguards as a source of entanglement. Those objections do not establish that the rule cannot operate or that its factual premise has collapsed. Wolman preserves properly administered neutral-site services, and the injunction has been administered through off-premises arrangements. The private-expression cases neither decide the public-instruction question nor silently replace Lemon. Ball's Shared Time program used full-time public teachers; its Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers teaching their pupils under their principals. Neither judgment is overruled.

This decision adopts no comprehensive replacement for establishment law and no categorical prohibition on every secular service delivered at a religious institution. Transportation, health, meals, disability services, private-directed benefits and a materially different separated-instruction program retain their own governing questions. Witters and Mueller remain within their existing limits. No particular alternative program is approved.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar and Ball are retained; Wolman supplies the surviving neutral-site alternative. Lemon is neither revised nor replaced. Witters and Mueller retain their bounded benefit holdings; the forum and printing-benefit precedents remain intact.

## Event

Cohen v. Brown University; on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, No. 95-2205, 101 F.3d 155. Argued May 19, 1997; decided June 23, 1997. ## Judgment & Remedy

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title IX liability and the as-applied challenge to federal enforcement | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | None | Liability remains established |

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join throughout. Part I governs statutory accommodation and liability; Part II governs the challenged federal sex classification; Part III governs the distinct preliminary and final remedies. There are no separate writings.

## Holdings

### I. Effective accommodation concerns actual opportunity through three independent routes

**Holding and operative rule:** A recipient may establish effective accommodation of athletic interests and abilities through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing practice of responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's demonstrated, competitively viable interests and abilities; any one route suffices. Numerical imbalance alone neither establishes liability nor requires preferential treatment, but comparative aggregate interests do not excuse actual viable unmet demand where the recipient satisfies neither of the other routes.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part I. All nine Justices join at this level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley makes proportionality a safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Section 1681(b) bars compelled preference merely from numerical imbalance while allowing relevant statistics. Brown's contrary argument would be substantial if actual interest were presumed from enrollment or the alternatives were illusory. They are not. The record establishes material underrepresentation, no continuing responsive expansion and capable existing women's gymnastics, fencing and ski teams and a water-polo club whose competitive interests were unmet. Liability rests on those operational findings together, not percentages or the loss of an individual team alone.

Evidence of real interest, ability and competitive viability remains relevant. A greater aggregate willingness among men does not extinguish the women's demonstrated demand or make unequal opportunity self-justifying. The Court applies the interpretation accepted in Kelley without deciding a broader administrative-deference question, adopting a new survey or counting formula, or requiring unsupported or infeasible opportunities. Title IX does not guarantee a particular team or sport-for-sport counterparts. The independent duties concerning facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits remain binding even when a participation safe harbor is met.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley is applied to the established failure of all three routes; its independent benefits requirements and prohibition on a quota remain intact. Chevron and Martin retain the bounded interpretive roles Kelley gave them; neither authorizes an interpretation contrary to §1681(b).

### II. The challenged federal enforcement satisfies independent sex-classification scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal implementation and judicial enforcement of a sex-conscious athletic-accommodation rule must serve an actual important governmental objective and be substantially related to it; statutory compliance, ratios, budget pressure, stereotype or pretext alone cannot justify the classification. The application here meets that standard because it addresses demonstrated program-wide opportunity inequality by requiring effective accommodation of proved, competitively viable unmet interests, without making proportionality compulsory or extending the correction beyond the demonstrated need.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part II. All nine directly join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley required independent constitutional scrutiny of a public university's sex-conscious restructuring. The same requirement applies to the identified governmental classification in federal implementation and enforcement. Brown remains a private university; receipt of federal aid is not a finding of state action. Craig and Hogan require genuine purpose and substantial fit, not an exemption for a measure labeled remedial.

Correcting this demonstrated opportunity deficit is an important objective. The application responds to actual interested and able students rather than assumptions about what most women or men prefer. The three independent routes and the viable-demand limitation connect the obligation to the disadvantage shown. Its accommodation requirement is satisfied when effective opportunity is achieved; the rule operates for the actually underrepresented sex. The defense that the scheme inevitably compels numerical parity therefore fails on this application. This ruling approves no future sex-conscious order without the same independent inquiry into its actual objective and fit.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley's independent constitutional rule is extended from public-university restructuring to the challenged federal enforcement. Craig and Hogan supply the unchanged important-objective and substantial-relationship standard. Virginia's treatment of willing, capable students is consistent with the result, but no broader Virginia proposition is needed for this unanimous holding.

### III. Effective relief preserves a genuine institutional choice before a necessary specific command

**Holding and operative rule:** After a proven athletics violation, the court must require prompt effective correction while initially permitting the institution to choose among lawful means when another effective route remains available. It may reject an ineffective plan and impose necessary specific relief upon supported findings that institutional choice will not cure the violation; liability alone does not establish a present entitlement to the court's selected team list.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part III. All nine support both the continued preliminary protection and the vacatur of the separate final four-team order.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley establishes the equality duty without guaranteeing a particular sport. The established violation cannot continue indefinitely under the heading of institutional autonomy, but a court must distinguish enforcement of that duty from selection of every lawful means. The First Circuit returned the final plan choice because permissible reductions had been rejected out of hand and the circumstances of the expedited appeal had changed. Its judgment does not declare team-specific relief categorically unlawful.

Brown must now have a genuine opportunity to submit an effective lawful plan, including lawful reductions if they achieve compliance. The District Court retains supervision and may enter necessary more specific relief if supported findings show that the institutional choice fails to cure the violation. The existing preliminary protection for gymnastics and volleyball remains until replaced through that process. It is not the vacated final direction concerning gymnastics, fencing, skiing and water polo. No budget, future compliance finding or unconditional right to a particular team follows from this judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley is applied to preserve lawful compliance alternatives while enforcing actual opportunity. The violation-linked remedial principle also applied in Virginia remains consistent with this relief; no identical-program requirement or new remedial procedure is imported.

## Event

Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho, No. 94-1474. Argued October 16, 1996; decided June 23, 1997. ## Holdings

### An ongoing federal property-right claim may proceed against responsible officers within real remedial limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar a claim for prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officials actually connected to an alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights merely because adjudicating that violation requires resolving a disputed federal title question or affects important sovereign interests. The decree must address proved unlawful future official conduct and may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or require the State itself to convey title.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by the other four members of the five-Justice Court coalition identified in Opinion Topology. The rule has five direct joins and requires no fractured-opinion synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** Ex parte Young permits federal courts to restrain officers' continuing violation of federal law. Tindal and the officer-property reasoning of Treasure Salvors distinguish adjudication affecting an officer's lawful conduct from a judgment binding the sovereign's own title. Oneida supplies the federal character of the pleaded tribal property rights. The complaint identifies land-regulating officials, including Land Board officials and the water-resources director, whose challenged authority allegedly causes the interference; a general duty to obey law would not suffice.

The State's strongest objection concerns the actual breadth of the prayer: exclusive use and exclusion of state regulation could transfer practical control of the waters. Calling relief prospective does not answer that objection. But the Ninth Circuit expressly withheld a decree binding Idaho's title, and the case remains at the pleading stage. Whether a federal right exists and what lawful officer remedy follows must be adjudicated, not assumed. Every Young injunction constrains asserted state authority; the importance of that authority cannot itself decide the federal claim or create a property-specific exemption from review.

Edelman and Green preserve the limits against retrospective relief; Pennhurst bars federal commands founded on state law, not these pleaded federal rights. Seminole's actual officer holding is consistent with bounded enforcement but does not decide every statute or the separate State-amenability question. No IGRA procedure is transferred to this property dispute. The separate United States case covers only part of the area and supplies neither complete substitute relief nor waiver. Affirmance permits ordinary adjudication and tailored relief against connected officers; it does not approve every requested remedy or determine title.

**Precedent treatment:** Young is applied without a sovereign-lands exception or generalized state-interest balance. Tindal and Treasure Salvors supply the officer/sovereign property distinction; Oneida supplies federal-question character. Edelman, Green and Pennhurst retain their respective limits. Seminole remains at its actual officer-route scope and is not recast as a universal immunity or abrogation ruling.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Young:** Applied to alleged ongoing federal violations by responsible officers; no categorical sovereign-lands exception or general state-interest balancing rule is added.
- **Tindal v. Wesley:** Applied to distinguish relief involving officers' possession from a judgment binding an absent sovereign's title.
- **Florida Department of State v. Treasure Salvors:** Its officer-property reasoning supports that distinction; the decision does not authorize a decree binding Idaho itself.

## Law After Decision

Prospective federal enforcement against responsible officers remains available when a disputed federal tribal title question must be decided, subject to genuine limits protecting the immune State and excluding retrospective or State-directed relief. The Court rejects a sovereign-lands exception to that route. The Tribe must still establish its federal rights, an ongoing violation and an appropriate remedy. The judgment supplies no title result or general Article I abrogation rule.

## Separate Writings

**O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would reverse the officer portion because this actual demand seeks comprehensive practical ownership and exclusion of Idaho's sovereign regulation over navigable waters. In their view that combination is the functional equivalent of the barred sovereign-title decree, despite nominally prospective language and the limitation that Idaho is not formally bound. Tindal and Treasure Salvors do not, for them, permit this complete displacement of sovereign control. Their proposed exception turns on the unusual scope and operation of the requested relief, not every suit involving land, costly compliance or an important state interest. They preserve ordinary Young actions and reject free-ranging balancing, state-forum exhaustion and the assumption that the narrower United States case provides complete relief. They would dismiss the present comprehensive officer demand without determining title or foreclosing a genuinely different prospective claim.
