<!-- source-record: Ayeni_v_Mottola_merits_1997-05-27.md -->
## Event
Ayeni v. Mottola; merits decision, 1997-05-27. On review of the Second Circuit’s judgment denying dismissal of a Fourth Amendment damages action arising from media attendance and recording during a residential search. The allegations are not findings of liability.
## Participation
Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court affirms the judgment denying dismissal.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 6–3.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The case returns for further proceedings; no liability or damages is determined.
## Opinion Topology
Ginsburg delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.
## Holdings
The Fourth Amendment claim may proceed where a warrant-authorized search allegedly admitted and recorded an unrelated media audience that did not assist the search. Qualified immunity remains a distinct, date-specific inquiry into whether the constitutional limit was sufficiently clear when the conduct occurred. The warrant’s execution purpose supplies the boundary; the Court does not create a general rule against all official documentation or assistance.
## Precedent Treatment
The Court applies the warrant-scope and particularized qualified-immunity principles supplied by the entering law; later decisions are not used as notice for the 1992 conduct.
## Law After Decision
The pleaded claim proceeds to ordinary merits and immunity development. No finding of liability, damages, or categorical media rule follows.
## Separate Writings
Scalia’s dissent would dismiss because the pleaded law did not give adequate conduct-date notice of the asserted boundary. Kennedy and Thomas join that judgment and ground.
## Procedure After Action
The case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the judgment.
## Source Notes
The supplied Second Circuit record establishes the pleading posture and challenged search. The public projection does not treat allegations as findings.

## Event
CompuServe, Inc. v. Patterson; merits decision, 1997-05-27. On review of the Sixth Circuit’s judgment concerning specific personal jurisdiction over a commercial dispute involving an electronic software-provider relationship.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court affirms the Sixth Circuit’s judgment.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 8–1.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia. The action proceeds in the lower court; liability, venue, and general jurisdiction are not decided.
## Opinion Topology
O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissented.
## Holdings
On a prima facie motion record, a continuing and deliberate commercial relationship centered on an Ohio network, together with the relationship between those contacts and the dispute, supports specific jurisdiction. Physical travel is not an indispensable condition of purposeful electronic commercial affiliation. The showing is provisional and does not resolve disputed facts or the merits.
## Precedent Treatment
International Shoe and Burger King supply purposeful affiliation and claim connection. The Court does not adopt a new Internet-specific test or a universal physical-presence rule.
## Law After Decision
Electronic commercial contacts are assessed by their actual course and forum connection. A prima facie ruling remains subject to later factual procedure.
## Separate Writings
Scalia’s dissent would require a more substantial forum connection on this record and would reinstate the Rule 12(b)(2) dismissal.
## Procedure After Action
The case is remanded for further proceedings.
## Source Notes
The supplied Sixth Circuit opinion and neutral packet establish the contacts and provisional posture.

## Event
Hincapie-Nieto v. INS; merits decision, 1997-05-27. On review of the Second Circuit’s dismissal of a direct petition from a deportation order after the statutory review-channel change.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court affirms the dismissal.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 6–3.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The judgment does not adjudicate an unfiled habeas petition or order a new waiver hearing.
## Opinion Topology
Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.
## Holdings
The enacted withdrawal of ordinary direct review applies to the pending petition where it changes the review channel rather than the elements of the underlying conviction or completed agency consideration. The Court preserves a qualifying §2241 channel for substantial legal and constitutional challenges, but a claimant must invoke that channel and satisfy its actual predicates; the present record does not establish its failure.
## Precedent Treatment
Landgraf supplies the temporal inquiry, and Felker supplies provision-specific treatment of review restrictions. Neither creates automatic retroactivity or a guaranteed merits review of discretionary relief.
## Law After Decision
Direct review remains unavailable under §440(a), subject to the retained legal and constitutional review channel described above.
## Separate Writings
Scalia’s dissent would preserve the direct petition because applying the restriction to this pending filing would attach a new consequence to the earlier filing path.
## Procedure After Action
The direct petition is dismissed; no separate habeas proceeding is entered.
## Source Notes
The supplied Second Circuit record establishes the filing history, concession of deportability, and limited detention concession.

## Event
Kinman v. Omaha Public School District; merits decision, 1997-05-27. On review of an Eighth Circuit judgment affirming §1983 summary judgments, reversing on Title IX, and leaving the original state tort dismissal outside review.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded.
## Judgment & Remedy
| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title IX | Vacated and remanded, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Apply recipient-specific responsibility and response rules; decide no liability |
| Supervisory §1983 claims | Vacated and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Reassess actual notice, response, causation, and defenses individually |
| Municipal §1983 dismissal | Affirmed, 9–0 | All participating Justices | — | No municipal liability without a supported policy attribution |
## Opinion Topology
Thomas delivered the Opinion of the Court for the mixed judgment, joined by the Justices supporting each component as stated. Scalia dissented from the vacatur of the Title IX and supervisory components; Kennedy joined that dissent in full, and O'Connor joined only as to the supervisory component.
## Holdings
Title IX recipient responsibility requires proof tied to the recipient’s own awareness, authority, response, and causal role; the record cannot be resolved by automatic vicarious liability or by a same-sex categorical bar. Supervisory §1983 claims require individualized review of what each official knew, could do, did, and caused, together with the applicable date-specific defense. The municipal claim remains governed by Monell’s policy-attribution requirement.
## Precedent Treatment
Franklin supplies the availability of a damages remedy without deciding the recipient-attribution question. Farmer and Doe supply the actual-awareness and response analogy used for this record. Monell and Pembaur remain distinct municipal-liability rules.
## Law After Decision
The remand requires component-specific factual and immunity analysis and does not establish liability, damages, or a general duty to discover misconduct.
## Separate Writings
The dissent would leave the lower court’s disposition intact, reasoning that the existing investigation and preexisting law did not support the ordered reconsideration.
## Procedure After Action
The case returns for component-specific proceedings; the state tort claim remains outside the judgment.
## Source Notes
The supplied Eighth Circuit record establishes the disputed reports, investigations, and procedural posture; allegations remain unresolved.

## Event
Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.; merits decision, 1997-05-27. On review of the Federal Circuit’s judgment concerning construction of “solder reflow temperature.”
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court affirms the Federal Circuit’s judgment and remands.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas. The remand concerns construction and subsequent infringement analysis only.
## Opinion Topology
Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissented, joined by Thomas.
## Holdings
Claim construction begins with the intrinsic record, including the claim, specification, and prosecution history when part of the record. Technical dictionaries, scientific publications, and expert evidence may assist with terminology and genuine usage disputes, but may not contradict the intrinsic meaning. The judge resolves construction; the Court decides no infringement, validity, damages, or equivalents issue here.
## Precedent Treatment
Markman supplies judicial responsibility for claim construction and preserves the finder’s role on infringement facts. This decision rejects an artificial rule that useful technical evidence must disappear whenever a judge sees no ambiguity.
## Law After Decision
The Federal Circuit’s construction governs the remand, subject to the retained separation between construction and infringement.
## Separate Writings
Scalia’s dissent would give the intrinsic record controlling exclusivity and would not permit extrinsic material to alter a clear construction.
## Procedure After Action
The case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the construction ruling.
## Source Notes
The supplied Federal Circuit opinion establishes the construction dispute and the absence of a jury infringement verdict.

## Event
Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas; merits decision, 1997-06-02. On review of the Eighth Circuit’s judgment allowing a federal action against Arkansas taxes to proceed.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court reverses and remands.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** **Supporting:** all participating Justices. The federal tax action is barred; the Court does not decide the separate tax-immunity merits.
## Opinion Topology
O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by all other participating Justices.
## Holdings
A federally chartered but privately owned cooperative is not the United States for the Tax Injunction Act’s exception merely because federal law created and regulates it. Where the State provides a plain, speedy, and efficient remedy, §1341 bars the federal injunction action. The statutory exception and any underlying tax immunity remain distinct questions.
## Precedent Treatment
The Court applies the Tax Injunction Act according to the plaintiffs’ legal status and the adequacy of the state remedy, without equating federal regulation with sovereign identity.
## Law After Decision
The associations’ federal tax-injunction action cannot proceed on the United States exception presented here.
## Separate Writings
No separate writing.
## Procedure After Action
The case returns for dismissal consistent with the judgment.
## Source Notes
The supplied Eighth Circuit record establishes the entities’ private cooperative status and asserted federal-instrumentality theory.

## Event
Boggs v. Boggs; merits decision, 1997-06-02. On review of the Fifth Circuit’s judgment concerning testamentary community-property claims to pension benefits.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court affirms.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Affirmed, 5–4.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. The judgment stands on the supplied claim; post-distribution assets and independent obligations are not decided.
## Opinion Topology
Breyer delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Stevens dissented, joined by Scalia, Souter and Thomas.
## Holdings
ERISA does not preempt the testamentary transfer of the deceased spouse’s community-property interest on this record where the claim does not direct plan administration, redefine a plan beneficiary, or transfer undistributed plan benefits contrary to the plan’s governing allocation. The Court does not decide claims concerning post-distribution assets or independent obligations.
## Precedent Treatment
ERISA’s survivor and anti-alienation provisions remain controlling within their terms; they do not automatically displace every state-law incident of a separate community-property interest.
## Law After Decision
The challenged testamentary claim may proceed within the limits stated; plan administration and prohibited alienation remain governed by ERISA.
## Separate Writings
Stevens’s dissent would treat the testamentary transfer as an impermissible attempt to alter ERISA’s allocation of the pension benefits and would reverse.
## Procedure After Action
The case returns for proceedings consistent with affirmance.

## Event
De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund; merits decision, 1997-06-02. On review of the Second Circuit’s judgment enjoining New York’s assessment of plan-owned health facilities.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court reverses and remands.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia. The assessment may be applied subject to ordinary proceedings; no refund or liability calculation is fixed.
## Opinion Topology
Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissented.
## Holdings
A generally applicable tax on health-care facilities is not preempted under ERISA merely because an ERISA plan owns a taxed facility and bears the economic cost directly. The assessment does not govern plan benefits, eligibility, or administration. Direct economic impact is not itself a plan command.
## Precedent Treatment
Travelers and Fort Halifax distinguish ordinary economic regulation from laws that govern plan administration. The Court resolves the previously reserved direct-ownership application without converting economic incidence into administrative preemption.
## Law After Decision
The challenged assessment is not preempted on the stated ground and the injunction falls.
## Separate Writings
Scalia’s dissent would treat the direct depletion of plan assets as materially different from the indirect effects in Travelers.
## Procedure After Action
The case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the preemption ruling.
## Source Notes
The supplied Second Circuit record establishes the assessment, plan ownership, and procedural history after Travelers.

## Event
McMillian v. Monroe County; merits decision, 1997-06-02. On review of the Eleventh Circuit’s dismissal of the county-policy theory under §1983.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court reverses and remands.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The remand concerns policymaker attribution and remaining Monell requirements; it does not establish liability.
## Opinion Topology
Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor dissented, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
## Holdings
Whether an official is a final policymaker for §1983 purposes is a function-specific question of state law. On the supplied Alabama allocation, the sheriff acts for the county when setting the challenged law-enforcement policy, so the county may not obtain dismissal solely on the contrary state-attribution theory. Underlying deprivation, causation, and other Monell requirements remain open.
## Precedent Treatment
Monell, Pembaur, Praprotnik, and Jett supply the policy-attribution framework. The Court does not treat an official state label as conclusive or decide functions beyond law enforcement.
## Law After Decision
County attribution may proceed for the challenged function; the remand does not resolve the merits of the alleged misconduct or individual-capacity claims.
## Separate Writings
O'Connor’s dissent would affirm because Alabama law makes the sheriff a state policymaker for law enforcement and the county cannot be liable on that theory.
## Procedure After Action
The case returns for further proceedings on the county claim.
## Source Notes
The supplied Eleventh Circuit record establishes the dismissal and the Alabama-law attribution dispute.

## Event
Saratoga Fishing Co. v. J.M. Martinac & Co.; merits decision, 1997-06-02. On review of the Ninth Circuit’s application of the maritime economic-loss rule to a skiff, net, and other user-added equipment.
## Participation
Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.
## Public Action
The Court reverses and remands.
## Judgment & Remedy
**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas. The remand addresses the remaining tort elements and relief.
## Opinion Topology
Kennedy delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia dissented, joined by Thomas.
## Holdings
Under East River, equipment added by the initial user after the manufacturer’s sale is other property rather than part of the original product when the defective vessel later destroys it. Transfer of the vessel and additions as a package to a later purchaser does not erase that distinction. Defect, causation, and damages remain undecided.
## Precedent Treatment
East River preserves the contract-tort boundary for damage to the product itself while allowing tort recovery for distinct other property. The Court applies that boundary to user-added equipment.
## Law After Decision
The user-added equipment may support a maritime tort claim subject to proof of the remaining elements.
## Separate Writings
Scalia’s dissent would treat the vessel and transferred equipment as one integrated product and would affirm.
## Procedure After Action
The case is remanded for further proceedings.
## Source Notes
The supplied Ninth Circuit record establishes the integrated-vessel theory and the equipment classification question.
