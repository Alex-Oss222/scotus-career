## Event

Federal Election Commission v. Legi-Tech, Inc.; D.C. Circuit Nos. 94-5379 and 95-5085, 75 F.3d 704. Merits decision, February 18, 1997. On review of the D.C. Circuit's February 16, 1996 judgment reversing dismissal without prejudice of the Commission's enforcement action. The questions concern whether actual reconsideration by the lawfully constituted Commission can cure the earlier structural defect, whether all investigation and conciliation steps must be repeated, and whether retroactive constitutional law requires dismissal despite a valid cure.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the D.C. Circuit's judgment.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The district court's dismissal remains reversed, and the enforcement action may continue under the lawfully constituted Commission's authorization. Specifically supported statutory-prerequisite, timing, evidentiary and remaining merits defenses remain available. The Court determines no FECA violation, penalty, final injunction or expired limitations period.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and II | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Actual lawful cure, retroactivity and remedy, and affirmance |

## Holdings

### Actual lawful reconsideration permits this enforcement action to continue

**Holding and operative rule:** After unconstitutional congressional participation has ended, a lawfully constituted Commission with available authority may independently adopt the necessary factual and legal determinations and authorize continued enforcement on an existing investigative and conciliation record. That cure is sufficient here because the lawful Commission actually reconsidered the matter, found probable cause and authorized continuation; it cannot replace an unperformed mandatory statutory opportunity, cure a lack of capacity, or evade an expired statutory deadline.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court accepts the original structural defect and gives the timely challenge effect. A prediction that the same commissioners inevitably would reach the same result would not supply lawful authority. The reported new recommendations, deliberation, probable-cause finding and authorization establish an actual lawful enforcement decision instead. The Commission may use existing evidence and adopt the completed attempted conciliation without replaying each step merely because the earlier body was defective. No distinct mandatory opportunity is shown to have been omitted here.

Ryder requires a real replacement for the lawful decision-making function denied; higher legal review there could not replace lost fact, credibility and sentence review. Here the properly constituted body itself performs the executive probable-cause and prosecution function, while the court retains the liability decision. FEC v. NRA prevents ratification beyond an expired period for authorized Supreme Court review; it establishes no categorical bar to a presently authorized enforcement decision. No such expired period is established in this case. Neither lack of personal bias nor administrative convenience is an independent basis for cure, and judicial examination of whether lawful reconsideration actually occurred remains available.

### Retroactive governing law and valid curative relief remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** A governing structural rule applies to this still-open case, but its equal application does not require dismissal after an independently valid lawful decision supplies the authority needed to proceed. Reliance on the former arrangement alone would not justify withholding the rule or preserving defective authority.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Harper requires the same governing rule on open direct review; Reynoldsville Casket prevents a remedy label from concealing selective prospectivity. The original Commission is therefore not treated as constitutionally valid. The action can continue because the subsequent lawful Commission itself made the required enforcement decision, an independent curative ground rather than reliance protection. The district court erred in treating the earlier remedy as mechanically mandatory despite this different remedial fact. Whether Legi-Tech violated the commercial-use restriction, and whether any particular remaining statutory or evidentiary defense succeeds, remain for the enforcement proceeding.

## Precedent Treatment

- Buckley v. Valeo supplies the structural principle and the distinction between defect and remedy; its treatment of past acts supplies no universal de facto defense against a timely challenge.
- Ryder v. United States is applied to require genuine lawful performance of the relevant function and distinguished because the lawful Commission supplied that executive function here; its fresh-adjudication remedy remains controlling in its own setting.
- FEC v. NRA Political Victory Fund is distinguished by available authority and timing. Its Supreme Court representation and expired-petition-period holdings remain intact; it decided no congressional-membership or structural-ratification merits.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation is applied to require equal governing-law treatment while keeping valid remedies distinct.
- Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde is applied to reject reliance-only relief and permit an independently valid curative basis.

## Law After Decision

A timely structural objection remains consequential, but an actual authorized decision by the lawful executive body can permit enforcement to continue without automatic repetition of every completed preliminary step. Statutory prerequisites, genuine conciliation where required, available authority and deadlines retain independent force. No generic ratification power, constitutional harmlessness test based on bias, or de facto validation is adopted. The underlying restriction and its exception for using a political committee's name and address to solicit that committee are not adjudicated.

No separate opinion is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The enforcement suit proceeds to ordinary judicial resolution under the valid Commission authorization. The Court orders no additional investigation or production, awards no penalty and directs no final injunction. Specifically supported remaining defenses may be considered in those proceedings.

## Event

Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567. Argued November 5, 1996; decided February 18, 1997. On review of the final judgment affirming summary judgment for five state administrators on the estate's individual-capacity §1983 substantive-due-process claim. The questions concern affirmative creation or intensification of danger, constitutional culpability and whether conduct-time law defeats qualified immunity. Official-capacity, survivors' personal and state-tort claims remain outside this review.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the reviewed judgment.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The eight Associate Justices rest solely on individual qualified immunity. The Chief Justice agrees with the judgment on constitutional merits and does not reach immunity. The reviewed individual-capacity federal damages claim ends; no damages, further factfinding or trial is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Qualified-immunity rule and application alone; affirms on the estate's supported favorable account |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Separate constitutional-merits ground for affirmance; no immunity ruling |

## Holdings

### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's creation of roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise concern affirmative conduct. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver. Neither lower-court disagreement nor absence of identical facts independently establishes immunity. Collins, decided after Uhlrig's death, and later Elder and Pinder govern present analysis but cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court leaves constitutional culpability, the affirmative-danger merits and the Tenth Circuit's five-part test undecided.

## Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald supplies objective qualified immunity and remains intact.
- Anderson v. Creighton supplies particularized apparent unlawfulness without requiring an identical prior case.
- Elder v. Holloway is applied to require examination of all relevant conduct-time authority while preserving the favorable factual account and ordinary claim/evidence limits; it supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Pinder v. Johnson is applied to distinguish an affirmative deprivation from omitted protection and to keep notice separate from merits. Its March 1989 result does not resolve this later conduct automatically, and its substantive reservation remains open.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights retains its no-general-workplace-duty rule and its separate, bounded arbitrariness holding. It does not reject every affirmative-danger claim or supply notice before its February 26, 1992 decision.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County and Daniels v. Williams preserve the distinctions between a general protective duty, an affirmative deprivation and negligence; the Court does not convert them into a complete constitutional answer here.
- Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are considered as conduct-time circuit authority at their actual scopes, without converting their formulations into Supreme Court holdings.

## Law After Decision

The decision establishes only the particularized immunity result for the five administrators on this reviewed claim. It supplies no general affirmative-danger test, categorical public-employment bar, mandatory deceit or assurance requirement, no-awareness finding or constitutional approval of budget choices. The distinction between a general workplace-safety duty and an attributable affirmative deprivation remains. A different official act, record or conduct-time legal landscape requires its own analysis.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment. He would reach the constitutional merits under Collins's workplace-duty boundary and a proposed rule for the reserved affirmative-danger claim. He would require an official's affirmative creation or material increase of a specific foreseeable danger, or knowing obstruction of available protection, with conscious disregard of a known serious risk when time and practical opportunity permit a response, and a causal connection to the injury. Assurances, reliance and imminence may bear on that inquiry but are not universally necessary elements; he would add no undefined independent conscience-shocking requirement after those conditions are proved.

On this record, closing the unit and delegating placement were affirmative policy acts, but the warnings, file access and clinical process do not demonstrate the personal culpable contribution his rule requires. No named defendant is shown personally to have selected Waddell's destination, given Uhlrig a concrete assurance inducing abandonment of available protection, concealed a known immediate threat or personally overridden clinical safeguards to direct her into the encounter. He does not find that all officials lacked awareness or that the decision was sound policy. The gravity of the harm and possible negligence cannot alone make senior administrators constitutional insurers.

He would preserve liability questions involving concealment of an imminent threat, an affirmative direction into a uniquely dangerous encounter after a safety assurance, or a personal override of safeguards with deliberate indifference. Independently pleaded federal rights and genuinely different compulsory or intentionally harmful conduct remain outside his no-general-duty reasoning. Because his merits inquiry finds no constitutional violation, he does not decide qualified immunity. His proposed framework and application are noncontrolling.

## Procedure After Action

The Tenth Circuit's judgment remains in force as to the individual-capacity federal claim. The Court orders no remand, damages or further production. Claims excluded from this appeal are not adjudicated anew.

## Event

United States v. Brockamp and United States v. Scott, No. 95-1225. Argued December 3, 1996; decided February 18, 1997. One petition brings up the Ninth Circuit judgments of October 5, 1995, reported at 67 F.3d 260 and 70 F.3d 120. Brockamp's case concerns summary judgment and a disputed dementia claim; Scott's incapacity was found after trial.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

## Holdings

### The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 6511's integrated filing, allowance and amount scheme does not permit the requested nonstatutory equitable extension for mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file. Assuming Irwin's rebuttable presumption and the taxpayers' private-collector analogy apply, the scheme rebuts that presumption; incapacity and absence of prejudice supply no additional period.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; direct unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress separately prescribed when a claim may be filed, when a refund may be allowed and which payments may be returned. For return taxes, §6511(a) uses three years from actual return filing or two years from payment, whichever expires later, and two years from payment if no return was filed. Stamp taxes separately use three years from payment. Section (b)(1) repeats the prohibition on late allowance absent a timely claim, while (b)(2) adds independent monetary limits. This coordinated and repeated structure, reinforced by Congress's particular statutory adjustments, defeats the requested general equitable insertion.

Irwin allows rebuttal from the enacted scheme; an express anti-equity sentence is unnecessary. The Court assumes that refund actions' private-collector history favors the taxpayers. Their strongest claim—actual inability followed by filing and no unfair prejudice—differs from ordinary attorney neglect, but cannot alter this scheme. Suspending both filing and the monetary interval, and adding the taxpayers' proposed matching assessment extension, would change distinct statutory operations. The scale of tax administration corroborates the reading but is not its independent ground. Specific exceptions alone likewise do not carry the decision.

Dalm preserves the connected statutory conditions without itself answering this disability question; Henderson requires this particular text-and-function inquiry. The Court neither finds Scott capable nor decides Brockamp's disputed dementia facts. It does not classify every tax deadline as jurisdictional, adjudicate a separate constitutional disability claim, or decide unpresented fraud, affirmative governmental inducement or distinct recoupment questions.

### The payment lookback independently bars these tax-payment recoveries

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming an otherwise timely return-based claim under §6511(a), §6511(b)(2)'s separately prescribed payment window is not enlarged by the requested mental-incapacity or extraordinary-inability tolling. These tax payments fall outside the applicable ordinary windows, including any supported return-filing extension; this amount limit independently defeats the ordinary tax-refund recoveries without deciding whether McGill's disputed remittance was instead a deposit.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; an independently sufficient unanimous ground.

**Controlling explanation:** A timely claim does not necessarily entitle the claimant to recover older payments. Under (b)(2)(A), a claim within the three-year filing route reaches payments in the immediately preceding three years plus any extension for filing the return; the stamp-tax proviso uses three years. Subparagraph (B) limits other claims to payments within two years. Subparagraph (C) separately limits an allowance without a claim as though the claim were filed on the allowance date. A return-filing extension is not an assessment-extension agreement under §6511(c).

Lundy supplies the distinction between selecting a claim route and measuring recoverable payments, although its Tax Court hypothetical differs from these actual claims. The Court need not reject a favorable late-return filing assumption to enforce the independent cap. The taxpayers argue that equitable filing relief must carry the amount interval with it to preserve an effective remedy. That reasoning presupposes the very equitable power the integrated text withholds; filing relief cannot itself move the dates of refundable payments. Williams preserves express refund consent with its actual conditions, and O'Gilvie's receipt rule creates no extension.

No qualifying assessment agreement, special-period tax item or mitigation circumstance is established. Their statutory conditions remain intact, but the Court announces no new operative construction of an unused exception. The result rejects the asserted equitable ground without treating McGill's deposit characterization as already decided.

### Brockamp's separate deposit contention remains for the appellate court

**Holding and operative rule:** Reversal of Brockamp's tolling ground does not dispose of her preserved contention that the remittance was a deposit rather than a tax payment, which the appellate majority did not reach. The remand leaves that contention and its lawful consequences for ordinary appellate consideration, without ordering repayment or unconditional restoration of the district judgment.

**Authority:** Part III's deposit-remand explanation in Justice Breyer's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices; the Chief Justice agrees with that remedial component without joining its reasons.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory ruling governs tax-payment refunds. The district court rejected the distinct classification contention, but the court of appeals left it unresolved after accepting tolling, and the Government acknowledges its preservation. Removing the tolling premise does not decide that independent appellate alternative. The Court neither treats suspense accounting or lack of assessment as conclusive proof of a deposit nor treats the extension form as conclusively disproving one. Those merits and the consequences of any lawful classification remain open. Brockamp's return reporting $427 liability is not a finding that all $7,000 must be refunded.

## Precedent Treatment

- Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs retains its rebuttable equitable-tolling presumption. The Court assumes its application and finds statutory rebuttal here; it does not treat established incapacity as ordinary attorney neglect.
- United States v. Dalm retains its connected waiver, administrative-claim and timing conditions and rejection of recoupment as independent refund jurisdiction. It is not redescribed as having decided disability tolling under Irwin.
- Commissioner v. Lundy supplies the distinct filing-route and payment-window functions; its Tax Court hypothetical and separate statutory routes remain confined to their terms.
- United States v. Williams preserves the express refund remedy with its administrative and timing requirements; its payer-eligibility holding supplies no equitable extension.
- Henderson v. United States supplies provision-specific attention to text, function and authorized enlargement; its service holding supplies no general power to extend tax limitations.
- O'Gilvie v. United States retains its payment-receipt trigger and expressly supplies no tolling rule.

Courts must distinguish actual return filing from a return's due date and filing timeliness from the separate amount recoverable. Express statutory adjustments retain their own conditions; none is established here. The decision supplies no universal jurisdictional classification, no later disability amendment and no adjudication of a separate fraud, inducement, recoupment or constitutional claim.

## Event

Auer v. Robbins, No. 95-897. Argued December 10, 1996; decided February 19, 1997. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit. St. Louis police sergeants and a lieutenant challenge their treatment as exempt executive or administrative employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act. The questions concern the salary-basis rule's validity for public employees, practical exposure to impermissible disciplinary deductions and correction of an improper deduction. The Board also asserts that it is an arm of Missouri. Salary amount and the appellate duties determination are uncontested predicates; the latter is not independently reconsidered.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms, subject to the correction conditions stated in its mandate.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed subject to actual correction, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Before disposing of the affected salary-basis claim on a corrected-exemption ground, the lower courts must give effect to both reimbursement of Guzy's improper deduction and a promise of future compliance. Neither is adjudged complete. Without satisfaction, the employer cannot rely on restored exemption, and the applicable compensation consequences remain for lawful determination.

The Board's state-arm defense is rejected. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with that threshold resolution without adopting its ground. The Court awards no fixed overtime sum, restores no part of the $9,284 pre-appeal district award and orders no general reopening of application evidence or the uncontested duties ruling.

## Holdings

### The Board's local relationship does not make it an arm of Missouri

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board is not an arm of Missouri entitled to the State's immunity in these actions because its combined financial, budgetary and operational relationship is local. State creation, statutory regulation and gubernatorial appointments do not alone make a separate public body the State; treasury exposure carries substantial weight alongside institutional control rather than serving as an indispensable sole condition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice agrees with the threshold resolution only.

**Controlling explanation:** Hess requires attention to the actual entity rather than its governmental label, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes local public bodies from the State itself. Missouri created and regulates the Board and the governor appoints four members, with the mayor serving as the fifth. Those controls matter, but the record's municipal financing, city budget and appropriation role, limits requiring city approval and local operational relationship establish the relevant character of this Board. No single appointment or financial fact substitutes for that combined inquiry. Because the Board is not a state arm, the Court need not decide whether litigation conduct effected an authorized waiver, whether Congress validly abrogated state immunity for this claim, or whether an officer route would permit the requested relief. Seminole Tribe supplies no categorical Article I answer to those unnecessary questions.

### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Fixed salary reasonably distinguishes exempt compensation from pay varying with work quality or quantity. Congress's delegation includes public employment. Disciplinary needs do not make the criterion unreasonable or establish an exception. Major-safety rules prevent serious danger to the plant or other employees; residency does not qualify. Specified absences and furloughs differ from general discipline. No rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith major-safety penalties retain the narrow scope stated above.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

### The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds

**Holding and operative rule:** The responsible Secretary's fair and considered interpretation of the salary regulation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Presentation in an amicus brief does not alone disqualify that interpretation, but supplies no automatic authority to an agency lawyer, power to amend operative text or deference to new factual assertions about this employer.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice proposes a different interpretive rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Thomas Jefferson University applies Seminole Rock's bounded own-regulation rule; Stinson preserves the difference between interpreting a rule and contradicting or amending it. Martin requires attention to the statutory allocation of administrative responsibility, while Bowen excludes unsupported litigation rationalizations. The Secretary's position here interprets the actual salary and correction text and is consistent with the supported administrative treatment, including the correction letter. It is a fair and considered legal construction, not an employer-specific factual determination resulting from a Department investigation. Its amicus form does not make it an impermissible invention or itself establish its correctness. The canon favoring narrow construction of exemptions does not prevent the Secretary from resolving actual regulatory ambiguity within the congressional delegation. Courts still determine whether the reading fits the text and lawful authority; they do not accept the agency's characterization of unproved facts merely because its legal construction controls.

### Practical exposure requires an actual practice or an effective particular policy

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The salary text concerns real susceptibility to reductions, not only deductions already made and not every abstract possibility listed in a broad disciplinary code. An effective policy can alter compensation arrangements before the first deduction; an undifferentiated range of sanctions governing exempt and nonexempt workers does not itself communicate that these salaried officers will receive impermissible deductions. Guzy's negotiated two-day residency suspension instead of termination was treated below as an exceptional event. The later rule made termination the exclusive residency sanction; it did not repeal every disciplinary authorization. That episode alone does not establish recurring deductions or a general significant likelihood.

The officers' argument that the manual itself defeats any salary agreement rests on the broader legal construction the Court rejects. Their additional examples, witness-understanding contentions and related evidentiary-burden submission remain disputed submissions, not grounds for a new blanket factual or forfeiture finding. The Court adopts no numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or universal employer-compliance presumption, and directs no general factual reopening. Ordinary preservation and mandate rules retain their force.

### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The text separates two eligibility alternatives from two cumulative acts of correction. Intentionality does not defeat the other-than-lack-of-work alternative. Nor does the text require payment immediately upon discovery. It nevertheless requires actual reimbursement and a promise; eligibility alone accomplishes neither. The correction mechanism does not convert a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. Deductions generally made when work is unavailable indicate no intention to pay on a salary basis and defeat exemption during that period.

The lower opinion quoted both conditions and required reimbursement, but its formal disposition did not expressly establish a remand implementation proceeding. This Court therefore states the operative condition in its own mandate: on return, the lower courts must secure both required acts before disposing of the affected claim on the ground of corrected exemption. Neither completed payment nor a completed promise is found. If the employer does not perform, it cannot rely on restored exemption, and lawful compensation consequences remain to be determined. No new correction condition, fixed compliance timetable, automatic overtime amount or damages award is added.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. and Mt. Healthy are applied to the Board's actual local relationship; treasury exposure remains important without becoming the exclusive condition of immunity.
- Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida remains limited to its actual holdings and unresolved State-amenability rationale. No waiver, abrogation or retrospective officer-remedy rule is added.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala and Bowles v. Seminole Rock retain controlling weight for a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation, with text, consistency, lawful authority and actual-ground limits intact.
- Stinson v. United States preserves authoritative interpretation within, rather than amendment of, the operative rule; its Sentencing Commission setting is not mechanically transferred to factual assertions here.
- Martin v. OSHRC supplies attention to actual interpretive responsibility and reasonableness; Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital preserves the exclusion of unsupported litigation rationalizations.

## Law After Decision

The Board may be subjected to these FLSA proceedings without deciding the State's own immunity. Salary basis remains a valid component of the public executive/administrative exemption. Practical exposure, actual deductions and correction are distinct inquiries, and every stated regulatory exception retains its conditions. The own-regulation controlling-weight rule remains law; the Chief Justice's proposed replacement receives no additional join. A correction-dependent exemption requires both reimbursement and the future promise, secured through the present mandate. Duties and the former district award are not reopened.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins validity, practical exposure and correction, but would replace the controlling-weight component of Thomas Jefferson University and the corresponding general Seminole Rock rule. He acknowledges that he joined TJU and that it already protects plain text, consistency, explanation and the actual agency ground. His disagreement concerns the remaining proposition that the administering agency's selection among permissible meanings controls because it wrote and administers the regulation.

He would have courts determine meaning independently using ordinary interpretive tools. If genuine uncertainty remains, an authoritative and considered agency interpretation should receive special persuasive weight when it is consistent or adequately explained, fairly noticed, within delegated authority, grounded in relevant expertise and more than a restatement of the statute. Weight would follow those reasons, rather than agency authorship alone. An interpretation may neither enlarge statute or regulation nor impose liability without the notice required by law. Agency amicus participation neither makes its reading controlling nor automatically disqualifies it as litigation invention.

In his view, delegation to prescribe an exemption differs from the judicial responsibility to determine the adopted rule's meaning. Expertise may persuade, and an actual delegation may assign policy discretion, without every ambiguity transferring interpretive authority. He accepts the Secretary's practical-exposure reading independently because it fits the text, salary concept and the distinction between an abstract sanction, an effective policy and an actual practice. He likewise accepts the correction provision's alternatives and mandatory reimbursement and future promise. He does not reach the separately asserted affirmative-burden contention because he regards it as inadequately preserved.

His proposed change would preserve lawful delegated policy choices, operative regulatory text, ordinary reasoned-decisionmaking requirements, review of the agency's actual ground and the earlier decisions' independent substantive holdings. He would not reopen TJU's completed reimbursement judgment or declare its actual construction substantively wrong. Statute-specific allocations of authority require separate examination. Concrete reliance involving settled transactions, compensation arrangements and fairly communicated obligations deserves weight; an administrative preference for controlling deference is not itself that reliance. This record identifies no concrete reliance requiring a different result where independent interpretation reaches the same salary construction and correction mechanism. He makes no finding about reliance across other programs, authorizes no general reopening, and leaves distinct retrospective-liability claims to their applicable notice, finality and remedial rules. This proposed doctrinal change is noncontrolling.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The remand concerns the sole Ex Post Facto claim actually pursued, the actual exhaustion predicate, and the scope and legal effect of the State's qualified concession. | Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |

## Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** Before a favorable habeas merits adjudication on Lynce's sole Ex Post Facto claim, the lower courts must determine the actual exhaustion predicate and the scope and legal effect of the State's qualified futility concession. The election to pursue only the cancellation claim neither creates a mixed petition from hypothetical additional theories nor by itself establishes exhaustion, express waiver, or the absence or ineffectiveness of available state corrective process.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight Justices adopt the same threshold rule, explanation and limited remand. The Chief Justice joins the judgment only and writes separately. No aggregation of distinct rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2254 requires exhaustion or an established absence or ineffectiveness of state corrective process before habeas relief may be granted. Granberry v. Greer establishes that exhaustion does not remove appellate power, but its discretionary holding concerned a State that omitted the district-court defense. Florida expressly raised that defense and refused waiver. Duckworth v. Serrano prevents clear constitutional error or efficiency alone from supplying the missing statutory predicate.

The State nevertheless affirmatively accepted futility for the common cancellation issue concerning prisoners still in custody, distinguished Lynce's prior release as potentially creating additional reincarceration claims, and later confirmed that he pursued only the Ex Post Facto claim. This supports a substantial claim-specific exception argument. But the district answer already identified that sole claim while retaining its custody qualification, available-procedure account and nonwaiver position. The later election does not conclusively resolve those limits, and counsel's legal characterization cannot alter the statute.

California Department of Corrections v. Morales supplies a concrete intervening reason to reconsider the older Florida decisions that treated overcrowding credits as merely procedural. Morales protects a demonstrated offense-date opportunity for meaningful release consideration; it rejects the procedural label as sufficient while retaining the prisoner's ultimate punishment-increase burden. The older adverse precedents therefore do not conclusively establish that state consideration would now be ineffective. The remaining comparison between Florida's offense-date and later credit laws also may give state proceedings meaningful work. This does not establish that relief in state court is necessarily available or that each accounting detail requires separate exhaustion.

Rose v. Lundy concerns claims actually asserted, so conceivable additional theories cannot manufacture a mixed petition. The Court returns the actual statutory question for decision without resolving the constitutional merits. The lower courts may accept a legally sufficient claim-specific exception; they must not order a state-court detour regardless of what the qualified concession means.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional violation, statutory continuity, protected-credit amount, release date or entitlement to restoration is decided. Neither a Florida procedural default nor cause and prejudice is established. The State's assertion that every other possible theory is foreclosed remains an argument, not a preclusion holding. The decision does not decide the 1996 amendment's general application to this already-pending proceeding: it rests on no relevant difference between versions and adopts no implied-waiver theory. Permission to deny a claim despite nonexhaustion does not authorize a favorable grant on an assumed predicate. The Court neither requires new filings nor treats the source record as missing litigation conduct.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment; no other Justice joins his writing. He agrees that exhaustion comes first and that the Court should not decide the Ex Post Facto merits on this record. His following view is conditional on the courts lawfully reaching those merits and decides nothing for the Court.

Applying Weaver v. Graham, which protects a legally available means of reducing confinement, and California Department of Corrections v. Morales, which protects meaningful mandatory reconsideration without requiring proof that the lost hearing certainly would have produced parole, he would hold that Florida violated the Ex Post Facto Clause when retrospective cancellation of release credits already awarded and used to release Lynce lengthened his confinement. The objective operation of the credit system determines the punishment effect; its original overcrowding purpose does not negate the actual return to prison. Florida used the credits to calculate and end imprisonment, then applied an offense-based cancellation backward and reincarcerated Lynce. That direct increase is more than the lost opportunity protected in Morales despite the Board's initial individualized prediction. A credit determining lawful release forms part of the effective measure of confinement; describing it as administrative does not erase its effect.

This position does not freeze every prison-administration rule, protect credits never awarded or lawfully unavailable at the offense date, or bar prospective changes that do not increase punishment for completed conduct. If the merits are reached, he would have the district court determine the protected amount under the correct temporal analysis, order release if the lawful sentence has expired, and recalculate any remaining confinement without the unconstitutional retrospective cancellation. He does not demand certainty that an omitted hearing would have yielded release. Those conditional merits and remedial views impose no present credit award or release command.

## Event

Maryland v. Wilson, No. 95-1268. Argued December 11, 1996; decided February 19, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Maryland Court of Special Appeals, which affirmed suppression of cocaine that fell when Wilson obeyed an order to leave a lawfully stopped vehicle. The question is whether Mimms's categorical driver-exit authority extends to passengers. The State presents that categorical theory; the delayed stop, passenger movements and nervousness are not a preserved independent ground for the order.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment and remands. Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.** Supporting: O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Kennedy, who would affirm suppression on the presented theory. Reversal removes the federal premise that a passenger-exit direction always needs an additional individualized justification; it neither orders admission of the cocaine nor reinstates a conviction. The State receives no new decision on its unpreserved alternative theory.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Kennedy files a dissent, joined by Stevens. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela files a separate dissent, joined by no other Justice. No dissenting writing supplies a join to the Court's rule.

## Holdings

### Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** During a lawful traffic stop, an officer may direct a passenger to leave the vehicle without additional passenger-specific suspicion as a limited safety measure incident to the stop. This authority concerns the exit direction itself; it does not establish categorical forcible detention for the entire stop, permission to frisk or search, or power to prolong detention after the lawful traffic business ends.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, counting its author within that six-Justice coalition; six direct rationale votes control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court extends Mimms's comparison of officer safety and incremental intrusion to the passenger's immediate relocation during an independently lawful traffic stop. A passenger may conceal a weapon and threaten an officer even though the passenger committed no traffic offense. The safety risk does not disappear when the potentially dangerous occupant sits beside the driver.

The passenger has a stronger initial liberty argument because the driver's offense supplies the reason for the stop. But the direction changes the passenger's location during an interruption already lawfully occasioned by the vehicle's stop. At that limited scope, the safety interest outweighs the additional intrusion. Terry and Brignoni-Ponce require attention to the nature of the intrusion and its justification; their particularized-ground rules for different intrusions remain. The Court does not resolve whether every passenger was already seized at the instant the vehicle stopped or whether a passenger may be forcibly held for its entire duration.

Whren permits an objectively justified ordinary initial stop while preserving execution limits. Robinette likewise separates lawful traffic measures from continued detention after their justification ends. Neither supplies automatic search or frisk authority. References in Michigan v. Long to occupants leaving cars and in the Rakas concurrence do not constitute an earlier controlling passenger-exit holding; the Court decides that question here. Because Maryland presented only categorical authority, the Court neither relies on the observed movements as a distinct justification nor eliminates preservation limits. The contrary categorical federal premise is reversed, leaving ordinary further proceedings rather than a final evidence-admission order.

**Precedent treatment:** Mimms's safety/incremental-intrusion rule extends to this passenger-exit direction. Terry, Brignoni-Ponce, Whren and Robinette retain their distinct predicates and limits; no frisk, search or prolonged-detention rule is displaced.

## Precedent Treatment

- Pennsylvania v. Mimms supplies the safety and incremental-intrusion comparison and is extended from drivers to the limited passenger-exit direction.
- Terry v. Ohio and United States v. Brignoni-Ponce retain their requirements for the distinct seizures and protective intrusions they govern; this decision does not make every additional police measure categorical.
- Whren v. United States preserves objective authority for the ordinary initial traffic stop and independent constraints on manner, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette preserves the distinction between measures during lawful traffic business and unsupported continuation after it ends, together with genuinely independent state law.
- Michigan v. Long and the Rakas concurrence are not treated as having already decided the passenger-exit question. Ornelas retains its independent ultimate-suspicion/probable-cause review and protection of historical facts without becoming a universal review rule.

## Law After Decision

Federal law permits the limited passenger-exit command during a lawful traffic stop without an additional passenger-specific reason. The decision adds that authority while leaving other seizure, duration, search, frisk and independent-state-law questions at their existing scopes. It does not authorize every subsequent command merely because the vehicle was lawfully stopped.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justice Stevens.** Stevens and Kennedy would affirm because the passenger's added restraint needs an objective circumstance connected to the actual stop. Mimms's driver committed the traffic offense and controlled the vehicle; a passenger did neither. Aggregate danger does not justify an otherwise arbitrary command to every innocent passenger. Officers retain practical latitude to act on supported safety circumstances, but an explanation tied to those circumstances must precede the exercise of additional control rather than depend on the officer's grace after categorical authority is conferred. The movements and nervousness are not denied; the State did not preserve them as an independent justification, so they supply no basis to concur in reversal.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would permit an exit direction when any objective circumstance reasonably supports it for safety, facilitating a lawful search or conducting the stop. The showing is modest and may arise from movements, numbers, location, conduct or other practical facts; neither probable cause nor a formal suspicion label is required. Mimms's driver-offense predicate does not by itself justify an added command to a passenger. Whren's ordinary stop authority should therefore remain subject to a reason connected to the actual stop, rather than general statistics or administrative ease. These observations might have supplied such a reason if properly preserved, but the Court cannot replace the State's categorical case. He would leave suppression in force and order no remand for a new justification. He reserves commands to remain in a vehicle and preserves lawful-search control and arrest on probable cause.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Maryland courts for proceedings consistent with the limited federal rule. No new hearing, final admissibility determination, conviction reinstatement or extended detention is ordered. Genuinely independent Maryland protections remain available within ordinary procedure.

## Holdings

### Collateral indemnification does not alter an assumed state arm's immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A third party's contractual promise to reimburse litigation expense or an adverse judgment does not by itself divest an otherwise immune state instrumentality of Eleventh Amendment immunity. The instrumentality remains the defendant and primary legal obligor; collateral reimbursement does not replace the complete inquiry into its legal, financial and institutional relationship with the State or make structural financing irrelevant to antecedent arm status.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine participating Justices join the rule, explanation and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Hess's distinction between an entity's actual institutional relationship with the State and a single financial fact. Hess denied immunity to PATH because its compact and self-supporting structure did not make the States responsible for its employee judgments. It did not reduce arm status to identifying the final source of every dollar spent.

The University remains the party against which judgment would run here. DOE's promise allocates reimbursement between the contracting parties; it neither substitutes the United States as defendant nor extinguishes the University's legal obligation to Doe. An otherwise immune arm does not lose its constitutional identity whenever it acquires insurance or a contractual right to repayment. The Ninth Circuit discussed several institutional factors, but treated federal reimbursement as the decisive difference from its accepted University precedent. That collateral difference cannot do the work assigned to it.

Doe's strongest argument is that assured federal payment eliminates the treasury concern central to immunity. Treasury exposure remains highly probative, but Hess makes it neither the sole consideration nor categorically indispensable. Mt. Healthy and Lake Country Estates require attention to the entity that is actually sued and its governing structure. A financing arrangement embedded in that structure may bear on arm status; this decision concerns a reimbursement promise on the express assumption that arm status otherwise exists.

The grant does not authorize deciding the University's status from first principles or the independent grounds for other relief. The Court reverses the indemnity-based premise and remands within those limits. It decides neither the employment agreement's merits nor whether any separate individual or prospective claim may proceed.

**Precedent treatment:** Hess's complete entity inquiry is applied and extended to the distinct collateral-indemnity question without a sole-treasury test. Mt. Healthy and Lake Country Estates retain their distinction between an arm of a State and an independent public entity.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. remains an entity-specific compact decision; its highly probative but nonexclusive treasury consideration does not make collateral repayment decisive.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle preserves the distinction between a separate public body and an arm of the State; a reimbursement contract does not itself change that identity.
- Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency retains attention to the actual creating structure rather than governmental function or funding labels alone.

## Law After Decision

An assumed state arm retains its immunity despite a third party's collateral promise to reimburse litigation costs or judgments. Initial arm status still requires the governing institutional inquiry; this decision adds no general funding immunity, sole-treasury rule, waiver or abrogation doctrine. The University's status is assumed only for the question answered.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for proceedings consistent with the rule that collateral indemnification cannot remove the assumed immunity. The District Court's accepted-arm immunity disposition cannot be displaced on that ground; independently open claims and defenses retain their lawful posture. No employment order, damages award or decision on prospective relief is entered.

## Holdings

### Fixed access restrictions satisfy the governing injunction standard on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** A content-neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve its corresponding significant interest. These fixed fifteen-foot demonstration restrictions at identified doorways, driveway entrances and parking-access points of covered facilities—all facilities where abortions are performed in the Western District of New York—with nonthreatening initial counseling by no more than two counselors per approached person or group, satisfy that requirement on the supported obstruction and crowding record; fifteen feet is not a universal safe harbor.

The injunction binds responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert; shared views, association or independent advocacy alone do not establish that relationship. This coverage boundary neither adjudicates new nonparty liability nor determines whether a particular speech restriction is sufficiently tailored.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's fixed-access sufficiency holding has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas additionally join the responsible-party, notice and actual-concert coverage boundary, giving that distinct boundary nine direct joins despite their dissent from the fixed restrictions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Madsen's provision-specific fit inquiry. Stable, identifiable access corridors protect entry and exit where crowding and obstruction occurred while leaving visible and audible advocacy nearby. The counseling exception permits limited conversational approach inside the otherwise cleared area. The decree regulates demonstrating, not mere presence, and the exception applies per person or group approached rather than to two counselors in an entire zone.

The strongest objection is that existing conduct prohibitions and substantial compliance with the temporary order make further speech restraints unnecessary. Those matters must be considered, but they do not erase the supported exceptions, repeated access interference and pre-order conduct. Madsen does not require proof that every conceivable narrower command failed or that a wholly speech-neutral predecessor was first disobeyed. The established access problem, fixed geography and retained communication together support this limited arrangement. The prior temporary order already contained personal-distance and counseling restrictions; the Court does not describe it as an obstruction-only order.

Carroll requires precision in speech relief, and Claiborne Hardware ties relief to supported misconduct and responsible actors. Their demands are met at this qualified fixed scope, without making general medical distress or disagreement with a message sufficient. The injunction binds responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert; shared views, association or independent advocacy alone do not establish that relationship. The Court neither adjudicates new nonparty liability nor validates a larger or differently supported exclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's heightened injunction-fit rule is applied without turning its earlier dimensions into safe harbors. Carroll's precision requirement and Claiborne Hardware's misconduct/coverage limits remain controlling.

### The fixed-zone counseling exception may end after a communicated refusal

**Holding and operative rule:** Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, the allowance for nonthreatening counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates verbally or nonverbally that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; the condition establishes neither silence everywhere nor a mobile personal radius beyond the fixed zone.

**Authority:** The same six Justices directly join Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's fixed-zone counseling holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court treats the counseling provision as an exception to valid access relief. It permits an initial peaceful approach without advance invitation. Its communicated-refusal condition keeps that exception from becoming the crowding or continued pursuit the valid corridor is designed to prevent. Unexpressed unwillingness does not trigger the condition.

Madsen rejected an advance-permission rule that obstructed peaceful initial advocacy across a broad area. This condition allows that initial encounter within a more confined, independently justified fixed zone, then ends the exception after a clear refusal. The distinction does not rest on a general right to avoid unwelcome ideas on a public street. Protected signs, leaflets, speech and approaches remain available outside the fixed corridor within lawful limits.

The contrary view treats the listener's refusal as an impermissible veto on protected speech. Refusal does not suppress the message here; it terminates the privilege to continue this close encounter inside a valid access restriction. The Court's holding rests on that remedial and geographic premise. It neither permits an unwilling listener to move a fifteen-foot boundary through otherwise available public space nor decides every independently drafted cessation rule.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's protection of peaceful initial approach is preserved, and its qualified access-remedy principle is applied to the limited counseling exception. No broader captive-audience rule is adopted.

### The moving-person restriction burdens more speech than the record warrants

**Holding and operative rule:** The injunction's fifteen-foot demonstration boundary moving with each person seeking access to or leaving a covered facility burdens more protected speech than necessary under Madsen and must be struck, even with its initial counseling exception and communicated-refusal qualification. This record-specific holding does not create a right to threaten, touch, obstruct, crowd or coercively pursue anyone, or invalidate every precisely supported separation rule.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's holding has eight direct rationale joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Breyer opposes present invalidation on the separately stated procedural ground.

**Controlling explanation:** A boundary that moves with a listener restricts ordinary conversational approach, passing leaflets and stationary advocacy outside stable access points. A speaker may enter it through another person's movement, and multiple moving zones make compliance uncertain. The initial counseling exception and refusal trigger reduce some burdens but do not cure the remaining uncertainty or protect every peaceful interaction nominally permitted.

The clinics rely on persistent pursuit, crowding and medical access needs. Those are substantial interests, and the existing decree directly forbids obstruction and physical interference. Madsen nevertheless requires a separate fit for the additional moving exclusion; supported misconduct does not authorize suppressing unrelated peaceful advocacy. The retained fixed corridors and independently lawful conduct provisions address the established access problems without this mobile speech boundary.

The lower court left the distance a zone could travel from the clinic unresolved and preserved a possible application concerning a different willing listener. The Court invents neither a travel cap nor an overlap prohibition to sustain the actual restraint. Carroll's precision principle bears special force where contempt enforces the boundary. The Court invalidates this provision at its established operation while leaving genuinely different and precisely supported lawful conduct relief open.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen supplies the distinct fit requirement and protection of peaceful initial approach; Carroll supplies precision. Claiborne Hardware preserves the connection between responsible misconduct and speech burdens.

### The moving-vehicle restriction independently fails

**Holding and operative rule:** The fifteen-foot demonstration radius moving with vehicles seeking access to or leaving covered facilities likewise burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be struck. The decision preserves traffic safety and fixed access protection without deciding the validity of a materially different, precisely supported vehicle restriction.

**Authority:** The same eight Justices directly join Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's vehicle-specific holding.

**Controlling explanation:** A moving vehicle radius can displace peaceful speakers on the sidewalk or curb as the vehicle changes position. The need to protect a driveway or parking entrance does not by itself establish a need for that further mobile exclusion. The valid fixed access corridors address the identified ingress and egress points, and independently lawful prohibitions on blocking or physical interference remain available.

Madsen requires examination of the burden added by this particular provision. General traffic safety is a significant interest, but it does not establish that all peaceful demonstration within fifteen feet of a moving vehicle must be forbidden. The Court neither denies that vehicles may require particular protection nor substitutes an untested smaller radius. This provision fails on its demonstrated fit, with any different proposed restriction requiring its own lawful basis.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's provision-specific tailoring is applied separately to vehicles; its fixed-access holding supplies no automatic mobile-zone permission.

### Removal of the moving predicate also removes the dependent retreat distance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once paragraph 1(b)'s moving-person and moving-vehicle restrictions are invalidated, paragraph 1(c)'s cross-reference cannot recreate an automatic fifteen-foot moving distance outside valid fixed zones. That dependent operation must be removed on remand; independently lawful conduct prohibitions remain, and the Court neither commands a substitute distance nor decides every cessation condition considered apart from the invalidated predicate.

**Authority:** The same eight Justices directly join Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's dependency and conforming-remedy holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Madsen requires removal of an invalidated injunction predicate and forbids restoring it through another clause. The retreat cross-reference derives its moving distance from the provision that now falls. Retaining the same mobile exclusion under a different label would leave the unconstitutional burden in place.

That consequence is distinct from sustaining the counseling condition inside an independently valid fixed zone. It is also distinct from deciding every conduct-only cessation rule that might be proposed. The lower court must conform the decree to the actual rulings, preserving only independently lawful unreviewed terms. The Court orders no replacement personal zone, travel endpoint or new contempt finding. Underlying state-law entitlement and other ungranted claims retain their ordinary unresolved posture.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's invalidated-predicate and no-evasion holdings directly govern the cross-reference; its refusal to require a personal-zone substitute remains intact.

## Precedent Treatment

- Madsen v. Women's Health Center supplies the content-neutral-injunction standard, provision-specific remedial fit, actual-concert coverage and removal of invalidated predicates. Its particular distances are applications; its rejection of broad advance permission does not prohibit every lawful response to pursuit, and its remand commands no substitute personal zone.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism retains its ordinary rule for generally applicable neutral regulation; the more demanding Madsen standard governs this injunction.
- Carroll v. President and Commissioners of Princess Anne supplies the need for precision where judicial restraints burden expression and contempt enforces them.
- NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co. preserves protected peaceful advocacy and confines relief to supported misconduct and responsible parties; viewpoint or association alone does not make a nonparty bound.
- Boos v. Barry preserves the distinction between neutral physical harm and offense at communicative content; no general unwilling-listener veto is recognized.

## Law After Decision

Madsen remains the governing standard. These qualified fixed access zones and their in-zone counseling condition survive, while the moving-person and moving-vehicle restraints and their dependent outside-zone distance fall. Stable geography and a retained initial counseling exception do not create a universal valid buffer size. Removal of a moving zone may not be evaded through a cross-reference. No general personal-space right, complete cessation prohibition, mandatory substitute injunction or newly adjudicated state-law remedial entitlement follows.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's fixed/mobile distinction and removal of the automatic retreat. In his view the supported repeated close pursuit permits a narrower provision on remand: preserve an initial peaceful approach, but after an objectively clear verbal or nonverbal refusal require the enjoined defendants to stop following and maintain a modest, precisely defined separation supported by the record. Direct conduct rules can address obstruction, touching, threats and harassment without making every speaker calculate an automatic moving fifteen-foot boundary. He would permit, not require, that carefully fitted provision. It would restrict neither nonparties, willing conversation, the initial peaceful approach, stationary advocacy nor speech addressed generally, and would create no undefined personal-space right or general listener's veto.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas.** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the Court's independent conclusion that the moving restraints fail Madsen and that their dependent distance must fall. They would invalidate the fixed expressive restrictions as well. Their stricter approach requires a legally supported remedial predicate and direct justification for restraining protected expression. The existing conduct prohibitions and substantial TRO compliance make the added expressive exclusion doubtful; appellate assumptions about what access relief might justify cannot replace the trial court's actual assessment. **Justice Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the qualified fixed access and counseling rulings but would leave the lower judgment intact pending the District Court's interpretation and possible modification of the disputed mobile terms. In his view contextual construction and first-instance administration should precede invalidating the disputed operation. He does not approve an unlimited radius, supply a travel cap himself or conclude that every literal floating zone is constitutional. His disagreement concerns the Court's decision to invalidate now on this posture.

## Event

Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc., 68 F.3d 525. Argued November 6, 1996; decided February 24, 1997. On review of the First Circuit's October 23, 1995 judgment affirming dismissal of the federal claims. ## Holdings

### The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Parental liberty protects choice among lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not give each parent authority to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. Accepting the alleged compulsory exposure and aftermath, this complaint establishes no displacement of family custody, educational choice or parental teaching that supports the requested substantive federal veto; the ruling does not approve pretextual curricular action or decide independent state-law permission rights.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined on each of the three granted holdings by every other Justice, giving each proposition nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Meyer and Pierce protect parental direction of education and upbringing. They do not make the public school a forum in which every parent may prescribe the particular lessons a child receives. The parents remain free to choose lawful educational alternatives and to instruct their children concerning the ideas encountered at school. Public-school enrollment does not waive that continuing role.

The Court acknowledges the difference from Curtis: attendance here was required, while Curtis concerned a separable optional resource and expressly reserved compulsory instruction. Its result cannot automatically decide this case. The particular compulsion alleged here is exposure to a presentation, not displacement of parental custody or instruction, compelled profession or personal performance by these plaintiffs. Those limits support the present conclusion without treating attendance as voluntary.

Settle requires a genuine pedagogical basis; calling an event curriculum is insufficient. The Court neither endorses the alleged crude conduct as sound teaching nor holds that any presentation bearing an educational label is constitutional. It decides that these allegations do not establish the particular parental deprivation asserted. The written-permission policy may impose an independent obligation, but its alleged breach does not itself create the substantive federal right the parents seek. State-law remedies and materially different claims remain outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Meyer and Pierce are applied at the scope of the parental interest they protect. Curtis's reservation of compulsory instruction is preserved and the present allegations are examined independently. Settle's genuine-pedagogy requirement remains intact.

### These allegations establish neither the claimed student privacy deprivation nor extreme official abuse

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory exposure to the alleged presentation and subsequent coarse imitation does not, on this complaint, establish the students' asserted decisional-privacy or bodily-integrity deprivation or extreme abuse of official power. The conclusion rests on the actual allegations, including the absence of compelled personal performance, affirmation, intimate disclosure, physical contact or treatment; it is no categorical rule that words, a single event or compulsory educational programming can never violate the Constitution.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this holding in the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** A constitutional privacy or bodily-integrity claim requires an invasion of the protected interest, not simply exposure to ideas or language the listener finds offensive. These students were required to attend. They were not alleged to have been selected for a skit, required to affirm the message, disclose intimate information, engage in sexual or religious conduct, or undergo bodily intrusion. The Court considers that account together with the alleged weeks of coarse imitation; it does not discard the aftermath or convert unfavorable facts into trial findings.

The parents' strongest objection is that the school's compulsory authority gave the presentation an intrusive character beyond ordinary offensive speech. That circumstance matters, but it does not establish the particular personal deprivation pleaded. The alleged program, taken as a whole and with favorable inferences, also does not establish the extreme official abuse asserted here. The Court announces no universal rule that verbal conduct cannot do so and does not decide the rights of a student personally subjected to a materially different act.

Curtis supplies no categorical answer because it reserved compulsory instruction. Settle likewise does not license religious exclusion, pretextual pedagogy or arbitrary conduct merely because it occurs in school. The present holding leaves protections against compelled profession or participation, bodily intrusion, religious establishment or targeting, and independently established discrimination intact. It does not transform authority to restrict certain school speech into an individual federal entitlement to censorship of every offensive presentation.

**Precedent treatment:** Curtis's limits and Settle's genuine-operation inquiry are preserved. Neither decision supplies general school immunity or resolves materially different personal coercion or invasion claims.

### The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Title IX requires discrimination, exclusion or a hostile educational condition imposed because of sex; sexual subject matter and offense alone do not establish that relationship. The complaint connects the alleged later hostility to disagreement with the presentation's message rather than the plaintiffs' sex, so this element's absence requires dismissal without deciding severity or pervasiveness, a comprehensive recipient-liability rule, or the treatment of materially different sex-based targeting.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this sufficient statutory ground. No severity, attribution or constitutional speech-defense holding supplies an alternative basis.

**Controlling explanation:** The presentation's sexual content and alleged offensiveness are accepted. So are the subsequent weeks of coarse imitation. Neither fact, alone or in their stated context, supplies the missing connection between the treatment of these plaintiffs and their sex. They allege disagreement with the program's message and ensuing hostility on that basis. The program's delivery to an audience containing both sexes is contextual evidence, not a categorical defense to sex discrimination.

Harris requires a contextual inquiry and rejects psychological injury as a prerequisite; DeAngelis likewise rejects mandatory deterioration in performance, physical advances or similar added thresholds. Those employment decisions inform the analysis by analogy, but the Court does not transplant a complete Title VII attribution formula into Title IX. Personal targeting, touching, measurable academic loss and psychological injury are not indispensable elements announced here. Nor does the Court hold that one episode can never suffice. It does not decide whether this entire program and aftermath meet a fully elaborated Title IX severity-or-pervasiveness standard.

Franklin recognizes compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination. Availability of that remedy cannot supply the statutory violation absent from this complaint. The decision leaves questions involving sex-specific humiliation, school-authorized sexual targeting and deliberate indifference to known sex-based harassment for cases that present them, without prescribing their complete liability rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris and DeAngelis retain their contextual protections and operate here only by analogy. Franklin's compensation rule remains available for a proved intentional violation; it does not dispense with the because-of-sex requirement.

## Event

Dunn v. Commodity Futures Trading Commission, No. 95-1181. Argued November 13, 1996; decided February 25, 1997. On certiorari to the Second Circuit's judgment affirming appointment of a temporary equity receiver. The question is whether the Treasury Amendment excludes the off-exchange foreign-currency options from Commodity Exchange Act coverage. Investors supplied funds for trades in defendants' names; options or participations were not sold directly to those investors. The reviewed proceedings have not finally adjudicated fraud liability.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Second Circuit's judgment and remands. Justice Scalia delivers the Opinion of the Court.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Relief dependent on CFTC authority over these statutorily excluded transactions must be vacated. The lower courts may address a genuinely independent lawful ground for a claim or remedy in its proper procedural posture. The Court makes no final fraud finding, orders no restitution and does not direct blanket dismissal of every possible claim.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivers the Opinion of the Court. The complete rationale and remedy are joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, with the author included in that coalition. No separate opinion is filed.

## Holdings

### Off-exchange foreign-currency options are within the Treasury Amendment's exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Options to buy or sell foreign currency are transactions in foreign currency within the Treasury Amendment, so these off-exchange trades are excluded from the Commodity Exchange Act's coverage. The statutory proviso restores coverage only for transactions involving a sale for future delivery **and** conducted on a board of trade; no bank-only or dealer-only restriction is added to the exclusion.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join the rule, explanation and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** A currency option is a transaction whose object is the underlying currency. The contract derives its substance from the currency and its fluctuating value; the statutory words do not postpone that relationship until exercise. The CFTC's distinction between trading currency and trading a right to transact in currency is too narrow. Applied consistently, that distinction would also unsettle the recognized treatment of futures and deprive the express future-delivery proviso of its natural work.

Congress separately listed foreign currency, security warrants, security rights, resales of installment loan contracts, repurchase options, government securities, and mortgages and mortgage purchase commitments. It then stated a particular exception. The Court applies that language and structure without adding a counterparty condition or deciding every listed category's boundary. Both future delivery and trading on a board of trade are required by the proviso; the off-exchange transactions here do not satisfy it.

The distinct regulatory trade-option exemption does not define this statutory exclusion. Its quoted conditions require the offeror reasonably to believe the option is offered to a producer, processor, commercial user or merchant solely for purposes of its business as such. The CFTC's representation below that interbank options qualify under that exemption is preserved at its stated scope; it establishes neither universal regulatory coverage nor qualification of every trade here. A permissible regulatory interpretation cannot create authority withheld by the statute. Auer's own-regulation decision concerns a different inquiry, and the Court need not decide the competing deference contentions to apply this clear statutory boundary.

The alleged fraud cannot supply absent authority. Exclusion from this Act does not protect fraud under other law. Because this is an interlocutory receiver appeal, reversal removes the rejected coverage premise and requires implementation below, without a final liability determination or automatic rejection of genuinely independent grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** The Second Circuit's American Board of Trade option/exercise distinction is rejected at this statutory scope; Salomon Forex's contrary inclusion of off-exchange currency options accords with the rule adopted here. Auer remains confined to its own-regulation setting and supplies no expansion of the enacted exclusion.

## Precedent Treatment

- CFTC v. American Board of Trade, Inc., 803 F.2d 1242, cannot sustain the contrary distinction between an option transaction and its exercise under this exclusion.
- Salomon Forex, Inc. v. Tauber, 8 F.3d 966, is consistent with inclusion of off-exchange foreign-currency options; the Court does not adopt every statement concerning other transactions or counterparties.
- Auer v. Robbins governs permissible interpretation of an agency's own regulation within lawful authority. The present statutory holding neither changes that rule nor decides a broader agency-deference dispute.

## Law After Decision

The Treasury Amendment excludes these off-exchange currency options. Coverage under its proviso requires both future delivery and conduct on a board of trade. The statute and the separate trade-option regulation remain distinct. Enforcement need alone cannot expand the CFTC's statutory authority, and other antifraud laws retain their independent reach.

No separate opinion is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for proceedings implementing the exclusion and vacating relief dependent on the rejected CFTC authority. Any independent lawful basis must be evaluated under its own requirements and the existing procedural posture. No final fraud judgment, damages award, restitution calculation or new factual inquiry is ordered by this decision.

## Event

United States v. Wells, No. 95-1228. Argued November 4, 1996; decided February 26, 1997. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's judgment vacating Wells's and Steele's conspiracy and bank-false-statement convictions and ordering a new trial. The questions are whether the Government may present its statutory argument despite its earlier treatment of materiality, and whether 18 U.S.C. §1014 makes materiality an element. Two other substantive counts ended in acquittals and are outside this review's remedial reach.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Eighth Circuit's judgment concerning the two appealed convictions and remands. Justice Thomas delivers the Opinion of the Court with the joins stated below.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 8–1.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Stevens, who would affirm the new-trial judgment. All nine agree to decide the statutory question and preserve the limits concerning acquittals and independently available remaining issues.

Vacatur removes the premise that §1014 requires materiality. It does not automatically reinstate the convictions or sentence, determine harmlessness, reject every other appellate issue or reopen the two acquittals. The court of appeals must determine which remaining grounds may properly be considered and resolve them under their own requirements.

## Holdings

### The Court may review the statutory question actually decided below

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government may argue that §1014 lacks a materiality element where that legal question was presented in appellate briefing, decided by the court of appeals and included in the question granted, despite its earlier request for instructions under then-prevailing circuit law. This bounded choice to review statutory meaning does not excuse every waiver or decide the independent effect of the indictment, instructions or case-specific prejudice.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron permits consideration of an included legal argument on an adequate established record while preserving limits on distinct claims, questions and missing facts. Here the Government presented its construction in supplemental briefing after Gaudin, the Eighth Circuit passed on it, and respondents have had notice and a full opportunity to contest it. The Court is reviewing that appellate statutory ruling, not introducing a new factual theory or deciding a question never presented below.

The Government's trial position does not become irrelevant. Rule 30's restriction on assigning error in an accepted charge and the doctrines governing invited error may matter to particular trial objections. They do not require the Court to leave the expressly decided statutory construction unreviewed. An allegation of materiality in the indictment or an instruction affecting the jury's understanding of falsity may have consequences apart from whether Congress made materiality an element. Those matters remain for lawful consideration rather than being resolved against respondents by the decision to reach the statute.

**Precedent treatment:** Lebron's discretionary included-argument principle is applied to an actually decided and granted legal question, without turning it into a universal preservation exception.

### Section 1014 does not add an objective materiality element

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1014 does not require an additional finding that a statement had a natural tendency or objective capacity to influence the institution's decision. The Government must still prove beyond a reasonable doubt the applicable conduct—knowingly making a false statement or report, or willfully overvaluing land, property or security—and the purpose of influencing an institution enumerated by the statute in a covered transaction.

The covered transactions are an application, advance, discount, purchase, purchase agreement, repurchase agreement, commitment or loan; a change or extension of one through renewal, deferment or otherwise; or acceptance, release or substitution of its security. The institutional requirement includes the FDIC-insured institutions relevant here; it is not displaced by calling a transaction financial.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight Justices also support vacating the appellate new-trial judgment's contrary statutory premise. Stevens dissents from this holding and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted provision addresses any knowing false statement and expressly requires a purpose to influence the specified institution. It does not add the different objective-capacity requirement respondents seek. Purpose and materiality are not identical, but recognizing their difference does not permit inserting the second condition. The statute's fault, institutional and transaction requirements must each be enforced; a practical expectation that consequential lies will predominate, prosecutorial restraint or sentencing discretion cannot replace them.

Respondents rely on received false-statement law and the consolidation note's assurance of no unlisted substantive change. Those sources warrant consideration, but they do not establish a settled meaning of the enacted phrase that overcomes this provision's complete wording and express purpose condition. The Court does not rest on an exact count of predecessor statutes or infer that every omission in every criminal statute is decisive. Nor does punishment severity create an element after the provision's text and context resolve the issue. Lenity applies when genuine interpretive ambiguity remains, not as an automatic addition of the disputed requirement.

Kay rejected a defense based on lack of actual influence under its predecessor text; it does not exhaustively construe every later provision. Williams held that the particular checks were not factual assertions; it did not establish universal materiality. Gaudin assigns an offense element to the jury when the statute contains it and does not create that element for §1014. Knowledge, the applicable false-statement or willful-overvaluation conduct, influencing purpose and statutory coverage remain necessary jury findings under the governing criminal standard. The Court decides neither every §1001 clause nor every fraud statute.

**Precedent treatment:** Kay and Williams retain their actual predecessor-statute and false-assertion holdings without supplying a universal materiality element. Gaudin's jury-allocation rule remains fully controlling for genuine offense elements.

### Acquittals and independently available trial issues remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The decision on materiality does not reopen the two acquittals or itself resolve independent indictment, instruction, evidentiary, conspiracy or sentencing grounds. The court of appeals must first determine which remaining grounds are properly available and then adjudicate them under their own law; reserving a ground neither certifies its preservation nor requires a new trial, conviction reinstatement or any particular sentence.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices as to these limits. The eight-Justice vacatur rests on Part II; Stevens would instead affirm the new-trial judgment under his retained materiality requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on a statutory element the Court rejects. Removing that premise does not answer whether the indictment's materiality allegation has an independent consequence or whether the actual falsity instruction affected a finding the statute genuinely requires. The lower court addressed some conspiracy and expert-evidence questions but left other materiality-expert, multiple-conspiracy and Government sentencing questions unresolved when it ordered retrial. None is automatically decided by the present holding.

Roy distinguishes a legally available harmless-error inquiry from an actual finding that a particular error did not affect a verdict. Its preserved personal-intent habeas holding supplies no automatic answer for this charge or direct appeal. The Court neither decides that an independent violation occurred nor substitutes appellate findings for the jury. Further proceedings concern the two appealed convictions only; the two acquittals remain outside every permitted remedy.

**Precedent treatment:** Gaudin and Roy retain their distinct element-allocation and error-review functions; neither supplies an automatic result on the independent grounds left open here.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp. permits the bounded review of an included legal argument; review here does not abolish waiver, invited error or limits on new claims and facts.
- United States v. Gaudin continues to require the jury to determine every actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt. This decision construes §1014 and does not narrow that constitutional rule.
- Kay v. United States supports rejecting an actual-influence defense within its predecessor setting without deciding every later false-statement statute.
- Williams v. United States retains its holding that the particular checks were not factual assertions; it supplies no universal materiality rule.
- California v. Roy preserves the distinction between permissible error review and an established harmlessness result. Its particular personal-intent habeas rule is not automatically transferred to this direct appeal.

## Law After Decision

Materiality is not an additional §1014 element. Actual statutory knowledge, false-statement or willful-overvaluation conduct, influencing purpose, institutional coverage and transaction coverage remain essential. The Government's earlier trial position does not bar this decided-and-granted statutory question, but independently available trial objections retain their force. No conviction, sentence or acquitted count is automatically restored.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He agrees that the Court may decide the statutory issue and that acquittals and independent trial matters retain their proper limits, but would construe §1014 to require materiality and affirm the new-trial judgment. The received meaning of false-statement offenses, the statute's objective and the consolidation assurance support retaining that limitation; Congress's omission of the word does not alone show an intention to abolish it.

In his view, a speaker's purpose to curry favor differs from a statement's objective capacity to matter to the institution. The majority's culpability requirements therefore do not answer the risk of imposing serious criminal liability for trivial flattery or an inconsequential lie. His Aguilar and X-Citement positions support examining the offense's received legal meaning and object, rather than grammar alone. Williams and restraint in construing serious criminal prohibitions reinforce the narrower reading. Kay rejected an actual-reliance defense under its predecessor; objective materiality does not require proof that the decisionmaker actually relied on the statement.

Once materiality is retained, Gaudin requires the jury to decide it. Respondents contested that issue and the judge expressly removed it from their jury's deliberations. Stevens would preserve the new-trial judgment on that record, not order acquittal or declare every omitted-element error automatically reversible. Roy permits harmlessness review within its category but does not establish harmlessness of this different withdrawal. His position leaves the two acquittals intact and decides no independent instruction, indictment, evidentiary, conspiracy or sentencing issue unnecessarily.

## Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit must proceed on the rule that §1014 contains no additional materiality element and consider any properly available independent grounds affecting the two appealed convictions. It must determine preservation before reaching newly asserted grounds. The Court orders neither an automatic retrial nor reinstatement of convictions or sentence, makes no harmlessness finding and disturbs neither acquittal.
