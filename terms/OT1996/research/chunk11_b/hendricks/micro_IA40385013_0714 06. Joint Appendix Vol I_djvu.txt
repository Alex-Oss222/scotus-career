PETITION 


COMES NOW Petitioner, State of Kansas, by and 
through its attorney, NOLA FOULSTON, District Attor- 
ney for the Eighteenth Judicial District, and for its cause 
of action herein alleges and states as follows: 


1. Petitioner Nola Foulston is the duly elected, qual- 
ified and acting District Attorney for the Eighteenth Judi- 
cial District, Sedgwick County, Kansas. 


2. Respondent, Leroy V. Hendricks, whose present 
address is Hutchinson Correctional Facility, Hutchinson, 
Kansas, is presently confined in the custody of the Kansas 
Department of Corrections. 


3. This action is brought pursuant to Kansas Stat- 
utes Annotated 59-29(a)01 et seq. (1994 Legislative Ses- 
sion Senate Bill 525), an act concerning persons who 
commit sexually violent offenses. 


ee 


4. By written notice received on the 6th day of July, 
1994, within forty-five days of the filing of this petition, 
Kansas Department of Corrections, the agency with juris- 
diction, has certified that respondent, Leroy V. Hendricks, 
may meet the criteria of a sexually violent predator as 
defined by statute, to wit: 


(a) That on the 26th day of November, 1984 
respondent Leroy V. Hendricks was con- 
victed in the Eighteenth Judicial District, 
District Court, Sedgwick County, Kansas, 
Case No. 84CR 1463 of a sexually violent 
offenses involving two victims as defined 
by law, to wit: K.S.A. 21-3503 Indecent Lib- 
erties with a Child; and K.S.A. 21-3503 

Indecent Liberties with a Child; and has a 
history of prior sexual offenses from other 
jurisdictions, to wit: 1960 Spokane, Wash- 
ington, Indecent Liberties with a Child; 
Seattle, Washington (1963 and 1967) Inde- 
cent Liberties with a Child (two separate 
cases). 


(b) That Respondent is anticipated to be 
released from total confinement on the 11th 
day of September, 1994. 


(c) That sufficient evidence exists to determine 
that respondent suffers from a mental 
abnormality or personality disorder which 
makes him likely to engage in predatory 
acts of sexual violence directed towards 
strangers or individuals with whom a rela- 
tionship has been established or promoted 
for the primary purpose of victimization. 


WHEREFORE, petitioner requests that respondent be 
taken into custody by the Sheriff of Sedgwick County and

ORDER and MEMORANDUM OPINION 


This court has before it at the present time the very 
limited issue — Is K.S.A. 59-29(a)01 et seq. enacted in the 
1994 legislative session, a criminal or civil proceeding? 


The state contends that it is a civil commitment pro- 
ceeding and the Respondents contend it is a criminal 
proceeding. The resolution of this issue will control over 
discovery procedures, including, but not limited to, 
requests for admissions, interrogatories, production and 
disclosure of relevant documents and witnesses, includ- 
ing expert witnesses. In addition, the matter of expert 
fees and costs of examination of the person as well as

attorney fees for counsel representing these persons, need 
to be addressed as to which governmental agency will be 


obligated to pay for these - the county if civil or the state 
if criminal? 


Counsel have presented their respective briefs. After 
reviewing the same and other consideration of this issue, 


the court finds that the Act is a civil commitment pro- 
ceeding. 


In determining whether a statute is categorized as 
civil or criminal in nature, the court looks to the language 
of the statute and legislative history analyzing the pur- 
pose and effect of the statutory scheme. Allen v, Illinois, 
478 U.S. 364, 368, 106 S.Ct. 2988, 291, 92 L.Ed. 2d 296 
(1986). In United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242, 248, 100 S.Ct. 
2636, 2641, 65 L.Ed. 2d 742 (1980), the Court adopted a 
two-part analysis: 


First, we have set out to determine whether 
Congress, in establishing the penalizing mecha- 
nism, indicated either expressly or impliedly a 
preference for one label or the other. Second, 
where Congress has indicated an intention to 
establish a civil penalty, we have inquired fur- 
ther whether the statutory scheme was so puni- 


tive either in purpose or effect as to negate that 
intention. (248-49) 


As the court did in In re Young, 857 P.2d 989 (Wash. 
1993), it looked first to the language of the statute and the 
legislative history, then turn to an analysis of the purpose 
and effect of the statutory scheme. Washington enacted 
the Community Protection Act of 1990. Part X of the Act 
(REW 71.09) is entitled “Civil Commitment.” Those 
defendants who are determined to be “sexually violent 


38 


predators” can be involuntarily committed after they 
have served their sentences. It is suffice to state that this 
statute is comparable to K.S.A. 59-29(a)01. 


1. Language of the Statute and Legislative History. 


The title refers to “An act concerning persons who 
commit sexually violent offenses; relating to such per- 
son’s civil commitment; evaluation, care and treatment; 
allegation of sexual motivation in criminal cases.” Section 
1 sets out legislative intent and history and concludes 
with the statement “. . . . therefore a civil commitment 
procedure for the long-term care and treatment of the 
sexually violent predator is found to be necessary by the 


legislature.” 


The procedural provisions set out in Section 4, 5 and 
6 are similar to a civil involuntary commitment under 
K.S.A. 59-2908 et seq. although persons under this Act 
have the right to a 12-person jury rather than six and a 
finding beyond a reasonable doubt rather than clear and 
convincing proof. 


Section 7 provides that upon a determination by 
court or jury, that the person is a sexually violent preda- 
tor, that person shall be committed to the custody of the 
secretary of social and rehabilitation services for control, 
care and treatment until such time as the person’s mental 
abnormality or personality disorder has so changed that 
the person is safe to be at large. It further provides that 
such control, care and treatment shall be provided at a 
facility operated by the department of social and reha- 
bilitation services and shall be kept in a secure facility 
and such persons shall be segregated at all times from

any other patient under the department's services. Com- 
mencing in June 1, 1995, these persons committed under 
this act shall be kept in a facility or building separate 


from any other patient under the supervision of the secre- 
tary. 


Section 8 provides for annual examination of the 
person’s mental condition with a right to assistance of 
qualified professional persons, a right to petition the 
court for discharge, a right to counsel, a right to be 
released if there is a determination by the secretary that 
the person’s mental abnormality or personality disorder 
has so changed that the person is not likely to commit 
predatory acts of sexual violence if released (Section 10). 


2. Purpose and Effect of the Act 


If the Act is punitive either in purpose or effect then 
the state’s categorization that the statute is civil in nature 
would not be controlling. Here the focus is on treatment 
not punishment nor on criminal culpability of past 
actions. The Department of Corrections plays no role in 
the care and custody of the person in the statutory 
scheme. The court has been informed that at the present 
time, the two persons involved in these proceedings are 
confined at the Security Behavior Ward of Larned State 
Hospital ~ the Jung Building, under security of a guard 
Their confinement is based upon a finding by the Honer- 
able Paul Clark, district judge that there is probable cause 
to believe that these persons are sexual violent predators 
and that they be taken into custody and transferred to an 
appropriate facility for the evaluation. (Section 4) The 
court assumes that this place of detention is temporary, as 


40 


Section 7(b) provides for a more permanent facility. This 
fact, standing alone, will not constitute such punishment 
as to negate the civil nature of this act. 


Allen, supra, held that an Illinois statute providing for 
civil commitment of sexually dangerous persons was 
properly categorized as civil, not criminal, particularly 
where the state had a statutory obligation to provide care 
and treatment designed to effect recovery for those com- 
mitted and allowed for release when no longer dan- 
gerous, as in the instant Act. 


The Act is focused on treating these persons for a 
current mental abnormality and not for any punitive 
measures for past actions. Incapacitation has often been 
recognized as a legitimate civil goal. Addington v. Texas, 
441 U.S. 418, 99 S.Ct. 1804, 60 L.Ed. 2d 323 (1979). 


Respondents argue that an examination of the overall 
tenor of the act reveals its criminal nature because of the 
references to specific provisions of the Criminal Code 
which they claim outweigh the number of references to 
the Probate Code. In State v. Boos, 232 Kan. 864, 659 P.2d 
224 (1983), the court was faced with a similar argument. 
The court, holding that K.S.A. 8-286 was civil in nature 
and not criminal, acknowledged the various references to 
the criminal code provisions but held that “the quasi- 
criminal language used is not the sole criteria for deter- 
mining whether the statute is criminal or civil”. Like the 
instant case, no criminal sanctions are imposed as are 
required in a criminal proceeding. 


It is the opinion of the court that K.S.A. 59-29(a)01 et. 
seq. is civil, not criminal, in nature. Both the language 
and history of the Act and its purposes and effect support

{

this conclusion. The Respondents have failed to establish 
that the statutory scheme of the Act is so punitive in 
purpose or effect as to negate legislative intent that this 
proceeding is civil in nature. Allen, supra, at 369. Based 
upon this holding, the court finds that matters of discov- 
ery including but not limited to requests for admissions, 
interrogatories, production and disclosure of documents 
and witnesses, including expert witnesses, shall be con- 
trolled by Chapter 60 of the Code of Civil Procedure. On 
the matter of necessary and reasonable expenses, costs 
and attorney fees, as set out in the Act, Sedgwick County 


will be obligated to provide funds as per court orders to 
be issued herein. 


The state has filed a Motion For An Order Compel- 
ling Discovery And Other Relief in both cases. The 
motions allege that the Respondents were advised by 
their counsel not to participate in the evaluation process 
and Respondents have not so participated. Sanctions 
against Respondents have been requested.

probable cause, that person shall be taken into custody

ally qualified person. Judge Clark found probable cause 
and ordered the Respondents to be taken to the Larned 
Security Hospital and submit to an evaluation to deter- 
mine whether the Respondents are sexually violent pred- 
ators as defined by law. 


Within 45 days after the filing of the petition the 
Respondents shall be provided a trial to determine 
whether that person is a sexually violent person. The 


42 


burden of proving that a person is a sexually violent 
predator is upon the state. 


The respondents have the right to be examined by 
experts of their choosing and this evaluation would be 
relevant if Respondents offer this testimony in the trial. 


Respondents’ refusal to participate may jeopardize 
the state’s position on the burden placed upon it. 


This court has found that this proceeding under 
K.S.A. 59-29(a)01 et seq. is civil in nature. There has been 
no specific finding by this court that the Fifth Amend- 
ment privilege against self-incrimination is applicable in 
a civil proceeding such as this; however, the prevailing 
view expressed in In re Young, supra, is that persons 
subject to involuntary civil commitment as sexually vio- 
lent predators have no constitutional right to remain 
silent unless it is conclusively shown that sanctions 
imposed upon that person are tantamount to a criminal 
proceeding. 


This court has found that the Department of Correc- 
tions has no involvement in the confinement of Respon- 
dents at the Larned Security Hospital. The evaluation is 
to be done by SRS staff. The only reason that Larned 
received the Respondents is because SRS at the present 
time has no “appropriate facility” to detain Respondents 
and do the evaluation required by law. John House, coun- 
sel for SRS has informed the court that if an order is 
entered to return Respondents back to Larned for evalua-
