### 1996-12-09 — Humane Society v. Babbitt; D.C. Circuit No. 93-5339; District of Columbia No. 92-cv-0952; no Supreme Court docket assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Humane_Society_v_Babbitt_merits_1996-12-09.md).

#### Holdings

#### Independent failure of traceability

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming sufficient personal aesthetic injury from Lota's absence, the Society has not shown that the challenged August 1991 ESA certificate amendment caused the lost zoo access. The independent earlier donation produced that loss, and this record establishes no mechanism connecting the later amendment to its continuation.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient jurisdictional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires support for the plaintiff's own injury and its connection to the challenged action at the litigation stage presented. A person can have a concrete aesthetic interest in observing a particular animal; species-wide depletion, ownership and a scientific profession are not prerequisites. The Court nevertheless assumes actual injury here rather than finding that the hypothetical regular observer described in the question exists in the evidence. The zoo's donation preceded the amendment, which governed later uses by Hawthorn. The Society abandoned its separate appeal concerning the original transfer and did not challenge the CITES certificate. Neither action can be restored to the case by calling this a challenge to continued absence. Government permission can be causally effective through supported, predictable private conduct; a government command is not invariably necessary. But this record supplies no such link between the later amendment and the lost Milwaukee observation. Sweet Home's treatment of actual injury to individual animals under a habitat regulation does not supply the missing human standing link for this different transportation permission.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the actual members' affidavits establish injury in fact. It adopts no categorical rule against third-party causation, no purchased-ticket requirement, and no general prohibition on standing to observe one animal.

#### Independent failure of likely beneficial relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Society also fails independently to show that suspending the challenged certificate or declaring the transportation interpretation unlawful would likely benefit a member's impaired observation. Complete restoration is unnecessary, but a regulatory change must offer some likely concrete improvement to the member's interest.

**Authority:** Part II of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices independently adopt this remedial standing ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Invalidating the amendment would leave Hawthorn able to retain Lota at its Illinois premises. It would not annul the donation, compel the zoo to receive her, or make renewed Milwaukee access substantially likely. No supported alternative benefit to an established observer appears in the affidavits or requested relief. Church of Scientology permits meaningful partial relief even when full restoration is impossible, but changing another person's legal options is not beneficial relief to a member by definition. Even with favorable reasonable inferences from the existing materials and assumed injury, the missing connection remains. The Winikoff letters were considered because the objection to their unsworn form was not preserved; considering them does not supply absent facts. The Owens letter addresses the Society's access, not its authors' personal observation. A remand to create a new return mechanism or observer would not correct an established error in this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical conclusion is reached about remedies in another properly supported action. The Court orders no return, certificate cancellation, damages or other substantive relief.

#### Procedural and representative claims retain the concrete-interest connection

**Holding and operative rule:** An asserted ESA procedure does not cure the missing connection between this challenged amendment and a represented member's concrete interest. Assuming that the asserted conservation-permit procedure exists, a claimant need not prove that proper procedure certainly changes the ultimate agency decision, but must still show the threatened personal interest that the procedure protects; representative litigation does not dispense with that constitutional requirement.

**Authority:** Part III of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices reject these proposed ways around the causal and remedial defects.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan preserves procedural protection for threatened concrete interests without requiring certainty of a different final result. It does not authorize general judicial supervision based on an interest in lawful conservation administration. No individual entitlement to Lota's Milwaukee residence or separate informational right is established here. Assuming the asserted section 1539 procedure for this analysis does not convert that section's permission into a found statutory command. Brown Group preserves concrete injury, traceability and redressability for represented members; its displacement of Hunt's prudential participation limit through WARN's express authorization supplies no ESA exemption. The procedure asserted in this case does not connect the later amendment to the completed transfer or establish likely benefit to the zoo observer. The Court therefore need not construe the commercial-activity provision or the asserted permit duty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether section 1539 actually imposes the claimed procedure, the commercial-activity interpretation, the unchallenged CITES certificate, the abandoned transfer claim and Hawthorn's Chaney objection remain undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife (June 12, 1992): applies its controlling elements, stage-specific evidence and concrete-interest/procedural-right rules while preserving reasonable inferences and cognizable personal aesthetic use. Its three-Justice absent-agency discussion is not made controlling.
- Church of Scientology of California v. United States (November 16, 1992): applies the sufficiency of meaningful partial relief; distinguishes a constraint on a third party that supplies no demonstrated benefit to the claimant.
- United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group (May 13, 1996): preserves represented members' constitutional standing requirements; its WARN-specific displacement of Hunt's prudential participation condition is not extended to an ESA exemption.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333: retains the need for a member with constitutional standing.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter (June 29, 1995): its actual-injury and statutory-source distinctions remain; its habitat-harm holding and separate deference rationale do not decide transportation permission or human standing here.

#### Law After Decision

The Court applies constitutional causation, redressability and procedural-standing rules to this certificate challenge. Actual injury is assumed and unresolved; the discussion of observing a particular animal does not find an injured member or independently decide the suit. No ESA commercial-activity or permit-procedure construction is adopted. Jurisdictional dismissal does not decide a genuinely different claim supported by adequate standing facts and does not reopen the abandoned transfer count.

### 1997-02-18 - General Motors Corp. v. Tracy; No. 95-1232.

Authority: [Record](../records/General_Motors_v_Tracy_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The assessed purchaser may bring this challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A purchaser directly liable for a tax has standing to challenge an exemption that allegedly burdens its interstate purchases. The adverse assessment supplies the purchaser's own economic injury, caused by the challenged tax rule and redressable through lawful relief from that assessment, whether or not payment has been established.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** General Motors contests a charge imposed on its own purchases. Its injury does not become another seller's claim merely because the tax also affects competing suppliers. Bacchus recognizes a taxed purchaser's ability to challenge a discriminatory exemption; Fulton likewise examines the taxpayer's own liability although the distinction concerns another market participant. Relief from an unlawful assessment would remove the asserted burden. Ohio's seller-only objection therefore fails. Standing establishes access to review, not the truth of the claimed comparison between the sellers. The Court does not find an assessment amount or prior payment and orders no refund on the strength of standing alone.

**Precedent treatment:** Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias supplies purchaser standing; Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner confirms that the claimant may contest its own tax burden without being the disfavored seller. Their merits and remedial boundaries remain.

##### II. This record does not establish the asserted discrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio's exemption for sales by regulated local distribution companies does not violate the Commerce Clause on this record, because their compulsory rate, reliability and service obligations materially distinguish their regulated supply business from independent marketers' commodity sales. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence of those offerings where the record does not establish a distinct industrial-sales category separated from the obligations relied upon; the holding confers no general immunity on utilities or all of their competitive transactions.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Stevens do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must examine the practical competitive relationship, rather than accept a utility label or the fact that local marketers also pay the tax. West Lynn requires examination of an integrated fiscal preference; Carbone requires attention to the actual service market. Here the favored sellers must maintain regulated supply and meet reliability and human-needs obligations rather than select only profitable customers. Those duties materially affect their offering. General Motors bought virtually all its gas from interstate marketers and often obtained local delivery, but the record gives little detail about the industrial market and does not establish the asserted unregulated negotiated LDC sales during the assessment period. The common gas commodity does not by itself remove the established service differences.

The objection that duties in a captive market cannot justify preference in a separate competitive market would require considering such a supported separation. This record does not establish it. The Court neither finds that every industrial sale funds a particular public duty nor presumes that any alternative would endanger service. The 1996 enactment cannot prove the character of 1986–1990 purchases. Oregon Waste and Fulton continue to require demanding justification and actual tax equivalence once discrimination is established; no compensatory defense is adopted here. A materially different proved sales category remains for its own case. No independent incidental-burden balancing claim or congressional-authorization theory is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** West Lynn's integrated-operation rule and Carbone's practical service-market inquiry apply without categorical utility immunity. Oregon Waste's discrimination-and-justification rule and Fulton's cumulative compensatory conditions remain unchanged; the Court does not reach justification after rejecting the asserted comparison.

##### III. The economic classification has a rational basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio may distinguish regulated local distribution companies from independent natural-gas marketers under ordinary economic equal-protection review because the companies' actual rate and service duties rationally relate to the tax classification. Competitive overlap does not eliminate that legitimate distinction, and this rational-basis holding does not reduce the independent Commerce Clause requirements.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Stevens reserve this question on their proposed Commerce Clause dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits an ordinary economic classification when a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis supports it, without requiring perfect fit or formal legislative findings. Ohio's rate, reliability and service obligations supply such a basis here. An industrial buyer's opportunity to purchase from either class does not make it irrational to distinguish the regulated service business from independent marketers. The Court does not equate rationality with proof that utility taxes exactly offset the challenged charge. Nor does the Court attribute this analysis to the Ohio court, which treated equal protection as submerged in its Commerce Clause discussion. The ordinary economic challenge fails on this separate ground; independent constitutional prohibitions retain their force.

**Precedent treatment:** FCC v. Beach Communications supplies the ordinary economic-classification rule and its limits; it does not displace Commerce Clause scrutiny.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias: applied to the assessed purchaser's own standing; local-product tax preferences remain subject to its substantive rule.
- West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy: its examination of integrated fiscal operation remains controlling; the present record differs in the established obligations of the compared service businesses.
- C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown: its practical service-market comparison is applied; neither a nominal utility classification nor equal taxation of local outsiders is sufficient by itself.
- Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality: the discrimination trigger and demanding justification survive unchanged; no new public-service exception is created.
- Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner: preserves all three cumulative compensatory-tax requirements and lawful fact-dependent relief; no exact compensation or payment is found here.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc.: supplies ordinary economic rational-basis review, separately from the Commerce Clause.

#### Law After Decision

The regulated service differences defeat the particular Commerce Clause comparison established here; no universal utility exemption or new evidentiary presumption follows. The economic classification independently survives equal protection. A taxpayer may challenge its own assessment even when the alleged competitive discrimination affects suppliers. The existing discrimination, compensation and conditional tax-remedy rules remain in force, and a materially different proved industrial-sales category is unresolved.

### 1997-02-19 - Auer v. Robbins; No. 95-897.

Authority: [Record](../records/Auer_v_Robbins_merits_1997-02-19.md).

#### Holdings

#### The Board's local relationship does not make it an arm of Missouri

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board is not an arm of Missouri entitled to the State's immunity in these actions because its combined financial, budgetary and operational relationship is local. State creation, statutory regulation and gubernatorial appointments do not alone make a separate public body the State; treasury exposure carries substantial weight alongside institutional control rather than serving as an indispensable sole condition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice agrees with the threshold resolution only.

**Controlling explanation:** Hess requires attention to the actual entity rather than its governmental label, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes local public bodies from the State itself. Missouri created and regulates the Board and the governor appoints four members, with the mayor serving as the fifth. Those controls matter, but the record's municipal financing, city budget and appropriation role, limits requiring city approval and local operational relationship establish the relevant character of this Board. No single appointment or financial fact substitutes for that combined inquiry. Because the Board is not a state arm, the Court need not decide whether litigation conduct effected an authorized waiver, whether Congress validly abrogated state immunity for this claim, or whether an officer route would permit the requested relief. Seminole Tribe supplies no categorical Article I answer to those unnecessary questions.

#### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Fixed salary reasonably distinguishes exempt compensation from pay varying with work quality or quantity. Congress's delegation includes public employment. Disciplinary needs do not make the criterion unreasonable or establish an exception. Major-safety rules prevent serious danger to the plant or other employees; residency does not qualify. Specified absences and furloughs differ from general discipline. No rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith major-safety penalties retain the narrow scope stated above.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

#### The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds

**Holding and operative rule:** The responsible Secretary's fair and considered interpretation of the salary regulation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Presentation in an amicus brief does not alone disqualify that interpretation, but supplies no automatic authority to an agency lawyer, power to amend operative text or deference to new factual assertions about this employer.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice proposes a different interpretive rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Thomas Jefferson University applies Seminole Rock's bounded own-regulation rule; Stinson preserves the difference between interpreting a rule and contradicting or amending it. Martin requires attention to the statutory allocation of administrative responsibility, while Bowen excludes unsupported litigation rationalizations. The Secretary's position here interprets the actual salary and correction text and is consistent with the supported administrative treatment, including the correction letter. It is a fair and considered legal construction, not an employer-specific factual determination resulting from a Department investigation. Its amicus form does not make it an impermissible invention or itself establish its correctness. The canon favoring narrow construction of exemptions does not prevent the Secretary from resolving actual regulatory ambiguity within the congressional delegation. Courts still determine whether the reading fits the text and lawful authority; they do not accept the agency's characterization of unproved facts merely because its legal construction controls.

#### Practical exposure requires an actual practice or an effective particular policy

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The salary text concerns real susceptibility to reductions, not only deductions already made and not every abstract possibility listed in a broad disciplinary code. An effective policy can alter compensation arrangements before the first deduction; an undifferentiated range of sanctions governing exempt and nonexempt workers does not itself communicate that these salaried officers will receive impermissible deductions. Guzy's negotiated two-day residency suspension instead of termination was treated below as an exceptional event. The later rule made termination the exclusive residency sanction; it did not repeal every disciplinary authorization. That episode alone does not establish recurring deductions or a general significant likelihood.

The officers' argument that the manual itself defeats any salary agreement rests on the broader legal construction the Court rejects. Their additional examples, witness-understanding contentions and related evidentiary-burden submission remain disputed submissions, not grounds for a new blanket factual or forfeiture finding. The Court adopts no numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or universal employer-compliance presumption, and directs no general factual reopening. Ordinary preservation and mandate rules retain their force.

#### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The text separates two eligibility alternatives from two cumulative acts of correction. Intentionality does not defeat the other-than-lack-of-work alternative. Nor does the text require payment immediately upon discovery. It nevertheless requires actual reimbursement and a promise; eligibility alone accomplishes neither. The correction mechanism does not convert a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. Deductions generally made when work is unavailable indicate no intention to pay on a salary basis and defeat exemption during that period.

The lower opinion quoted both conditions and required reimbursement, but its formal disposition did not expressly establish a remand implementation proceeding. This Court therefore states the operative condition in its own mandate: on return, the lower courts must secure both required acts before disposing of the affected claim on the ground of corrected exemption. Neither completed payment nor a completed promise is found. If the employer does not perform, it cannot rely on restored exemption, and lawful compensation consequences remain to be determined. No new correction condition, fixed compliance timetable, automatic overtime amount or damages award is added.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. and Mt. Healthy are applied to the Board's actual local relationship; treasury exposure remains important without becoming the exclusive condition of immunity.
- Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida remains limited to its actual holdings and unresolved State-amenability rationale. No waiver, abrogation or retrospective officer-remedy rule is added.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala and Bowles v. Seminole Rock retain controlling weight for a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation, with text, consistency, lawful authority and actual-ground limits intact.
- Stinson v. United States preserves authoritative interpretation within, rather than amendment of, the operative rule; its Sentencing Commission setting is not mechanically transferred to factual assertions here.
- Martin v. OSHRC supplies attention to actual interpretive responsibility and reasonableness; Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital preserves the exclusion of unsupported litigation rationalizations.

#### Law After Decision

The Board may be subjected to these FLSA proceedings without deciding the State's own immunity. Salary basis remains a valid component of the public executive/administrative exemption. Practical exposure, actual deductions and correction are distinct inquiries, and every stated regulatory exception retains its conditions. The own-regulation controlling-weight rule remains law; the Chief Justice's proposed replacement receives no additional join. A correction-dependent exemption requires both reimbursement and the future promise, secured through the present mandate. Duties and the former district award are not reopened.

### 1997-02-18 - General Motors Corp. v. Tracy; No. 95-1232.

Authority: [Record](../records/General_Motors_v_Tracy_merits_1997-02-18.md).

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Part I on General Motors's own assessment injury. He would reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the tax on the competitive commodity-gas purchases and remand for nondiscriminatory treatment. His comparison focuses on the transaction bought by a noncaptive industrial customer: LDCs and interstate marketers can compete to supply that commodity even though LDCs also serve captive customers. West Lynn's examination of the actual fiscal preference and Oregon Waste's rule against unjustified discrimination require a connection between the favored transaction and the service burden said to justify it. In his view, a blanket seller exemption lacks that connection on this record. This is a disagreement over the legal significance of the established competitive commodity purchase, not a finding that particular unregulated special contracts existed. He would preserve local-distribution regulation and fitted support for documented backup, low-income, credit or distribution obligations through nondiscriminatory means, including a neutral subsidy, cost-based credit or exemption confined to regulated bundled service. State courts would select lawful consequent tax relief, including a refund only where actual payment and governing remedial law support it, or a lawful neutral adjustment. He would not decide equal protection.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Part I but would vacate and remand the Commerce Clause merits for an actual industrial-market comparison. West Lynn requires examination of how the linked fiscal scheme operates; Carbone prevents equal treatment of local and interstate outsiders from curing a preference for a favored local service. Regulated obligations are material, but in his view do not justify closing the inquiry into sales competing for noncaptive buyers. He would not find unregulated contracts, calculate cross-subsidies or conclusively invalidate the exemption on this sparse record. If the comparison establishes discrimination, Ohio must satisfy the existing justification rule; any compensatory defense must meet Fulton's actual-burden, approximate-equivalence and equivalent-events requirements. He would reserve equal protection and order neither a particular subsidy nor a predetermined refund.

### 1997-02-19 - Auer v. Robbins; No. 95-897.

Authority: [Record](../records/Auer_v_Robbins_merits_1997-02-19.md).

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins validity, practical exposure and correction, but would replace the controlling-weight component of Thomas Jefferson University and the corresponding general Seminole Rock rule. He acknowledges that he joined TJU and that it already protects plain text, consistency, explanation and the actual agency ground. His disagreement concerns the remaining proposition that the administering agency's selection among permissible meanings controls because it wrote and administers the regulation.

He would have courts determine meaning independently using ordinary interpretive tools. If genuine uncertainty remains, an authoritative and considered agency interpretation should receive special persuasive weight when it is consistent or adequately explained, fairly noticed, within delegated authority, grounded in relevant expertise and more than a restatement of the statute. Weight would follow those reasons, rather than agency authorship alone. An interpretation may neither enlarge statute or regulation nor impose liability without the notice required by law. Agency amicus participation neither makes its reading controlling nor automatically disqualifies it as litigation invention.

In his view, delegation to prescribe an exemption differs from the judicial responsibility to determine the adopted rule's meaning. Expertise may persuade, and an actual delegation may assign policy discretion, without every ambiguity transferring interpretive authority. He accepts the Secretary's practical-exposure reading independently because it fits the text, salary concept and the distinction between an abstract sanction, an effective policy and an actual practice. He likewise accepts the correction provision's alternatives and mandatory reimbursement and future promise. He does not reach the separately asserted affirmative-burden contention because he regards it as inadequately preserved.

His proposed change would preserve lawful delegated policy choices, operative regulatory text, ordinary reasoned-decisionmaking requirements, review of the agency's actual ground and the earlier decisions' independent substantive holdings. He would not reopen TJU's completed reimbursement judgment or declare its actual construction substantively wrong. Statute-specific allocations of authority require separate examination. Concrete reliance involving settled transactions, compensation arrangements and fairly communicated obligations deserves weight; an administrative preference for controlling deference is not itself that reliance. This record identifies no concrete reliance requiring a different result where independent interpretation reaches the same salary construction and correction mechanism. He makes no finding about reliance across other programs, authorizes no general reopening, and leaves distinct retrospective-liability claims to their applicable notice, finality and remedial rules. This proposed doctrinal change is noncontrolling.
