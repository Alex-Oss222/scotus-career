- **Question Presented:** Whether Title IV-D of the Social Security Act creates individual federal rights enforceable under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, including a right to compel a state child-support program to achieve systemwide substantial compliance.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the mothers may enforce Title IV-D through § 1983 and whether Congress displaced that remedy through the federal oversight scheme.
- **Legal Field:** Section 1983, Spending Clause statutes, child-support enforcement, and federal remedies.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment for Arizona and held broadly that families entitled to Title IV-D services possessed enforceable rights to substantial compliance.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Arizona's director asks the Court to reverse and dismiss. The mothers seek affirmance and systemic declaratory and injunctive relief.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Five mothers alleged that Arizona failed to establish paternity, locate absent parents, obtain support orders, and collect payments because of staffing shortages, backlogs, and structural defects. Their complaint invoked Title IV-D as a whole and requested an injunction requiring substantial compliance throughout all program operations. The statute contains detailed duties, federal audits, corrective plans, and funding penalties, but the lower court did not identify which particular provisions created which individual rights.

### Issue 1: Identification of enforceable Title IV-D rights

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Livadas v. Bradshaw, 512 U.S. 107 (1994), distinguishes a concrete federal right enforceable under § 1983 from preemption alone and preserves the separation among statutory duty, cause of action, defendant, and remedy. No simulated holding treats a complex funding statute as an indivisible source of rights.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered title governs Title IV-D. The supplied statutory-interpretation and remedies principles require rights-creating language, an identified beneficiary, a concrete duty, and separate consideration of the enforcement scheme.
- **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Title IV-D; Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority; Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association; Suter v. Artist M.; Pennhurst.

### Issue 2: Statutory displacement of § 1983

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Livadas holds that Congress may foreclose § 1983 expressly or through a comprehensive and incompatible remedial scheme, but administrative oversight alone does not automatically displace a concrete federal right.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No exact registered doctrine is identified. The cause-of-action inquiry remains separate from whether the underlying provision creates a right.
- **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Title IV-D's audit, corrective-action, and funding-reduction provisions; Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association; Smith v. Robinson.
- **Open Point:** Whether a specific Title IV-D provision carries its own incompatible remedy cannot be answered until the provision and claim are identified.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Application of Suter’s provision-specific holdings

Simulated Suter v. Artist M. (1992) has six direct joins rejecting the asserted §671(a)(15) reasonable-efforts and §671(a)(9) reporting rights under §1983 and their separate implied-action routes. It unanimously rejects a blanket disposition of the independent §671(a)(16) case-plan/review claim, leaving that claim to provision-specific adjudication. Suter therefore cuts against treating Title IV-D as a single enforceable promise of systemwide substantial compliance, but also against dismissing every concrete entitlement simply because it appears in a state-plan statute. It did not hold that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right.
