### 1997-01-14 - Castano v. American Tobacco Co.; Fifth Circuit No. 95-30725; 84 F.3d 734; interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b).

Authority: [Record](../records/Castano_v_American_Tobacco_Co_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. All four holdings command nine votes.

##### I. Certification requires a demonstrated legal and evidentiary basis

**Holding and operative rule.** Before certifying a Rule 23(b)(3) class, a court must determine from the actual claims, governing law and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and class adjudication is superior to the available alternatives, together with satisfaction of Rule 23(a). Conditional certification does not permit postponing that showing, and a claim's novelty neither dispenses with rigorous analysis nor categorically bars certification until individual verdicts exist.

**Controlling explanation.** Falcon requires rigorous examination of the Rule's prerequisites. Eisen forbids awarding or denying certification merely because the court predicts success on the merits; it does not make the inquiry pleadings-only when the substantive elements determine whether a common trial can work. The proponents must identify what the class proceeding would decide and how that work connects to remaining claims and defenses. An untested liability theory makes that explanation especially necessary, but the Rule contains no tort-maturation requirement. Common evidence of manufacturer conduct cannot itself establish that the resulting questions predominate over the unresolved legal and individual work. Administrative convenience cannot replace a prerequisite. Matsushita preserves constitutionally adequate representation as distinct from notice or opt-out rights, and Richards preserves the lawful basis needed to bind absent persons; neither supplies a finding of actual adequacy or conflict in this case. A renewed proposal must satisfy the representation requirements as well as predominance and superiority.

##### II. The present core-liability class cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule.** This nationwide core-liability certification fails Rule 23(b)(3) because the court deferred the material state-law inquiry and did not show how common issues, individual addiction, reliance, causation and defenses would fit into lawful, manageable adjudication. Differences among state laws do not automatically prohibit every multistate class, but proponents must demonstrate materially common answers or workable groupings rather than assume uniformity or rely on subclass labels.

**Controlling explanation.** Shutts prevents applying a single State's law to materially disconnected claims merely for convenience; it does not select the governing law here. The pleaded fraud, misrepresentation, negligence, warranty and consumer-protection claims require examination of their actual duties, defenses and burdens. The District Court certified core conduct while excluding injury, causation, reliance, defenses and individual compensatory damages. Without explaining how the common answers would materially resolve the legally varied claims, the court could not establish predominance or make a grounded superiority comparison. The proponents' strongest point is that common manufacturer evidence is substantial and individual litigation expensive. But an imagined future trial structure cannot supply the missing showing for the order entered. Workable legal groupings or genuinely separable common issues may matter on a different proposal. The Court requires a credible account of claims and proof, not a universal separate document bearing the title trial plan. It does not decide the full relationship between Rule 23(b)(3) and (c)(4), including whether issue certification may ever proceed when an entire cause of action fails an action-wide predominance inquiry.

##### III. The punitive component and superiority finding also fail

**Holding and operative rule.** The certified punitive-ratio component fails because its state-law predicates, burdens, relation to liability and coordination with individual proceedings were not demonstrated; those same gaps defeat the present superiority finding. Settlement pressure from a procedure that bypasses defenses or detaches punitive assessment from established claims may reinforce concrete certification defects, but aggregate size or ordinary settlement pressure alone is not a categorical bar.

**Controlling explanation.** The certification order incorporated important elements of the proposed plan, including its punitive-ratio arrangement, without adopting that plan in full. The court did not establish what one jury could lawfully decide about punishment before the remaining individualized liability work, or how later proceedings would use that decision consistently with each governing law. Predicted savings from millions of trials cannot substitute for identifying the work the common trial would actually accomplish. The manufacturers' concern about repeated jury determinations underscores the need for that account, but repetition of evidence is not automatically reexamination of a decided fact. The Court rests on Rule 23 and does not decide the constitutionality of every punitive ratio, issue class or bifurcated trial. The proposed Liggett settlement does not establish a completed release or make the certification controversy moot. No manufacturer liability, individual recovery, punitive amount, Article III severance question, new Seventh Amendment rule or complete resolution of every asserted Erie objection is decided.

##### IV. Decertification does not require a categorical ban on reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The rejection of the present core-liability and punitive certification is affirmed, but the mandate is vacated insofar as it requires dismissal of the class pleading and bars reconsideration merely because the tort has not matured through individual trials. The case is remanded for ordinary proceedings on individual claims or consideration of a materially narrower, supported class proposal that independently satisfies all applicable Rule 23 requirements; the existing class remains uncertified.

**Controlling explanation.** The lower court identified decisive defects in this proposal, then treated the asserted novelty of the tort as making correction on remand categorically unavailable. The Rule requires a present showing, not a quota of prior trials or an existing crisis in individual litigation. Rejecting an unsupported class does not decide whether a materially different proposal can establish its prerequisites. The proponents retain their burden; remand creates no presumption of certification, discovery entitlement, preferred trial plan or deadline. It adjudicates no individual claim on the merits. The separate denial of Rule 23(b)(2) equitable and medical-monitoring certification was not cross-appealed and is not reopened.

#### Precedent Treatment

- General Telephone Co. of Southwest v. Falcon, 457 U.S. 147: applied to require rigorous determination of the actual certification prerequisites, including where the inquiry overlaps substantive issues.
- Eisen v. Carlisle & Jacquelin, 417 U.S. 156: preserved as forbidding certification based merely on a preliminary prediction of merits success; it does not forbid the substantive inquiry needed to determine whether class adjudication can work.
- Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts, 472 U.S. 797: applied against assuming one State's substantive law for materially disconnected claims; no particular choice-of-law result is directed.
- Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996: its adequate-representation requirement remains distinct from notice and opt-out rights; its judgment-recognition holding does not itself decide prospective certification or the actual adequacy of this class.
- Richards v. Jefferson County, June 10, 1996: preserved for the lawful basis necessary to bind absent persons; it does not constitutionalize every Rule 23 defect or resolve the reserved certification questions.

#### Law After Decision

Rule 23 requires the concrete law-and-proof showing before certification. Neither novelty nor ordinary aggregation pressure creates an independent exclusion, and differing state laws require demonstrated analysis rather than an automatic national-class prohibition. The present certification is defeated by its own missing prerequisites. The partial vacatur preserves lawful reconsideration without reinstating or presuming any class, modifying individual substantive burdens, or creating a federal tobacco tort.

### 1997-01-14 - Castano v. American Tobacco Co.; Fifth Circuit No. 95-30725; 84 F.3d 734; interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b).

Authority: [Record](../records/Castano_v_American_Tobacco_Co_merits_1997-01-14.md).

The case returns through the Court of Appeals for ordinary proceedings. Individual litigation may continue, and a materially narrower supported certification proposal may be considered under Rule 23(a) and the applicable subdivision of (b). The present core-liability and punitive components remain uncertified. The Rule 23(b)(2) denial is not reopened, and the mandate supplies no decision on a manufacturer's liability, a completed Liggett release or an individual's recovery.
