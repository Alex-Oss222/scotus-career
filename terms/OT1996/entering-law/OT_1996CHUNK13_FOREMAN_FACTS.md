# Foreman — objective source receipt for neutral preflight

Source: Cornell's primary opinion transcription, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-987.ZPC.html, record recitations only. The source's Supreme Court adjudication, reasoning and lineup are withheld from this receipt.

Texas authorizes counties to appoint one election judge for each precinct. Those officials supervise election-day voting. Dallas County changed selection procedures in 1983 and several later years; the successive methods used partisan-affiliation formulas. The latest identified change was in 1996.

The three-judge district court denied injunctive relief and subsequently dismissed the complaint under Rule 12(b)(6). The two appeals concern those respective rulings. The source does not give their exact entry dates or characterize the earlier request as a temporary restraining order. No such characterization should be inferred. Section 1253 permits qualifying interlocutory injunction appeals; nonfinality alone therefore does not establish absence of its jurisdiction. The effect of a subsequent final judgment on a pending interlocutory appeal is a distinct question under Shaffer v. Carter, 252 U.S. 37, 44 (1920).

The 1985 submission contained the recodified election code and a thirty-page change summary. For election judges, the described change concerned appointment beginning dates and duration. Neither the recodified text nor the State's explanation identified the particular county partisan-selection formulas. The parties agree that the record does not establish the county's appointment procedure on November 1, 1972. No baseline finding, particular later preclearance, or completed-election remedy is supplied by this receipt.
