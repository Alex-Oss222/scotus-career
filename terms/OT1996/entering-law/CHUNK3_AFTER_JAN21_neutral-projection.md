### 1996-10-21 — Department of State v. Legal Assistance for Vietnamese Asylum Seekers, Inc.; No. 95-1521

Authority: [Record](../records/Department_of_State_v_Legal_Assistance_for_Vietnamese_Asylum_Seekers_post_grant_remand_1996-10-21.md).

#### Holdings

##### Reconsideration after the intervening visa-processing enactment

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 633 directly addresses the processing-location premise of the judgment below and creates a reasonable probability that reconsideration will alter an outcome-material statutory premise or continuing relief. Independent consideration of the equities warrants vacatur and a confined remand, without deciding the old-law merits or the new provision's final construction and temporal reach.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls. The Court applies the reasonable-probability standard; a reasonable possibility alone is not its rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the new enactment warrants reconsideration because it addresses the very activity regulated by the judgment. The D.C. Circuit applied the nationality and residence nondiscrimination provision to the place of immigrant-visa processing. Congress has now added a clause concerning the Secretary's authority over processing procedures and locations. This is an enacted legal development, not simply a Government request for a different outcome.

Lawrence v. Chater supplies the separate requirements of outcome-material probability and independent equitable judgment. Both are met. Review remains timely; the amendment followed the appellate decisions; and the parties have submitted their opposing interpretations. The applicants have already endured substantial delay. Their asserted danger of forcible repatriation, allegedly following the end of a Hong Kong undertaking upon this Court's disposition, deserves concrete weight. But neither dismissal of this writ nor remand establishes continuing foreign protection, and leaving the judgment intact would not settle the amendment's effect on actual processing relief. A prompt, bounded remand permits the new clause, the asserted matured claims and the independent claims to be considered together.

Landgraf and Rivers preserve the distinction between new consequences for completed conduct and regulation of future activity. The Court does not decide that an injunction is necessarily prospective in every relevant sense, that corrective legislation necessarily reaches past conduct, or that the pending case automatically receives the new provision's asserted effect. The lower courts must resolve those questions under governing law. Congress's enactment alone neither moots the independent APA and constitutional claims nor establishes that any applicant must lose.

**Precedent treatment:** Lawrence is applied to an actual intervening statute without enlarging its agency-development holding. Landgraf and Rivers remain controlling on their distinct temporal-reach questions; the remand decides no conflict between their previously published rationales.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Lawrence v. Chater:** Applies its reasonable-probability and independent-equities requirements; the receiving court retains responsibility for the development's actual effect.
- **Landgraf v. USI Film Products:** Preserves provision-specific temporal analysis and rejects disposition based merely on a pending-case or injunction label.
- **Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc.:** Preserves the rule that a corrective legislative purpose does not itself establish retrospective reach.

#### Law After Decision

The decision applies the existing reconsideration rule to §633 and leaves its substantive construction open. The amendment is effective legislation; that fact does not decide its application to completed conduct or reparative relief. The APA and constitutional theories remain live for lawful consideration. No asylum status, visa eligibility, entitlement to Hong Kong processing, damages, enforceable foreign undertaking, repatriation event or specific interim deadline is adjudicated. Courts may consider prompt interim relief within their authority; none is promised or ordered by this disposition.

### 1996-11-04 — California v. Roy, No. 95-2025; October Term 1996, chunk 1.

Authority: [Record](../records/California_v_Roy_summary_review_1996-11-04.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The existing official appellate party may seek review

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1254(1) requires a petitioner who was a party in the court of appeals; on this record, that court's repeated identification of the Attorney General as an official-capacity respondent and the timely petition expressly naming the incumbent in that capacity satisfy the requirement. The Court accepts the existing official party's identity despite the contrary earlier custodial pleadings; it neither adds a party nor holds that a caption, counsel's participation or a State's interest invariably establishes party status.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejecting the objection and reaching the case, without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the official litigant identified by the court of appeals may seek review through the incumbent Attorney General. Scofield requires a party below; Perini keeps that statutory requirement distinct from injury. California's interest in its conviction alone would not suffice.

The amended habeas petition names Gomez and Borg, the answer identifies them as proper respondents, and the subsequent order substitutes Merkle for Borg. Those documents establish custodial participation and replacement. They do not supply an appellate ruling removing the Attorney General's office. The June 1995 panel opinion, September rehearing order and April 1996 en banc opinion repeatedly name Van de Kamp as a respondent. The Court accepts that appellate identification on these competing documents. Lungren's timely, expressly official petition identifies the incumbent of the same office.

FRAP 43(c)(2) and FRCP 25(d)(2) permit designation of an existing official-capacity party by title, subject to the court's authority to require a name. Their corresponding automatic-substitution provisions operate when an existing official-capacity party leaves office during the respective appellate proceeding or action. They disregard an immaterial misnomer, and omission of a formal substitution order does not defeat substitution. These rules continue an existing party; they do not create one. The Court does not assume that the change of Attorney General occurred during this appeal or this Court's proceeding. The appellate identification is the decisive predicate, not an imagined intervention or succession order.

Smith v. Barry supports reading a timely document for the litigant it actually identifies, while retaining mandatory party information. No custodian is added to the petition under Rule 12.4, and Rule 35.3 does not establish original party status below. Whether the People separately qualify is unnecessary to decide. The Attorney General's appearance as counsel does not independently establish party status.

**Precedent treatment:** Scofield and Perini retain their actual-party requirement; Smith is applied to identification in a timely filing without dispensing with that requirement.

##### II. This personal-intent omission permits harmless-error review under Chapman

**Holding and operative rule:** The preserved omission of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery is a constitutional trial error subject to harmlessness review, not an error requiring automatic reversal merely because an element was omitted. For this personal-intent violation properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict; neither collateral posture nor absence of accompanying prosecutorial argument substitutes a weaker burden.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously. Agreement on this category and burden does not establish agreement on the method or result of harmlessness review.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the jury's responsibility to determine the defendant's required intent remains protected, while the effect of this instructional omission may be examined. Gaudin requires jury determination of an offense element but does not classify every failure to submit an element as structural. Sullivan addresses a different defect: an unconstitutional reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings leaves no valid reasonable-doubt verdict to examine. The omission here does not destroy that standard throughout the charge.

O'Neal requires the State's Chapman showing for its preserved personal-intent category. That case involved combined instructions and prosecutorial argument permitting conviction without the defendant's own required intent. This charge permits the same constitutional failure by omitting the purpose to facilitate robbery. An additional prosecutorial statement is not the basis of the protection and supplies no reason to withhold it here. Knowledge of the perpetrator's unlawful purpose and affirmative assistance were required, so mere presence was insufficient; neither knowledge nor assistance is legally identical to the omitted purpose.

The State retains the burden. Roy need not prove innocence, and the reviewing court may not supply the missing intent itself. This decision does not adopt Chapman for every constitutional habeas claim, reopen a resolved exhaustion issue, or decide the temporal operation of AEDPA.

**Precedent treatment:** Gaudin's jury-allocation rule is applied and its structural-error reservation respected; Sullivan's rule remains intact at its stated scope; O'Neal's protected personal-intent category applies to the omission without accompanying argument; Chapman's actual-contribution burden remains the governing burden.

##### III. Necessary equivalence is sufficient, but not the exclusive permissible inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Chapman, the State may establish that this omission did not contribute to the actual verdict through supported whole-record inferences as well as findings necessarily embracing the omitted purpose; it may not substitute evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction, equate knowledge plus assistance with purpose, or infer purpose from general mental capacity. Under Yates, the court must examine whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting the proposed inference; if the omission kept the relevant personal-purpose question from the jury so that the inference cannot reliably address its actual verdict, strong evidence alone does not carry the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, controls by five direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports the remand on his separately stated whole-record Chapman ground but does not join Part III's further analysis. Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg dissent from the remand. No combination of separate opinions or narrower-ground synthesis supplies this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the inquiry concerns what the error did to the verdict actually returned. Yates requires examination of the evidence the instructions put before the jury and distinguishes actual contribution from proof sufficient to convict. Necessary equivalence between a finding and the omitted purpose is one sufficient route. It is not invariably the only way to determine the effect of this knowledge-and-assistance charge.

The Ninth Circuit expressly found the omission not harmless under Chapman before applying a separate collateral-review analysis. Remand is warranted because its necessary-findings restriction foreclosed the qualified actual-effect inquiry, not because Chapman was never considered. The charge submitted knowledge, affirmative aid and related mental-state questions. That permits examination of their connection to the actual verdict, without assuming the jury considered the omitted purpose. The receiving court must address the possibility that the omission itself prevented the relevant consideration; inability to establish a reliable connection defeats the State's showing.

The record requires more than an overwhelming-evidence label. Possession of property, wet clothing, assistance testimony and reported statements must be assessed with the verdicts and the conflicts in those accounts. The clothing allocated to McHargue in the lower opinions cannot be treated as undisputed property in Roy's backpack. Hudspeth's impeachment and rebuttal and Hall's reported statements and retraction matter together; no verdict establishes wholesale acceptance or rejection of every witness. The possible inference that Roy helped a companion without intending to facilitate robbery is an inference to assess, not a newly found event. The State's and district court's functional-equivalence conclusions are legal assessments, not express jury findings. These matters remain for the proper inquiry, and the Ninth Circuit may again conclude that the State fails its burden.

**Precedent treatment:** Chapman and Yates are applied together to permit a supported actual-effect inquiry while preserving the jury's role; neither sufficiency alone nor a hypothetical verdict satisfies them. O'Neal's whole-record command remains subject to that actual-verdict requirement.

##### IV. The limited remand preserves the record, argument and remedial boundaries

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is vacated for a qualified Chapman review of the preserved personal-intent claim affecting the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions, with District Court proceedings if otherwise proper; no present harmlessness finding follows. If an available violation is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, conditional relief must allow the State a reasonable period to retry those convictions; proper harmlessness permits denial, and neither this remand nor the special-circumstance argument restores reversed punishment, affects the Clark conviction or orders immediate release.

**Authority:** Part III's remand instructions have the five direct joins stated above; Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote and agrees separately with the Chapman and reasonable-retrial remedy. The five-Justice rationale itself controls without enlarging Stone-Zsela's join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that renewed review must apply the proper method to the supported record and respect genuine preservation limits. O'Neal distinguishes a corrected harmlessness inquiry from an automatic writ and requires a reasonable retrial opportunity if an available violation warrants relief. No fixed retrial deadline is set.

Yee distinguishes an additional argument about a preserved question from a new constitutional claim. The special-circumstance argument concerns this preserved harmlessness question, but Roy's objection to its first-instance use still requires consideration. The asserted oral concession is not established, and the objection alone creates no forfeiture.

The instructions must be read together. CALJIC 8.80's intentional-aid requirement applies conditionally to a defendant who was not the actual killer; it is not an unconditional finding of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery. Absence of personal knife use does not itself eliminate every actual-killer theory. CALJIC 8.81.17's robbery-related purpose of the killing differs from Roy's own aiding purpose. The state reversal prevents restoration of the special-circumstance punishment; it does not automatically determine every evidentiary implication of the findings. General specific-intent or diminished-capacity instructions do not silently insert the omitted purpose clause.

The Court therefore adopts neither an automatic special-circumstance cure nor a categorical exclusion of the findings from consideration. It supplies no new trial finding, admissibility ruling on a juror declaration, exhaustion bar, or factual resolution from an unavailable transcript passage. Preservation, independent habeas requirements and any event-date statutory transition remain distinct. This remand addresses the identified method defect, without reviving a resolved gate or ordering new factfinding merely because a source is unavailable.

**Precedent treatment:** Yee preserves the distinction between a new argument and a new claim without compelling disputed first-instance application; O'Neal supplies the conditional-relief and reasonable-retrial rule, without automatic release.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **UAW v. Scofield, 382 U.S. 205:** Applies the requirement that a petitioner under §1254(1) be an actual party below; official identification satisfies it here without permitting review by any interested nonparty.
- **Director, OWCP v. Perini North River Associates, 459 U.S. 297:** Preserves the distinction between statutory party status and injury; a State's enforcement interest alone does not answer the party question.
- **Smith v. Barry:** Applies practical identification in a timely filing while retaining mandatory information about the actual litigant; no late party is added.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury allocation of an offense element and respects its reservation of universal structural-error treatment.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes a particular purpose omission from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard for all findings; its structural rule remains intact.
- **O'Neal v. McAninch:** Applies its State-borne Chapman rule to this preserved personal-intent omission without requiring accompanying argument, and retains conditional relief with a reasonable retrial opportunity. Its unadopted general extension to all ordinary constitutional trial errors does not become law.
- **Chapman v. California:** Retains the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to show that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies the actual-contribution and instructional-focus requirements; the Court rejects both sufficiency alone and an exclusive necessary-findings method for this charge, subject to the stated reliability qualification.
- **Yee v. City of Escondido:** Applies the difference between an additional argument about a preserved question and a new claim without eliminating legitimate preservation and first-instance limits.

#### Law After Decision

Effective November 4, 1996, the defined personal-intent habeas category includes this preserved omission of the defendant's purpose to facilitate robbery. It does not depend on accompanying prosecutorial argument. The State must satisfy Chapman through a permissible inquiry into the actual verdict. Necessary findings may suffice, but the controlling method also permits supported inferences meeting the express Yates condition; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to the actual verdict. Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive actual-findings approach does not control, and Stevens's vote to affirm does not join that approach.

The existing official-party requirement remains unchanged. This record satisfies it through the appellate identification of the Attorney General's office and the timely official petition, without a rule making every caption conclusive. No general Chapman rule for all habeas errors, new innocence gate, automatic special-circumstance cure, exhaustion ruling, statutory-transition ruling or universal structural-error rule is established. No holding on harmlessness of this actual record is made. The conditional-relief rule remains operative if the State fails its showing on an available violation.

### 1996-11-06 — Lopez v. Monterey County; No. 95-1201

Authority: [Record](../records/Lopez_v_Monterey_County_merits_1996-11-06.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–IV, joined by all eight other Justices, control all four holdings. The judgment coalition and full-opinion joins are unanimous; the additional grounds in the separate concurrence are not necessary to these holdings.

##### I. State compulsion does not exempt a covered County's administration

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered political subdivision administers a voting change when its officials give the change operative effect within the subdivision. Section 5 therefore requires Monterey County to obtain preclearance before administering a postbaseline judicial-election change even if noncovered California compels the change and leaves the County no discretion; the duty is the County's and does not require California to preclear its laws in the abstract.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that execution of an electoral change is distinct from enactment of it. Section 5 separately reaches a covered jurisdiction that enacts or seeks to administer a change. A condition of local policy discretion would erase the second route whenever a noncovered State required its covered subdivision to operate the new system. The statute contains no such condition. Monterey County conducts these public judicial elections and gives the countywide electorate operative effect; the voters experience the same change regardless of its legislative origin.

Presley supplies the distinction between a change in voting and a transfer of internal governing duties. Morse confirms the importance of the practice's actual electoral operation while preserving the designated substantive-preclearance process. Neither already decided this local-discretion question, and the Court adopts no general state-delegation test. The holding answers the legal question even assuming California law entirely displaced the County ordinances; it does not decide that disputed state-law characterization. Nor does it impose a statewide submission obligation, determine discriminatory purpose or retrogression, or confer general federal power to redesign California's judiciary.

**Precedent treatment:** Presley is applied to the direct electoral operation; its internal-governance exclusion remains. Morse's bounded functional and statutory-sequencing principles are applied without enlarging its party-convention holding.

##### II. Electorate changes and the limits of prior submission

**Holding and operative rule:** Replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate for municipal judges is a change affecting voting under §§5 and 14(c)(1). A later related submission does not preclear earlier changes that it failed to identify; the district court must determine the precise reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance while treating the earlier unidentified consolidations as unprecleared.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this consolidation changes who elects each judge, rather than merely the work performed after election. At the November 1, 1968 baseline, voters elected the judges of their separate districts. The six ordinances adopted between 1972 and 1983 ultimately placed the judges before one countywide electorate. Presley preserves coverage for that direct alteration of electoral choice. Allen and Perkins supply the same distinction through their treatment of electoral mechanisms and boundaries.

McCain and Clark require an actual, identifiable submission of the change for which preclearance is claimed; ambiguity is not blanket approval of earlier practices. The record establishes that California's 1983 submission did not identify or describe the earlier ordinances. Whether it reached the final 1983 ordinance must be determined from the submission's actual terms. The County cannot obtain approval of the whole consolidation sequence merely by pointing to that later submission. Coverage and lack of preclearance establish the nonuse obligation, not a finding that the system has discriminatory purpose or retrogressive effect.

**Precedent treatment:** Presley, Allen and Perkins retain coverage for direct changes in electoral choice. McCain v. Lybrand and Clark v. Roemer retain their requirements of specific submission and narrow treatment of claimed administrative approval.

##### III. Effective designation and prospective enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4(b)'s administrative coverage determination may not be collaterally retried in this §5 enforcement action; statutory bailout under §4(a) is a distinct route. Delay cannot authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change, although reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on lawfully available retrospective relief or the form of a lawful transition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the County's published designation remains operative in this case. Congress separately restricted review of the coverage determination and provided a judicial bailout process. The County has not obtained release from coverage through that process. Its objection cannot convert this enforcement suit into a collateral retrial of the administrative findings.

Nor does the age of the ordinances excuse their future use without submission. Section 5 conditions administration on preclearance; the passage of time supplies no substitute. Clark enforces that statutory sequence, and Hampton County confirms that elapsed time and electoral implementation do not themselves remove the review obligation. The practical cost of delay warrants a fitted transition, not continued use of the same uncleared governmental plan as an ordinary electoral system. The Court makes no factual bailout determination, awards no damages, and decides no backward-looking relief concerning a completed election, judicial tenure or completed judicial acts.

**Precedent treatment:** Clark and NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission are applied to prospective enforcement; their remedial attention to actual circumstances is preserved. No new general retrospective equitable rule is announced.

##### IV. Enforcement and a genuinely judicial temporary remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** A §5 enforcement court may determine coverage and submission status and enforce nonuse, but may neither grant substantive preclearance nor authorize an unprecleared County or State electoral plan by calling it court ordered. A necessary, temporary plan wholly formulated by the court may operate without separate §5 preclearance only when independent lawful remedial authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary, and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial approval does not change the governmental origin of an electoral plan. Connor distinguishes a wholly judicial remedy; McDaniel prevents a court order from insulating governmental policy choices from submission. Here the district court reinstated substantially the County's at-large system and authorized ordinary six-year terms. It did not formulate a wholly judicial temporary remedy. Clark's reservation for an extreme circumstance supplies no basis on this record: prolonged notice of the problem, abandonment of the County's D.C. preclearance action and ordinary terms do not establish a last-minute necessity. This decision recognizes no broader emergency exception.

Morse and Shaw preserve Congress's separate preclearance channels. The enforcement court cannot decide purpose or effect itself, even in light of the County's stipulation concerning its inability to prove nonretrogression. Administrative difficulty does not enlarge its authority.

The earlier four-division arrangement is a material option for consideration. It retained a single municipal court and received preclearance on March 6, 1995; its terms reportedly end in January 1997. Johnson v. Miller prevents racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate, standing alone, from supplying a constitutional defect. But neither that rule nor preclearance answers the separate objections concerning electoral and jurisdictional linkage, minimum population, divided municipalities and candidate residence, or establishes independent remedial authority. The Court selects no interim plan. Those matters, the exact 1983 submission and any actual officeholder circumstances require lawful determination below.

**Precedent treatment:** Connor and McDaniel preserve the genuinely judicial/governmental-plan distinction; Clark supplies the nonuse rule and bounded emergency reservation; Morse and Shaw preserve the designated channels; Johnson governs the design-only constitutional objection without approving this particular plan.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission:** Distinguishes internal allocations of governing authority from the direct electorate change here; the former exclusion remains intact.
- **Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia:** Applies its bounded concern with actual electoral operation and its separate preclearance channels; no general delegation test or new private-action rule is adopted.
- **Allen v. State Board of Elections:** Applies protection of electoral choice to the changed means of electing judges.
- **Perkins v. Matthews:** Applies coverage of changes in electoral boundaries and the electorate, without equating coverage with discrimination.
- **McCain v. Lybrand:** Requires identification of the submitted change and rejects blanket approval of unidentified earlier practices.
- **Clark v. Roemer:** Enforces nonuse pending preclearance and preserves, without applying, its narrow extreme-circumstance reservation; the present ordinary six-year elections do not qualify.
- **NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission:** Passage of time and implementation do not eliminate the submission requirement; actual circumstances remain relevant to lawful relief.
- **Connor v. Johnson:** Retains the distinction for a wholly judicial remedial plan.
- **McDaniel v. Sanchez:** A court's adoption of governmental policy does not transform it into an exempt judicial plan.
- **Shaw v. Reno, designated-channel holding:** Substantive preclearance remains with the Attorney General or D.D.C.; no enforcement-court substitute is authorized.
- **Johnson v. Miller:** Racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate alone does not establish constitutional injury or trigger strict scrutiny; separate statutory and constitutional objections remain open.

#### Law After Decision

Section 5 now expressly reaches a covered County's nondiscretionary administration of state-mandated voting changes. The election must not proceed under the unprecleared governmental system merely because the State requires it, delay has occurred or a federal court has ordered it used. Existing direct-voting coverage, specific-submission, designated-channel and genuinely judicial-remedy rules remain. The new construction neither places all California law under §5 nor changes the substantive purpose/effect burdens. The temporary divisions remain an option for lawful consideration, with their independent defects and authority unresolved.

### 1996-11-13 — INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang, No. 95-938; October Term 1996.

Authority: [Record](../records/INS_v_Yueh_Shaio_Yang_merits_1996-11-13.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Review under the corrected 1996 transition provisions

**Holding and operative rule.** Review of this existing deportation proceeding remains available under former INA §106 and 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) on November 13, 1996. Corrected IIRIRA §306(c)(1) gives new §242(g) reach across past, pending and future proceedings when that provision commences; it does not advance commencement of the new jurisdictional rule from April 1, 1997, and the other review restrictions establish no present bar on this record.

**Authority.** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies an eight-Justice controlling rationale. Stone-Zsela concurs only in this threshold resolution and does not join its reasons.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court reads the enacted timing instructions together. Section 309(a) sets April 1, 1997 as the general commencement date, expressly excepting §309 and §§303(b)(2), 306(c), 308(d)(2)(D) and 308(d)(5). Corrected §306(c)(1) applies the amendments as provided in §309, then separately extends §242(g) to old as well as new proceedings. The immediate-operation argument relies on those exceptions and the unrestricted reference to pending claims. But the exceptional clause specifies covered proceedings without supplying a replacement commencement date. Its present operation as an applicability instruction has independent work: after commencement, §242(g) reaches proceedings otherwise protected by grandfathering. This reading does not presume that jurisdiction can never change during litigation.

Corrected §309(c)(1), subject to its succeeding provisions, preserves the earlier proceedings and judicial review. No paragraph (2) or (3) agency election is established. Paragraph (4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; it lists §§212(c), 212(h), 212(i), 244 and 245. The 1995 posture supplies no new triggering order, and that list omits former §241(a)(1)(H). Permanent §242(a)(2)(B)(i) instead lists §§212(h), 212(i), 240A, 240B and 245; clause (ii) separately addresses other statutorily discretionary authority, except §208(a) grants. Neither permanent clause governs this event.

Section 306(c)(2) protects existing §106 judgments and orders from invalidation or compelled reconsideration by paragraph (1); it does not independently establish appellate jurisdiction. Section 306(d)'s AEDPA-related criminal amendment supplies no bar without a covered conviction, which this record does not establish. The Court leaves open §242(g)'s scope after commencement, including its three subjects—commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing removal orders—and its exception for review provided in §242. *Landgraf* and *Rivers* supply attention to Congress's actual temporal directions, not a new unanimous adoption of every earlier temporal rationale.

#### II. Eligibility does not exclude the continuing fraud pattern from discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** Former INA §241(a)(1)(H), 8 U.S.C. §1251(a)(1)(H), permits the Attorney General to consider a continuing pattern of immigration fraud when choosing among eligible waiver applicants, even though particular acts are connected with entry. Eligibility does not erase that conduct or compel a waiver; the Court neither permits automatic denial to every eligible applicant because of the qualifying fraud nor decides whether the minimum qualifying fraud alone could sustain a particular individualized denial.

**Authority.** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute makes eligibility and favorable discretion distinct. It reaches covered entry fraud or misrepresentation, whether willful or innocent. An applicant must be the spouse, parent, son or daughter of a citizen or lawful permanent resident, have possessed an immigrant visa or equivalent document, and otherwise have been admissible at entry. The exceptions for labor-certification and immigrant-documentation grounds under §§1182(a)(5)(A) and (7)(A) apply only when those grounds directly resulted from the fraud. The separate exclusion for assistance in Nazi persecution or participation in genocide remains. An actually granted waiver also reaches entry-inadmissibility grounds directly resulting from the fraud. These provisions define eligibility and the consequence of relief; they do not remove conduct from the discretionary decision.

*Jay v. Boyd* supplies the distinction between statutory eligibility and discretionary selection. *INS v. Errico* construed a mandatory predecessor; its family-preservation concern does not convert the later discretionary provision into an entitlement. *National Center for Immigrants' Rights* supports discretion exercised within the statute while preserving required individual consideration.

The Board accepted eligibility and expressly disregarded the initial entry fraud. It considered the wife's false-identity scheme and sham marital arrangements, the later naturalization fraud, further marital arrangements and lack of candor, together with favorable residence, business, property, family and health considerations and Taiwan connections. Those are the Board's findings and assessments. The Ninth Circuit allowed dishonest testimony to count but wrongly excluded collaboration and naturalization conduct because of their connection with entry. The argument that waiver discretion must remain meaningful therefore does not justify its categorical rule. A continuing pattern distinguishes applicants without making every eligible applicant lose. The Court does not reweigh the balance.

#### III. Permissible factors remain subject to reasoned and consistent administration

**Holding and operative rule.** Statutory permission to consider a factor does not excuse arbitrary decisionmaking, pretext, disregard of a binding rule, or an unexplained departure from a genuinely settled agency policy. Here the Board rationally distinguished continuing immigration fraud from the initial entry fraud it disregarded; the record supplies no policy reversal requiring a new administrative balance.

**Authority.** Part III of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court separates statutory authority from the lawfulness of its exercise. *Smiley*, applying the administrative-change principles preserved from *Good Samaritan*, requires examination of the agency's actual reasons, prior guidance and legitimate reliance. A court cannot supply a litigation explanation for an unexplained change. An agency may revise a policy openly with adequate explanation, subject to binding law; the existence of discretion alone supplies no answer.

The Board retained its initial-fraud distinction and explained the significance of the continuing pattern. Its actual reasons defeat the claim that this decision irrationally abandoned that distinction. The Court neither finds a different binding policy nor commands a new explanation for an unproved reversal. The mandate removes the Ninth Circuit's compelled reweighing based on categorical exclusion. Any genuinely remaining preserved objection is for ordinary proceedings within the record and mandate; the Court directs neither a waiver nor its denial, reopening, a new hearing, or a fresh Board balance. This decision concerns the statutory immigration function presented, not classifications outside it.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Landgraf v. USI Film Products* and *Rivers v. Roadway Express*: applied to the actual congressional timing instructions; their distinct temporal rationales are not combined into a broader unanimous framework.
- *Jay v. Boyd*, 351 U.S. 345: applied for eligibility's separation from discretionary selection; it remains authority for that distinction.
- *INS v. Errico*, 385 U.S. 214: distinguished because its mandatory predecessor differs from the discretionary text here; its own construction is not overruled.
- *INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights* (December 16, 1991): applied by analogy for statutory boundaries and legally required individual consideration; its release-condition holding is not transformed into a waiver decision.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* and *Smiley* (June 3, 1996): applied for reasoned administrative interpretation and explained treatment of settled policy and reliance; statutory discretion does not displace those limits.
- *INS v. Doherty* (January 15, 1992): its fractured disposition supplies no controlling substantive reopening rationale for this waiver question; no separate rationale is promoted to Court law.

#### Law After Decision

Effective November 13, 1996, courts may not exclude a continuing immigration-fraud pattern from former §241(a)(1)(H) discretion merely because its acts are connected with entry. The eligibility conditions, the distinct consequence of a granted waiver, meaningful individual discretion and ordinary review of actual agency reasons remain operative. The Court leaves the minimum-fraud-only individualized case unresolved and permits no universal eligibility-based denial. The specific transition construction preserves review at this event; it decides no post-April §242(g) scope question. No waiver is awarded or denied by this Court, and the agency balance is not judicially redone.

### 1996-11-18 — Ohio v. Robinette; No. 95-891

Authority: [Record](../records/Ohio_v_Robinette_merits_1996-11-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Review of the interwoven federal and state grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** When a state judgment fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven with federal law and does not clearly state an independently sufficient state ground, the Court may review its federal basis. Ohio's intertwined constitutional explanation supplies no clear independent ground barring review here; Ohio remains free to supply greater protection on genuinely independent state-law grounds.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by six direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees to exercise review without joining this explanation; Ginsburg and Stevens accept review in their separate writings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's reference to its own Constitution does not end the jurisdictional inquiry. Michigan v. Long supplies the rule for opinions that rest on federal law or interweave federal and state law without a clear independent basis. Arizona v. Evans retains that rule. The Ohio opinion invokes both provisions and develops the suppression analysis with federal authorities; it does not announce that an independent Ohio ground alone sustains the judgment. Federal review therefore lies. Deciding the federal question neither erases the Ohio provision nor prevents the Ohio courts from adopting a clearly independent state rule in further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Long and Evans are applied without changing the independent-state-ground rule or creating an exception to the ordinary final-judgment requirement.

##### II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer's uncommunicated investigative motive or private decision to issue only a warning does not invalidate an objectively lawful traffic stop or an exit order while lawful traffic business is being completed. Once that justification ends, further detention requires independent legal grounds; the court must examine the objective circumstances to determine whether the person could terminate the encounter, and return of a license is neither an automatic end of seizure nor an automatic license to continue it.

**Authority:** Part II.A's rule concerning motive and the exit order has six direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Part II.B's distinct continued-detention rule has those six plus Stone-Zsela, seven. Stone-Zsela supplies no additional reason for Part II.A.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an objectively justified beginning and a lawful continuation are separate questions. Whren rejects subjective purpose as an extra condition for an ordinary probable-cause traffic stop while preserving limits on manner, scope and duration. Mimms permits an exit order during a lawful traffic stop without a separate justification for that direction. The Ohio court could not deem the direction unlawful merely because the deputy privately intended a warning or hoped to ask unrelated questions.

That correction does not establish that detention lawfully continued through the later consent request. Bostick requires an objective assessment of whether the person could decline the request or terminate the encounter. The returned license and completed warning bear on that inquiry, but so do the immediacy and actual wording of further questioning, tone, positioning and continuity of official authority. Neither the officer's secret willingness to permit refusal nor Robinette's subjective impression alone decides the seizure question. Supported trial findings matter; the Court does not replace the record with a categorical conclusion in either direction. Ornelas's distinction between historical fact and ultimate constitutional sufficiency remains applicable within its decided scope; this decision announces no universal appellate-review rule for every mixed or consent question. The encounter's classification remains for proper application below.

**Precedent treatment:** Whren's objective initial-stop rule and independent duration limits remain intact; Mimms is confined to lawful traffic business. Bostick's actual-circumstances inquiry is applied. Ornelas is not extended into a general rule for all consent issues.

##### III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not invariably require an officer to give a free-to-go warning before seeking consent to search. Consent must be voluntary under all the circumstances, and when an unlawful detention preceded or accompanied it, voluntariness alone does not establish that the consent was sufficiently independent of that illegality to permit use of the evidence.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by seven direct joins. Ginsburg and Stevens separately reject an indispensable federal script; neither additional agreement is needed to establish the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a useful warning is not automatically an indispensable constitutional condition. Schneckloth requires voluntary consent under the total circumstances and treats knowledge of the right to refuse as relevant without making advice of that right universally mandatory. A fixed release formula would replace that inquiry rather than perform it. The absence of a clear release signal nevertheless remains important evidence; rejecting a script does not make every post-warning request consensual.

The supported trial findings concerning the manner, wording and positions of the parties, Robinette's education and the conclusion of traffic business must be considered with the returned license and actual continuation words. His testimony includes both an initial belief that he could leave and a later feeling that he could not refuse. Neither fragment decides voluntariness by itself. The Court holds neither that this consent was valid nor that it was invalid.

Royer and Brown keep consent's voluntariness separate from its causal independence of unlawful detention. If the court finds an unsupported continued seizure, it must address that additional question; voluntary consent does not automatically erase the illegality, and illegality does not establish that every later consent is incurably tainted. Suppression remains available when the applicable inquiry fails. Nothing in federal law prevents Ohio from requiring clearer warnings under independent state law or police policy.

**Precedent treatment:** Schneckloth is applied without a new warning element. Royer and Brown preserve the separate inquiry into the effect of prior illegality; no automatic cure or automatic suppression rule is created.

##### IV. The remedy corrects the federal rules without deciding admissibility

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the warning rule and the motive-based exit-order premise requires further proceedings on the actual objective detention, voluntary consent and any necessary attenuation question. It does not itself establish the search's validity, require admission of the evidence or reinstate the conviction.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer directly join Part IV; Stone-Zsela joins its stated inquiries and limits while selecting vacatur instead of reversal. Ginsburg separately supports reversal with the same unresolved-application boundary. Seven Justices support the Court's formal reversal and remand; the holdings do not merge Stone-Zsela's different remedy into that coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's two grounds must be addressed separately. The federal script requirement fails, and the detention ruling cannot rest on undisclosed motives during lawful traffic business. Yet those errors do not decide whether the later encounter was consensual, lawfully justified or unlawfully extended, or whether consent was both voluntary and independent of any illegality. The state courts must apply the rules to the supported findings and complete encounter. They may consider genuinely independent state law. A new hearing is not commanded merely by this remand, and the Court enters no final evidence-admission or conviction order.

**Precedent treatment:** The rule/application distinction retained in Evans and the separate consent and causation inquiries in Schneckloth, Royer and Brown govern the scope of remand without enlargement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Michigan v. Long:** Applies the presumption permitting review of interwoven federal grounds absent a plain independent-state-law basis.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Retains that jurisdictional rule and the distinction between correcting a federal rule and deciding its unresolved factual application; its court-record-error exclusion rule is not extended here.
- **Whren v. United States:** Applies objective legality to the initial traffic business and preserves independent limits on manner, scope and duration.
- **Pennsylvania v. Mimms:** Applies the permission to order a driver out during a lawful stop; no indefinite post-stop detention authority follows.
- **Florida v. Bostick:** Requires assessment of the actual objective capacity to decline requests or terminate the encounter, without a categorical seizure or release rule.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Preserves its historical-fact/ultimate-sufficiency distinction within its actual scope; no general review rule for all consent questions is added.
- **Schneckloth v. Bustamonte:** Applies total-circumstances voluntariness and treats knowledge as relevant rather than an indispensable warning requirement.
- **Florida v. Royer:** Preserves the need to consider whether consent follows or remains a product of unlawful detention.
- **Brown v. Illinois:** Preserves attenuation as distinct from voluntariness; neither a voluntary response nor the occurrence of illegality alone answers the remedial question.

#### Law After Decision

Federal law imposes no universal release script, but the objective seizure, voluntary-consent and necessary attenuation inquiries remain distinct and enforceable. The decision supplies no categorical rule that a returned license ends a seizure, that a traffic offense authorizes later unrelated detention, or that a polite request ensures admissibility. The actual search result remains unresolved under the corrected federal standards, and genuinely independent Ohio protection remains available.

### 1996-11-25 — Hopwood v. Texas; Fifth Circuit No. 94-50664, 78 F.3d 932; no invented Supreme Court docket; OT1996, chunk 1, matter 6.

Authority: [Record](../records/Hopwood_v_Texas_merits_1996-11-25.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. The established unequal admissions process

**Holding and operative rule.** The State’s racial admissions classifications must satisfy Adarand’s common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its racial treatment and that objective, and must justify the classification’s coverage and actual burdens. The 1992 law-school process fails because it subjected applicants to unjustified, separate racial decision systems rather than genuinely competitive individual consideration; unequal opportunity to compete is an injury without proof that an applicant would have received a seat.

**Authority.** Part I of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by all nine Justices. The common holding concerns the actual separate competitive process. It neither declares each index differential independently unconstitutional nor adopts a general prohibition on every consideration of race.

**Controlling explanation.** City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co. requires a State to substantiate the justification and fit of its racial classifications; Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education requires attention to the evidence, alternatives and burdens on individuals. Bakke’s rejection of reserved seats and its recognition of competitive consideration distinguish a genuine comparison of applicants from separate racial allocation. Adarand’s equal-competition holding establishes the personal injury from unequal competition, and its common substantial-justification standard governs Texas’s classification.

The 1992 process used different presumptive thresholds, separate discretionary committees and waiting lists, racial coding, and enrollment aspirations. There were individual exceptions to the score bands, and the aspirations were not an established fixed set-aside. Those qualifications do not answer the proved injury: applicants received individualized consideration within materially different racial decision systems, without a sufficient justification for separating their competitive treatment. Neither general educational benefits nor the State’s history establishes the necessary connection between that treatment and its asserted purpose. Correcting the appellate court’s categorical rules therefore does not reopen the established violation. The Court makes no finding that race fixed every final seat, that every feature independently failed, or that any of these four applicants certainly would have been admitted.

#### II. A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available

**Holding and operative rule.** A public university’s interest in concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be an important objective on a properly supported record. The university must establish the educational objective, a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its actual racial classification and that objective, and justified burdens; a diversity label, racial balance, or an assumption that racial identity determines viewpoint does not suffice.

**Authority.** Part II of O’Connor’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices adopt this affirmative proposition. Kennedy agrees with vacating the categorical appellate prohibition but does not join the affirmative interest holding. Scalia and Thomas oppose it. No Marks aggregation is used.

**Controlling explanation.** Bakke did not produce five votes for every part of Justice Powell’s educational-diversity reasoning. It did produce five express joins in Part V-C’s recognition that a properly designed program using competitive consideration of race and ethnic origin may serve a substantial state interest, and in rejection of an injunction against every use of race. The Court preserves that actual proposition and now adopts the more specific constitutional rule stated here; it does not attribute today’s holding to a nonexistent earlier majority.

A university may substantiate educational benefits from interaction among students with different experiences. It must show what the benefit is and why the means advance it, while accounting for the burden imposed on applicants. The argument that all racial consideration necessarily assigns a viewpoint to every member of a group does not defeat a supported, genuinely individual educational judgment. It does require rejecting racial proxies, numerical representation as an end, and assumed validity from academic preference alone. Adarand’s standard continues to govern the means. That distinction permits recognition of the interest while invalidating the actual separate process. The categorical prohibition also controls the admissions counterfactual used on remand, so correcting it has work to do in these live individual claims. No later Texas program is approved, and no university is ordered to use race.

#### III. The responsible State and the continuing dismantlement duty

**Holding and operative rule.** A State that operated a legally segregated university system must eliminate traceable policies that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; good faith and facial neutrality alone do not discharge that duty, and the State bears the showing of dismantlement. The responsible actor cannot categorically be confined to the law school as an administrative unit, but a remedial racial classification requires a demonstrated connection and fit to a continuing wrong rather than generalized societal discrimination or racial imbalance alone.

**Authority.** Part III of O’Connor’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Scalia does not join this affirmative institutional rule or its further relevant inquiry.

**Controlling explanation.** Fordice and Ayers establish the State’s continuing responsibility for its former dual university system, including the interaction of inherited policies. They do not demand fresh discriminatory intent for each established remnant or allow a State to prove discharge solely by adopting neutral rules. They equally do not make present racial imbalance itself a violation. Croson requires evidence of the wrong and the fit of the proposed racial remedy; a new formal adjudication of intentional wrongdoing is not invariably a prerequisite to supported voluntary repair. Jenkins preserves violation-linked educational repair and causally bounded remedies without authorizing general educational improvement or an unproved interdistrict remedy.

The District Court’s university-level findings remain in the record. Neither the law school’s administrative identity nor the absence of recent overt officially sanctioned discrimination alone resolves their legal significance. The courts below must determine whether a traceable, continuing remnant bears on the lawful admissions counterfactual or a properly presented remedy, and whether a proposed response is fitted to it. Findings retain their ordinary review; their legal adequacy is a distinct question. No remnant, necessary preference, or completed discharge is found here. The already established 1992 process violation stands regardless of that inquiry. The remand does not reopen unrelated parts of Texas education or authorize a new general structural proceeding in this four-person suit.

#### IV-A. Individual causation and the same-decision burden

**Holding and operative rule.** Once these applicants have established the unconstitutional admissions process, the school must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that each applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs still must establish the existence and amount of compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; failure of the school’s defense yields neither an automatic damages amount nor unconditional admission.

**Authority.** Part IV-A of O’Connor’s opinion is joined by all seven other Associates: eight Justices adopt the burden and proof rule. Stone-Zsela concurs in the individual-remand judgment on his separate causation ground and does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation.** Mt. Healthy supplies the same-decision defense after an unconstitutional consideration is established; Arlington Heights applies that causal distinction to a discriminatory decision. Carey distinguishes compensation for actual injury from nominal recognition of a completed violation. The District Court’s Title VII analogy cannot leave ultimate persuasion about the same denial with applicants who have already established the unconstitutional process. Nor does equal-competition standing prove entitlement to the seat.

Bakke’s admission relief followed the university’s concession that it could not carry its burden; there is no equivalent established outcome for these four applicants. The lawful counterfactual must follow this Court’s substantive rules. It cannot assume that every possible consideration of race is forbidden. Scalia and Thomas join the general allocation and quantum while adhering to their separate, narrower view of constitutionally permissible admissions; their votes do not support Part II. The District Court must reconsider the tension between its nonadmission conclusion and its finding of an uncommunicated late offer to Elliott. It must not treat that offer as communicated or accepted. Carvell’s tuition testimony identifies a possible loss to evaluate, not an awarded sum. A successful same-decision defense does not erase the separate unequal-process injury.

#### IV-B. Purposeful treatment and statutory intent

**Holding and operative rule.** Deliberately using race to give applicants unequal admissions treatment is intentional racial discrimination for the Title VI issue presented, even when officials act in good faith and intend educational or remedial benefits. That conclusion does not establish every element of a statutory remedy, a proper defendant, or a waiver of immunity.

**Authority.** Part IV-B of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by all seven other Associates: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition retaining that determination and remanding the remaining claim-specific questions.

**Controlling explanation.** The racial treatment here was expressly designed and administered. The relevant intent question is whether the different treatment was purposeful, not whether officials were hostile to the applicants. Bakke’s attention to the individual applicant’s statutory protection is consistent with separating purposeful unequal treatment from the availability of a particular award. A benevolent aim may be offered in justification of a classification; it does not turn the classification into an unintended disparate effect. Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI retain their distinct requirements. The Court adjudicates no independent disparate-impact challenge to the index and no claim belonging to the unsuccessful intervenors.

#### IV-C. The punitive request on this record

**Holding and operative rule.** Smith v. Wade permits punitive damages in its applicable civil-rights setting for evil motive or intent, or for reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights; lack of malice alone is not the whole standard. The punitive denial is affirmed on this record’s accepted good-faith finding and unsettled legal setting, without establishing a universal good-purpose defense or deciding the availability of punitive relief under every pleaded statute.

**Authority.** All eight Associates join Part IV-C of O’Connor’s opinion and its complete Smith standard. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment affirming the punitive denial but not this part’s rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** The applicants invoked reckless indifference, and the precise formulation used by the District Court is not fully established. The Court therefore does not affirm an animus-only test or suppose that an asserted beneficial purpose always answers recklessness. Smith’s alternative culpability paths remain intact. The accepted good-faith determination, considered in the legal setting of the challenged conduct, supports the bounded denial actually before the Court. Purposeful racial treatment sufficient for the statutory-intent issue is not itself a finding of the culpability necessary for punishment. No later official’s state of mind is determined, and no new defendant or punitive cause is supplied.

#### IV-D. Source-specific relief and undecided defenses

**Holding and operative rule.** The established unequal process supports declaratory and nominal recognition independently of proof of entitlement to admission, subject to the available cause, proper defendant and immunity rules. Preserve that relief and fee-free lawful reconsideration if still practically available, while remanding the remaining claim-specific questions; no compensation, admission, immunity waiver, or personal-capacity liability is awarded here.

**Authority.** Part IV-D of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** Carey preserves nominal recognition when actual compensable injury has not been proved. Adarand’s competition-injury rule does not determine the distinct availability of a remedy. The named officials were sued in their official capacities, and the court below expressly reserved Eleventh Amendment immunity. The four plaintiffs’ claims under Title VI and §§1981 and 1983 cannot be merged into an undifferentiated money judgment against every named defendant. Proper defendants, waiver, any properly arising personal-immunity question, statutory damages, fees and the precise remaining relief must be resolved under their own sources. The Court creates no individual-capacity claim from the pleadings before it. Fee-free reapplication is consideration under a lawful process, not a right to a seat; admission relief additionally depends on the individual causal findings and equitable circumstances.

#### V. Prospective relief and the bounded remand

**Holding and operative rule.** The refusal to issue a broad prospective injunction remains intact, and directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs beyond resolution of these 1992 claims are vacated. Any later request for prospective relief requires its own sufficiently concrete future exposure, supported record and equitable basis; neither the discontinued procedure nor the existence of retrospective claims alone supplies those requirements.

**Authority.** Part V of O’Connor’s opinion, joined by all nine Justices. Scalia and Thomas’s disagreement with the Court’s substantive diversity rule does not supply their assent to a new injunction or an adjudication of the later program.

**Controlling explanation.** Adarand separates injury from past unequal competition from the real renewed exposure necessary for prospective relief. The four individual damages claims keep the 1992 controversy live, but do not authorize a perpetual order concerning every future admissions practice. The later procedure is described as using a small administrative group without the old presumptive scores; its full operation and these plaintiffs’ exposure are not established. Changed procedures therefore establish neither universal mootness nor a future violation. The Court decides no later design and enters no injunction. The corrected substantive rules govern the lawful counterfactual and properly presented relief below. They do not commission an advisory review of other institutions, educational levels, employment practices or an unidentified replacement program.

#### Limits and questions not reached

No separate intervention disposition or independent intervenor Title VI index claim is before the Court. The Court does not decide whether any particular holistic program with race as one factor is constitutional, prescribe a universal admissions model or numerical test, require race-conscious action, or adjudicate outreach, barrier removal, race-neutral contextual review or demographic data collection. It does not find a current Texas remnant, a particular applicant’s lawful admission outcome, the amount of actual loss, or a claim-specific immunity waiver. Federal classifications, admissions at other educational levels and race-conscious employment decisions are outside this decision.

### 1996-12-02 — United States v. Jose; No. 95-2082.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Jose_summary_review_1996-12-02.md).

#### Holdings

#### Immediate review of the completed conditional enforcement judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** An order completing a summons-enforcement proceeding is final under 28 U.S.C. §1291, including its presently binding notice condition. The Government's challenge to this condition is ripe before an interdivision transfer occurs because the judgment already fixes its legal obligation; it need not violate the order or await contempt to obtain review.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt ordinary finality and the concrete present-restraint ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court enforced two summonses directed to Jose as trustee of two trusts, while requiring five days' notice before circulating, transferring or copying the produced material to another IRS division, including Criminal Investigation. Jose could then file and serve a motion to prohibit the proposed action. The enforcement proceeding ended with that conclusive obligation. The Ninth Circuit expressly accepted section 1291 jurisdiction but dismissed for ripeness because no transfer had been attempted and the investigation was represented to be civil. Those facts do not make the existing judicial condition hypothetical. Removing it would give the Government effectual legal relief. Church of Scientology distinguishes meaningful appellate relief from entitlement on the enforcement merits. Digital Equipment's limits on collateral appeals are preserved: this is an ordinary appeal from a completed proceeding, not a special right arising from compliance costs. Quackenbush likewise requires the actual appellate route and conclusive effect to be identified. The Court does not hold that every final order necessarily presents a ripe controversy; this particular binding restraint does.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The remand concerns the notice condition's legality and preserved related enforcement-power issues. The uncontested findings that the civil-purpose summonses are valid and should be enforced are not reopened. Privacy interests and hypothetical future criminal uses are not adjudicated by this threshold ruling. Zolin's equal division supplies no controlling answer about conditions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Church of Scientology of California v. United States (November 16, 1992): applies the distinction between effectual appellate relief and ultimate enforcement entitlement; its reservation of conditions and future-use restrictions remains.
- Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. (June 6, 1994): preserves the cumulative collateral-order requirements and rejects a free-standing hardship exception; this order is final in the ordinary completed-proceeding sense.
- Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. (June 3, 1996): applies source-specific appellate analysis and conclusive legal effect; no additional collateral-order category is created.
- United States v. Zolin, 491 U.S. 554: its equal division over enforcement-condition authority supplies no controlling answer to the reserved merits question.

#### Law After Decision

The existing final-judgment and live-controversy rules permit immediate review of this actual conditional enforcement judgment. The holding does not expand collateral-order jurisdiction or decide the permissible scope of IRS protective conditions.

### 1996-12-09 — Humane Society v. Babbitt; D.C. Circuit No. 93-5339; District of Columbia No. 92-cv-0952; no Supreme Court docket assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Humane_Society_v_Babbitt_merits_1996-12-09.md).

#### Holdings

#### Independent failure of traceability

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming sufficient personal aesthetic injury from Lota's absence, the Society has not shown that the challenged August 1991 ESA certificate amendment caused the lost zoo access. The independent earlier donation produced that loss, and this record establishes no mechanism connecting the later amendment to its continuation.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient jurisdictional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires support for the plaintiff's own injury and its connection to the challenged action at the litigation stage presented. A person can have a concrete aesthetic interest in observing a particular animal; species-wide depletion, ownership and a scientific profession are not prerequisites. The Court nevertheless assumes actual injury here rather than finding that the hypothetical regular observer described in the question exists in the evidence. The zoo's donation preceded the amendment, which governed later uses by Hawthorn. The Society abandoned its separate appeal concerning the original transfer and did not challenge the CITES certificate. Neither action can be restored to the case by calling this a challenge to continued absence. Government permission can be causally effective through supported, predictable private conduct; a government command is not invariably necessary. But this record supplies no such link between the later amendment and the lost Milwaukee observation. Sweet Home's treatment of actual injury to individual animals under a habitat regulation does not supply the missing human standing link for this different transportation permission.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the actual members' affidavits establish injury in fact. It adopts no categorical rule against third-party causation, no purchased-ticket requirement, and no general prohibition on standing to observe one animal.

#### Independent failure of likely beneficial relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Society also fails independently to show that suspending the challenged certificate or declaring the transportation interpretation unlawful would likely benefit a member's impaired observation. Complete restoration is unnecessary, but a regulatory change must offer some likely concrete improvement to the member's interest.

**Authority:** Part II of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices independently adopt this remedial standing ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Invalidating the amendment would leave Hawthorn able to retain Lota at its Illinois premises. No supported alternative benefit to an established observer appears in the affidavits or requested relief. Church of Scientology permits meaningful partial relief even when full restoration is impossible, but changing another person's legal options is not beneficial relief to a member by definition. Even with favorable reasonable inferences from the existing materials and assumed injury, the missing connection remains. The Winikoff letters were considered because the objection to their unsworn form was not preserved; considering them does not supply absent facts. The Owens letter addresses the Society's access, not its authors' personal observation. A remand to create a new return mechanism or observer would not correct an established error in this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical conclusion is reached about remedies in another properly supported action. The Court orders no return, certificate cancellation, damages or other substantive relief.

**Holding and operative rule:** An asserted ESA procedure does not cure the missing connection between this challenged amendment and a represented member's concrete interest. Assuming that the asserted conservation-permit procedure exists, a claimant need not prove that proper procedure certainly changes the ultimate agency decision, but must still show the threatened personal interest that the procedure protects; representative litigation does not dispense with that constitutional requirement.

**Authority:** Part III of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices reject these proposed ways around the causal and remedial defects.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan preserves procedural protection for threatened concrete interests without requiring certainty of a different final result. No individual entitlement to Lota's Milwaukee residence or separate informational right is established here. Assuming the asserted section 1539 procedure for this analysis does not convert that section's permission into a found statutory command. Brown Group preserves concrete injury, traceability and redressability for represented members; its displacement of Hunt's prudential participation limit through WARN's express authorization supplies no ESA exemption. The procedure asserted in this case does not connect the later amendment to the completed transfer or establish likely benefit to the zoo observer. ### 1996-12-09 — In re Askin; Fourth Circuit No. 94-5428, 47 F.3d 100; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; October Term 1996.

Authority: [Record](../records/In_re_Askin_merits_1996-12-09.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority.** Justice Scalia's opinion is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins only Parts II-A, III's completed-acquisition ruling, and IV's rejection of the categorical constitutional grounds. The precise eight- or nine-Justice authority of each holding is stated below.

#### I. The contempt adjudication remains reviewable

**Holding and operative rule.** Completion of the underlying trial does not moot review when setting aside the outstanding civil-contempt adjudication can afford meaningful relief from identified collateral consequences. The possible effect on Askin's West Virginia Bar status and the already pending related criminal-contempt proceeding preserve a live controversy here without any assumption of continuing civil confinement.

**Authority.** Part I controls with the eight full-opinion joiners identified above. Stone-Zsela agrees only with proceeding to the merits and supplies no additional threshold rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** *Church of Scientology* permits review where some meaningful relief remains, and *Calderon v. Moore* applies that principle even when relief cannot undo every past event. Ending confinement would not alone supply prospective relief, and abstract reputational concern would not suffice. But the challenged adjudication still has the concrete consequences identified in this record. Vacating it can matter to those consequences. The Court decides neither the criminal-contempt charge nor professional status and assumes no later disposition of either matter.

#### II-A. The former wire and electronic definitions exclude the acquired radio portion

**Holding and operative rule.** Former 18 U.S.C. §2510(1) and (12)(A) exclude the radio portion transmitted between a cordless handset and its base from the statutory wire and electronic categories, including both speakers' words acquired at that link. The other participant's use of a landline or cellular telephone does not overcome those particular exclusions; the ruling does not classify every segment of the complete call as unprotected.

**Authority.** Part II-A controls with all nine Justices joining.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court applies the statute to the portion the agents acquired. General language covering transmission in whole or part cannot erase the specific handset-to-base exclusions. Askin's protected endpoint therefore does not make the intercepted portion a wire or electronic communication under the former text. Former §2510(12) also separately excluded wire or oral communications, tone-only paging and tracking-device communications; those distinctions are not interchangeable. The oral category has separate terms and is addressed in Part II-B. This construction neither authorizes acquisition at another point nor decides constitutional privacy.

#### II-B. The former oral definition requires its own inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** The former exclusions from the wire and electronic categories do not categorically foreclose oral-communication coverage. Former §2510(2) requires an exhibited expectation against interception under circumstances justifying that expectation and excludes statutory electronic communication; because former §2510(12)(A) excludes this radio portion from that electronic category, the lower court must determine whether the distinct expectation requirements are met.

**Authority.** Part II-B controls with eight Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join and would affirm complete former-Title-III noncoverage of the acquired segment.

**Controlling explanation.** The electronic exclusion within the oral definition uses the statute's category, not the everyday description of technologically transmitted sound. Treating the radio portion as necessarily electronic would disregard the very statutory exclusion the Government invokes. Yet exclusion from one definition does not establish either of the oral definition's expectation requirements.

The strongest objection is that oral coverage would undo Congress's two express exclusions and make their 1994 deletion unnecessary. It does neither. Conditional oral protection requires additional personal expectation findings, whereas deletion later supplies category protection without those findings. The two exclusions retain their full operation.

The lower court's assertion that Congress treated cordless calls only as wire or electronic communications supplied no independent determination under the complete oral definition and a correct personal inquiry. Askin's chosen endpoint and private interpersonal conversation are relevant, as are actual signal accessibility and established knowledge. Neither knowledge nor ignorance may be presumed. The Court vacates the categorical ruling but finds neither that Askin had nor that he lacked the required expectation, and creates no mandatory multifactor test.

#### III. The 1994 deletion does not reclassify completed acquisition

**Holding and operative rule.** Public Law 103-414 §202 does not make completed preenactment interception unlawful by retroactively deleting the former category exclusions. A genuinely distinct later disclosure, use, evidentiary reception or refusal must be assessed on its own actual date and statutory predicate; pending appellate review alone establishes no such later-act violation, and preexisting oral-communication protection remains available on its own terms.

**Authority.** Part III's completed-acquisition ruling has nine joins. Its treatment of unestablished later acts and preservation of the oral route has the eight full-opinion joiners; Stone-Zsela supplies no additional later-act rationale and rejects oral coverage here.

**Controlling explanation.** The October 25, 1994 amendment deleted the cordless exceptions but did not amend §2510(2). Title I §111(b)'s four-year delay names §§103 and 105, and §111(a) likewise concerns its own title; neither delays Title II §202. Immediate enactment, however, does not direct retrospective reclassification. The interception preceded the early January 1994 indictment. Applying new interception prohibitions to that completed acquisition would attach a new legal consequence without the necessary congressional direction. *Landgraf* and *Rivers* require attention to the actual regulated conduct; neither a corrective purpose nor a pending appeal supplies missing temporal direction. Agreement on this case-specific result does not eliminate the distinct temporal methods in those earlier opinions.

Disclosure, use, reception and refusal can be separate events. But §§2511(1)(c) and (d) ordinarily require knowledge or reason to know that the interception violated the subsection, and §2515 has its own chapter-violation condition. A later use therefore cannot simply be assumed to convert an acquisition outside coverage into an unlawful interception. No exact post-amendment use or refusal date is established; the April 6 subpoena is not that event. A preserved independently applicable later-act claim requires its actual predicate. The Court makes no such finding and orders no inquiry merely because review remained pending. The older oral category is a separate possible source of coverage, unaffected by rejection of retrospective expanded categories.

#### IV. Statutory noncoverage and another caller's endpoint do not decide personal constitutional privacy

**Holding and operative rule.** A statute's failure to prohibit an interception is not affirmative constitutional permission, and the other speaker's vulnerable endpoint alone does not establish that Askin surrendered every legitimate privacy interest in his own conversation. If the constitutional question remains necessary after the statutory inquiry, the lower courts must assess his personal interest, actual exposure and any legally available justification on the record, without presuming his knowledge or ignorance.

**Authority.** Part IV's rejection of the two categorical grounds controls with all nine Justices joining. The eight full-opinion joiners adopt the stated sequence and reserve a final privacy determination; Stone-Zsela's further conditional privacy rule appears only in his separate writing.

**Controlling explanation.** *Katz* protects private conversation without requiring trespass. *Wellons* requires examination of the claimant's own legitimate interest; another person's relation to the medium cannot replace it. *Pinson*, as applied in *Myers*, rejects a receiver-location shortcut within its technological home-information setting, but protects neither every emission nor every cordless call. *Alderman* and *Rakas* keep the personal-interest inquiry distinct from another person's rights and from remedy.

The Government's strongest argument is that both sides of this call traveled over a signal readily received with ordinary equipment. *White* recognizes the risk of disclosure through an unreliable participant. But that analogy supplies no finding that a participant cooperated with the Government or that Askin knowingly exposed his words. Actual conversational content differs from an inference drawn from exterior warmth. Conversely, selecting a landline or cellular endpoint and asserting a private conversation do not alone establish an objectively justified expectation.

The lower court treated statutory exclusion as congressional permission and applied its constitutional deference and unitary-conversation reasoning categorically. Those legal shortcuts must be corrected; no invalidation of the former statute is necessary. The Court does not finally hold that a search occurred, that the acquisition was unjustified, or that suppression follows. No warrant is established, but other exceptions require their actual legal and factual predicates rather than presumptions either way.

#### V. Statutory just cause is conditional; the constitutional remedial extension is reserved

**Holding and operative rule.** Under *Gelbard*, a witness may establish just cause under §1826(a) when his own covered wire or oral communication was intercepted in violation of Title III, after consideration of applicable authorization or exceptions, and §2515 prohibits reception of the compelled testimony as intercepted contents or derived evidence. The surviving potential category here is oral, and each refused question's derivation and the witness's personal remedial entitlement must be determined; the Court leaves unresolved whether independently unconstitutional acquisition alone supplies just cause for this immunized witness's criminal-trial refusal.

**Authority.** Part V and its bounded implementing remand control with the eight full-opinion joiners. Stone-Zsela agrees with *Gelbard*'s statutory principle but rejects oral coverage here and would adopt the separate constitutional just-cause rule described in his writing; he does not join Part V.

**Controlling explanation.** *Gelbard* supplies a statutory defense, not an automatic constitutional testimonial privilege. Section 2515 protects wire or oral communications on its own violation condition; electronic coverage alone does not obtain that remedy. Use immunity does not repeal it. The Government's admitted monitoring satisfies §3504's occurrence-response requirement without admitting illegality or derivation.

Askin refused questions about knowing McNulty, Brumbaugh and Dabbs, using cocaine, and receiving cocaine from McNulty. A subject's appearance in the calls does not establish exclusive derivation, independent source or attenuation. Each actual question requires the appropriate connection and personal entitlement; no blanket refusal follows. Naming an exception likewise does not prove it.

*Calandra* declines constitutional exclusion for grand-jury questioning and distinguishes injury from remedy; derivative questioning is not itself a new search. This case concerns a criminal trial, so its grand-jury holding supplies neither an automatic bar nor an automatic right to refuse. Direct exploitation of this speaker's words to coerce his testimony supports the proposed extension; his nonparty, immunized status and the usual personal-use limits support the objection. The potentially dispositive statutory inquiry permits reserving that constitutional remedial issue.

*Leon* and *Krull* establish no automatic good-faith result: there was no warrant, and the former coverage exclusions did not themselves direct a constitutionally valid search. Any preserved reliance claim must fit an existing exception on objective facts. Independent source, attenuation and other lawful grounds likewise require their own predicates. No subjective-bad-motive requirement or new technology exception is adopted. The remand permits lawful reinstatement or setting aside of the civil-contempt adjudication after the necessary inquiries; it commands neither final discharge nor a new hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Church of Scientology* (November 16, 1992) and *Calderon v. Moore* (June 17, 1996): applied to concrete surviving relief from the outstanding adjudication; completion of trial is not itself dispositive.
- *Landgraf* and *Rivers*: applied to the completed acquisition and the distinct possibility of genuinely later conduct; the result does not merge their separate temporal methods or retrospectively add new joins to them.
- *Katz v. United States*, 389 U.S. 347: applied to the claimant's own conversational privacy; trespass and endpoint ownership are not prerequisites.
- *United States v. White*, 401 U.S. 745: distinguished as to willing-participant disclosure; its recipient-risk principle does not establish cooperation or knowing public exposure on this record and remains operative in its proper setting.
- *United States v. Pinson* (May 8, 1995), as applied in *United States v. Myers* (June 11, 1996): applied against categorical inference from external reception, preserving their limited home-information holdings; no universal emitted-information rule is adopted.
- *United States v. Wellons* (May 30, 1995): applied for separate personal-interest, justification and remedy inquiries; the relation of a third person to the medium does not decide the claimant's interest.
- *Alderman v. United States*, 394 U.S. 165, and *Rakas v. Illinois*, 439 U.S. 128: applied to personal rather than vicarious rights; neither establishes entitlement to exclusion in every proceeding.
- *Gelbard v. United States*, 408 U.S. 41: applied to the conditional statutory §2515/§1826(a) defense for the witness's own communications and derived testimony; it does not settle the independent constitutional extension.
- *United States v. Calandra*, 414 U.S. 338: its grand-jury rule remains intact; its injury/remedy distinction informs but does not decide the reserved criminal-trial constitutional issue.
- *United States v. Leon*, 468 U.S. 897, and *Illinois v. Krull*, 480 U.S. 340: remain potential objective reliance doctrines only within their actual legal and factual conditions; neither supplies an automatic defense here.

#### Law After Decision

Effective December 9, 1996, the former cordless exceptions govern the acquired wire/electronic segment, but do not foreclose the old oral category's separately established expectation requirements. The 1994 amendment does not retroactively reclassify completed acquisition. Statutory coverage, constitutional privacy, justification, personal remedy, question-specific derivation and just cause remain separate inquiries. A new controlling rule rejects the lower court's categorical oral and personal-privacy shortcuts; no final privacy, illegality or suppression finding follows. The conditional *Gelbard* route remains available. No majority adopts a constitutional right for an immunized trial witness to refuse direct-fruit questions; Stone-Zsela's proposed extension is noncontrolling. ### 1996-12-10 — Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis; No. 95-1263.

Authority: [Record](../records/Caterpillar_v_Lewis_merits_1996-12-10.md).

#### Holdings

#### The erroneous refusal to remand remains error

**Holding and operative rule:** Complete diversity was absent when Caterpillar removed, and Lewis's timely motion required remand under the applicable removal and remand statutes. The later dismissal of the nondiverse party does not make the earlier ruling correct, waive Lewis's preserved objection, or require him to have sought extraordinary pretrial relief.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion of the Court, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt this preserved-error proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Lewis and Whayne were Kentucky citizens, and Liberty's surviving subrogation claim kept Whayne in the action when Caterpillar removed on June 21, 1990. The district court should have granted the timely remand motion instead of denying it on September 24. Lewis's separate contention that his own settlement was partial need not be resolved because Liberty's claim already defeated complete diversity. Sections 1441 and 1447 retain their actual requirements; finality is no permission to disregard them. Things Remembered and Quackenbush preserve the mandatory-remand and review rules at their proper procedural stages. This case concerns refusal to remand followed by a later trial, not appellate review of an actually entered remand covered by section 1447(d).

**Limits and questions not reached:** No collateral challenge to an entered covered remand is authorized. No late alternative removal argument or independent fee or sanction entitlement is decided.

#### Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect

**Holding and operative rule:** When complete diversity actually arises through the nondiverse party's lawful dismissal before trial and persists through judgment, an earlier erroneous refusal of a timely remand motion does not by itself require vacating the completed federal judgment. The rule depends on actual section 1332 jurisdiction during trial and judgment; consent, finality or efficiency cannot cure a judgment entered without that jurisdiction.

**Authority:** Part II of the same opinion, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt this bounded extension to a preserved removal objection.

**Controlling explanation:** Whayne's dismissal following the June 1993 settlement left complete diversity before the November trial and through judgment. Finn required correction where the nondiverse party remained through judgment; it does not require destruction of a judgment that possessed original jurisdiction when tried. Grubbs distinguishes a completed judgment's jurisdictional foundation from earlier removal error, but involved trial without objection. The Court expressly extends that distinction to this timely preserved objection only after the actual pretrial cure. Newman-Green recognizes that party dismissal can cure statutory diversity, subject to its separate dispensability and prejudice limits. This case does not require an appellate court to dismiss a party or manufacture jurisdiction over a completed nondiverse trial. The strongest contrary argument gives Lewis's preserved forum objection a judgment-destroying remedy. Preservation makes the earlier error reviewable, but does not dictate that consequence after the obstacle ended before merits adjudication. Willy separates independently authorized collateral consequences from merits jurisdiction; it supplies no jurisdiction and does not save an uncured trial. Existing jurisdiction and the final-judgment interest, not economy alone, sustain this limited result.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal cure for every removal defect follows. The decision authorizes no collusive manufacture of diversity, finds no manipulation, and does not extinguish a separately authorized fee or sanction request.

#### Restoration against jurisdictional vacatur leaves the ordinary appeal for decision

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's jurisdiction-based vacatur and state-court-remand direction are reversed; the district-court judgment is restored against that jurisdictional ground, subject to the unresolved ordinary appeal. The case returns to the Sixth Circuit to decide the five preserved issues it did not reach: prior-accident discovery restrictions, the discovery-sanction request, excluded exhibits, excluded witnesses and the omitted failure-to-warn instruction.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion, adopted by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt the remand explanation. The Chief Justice agrees with this remand disposition without joining Part III. All nine support reversal and the stated disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The granted jurisdictional question does not decide the other appellate challenges to the defense verdict. Restoring a judgment against an erroneous jurisdictional vacatur does not make it immune from ordinary appellate correction. The Court therefore returns those distinct questions rather than finding the discovery, evidentiary, sanction or instruction objections meritless without deciding them. No fee, sanction or new trial is awarded here. The Sixth Circuit must decide the preserved issues under their governing law.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not finally affirm the defense verdict against the five remaining challenges or decide any of their merits.

#### Precedent Treatment

- American Fire & Casualty Co. v. Finn, 341 U.S. 6: distinguishes its jurisdictional defect persisting through judgment from actual complete diversity before this trial; its prohibition on jurisdiction by consent remains.
- Grubbs v. General Electric Credit Corp., 405 U.S. 699: extends its final-judgment distinction beyond its no-objection facts only to the preserved error and actual pretrial cure here; it is not described as already deciding this case.
- Newman-Green, Inc. v. Alfonzo-Larrain, 490 U.S. 826: preserves dismissal-based diversity cure and its dispensability/prejudice limits; no appellate dismissal or retrospective creation of this trial's jurisdiction is needed.
- Willy v. Coastal Corp. (March 9, 1992): applies the separation between independently authorized collateral consequences and merits jurisdiction; its holding does not itself confer jurisdiction or validate an uncured merits judgment.
- Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca (December 5, 1995), and Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. (June 3, 1996): preserve the covered-remand review bar and source/stage distinctions; no bar concerning an entered covered remand is displaced.

#### Law After Decision

The earlier statutory remand duty and Lewis's timely objection remain valid. The Court adds a bounded rule that actual diversity before trial and through judgment prevents this preserved earlier removal error alone from destroying the completed judgment. Original-jurisdiction requirements, lawful party dismissal, covered-remand review limits and ordinary appellate remedies remain. No doctrine of universal harmless removal error or unconditional verdict affirmance results.

### 1996-12-10 — O'Gilvie v. United States; Nos. 95-966 and 95-977

Authority: [Record](../records/Ogilvie_v_United_States_merits_1996-12-10.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. The Government's appeal in the father's action was timely

**Holding and operative rule:** The December 7, 1993 judgment, which finally resolved the punitive-damages claim after withdrawal of the August judgment and an intervening interest-only ruling, started the Government's sixty-day civil appeal period. Its February 1, 1994 notice was timely; identifying that actual final judgment does not establish that every amended or clerically corrected judgment restarts an appeal period.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appeal period runs from the judgment that actually disposed of the claim. The August 26, 1992 judgment was withdrawn. The November 30, 1993 order addressed interest rather than finally disposing of the punitive-damages claim. December 7 supplied that final disposition, and February 1 fell within Rule 4(a)(1)'s sixty days when the United States is a party. The objection that a later amendment cannot revive an expired appeal is sound in its proper setting, but its premise is absent here. Jose directs attention to the conclusive effect of the actual judgment. Ibarra concerns timely substantive reconsideration in criminal proceedings; it creates no civil extension here. Smith v. Barry permits timely functional notice but does not excuse lateness. No tolling or substitute-notice ruling is necessary.

**Precedent treatment:** Jose's ordinary-finality approach is applied; Ibarra's criminal-reconsideration rule and Smith's timely-notice requirement remain distinct and unchanged.

#### II. These noncompensatory punitive receipts are taxable

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the applicable pre-1989 §104(a)(2), the taxpayer must satisfy both the tort-or-tort-type-right requirement and the independent requirement that the particular damages be received on account of personal injuries or sickness; amounts attributable to §213 deductions allowed in a prior year remain excepted. The Kansas punitive component serves punishment and deterrence rather than compensation for injury, fails the independent causal requirement, and is included in gross income under §61.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent from this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the function of this separate payment matters independently of the character of the lawsuit. Schleier establishes the two requirements and refuses to treat tort-type status as sufficient by itself. Its treatment of willfulness-based liquidated damages supplies the causal reasoning applied here, although it reserved a general punitive-damages question. The wrongful-death injury is real and necessary to the Kansas action. But this distinct award responds to the wrongdoer's culpability and the purposes of punishment and deterrence; it does not restore the taxpayers' injury loss. Injury's role as an indispensable antecedent cannot alone establish the required connection for every resulting receipt. The statutory phrase is broader than an express compensation formula, as the taxpayers emphasize, but reading every injury-dependent recovery as excluded would erase Schleier's independent inquiry. Burke supplies no controlling remedial-menu or health-only test, and none is added. The decision concerns the actual noncompensatory award; compensatory payments, mixed allocations and awards serving a materially different function remain open. Later punitive-damages and physical-injury amendments do not govern these receipts.

**Precedent treatment:** Schleier's receipt-specific causal rule is extended to this wholly punitive Kansas award, without pretending its reserved question was already decided. Burke's fractured judgment remains without a general controlling statutory rationale.

#### III. Receipt of the refund checks starts the children's recovery period

**Holding and operative rule:** For these refund checks, a refund is made for §§6532(b) and 7405(b) when the taxpayers receive payment, rather than when the Government merely writes or mails the checks. Receipt on July 9, 1990 makes the recovery actions filed July 9, 1992 timely under the two-year period.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas do not reach the children's limitation defense.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that delivery of the mistaken payment connects the refund to the Government's enforceable recovery claim. Wurts distinguishes payment from administrative allowance and explains why an allowance does not itself begin the period for recovering money. A written or mailed check has not necessarily delivered the benefit to its recipient. The traditional mistaken-payment action likewise rests on receipt of that benefit. A postmark might offer convenient evidence, but convenience does not replace the statutory connection between making and recovering a refund. The children conceded receipt on July 9; the suits were brought exactly two years later. July 6 is the check-writing date, not an established mailing date. The Court adopts the receipt rule for these checks without announcing equitable tolling, a new deadline exception or a rule for every other payment mechanism.

**Precedent treatment:** Wurts's payment-and-recovery reasoning is applied to the receipt question it did not itself decide; administrative allowance remains distinct from payment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Commissioner v. Schleier, June 14, 1995:** Extends its independent receipt-specific causation rule to the noncompensatory punitive award; its two statutory conditions and prior-§213 exception survive.
- **United States v. Burke, May 26, 1992:** Preserves the fractured judgment; no remedial-menu, health-only or general punitive-damages rationale is promoted to controlling law.
- **United States v. Wurts, 303 U.S. 414:** Applies its distinction between administrative allowance and actual payment to determine when these recovery claims begin.
- **United States v. Jose, December 2, 1996:** Applies ordinary finality by examining what the order actually completed; its distinct IRS-condition merits reservation is unaffected.
- **United States v. Ibarra, October 15, 1991:** Distinguishes timely criminal reconsideration from this civil final-judgment sequence.
- **Smith v. Barry, January 14, 1992:** Preserves the requirement of timely notice; no functional-notice exception to lateness is created.

#### Law After Decision

Effective December 10, 1996, the former exclusion does not cover punitive receipts having the noncompensatory character established here. The receipt-specific rule develops Schleier without adopting Burke's noncontrolling rationales. For the checks at issue, receipt starts the two-year erroneous-refund recovery period. Actual civil finality governs the father's appeal without any general restart rule. Compensatory damages, mixed allocations, different payment mechanisms and later statutory texts retain their separate questions.

### 1996-12-16 — Greene v. Georgia; No. 96-5369

Authority: [Record](../records/Greene_v_Georgia_summary_review_1996-12-16.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. The stated federal premise permits review

**Holding and operative rule:** When the state judgment rests on a stated federal premise without a clearly independent sufficient state ground, the Court may review that federal premise. Georgia's treatment of Witt as controlling direct-review deference supplies that basis here; the Court's review does not prevent Georgia from independently choosing a lawful state review rule.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees to reach the source-of-review question without joining this distinct jurisdictional explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Georgia's express reliance on federal law permits correction of its federal premise. Long allows review where the judgment fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven with federal law and supplies no clear independent sufficient state ground. Evans retains that rule, and Robinette applies it without displacing independent state authority. The possibility that Georgia could adopt equivalent deference under its own law is not a finding that it already did so. The Court addresses the attributed federal command and leaves the State's lawful independent choice open.

**Precedent treatment:** Long, Evans and Robinette's interwoven-ground rule is applied without enlargement or displacement of an actually independent sufficient state ground.

#### II. Federal habeas deference does not bind Georgia's direct review

**Holding and operative rule:** Wainwright v. Witt's treatment of a federal habeas court's statutory factual presumption does not require Georgia's appellate courts to use that allocation on direct review. Georgia must reconsider the five challenged cause exclusions under a correct understanding of federal law and its own lawful appellate standard; it may choose appropriate deference to trial-level credibility and demeanor findings, but may not mistake federal collateral-review deference for a command governing its direct appeal.

**Authority:** The source-of-review proposition in Part II of the per curiam opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, nine. Part III's formal reversal and remand is joined by the eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the stated reconsideration inquiry and limits but would vacate rather than reverse.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the source of a review rule matters. Witt permits excluding a capital juror whose views prevent or substantially impair performance under the instructions and oath. It also addresses former §2254(d)'s presumption for a federal court reviewing a state judgment on habeas. The Georgia court treated Witt as controlling its direct-review deference as well as the qualification rule. The federal habeas statute does not assign Georgia's own appellate responsibility.

Trial judges have real advantages in observing demeanor and assessing credibility. That consideration supports Georgia's freedom to choose lawful deference; it does not establish that Georgia already made an independent choice or that the identified error was immaterial. Morgan preserves Witt's substantial-impairment rule. Thompson's distinction between historical facts and ultimate constitutional questions operates in its own habeas setting; it supplies no universal de novo juror-review requirement. AEDPA likewise concerns federal habeas rather than this state direct appeal.

Robinette separates correction of a federal rule from its unresolved application. The Court reverses and remands so Georgia may perform that application. It determines neither that any of the five jurors was qualified nor that any exclusion was unconstitutional. No guilt retrial, new sentencing proceeding or harmless-error result follows from this source error alone.

**Precedent treatment:** Witt's qualification rule is preserved and its statutory deference confined to its source. Morgan, Thompson and Robinette retain their distinct rules; no earlier decision is overruled.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Wainwright v. Witt, 469 U.S. 412:** Preserves substantial impairment as the qualification standard and confines federal statutory factual deference to its own review setting.
- **Morgan v. Illinois, June 15, 1992:** Retains its requested-inquiry and impartiality protections without changing Witt or deciding any juror's qualification here.
- **Thompson v. Keohane, November 29, 1995:** Preserves source-specific treatment of historical facts and ultimate constitutional questions; its Miranda-custody rule is not a universal juror-review rule.
- **Michigan v. Long, 463 U.S. 1032:** Permits review of the stated federal premise absent a clearly independent sufficient state ground.
- **Arizona v. Evans, March 1, 1995:** Retains Long and the distinction between correction of a federal rule and its factual application.
- **Ohio v. Robinette, November 18, 1996:** Applies that same separation of federal correction from unresolved state application; no substantive search rule enters this jury question.

#### Law After Decision

Federal habeas deference is not a mandatory state direct-review rule. States remain free to choose lawful standards of appellate deference while applying the federal juror-qualification rule. The decision resolves the mistaken federal-source premise, not the five exclusions themselves, and leaves the substantive capital-jury precedents unchanged.

### 1996-12-16 — M.L.B. v. S.L.J.; No. 95-853

Authority: [Record](../records/MLB_v_SLJ_merits_1996-12-16.md).

#### Holdings

#### Meaningful first review of permanent parental termination cannot depend on ability to buy the necessary record

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State provides a first appeal from a decree permanently terminating the legal parent-child relationship, due process and equal protection forbid denying an indigent parent meaningful review solely because she cannot prepay the necessary appellate record. The State must permit that appeal without prepayment beyond her means and supply a transcript, agreed statement, settled narrative or other record adequate to review the asserted errors.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins support this access rule, converging due-process/equality explanation, adequate-record qualification and remedy. Kennedy supplies a separate judgment vote on due process without joining the opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Mississippi cannot make its provided first review of irreversible parental termination depend on wealth. Griffin supplies meaningful record access in provided criminal appeals; Mayer confirms that incarceration is not indispensable and that adequate alternatives may suffice. Boddie identifies the significance of family status and the State's exclusive control over its legal alteration. Santosky recognizes permanent termination's exceptional loss and the need to guard against error. Together those principles support this bounded extension of access protection. Fairness and equality converge here without making poverty a suspect classification.

The father and stepmother initiated the action, but the State alone enters and enforces the termination decree and sets the appellate barrier. M.L.B. paid the $100 filing fee and timely appealed. Established indigency prevents her from paying the separate $2,352.36 record charge. Her evidentiary challenge cannot be meaningfully assessed from a decree that states the legal standard without recounting the evidence. That feature shows the need for an adequate record, not that termination was erroneous. No adequate substitute was offered on this record.

Kras and Ortwein permit ordinary financing choices for bankruptcy and review of economic benefits after prior process. Neither makes the civil label dispositive of permanent destruction of family status. Prior trial process does not justify making the State's chosen first error-correction process available only to parents able to pay. The remedy reaches necessary review material, not every preferred transcript. No general civil appeal, appointed appellate counsel, free discovery or subsidy for property, money, ordinary custody scheduling or visitation litigation follows. The state courts retain the termination merits.

**Precedent treatment:** Griffin and Mayer's meaningful-record rule is extended to this first permanent-termination appeal. Boddie and Santosky supply the family-status and reliability premises. Kras and Ortwein retain their economic-litigation rules; Lassiter's distinct counsel analysis is unchanged.

#### II. A colorable need for the complete transcript requires a showing that an alternative will suffice

**Holding and operative rule:** In the provided first appeal from permanent parental termination, when an indigent parent's asserted grounds establish a colorable need for a complete transcript, the State bears the burden of showing that a partial transcript or alternative record permits effective review. The rule requires an adequate account of the evidence and rulings relevant to the errors, not a complete transcript regardless of need.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela joins Part I and the remedy but does not join this additional prospective burden allocation. Kennedy concurs in the judgment without an opinion join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that permission to use an equivalent record requires actual equivalence for the appellate questions. Mayer places the showing on the State once the asserted grounds demonstrate a colorable need for the full transcript. An indigent parent cannot be denied review merely because an unspecified cheaper record could conceivably exist. The State retains the choice of a sufficient partial transcript, narrative or other equivalent, and need not finance material unrelated to the errors. Here record necessity and indigency are established, and no adequate alternative was actually offered. The sparse decree does not let the appellate court evaluate the evidence; it also does not establish that termination was wrong. Mississippi must provide meaningful review rather than rely on a hypothetical substitute.

**Precedent treatment:** Mayer's sufficient-record burden is extended at the same permanent-termination boundary as the access rule; its adequate-alternative qualification remains intact.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Griffin v. Illinois, 351 U.S. 12:** Extends meaningful record access in a provided appeal to the stated permanent-parental-termination category; creates no general right to an appeal.
- **Mayer v. City of Chicago, 404 U.S. 189:** Applies adequate substitutes and the State's burden when the asserted grounds show a colorable need for a full transcript; incarceration is not indispensable to its access protection.
- **Boddie v. Connecticut, 401 U.S. 371:** Uses fundamental family status and exclusive state authority as support; extends protection beyond its indispensable initial-access setting at the present narrow boundary.
- **Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745:** Preserves termination's heightened-proof rule and applies its irreversible-loss and reliability principles to necessary appellate review material.
- **United States v. Kras, 409 U.S. 434:** Distinguishes bankruptcy's economic benefit and alternative adjustment routes; its ordinary cost-allocation holding remains.
- **Ortwein v. Schwab, 410 U.S. 656:** Distinguishes economic-benefit review after administrative process; does not treat its discussion of state exceptions as an already established federal termination-fee mandate.
- **Lassiter v. Department of Social Services, 452 U.S. 18:** Preserves its distinct counsel inquiry; no automatic appellate-counsel entitlement or all-or-nothing inference about record access follows.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C., November 20, 1995:** Preserves its specified complete-contact proof rule; that decision did not itself decide appellate record fees.
- **Baby Richard v. Kirchner, June 21, 1996:** Preserves its bounded child-transition interest and fitted procedures without making every custody dispute a termination case.

#### Law After Decision

Effective December 16, 1996, the necessary-record access rule covers the provided first appeal from permanent parental termination on established indigency and actual record necessity. Due process and equality support that rule directly through six opinion joins; five Associates also adopt Mayer's burden on the State after a colorable showing of need for a full transcript. Kennedy's separate ground is unnecessary to either holding's controlling force. The Constitution does not thereby require every civil appeal, a verbatim transcript for every claim, appellate counsel, free discovery or public financing of ordinary private litigation. The prior proof, counsel, economic-fee and custody-transition doctrines retain their own limits.

### 1996-12-16 — United States v. Sahhar; Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-50186 and 94-50356; separate Supreme Court docket unassigned

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Sahhar_merits_1996-12-16.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. A hypothetical criminal maximum does not end otherwise lawful civil custody

**Holding and operative rule:** A separately lawful §4246 commitment does not end solely because the incompetency-related prosecution has been dismissed or custody has exceeded the maximum sentence that a conviction could have carried. Continued psychiatric custody must remain supported by current mental disease or defect causing the statutory danger, an applicable civil-commitment basis and effective review, release and state-placement safeguards; a former charge, incompetence alone, danger without qualifying illness or a stale finding cannot substitute.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins. The distinct statutory commitment and discharge rules are stated in Parts IV–V at their own join scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a possible punishment does not measure recovery from a present disorder. Section 4246 supplies a civil basis distinct from prosecution for the former threat offense. Jones rejects an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling, but its initial inference from an insanity acquittal cannot be transferred to someone never adjudicated guilty of criminal conduct. Independent civil grounds must do the work here.

Jackson requires a relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose. Foucha requires current lawful psychiatric predicates and prevents a past custody basis from supporting indefinite restraint after its justification ends. Addington supplies ordinary civil commitment's adequate proof protection, while Baxstrom prevents an expired prior basis from dispensing with the new route's safeguards. Those rules reject both automatic release at five years and unlimited retention based on an old charge. A civil label or public-safety interest alone is insufficient.

The District Court's clinical findings belong to its proceeding; they are not new findings of present illness and danger as of this decision. The remand requires contemporaneous application of the lawful predicates and placement duties. The unappealed indictment dismissal remains undisturbed. No distinct enumerated-power challenge is decided because its preservation is not established, and no freestanding federal police power is recognized.

**Precedent treatment:** Jones's duration principle is applied without its acquittal-based initial presumption. Jackson, Foucha, Addington and Baxstrom preserve independent current grounds and adequate procedures; none makes criminal punishment the measure of civil treatment.

#### II. The sentence-based classification objection does not require release

**Holding and operative rule:** Equal protection does not require release merely because §4246 custody outlasts the maximum possible sentence for the former federal charge. The lawful federal-custody origin and limited transition toward appropriate state responsibility relate to this civil class, while sentence severity and the duration of present illness-caused danger address different questions; this holding selects no universal scrutiny tier and does not merge equal protection with due process.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the objection through his separate even-under-intermediate-scrutiny reasoning and does not join Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual disparity must be examined. Heller requires a relationship between the classification and the disadvantage imposed; it does not make every different commitment rule invalid or impose a universal heightened tier. Section 4246 concerns safe disposition of persons within specified federal custody channels, with treatment, present danger requirements, release routes and a continuing preference for state responsibility. The possible sentence addresses punishment after conviction, not the persistence of those civil conditions. Sahhar's lack of a conviction makes independent civil grounds necessary, but does not itself make five years a constitutional medical endpoint. This conclusion leaves each safeguard operative. Passing this equality inquiry does not establish that any individual confinement satisfies due process. Current grounds and meaningful procedures require separate examination under Part I and the statutory rules below.

**Precedent treatment:** Heller's actual-disadvantage inquiry is applied without restoring its rejected proof disparity or selecting a new tier. Foucha's noncontrolling equality discussion remains noncontrolling.

#### III. State placement requires actual and periodically renewed efforts

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §4246(d), the Attorney General must make all reasonable efforts, continued periodically, to secure the domicile or trial State's assumption of custody, care and treatment, and must release the person to the appropriate state official when an eligible State assumes that responsibility. If neither State assumes responsibility despite those efforts, suitable federal hospitalization may continue only until state assumption or a condition permitting safe unconditional or prescribed-regimen release, whichever occurs first.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the state-placement requirement must operate in practice. The statute commands all reasonable efforts and their periodic renewal; it does not make a single unsuccessful request permanently sufficient. Actual acceptance requires transfer. The alternative federal hospitalization branch operates only while its own predicates and the statute's earliest endpoint remain unsatisfied.

Arizona had not been asked to assume responsibility after dismissal of the indictment. Earlier conditional placement ended after an assault injured another inmate. That episode establishes neither present acceptance nor present refusal and cannot replace the required contemporary inquiry. The lower courts must determine Arizona's actual response, the Attorney General's efforts and the current lawful basis for custody. The Court affirms the Ninth Circuit's remand without ordering final placement, finding acceptance or directing unconditional release. Foucha and Jackson reinforce that continuing custody must retain its actual lawful basis; absence of a state placement cannot cure missing psychiatric grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Foucha and Jackson support continuing lawful justification; the concrete transfer and periodic-efforts commands come from §4246(d), not a new judicial review schedule.

#### IV. The statutory commitment categories and the unrelated-dismissal route remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4246(a) applies to a person whose sentence is about to expire, one committed under §4241(d), or one whose charges were dismissed solely for reasons related to mental condition, upon the required director's certification of present illness-caused statutory danger and unavailable suitable state arrangements. A dismissal for reasons not related to mental condition instead triggers §4246(g)'s state referral and, if neither eligible State assumes responsibility, release upon refusal notice and no later than ten days after the director's certification; it cannot be converted into subsection (d)'s continuing-hospitalization branch.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this distinct statutory-category explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that former federal status alone is not a commitment category. The specified §4246(a) certification concerns mental disease or defect causing a substantial risk of bodily injury to another person or serious damage to another's property, together with unavailable suitable state custody and care. Filing stays release pending the statutory procedures and requires a hearing. Section 4246(d) requires clear and convincing evidence of that illness-caused danger for commitment. A free-standing danger-to-self formula is not its test.

The supplied history concerns incompetency-connected custody and dismissal; it establishes no separate unrelated-dismissal ground. The Court neither finds that all dismissals are mental-condition-related nor resolves an unsupported new trigger here. Greenwood supplies no authority in this case for a general post-dismissal federal commitment power; the separate enumerated-power question remains outside review.

**Precedent treatment:** Greenwood's connection to the federal criminal process is not enlarged into a general police power. Addington's ordinary commitment protection is consistent with subsection (d)'s initial clear-and-convincing requirement; no universal discharge burden is inferred.

Counsel, adversarial participation, annual reports, habeas and the independent discharge-motion route remain operative; neither annual reporting nor the 180-day restriction on repeated discharge motions creates an automatic judicial hearing schedule.

**Authority:** Part V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this discharge-burden and detailed procedural formulation. His concurrence preserves his current clear-and-convincing proof requirement and release when the statutory route requires it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that commitment and discharge provisions cannot be collapsed. A director finding sufficient recovery must promptly certify it. Under §4246(e), the court orders discharge or conducts the hearing authorized on the Government's motion or its own motion. After that hearing the statutory recovery finding uses preponderance, with unconditional or prescribed-regimen release as the finding warrants. After a hearing on the same criteria the court may modify or eliminate the regimen. The Court decides no unpresented constitutional attack on that discharge provision.

Section 4246(f) permits action on notice of conditional-release noncompliance or other probable cause, including possible arrest and prompt presentation, followed by a hearing on whether noncompliance makes continued release create the statutory danger. It is no general authority to confine everyone once federally charged.

Section 4247(d) supplies counsel, appointed when financially necessary, and opportunities to testify, present evidence, subpoena witnesses, confront and cross-examine. Section 4247(e) requires annual reports with continued-hospitalization recommendations. Section 4247(g) preserves habeas. Section 4247(h) permits counsel or a guardian to seek discharge without the director's certificate, but bars another such motion within 180 days of a determination that hospitalization should continue. A restriction on repeat motions is not a mandatory hearing every 180 days. These actual safeguards, alongside the continuing predicates and placement duty, require lawful current application on remand; they establish no present medical or release finding.

**Precedent treatment:** Addington and Foucha preserve appropriate commitment grounds and proof without replacing §4246(e)'s separate discharge text. Jackson's relationship between custody and lawful purpose remains enforceable through the actual statutory channels.

#### Holdings

#### I. A general acquittal does not categorically bar these ordinary sentencing findings

**Holding and operative rule:** A general acquittal does not prevent a sentencing court from considering reliable relevant conduct proved by a preponderance for ordinary Guidelines sentencing within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. That permission does not authorize a new conviction, a separate disguised punishment for the acquitted offense, punishment beyond conviction-based authority, removal of an offense element from the jury, or disregard of independent due-process, evidentiary, Guidelines or Rule 32 requirements.

Section 3661 permits consideration of sentencing information, while the applicable Guidelines provide the firearm and relevant-quantity inquiries. Receipt of information and its legal use remain subject to distinct constitutional limits.

On double jeopardy, these ordinary findings calibrate lawful punishment for the offenses of conviction without reopening guilt or entering an independent judgment for the acquitted charges. Witte supports that bounded distinction and reserved this acquitted-conduct application, which the Court now resolves. Retained Grady and Dixon do not make this sentencing inquiry a new prosecution; they remain operative for actual successive prosecutions. The Court does not hold that an unchanged statutory ceiling alone answers every punishment objection.

On due process and jury allocation, McMillan permits ordinary judicial sentencing findings under a preponderance burden within lawful authority. The binding Guidelines consequences are real, but compulsion alone does not establish that these considerations are offense elements. Gaudin requires jury determination beyond reasonable doubt of every element; neither it nor Roy classifies the present sentencing facts as elements. No demonstrated element evasion is sanctioned.

Watts's possession finding is not identical to the full firearm-use offense, and Putra's second transaction is not the convicted sale. An extraordinary sentence-magnification question, a special factual acquittal and broader sentencing arrangements are not decided. Missing numerical sentencing details supply no finding that every possible increase is modest.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join the per curiam remedial direction.

**Controlling explanation:** The error concerns the legal prohibition, not a calculation this Court can supply from the reported facts. Reliable findings, the governing burden, legal sentencing authority and properly preserved ordinary objections remain necessary. Koon separates correction of a sentencing legal error from an unexamined determination that a particular term should stand. Each acquittal remains effective against conviction on the acquitted charge. Witte's conditional sentence-coordination discussion supplies no automatic aggregate cap or duplicate-punishment credit here. The Court decides no exact range, term, extraordinary-effect claim or unresolved ordinary evidentiary objection.

### 1997-01-07 — Old Chief v. United States; No. 95-6556

Authority: [Record](../records/Old_Chief_v_United_States_merits_1997-01-07.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison

**Holding and operative rule:** When an actually available, unconditional and legally adequate admission establishes the complete prior-conviction status element, that element is the Government's sole legitimate purpose for the evidence, and the name or nature of the conviction presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, Rule 403 requires exclusion of that name or nature. On this record, admitting the similar-assault judgment after rejecting the offered substitute on general prosecutorial-choice grounds was an abuse of discretion; the Ninth Circuit's no-error judgment is reversed.

The admission must establish a conviction qualifying under §922(g)(1) and §921(a)(20). The latter excludes Federal or State antitrust, unfair-trade, restraint-of-trade and similar business-regulation offenses, and State offenses classified as misdemeanors and punishable by two years or less. Conviction status follows the law of the jurisdiction of the proceedings. Expunged or set-aside convictions, pardoned convictions and convictions for which civil rights have been restored do not count, subject to the statute's qualification when the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No such exclusion is found applicable here; the offered instruction requires appropriate consensual clarification rather than an assumption that its original wording was flawless.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins to the complete evidentiary rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The prior judgment remains relevant under Rule 401. The question is whether its legitimate evidentiary value, assessed with actually available alternatives, justifies its unfair-prejudice danger. Huddleston recognizes Rule 403's contextual safeguards and attention to other proof. Review concerns the circumstances when the trial judge ruled, not hindsight.

The Government ordinarily may present convincing, coherent evidence instead of a defendant's preferred abstraction. Narrative integrity helps the jury understand the charged event and need not disappear whenever a fact is conceded. Here, however, the prior conviction supplies a detached legal status. An adequate admission supplies that complete proposition without reconstructing the earlier assault. Its name and nature add negligible legitimate force for the sole status purpose while inviting the jury to infer a disposition toward violence in a prosecution involving assault and firearms. General prosecutorial choice does not resolve that balance.

The rule does not let defendants stipulate away the narrative of a charged event, require acceptance of partial or qualified admissions, or exclude every prior-conviction record. A distinct permissible purpose requires its own lawful showing and Rule 403 balance. A sanitized judgment may also be considered if it supplies legally complete status without the impermissible prejudice. Status remains an element to be established through adequate evidence and instructions; it is not simply removed from the jury. The curable wording issue supplies no new waiver or independent affirmance ground, and the separate credibility-instruction history does not forfeit the preserved objection to the judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Huddleston's safeguards are applied to comparative status proof. Gaudin's requirement that the element be established remains intact; no general compulsory-stipulation doctrine is adopted.

#### II. Harmlessness remains a separate whole-record inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The established preserved Rule 403 error is subject to Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos: the Government must establish, from the whole record, fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt defeats harmlessness. The case is remanded for that inquiry and lawful consequences for affected convictions; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, without a present finding that every count was affected.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins to the mandate and its governing standard. Stone-Zsela does not join the decision to leave the prejudice result open.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson separates a sufficient record for identifying evidentiary error from the full record needed to assess its effect. The Ninth Circuit decided no-error admissibility and did not reach harmlessness. That omission establishes neither harmlessness nor prejudice. Kotteakos requires assessment of the error's influence, not simply whether other evidence could support guilt, and puts grave doubt against a harmlessness conclusion. Rule 52(a) preserves that inquiry; Olano's ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error does not govern this preserved objection.

The error found is nonconstitutional. Chapman is not substituted merely because the criminal trial involved a jury, and Roy's omitted personal-intent-element rule addresses a different defect. Reversal of the erroneous appellate legal conclusion does not itself require unconditional conviction vacatur and retrial. The lower court must assess properly available arguments on the actual whole record and determine the lawful effect on any affected convictions. No new hearing, acquittal, automatic retrial or unsupported count-specific disposition is ordered.

**Precedent treatment:** Williamson's preserved nonconstitutional-error rule is applied unchanged; Kotteakos supplies the substantial-influence inquiry, Olano is distinguished at preservation, and Chapman and Roy remain confined to the constitutional errors they govern.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Huddleston v. United States: applies contextual Rule 403 safeguards, including actually available evidentiary alternatives, while preserving legitimate purposes and ordinary narrative evidence.
- United States v. Gaudin: preserves the need to establish the status element through adequate evidence and jury instructions; an admission does not authorize deletion of an element.
- Williamson v. United States: applies its controlling separation of evidentiary error from whole-record prejudice and its Government-burden remand rule; the formal reversal here does not eliminate harmlessness.
- Kotteakos v. United States: applies whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, with grave doubt defeating harmlessness.
- United States v. Olano: distinguishes the ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error; no forfeiture of the preserved judgment objection is inferred.
- Chapman v. California and California v. Roy: retain their constitutional-error scope; neither supplies the standard for this ordinary Rule 403 violation.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 7, 1997, a legally complete available status admission must be considered in Rule 403's balance when a prior conviction's name or nature is offered solely for felon-status proof. Exclusion follows under the stated substantial-outweighing conditions; the conviction record is not rendered irrelevant and other permissible purposes remain distinct. The Court adds that controlling evidentiary application while applying Williamson's existing preserved-error remedy without change. It makes no present harmlessness finding. Stone-Zsela's mandatory new-trial position and the three-Justice no-abuse position are noncontrolling.

### 1997-01-14 — Abebe-Jira v. Negewo, No. 95-8873; Eleventh Circuit No. 93-9133.

#### Holdings

#### I. Jurisdiction does not itself establish a damages action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350 supplies district-court jurisdiction over an alien's civil tort action alleging a violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty; these substantial allegations of official torture fall within that jurisdiction. Jurisdiction alone neither supplies a domestic damages action nor establishes liability, sovereign-immunity waiver or authority for every asserted international claim.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute identifies the forum and category of controversy. Whether domestic law provides the asserted action is a separate question. Suter requires attention to the source of an enforceable right and action, and Meyer distinguishes federal adjudicative authority and consent from substantive liability. Those distinctions do not make a substantial source-of-action dispute a reason to deny jurisdiction over this pleaded category. The plaintiffs are aliens alleging torts violating the established prohibition of official torture. Their allegations establish the statutory kind of controversy; findings and governing law must separately support relief. No sovereign is made a defendant and no immunity issue is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Suter's source/action distinction and Meyer's separation of jurisdiction, consent and substantive liability are preserved; neither previously decided §1350's present application.

#### II. A confined received action for personally committed official torture

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350's reception of judicial adjudication of law-of-nations torts, read in its common-law setting, supports a confined domestic civil action against an individual for deliberate participation in or personal supervision of official torture. The actionable norm forbids severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation; international law supplies that specific obligatory prohibition, while the received domestic action supplies authority to enforce it.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Universal condemnation alone is not domestic remedial authority, and the jurisdictional label alone cannot resolve the action question. Congress expressly provided adjudication of law-of-nations torts against the common-law background in which such wrongs received judicial remedies. That particular statutory function supports this determinate, established personal-tort action. Filartiga supplies persuasive evidence of judicial recognition of the official-torture prohibition and its civil enforcement. Central Bank's confined received-action reasoning and Musick, Peeler's treatment of incidents of an established action offer analogies; neither itself creates an action under §1350. Suter's administrative-plan provisions supply no contrary rule for this received tort, and O'Melveny rejected a new receiver preference rather than this source-specific construction. The contrary argument correctly separates jurisdiction from liability but gives insufficient effect to the statute's particular reception of tort adjudication. The supported trial findings establish Negewo's direct participation and personal supervision concerning Abebe-Jira and Taye, and personal supervision of some torture concerning Demissie. Official title alone is insufficient. The Court sustains the judgment on torture, without adopting a general Lincoln Mills or legislative-history ground, and announces no action for every customary norm, private nonstate violence, corporate liability, absent-superior liability or every cruel, inhuman or degrading act.

**Precedent treatment:** Suter and O'Melveny are distinguished at their source-specific limits; Central Bank and Musick, Peeler supply confined received-action analogies, not existing ATS holdings. Filartiga is persuasive lower-court authority for this established personal tort.

**Holding and operative rule:** The recognized action permits compensatory damages for proved injury caused by the individual's qualifying official torture. The established trial findings support categorical compensatory authority for each plaintiff, without substituting allegations or official position for personal responsibility and causation.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Traditional tort reparation compensates an injured person for loss caused by the defendant's wrongful conduct. Franklin preserves appropriate relief within a cognizable action, while requiring the action to be established first. Part II supplies that separate source here. The bench-trial findings establish the personal torture ground; this is not a damages award based solely on the complaint. The source objection therefore does not defeat compensation once the confined action is recognized. Each plaintiff's $200,000 compensatory award remains in place against the granted categorical objection. The Court neither enlarges the judgment nor adjudicates an independent challenge to amount or trial allocation.

**Precedent treatment:** Franklin's action/remedy distinction is applied without turning its Title IX holding into an independent ATS cause or an amount-of-damages ruling.

#### IV. Punitive authority requires an independently justified aggravated-tort remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Within this established action for intentionally inflicted official torture, traditional common-law remedies permit punitive damages for malicious, willful or equivalently reckless aggravated wrongdoing; punitive relief is permitted, not required in every case. The punitive culpability standard does not dilute the underlying action's intentional-official-torture predicate, and the proved deliberate personal torture supports punitive availability here while amount and lawful assessment remain distinct questions.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stevens's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices directly adopt its complete punitive-culpability formulation. Stone-Zsela joins the authority for punitive relief and its application to the proved deliberate personal torture, but does not join the formulation concerning equivalently reckless aggravation; seven directly support the deliberate-torture ground sustaining this judgment. Scalia and Thomas reject relief on this asserted action because they reject its source.

**Controlling explanation:** Punishment cannot be inferred from compensation alone. Arms distinguishes compensation from the traditional punitive response to aggravated personal wrongdoing. Filartiga's remedial decision supplies a persuasive application to official torture. The domestic action's common-law remedial character and that independently supported aggravated-tort remedy authorize punishment here; Franklin expressly left punitive authority open and does not answer this question. The concern that punitive relief has penal and international consequences warrants attention to the source, culpability and remedy, but does not eliminate an ordinary remedy for this established intentional personal tort. No sovereign or population is punished and no criminal conviction is entered. The Court does not import Filartiga's default, its assessment of a foreign prosecution, its choice-of-law findings or its dollar amounts. Each plaintiff's $300,000 punitive award survives the categorical authority objection. TXO and BMW retain their actual constraints and disagreements: this grant supplies no independent amount challenge, wealth finding, assessment formula, required ratio or certification of every possible calculation. The Court authorizes no universal punitive remedy for international wrongs.

**Precedent treatment:** Arms supplies the traditional distinction between reparation and punishment for aggravated wrongdoing; Franklin's punitive reservation remains intact and is not treated as authorization. TXO and BMW remain controlling at their existing procedural and substantive scopes; no new excessiveness test is announced.

#### V. The foreign setting does not make this individual tort a political question

**Holding and operative rule:** Adjudication of these individual official-torture claims is not categorically barred as a political question because it applies a determinate prohibition to personal conduct without deciding the legitimacy of Ethiopia's government, directing United States diplomacy or resolving a constitutionally committed war-powers dispute. A concrete immunity defense or contrary political-branch determination would require its own consideration; none is supplied or decided here.

**Authority:** Part V of Stevens's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins. Scalia and Thomas's agreement does not supply an action or their support for the damages judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires a concrete constitutional commitment or obstacle to judicial decision, not merely a foreign setting. Montana and Nixon preserve that issue-specific inquiry. Courts can decide personal conduct under an established torture prohibition and apply ordinary remedial law without choosing a foreign government or supervising diplomacy. Sanchez-Espinoza concerned United States foreign-policy operations and domestic sovereign immunity; its express distinction of suits against foreign officials defeats using it as a categorical prohibition here. The Court does not decide a new immunity question or an unpresented defense. The absence of a political-question bar does not answer the independent source-of-action and remedy inquiries.

**Precedent treatment:** Baker's issue-specific inquiry, as retained in Montana and Nixon, is applied. Sanchez-Espinoza is distinguished at its domestic-immunity and United States foreign-policy setting; its result is not extended to this defendant.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Suter v. Artist M.: its separation of enforceable right, action and relief remains; its administrative-plan ruling does not foreclose this source-specific received action.
- FDIC v. Meyer: jurisdiction and consent remain distinct from a substantive action; no sovereign-immunity rule is enlarged.
- O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: the rejection of a new receiver preference is distinguished from §1350's particular common-law function.
- Central Bank of Denver v. First Interstate Bank and Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance: their confined received-action reasoning supports analogy, without importing securities liability or independently establishing an ATS action.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools: appropriate remedies presuppose an action; compensation is supported at that scope, and punitive authority is independently decided here.
- Milwaukee & St. Paul Railway Co. v. Arms: its traditional distinction between compensation and punitive relief for aggravated wrongdoing informs the punitive rule.
- TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources and BMW of North America v. Gore: their actual procedural and substantive punitive limits remain unchanged; no new amount-review rule is adopted.
- Baker v. Carr, United States Department of Commerce v. Montana and Nixon v. United States: their concrete political-question inquiry is applied without a foreign-affairs category bar.
- Filartiga's Second Circuit and damages decisions supply persuasive support for this established official-torture action and its remedies. Sanchez-Espinoza's domestic-immunity and United States foreign-policy setting is distinguished, consistently with its express distinction of foreign-official suits. These lower-court decisions are not treated as prior Supreme Court holdings.

#### Law After Decision

Section 1350's forum grant remains distinct from an actionable norm, a domestic action and the permitted relief. The Court recognizes a confined received action for proved personal official torture, with compensatory relief and independently justified punitive authority. It does not make every customary-international-law norm privately enforceable, impose liability from official title, adjudicate a sovereign's immunity or authorize general supervision of foreign policy. ### 1997-01-14 — Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, No. 95-928.

Authority: [Record](../records/Atherton_v_FDIC_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Review of the source-of-law argument

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court may consider the argument that no federal common-law duty exists because it concerns the same restored federal claims and is included within the presented dispute over §1821(k)'s displacement or preservation of those claims. Both sides have addressed that legal question on the existing record; consideration introduces no separate claim, additional factual finding or different requested relief.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that the merits should be reached but does not join this procedural explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must distinguish a supporting legal argument from a separate question. Yee permits new arguments for a preserved claim but retains the independent limit imposed by the question presented. United States National Bank permits consideration of necessary governing law after the parties have a fair opportunity to address it. Lebron permits, without requiring, consideration of an included legal argument on an established record. Here the existence of the asserted duty is part of deciding what federal claim survives the statute. Izumi is different: its separate adverse intervention judgment had to be displaced before the presented question could be reached. Merits briefing alone does not enlarge the grant, and the withdrawal of the state claims remains a separate procedural matter. **Precedent treatment:** Yee's argument/question distinction, United States National Bank's governing-law authority and Lebron's discretionary included-argument rule are applied within their limits; Izumi's separate-judgment limit is distinguished and preserved.

#### II. State law supplies the ordinary corporate duty

**Holding and operative rule:** State law supplies the ordinary standard of care for these acquired corporate-governance claims against officers and directors of a federally chartered savings association unless enacted federal law or a specifically demonstrated significant conflict with an identified federal policy displaces it. Federal chartering, insurance, receivership recovery and general administrative uniformity do not establish that conflict here.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** O'Melveny applies Erie's rejection of general federal common law and Wallis's requirement of a particular significant conflict with federal policy. Its independently sufficient pre-FIRREA ground does not depend on applying the later statute to earlier conduct. The asserted duties concern lending decisions, fiduciary conduct and corporate governance, subjects ordinarily governed by state law. The receiver identifies no charter command or operational obligation requiring a different rule. Kimbell Foods distinguishes rights created by federal programs and rejects automatic national uniformity even there. A desire to increase recoveries cannot supply the missing conflict. New Jersey law therefore supplies the ordinary duty for any properly available state theory, subject to federal displacement actually established. The Court neither selects New Jersey's precise standard or business-judgment rule nor decides breach, causation, defenses or liability. Congress or a valid federal regulation may prescribe a specific duty; federal enforcement of distinct statutory obligations is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** O'Melveny's acquired-claim and specific-conflict rules are applied to these corporate duties; Erie and Wallis retain their general-law and conflict limits; Boyle's exceptional-displacement requirements remain available; Kimbell Foods supplies no automatic uniform federal rule.

#### III. Section 1821(k) establishes a federal minimum and preserves other rights

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1821(k), when applicable, permits personal monetary liability of a director or officer of an insured depository institution, whether federally or state chartered, for gross negligence, similar conduct or conduct demonstrating a greater disregard of duty, including intentional tortious conduct, with those culpability terms defined under applicable state law; the action must be by, on behalf of, or at the request or direction of the Corporation, prosecuted wholly or partly for its benefit, with the Corporation acting as receiver or conservator, on a claim purchased from, assigned by or otherwise conveyed by such a receiver or conservator, or on a claim purchased from, assigned by or otherwise conveyed wholly or partly by an insured institution or its affiliate in connection with §1823 assistance. That authorization defeats a more protective state liability shield but preserves rights actually available under other applicable law, including a state duty permitting recovery for less culpable conduct: gross negligence is a federal floor, not a ceiling.

**Authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins to the textual construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted provision describes insured institutions without excluding federal charters. Its final sentence preserves other applicable rights; restricting that sentence to other provisions of the same federal enactment would deny its ordinary breadth. State law imposing an ordinary-negligence duty can therefore remain effective. Preservation of other law does not manufacture a federal common-law action or restore a withdrawn pleading. The section's authorization, the three routes by which the Corporation acts, the state-law definitions of culpability and the savings sentence each retain their separate work. Permission to impose liability does not establish breach, causation, the absence of defenses or an amount due. The text resolves the construction without reliance on legislative history. O'Melveny's rejection of an additional receiver preference supplies the source-of-law boundary; it did not previously decide this provision's minimum or savings rule. The section's temporal application is unnecessary to resolve the independently defective federal-common-law premise and remains open.

**Precedent treatment:** O'Melveny's conditional FIRREA analysis is preserved without converting it into a retroactivity decision; its source-of-law rule supplies no additional federal action. Landgraf's provision-specific temporal inquiry remains available when application to earlier conduct must actually be decided.

#### IV. Vacatur requires further proceedings under the correct sources

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit judgment is vacated because it restored the asserted general federal-common-law theories and excluded federally chartered institutions from §1821(k). On remand the lower courts must apply the state-law starting point and the federal provision at its lawful scope, resolve properly available claims and defenses, and determine any liability and relief without treating this decision as reinstatement of withdrawn state claims.

**Authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate court reserved the content of its proposed federal standard, including ordinary versus gross negligence. Its judgment is not an adjudication of ordinary-negligence liability. The statutory pleading remains for ordinary adjudication. State claims were treated as withdrawn below; the effect of that treatment, permissible amendment and any properly available state theory remain for the lower courts. Neither the savings sentence nor correction of the federal source resurrects those claims. O'Melveny distinguishes correction of an erroneous federal rule from automatic reinstatement of an earlier judgment. Its same distinction supports this bounded remand. The Court decides neither the precise New Jersey standard nor FIRREA's temporal application, and awards no damages.

**Precedent treatment:** O'Melveny's separation of the governing-law correction from final relief is applied; no liability or temporal rule is added by the mandate.

#### Precedent Treatment

- O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: its state-law starting point, specific-conflict requirement and separation of source correction from ultimate relief govern; the §1821(k) floor and savings construction is decided here. Its conditional FIRREA analysis remains conditional.

### 1997-01-14 — Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc., No. 95-259; Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc., No. 95-779.

Authority: [Record](../records/Walters_v_Metropolitan_Educational_Enterprises_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Count genuine employment relationships on each working day

**Holding and operative rule:** For Title VII's requirement of fifteen or more employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year, count an individual on every working day during an existing employment relationship, whether or not that person worked or was paid that day. Payroll ordinarily evidences that relationship but is not conclusive: a person not yet hired, already separated, an independent contractor or within an express statutory exclusion does not become an employee merely by appearing on a list.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute counts employees the employer has, not daily units of compensated labor. Employment continues across ordinary unpaid days, part-time schedules and leave. The daily requirement still does real work: an employee must have the relationship on each pertinent working day, so days before hiring or after separation do not count. Metropolitan's alternative counted paid leave; it is therefore a compensation rule, not simply an actual-work rule. But compensation on a particular day does not follow from Congress's choice of the employment relationship. Darden supplies the common-law agency approach to a circular employee definition, considering all relevant incidents without a single decisive factor. Town & Country's compatible-service and actual-control analysis supports attention to the real relationship within each statute's terms. Neither case had already decided Title VII's daily-counting language. Payroll offers ordinary administrable evidence without displacing legal status. The Court adopts no exclusion or worker-status dispute absent from this stipulated record, and does not decide whether numerosity is jurisdictional.

**Qualifications:** The industry-affecting-commerce requirement and employer-agent language remain. Employer exclusions remain for the United States, wholly United States-owned corporations, Indian tribes, District of Columbia departments or agencies subject by statute to competitive-service procedures, and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations exempt under §501(c). The first-year-after-March-24-1972 transition for employers with fewer than twenty-five employees does not govern this period. The separate employee exclusions remain for elected state or local public officers, their chosen personal staff, policymaking appointees and immediate advisers concerning the office's constitutional or legal powers; those exclusions do not cover employees subject to applicable state or local civil-service laws. Section 2000e(f)'s inclusion of United States citizens employed abroad does not alter this domestic count. No exclusion is found to cover the two part-time employees here.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden's genuine-relationship framework is applied by analogy at its common-law scope; Town & Country's attention to compatible employment and enacted exclusions is preserved without transplanting NLRA exclusions into Title VII.

#### II. The stipulated minimum satisfies the 1990 numerical requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Under relationship counting, the stipulated 1990 record establishes at least thirty-eight weeks in which Metropolitan had the required employees on each working day, exceeding the twenty-week requirement. That minimum resolves the 1990 numerical objection to the retaliatory-discharge claim without a remand to recompute an exact total or litigate an unsupported worker-status dispute.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The pertinent years for the retaliatory-discharge claim are 1990 and 1989. The stipulated failure to reach the threshold in 1989 remains, but success in 1990 suffices. The original count identified forty-seven weekly payroll counts without ensuring presence on every working day in each week. Even excluding all nine weeks potentially affected by midweek employment changes leaves forty-seven minus nine, or thirty-eight, qualifying weeks. No larger exact total is needed, and the Court does not decide whether an excluded week might also qualify. The separate nine-week figure under the rejected compensation method describes a different calculation; the record does not identify those as the same weeks. Metropolitan disputed the relationship criterion rather than the use of payroll evidence to establish the stipulated relationships. The legal possibility that payroll can be rebutted elsewhere therefore supplies no present factual contest. This application does not classify every possible payroll entry or establish continuous threshold employment throughout all fifty-two weeks.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden and Town & Country preserve the distinction between legal status and an evidentiary list; neither requires relitigation of these stipulated relationships.

#### III. The unpresented promotion-year issue receives no relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to review the separate coverage-year discrepancy for Walters's September 1989 promotion claim because she raised no objection to it in her petition or merits brief and no supported exceptional reason warrants review. The existing promotion dismissal remains undisturbed on that prudential basis, without deciding its substantive correctness, an unknown 1988 count or intentional waiver.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the disposition leaving the promotion dismissal undisturbed and does not join this explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory current-or-preceding-year formula identifies 1988 and 1989 for the September 1989 promotion claim. The District Court instead used 1989 and 1990. That mismatch is a separate claim-specific issue; the supported omission from both petition and merits brief is an actual filing omission, not an inference from unavailable papers. Yee preserves the independent question-presented limit. Williams permits review of a question actually passed upon in its particular circumstances; the district court's choice of years does not establish such appellate consideration. Zafiro shows that declining review may leave a judgment undisturbed without a merits rejection or intentional-waiver finding. United States National Bank and Lebron permit consideration of included legal questions on established records, but do not require it or authorize automatic restoration of a claim after the Court declines review. The retaliation counting question can be answered without a 1988 finding. The Court neither treats the missing count as evidence against Walters nor uses its absence to reopen the promotion judgment. It does not hold that 1990 is a proper year for that claim, that the promotion was lawful or that review is jurisdictionally barred.

**Precedent treatment:** Yee and Zafiro support bounded nonreview and its undisturbed-judgment consequence; Williams, United States National Bank and Lebron retain their permissive, circumstance-specific scope and supply no mandatory promotion remand.

#### IV. The remand concerns retaliation, with the numerical objection resolved

**Holding and operative rule:** The compensation-based judgment is reversed within the retaliatory-discharge dispute in the consolidated cases, and that dispute is remanded for unresolved liability, genuinely remaining defenses and appropriate relief under the correct counting rule. The 1990 numerical objection is resolved, while the promotion dismissal remains undisturbed and this mandate supplies no authority to reopen its calendar years, preservation or unknown 1988 count.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion has nine direct joins concerning reversal and further retaliation proceedings; its promotion-disposition explanation has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part III, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only to that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Meeting the statutory count establishes no retaliation, discrimination, causation, damages or ultimate liability. Those questions remain for ordinary adjudication. Conversely, the established minimum requires no remand merely to determine whether thirty-eight exceeds twenty. The mandate must respect the claim-specific limit stated in Part III: nonreview leaves an existing dismissal in place without a new affirmance of its substantive correctness. The Court expresses no view about an independently available procedure outside this mandate. The lower courts' description of dismissal as jurisdictional remains procedural history, not a new Supreme Court classification of numerosity.

**Precedent treatment:** Zafiro's distinction between nonreview and merits adjudication governs the promotion limit; Darden and Town & Country provide no automatic liability or remedial award from employee classification.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden: its common-law agency approach to a circular employee definition informs genuine relationship counting; no individual status is inferred from a payroll label alone.
- NLRB v. Town & Country Electric: its compatible-service and actual-control analysis is used at its stated NLRA scope; Title VII's own exclusions govern here.
- Yee v. City of Escondido: the distinction between an included argument and a separate question remains, together with the prudential question-presented limit.
- United States v. Williams: permission to review a question actually passed upon below does not require review of this omitted promotion-year issue.
- Zafiro v. United States: prudential nonreview can leave the existing judgment undisturbed without adjudicating merits, jurisdictional character or intentional waiver.
- United States National Bank of Oregon and Lebron: their permissive governing-law and included-argument rules do not mandate review or restoration of the promotion claim.

#### Law After Decision

Title VII's daily employee count follows genuine employment relationships, ordinarily shown by payroll, with hiring, separation, legal status and enacted exclusions preserved. Payment on every working day is not required. The stipulated minimum resolves this employer's 1990 numerical objection for retaliation; it does not resolve ultimate liability or the jurisdictional character of numerosity. The separate September 1989 promotion claim receives no reopening through this mandate.

### 1997-01-14 - Castano v. American Tobacco Co.; Fifth Circuit No. 95-30725; 84 F.3d 734; interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b).

Authority: [Record](../records/Castano_v_American_Tobacco_Co_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. All four holdings command nine votes.

##### I. Certification requires a demonstrated legal and evidentiary basis

**Holding and operative rule.** Before certifying a Rule 23(b)(3) class, a court must determine from the actual claims, governing law and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and class adjudication is superior to the available alternatives, together with satisfaction of Rule 23(a). Conditional certification does not permit postponing that showing, and a claim's novelty neither dispenses with rigorous analysis nor categorically bars certification until individual verdicts exist.

**Controlling explanation.** Falcon requires rigorous examination of the Rule's prerequisites. Eisen forbids awarding or denying certification merely because the court predicts success on the merits; it does not make the inquiry pleadings-only when the substantive elements determine whether a common trial can work. The proponents must identify what the class proceeding would decide and how that work connects to remaining claims and defenses. An untested liability theory makes that explanation especially necessary, but the Rule contains no tort-maturation requirement. Common evidence of manufacturer conduct cannot itself establish that the resulting questions predominate over the unresolved legal and individual work. Administrative convenience cannot replace a prerequisite. Matsushita preserves constitutionally adequate representation as distinct from notice or opt-out rights, and Richards preserves the lawful basis needed to bind absent persons; neither supplies a finding of actual adequacy or conflict in this case. A renewed proposal must satisfy the representation requirements as well as predominance and superiority.

##### II. The present core-liability class cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule.** This nationwide core-liability certification fails Rule 23(b)(3) because the court deferred the material state-law inquiry and did not show how common issues, individual addiction, reliance, causation and defenses would fit into lawful, manageable adjudication. Differences among state laws do not automatically prohibit every multistate class, but proponents must demonstrate materially common answers or workable groupings rather than assume uniformity or rely on subclass labels.

**Controlling explanation.** Shutts prevents applying a single State's law to materially disconnected claims merely for convenience; it does not select the governing law here. The pleaded fraud, misrepresentation, negligence, warranty and consumer-protection claims require examination of their actual duties, defenses and burdens. The District Court certified core conduct while excluding injury, causation, reliance, defenses and individual compensatory damages. Without explaining how the common answers would materially resolve the legally varied claims, the court could not establish predominance or make a grounded superiority comparison. The proponents' strongest point is that common manufacturer evidence is substantial and individual litigation expensive. But an imagined future trial structure cannot supply the missing showing for the order entered. Workable legal groupings or genuinely separable common issues may matter on a different proposal. The Court requires a credible account of claims and proof, not a universal separate document bearing the title trial plan. It does not decide the full relationship between Rule 23(b)(3) and (c)(4), including whether issue certification may ever proceed when an entire cause of action fails an action-wide predominance inquiry.

##### III. The punitive component and superiority finding also fail

**Holding and operative rule.** The certified punitive-ratio component fails because its state-law predicates, burdens, relation to liability and coordination with individual proceedings were not demonstrated; those same gaps defeat the present superiority finding. Settlement pressure from a procedure that bypasses defenses or detaches punitive assessment from established claims may reinforce concrete certification defects, but aggregate size or ordinary settlement pressure alone is not a categorical bar.

**Controlling explanation.** The certification order incorporated important elements of the proposed plan, including its punitive-ratio arrangement, without adopting that plan in full. The court did not establish what one jury could lawfully decide about punishment before the remaining individualized liability work, or how later proceedings would use that decision consistently with each governing law. Predicted savings from millions of trials cannot substitute for identifying the work the common trial would actually accomplish. The manufacturers' concern about repeated jury determinations underscores the need for that account, but repetition of evidence is not automatically reexamination of a decided fact. The Court rests on Rule 23 and does not decide the constitutionality of every punitive ratio, issue class or bifurcated trial. The proposed Liggett settlement does not establish a completed release or make the certification controversy moot. No manufacturer liability, individual recovery, punitive amount, Article III severance question, new Seventh Amendment rule or complete resolution of every asserted Erie objection is decided.

##### IV. Decertification does not require a categorical ban on reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The rejection of the present core-liability and punitive certification is affirmed, but the mandate is vacated insofar as it requires dismissal of the class pleading and bars reconsideration merely because the tort has not matured through individual trials. The case is remanded for ordinary proceedings on individual claims or consideration of a materially narrower, supported class proposal that independently satisfies all applicable Rule 23 requirements; the existing class remains uncertified.

**Controlling explanation.** The lower court identified decisive defects in this proposal, then treated the asserted novelty of the tort as making correction on remand categorically unavailable. The Rule requires a present showing, not a quota of prior trials or an existing crisis in individual litigation. Rejecting an unsupported class does not decide whether a materially different proposal can establish its prerequisites. The proponents retain their burden; remand creates no presumption of certification, discovery entitlement, preferred trial plan or deadline. It adjudicates no individual claim on the merits. The separate denial of Rule 23(b)(2) equitable and medical-monitoring certification was not cross-appealed and is not reopened.

#### Precedent Treatment

- General Telephone Co. of Southwest v. Falcon, 457 U.S. 147: applied to require rigorous determination of the actual certification prerequisites, including where the inquiry overlaps substantive issues.
- Eisen v. Carlisle & Jacquelin, 417 U.S. 156: preserved as forbidding certification based merely on a preliminary prediction of merits success; it does not forbid the substantive inquiry needed to determine whether class adjudication can work.
- Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts, 472 U.S. 797: applied against assuming one State's substantive law for materially disconnected claims; no particular choice-of-law result is directed.
- Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996: its adequate-representation requirement remains distinct from notice and opt-out rights; its judgment-recognition holding does not itself decide prospective certification or the actual adequacy of this class.
- Richards v. Jefferson County, June 10, 1996: preserved for the lawful basis necessary to bind absent persons; it does not constitutionalize every Rule 23 defect or resolve the reserved certification questions.

#### Law After Decision

Rule 23 requires the concrete law-and-proof showing before certification. Neither novelty nor ordinary aggregation pressure creates an independent exclusion, and differing state laws require demonstrated analysis rather than an automatic national-class prohibition. The present certification is defeated by its own missing prerequisites. The partial vacatur preserves lawful reconsideration without reinstating or presuming any class, modifying individual substantive burdens, or creating a federal tobacco tort.

### 1997-01-14 - Chamber of Commerce v. Reich; D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; 74 F.3d 1322; no Supreme Court docket number assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Chamber_of_Commerce_v_Reich_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. Each proposition below therefore has nine votes; judgment agreement and reasoning agreement coincide.

##### I. Review of the implementing officer

**Holding and operative rule.** A person injured by an officer's implementation of a restriction outside delegated statutory authority may seek prospective equitable restraint under federal-question jurisdiction unless Congress has displaced that remedy. Section 702 waives sovereign immunity for this nonmonetary official-capacity suit against the implementing Secretary even without a pleaded APA review cause, while preserving other legal and equitable review limits and relief prohibitions in another consent-to-suit statute; any injunction must identify the officer or successor responsible for compliance.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 1331 supplies jurisdiction; traditional officer-directed equity supplies the action; §702 addresses sovereign immunity. None independently creates a damages claim or a cause for every statutory error. McAnnulty, Stark and Harmon establish that the absence of an express review provision does not itself eliminate prospective officer relief. Larson and Dugan support the confined distinction between authorized action and action beyond delegated power, without making every allegation of error sufficient. The employers challenge an operative disqualification imposed by the Secretary, not merely the wisdom of an authorized purchase. Their claim presents a judicially manageable boundary between two federal statutes. Dalton bars reopening its particular completed base-closure package; the Procurement Act contains no comparable structure foreclosing this action. Franklin preserves review of a completed subordinate act despite later presidential involvement. The origin of this restriction in a presidential order does not answer whether its implementing officer has statutory authority. The Court neither decides the President's APA status nor authorizes coercive relief against him.

##### II. The procurement delegation does not authorize this labor-policy sanction

**Holding and operative rule.** The Procurement Act's general economy-and-efficiency delegation does not authorize Executive Order 12954's government-wide disqualification based on permanent replacement of economic strikers, because that sanction alters the bargaining choices Congress preserved under the NLRA. This statutory holding leaves Congress free to change those choices and preserves ordinary review of materially different proprietary conditions and lawful centralized purchasing standards.

**Controlling explanation.** Mackay Radio recognizes the use of permanent replacements for economic strikers within the NLRA's remaining protections; it does not equate those strikers with unfair-labor-practice strikers. Insurance Agents and Machinists identify the economic weapons Congress left to the bargaining parties. A general purchasing delegation must operate with that allocation, not silently displace it. Boston Harbor protects a lawful bargain justified by the public purchaser's project interests; a procurement label alone does not turn general labor policy into proprietary conduct. This order makes replacement itself the disqualifying event and reaches affiliates capable of supplying the contract, even when the striking workers do not perform that contract. The Government's interest in dependable supply and retained experience is legitimate, but the policy's actual operation goes beyond a supported performance bargain. Its threshold and discretionary exceptions do not cure that statutory mismatch. The Court neither imposes a universal one-project rule nor invalidates purchasing standards because of their size. It does not guarantee any employer government business. The ground is construction of the federal enactments together, not mechanical application of state-preemption labels or a constitutional limit on Congress.

**Operative qualifications.** The order concerns contracts above $100,000. After finding permanent replacement of lawfully striking workers, the Secretary may find termination for convenience appropriate unless the contracting agency head objects; termination is not mandatory. Debarment retains exceptions when the precipitating dispute has been resolved as determined by the Secretary or the agency head finds a compelling reason to lift it. Debarment normally reaches the organizational units found to have used permanent replacements; the regulations define a unit to include the responsible prime-contracting division or element and any affiliate capable of supplying the contracted goods or services. In deciding whether a dispute ended, the regulations consider a formal settlement or agreed dispute-resolution procedure, informal resolution, the strikers' return, and other factors tending to show the dispute has ended. The Court does not adopt the lower court's reading of those factors as a universal requirement of union approval or find that every application compels unlawful recognition.

##### III. The relief follows the statutory defect

**Holding and operative rule.** The judgment reversing dismissal and rejecting the challenged implementation is affirmed, and the case is remanded for appropriate prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against the implementing Secretary confined to the unlawful procurement restriction. No injunction against the President, damages, contract award, striker reinstatement or employee-specific bargaining remedy is ordered.

**Controlling explanation.** The reviewable defect concerns the Secretary's implementation of an unauthorized restriction. Prospective officer relief addresses that defect without transferring purchasing decisions to the courts or deciding individual labor rights. The lower court must formulate the appropriate decree consistently with §702's responsible-officer requirement and retained legal and equitable limits. No independent nondelegation ruling, mandatory procurement-findings procedure or decision on a materially different proprietary measure is necessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

- McAnnulty, 187 U.S. 94: applied for prospective review of officer action beyond delegated statutory authority despite the absence of an express review provision.
- Stark v. Wickard, 321 U.S. 288: applied for the availability of judicial protection against officer action exceeding statutory limits.
- Harmon v. Brucker, 355 U.S. 579: applied for judicial determination of an officer's statutory authority before accepting a claim of unreviewable discretion.
- Larson v. Domestic & Foreign Commerce Corp., 337 U.S. 682: applied at its confined officer-action scope; statutory error is not converted into a universal immunity exception.
- Dugan v. Rank, 372 U.S. 609: applied for the corresponding distinction between sovereign relief and action outside lawful officer authority.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992: its review of completed subordinate action is preserved; presidential APA status and coercive presidential relief remain reserved.
- Dalton v. Specter, May 23, 1994: distinguished because its completed base-closure scheme supplies preclusion absent here; its rule is not general executive immunity.
- NLRB v. Mackay Radio & Telegraph Co., 304 U.S. 333: applied for permanent replacement of economic strikers within the NLRA's retained protections, without changing the protection of unfair-labor-practice strikers.
- NLRB v. Insurance Agents' International Union, 361 U.S. 477: applied for Congress's preservation of economic pressure in bargaining.
- Machinists v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Commission, 427 U.S. 132: its account of the economic choices Congress left unregulated informs the statutory construction; it is not mechanically transferred from state regulation to every federal enactment.
- Boston Harbor, March 8, 1993: distinguished on the actual proprietary bargain; its protection of lawful project purchasing and its rejection of procurement labels as sufficient remain intact. No universal one-project restriction is added.

#### Law After Decision

Federal courts may hear this confined prospective challenge to the implementing Secretary. Executive Order 12954's challenged replacement-based restriction cannot operate under the general procurement delegation. Lawful proprietary measures and centralized purchasing standards remain subject to their own statutory justification; no entitlement to a government contract follows. The Court adds this statute-specific limit without changing Dalton's actual preclusion rule or deciding presidential APA status.

### 1997-01-14 - In re Deuel; Federal Circuit No. 94-1202; 51 F.3d 1552; application 07/542,232; no Supreme Court docket number assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/In_re_Deuel_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. Every rule and each claim-group disposition below commands nine votes.

##### I. Product obviousness turns on supported ordinary skill, not exact prior disclosure

**Holding and operative rule.** Under §103(a), the claimed subject matter as a whole is compared with the prior art and the capabilities of a person of ordinary skill when the invention was made; absence of the product's exact nucleotide sequence from the art is not a safe harbor. A known method may establish obviousness with the relevant art when evidence connects a sufficiently identified target, appropriate source material and a predictably effective route to the claimed product, but a useful research objective and a familiar general technique alone do not establish that connection.

**Controlling explanation.** Graham requires consideration of the prior art, differences from the claim, ordinary skill and relevant objective evidence. Bonito Boats preserves nonobviousness as a threshold beyond literal novelty. Section 103 expressly addresses inventions not identically disclosed under §102, so requiring the art to print the precise product would collapse that distinction. Conversely, the availability of cloning does not establish that every product ultimately obtained by it was within ordinary skill. The Office must support the route from the actual art to the actual subject matter; the applicant's success cannot retrospectively supply that evidence. The manner in which an invention was made does not itself defeat patentability. Predictable-method evidence is therefore relevant and may be sufficient with the necessary supporting art, while generalized encouragement to investigate is not enough. Genetic-code redundancy bears on what the art disclosed and what a worker could predict; it does not establish nonobviousness by arithmetic alone. The Court rejects the appellate opinion's categorical separation of familiar methods from product obviousness, without finding that the Office supplied the missing technical proof here.

##### II. The rejection of the specific sequences lacks the necessary evidentiary connection

**Holding and operative rule.** The reversal of the §103 rejection of claims 5 and 7, which recite the particular human placental and bovine uterine cDNA sequences, is affirmed because the agency did not support the connection between Bohlen's incomplete protein disclosure, Maniatis's general screening method and these claimed products. A label of routine cloning and the applicants' failure to disprove unsupported agency assumptions do not discharge the Office's initial burden.

**Controlling explanation.** Bohlen supplies the first nineteen amino acids of brain mitogens, not complete protein sequences or the claimed DNA. Maniatis supplies general library screening rather than the target-specific library selection and probe design needed here. The agency assumed identity between the disclosed proteins and the claimed growth factors and treated tissue and species differences as immaterial without adequate support. A motivation to clone a useful protein's gene leaves those gaps unresolved. Graham's ordinary-skill inquiry permits proven laboratory capabilities to matter; it does not authorize the Court or litigation counsel to invent the missing factual bridge. Even under the correct noncategorical standard, the grounds actually given fail. No findings of commercial success, unexpected results or others' failed research are supplied to create a different basis for decision.

##### III. The encoding genera require their own analysis and fail on the agency's stated grounds

**Holding and operative rule.** The reversal of the §103 rejection of claims 4 and 6, covering DNA encoding the identified 168-amino-acid proteins, is separately affirmed because the agency articulated no independently supported genus ground and left unresolved the same target-identification and recovery assumptions. A genus does not become nonobvious merely because every member was not previously identified: a supported obvious species may matter, and the Court does not declare these genera nonobvious against all possible art.

**Controlling explanation.** The broader claims cannot automatically receive every defense applicable to the two particular sequences. Their actual scope may make a rejection easier to establish, but scope cannot substitute for a supported rejection. The Office supplied no independent explanation showing that a product within these genera would have been obvious on this record. New technical assertions by counsel cannot cure that omission. Markman requires attention to the operative claim boundary while retaining the distinction between construction and factual work; it supplies no DNA-obviousness test and decides no standard for all PTO fact review. Separate examination of the broad claims therefore leads to the same disposition for an independently stated reason, without converting them into narrow sequence claims or declaring the entire genus patentable.

##### IV. Affirmance removes these rejections without ordering allowance

**Holding and operative rule.** The Federal Circuit's judgment reversing the existing §103 rejections of claims 4–7 is affirmed on the grounds and record presented; no patent issuance, new rejection or mandatory reopening to rehabilitate the unsupported grounds is ordered. Independently proper further examination remains available under ordinary notice and procedure, and §101 eligibility, §112 disclosure or enablement, priority and infringement are not decided.

**Controlling explanation.** Correcting an erroneous categorical legal rule may warrant remand when a necessary factual determination remains blocked by it. Here the agency's unsupported premises independently fail for both claim groups, so correcting the appellate overstatement does not restore the rejections. Any different statutory defect needs its own ground, notice and record. The 1995 amendment retained the product rule in §103(a) and added qualified treatment of biotechnological process claims; the claims here are products, and no process-claim election is assumed. The Court does not turn the amendment into a product exception. The judgment respects lawful examination without awarding exclusivity for research effort or deciding a reserved question.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Graham v. John Deere Co., 383 U.S. 1: applied for the prior-art, claim-difference, ordinary-skill and relevant objective-evidence inquiry; the Court adds no exact-sequence prerequisite or biotechnology exception.
- Bonito Boats, Inc. v. Thunder Craft Boats, Inc., 489 U.S. 141: applied for the distinction between novelty and nonobviousness, including what ordinary skill can deduce.
- Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., April 23, 1996: applied only for preserving actual claim boundaries and the separation of construction from factual work. Its embedded-construction allocation remains intact; the decision supplies no obviousness rule, universal PTO fact-review standard or mandatory construction hearing.

The Federal Circuit's categorical suggestion that familiar methods cannot establish product obviousness without advance exact structural disclosure is rejected. Its reversal of the particular rejections is affirmed on the record-specific grounds stated here. No earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Section 103(a) governs these product claims without a special DNA safe harbor or presumption against biological inventions. Supported evidence of a predictive route can establish obviousness even without an advance printed sequence; a research objective plus general technique cannot alone carry the Office's burden. The present sequence and genus rejections fail independently on the agency grounds supplied. That result grants no patent and does not foreclose a different, properly supported examination ground with ordinary procedure.

### 1997-01-21 - Babbitt v. Youpee, No. 95-1595; OT1996.

Authority: [Record](../records/Babbitt_v_Youpee_merits_1997-01-21.md).

#### Holdings

##### The amended escheat provision still takes the presented succession interests without compensation

**Holding and operative rule:** Amended §207 of the Indian Land Consolidation Act effects an uncompensated taking as applied to these fractional trust-land interests: it eliminates intestate succession and confines devise to existing co-owners of the same parcel, leaving these owners unable to transmit valuable interests to their chosen descendants even though each devise preserves an existing fraction. The prospective low-income trigger and rebuttable presumption, retained lifetime powers, and statutory option for a federally approved tribal code do not cure that operation where no operative substitute code is identified for this estate.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes support this holding and its remedy. Justice Stevens dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Hodel v. Irving, which protects the valuable power to transmit property at death while permitting Congress to address the serious problem of fractionated Indian lands. Penn Central supplies the examination of economic impact, investment-backed expectations, and the character of the governmental action. Low annual income does not establish that these interests lack value: their reported aggregate value is $1,239. Expectations of investment return are not strong, but that consideration does not answer the extraordinary destruction of succession.

Congress made real changes. Subsection (a) reaches an interest of 2 percent or less incapable of earning $100 in any one of the five years from death. Earnings below $100 in any one of the preceding five years create a rebuttable presumption of that future incapacity. Subsection (b) permits devise to another owner of an undivided interest in the same parcel. Subsection (c) permits a Secretary-approved tribal disposition code to take precedence over subsection (a), but prohibits approval of a code that fails to prevent further descent or fractionation of escheatable interests.

Those changes remain insufficient here. Intestacy is wholly barred, the devise class excludes these descendants, and no operative alternative code is identified for this estate. Each devise passes one existing fraction to one descendant; none divides that fraction further. Retained lifetime enjoyment and transfer do not adequately replace succession. Congress's important consolidation objective cannot by itself answer the uncompensated loss. Nearly six years to arrange a lifetime transfer or qualifying devise do not cure the inadequate transmission choices this mechanism preserves. The Court therefore sustains the existing declaratory and injunctive protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt respondents' proposed categorical extension of Lucas, hold every inheritance restriction a taking, value compensation, prescribe a consolidation plan, or decide a different tribal code. Purchase, compensated acquisition, voluntary consolidation, and other constitutional measures remain available; no particular alternative receives advance approval. The Court decides no independent individualized-notice or due-process claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hodel v. Irving, 481 U.S. 704 (1987):** Applies its protection against the extraordinary uncompensated destruction of succession and resolves the amended statute that Irving reserved. Its recognition of legislative power to address fractionation remains intact; no universal beneficiary-choice right is adopted.
- **Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978):** Applies its impact, expectations, and governmental-character analysis in the succession setting; no factor becomes conclusive by itself.
- **Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, June 29, 1992:** Leaves its complete-economic-use rule and background-law exception unchanged. The Court does not extend that category to destruction of every separate property attribute.
- **Dolan v. City of Tigard, June 24, 1994:** Leaves its individualized permit-exaction doctrine unchanged; this case presents no development-permit dedication.
- **Andrus v. Allard, 444 U.S. 51 (1979):** Retains its treatment of the particular ban on sale of lawfully possessed artifacts. Retention of some property powers does not displace Irving's analysis of the succession restriction here; the Court makes no broader revision of Allard.
- **Texaco, Inc. v. Short, 454 U.S. 516 (1982):** Does not make the opportunity for advance planning conclusive of the different uncompensated succession burden here. Its treatment of the mineral-interest lapse law is not overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 21, 1997, Irving's succession protection governs the amended mechanism as applied to these interests. The co-owner devise route, income provisions, and tribal-code option do not sustain this uncompensated escheat. Congress and the tribes retain authority to pursue constitutional consolidation measures. The decision adds this application of Irving; it creates no categorical rule governing every inheritance restriction and changes no Lucas or Dolan test.

### 1997-01-21 - Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth; historical petition identifier No. 95-617; no separate merits docket assigned; OT1996.

Authority: [Record](../records/Curtis_v_School_Committee_of_Falmouth_merits_1997-01-21.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Every holding below has nine direct rationale votes.

##### I. The optional program does not establish the claimed parental-liberty infringement

**Holding and operative rule:** The described school condom-availability program does not violate parental liberty or familial privacy merely because parents cannot require the school to exclude their children or notify them of participation. Program entry is optional, abstention carries no educational or disciplinary consequence, and the program does not prohibit parental teaching, alter custody, compel treatment or sexual conduct, or require assent to a message; the junior-high counseling condition applies only after a student elects to request condoms.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Meyer and Pierce, which protect parental direction of upbringing and education, together with Yoder's protection against the particular compulsory educational interference with religious family life and Prince's recognition that parental authority does not exclude every public role concerning children. Those decisions do not establish the particular school-enforced veto sought here.

The school provides the item directly, not merely information about it. That difference matters, but it does not itself displace parental authority. Parents remain free to teach against participation and instruct their children to abstain. Students may comply without losing instruction, grades, benefits, or standing at school. Compulsory school attendance does not turn every separable optional resource into compelled participation.

A junior-high student requesting condoms must receive counseling before receipt. The Court does not erase that condition or approve unknown counseling content. Nothing in the stated condition requires entry into the program, a prescribed affirmation, sexual activity, or a medical procedure. No independent coercive-counseling claim is established here.

A. St. P. C. protects against a complete, potentially indefinite parental-contact cutoff; Baby Richard protects against serious avoidable harm in the manner of compulsory custody transfer; M.L.B. protects necessary-record access in a provided first appeal from permanent parental termination. None of those deprivations occurs here. Practical difficulty in securing a child's adherence to parental teaching does not, on these facts, establish a constitutional right to compel the school to enforce the requested refusal.

**Limits:** This holding does not decide confidential medical treatment, prescription drugs, compelled sex education, distribution to a child unable to consent, or a program using penalty or deception to undermine parental authority. It neither denies parental liberty nor recognizes an independent constitutional entitlement to receive condoms.

##### II. The stated program imposes no established constitutional burden on religious exercise

**Holding and operative rule:** A program that students may decline without sacrificing ordinary education or another benefit, and that requires neither religiously prohibited participation nor assent, does not burden these families' free exercise merely by making the described condoms available contrary to their religious teaching. The stated program establishes no religious targeting, unequal treatment, penalty for religious refusal, or comparable official pressure to participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Lyng and Bowen v. Roy distinguish sincere conflict with government activity from interference with protected religious exercise; they do not place all government services beyond constitutional review. Here the school demands no endorsement of contraception or premarital sex, no use of condoms, and no surrender of worship or religious instruction. The junior-high receipt condition does not compel anyone to enter the program and supplies no established contrary religious affirmation.

The parents' concerns about peer influence and compulsory attendance deserve consideration. Lee's treatment of substantial official pressure at school-directed graduation prayer remains intact. Students here may avoid the separable resource without giving up the educational occasion or apparently joining a state-directed religious exercise. The asserted generalized peer influence does not establish comparable official pressure under these program terms. The holding therefore does not depend on a universal rule that only formal punishment can coerce.

The claim fails at its constitutional burden threshold. The Court need not decide whether Smith would independently defeat a claim involving an actual burden under a neutral law. Invoking parental liberty and free exercise together supplies neither the missing infringement nor automatic strict scrutiny. Lukumi's religious-targeting protections and Settle's prohibition on supported religious exclusion remain fully applicable to materially different facts.

**Limits:** The Court does not approve religious targeting, compelled participation in sexual instruction, denial of an exemption from a mandatory practice, or benefits conditioned on conduct forbidden by faith. The separate statutory inquiry under RFRA is not decided.

##### III. Neither parental exclusion nor notification is constitutionally required on these grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** Because neither claimed constitutional deprivation is established by this program's stated operation, the Constitution does not require the requested parental exclusion or notification mechanism, and summary judgment for the school defendants stands on the granted constitutional claims. No injunction, new evidentiary proceeding, or prescribed parental procedure follows.

**Controlling explanation:** An exemption from compelled conduct differs from a parental right to bar a child's voluntary access. The child can already decline this program without consequence; the claimed school-enforced veto requires a substantive constitutional source that the presented parental and religious claims do not supply. Casey permitted a qualified parental-consent arrangement for abortion while preserving an independent judicial route; permission for such a statute does not command parental control over every preventive item.

The parents' expert affidavits concerning possible adverse consequences are not findings of harm or harmlessness. Under the adopted no-infringement grounds, disputed policy wisdom and comparative health effects do not create a material constitutional fact requiring trial. The judgment does not establish that the program is medically necessary, effective, harmless, or the least restrictive policy.

**Limits:** The Court does not prohibit voluntary parental accommodation, resolve different consent or disclosure laws, or decide an independent RFRA claim. Unargued state statutory claims concerning statutory-rape accomplice liability, abuse reporting, and moral education, and unpresented state constitutional claims, remain outside review. No finding that a medical-consent claim was waived is made.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meyer v. Nebraska, 262 U.S. 390, and Pierce v. Society of Sisters, 268 U.S. 510:** Apply the protected parental role without extending it to compulsory school enforcement of the refusal sought here.
- **Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205, and Prince v. Massachusetts, 321 U.S. 158:** Preserve their distinct treatment of family religious direction and public authority concerning children; neither compels the requested veto over this optional resource.
- **Lyng v. Northwest Indian Cemetery Protective Association, 485 U.S. 439, and Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693:** Apply their distinction between conflict with belief and interference with protected exercise, without creating immunity for all school services.
- **Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872:** Does not supply an additional neutral-law holding; pleading combined interests does not itself establish an infringement. Its application to a genuinely burdening program is unnecessary to decide.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992:** Preserves the rule against substantial official participation pressure in the school-directed religious exercise there. The separable optional resource here does not present the same state-created pressure; formal voluntariness is not universally conclusive.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah, June 11, 1993, and Settle v. Dickson County School Board, June 25, 1996:** Preserve protection against religious targeting and supported religious exclusion; no such act is established by this program's stated terms.
- **Planned Parenthood v. Casey, June 29, 1992:** Distinguishes permission for the abortion-consent statute from a constitutional command for parental control over this item. Outside its medical-emergency exception, the sustained statute ordinarily requires the minor's and one parent's informed consent, allows family substitutions, and permits independent court authorization without first approaching a parent, on maturity and actual informed consent or alternatively best interests. Nothing here modifies that statute's safeguards or makes parental involvement an absolute veto.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C., November 20, 1995; Baby Richard v. Kirchner, June 21, 1996; and M.L.B. v. S.L.J., December 16, 1996:** Distinguish their respective complete-contact cutoff, compulsory custody-transition injury, and first-appellate-review termination barriers. Their rules remain unchanged.
- **Church of Scientology v. Clearwater, December 5, 1994; Swanner, March 27, 1995; and American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995:** Preserve RFRA's separate person-and-application burden and justification inquiry. None supplies a constitutional rule that neutral administration can never burden religion; no statutory RFRA claim is resolved here.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 21, 1997, the described optional school program requires neither the requested parental exclusion nor notification mechanism under the presented parental-liberty and free-exercise theories. The decision establishes that confined constitutional boundary; it creates neither general school-health immunity nor a child's constitutional entitlement to the service. Existing family-status protections, actual-pressure and religious-targeting rules, qualified abortion-consent law, and RFRA's separate statutory inquiry retain their prior force.

### 1996-12-09 — In re Askin; Fourth Circuit No. 94-5428, 47 F.3d 100; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; October Term 1996.

### 1997-01-06 — DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association; Fifth Circuit No. 93-8700, 51 F.3d 591; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment; no joins.** He joins the retaliation holding but rejects statutory insufficiency on hostile environment. The repeated sex-derogatory message, distribution to more than seven hundred colleagues, connections to DeAngelis, recurring comments and workplace incidents, humiliation, claimed loss of authority, promotion deterrence and refusal to identify the columnist permit a rational jury's severe-or-pervasive finding under Harris. Physical threats, supervisory power, reduced performance and the written medium are not statutory barriers. He therefore reaches the preserved expressive defense.

In his view, this award punishes protected editorial opinion or satire solely for its sex-derogatory viewpoint. Such civil liability is content based and is not narrowly tailored here to the compelling interest in eradicating sex discrimination. The publication itself is the independently asserted wrong; the record supplies no separate discriminatory employment act, true threat, authority-backed directive or denial of employment or associational rights to which the expression is integral or evidentiary. He would prohibit Title VII damages resting solely on such protected opinion or satire, while preserving liability for discriminatory decisions, quid pro quo demands, true threats, authority-backed directives and expression integral to or used as evidence of independently unlawful employment conduct. His rule is confined to this expressive application and does not declare that speech cannot itself be workplace harassment. On retaliation, he finds no material employment or membership disadvantage caused by the complaint and leaves stronger threats, harassment and public-shaming cases open.

### 1997-01-06 — United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra; No. 95-1906, one Rule 12.4 petition concerning two Ninth Circuit judgments

**Justice Scalia, concurring; no joins.** He joins the bounded Court opinion, which does not say that every punishment below a statutory ceiling is necessarily punishment only for the conviction. His additional disagreement concerns Commission authority. In his view, §994(b)(1)'s requirement of consistency with pertinent provisions of Title 18 and §3661's protection of sentencing-information consideration forbid a Commission or judicial rule excluding otherwise lawful acquitted-conduct consideration. ### 1997-01-07 — Old Chief v. United States; No. 95-6556
