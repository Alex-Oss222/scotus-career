## Event

Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc.; Fourth Circuit No. 95-2476; judgment below reported at 88 F.3d 258. Argued and decided February 26, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). The questions concern responsibility under Title VII for harassment aided by delegated supervisory authority without higher-management notice, the distinction between agency attribution and negligence, and the required connection between workplace authority and misconduct. The jury awarded $25,000; the District Court entered renewed judgment as a matter of law for the employer, and the Court of Appeals affirmed. Constructive discharge is outside the grant.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment on hostile-environment employer attribution and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated in the reviewed part and remanded, 9-0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** none. The employer-attribution issue returns for application of the correct agency rule under Rule 50. Neither reinstatement of the $25,000 verdict nor a new trial is automatic. Constructive discharge remains undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | All holdings and the judgment in full. |

## Holdings

All holdings below are in Justice Breyer's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices. Each is the holding of the Court, not an aggregation of separate reasons.

### Employer responsibility through delegated authority

**Operative rule.** An employer may be responsible under Title VII for a supervisor-created hostile environment when actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the creation or maintenance of that environment, even without actual or constructive notice to higher management. A supervisory title or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; the employee must establish the connection between delegated authority and the misconduct, while negligent failure to correct known or reasonably knowable harassment remains a distinct route to liability.

**Controlling explanation.** Meritor directs courts to agency principles and rejects both automatic imputation of every supervisor's misconduct and a universal defense based on lack of notice. The statutory employer-agent relationship gives legal significance to authority the employer actually entrusted to the wrongdoer. Hiring, firing, discipline and threatened employment action may establish the necessary connection. An express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; daily control of working conditions can also aid unlawful conduct. The employer's argument that personal misconduct necessarily falls outside its responsibility would eliminate the agency inquiry Meritor preserves. Conversely, the employee cannot establish attribution simply by pointing to the administrator's rank. Harris and DeAngelis define an actionable hostile environment without supplying this separate attribution predicate. The administrator's control and hiring and firing authority therefore require examination, even though he reported to a corporate officer. The Court does not adopt a general liability rule for every supervisor's private act or a new affirmative defense.

### Corrected review of the employer's judgment

**Operative rule.** A Rule 50 judgment resting on the legal premise that higher management's lack of notice necessarily defeats supervisor-agency attribution must be reconsidered under the proper agency standard, with the evidence and permissible inferences viewed in the verdict winner's favor. Vacatur does not itself establish attribution, restore the verdict or require a new trial; preserved alternative grounds, evidentiary sufficiency and genuine instruction or preservation questions remain for the lower court.

**Controlling explanation.** The jury's $25,000 award does not answer whether the correct attribution question was submitted or whether the record legally supports the verdict. The lower courts foreclosed the agency route because the administrator was not a proprietor, partner or corporate officer and higher management lacked notice. That categorical barrier cannot stand under Meritor. The proper response is to return the preserved attribution issue for review under the corrected rule, not to treat every allegation as a finding or assume that no other preserved ground can matter. Constructive discharge was separately rejected and lies outside the granted questions; this remand does not reopen it.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied to require an actual agency inquiry; neither supervisor status nor absence of higher-management notice supplies a categorical answer.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Preserved as the objective-and-subjective hostile-environment standard without mandatory psychological injury; it does not establish employer attribution.
- **DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers Association:** Preserved for the severity-or-pervasiveness inquiry and its context; proof of an unlawful environment does not dispense with the separate attribution requirement.

No precedent is overruled. The Court resolves neither constructive discharge nor a general affirmative-defense regime.

## Law After Decision

Title VII retains distinct routes through agency attribution and employer negligence. Courts must examine whether delegated authority actually enabled the supervisor's harassment; higher management's lack of notice alone cannot foreclose that route. The hostile-environment threshold remains separate. The legal rule changes the analysis of the employer's Rule 50 judgment, while leaving factual sufficiency, preserved alternative grounds and the appropriate next proceeding to the lower court.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is published.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit judgment is vacated on the hostile-environment attribution issue and the case is remanded for application of the corrected agency rule in the proper Rule 50 posture. The damages verdict is not reinstated by this mandate. No new trial is automatically ordered, and the constructive-discharge disposition remains undisturbed. The lower court must decide the preserved issue and any properly remaining grounds; no further Supreme Court filing or retained proceeding is ordered.

## Source Notes

The [reported Fourth Circuit opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-4th-circuit/1278633.html) supplies the record, Rule 50 posture and distinct constructive-discharge disposition. The Court's decision is confined to the granted attribution questions; unresolved instruction, preservation and alternative-ground questions remain for the lower court. No finding about an unreproduced trial instruction is necessary to the mandate.

## Event

In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer; Seventh Circuit No. 94-3912; 51 F.3d 1293. Argued and decided February 26, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit. The questions concern nationwide issue certification under differing state laws, predominance and superiority including jury protections and settlement pressure, and the exceptional conditions for mandamus review. Manufacturers challenged a common-negligence issue class for hemophiliacs allegedly infected with HIV through blood-clotting concentrates. The Court of Appeals ordered decertification by mandamus; no common verdict had been returned. Review concerns that extraordinary writ, not an ordinary immediate appeal from certification.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the mandamus judgment and remands with instructions to deny the writ petition.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The appellate decertification command falls; the District Court retains ordinary certification authority subject to the substantive and jury limits stated below.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

All propositions below are adopted in Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight participating Justices. The judgment and each stated controlling proposition are unanimous.

### Extraordinary review of this revisable certification

**Holding and operative rule.** Mandamus requires a clear and indisputable entitlement, no adequate ordinary means of relief and circumstances warranting the extraordinary writ. Serious possible certification error, aggregate exposure and ordinary settlement pressure do not establish those conditions where the trial arrangements remain revisable and eventual named-plaintiff final judgment and appellate review are available, without a demonstrated otherwise-unavoidable loss of jury rights.

**Controlling explanation.** Allied Chemical and Kerr require more than reversible error; Blodgett requires examination of actual ordinary avenues rather than assuming their inadequacy. The appellate court expressly assumed that final judgment for the named plaintiffs would permit review. The certification and trial questions remain open to correction before trial. Digital Equipment and Swint concern ordinary appellate routes, not a categorical bar on mandamus, but reject litigation burdens as a general entitlement to immediate review. Coopers & Lybrand applies that finality discipline to class certification. Estimated exposure and predicted settlement cannot make every consequential class action extraordinary. Twelve earlier defense verdicts neither adjudicate these remaining claims nor establish that the earlier cases had identical law and proof. The manufacturers' strongest objection is the jury right, not expense. Beacon Theatres and Dairy Queen permit protection before a jury entitlement is destroyed. Here, however, no jury has returned a common verdict and no fixed unavoidable sequence establishes the feared deprivation. A definitively ordered inseparable scheme with no adequate ordinary protection could present a different case; parties need not invariably suffer a completed violation before requesting focused relief.

### Certification and jury limits remain binding

**Holding and operative rule.** Reversing this writ does not approve the nationwide negligence plan: any continued issue certification must satisfy the applicable Rule 23 requirements under the actual governing state laws and preserve genuine defenses and jury functions. Material legal equivalence, lawful groupings or genuinely separable issues must be demonstrated; management convenience cannot supply an average national negligence rule or make a later federal jury redecide facts committed to the first.

**Controlling explanation.** Castano requires a concrete account of governing law and anticipated proof, while rejecting tort novelty and ordinary aggregation pressure as independent prohibitions. Rule 23(c)(4)(A) permits appropriate treatment of particular issues, but the Rules Enabling Act does not permit alteration of substantive rights. Erie, Klaxon and O'Melveny require the governing state-law inquiry, and Gasperini preserves substantive state rules together with lawful federal allocation. Foreseeability, professional custom and comparative fault may materially change what a common negligence answer means. Gasoline Products requires distinct issues where separate federal juries are used; a label separating common negligence from individual causation or comparative fault cannot establish that distinction. Genuinely independent historical facts may remain suitable for common treatment. Matsushita and Richards separately preserve adequate representation and a lawful basis for binding absent persons. The Court supplies no replacement trial plan, approves no existing instruction and decides neither the negligence claims nor the full relationship between action-wide predominance and issue certification reserved in Castano. The appellate command is reversed because the extraordinary-remedy showing fails, not because these continuing restrictions disappear.

## Event

Adams v. Robertson, No. 95-1873. Argued January 14, 1997; decided March 3, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Alabama, which approved a mandatory settlement of policyholders' claims against Liberty National Life Insurance Company. The granted question is whether due process required an opportunity for every class member to exclude himself or herself from the class and settlement.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated. No Justice was excluded.

## Public Action

The writ of certiorari is dismissed as improvidently granted. The Court issues a per curiam explanation; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment.

The Court declines to decide the constitutional question in this vehicle. The state appellate presentation leaves substantial doubt whether the broad federal claim now advanced was adequately framed for decision below. The due-process heading, another party's issue statement, and the later rehearing presentation are relevant, but they do not require the Court to resolve every dispute about proper presentation in order to retain this writ. The state opinion did not decide the broad federal question, and petitioners' developed arguments focused on the state jury guarantee and nonresident concerns. Respondents' failure to raise the objection at the certiorari stage does not compel use of this doubtful grant. The Court leaves the constitutional issue for a case that permits its proper decision.

## Judgment & Remedy

Dismissal leaves the Alabama judgment undisturbed. It is not an affirmance of that judgment's constitutional correctness. No remand or affirmative relief is ordered.

## Event

Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona, No. 95-974. Argued December 4, 1996; decided March 3, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit. Its en banc judgment, 69 F.3d 920, sustained a declaration invalidating Arizona Constitution Article XXVIII and inferred nominal damages to preserve the dispute. The questions concern whether Yniguez's individual challenge remained live after leaving state employment, whether the initiative sponsors could pursue review, and whether federal constitutional adjudication was permissible without a live controversy and authoritative state construction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the appellate judgment and directs vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal as moot.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated and remanded with directions to vacate the District Court judgment and dismiss the action, 9-0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Both constitutional merits judgments are cleared; no First Amendment question is decided.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Entire opinion and judgment. |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice joins | Joins the Court in full; adds the distinct standard governing discretionary certification in a future live case. |

## Holdings

Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined throughout by the other eight participating Justices. Each proposition below commands all nine votes; no alternative rationale or combination of separate writings is needed.

### A continuing personal controversy is absent

**Holding and operative rule.** An individual employee's prospective challenge to an employment rule becomes moot when she voluntarily leaves the covered employment, alleges no intention to return and establishes no realistic recurrence or surviving claim. A general prayer for relief cannot preserve that controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person.

**Controlling explanation.** Preiser requires a live controversy throughout review. Yniguez brought an individual action and left state employment for another job; no class, replacement claimant or supported repetition exception remains. Will's statutory limit and Hafer's distinction between official and individual capacities defeat the inferred nominal-damages route. The initially named officers' personal-capacity claims had been dismissed, and the appeal did not keep those claims or a former co-plaintiff alive. This is not a general requirement that every complaint expressly say nominal damages: the obstacle here is the absence of a legally available damages claim against a remaining defendant. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity. A proposed consensual payment cannot create the cause of action, and the Attorney General's permitted constitutional argument under §2403(b) did not reverse the appellate court's unrevised denial of State party status. Agreement among litigants cannot supply Article III power. A pleaded claim against an amenable individual defendant would present a different question, which the Court does not decide.

### Corrective authority and equitable judgment clearing

**Holding and operative rule.** The Court may correct a lower federal court's jurisdictional error without exercising authority over the underlying constitutional merits. Exercising that limited authority here, it vacates the appellate judgment and directs vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal as moot, leaving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing unresolved.

**Controlling explanation.** Bender, applying Corrick, supplies the limited power to correct the lower court's exercise of jurisdiction; §2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Neither intervention, a notice of appeal nor political commitment establishes a sponsor's automatic standing. Lujan and Brown Group preserve the need for constitutional injury or legally sufficient representation. Once the corrective basis is established, Bancorp requires an equitable judgment-disposition inquiry, not automatic vacatur. The prevailing plaintiff left the covered employment during postjudgment review efforts; this was not a reviewing loser's settlement or abandonment. The notices and attempted State intervention preceded her departure, and the appellate court later adjudicated constitutionality after her individual controversy had ended. With no lawful damages residue, leaving either constitutional judgment operative would preserve unreviewable merits adjudication. Munsingwear's judgment-clearing practice and Anderson support that remedy at Bancorp's equitable scope. The Court does not declare the original declaration void when entered, resolve every intervention ruling or the complete Attorney General notice, fix the precise day within April 1990 when mootness attached, or infer improper conduct from the resignation or its reporting.

### Certification cannot restore the controversy

**Holding and operative rule.** Certification of an unsettled state-law question may assist a live federal controversy, but it cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction. No certification order issues in this moot action.

**Controlling explanation.** Arizona's highest court is the authoritative expositor of Article XXVIII. Authoritative construction of an unsettled provision may clarify a constitutional dispute in later live litigation. That discretionary means of clarification supplies neither a new plaintiff nor a surviving injury here. The Court neither construes Article XXVIII nor decides its First Amendment validity, and it imposes no mandatory certification rule for every future case.

- **Hafer v. Melo**: Applied to preserve State and official-capacity nonpersonhood for §1983 damages while distinguishing genuine individual-capacity liability; immunity waiver cannot erase the statutory limit.
- **Bender v. Williamsport Area School District**: Applied for limited correction of lower-court jurisdictional error without reaching the underlying merits.
- **United States v. Corrick**: Applied for limited correction of lower-court jurisdictional error without reaching the underlying merits.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife**: Their constitutional injury and representation limits remain intact; Brown Group's particular statutory relaxation of a prudential condition creates no initiative-sponsor exception to Article III.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group**: Their constitutional injury and representation limits remain intact; Brown Group's particular statutory relaxation of a prudential condition creates no initiative-sponsor exception to Article III.
- **U.S. Bancorp Mortgage v. Bonner Mall Partnership**: Applied as an equitable vacatur rule, distinguishing unilateral action by the prevailing party from a reviewing loser's voluntary settlement.
- **United States v. Munsingwear**: Applied to judgment clearing at that equitable scope, without an automatic-vacatur command.
- **Anderson v. Green**: Applied to judgment clearing at that equitable scope, without an automatic-vacatur command.

## Law After Decision

A past individual employment dispute does not authorize continued constitutional review after its prospective injury ends, and a legally unavailable damages theory cannot preserve it. Limited corrective review and equitable vacatur remain distinct from merits jurisdiction. The law creates no automatic federal standing for initiative sponsors and leaves Article XXVIII for authoritative construction and any proper constitutional challenge in live litigation.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs, joining the Court's opinion in full. He adds that a novel, potentially narrowing question of state constitutional law may be certified in later live litigation without satisfying abstention's separate exceptional-circumstances standard. Arizona's courts should have the opportunity to state the authoritative meaning of Article XXVIII before an unnecessary federal constitutional ruling. This is a discretionary means of clarification, not a mandatory certification rule, and it cannot revive the present controversy. He expresses no view on the provision's correct construction, its First Amendment validity or the sponsors' ultimate standing.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit's judgment is vacated. The case is remanded with directions to vacate the District Court's declaratory judgment and dismiss the action as moot. No injunction, damages award, certification order or constitutional ruling survives or issues through this mandate. A later claimant with a live controversy may seek an authoritative state construction and litigate any remaining federal question; this action supplies no retained Supreme Court proceeding.

## Event

United States v. Gonzales, No. 95-1605. Argued December 11, 1996; decided March 3, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 65 F.3d 814. The question is whether a federal court may make a §924(c) firearm term concurrent with an already imposed state term. Respondents received state terms arising from a drug sting and later federal drug and firearm convictions. The District Court made the drug terms concurrent with state imprisonment and the five-year firearm portions consecutive. The Court of Appeals accepted concurrency with state terms.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Tenth Circuit judgment and remands to correct its state-term concurrency interpretation.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated and remanded, 7-2. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. **Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer. The latter would affirm the permissive concurrency ruling; they would not require concurrent service. Unreviewed convictions and sentencing components remain outside the disposition.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | All holdings and the vacatur/remand judgment. |
| Dissent | Stevens | Breyer | Federal statutory context and prosecution-order objection; would affirm permissive concurrency. |
| Dissent | Breyer | Stevens | Practical sentence-coordination ground; would affirm permissive concurrency. |

## Holdings

Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those seven Justices adopt both holdings and their explanations. Justices Stevens and Breyer dissent; no proposition depends on combining separate writings.

### Existing state imprisonment is within the concurrency prohibition

**Holding and operative rule.** A federal district court imposing a term under 18 U.S.C. §924(c) may not direct it to run concurrently with an already imposed state or federal term of imprisonment. The statute's unqualified reference to any other term includes state imprisonment; the general sentencing discretion in §3584 supplies no exception to that specific command.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress expressly confined the predicate crime to one prosecutable in federal court but repeated no federal limitation in the distinct concurrency prohibition. The reference that includes the predicate sentence gives an example within the comprehensive class rather than limiting that class. The specific notwithstanding command therefore controls over general discretion. Respondents' strongest argument is that mandatory addition can depend on whether state or federal proceedings occurred first. That concern does not insert a federal-only qualification Congress omitted, and reserving the later-state question preserves the limits of this case rather than answering that different question. Witte's sentence coordination remains conditional on governing statutes and Guidelines; it does not authorize overriding this statutory restriction. Deal construed the separate second-or-subsequent-conviction language and does not independently decide this phrase. Text and context leave no residual ambiguity requiring lenity. Aggregate punishment concerns cannot create an unprovided exception, though lawful choices concerning other terms remain available.

### Other sentencing choices and service sequence remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 924(c) does not otherwise withdraw the federal court's lawful authority to arrange non-§924(c) sentences under §3584 and applicable law, and it does not require the firearm term to be served first. The erroneous federal-only concurrency premise must be removed on remand without indiscriminately reinstating every original sentence or disturbing unrelated appellate rulings.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 3584 generally makes simultaneously imposed terms concurrent unless the court orders otherwise or a statute requires consecutiveness, and makes terms imposed at different times consecutive unless the court orders concurrency. Its separate prohibition of consecutive terms for an attempt and the offense that was the attempt's sole objective remains intact; no such pair is found here, and that provision supplies no firearm exception. Section 3584(b) requires attention to §3553(a)'s factors as to each offense when exercising the discretion the law permits. Administrative aggregation under §3584(c) does not establish which term must be served first. The District Court's concurrent treatment of the drug terms therefore presents a separate inquiry from the mandatory firearm addition. The Court does not decide a later state sentencing court's authority, credit calculations, the administrative order of service, the validity of underlying convictions or any independent constitutional or duplicative-enhancement challenge.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Witte v. United States**: Its conditional coordination rule is preserved; applicable statutes and Guidelines govern the available adjustment, and coordination cannot override §924(c)'s particular command or cure an independent successive-prosecution bar.
- **Deal v. United States**: Its construction of second-or-subsequent firearm convictions remains controlling at its own scope; the different phrase does not independently resolve state-term concurrency.

No earlier decision is overruled. The Court supplies no general sentencing-severity cap or new constitutional rule.

## Law After Decision

For the later-sentencing federal court, the firearm term must be consecutive to existing state and federal imprisonment. Lawful arrangement of other terms remains distinct. The holding creates no rule commanding a later state court, no administrative sequence and no determination of an independent constitutional challenge.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Breyer, dissents. He reads the concurrency language within a statute defining federal offenses and a federal sentencing scheme. The express federal limitation earlier in the provision, its operational setting and the consequence of prosecution order make a federal-only reading plausible despite the breadth of any. On that reading, the later federal court retains discretion over concurrency with an existing state term. Reserving what a later state court might do does not answer the anomaly imposed on defendants sentenced by the State first. Witte's attention to coordinated punishment supports preserving that choice. He would affirm the Tenth Circuit's permissive concurrency interpretation, without requiring concurrent sentences or disturbing Deal's separate rule.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Stevens, also dissents. His distinct emphasis is the practical coordination of punishment for related state and federal conduct. Leaving discretion only over nonfirearm terms does not restore the lost ability to coordinate the firearm term itself, and mandatory accumulation depending on prosecution order supports reading this federal scheme to preserve sentencing choice. Witte makes coordination conditional; that condition requires deciding the disputed statute in context, not assuming the majority's answer. He would allow the later federal judge to choose concurrency or consecutiveness as lawfully appropriate. He proposes neither obligatory concurrency, a universal lenity rule, a total-sentence cap nor a constitutional bar to successive prosecutions. His position rests on the general potential accumulation of related punishment, without a finding that these state sentences included a particular firearm enhancement.

## Procedure After Action

The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with the prohibition on making these §924(c) terms concurrent with respondents' existing state imprisonment. The lower courts must implement the corrected statutory rule while preserving lawful discretion concerning other federal terms and every unreviewed conviction or sentencing component. The mandate does not indiscriminately reinstate the entire original sentences, calculate credit or direct an administrative order of service.

## Event

Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co., No. 95-728. Argued October 15, 1996; decided March 3, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit. The en banc court affirmed a jury verdict of nonliteral patent infringement and a permanent injunction. The questions concern survival of the doctrine of equivalents after the 1952 Act, claim-element limits and prosecution-history estoppel. Hilton Davis's amended claim covers approximately pH 6.0 to 9.0; Warner-Jenkinson uses pH 5.0, and Hilton Davis concedes no literal infringement. The reason for the lower pH boundary was not established.

## Participation

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Federal Circuit judgment and remands for the required claim-element and prosecution-history analysis.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The pH amendment's legal effect must be determined before any still-available factual equivalence claim proceeds. No final infringement or invalidity ruling is made.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |

| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer in full; Stone-Zsela except the objective-intent proposition | Eight direct joins on objective intent; nine on all other propositions, including the old-prosecution qualification, and the judgment. |

## Holdings

Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins all propositions except the statement that bad intent is not an element, which therefore has eight direct joins; he reserves that issue. Every other proposition below, including the contextual treatment of older prosecution records, has nine direct joins.

### Equivalence survives but cannot erase a claim element

**Holding and operative rule.** The doctrine of equivalents survives the 1952 Patent Act, but nonliteral infringement must be assessed against every material claim element in its patent and technical context; overall similarity cannot eliminate a limitation. Function, way and result may assist the substantial-difference inquiry without serving as an exclusive mechanical formula, and bad intent is not an element of infringement under this doctrine.

**Controlling explanation.** Graver Tank preserves protection against insubstantial substitutions that would evade a lawful claim while requiring attention to the actual invention and technical evidence. Nothing in the 1952 Act displaces that doctrine. The claim nevertheless defines the public grant: similarity in a process's ultimate result cannot replace proof concerning an omitted element. Known interchangeability and relevant technical evidence can inform whether the difference is substantial. Here the patent, as amended, claims dye ultrafiltration at approximately pH 6.0 to 9.0, while Warner-Jenkinson uses pH 5.0; Hilton Davis concedes the absence of literal infringement. The lower pH boundary therefore cannot be ignored under an overall-equivalence assessment. Deuel likewise requires attention to actual claims and supported technical evidence, though its obviousness burden is a different inquiry. The Court neither abolishes equivalents nor decides whether this particular process infringes. It adopts no categorical rule barring every foreseeable but unclaimed substitute absent a narrowing amendment.

### The patentee must explain an otherwise unexplained narrowing amendment

**Holding and operative rule.** Prosecution-history estoppel prevents recapture of subject matter surrendered through narrowing to meet a patentability objection. When the record does not disclose a legally sufficient reason for a required narrowing amendment, the patentee seeking the disputed equivalent bears the burden of supplying one; after fair consideration, an inadequate explanation supports a presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the affected limitation.

**Controlling explanation.** Exhibit Supply preserves the actual subject matter surrendered to obtain a patent; it does not make every amendment a universal bar to equivalents. The claim and prosecution record protect public notice as well as the patentee's legitimate coverage. An unexplained narrowing cannot simply disappear whenever infringement by an equivalent is alleged, and the patentee bears the explanatory burden rather than forcing the accused infringer to reconstruct the omission. But the presumption is rebuttable. The court must evaluate a supported nonpatentability explanation and its legal effect rather than treat every amendment as conclusive or extinguish equivalents unrelated to the surrender. The reason for this lower pH limit remains unestablished; no finding supplies a particular examiner demand for pH 6.0. The lower court must consider whether a reason was previously offered and whether a further opportunity is proper, recognizing that old prosecution files were completed without the newly express burden. It must not demand nonexistent contemporaneous magic words or treat historical silence as misconduct. This contextual consideration neither transfers the burden nor creates an automatic right to reopen evidence, a mandatory evidentiary hearing or endless opportunities to repair failed proof. The remand requires decision of the unresolved amendment question without predetermining the adequacy of any explanation.

### Legal claim boundaries precede unresolved infringement facts

**Holding and operative rule.** The court must determine claim meaning and enforce the legal effect of prosecution surrender before an asserted equivalent may be used to establish infringement. The judgment must be reversed and remanded for that determination; if the equivalent remains legally available, genuine disputed factual equivalence remains for the jury under ordinary procedures, without a final infringement decision or automatic new trial here.

**Controlling explanation.** Markman assigns legally operative claim meaning to the judge, including genuine technical-usage and credibility disputes embedded in construction under its controlling eight-Justice holding. It also preserves external infringement facts and does not permit a jury to erase an element of the construed claim. The unexplained amendment presents a legal gate to the equivalence claim that the Federal Circuit did not fully resolve. Once the court determines the permitted boundary, any remaining properly factual infringement work retains its ordinary place. Neither this remand nor Markman answers the complete judge-jury allocation for every equivalents issue or every appellate review standard. The Court orders neither patent invalidation nor an automatic new trial and makes no final comparison of the accused process with a legally available equivalent.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Graver Tank & Manufacturing v. Linde Air Products**: Retained as authority for equivalents after the 1952 Act, with technical context and public claim boundaries preserved; the doctrine does not authorize deleting a material element.
- **Exhibit Supply Co. v. Ace Patents Corp.**: Applied to prohibit recapture of subject matter actually surrendered to obtain the patent; it supplies no universal bar from every amendment.
- **Markman v. Westview Instruments**: Applied for judicial construction, including its controlling embedded-technical-dispute allocation, while preserving external infringement facts; no universal appellate standard or complete equivalents allocation is inferred.
- **In re Deuel**: Its claim-specific and evidentiary discipline remains intact. The Office's initial obviousness burden is not transferred into this different patentee explanation burden.

No earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Equivalents remains available within each material claim element and the legally permitted prosecution boundary. The patentee must explain an otherwise unexplained narrowing amendment; the court applies the rebuttable consequence in context rather than treating old silence as either irrelevant or irrebuttable surrender. Construction and surrender limits do not eliminate ordinary factual infringement work.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins the Court's opinion except its statement that bad intent is not an element of infringement under the doctrine of equivalents. He reserves intent-based infringement and supports the element-specific remand and the patentee's explanatory burden without deciding that issue. No other Justice joins this reservation.

## Procedure After Action

The court must consider the adequacy of any supported reason and whether further opportunity is proper in the circumstances. If an asserted equivalent remains legally available, remaining factual infringement issues proceed under ordinary procedures. The mandate neither finally finds infringement or noninfringement, invalidates the patent, presumes an examiner's actual reason nor automatically orders a hearing or new trial.

## Event

Wright v. MetroHealth Medical Center; Sixth Circuit No. 94-3548; 58 F.3d 1130. Argued November 12, 1996; decided March 3, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit. The questions concern whether an anti-nepotism assignment policy directly and substantially burdens marriage, the resulting level of scrutiny and the justification supplied by emergency-flight operations. Martha Wright worked as a MetroHealth nurse and John Wright as a PHI helicopter pilot in the same LifeFlight unit. The Sixth Circuit affirmed federal summary judgment and Ohio-law dismissals under Rule 12(b)(6); the grant reaches the federal marriage and equal-protection questions. PHI settled during that appeal and ceased to be a party to it.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment on the granted federal questions and leaves the ungranted state-law dispositions undisturbed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed on the granted federal marriage and equal-protection questions, 9-0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Ohio-law dismissals remain outside review, and no new judgment is entered against settled party PHI. No further federal relief is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Both federal holdings, corrected record application and limited affirmance. |

## Holdings

Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other eight participating Justices. Both federal holdings below command nine votes. The Ohio-law dispositions are outside the granted review and are not independently adopted as holdings of this Court.

### The actual assignment burden does not require heightened scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule.** An employment rule does not require heightened constitutional scrutiny merely because marriage triggers an assignment consequence; its actual operation must directly and substantially interfere with the decision to marry. On this record, the close-interaction assignment restriction, considered with the actual same-grade-and-pay hospital-transfer alternative and the entire threatened-transfer sequence, imposes an incidental employment consequence rather than that substantial marriage barrier.

**Controlling explanation.** Loving and Zablocki protect marriage as a fundamental liberty; Jobst distinguishes substantial interference with marrying from incidental marital-status consequences. The fact that the Wrights married does not alone defeat their challenge, and the absence of a completed discharge is not a universal safe harbor. Paragraph C allows affected employees to choose who moves, gives thirty calendar days after notice before the less senior hospital employee moves by default, and requires all reasonable attempts to provide comparable responsibility and pay. That effort obligation is not a guarantee. Separately, MetroHealth actually offered Martha a transfer to any available same-grade-and-pay position in another unit; she declined. John worked for contractor PHI, so the general policy does not establish that both spouses were hospital employees. After their marriage, MetroHealth sought John's transfer and PHI announced relocation to Louisiana effective January 19, 1993. That threatened geographic disruption was substantial enough to require consideration, but no completed move, discharge or demonstrated pay reduction occurred, and the reported hospital-transfer alternative distinguishes an unavoidable marriage barrier. After suit, the defendants agreed that John would remain and Martha would move; during trial the spouses stayed in LifeFlight on separate flight schedules. Those accommodations neither erase the original threat nor promise permanent protection. The Court does not find an unreported vacancy or approve future loss of livelihood, unavoidable separation or materially inferior work.

### The restriction bears a rational operational relationship

**Holding and operative rule.** Under ordinary rational-basis review, restricting spouses' simultaneous assignments in a close-interaction emergency-flight unit is constitutionally permissible when the restriction rationally relates to conflicts, supervision, safety and effective teamwork. The rule need not be the least restrictive arrangement or rest on proof that every married pair will impair operations, but it may not rest on bare harm or irrational prejudice unrelated to the disadvantage imposed.

**Controlling explanation.** Heller and Romer preserve the relationship between the actual classification and a legitimate objective while permitting reasonable predictions and imperfect fit. Tracy illustrates an operational distinction at its own scope; it supplies no special rule about marriage. The hospital may reasonably address perceived favoritism, divided loyalty and coordination in a small, high-pressure medical-flight team. The spouses' nominally different employers do not eliminate their recurring interaction in that unit. Their workable separate schedules during litigation provide a less burdensome alternative, but ordinary review does not compel the hospital to adopt it permanently or prove this couple unsafe before acting. The actual comparable hospital-transfer offer supports the limited application before the Court; a policy adopted to deter marriage, automatic discharge, sex-based administration, animus or materially inferior pay or responsibility is not approved. No general immunity for anti-nepotism policies, suspect marital-status classification, mandatory individualized exemption or compulsory safety study is created.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Loving v. Virginia**: Preserved as protection of the fundamental decision to marry; the holding does not reduce protection against an actual marriage barrier.
- **Zablocki v. Redhail**: Applied to distinguish direct and substantial interference requiring heightened scrutiny from incidental employment consequences; completing a marriage is not itself dispositive.
- **Califano v. Jobst**: Applied for the distinction between a substantial marriage obstacle and a rational incidental marital-status consequence, at the scope of the actual burden shown.
- **Heller v. Doe**: Its rational-basis principles permit reasonable predictions and imperfect fit while requiring a pertinent relation to the challenged disadvantage.
- **Romer v. Evans**: Its prohibition of bare harm and irrational prejudice remains a limit on ordinary review; the supported emergency-team objectives satisfy that review here.
- **General Motors v. Tracy**: Its recognition of relevant operational distinctions remains confined to its setting and supplies no automatic rule validating marriage-related employment restrictions.

No precedent is overruled. The Court reaches no additional First Amendment association, career-liberty, sex-discrimination or Ohio-law question.

## Law After Decision

The constitutional distinction remains between direct, substantial interference with marrying and a rational incidental assignment consequence. This application survives because the actual burden and available hospital-transfer alternative do not establish the former, and the shared emergency-unit work supplies a legitimate operational relationship. Different coercive, discriminatory or economically destructive applications require their own assessment.

No separate writing is published.

## Procedure After Action

The existing Ohio-law Rule 12(b)(6) dispositions remain undisturbed because they are outside the grant; they are neither newly approved on their merits nor relabeled as dismissals for lack of a federal claim or as dismissals without prejudice. PHI's settlement remains outside review, and no new judgment runs against it. No injunction, damages, reinstatement, employment transfer, permanent scheduling order, hearing or remand is directed. MetroHealth's further employment choices remain subject to the policy's actual terms and ordinary employment law; this judgment commands none.

## Event

Nabozny v. Podlesny; Seventh Circuit No. 95-3634, 92 F.3d 446. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit. Decided March 10, 1997, following plenary merits review.

The Seventh Circuit’s July 31, 1996 judgment reinstated sex and sexual-orientation equal-protection claims, rejected the preserved due-process theories and denied individual qualified immunity on the supported equality account. The questions concern intentional discriminatory denial of school protection, the asserted due-process duty arising from attendance and known abuse, and individual immunity. The preserved due-process theories allege increased risk through failure to punish and a policy encouraging a harmful environment; the generalized special-relationship argument was expressly disavowed below and is not revived.

Nabozny’s favorable account describes repeated anti-gay abuse, assaults, a classroom mock assault and a beating causing internal bleeding, together with complaints, promises, allegedly discriminatory responses and departures from ordinary enforcement. Defendants dispute that account. The whole record is available on equality because the District Court ventured beyond the moving grounds; the Court assumes its availability for due process without resolving that separate local-rule issue.

## Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. That direct common coalition controls each holding below.

#### Intentionally unequal school protection because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials who intentionally withhold ordinarily available protection because a student is male must establish that the sex distinction substantially serves an important governmental objective. The supported evidence permits a finding of purposeful unequal administration without a sufficient objective, so the sex claim independently survives summary judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Yick Wo forbids deliberately unequal administration of an existing protection. Feeney requires purposeful selection because of the adverse effect, and Arlington Heights permits that purpose to be shown through statements, departures and context. Hogan and Virginia supply the demanding important-objective and substantial-relationship inquiry for sex discrimination. Officials need not give identical discipline in every case, and negligence, unequal outcomes or violation of a state policy alone does not establish this claim. Here the alleged responses to male-on-male abuse, admitted ordinary enforcement and differing treatment of male-on-female abuse permit the requisite finding. Missing names and dates in some comparator examples affect proof but do not erase the direct evidence. Defendants may establish their contrary account at trial; credibility cannot be resolved against Nabozny now.

#### Intentionally unequal protection because of sexual orientation

**Holding and operative rule:** Intentional denial of ordinarily available school protection because of sexual orientation violates equal protection when the distinction has no rational relation to a legitimate objective. The orientation claim separately survives on the supported account without assignment of a heightened scrutiny tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer preserves ordinary rational-basis review, including conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit, while enforcing Moreno’s and Cleburne’s prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice. Walmer applies that inquiry in its particular military setting; its service-related rationale supplies no reason to deny this student ordinary protection against battery. Wright likewise permits reasonable operational predictions but supplies no school justification and expressly reserves discriminatory administration; its marriage threshold does not govern this equality claim. Bowers’s specified-conduct holding does not authorize private violence or discriminatory nonprotection. The alleged statements and enforcement departures support purposeful selection for disadvantage because Nabozny was openly gay, and no legitimate disciplinary objective explains that treatment on the favorable account. Knowledge, deliberate indifference without discriminatory purpose and inconsistent enforcement alone do not suffice. Virginia establishes no sexual-orientation tier. Neither Curtis nor Brown school excuses intentional discrimination merely because it occurs in an educational setting.

#### Conduct-time equality law defeats individual immunity on these facts

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual officials receive no qualified immunity when the supported facts show purposeful, irrational withholding of an ordinary protective policy whose unlawfulness was apparent from the relevant law when they acted; a factually identical harassment case is unnecessary. The clearly established sex-equality ground independently defeats immunity, and the narrow irrational-discrimination ground also supports denial on the orientation claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson require objectively apparent unlawfulness at the particularized level of the conduct, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant conduct-time authority. Before the conduct began in 1988, Reed, Weinberger and Hogan prohibited arbitrary sex distinctions; Yick Wo, Feeney and Arlington Heights supplied the purposeful-administration rule; Moreno and Cleburne rejected bare harm and irrational prejudice. Bohen provided relevant circuit notice concerning intentional sex discrimination, although its setting was employment. This is alleged deliberate discrimination in an existing protection, not notice of a novel affirmative-danger duty. Pinder’s contrary immunity result concerned that distinct due-process uncertainty as of its conduct date. DeShaney’s later equality reservation can confirm the distinction only for conduct after its date and is unnecessary to pre-1989 notice. Romer, Virginia and later decisions furnish no retroactive notice. The school’s policy is evidence of practice and knowledge, not an independent federal cause or automatic immunity defeat. Each individual’s actual participation, purpose and causation remain disputed; denial of legal immunity on assumed facts does not decide liability.

#### The preserved due-process theories fail on their own actor-specific record

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that sufficiently culpable official creation or increase of danger, or an attributable policy affirmatively encouraging private injury, can establish a constitutional deprivation, the preserved failure-to-punish and harmful-policy theories do not establish that contribution here. Their dismissal therefore stands without a categorical rule against all affirmative-danger claims or a due-process immunity holding.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a general governmental duty to protect from private violence. Pinder separates omission of public protection from affirmative deprivation of available protection; Uhlrig leaves the merits of the latter theory open. Lack of custody alone cannot defeat that distinct claim, and no assurance/reliance chain is made indispensable.

The Court examines the favorable account beyond mere silence. Promises, stereotyped responses, counseling for leaving after the mock assault and return to the ordinary schedule support the equality claim but do not establish a separate act directing a new assault or depriving available protection. Davis and Blauert referred Nabozny for a protective schedule change; the later special-education placement alongside assailants does not identify a remaining defendant’s deliberate decision or establish its increased-danger contribution to a particular later injury. Social Services’ return order is not attributed to these defendants or a District policy. Crawford’s discouragement of charges followed the completed beating, cannot have caused it, and establishes no later injury or attributable District policy. He is not a remaining defendant. Repeated nonenforcement does not become affirmative injury merely by being called a policy. The Court neither adjudicates a new Crawford claim nor rules out liability for proved obstruction, culpable placement or other materially different affirmative conduct.

The Court decides no sexual-orientation scrutiny tier, general school-safety entitlement, complete affirmative-danger test, municipal policy, actual liability or measure of damages.

## Holdings

All eight Associate Justices directly join Parts I–IV of the Court’s opinion. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also joins Parts I, II and IV. Part III’s same-decision and closed-liability reasons therefore command eight votes; the other holdings command nine. No proposition rests on aggregation of separate writings.

#### Part I — Religion-specific commands exceed the demonstrated justification

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer may enforce lawful controls on work, resources, coercion and official religious activity, but a religion-specific prohibition extending beyond those concerns into private religious expression and personal religious possession must satisfy the constitutional justification its discrimination demands. The undifferentiated witnessing, proselytizing and counseling command and removal of all religious items, including a private desk Bible, fail that inquiry at their demonstrated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith preserves neutral rules, while Lukumi requires justification and tailoring for religious targeting. Preventing official worship, supervisory pressure, favoritism and diversion of paid resources supplies legitimate and potentially compelling concerns. Those concerns do not justify suppressing religious possession without an established operational consequence or every act described in religious terms. Narrower lawful controls cannot save the overreaching directives actually issued.

The supervisor setting matters. Voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayers are not automatically private, and nominal voluntariness cannot erase coercive pressure or government adoption. The Court need not recognize a general constitutional right to organize worship during department meetings to decide the orders’ excess. Pickering, Connick and Givhan retain the actual employment-speech inquiry. NTEU addresses broad burdens on protected citizen expression without converting supervisory instructions into citizen speech; Waters concerns genuine reliance on a disciplinable account, not blanket religious suppression. Pinette establishes no comprehensive majority test, and Chabad and Good News do not make this office a forum. The free-exercise ground suffices; no new equal-protection tier or constitutional discharge rule is adopted. A Title VII violation does not automatically establish a First Amendment violation.

#### Part II — Accommodation turns on the actual practice and supported burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §2000e(j), the employee must establish the sincere practice, employment conflict, employer knowledge and adverse treatment implicated by the claim; the employer must demonstrate effective reasonable accommodation or inability to accommodate without more than a de minimis hardship under the governing law. Concrete noncash burdens and supported professional predictions count without realized injury, an experiment or an actual complaint; an effective accommodation need not be preferred, and no independent duty to negotiate or try every alternative remains after effective accommodation or sufficient hardship is shown.

**Controlling explanation:** Hardison, Ansonia and Beadle require attention to the practice and actual operational justification. Diverting a paid secretary to Bible-study work supplies a sufficient resource burden. The particular prework office use likewise establishes no protected access: the County treats arrival as starting work and prohibits personal resource use, and no public or limited forum or comparable allowed personal use was shown. That conclusion is not a categorical prohibition of off-clock prayer.

The department-meeting prayers and one Christian work-ethic reference require separate assessment. The testimony described voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayer, no compelled participation, and a single reference. Indirect reports, absence of direct complaints or observed discomfort, testimony denying favoritism and accounts attributing disputes to organization did not connect those practices to the asserted polarization or favoritism burden. Supervisor status remains relevant but is not conclusive proof of coercion. Infrequency is no immunity, and realized disruption is not indispensable; the Court rejects those broader suggestions below and sustains the result because the actual proof fails under the correct predictive standard. The proved cost of secretarial work cannot be exported to every religious act. Knowledge and the absence of attempted accommodation alone do not decide liability, and no new opportunity to invent coercion or retry the completed hardship question is ordered.

#### Part III — The original mixed-motive defense fails on this completed record

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the original Price Waterhouse rule applicable to this pre-1991 conduct, direct decisional evidence that protected religion was a substantial factor actually relied on, proved by a preponderance, shifts to the employer the burden to prove by a preponderance that it would have made the same decision without that consideration; success defeats liability, not merely relief. That narrow trigger is met here, the County did not carry its defense on the completed trial record, and statutory religious-discharge liability remains resolved without another same-decision trial or evidentiary opportunity.

**Controlling explanation:** The decisionmaker’s acknowledgment that the religious reprimand played a role, together with the connected testimony and protected activities encompassed by the reprimand, meets O’Connor’s narrower Price Waterhouse concurrence as well as the plurality. Mere religious awareness, stray remarks or unprotected resource use would not suffice; the Court settles no other disagreement among those writings.

The District Court treated inadequate performance and religious causation as mutually exclusive. Performance evaluations, delays, restructuring, budget concerns and the computer investigation nevertheless remain genuine evidence of legitimate reasons. Such reasons can justify discharge without proving that this discharge would have occurred on those reasons alone. On the whole authenticated trial account, including the administrator’s inability to say whether discharge would have followed without the religious reprimand and the insufficient contrary proof, the County failed its counterfactual burden. Uncertainty is not a universal conclusive admission; neither a sole legitimate cause, special objective-evidence category nor formulaic answer is required.

The alternative of returning the existing record for a factual finding respects Rule 52’s ordinary protection of permissible choices. Here the Court sustains the appellate determination that the completed record cannot carry the defense, rather than announcing power to replace any disputed trial finding. Reopening evidence is different again: the five-day trial afforded the opportunity, no relevant exclusion or newly announced burden is shown, and the August rehearing refusal preserves closure. Warner-Jenkinson’s contextual treatment of a newly express patent burden supplies no reset under this preexisting rule. Landgraf’s temporal principle preserves the original defense; the 1991 Act’s altered consequences would change the legal effect of completed conduct and no express retrospective command is supplied.

#### Part IV — The remedial tracks remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Remand addresses appropriate relief on the two constitutional restriction claims and, separately, backpay and reinstatement on the established statutory religious-discharge claims. The constitutional claims require their own lawful compensatory or declaratory predicates, while the statutory remand concerns amount, mitigation and equitable conditions and does not reopen liability or guarantee reinstatement.

**Controlling explanation:** Brown actually requested a declaration and compensatory damages for the constitutional restrictions. Complaint language suggested constitutional discharge, but no argument supported it at trial closing or in appellate argument or briefs; the Court adds neither that theory nor constitutional backpay or reinstatement. No independent constitutional injunction request is established. The lower court’s coordinated Iowa disposition is preserved without an independent construction of Iowa law; the affirmed statutory race judgment is outside the religious grant.

Landgraf and Rivers preclude retrospective use of §102’s new Title VII compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury election. They do not eliminate preexisting statutory employment relief or independently available §1983 compensation. Constitutional injury, causation, personal involvement and capacity, municipal responsibility and any properly preserved individual immunity remain distinct; no new defense or chance to undo settled invalidity is conferred. Later merits law does not furnish earlier conduct-time notice. Arizonans creates no automatic mootness where actual compensation claims against the County and identified officials remain; prospective declaratory relief still requires its own continuing controversy. Nabozny preserves those defendant-specific and remedial distinctions without reopening this completed trial.

## Event

Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert, No. 95-1402. Argued November 12, 1996; decided March 18, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, which affirmed the Tax Court in Estate of Hubert v. Commissioner, 63 F.3d 1083, affirming 101 T.C. 314.

The question is whether administration expenses paid from postmortem income attributable to marital and charitable residuary bequests require reduction of their estate-tax deductions. Nearly $2 million in expenses was paid from an estate exceeding $30 million: approximately $500,000 from principal and the remainder from income. The estate reduced deductions for principal-funded expenses. The Commissioner challenges the treatment of the income-funded amount. Qualification of the trusts was stipulated; the dispute is valuation.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Eleventh Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Breyer.

The reviewed result stands for both deductions. The Court directs no new calculation or factual hearing. The dissenters would reverse the no-reduction judgment and remand for a lawful calculation, without entering a dollar amount.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Affirms both deductions | Whole confined opinion; material limitation and case-specific burden, without a valuation-date-only rule |
| Dissent | Scalia | Breyer | Would reverse and remand for calculation | Reduction principle; method and amount reserved, with Breyer’s additional economic qualification stated below |

## Holdings

The Court’s opinion is directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. That seven-Justice coalition controls both holdings; no fractured-opinion synthesis is necessary.

#### Income-funded expenses do not invariably require reduction

**Holding and operative rule:** Payment of administration expenses from postmortem income attributable to a marital bequest does not alone require a dollar-for-dollar reduction of the estate-tax deduction. Under then-effective Treasury Regulation §20.2056(b)-4(a), valuation must account for a material limitation on the spouse’s income rights; the actual effect on the qualifying beneficial interest, rather than the allocation label alone, governs.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2056 concerns the qualifying property interest, and §2056(b)(4)(B) requires account of encumbrances without prescribing the Commissioner’s universal arithmetic here. The regulation’s net-value direction and material-limitation qualification operate together. Its administration-income example says the rule may apply; it makes neither every payment automatically dispositive nor all income-funded expenses exempt. Lawful allocation under an instrument and Georgia law cannot alone establish immateriality, and exclusion of actual postmortem income from the gross estate does not resolve the effect on the beneficial interest.

Thomas Jefferson University and Auer give controlling weight to the responsible agency’s fair and considered interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. Another reasonable reading would not by itself defeat the Commissioner. Here the asserted inevitable reduction eliminates the material-limitation inquiry required by the qualified text. Smiley preserves statutory boundaries and reasoned interpretive authority but supplies no tax measurement formula. Revenue Ruling 93-48’s treatment of deferred-tax interest informs the comparison without expressly deciding every administration expense or wholly withdrawing Revenue Ruling 73-98.

The Court does not decide whether every pertinent valuation fact must have been predictable at death, whether later events may enter valuation, or what complete calculation method applies. Expenses paid from qualifying principal retain their separate reduction treatment. The charitable deduction receives the same disposition because the parties treat this expense issue alike, not because §§2055 and 2056 or their regulations have identical terms.

#### The actual burden and preserved theory require affirmance

**Holding and operative rule:** For this administration-expense adjustment omitted from the original deficiency notice, Tax Court Rule 142(a) placed the burden on the Commissioner. Having pursued categorical reduction without offering and maintaining a quantitative-materiality ground for upsetting the Tax Court’s finding, the Commissioner has established no basis for reversal, and the Court does not reopen proof to supply that alternative theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The allocation of the burden is particular to this adjustment; it is not a general reversal of the taxpayer’s ordinary burden. The Tax Court found no material limitation on the beneficial interests, and the Commissioner’s legal theory sought to make the payment itself conclusive. Once that theory fails, a court cannot manufacture an alternative evidentiary demonstration. The estate’s total size is not made the decisive denominator, and the approximately $1.5 million paid from income is not declared immaterial in every estate. A properly litigated case must assess the material effect on the relevant interest under the governing law. This record supports neither a numerical threshold, percentage safe harbor, mandatory factor list nor automatic further hearing. The stipulated qualification of these trusts is not reopened, and no deficiency amount is recalculated.

The Court leaves §642(g), distinct qualification rules, principal-funded expenses and properly preserved valuation methods to their own provisions. A clearer prospective rule remains subject to lawful statutory and administrative authority; none is announced here.

## Precedent Treatment

- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala: applies controlling weight to permissible own-regulation interpretation while enforcing text, consistency and actual-ground limits; the agency cannot remove an operative qualification.
- Auer v. Robbins: applies the fair-and-considered construction rule within the regulation’s terms; no deference is given to missing facts or an amendment presented as interpretation.
- Smiley v. Citibank: preserves statutory limits and adequately explained interpretation within delegated authority; its banking rule supplies no estate-tax valuation formula.

## Law After Decision

The deductions remain governed by their distinct statutes and regulations. For the disputed income-funded expenses, the material limitation on the qualifying interest must be given legal effect, and allocation to income neither guarantees a full deduction nor automatically reduces it dollar for dollar. The Commissioner’s special burden and unmaintained alternative support this affirmance. No universal valuation-date theory, calculation formula or safe harbor becomes law.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Breyer. They would require a reduction because income diverted to estate administration no longer passes as beneficial value to the spouse or charity. The net-value direction and express administration-income example permit the Commissioner to treat a limitation consequential to value as material without an additional unspecified substantiality threshold. Thomas Jefferson University and Auer support that permissible interpretation. Qualification does not answer valuation, and bookkeeping allocation cannot eliminate the economic loss. Revenue Ruling 93-48’s distinct deferred-tax-interest treatment did not abandon the administration-expense position.

They do not finally adopt dollar-for-dollar subtraction. The proper measure, including any discounted-value calculation, was insufficiently resolved and should be determined on remand. The Commissioner retains the burden for this adjustment; their disagreement concerns the legal need for an additional quantitative threshold, not a shifted burden or invented evidence. Breyer particularly emphasizes that future payment and present value may differ. A justified administrative simplification could conceivably be reasonable, but no such explanation or final arithmetic is supplied here. Both dissenters would reverse and remand without a new deficiency amount.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit’s judgment and the reviewed Tax Court result remain in force. The Court orders no recomputation, new evidence, reopened trust qualification or revised deficiency. Future disputes involving materially limited interests or preserved measurement questions retain their own governing rules.

## Event

Young v. Harper, No. 95-1598. Argued December 9, 1996; decided March 18, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, which reversed the denial of federal habeas relief. The question is whether termination of Oklahoma preparole community release requires the procedures governing revocation of parole.

Harper was released in October 1990 to live at home, work and report to a parole officer under parole-like conditions. After five apparently uneventful months, the Governor denied formal parole; on March 14, 1991, an officer telephoned and directed him to return immediately to prison without the required revocation process. An August 1991 procedure later addressing parole denial postdated that return. The original procedure was silent on the effect of parole denial, and Harper’s acknowledgment tied community status to compliance with release conditions.

The appellate rehearing petition was two days late, but the Tenth Circuit granted leave, treated it as timely and withheld the mandate until denial. Certiorari was sought within 90 days of that denial.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate.

## Public Action

The Court holds review timely and affirms the judgment of the Tenth Circuit.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

All nine support timely review; the Chief Justice agrees only with that resolution, while eight Associates adopt Part I’s timing rationale. All nine support the merits and bounded relief. Oklahoma must provide constitutionally adequate revocation process for this conditional liberty, but the judgment grants no unconditional release, formal parole or fixed release date.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Supports timely review and affirmance | Parts I–IV in full |

## Holdings

Part I is directly joined by all eight Associate Justices. Parts II–IV are directly joined by all nine Justices. These are majority holdings at their stated scopes; no separate-rationale synthesis is involved.

#### Part I — Accepted rehearing makes this petition timely

**Holding and operative rule:** When the appellate court expressly permits a late rehearing filing, treats it as timely and keeps the proceeding open pending its resolution, the accepted rehearing’s denial is the relevant finality point for the ensuing certiorari period. This petition, filed within 90 days of denial on that sequence, is timely.

**Controlling explanation:** The governing Rule 13.3 measures the period from denial of timely rehearing, and FRAP 40 permits enlargement by court order. Bowman distinguishes an unauthorized late submission from one the court permits and entertains. Jenkins similarly respects the lower court’s actual treatment as substantive rehearing without allowing manipulation of this Court’s statutory period. Here leave was granted, the filing was treated as timely and the mandate was withheld. A merely unauthorized late paper, an en banc suggestion not treated as rehearing, a mandate date alone or a later order entered solely to extend this Court’s filing time would not establish the same result. The Court waives no statutory period and announces no rule that every late submission restarts it.

#### Part II — Existing parole-like community liberty requires protection

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State releases a prisoner to live at home, work and conduct ordinary community life under parole-like supervision and conditions, termination of that existing conditional liberty requires Morrissey’s protections. Retained legal custody, an overcrowding objective, the preparole label and a separate decision about formal parole do not eliminate that interest.

**Controlling explanation:** Morrissey protects substantial conditional freedom already possessed while accounting for a continuing sentence and lawful grounds for return. Greenholtz distinguishes a request for initial release, and Meachum’s ordinary internal-transfer setting does not describe this community life. Sandin expressly preserves directly constitutional liberty alongside its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement inquiry; no exhaustive program-entitlement finding is needed here. Residence, employment and supervision establish the relevant difference, without making five months a minimum constitutional duration.

The Governor’s denial is not automatically an adequate revocation proceeding. The August 1991 procedure cannot establish a return condition communicated at Harper’s October 1990 release or validate his March return by itself. Yet the earlier procedure’s silence on parole denial does not establish the whole program’s terms, prove compliance with every condition or make release irrevocable. The Court does not invalidate the later procedure prospectively or decide every lawful ground for return. Formal parole, short furloughs, work details and ordinary internal transfers remain distinct.

#### Part III — Morrissey’s ordinary stages and qualifications govern

**Holding and operative rule:** Termination of this community liberty requires the ordinary preliminary and final revocation protections described below. Final revocation must rest on a constitutionally adequate determination, but a completed final hearing is not invariably required before initial arrest or detention.

**Required process and operative qualifications:** When the participant is arrested and detained pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the place of the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground for detention for a release-condition violation. A person not directly involved in the case decides; that person need not be a judge and may be a different parole officer. The participant receives notice of the inquiry, its purpose and the alleged violations, may appear and speak, and may present relevant documents and witnesses. On request he may question an adverse informant in his presence. If the officer determines that identifying the informant would expose that person to harm, confrontation and cross-examination of that informant may be withheld. The officer summarizes the proceedings and states the reasons and evidence supporting the determination; formal judicial findings are unnecessary. Supported probable cause permits continued detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final revocation hearing, if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation. It must resolve contested relevant facts and whether the circumstances warrant revocation, allowing denial and mitigation. Minimum protections are written notice of the claimed violations or asserted grounds; disclosure of adverse evidence; an opportunity to be heard personally and present witnesses and documents; confrontation and cross-examination unless the officer specifically finds good cause to withhold them; a neutral and detached decisionmaker, whose members need not be judges or lawyers; and a written statement of the evidence relied on and reasons.

Informal evidence, including letters and affidavits, may be considered. Issues already determined adversely in another forum, including a relevant criminal conviction, need not be relitigated. Morrissey’s discussion of two months establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor. Preliminary informant risk and final-stage specific good cause are distinct exceptions, each permitting rather than requiring withholding upon the necessary finding. No such finding, valid waiver, dispositive admission, prior determination or supported probable-cause decision is established here. This case decides no separate counsel question.

**Controlling explanation:** Morrissey’s flexible administrative process protects reliable identification of a lawful ground and the judgment whether revocation is warranted. Calling the program preparole cannot replace those safeguards with a telephone return instruction. Conversely, procedural protection does not convert revocation into a criminal trial or require a judicial decisionmaker. Temporary detention remains distinct from final deprivation and must receive the appropriate prompt preliminary and final protections. The Court adds no evidentiary burden, fixed deadline, automatic-release consequence or new adjudicatory code.

#### Part IV — Affirmance preserves lawful grounds and bounded relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tenth Circuit correctly reversed denial of habeas relief because the termination of Harper’s community liberty required Morrissey process. Proceedings must apply that determination, while Oklahoma retains authority to establish a legally available revocation ground through adequate procedures or another independent lawful basis for custody.

**Controlling explanation:** The procedural violation does not establish formal-parole entitlement, expiration of sentence, absence of every possible release-condition violation or a particular release date. The original telephone instruction does not itself supply an adequate revocation determination. The Court neither directs unconditional release nor prescribes a new deadline for state proceedings; any subsequent revocation or continuing custody must be assessed under the governing law and actual record. Lynce supplies no hypothetical exhaustion defect and no implied waiver here.

- Meachum v. Fano: its ordinary internal-transfer setting is distinguished from actual community release.
