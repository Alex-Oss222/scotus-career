- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** Thomasson asks the Court to reverse, declare the statement-based discharge unlawful, and order reinstatement and correction of his records. The Government seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Lieutenant Paul Thomasson completed ten years of highly rated Navy service and was repeatedly praised for leadership and performance. In March 1994 he wrote four admirals that he was gay and opposed the new policy. A Board of Inquiry treated the statement as creating a rebuttable presumption of a propensity or intent to engage in prohibited acts. Thomasson presented his service record and supporting testimony but refused to disprove uncharged private conduct. The Navy identified no past prohibited act, performance failure, security breach, or operational impairment and honorably discharged him. The reported appellate record states that the Board received the service record, live and written testimony, and expert evidence, but found the statutory presumption unrebutted because no specific evidence rebutting conduct, propensity or intent was tendered. Receipt followed by a finding of insufficiency is distinct from exclusion of evidence or an instruction requiring denial of identity. Whether that treatment was legally erroneous remains a question for the competing constructions; the present packet supplies no separate finding that the Board expressly required recantation.
- **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The discharge and request for reinstatement keep the dispute live. The constitutional claims were preserved below. Neutral rules directed to separately established sexual conduct, fraternization, coercion or operational misconduct present different predicates and are not resolved merely by deciding the status-or-statement challenge.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Application of Romer’s actual rational-basis holding

Romer rejects the breadth of Amendment 2’s class-specific disability under ordinary rational basis, with conceivable grounds and reasonable prediction still available; it does not establish that every narrower orientation distinction is unconstitutional or that government must produce findings and empirical certainty. Virginia supplies the form of intermediate scrutiny for its sex classification, not the classification of orientation. Section 654’s identity-to-propensity presumption must therefore receive its own constitutional analysis, and the service record must be evaluated for what it actually shows rather than as conclusive proof about every private act.

## G. RECONCILED QUESTIONS, LAW, AND AVAILABLE PATHS

Unlike Walmer, this case concerns §654’s statutory statements rule. Its presumption is formally rebuttable; the question is what evidence can actually rebut it without requiring denial of identity. Romer retained rational-basis review for Amendment 2. Virginia supplies the form of sex-based intermediate scrutiny at its own classification scope, not an automatic transfer to orientation. Thomasson invokes his identity statement, record and lack of individualized prohibited-conduct evidence; the Navy invokes the statutory prediction and military judgment. A statement used as evidence of conduct and a penalty imposed for protected expression are not interchangeable characterizations. A supported as-applied equal-protection remedy could dispose of the discharge without resolving every independent speech or substantive-liberty theory.
