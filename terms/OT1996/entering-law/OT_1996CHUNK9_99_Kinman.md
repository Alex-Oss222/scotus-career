## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

### Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

**Governing question:** Whether remedial silence or Spending Clause notice bars compensatory damages in the established Title IX private action.

**Current rule:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing relief does not itself create a new cause of action. Title IX's established private action permits compensatory damages for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation, and compensable loss. The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar that compensation for intentional breach of Title IX's express nondiscrimination condition.

**Authority by component:**

- Appropriate relief in an existing action and availability of Title IX compensation: Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools — Compensatory relief in the established Title IX action, February 26, 1992.
- Rejection of the categorical funding-notice bar: Franklin — Notice does not bar damages for the pleaded intentional violation, February 26, 1992. Both holdings rest on White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; Scalia and Thomas supply judgment votes only on a narrower statutory ground.

**Present operation effective:** *Franklin*, February 26, 1992.

**Open question:** The decision supplies no final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, liability for unintended discriminatory effects, or resolution of genuinely ambiguous funding conditions.

**Holdings navigation:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992) — Statutory discrimination remedies.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing supervisory acquiescence in school-authority sexual abuse

### Knowing supervisory acquiescence in school-authority sexual abuse

**Governing question:** When a school supervisor's own inaction violates a pupil's bodily integrity.

**Current rule:** A supervisor is personally responsible when the supervisor actually knows of sexual abuse through school authority or a substantial risk of it, deliberately and unreasonably disregards that known danger despite authority and a reasonable opportunity to protect the pupil, and that culpable inaction causes the injury or its continuation. A reasonable response is not actionable merely because injury continues. Circumstantial evidence may establish awareness, but warning signs do not compel that finding and what the official merely should have known is insufficient. Supervisory status, negligence, failure to discover misconduct, or breach of a state reporting duty alone does not establish the federal wrong.

**Authority by component:**

- Personal culpability and causation: Knowing, causally effective acquiescence in a teacher’s sexual abuse is personal wrongdoing, 1995-10-16; Ginsburg Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Doe v. Taylor ISD, 1995-10-16.

**Exceptions and limits:** Individual qualified immunity requires its separate conduct-time notice inquiry; recognition of the current rule supplies no retroactive notice. No general monitoring duty, vicarious liability, or finding of ultimate liability follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Doe v. Taylor Independent School District.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective and subjective requirements, totality and absence of an injury floor: Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc — Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Present operation effective:** Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc, 1993-11-09.

**Open question:** A sex-specific reasonable-person formulation, constructive discharge and employer-attribution questions are not resolved by Harris.

**Holdings navigation:** Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.

#### Doe v. Taylor Independent School District

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** October 16, 1995.

##### Knowing, causally effective supervisory acquiescence in sexual abuse through school authority

**Holding:** A school supervisor violates a pupil's bodily-integrity right by actually knowing of sexual abuse through school authority or a substantial risk of it, deliberately and unreasonably disregarding the known danger despite authority and a reasonable opportunity to protect, and thereby causing injury or its continuation. A reasonable response is not actionable merely because injury occurs; awareness may be proved circumstantially, but warning signs do not compel it and what the official merely should have known is insufficient. The properly assumed Lankford facts describe this personal knowing acquiescence, for which preconduct law gave fair notice, so immunity does not bar this claim at this stage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct votes.

**Material application:** The teacher's exploitation of official authority and access supplies the governmental connection although some acts occurred away from school. The alleged warnings and culpable nonresponse are assumed for legal review; earlier contact, later intercourse and continuing abuse remain distinct for causation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Negligent failure to discover misconduct, a reporting-law breach alone, supervisory office or vicarious liability does not suffice. No communicated approval, actual liability, damages, district liability, separate Title IX claim or additional equal-protection merits ruling is established. May 1987 authorities cannot give retroactive notice for earlier omissions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Lankford's immunity denial, 5–4, and return the surviving claim for proof of actual awareness, culpable inaction and attributable injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ingraham supplies bodily security; Monell and Rizzo preserve personal attribution and causal linkage. Hafer confirms individual responsibility; Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law. Collins's voluntary-employment omissions do not decide knowing participation in deliberate bodily abuse. Farmer is an actual-awareness analogy, not retroactive notice. Albright supplies no controlling general rule displacing this claim. The earlier supervisory cases inform the specific notice inquiry without becoming interchangeable general duties; Jefferson and Lopez's later dates and distinct direct-action/supervisory settings limit their use.

##### Caplinger's immunity on the distinct particularized notice question

**Holding:** Caplinger receives qualified immunity because conduct-time law did not clearly establish that his particular supervisory conduct, on his own assumed information and responses, violated Doe's federal right. The Court neither decides that conduct's constitutional merits nor imputes Lankford's fuller knowledge to him.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela concurs only in the judgment on a separate merits ground; that ground is not controlling.

**Material application:** Caplinger had limited later information and undertook inquiries, an interview at which Doe denied sex, and warnings; a promised wider meeting did not occur. These circumstances are distinct from Lankford's alleged knowing acquiescence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional approval of Caplinger's response, broad school-official immunity, mandatory merits-first sequence, or resolution of the conflicting January/February notice descriptions follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Caplinger's immunity, unanimous in judgment. The legal/factual appellate boundary is separately recorded under Federal Courts, Doe, October 16, 1995, Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Elder and Hafer require individualized conduct-date examination. The May 1987 Jefferson and Lopez decisions neither supply earlier notice nor equate the superintendent's circumstances with the principal's.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

#### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.

#### Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-681.  
**Decided:** November 5, 1991.

##### Whether a state officer sued individually is a section 1983 person for acts undertaken through official authority

**Holding:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under 42 U.S.C. §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority, and state sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by that officer personally. The plaintiff must still prove the officer's own violation under color of law, and the officer retains applicable personal immunities.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The Court of Appeals construed the damages claims as personal claims against Hafer. Her authority to discharge employees supplies the state-law connection without converting the claims into claims against Pennsylvania.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional violations, qualified immunity, damages, and entitlement to reinstatement remain unresolved; no immunity standard changes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. The individual-capacity claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded by this decision.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kentucky v. Graham supplies the distinction between litigation capacities. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police is clarified and preserved for States and official-capacity damages defendants. Scheuer v. Rhodes confirms possible personal liability of executive officers; Monroe v. Pape supplies the under-color-of-law rule for misuse of official power. Ex parte Young's prospective-relief channel is unchanged.

### 1997-02-24 - Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.; First Circuit No. 95-1275, 68 F.3d 525; no separate Supreme Court merits docket assigned; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Hot_Sexy_Safer_Productions_merits_1997-02-24.md).

#### Holdings

#### The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Parental liberty protects choice among lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not give each parent authority to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. Accepting the alleged compulsory exposure and aftermath, this complaint establishes no displacement of family custody, educational choice or parental teaching that supports the requested substantive federal veto; the ruling does not approve pretextual curricular action or decide independent state-law permission rights.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined on each of the three granted holdings by every other Justice, giving each proposition nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Meyer and Pierce protect parental direction of education and upbringing. They do not make the public school a forum in which every parent may prescribe the particular lessons a child receives. The parents remain free to choose lawful educational alternatives and to instruct their children concerning the ideas encountered at school. Public-school enrollment does not waive that continuing role.

The Court acknowledges the difference from Curtis: attendance here was required, while Curtis concerned a separable optional resource and expressly reserved compulsory instruction. Its result cannot automatically decide this case. The particular compulsion alleged here is exposure to a presentation, not displacement of parental custody or instruction, compelled profession or personal performance by these plaintiffs. Those limits support the present conclusion without treating attendance as voluntary.

Settle requires a genuine pedagogical basis; calling an event curriculum is insufficient. The Court neither endorses the alleged crude conduct as sound teaching nor holds that any presentation bearing an educational label is constitutional. It decides that these allegations do not establish the particular parental deprivation asserted. The written-permission policy may impose an independent obligation, but its alleged breach does not itself create the substantive federal right the parents seek. State-law remedies and materially different claims remain outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Meyer and Pierce are applied at the scope of the parental interest they protect. Curtis's reservation of compulsory instruction is preserved and the present allegations are examined independently. Settle's genuine-pedagogy requirement remains intact.

#### These allegations establish neither the claimed student privacy deprivation nor extreme official abuse

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory exposure to the alleged presentation and subsequent coarse imitation does not, on this complaint, establish the students' asserted decisional-privacy or bodily-integrity deprivation or extreme abuse of official power. The conclusion rests on the actual allegations, including the absence of compelled personal performance, affirmation, intimate disclosure, physical contact or treatment; it is no categorical rule that words, a single event or compulsory educational programming can never violate the Constitution.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this holding in the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** A constitutional privacy or bodily-integrity claim requires an invasion of the protected interest, not simply exposure to ideas or language the listener finds offensive. These students were required to attend. They were not alleged to have been selected for a skit, required to affirm the message, disclose intimate information, engage in sexual or religious conduct, or undergo bodily intrusion. The Court considers that account together with the alleged weeks of coarse imitation; it does not discard the aftermath or convert unfavorable facts into trial findings.

The parents' strongest objection is that the school's compulsory authority gave the presentation an intrusive character beyond ordinary offensive speech. That circumstance matters, but it does not establish the particular personal deprivation pleaded. The alleged program, taken as a whole and with favorable inferences, also does not establish the extreme official abuse asserted here. The Court announces no universal rule that verbal conduct cannot do so and does not decide the rights of a student personally subjected to a materially different act.

Curtis supplies no categorical answer because it reserved compulsory instruction. Settle likewise does not license religious exclusion, pretextual pedagogy or arbitrary conduct merely because it occurs in school. The present holding leaves protections against compelled profession or participation, bodily intrusion, religious establishment or targeting, and independently established discrimination intact. It does not transform authority to restrict certain school speech into an individual federal entitlement to censorship of every offensive presentation.

**Precedent treatment:** Curtis's limits and Settle's genuine-operation inquiry are preserved. Neither decision supplies general school immunity or resolves materially different personal coercion or invasion claims.

#### The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Title IX requires discrimination, exclusion or a hostile educational condition imposed because of sex; sexual subject matter and offense alone do not establish that relationship. The complaint connects the alleged later hostility to disagreement with the presentation's message rather than the plaintiffs' sex, so this element's absence requires dismissal without deciding severity or pervasiveness, a comprehensive recipient-liability rule, or the treatment of materially different sex-based targeting.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this sufficient statutory ground. No severity, attribution or constitutional speech-defense holding supplies an alternative basis.

**Controlling explanation:** The presentation's sexual content and alleged offensiveness are accepted. So are the subsequent weeks of coarse imitation. Neither fact, alone or in their stated context, supplies the missing connection between the treatment of these plaintiffs and their sex. They allege disagreement with the program's message and ensuing hostility on that basis. The program's delivery to an audience containing both sexes is contextual evidence, not a categorical defense to sex discrimination.

Harris requires a contextual inquiry and rejects psychological injury as a prerequisite; DeAngelis likewise rejects mandatory deterioration in performance, physical advances or similar added thresholds. Those employment decisions inform the analysis by analogy, but the Court does not transplant a complete Title VII attribution formula into Title IX. Personal targeting, touching, measurable academic loss and psychological injury are not indispensable elements announced here. Nor does the Court hold that one episode can never suffice. It does not decide whether this entire program and aftermath meet a fully elaborated Title IX severity-or-pervasiveness standard.

Franklin recognizes compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination. Availability of that remedy cannot supply the statutory violation absent from this complaint. The decision leaves questions involving sex-specific humiliation, school-authorized sexual targeting and deliberate indifference to known sex-based harassment for cases that present them, without prescribing their complete liability rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris and DeAngelis retain their contextual protections and operate here only by analogy. Franklin's compensation rule remains available for a proved intentional violation; it does not dispense with the because-of-sex requirement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Meyer v. Nebraska and Pierce v. Society of Sisters protect parental educational choice and instruction; they do not supply the lesson-by-lesson veto sought on these allegations.
- Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth retains its rule for the optional program there and its express reservation of compulsory instruction and genuine religious compulsion. Today's required attendance receives its own analysis.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board continues to require genuine pedagogical reasons and to forbid religious exclusion disguised as curricular administration; an educational label is insufficient.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems and DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association preserve contextual harassment analysis without mandatory psychological injury, physical advances or measurable performance loss. Their employment rules do not automatically determine Title IX attribution or its complete harassment standard.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools preserves compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination without supplying a missing statutory element or deciding the reserved recipient-liability questions here.

#### Law After Decision

The three pleaded federal theories fail at their stated grounds. Compulsory attendance remains a material fact, but these allegations establish neither the asserted parental or personal constitutional deprivation nor discrimination because of sex. The Court adopts no general curricular immunity, categorical exemption for a single offensive episode, added injury prerequisite or comprehensive Title IX recipient rule. The separate ungranted constitutional and statutory dismissals and the state-law dispute receive no new controlling merits determination.

### 1997-02-26 — Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc.; Fourth Circuit No. 95-2476; 88 F.3d 258.

Authority: [Record](../records/Andrade_v_Mayfair_Management_Inc_merits_1997-02-26.md).

#### Holdings

All holdings below are in Justice Breyer's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices. Each is the holding of the Court, not an aggregation of separate reasons.

##### Employer responsibility through delegated authority

**Operative rule.** An employer may be responsible under Title VII for a supervisor-created hostile environment when actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the creation or maintenance of that environment, even without actual or constructive notice to higher management. A supervisory title or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; the employee must establish the connection between delegated authority and the misconduct, while negligent failure to correct known or reasonably knowable harassment remains a distinct route to liability.

**Controlling explanation.** Meritor directs courts to agency principles and rejects both automatic imputation of every supervisor's misconduct and a universal defense based on lack of notice. The statutory employer-agent relationship gives legal significance to authority the employer actually entrusted to the wrongdoer. Hiring, firing, discipline and threatened employment action may establish the necessary connection. An express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; daily control of working conditions can also aid unlawful conduct. The employer's argument that personal misconduct necessarily falls outside its responsibility would eliminate the agency inquiry Meritor preserves. Conversely, the employee cannot establish attribution simply by pointing to the administrator's rank. Harris and DeAngelis define an actionable hostile environment without supplying this separate attribution predicate. The administrator's control and hiring and firing authority therefore require examination, even though he reported to a corporate officer. The Court does not adopt a general liability rule for every supervisor's private act or a new affirmative defense.

##### Corrected review of the employer's judgment

**Operative rule.** A Rule 50 judgment resting on the legal premise that higher management's lack of notice necessarily defeats supervisor-agency attribution must be reconsidered under the proper agency standard, with the evidence and permissible inferences viewed in the verdict winner's favor. Vacatur does not itself establish attribution, restore the verdict or require a new trial; preserved alternative grounds, evidentiary sufficiency and genuine instruction or preservation questions remain for the lower court.

**Controlling explanation.** The jury's $25,000 award does not answer whether the correct attribution question was submitted or whether the record legally supports the verdict. The lower courts foreclosed the agency route because the administrator was not a proprietor, partner or corporate officer and higher management lacked notice. That categorical barrier cannot stand under Meritor. The proper response is to return the preserved attribution issue for review under the corrected rule, not to treat every allegation as a finding or assume that no other preserved ground can matter. Constructive discharge was separately rejected and lies outside the granted questions; this remand does not reopen it.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied to require an actual agency inquiry; neither supervisor status nor absence of higher-management notice supplies a categorical answer.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Preserved as the objective-and-subjective hostile-environment standard without mandatory psychological injury; it does not establish employer attribution.
- **DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers Association:** Preserved for the severity-or-pervasiveness inquiry and its context; proof of an unlawful environment does not dispense with the separate attribution requirement.

No precedent is overruled. The Court resolves neither constructive discharge nor a general affirmative-defense regime.

#### Law After Decision

Title VII retains distinct routes through agency attribution and employer negligence. Courts must examine whether delegated authority actually enabled the supervisor's harassment; higher management's lack of notice alone cannot foreclose that route. The hostile-environment threshold remains separate. The legal rule changes the analysis of the employer's Rule 50 judgment, while leaving factual sufficiency, preserved alternative grounds and the appropriate next proceeding to the lower court.

### 1997-03-10 — Nabozny v. Podlesny; Seventh Circuit No. 95-3634, 92 F.3d 446; no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Nabozny_v_Podlesny_merits_1997-03-10.md).

#### Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. That direct common coalition controls each holding below.

###### Intentionally unequal school protection because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials who intentionally withhold ordinarily available protection because a student is male must establish that the sex distinction substantially serves an important governmental objective. The supported evidence permits a finding of purposeful unequal administration without a sufficient objective, so the sex claim independently survives summary judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Yick Wo forbids deliberately unequal administration of an existing protection. Feeney requires purposeful selection because of the adverse effect, and Arlington Heights permits that purpose to be shown through statements, departures and context. Hogan and Virginia supply the demanding important-objective and substantial-relationship inquiry for sex discrimination. Officials need not give identical discipline in every case, and negligence, unequal outcomes or violation of a state policy alone does not establish this claim. Here the alleged responses to male-on-male abuse, admitted ordinary enforcement and differing treatment of male-on-female abuse permit the requisite finding. Missing names and dates in some comparator examples affect proof but do not erase the direct evidence. Defendants may establish their contrary account at trial; credibility cannot be resolved against Nabozny now.

###### Intentionally unequal protection because of sexual orientation

**Holding and operative rule:** Intentional denial of ordinarily available school protection because of sexual orientation violates equal protection when the distinction has no rational relation to a legitimate objective. The orientation claim separately survives on the supported account without assignment of a heightened scrutiny tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer preserves ordinary rational-basis review, including conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit, while enforcing Moreno’s and Cleburne’s prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice. Walmer applies that inquiry in its particular military setting; its service-related rationale supplies no reason to deny this student ordinary protection against battery. Wright likewise permits reasonable operational predictions but supplies no school justification and expressly reserves discriminatory administration; its marriage threshold does not govern this equality claim. Bowers’s specified-conduct holding does not authorize private violence or discriminatory nonprotection. The alleged statements and enforcement departures support purposeful selection for disadvantage because Nabozny was openly gay, and no legitimate disciplinary objective explains that treatment on the favorable account. Knowledge, deliberate indifference without discriminatory purpose and inconsistent enforcement alone do not suffice. Virginia establishes no sexual-orientation tier. Neither Curtis nor Brown school excuses intentional discrimination merely because it occurs in an educational setting.

###### Conduct-time equality law defeats individual immunity on these facts

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual officials receive no qualified immunity when the supported facts show purposeful, irrational withholding of an ordinary protective policy whose unlawfulness was apparent from the relevant law when they acted; a factually identical harassment case is unnecessary. The clearly established sex-equality ground independently defeats immunity, and the narrow irrational-discrimination ground also supports denial on the orientation claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson require objectively apparent unlawfulness at the particularized level of the conduct, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant conduct-time authority. Before the conduct began in 1988, Reed, Weinberger and Hogan prohibited arbitrary sex distinctions; Yick Wo, Feeney and Arlington Heights supplied the purposeful-administration rule; Moreno and Cleburne rejected bare harm and irrational prejudice. Bohen provided relevant circuit notice concerning intentional sex discrimination, although its setting was employment. This is alleged deliberate discrimination in an existing protection, not notice of a novel affirmative-danger duty. Pinder’s contrary immunity result concerned that distinct due-process uncertainty as of its conduct date. DeShaney’s later equality reservation can confirm the distinction only for conduct after its date and is unnecessary to pre-1989 notice. Romer, Virginia and later decisions furnish no retroactive notice. The school’s policy is evidence of practice and knowledge, not an independent federal cause or automatic immunity defeat. Each individual’s actual participation, purpose and causation remain disputed; denial of legal immunity on assumed facts does not decide liability.

###### The preserved due-process theories fail on their own actor-specific record

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that sufficiently culpable official creation or increase of danger, or an attributable policy affirmatively encouraging private injury, can establish a constitutional deprivation, the preserved failure-to-punish and harmful-policy theories do not establish that contribution here. Their dismissal therefore stands without a categorical rule against all affirmative-danger claims or a due-process immunity holding.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a general governmental duty to protect from private violence. Pinder separates omission of public protection from affirmative deprivation of available protection; Uhlrig leaves the merits of the latter theory open. Lack of custody alone cannot defeat that distinct claim, and no assurance/reliance chain is made indispensable.

The Court examines the favorable account beyond mere silence. Promises, stereotyped responses, counseling for leaving after the mock assault and return to the ordinary schedule support the equality claim but do not establish a separate act directing a new assault or depriving available protection. Davis and Blauert referred Nabozny for a protective schedule change; the later special-education placement alongside assailants does not identify a remaining defendant’s deliberate decision or establish its increased-danger contribution to a particular later injury. Social Services’ return order is not attributed to these defendants or a District policy. Crawford’s discouragement of charges followed the completed beating, cannot have caused it, and establishes no later injury or attributable District policy. He is not a remaining defendant. Repeated nonenforcement does not become affirmative injury merely by being called a policy. The Court neither adjudicates a new Crawford claim nor rules out liability for proved obstruction, culpable placement or other materially different affirmative conduct.

The Court decides no sexual-orientation scrutiny tier, general school-safety entitlement, complete affirmative-danger test, municipal policy, actual liability or measure of damages.

### 1997-03-19 — Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.; court-of-appeals No. 95-30510.

Authority: [Record](../records/Oncale_v_Sundowner_Offshore_Services_Inc_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII

**Rule:** Title VII does not exclude discriminatory harassment merely because the offender and employee share a sex. The claimant must plead and ultimately prove that the challenged employment conditions were imposed because of sex and must satisfy the remaining requirements of the asserted claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor establishes that abusive working conditions can constitute statutory discrimination. The enacted protection concerns the individual employee's treatment, not an unstated requirement that employee and offender belong to different sex categories. A categorical exclusion therefore cannot support this dismissal. Rejecting that exclusion does not prove any allegation, create liability for all objectionable conduct, or adopt a free-standing dignity cause of action. The independent statutory-employer ruling outside the grant remains untouched.

##### Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element

**Rule:** Discrimination because of sex may be established through competent direct or circumstantial evidence without proving sexual desire, a particular sexual orientation, or the presence of an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish discriminatory causation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hicks preserves the claimant's ultimate persuasion burden without making one evidentiary route compulsory. Sex-directed hostility, selective conduct, discriminatory statements, threats and the circumstances can support a causal inference; this illustrative list is neither exclusive nor a checklist. Desire can be relevant, but making it indispensable would exclude sex-based mistreatment for reasons the statute does not recognize. The trier must evaluate the actual inference and competing explanations. Ordinary interpersonal hostility unconnected to sex remains outside this cause, and no orientation identity is found here.

##### Apply Harris in the actual workplace context

**Rule:** A hostile-environment claim requires sex-based conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively hostile or abusive environment in context, which the claimant actually perceived as abusive. An all-male workforce supplies context, not categorical immunity; neither psychiatric injury nor measurable productivity loss is indispensable.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires an objective and subjective inquiry into the circumstances and supplies the severe-or-pervasive threshold. Context helps distinguish actionable abuse from ordinary offensive banter; it cannot excuse forced physical humiliation or rape threats simply by labeling them rough camaraderie. The alleged conduct may satisfy the threshold, but allegations are not findings and gravity does not substitute for unwelcome treatment, discriminatory causation or employer attribution. No single factor, diagnosis, treatment, universal reasonable-woman standard or medical proof is made compulsory.

##### Attribution, other claims and relief remain distinct on remand

**Rule:** If employer attribution is reached, Andrade requires examination whether actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the hostile environment; job title alone is insufficient, and absence of higher-management notice alone does not defeat that agency route. Negligence and any preserved quid-pro-quo claim remain distinct inquiries, and this reversal decides neither their factual predicates nor relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Andrade supplies the governing agency rule, including that employment action or economic loss is not invariably necessary for its authority-aided-environment route. A quid-pro-quo theory separately requires the alleged demand, use of employment power and employment-condition consequence to be identified; coverage does not collapse the two theories. The lower courts must apply the correct rules to the affected claims and any properly presented independent defenses in ordinary course. The Court does not reinstate an unchallenged coworker-employer theory, decide Lyons's status, find constructive discharge, order discovery or trial, or award reinstatement, backpay or punitive damages. Remedy questions for the 1991 conduct remain open.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied the recognition of discriminatory abusive working conditions, extending its statutory application to employees and offenders who share a sex without eliminating any element.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Applied its contextual severe-or-pervasive, objective and subjective inquiry; no psychiatric injury or measurable economic harm is added.
- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks:** Preserved ultimate proof of discrimination and the availability of circumstantial inference; no compulsory comparator or desire showing is created.
- **Andrade v. Mayfair Management:** Applied the actual-authority/material-aid employer-attribution rule, keeping negligence and employment consequences distinct.
- **Brown v. Polk County and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Their separate statutory and constitutional grounds do not replace Title VII's requirements or decide a sexual-orientation coverage question here.
- **Garcia's categorical same-sex exclusion:** The lower-court rule cannot govern Title VII after this decision.

#### Law After Decision

Title VII reaches discriminatory harassment without an opposite-sex requirement. Desire and workforce composition create no categorical gates or immunity. The employee still must establish sex causation and every element of the applicable harassment theory; Harris and Andrade retain their independent force. No sexual-orientation coverage holding or automatic liability rule is adopted.

### 1997-03-31 — United States v. Lanier; No. 95-1717.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Lanier_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted

**Rule:** Section 242, alone or as authoritatively construed, must make the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; a novel or unforeseeable criminal application is forbidden. Willful bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws supplies the statute's willfulness and definite-right limitations. Vagueness, lenity and the prohibition on unexpected retrospective judicial enlargement protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. The courts interpret Congress's criminal enactment rather than create common-law crimes. The reprehensibility or state criminality of the alleged conduct does not substitute for identification of a federal right and adequate notice of its application. Current decisions govern the reviewing court's legal method at their actual scopes; a decision issued after the conduct cannot itself supply earlier notice.

##### Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions

**Rule:** Fair warning under §242 may be supplied by the relevant pre-existing legal landscape, including lower-court authority; it does not categorically require a Supreme Court decision on the particular application. Neither one lower-court decision nor a fixed numerical consensus is automatically sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws did not prescribe the Sixth Circuit's Supreme-Court-only restriction. Elder supplies the examination of all relevant authority in assessing clear constitutional law; actual conflicts, source authority and their relation to the charged acts matter. Excluding a whole relevant class of decisions can wrongly convert established law into unknowable law. Conversely, source breadth does not eliminate the demand for sufficient clarity in the particular context.

##### Obvious applications need no factual twin

**Rule:** Existing constitutional law can give fair warning of an obviously covered application without an earlier case involving fundamentally similar facts. A broad invocation of liberty or bodily security is insufficient where the particular application remains unsettled, including where an earlier decision expressly leaves it open.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson supplies a particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness, not a demand for repetition of every factual detail. Officials cannot insist upon a decision matching their office, victim and precise method when established law applies with obvious clarity. At the same time, Elder and the actual Pinder and Uhlrig decisions preserve attention to unsettled applications and personal responsibility. Those distinctions prevent an abstract right from automatically resolving each charged assault, official nexus or notice question. The Court rejects the categorical similarity prerequisite while leaving those count-specific determinations below.

##### The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact

**Rule:** Section 242 fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: constitutional unlawfulness must be apparent in light of pre-existing law. That common protection supplies neither automatic criminal liability nor a substitute for constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the Government's criminal burden of proof and every other statutory element.

**Controlling explanation:** Due process does not demand a categorically clearer legal rule in the criminal case than the clarity protection furnished in the civil inquiry. But a conclusion about civil damages immunity cannot itself convict anyone. Screws's willfulness requirement performs separate work, and judicial title alone does not establish that each act occurred under color of state law. The Sixth Circuit must keep legal notice and proof of each criminal predicate distinct, rather than use either to erase the other.

##### Review the counts without the rejected legal shortcuts

**Rule:** The appellate court must reconsider the case without a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite and without categorical exclusion of due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody from §242. The judgment resolves no count's ultimate constitutional basis, fair warning, official nexus, proof or sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws and United States v. Price foreclose excluding rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process wholesale. DeShaney's limits on affirmative protection against private violence do not immunize an official's own assault merely because the victim was outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendment. Actual Collins, Pinder, Uhlrig and Nabozny preserve the distinction among personal official wrongdoing, omitted protection and supervisory attribution. These are legal directions for review, not new findings of assault, automatic restoration of the verdicts, a universal constitutional-assault formula or an order for a new evidentiary hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Screws v. United States:** Applied definite-right and willfulness protections; rejected a supposed Supreme-Court-only source restriction and categorical exclusion of due-process rights.
- **United States v. Price:** Preserved §242's reach to protected due-process rights without deciding the elements of each charged count.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied particularized apparent unlawfulness; no factual-twin requirement follows.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Applied consideration of relevant existing authority while preserving temporal limits.
- **Pinder v. Johnson and Uhlrig v. Harder:** Preserved their particularized notice, unsettled-application and personal-attribution boundaries; later holdings cannot create earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Kept constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation and supervisory theories distinct; their later dates cannot establish notice for earlier conduct.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserved the difference between a constitutional deprivation and every injurious official act; no broad federal tort rule is created.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Distinguished limits on protection from private violence from the official's own asserted assault.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied the specific-provision rule when a particular Amendment governs, without treating the Fourth and Eighth Amendments as exhaustive of every abusive official act.

#### Law After Decision

Section 242 has no Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite. Pre-existing law must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness apparent, using the same legal-clarity protection as clearly-established-law analysis while preserving every distinct criminal element and burden. The Court creates no new substantive right or count-specific liability holding.
