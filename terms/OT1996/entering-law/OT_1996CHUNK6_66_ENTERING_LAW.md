## Standards and Tests selection: Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority

### Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority

**Governing question:** Whether a transfer of duties among government officials changes a voting practice subject to preclearance.

**Current rule:** Section 5 requires a direct relation to voting; a redistribution of governing authority does not qualify merely because it diminishes an elected official's influence over a function. Changes in voting procedure, candidacy, electorate, or method of office selection supply familiar but nonexhaustive covered categories. Reallocation of road funds within an elected commission and transfer of road operations to an appointed engineer while the elected commission retains substantial appointment and budget authority do not, on those premises, require preclearance. The Attorney General's interpretation receives substantial consideration within the statutory boundary, but a significant-transfer/different-constituencies formulation cannot substitute for a connection to voting. Postelection timing alone creates no exemption, and coverage remains separate from discriminatory purpose or effect.

**Authority by component:**

- Voting/governing boundary and retained-office scope: Presley v. Etowah County Commission — The two resolutions do not change a voting practice, January 27, 1992.
- Limit on administrative and different-constituencies constructions: Presley — Administrative interpretation does not eliminate the voting boundary, January 27, 1992. Kennedy's opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas controls both.

**Present operation effective:** *Presley*, January 27, 1992.

**Open question:** Whether an extreme transfer effectively replacing an elective office with an appointive office requires preclearance is unresolved. The decision does not dispose of independent §2, Title VI, or constitutional claims.

**Holdings navigation:** Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992) — Voting Rights Act preclearance.

## Standards and Tests selection: Voting Rights Act preclearance of primary-like party participation fees

### Voting Rights Act preclearance of primary-like party participation fees

**Governing question:** A changed financial prerequisite to participation in a qualifying party convention selecting its preferential general-ballot nominee.

**Current rule:** Section 5 covers a changed fee where the primary-like electoral function, participation practice and State's statutory nomination relationship together supply the voting connection and covered-jurisdiction authority. A convention label does not create an exemption; ballot preference alone does not decide every state-action issue. The change must affect voting, perform a public electoral function and rest on explicit or implicit authority from a covered jurisdiction; exclusions for recruitment, campaigning and platform drafting remain. Coverage does not itself require or establish racial purpose, retrogression or substantive illegality. The local court enforces submission for a legally relevant unprecleared change from the operative baseline, leaving preclearance purpose and effect to D.D.C. or the Attorney General.

**Authority by component:**

- Combined coverage predicates and separate submission process: II. This pleaded primary-like convention fee is subject to §5 and IV. Coverage, preclearance merits and relief remain separate, 1996-03-27; Souter’s Parts II and IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer (six).

**Present operation effective:** Morse, 1996-03-27.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** An appropriate party official may submit under §51.23(b). Section 5 permits the prescribed D.D.C. declaration or Attorney General submission with sixty-day nonobjection; good cause permits earlier affirmative nonobjection, subject to reexamination on additional information during the remaining sixty days. Neither route bars a later enforcement challenge. Virginia’s applicable baseline is November 1, 1964.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general governmental status for every party act, compulsory party message or candidate, universal regulation of convention rules, refund remedy or §5 damages action is established. Ordinary associational boundaries remain.

**Related current rules:** “Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority” preserves the direct-voting boundary; “The designated channel for preclearance review” preserves the separate federal review restrictions.

**Holdings navigation:** Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia, 517 U.S. 186.

## Standards and Tests selection: The designated channel for preclearance review

### The designated channel for preclearance review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern the designated channel for preclearance review?

**Current rule:** A district court outside the District of Columbia cannot review the Attorney General's Voting Rights Act preclearance determination through a challenge to a State's plan; §14(b)'s designated channel and Morris's review restriction govern. Dismissal on that basis does not immunize the State's plan from an independently cognizable constitutional or statutory challenge.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal preclearance dismissal remains separate: Shaw v. Reno — Federal preclearance dismissal remains separate, 1993-06-28. Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-28; Shaw v. Reno, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993); Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal election registration duties through state agencies

### Federal election registration duties through state agencies

**Governing question:** Congressional authority and the operative NVRA duties for federal-election registration through state agencies.

**Current rule:** Article I, §4 authorizes the challenged congressional-election registration duties, including distribution, assistance, acceptance and transmittal through motor-vehicle, public-assistance and disability-service offices; Congress may alter shared state administration without creating a wholly federal apparatus or reimbursing the State. For a State’s chosen popular presidential election, the challenged safeguards rest separately on the national protective authority recognized in Burroughs and necessary-and-proper legislation, not Article I, §4 alone. These grounds do not confer general power to command state government or select the State’s method of appointing presidential electors. Prescribed procedures do not themselves redefine substantive voter qualifications: officials retain eligibility decisions subject to the statute, which requires signed eligibility attestations including citizenship under penalty of perjury.

The Act governs federal elections. Section 4(b) exempts States with no federal-election registration requirement continuously effective on and after March 11, 1993, or qualifying election-day registration for every voter continuously effective on and after that date or enacted by that date to take effect upon the NVRA’s enactment and continuously maintained. Under §5(a), a license or renewal application serves as a registration application unless the applicant fails to sign the registration application; under the separate §5(d), a license change-of-address serves as a registration address change unless the registrant states otherwise. Registration may not demand duplicate license information except a second signature or information necessary for the eligibility statement and attestation, and only the minimum information needed to prevent duplication and permit eligibility assessment and administration may be required. The federal mail form may not require notarization or other formal authentication.

Section 7 requires designation of all public-assistance offices and offices administering state-funded programs primarily serving persons with disabilities, plus other offices; federal and nongovernmental offices may be designated with consent. Designated offices must distribute forms, assist and accept them for transmittal; disability offices serving a person at home must supply registration services there. Distribution with an application, recertification, renewal or address-change form may be omitted if the applicant declines registration in writing. Applicants who do not decline receive the same assistance as with the office’s own forms unless they refuse; failing to mark either choice box counts as declination for the assistance provision. Staff may not influence or display political preference, discourage registration or suggest that the registration choice affects benefits or services; declination and source information retain statutory confidentiality. Sections 5(e) and 7(d) require transmittal within ten days after acceptance, or within five days when acceptance occurs within five days before the last registration day for an election.

Section 11(a) independently authorizes Attorney General declaratory and injunctive enforcement. Private §11(b) notice conditions do not govern that public action; State immunity cannot erase the United States’ authorized prospective enforcement by treating it as private damages litigation.

**Authority by component:**

- Separate constitutional grounds, qualification boundary, public enforcement and full retained statutory qualifications: Voting Rights Coalition — four controlling propositions and operative statutory limits, 1996-02-26; unanimous Court opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson, 1996-02-26, for the constitutional and enforcement construction; the sustained statutory qualifications retain their enacted operation.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding decides no general private-enforcement immunity issue, private notice compliance, damages liability, exact State/federal roll identity, or every implementation practice. Concrete form-information, eligibility and maintenance disputes remain open. The alternative Fourteenth/Fifteenth Amendment ground is not reached.

**Related current rules:** “Compulsory state regulation or ownership of private obligations” retains its ordinary-commerce structural rule; the NVRA conclusion rests on distinct federal-election authority.

**Holdings navigation:** Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson, judgment below 60 F.3d 1411.

#### Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson, judgment below 60 F.3d 1411

**Docket or dockets:** Petition identifier No. 95-673; Ninth Circuit No. 95-15449; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** February 26, 1996.

##### Whether Congress may impose the challenged congressional-election registration duties

**Holding:** Article I, §4 authorizes the NVRA's challenged distribution, assistance, acceptance and transmittal commands to motor-vehicle, public-assistance and disability-service personnel, including registration at home where the designated disability office already serves the person there. Assigning electoral work to previously multipurpose agencies is within that allocation, and California's demand for federal reimbursement does not excuse compliance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The duties are not sustained as costless, trivial or voluntary; no general authority to command state administration follows. The sustained duties retain the following enacted qualifications, rather than new judicial conditions:

- The Act governs federal elections. Section 4(b) exempts a State with no federal-election registration requirement under law continuously effective on and after March 11, 1993; it also exempts a State permitting every voter to register at the polling place during a general federal election under law continuously effective on and after that date, or enacted by that date to take effect on enactment of the NVRA and continuously maintained thereafter. Those conditions are not a present opt-out for California.
- Under §5(a), a driver's-license or renewal application serves as a federal voter-registration application unless the applicant fails to sign the registration application; a submitted registration application updates prior registration. Under the distinct §5(d) exception, a driver's-license change-of-address form serves as a voter-registration address change unless the registrant states that it is not for that purpose. The license-registration portion may not demand duplicate license information except a second signature or information necessary for the required eligibility statement and attestation, and may require only the minimum information needed to prevent duplicates and permit eligibility assessment and administration.
- Section 7 requires designation of all public-assistance offices and offices providing state-funded programs primarily serving persons with disabilities, as well as other registration offices. Federal and nongovernmental offices may be designated with their agreement. Designated offices must distribute, assist with and accept forms for transmittal; a designated disability office providing services at home must provide those registration services there. An applicant may refuse assistance. Distribution with the public-assistance or disability-service application, recertification, renewal or address-change form may be omitted when the applicant declines registration in writing; applicants who do not decline receive the same degree of assistance as with the office's own forms unless they refuse it. Failure to mark either registration-choice box counts as declination for the assistance provision. Staff may not seek to influence or display political preference, discourage registration, or suggest that registration choice affects services or benefits; declination and source information retain their statutory confidentiality protections.
- Sections 5(e) and 7(d) ordinarily require transmittal to the proper state election official within ten days after acceptance. When an application is accepted within five days before the last registration day for an election, transmittal is due within five days after acceptance. These are statutory duties, distinct from the district court's implementation-plan direction. State officials retain registration and roll decisions within the statute; the decision does not require identical federal and state rolls or determine a particular implementation practice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smiley includes registration in the manner power; Siebold permits partial federal alteration through shared state administration. New York's ordinary-commerce structural prohibition remains, distinguished by Article I, §4's express allocation.

##### The distinct constitutional basis for presidential registration

**Holding:** The challenged safeguards for California's chosen popular presidential election are valid under the national protective authority recognized by Burroughs, implemented through necessary-and-proper legislation. Article I, §4 alone does not supply presidential-election authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Act neither chooses the appointment method nor selects or allocates electors. This holding concerns registration opportunities, eligibility attestations and reliable rolls for the chosen popular election, not unlimited redesign or identical scope of congressional and presidential authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burroughs's national protective principle extends from its political-committee corrupt-practices setting to these registration safeguards at the stated scope.

##### Whether the methods themselves redefine substantive voter qualifications

**Holding:** The challenged prescribed registration methods do not themselves redefine who is qualified to vote. Article I, §2 and the Seventeenth Amendment retain their qualification rules; eligibility statements, citizenship information and signed perjury attestations remain, with state officials assessing eligibility within the Act's procedural limits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The federal mail form may not require notarization or other formal authentication. Permissible information and roll maintenance remain federally regulated; concrete eligibility, information and maintenance controversies are unresolved. Identical state and federal rolls are not required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Term Limits preserves the genuine-administration/additional-qualification distinction without expanding its candidate holding into every voter-qualification dispute.

##### The public-enforcement basis and limits of the compliance injunction

**Holding:** NVRA §11(a) independently authorizes the Attorney General's declaratory and injunctive action against California's established categorical noncompliance; private §11(b) notice conditions do not govern that action. The State's immunity objection cannot defeat the United States' authorized prospective enforcement by treating the action as a private damages suit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No damages, general private-enforcement immunity holding or finding of private notice compliance is made. The Fourteenth/Fifteenth Amendment alternative remains unneeded and undecided; implementation complexity alone supplies no basis for vacating a permanent injunction without an identified unauthorized command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the permanent injunction, existing implementation remand and retained appellate jurisdiction. Implementation must impose no burden unauthorized by the Act impairing retained state-election administration and must respect federalism. No original ten-day plan deadline is restarted; no new plan, process, severance or compliance finding is ordered. Reserved coalition claims and dismissals without prejudice of the FEC and chair remain as entered; appellate costs remain denied to every party.

#### Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia, 517 U.S. 186

##### The identified convention payment preserves a live statutory controversy

**Holding:** The convention's completion does not moot this appeal because Morse seeks return of his identified $45 payment on a substantial statutory restoration theory not conclusively eliminated by the judgment. That personal controversy permits decision of coverage and private-action questions without establishing repayment, the proper refund defendant or every plaintiff's prospective standing.

##### Section 5 covers the pleaded primary-like convention's changed financial prerequisite

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by the same six Justices as Part II.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §10(a) findings and constitutional declaration, subsection (b)'s official-enforcement direction and subsection (c)'s judicial channel retain distinct functions. #### Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-711 and 90-712.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Presley concerns the Common Fund Resolution in No. 90-711; Mack concerns the Unit System in No. 90-712. One opinion supplies the same controlling grounds for both appealed noncoverage judgments.

##### Whether the Common Fund Resolution and Unit System require §5 preclearance

**Holding:** Section 5 does not require preclearance of these reallocations of governing authority because they do not alter voting procedures, candidacy, the electorate, or the method of selecting the relevant offices and have no direct relation to voting. This ruling does not resolve whether an extreme transfer could effectively replace an elective office with an appointive one, nor does it exempt a change merely because it occurs after an election.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The Common Fund Resolution replaces individual district road-fund authority with collective commission control. The Unit System places road operations under an appointed engineer while retaining substantial authority, including appointment and budget powers, in the elected commission. Neither changes the relevant selection mechanism or electorate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The listed electoral categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. Effective replacement of an elective office with an appointive office remains unresolved. The Court decides no racial-purpose or discriminatory-effect question, §2 claim, Title VI claim, or constitutional claim; the unappealed Road Supervision ruling is undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appealed noncoverage judgments are affirmed. Neither challenged resolution requires §5 preclearance or is enjoined under §5 on these grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Allen v. State Board of Elections*, 393 U.S. 544 — broad coverage of changes affecting electoral choice remains; elective-to-appointive selection is distinguished from these retained elected offices.
- *Perkins v. Matthews*, 400 U.S. 379 — changes to the electorate and voting arrangements remain covered; coverage remains distinct from discrimination merits.
- *Dougherty County Board of Education v. White*, 439 U.S. 32 — candidacy restrictions directly related to electoral choice remain covered.
- *McCain v. Lybrand*, 465 U.S. 236, and *City of Lockhart v. United States*, 460 U.S. 125 — changes in electoral arrangements remain within §5; neither decides ordinary internal governing duties.
- *NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission*, 470 U.S. 166 — practical changes to election timing or procedure require proper review; postelection timing alone supplies no exemption here.

##### Whether the Attorney General's interpretation permits coverage without the statutory connection to voting

**Holding:** The Attorney General's interpretation of §5 is entitled to substantial consideration but cannot extend preclearance to governing changes that lack the statutory connection to voting. The proposed significant-transfer/different-constituencies test cannot substitute for that connection in these appeals.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The rejected test concerns significant transfers of authority between officials responsible to substantially different constituencies. That formulation can reach governing responsibilities without identifying any change in voting and therefore cannot substitute for the required connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The distinction between §5 coverage and review of discriminatory effect remains intact. Administrative expertise retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Board of Commissioners of Sheffield*, 435 U.S. 110 — administrative interpretation retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837 — preserved; deference does not authorize exceeding the statute's scope.
- *Beer v. United States*, 425 U.S. 130 — coverage and discriminatory-effect review remain separate inquiries.
- *South Carolina v. Katzenbach*, 383 U.S. 301 — §5's remedial function is preserved; it does not by itself establish coverage of every governing change.

### 1996-11-06 — Lopez v. Monterey County; No. 95-1201

Authority: [Record](../records/Lopez_v_Monterey_County_merits_1996-11-06.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–IV, joined by all eight other Justices, control all four holdings. The judgment coalition and full-opinion joins are unanimous; the additional grounds in the separate concurrence are not necessary to these holdings.

##### I. State compulsion does not exempt a covered County's administration

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered political subdivision administers a voting change when its officials give the change operative effect within the subdivision. Section 5 therefore requires Monterey County to obtain preclearance before administering a postbaseline judicial-election change even if noncovered California compels the change and leaves the County no discretion; the duty is the County's and does not require California to preclear its laws in the abstract.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that execution of an electoral change is distinct from enactment of it. Section 5 separately reaches a covered jurisdiction that enacts or seeks to administer a change. A condition of local policy discretion would erase the second route whenever a noncovered State required its covered subdivision to operate the new system. The statute contains no such condition. Monterey County conducts these public judicial elections and gives the countywide electorate operative effect; the voters experience the same change regardless of its legislative origin.

Presley supplies the distinction between a change in voting and a transfer of internal governing duties. Morse confirms the importance of the practice's actual electoral operation while preserving the designated substantive-preclearance process. Neither already decided this local-discretion question, and the Court adopts no general state-delegation test. The holding answers the legal question even assuming California law entirely displaced the County ordinances; it does not decide that disputed state-law characterization. Nor does it impose a statewide submission obligation, determine discriminatory purpose or retrogression, or confer general federal power to redesign California's judiciary.

**Precedent treatment:** Presley is applied to the direct electoral operation; its internal-governance exclusion remains. Morse's bounded functional and statutory-sequencing principles are applied without enlarging its party-convention holding.

##### II. Electorate changes and the limits of prior submission

**Holding and operative rule:** Replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate for municipal judges is a change affecting voting under §§5 and 14(c)(1). A later related submission does not preclear earlier changes that it failed to identify; the district court must determine the precise reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance while treating the earlier unidentified consolidations as unprecleared.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this consolidation changes who elects each judge, rather than merely the work performed after election. At the November 1, 1968 baseline, voters elected the judges of their separate districts. The six ordinances adopted between 1972 and 1983 ultimately placed the judges before one countywide electorate. Presley preserves coverage for that direct alteration of electoral choice. Allen and Perkins supply the same distinction through their treatment of electoral mechanisms and boundaries.

McCain and Clark require an actual, identifiable submission of the change for which preclearance is claimed; ambiguity is not blanket approval of earlier practices. The record establishes that California's 1983 submission did not identify or describe the earlier ordinances. Whether it reached the final 1983 ordinance must be determined from the submission's actual terms. The County cannot obtain approval of the whole consolidation sequence merely by pointing to that later submission. Coverage and lack of preclearance establish the nonuse obligation, not a finding that the system has discriminatory purpose or retrogressive effect.

**Precedent treatment:** Presley, Allen and Perkins retain coverage for direct changes in electoral choice. McCain v. Lybrand and Clark v. Roemer retain their requirements of specific submission and narrow treatment of claimed administrative approval.

##### III. Effective designation and prospective enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4(b)'s administrative coverage determination may not be collaterally retried in this §5 enforcement action; statutory bailout under §4(a) is a distinct route. Delay cannot authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change, although reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on lawfully available retrospective relief or the form of a lawful transition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the County's published designation remains operative in this case. Congress separately restricted review of the coverage determination and provided a judicial bailout process. The County has not obtained release from coverage through that process. Its objection cannot convert this enforcement suit into a collateral retrial of the administrative findings.

Nor does the age of the ordinances excuse their future use without submission. Section 5 conditions administration on preclearance; the passage of time supplies no substitute. Clark enforces that statutory sequence, and Hampton County confirms that elapsed time and electoral implementation do not themselves remove the review obligation. The practical cost of delay warrants a fitted transition, not continued use of the same uncleared governmental plan as an ordinary electoral system. The Court makes no factual bailout determination, awards no damages, and decides no backward-looking relief concerning a completed election, judicial tenure or completed judicial acts.

**Precedent treatment:** Clark and NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission are applied to prospective enforcement; their remedial attention to actual circumstances is preserved. No new general retrospective equitable rule is announced.

##### IV. Enforcement and a genuinely judicial temporary remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** A §5 enforcement court may determine coverage and submission status and enforce nonuse, but may neither grant substantive preclearance nor authorize an unprecleared County or State electoral plan by calling it court ordered. A necessary, temporary plan wholly formulated by the court may operate without separate §5 preclearance only when independent lawful remedial authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary, and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial approval does not change the governmental origin of an electoral plan. Connor distinguishes a wholly judicial remedy; McDaniel prevents a court order from insulating governmental policy choices from submission. Here the district court reinstated substantially the County's at-large system and authorized ordinary six-year terms. It did not formulate a wholly judicial temporary remedy. Clark's reservation for an extreme circumstance supplies no basis on this record: prolonged notice of the problem, abandonment of the County's D.C. preclearance action and ordinary terms do not establish a last-minute necessity. This decision recognizes no broader emergency exception.

Morse and Shaw preserve Congress's separate preclearance channels. The enforcement court cannot decide purpose or effect itself, even in light of the County's stipulation concerning its inability to prove nonretrogression. Administrative difficulty does not enlarge its authority.

The earlier four-division arrangement is a material option for consideration. It retained a single municipal court and received preclearance on March 6, 1995; its terms reportedly end in January 1997. Johnson v. Miller prevents racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate, standing alone, from supplying a constitutional defect. But neither that rule nor preclearance answers the separate objections concerning electoral and jurisdictional linkage, minimum population, divided municipalities and candidate residence, or establishes independent remedial authority. The Court selects no interim plan. Those matters, the exact 1983 submission and any actual officeholder circumstances require lawful determination below.

**Precedent treatment:** Connor and McDaniel preserve the genuinely judicial/governmental-plan distinction; Clark supplies the nonuse rule and bounded emergency reservation; Morse and Shaw preserve the designated channels; Johnson governs the design-only constitutional objection without approving this particular plan.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission:** Distinguishes internal allocations of governing authority from the direct electorate change here; the former exclusion remains intact.
- **Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia:** Applies its bounded concern with actual electoral operation and its separate preclearance channels; no general delegation test or new private-action rule is adopted.
- **Allen v. State Board of Elections:** Applies protection of electoral choice to the changed means of electing judges.
- **Perkins v. Matthews:** Applies coverage of changes in electoral boundaries and the electorate, without equating coverage with discrimination.
- **McCain v. Lybrand:** Requires identification of the submitted change and rejects blanket approval of unidentified earlier practices.
- **Clark v. Roemer:** Enforces nonuse pending preclearance and preserves, without applying, its narrow extreme-circumstance reservation; the present ordinary six-year elections do not qualify.
- **NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission:** Passage of time and implementation do not eliminate the submission requirement; actual circumstances remain relevant to lawful relief.
- **Connor v. Johnson:** Retains the distinction for a wholly judicial remedial plan.
- **McDaniel v. Sanchez:** A court's adoption of governmental policy does not transform it into an exempt judicial plan.
- **Shaw v. Reno, designated-channel holding:** Substantive preclearance remains with the Attorney General or D.D.C.; no enforcement-court substitute is authorized.
- **Johnson v. Miller:** Racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate alone does not establish constitutional injury or trigger strict scrutiny; separate statutory and constitutional objections remain open.

#### Law After Decision

Section 5 now expressly reaches a covered County's nondiscretionary administration of state-mandated voting changes. The election must not proceed under the unprecleared governmental system merely because the State requires it, delay has occurred or a federal court has ordered it used. Existing direct-voting coverage, specific-submission, designated-channel and genuinely judicial-remedy rules remain. The new construction neither places all California law under §5 nor changes the substantive purpose/effect burdens. The temporary divisions remain an option for lawful consideration, with their independent defects and authority unresolved.
