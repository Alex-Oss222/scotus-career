### 1996-11-18 — Ohio v. Robinette; No. 95-891

Authority: [Record](../records/Ohio_v_Robinette_merits_1996-11-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Review of the interwoven federal and state grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** When a state judgment fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven with federal law and does not clearly state an independently sufficient state ground, the Court may review its federal basis. Ohio's intertwined constitutional explanation supplies no clear independent ground barring review here; Ohio remains free to supply greater protection on genuinely independent state-law grounds.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by six direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees to exercise review without joining this explanation; Ginsburg and Stevens accept review in their separate writings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's reference to its own Constitution does not end the jurisdictional inquiry. Michigan v. Long supplies the rule for opinions that rest on federal law or interweave federal and state law without a clear independent basis. Arizona v. Evans retains that rule. The Ohio opinion invokes both provisions and develops the suppression analysis with federal authorities; it does not announce that an independent Ohio ground alone sustains the judgment. Federal review therefore lies. Deciding the federal question neither erases the Ohio provision nor prevents the Ohio courts from adopting a clearly independent state rule in further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Long and Evans are applied without changing the independent-state-ground rule or creating an exception to the ordinary final-judgment requirement.

##### II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer's uncommunicated investigative motive or private decision to issue only a warning does not invalidate an objectively lawful traffic stop or an exit order while lawful traffic business is being completed. Once that justification ends, further detention requires independent legal grounds; the court must examine the objective circumstances to determine whether the person could terminate the encounter, and return of a license is neither an automatic end of seizure nor an automatic license to continue it.

**Authority:** Part II.A's rule concerning motive and the exit order has six direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Part II.B's distinct continued-detention rule has those six plus Stone-Zsela, seven. Stone-Zsela supplies no additional reason for Part II.A.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an objectively justified beginning and a lawful continuation are separate questions. Whren rejects subjective purpose as an extra condition for an ordinary probable-cause traffic stop while preserving limits on manner, scope and duration. Mimms permits an exit order during a lawful traffic stop without a separate justification for that direction. The Ohio court could not deem the direction unlawful merely because the deputy privately intended a warning or hoped to ask unrelated questions.

That correction does not establish that detention lawfully continued through the later consent request. Bostick requires an objective assessment of whether the person could decline the request or terminate the encounter. The returned license and completed warning bear on that inquiry, but so do the immediacy and actual wording of further questioning, tone, positioning and continuity of official authority. Neither the officer's secret willingness to permit refusal nor Robinette's subjective impression alone decides the seizure question. Supported trial findings matter; the Court does not replace the record with a categorical conclusion in either direction. Ornelas's distinction between historical fact and ultimate constitutional sufficiency remains applicable within its decided scope; this decision announces no universal appellate-review rule for every mixed or consent question. The encounter's classification remains for proper application below.

**Precedent treatment:** Whren's objective initial-stop rule and independent duration limits remain intact; Mimms is confined to lawful traffic business. Bostick's actual-circumstances inquiry is applied. Ornelas is not extended into a general rule for all consent issues.

##### III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not invariably require an officer to give a free-to-go warning before seeking consent to search. Consent must be voluntary under all the circumstances, and when an unlawful detention preceded or accompanied it, voluntariness alone does not establish that the consent was sufficiently independent of that illegality to permit use of the evidence.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by seven direct joins. Ginsburg and Stevens separately reject an indispensable federal script; neither additional agreement is needed to establish the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a useful warning is not automatically an indispensable constitutional condition. Schneckloth requires voluntary consent under the total circumstances and treats knowledge of the right to refuse as relevant without making advice of that right universally mandatory. A fixed release formula would replace that inquiry rather than perform it. The absence of a clear release signal nevertheless remains important evidence; rejecting a script does not make every post-warning request consensual.

The supported trial findings concerning the manner, wording and positions of the parties, Robinette's education and the conclusion of traffic business must be considered with the returned license and actual continuation words. His testimony includes both an initial belief that he could leave and a later feeling that he could not refuse. Neither fragment decides voluntariness by itself. The Court holds neither that this consent was valid nor that it was invalid.

Royer and Brown keep consent's voluntariness separate from its causal independence of unlawful detention. If the court finds an unsupported continued seizure, it must address that additional question; voluntary consent does not automatically erase the illegality, and illegality does not establish that every later consent is incurably tainted. Suppression remains available when the applicable inquiry fails. Nothing in federal law prevents Ohio from requiring clearer warnings under independent state law or police policy.

**Precedent treatment:** Schneckloth is applied without a new warning element. Royer and Brown preserve the separate inquiry into the effect of prior illegality; no automatic cure or automatic suppression rule is created.

##### IV. The remedy corrects the federal rules without deciding admissibility

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the warning rule and the motive-based exit-order premise requires further proceedings on the actual objective detention, voluntary consent and any necessary attenuation question. It does not itself establish the search's validity, require admission of the evidence or reinstate the conviction.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer directly join Part IV; Stone-Zsela joins its stated inquiries and limits while selecting vacatur instead of reversal. Ginsburg separately supports reversal with the same unresolved-application boundary. Seven Justices support the Court's formal reversal and remand; the holdings do not merge Stone-Zsela's different remedy into that coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's two grounds must be addressed separately. The federal script requirement fails, and the detention ruling cannot rest on undisclosed motives during lawful traffic business. Yet those errors do not decide whether the later encounter was consensual, lawfully justified or unlawfully extended, or whether consent was both voluntary and independent of any illegality. The state courts must apply the rules to the supported findings and complete encounter. They may consider genuinely independent state law. A new hearing is not commanded merely by this remand, and the Court enters no final evidence-admission or conviction order.

**Precedent treatment:** The rule/application distinction retained in Evans and the separate consent and causation inquiries in Schneckloth, Royer and Brown govern the scope of remand without enlargement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Michigan v. Long:** Applies the presumption permitting review of interwoven federal grounds absent a plain independent-state-law basis.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Retains that jurisdictional rule and the distinction between correcting a federal rule and deciding its unresolved factual application; its court-record-error exclusion rule is not extended here.
- **Whren v. United States:** Applies objective legality to the initial traffic business and preserves independent limits on manner, scope and duration.
- **Pennsylvania v. Mimms:** Applies the permission to order a driver out during a lawful stop; no indefinite post-stop detention authority follows.
- **Florida v. Bostick:** Requires assessment of the actual objective capacity to decline requests or terminate the encounter, without a categorical seizure or release rule.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Preserves its historical-fact/ultimate-sufficiency distinction within its actual scope; no general review rule for all consent questions is added.
- **Schneckloth v. Bustamonte:** Applies total-circumstances voluntariness and treats knowledge as relevant rather than an indispensable warning requirement.
- **Florida v. Royer:** Preserves the need to consider whether consent follows or remains a product of unlawful detention.
- **Brown v. Illinois:** Preserves attenuation as distinct from voluntariness; neither a voluntary response nor the occurrence of illegality alone answers the remedial question.

#### Law After Decision

Federal law imposes no universal release script, but the objective seizure, voluntary-consent and necessary attenuation inquiries remain distinct and enforceable. The decision supplies no categorical rule that a returned license ends a seizure, that a traffic offense authorizes later unrelated detention, or that a polite request ensures admissibility. The actual search result remains unresolved under the corrected federal standards, and genuinely independent Ohio protection remains available.


### 1997-02-18 - General Motors Corp. v. Tracy; No. 95-1232.

Authority: [Record](../records/General_Motors_v_Tracy_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The assessed purchaser may bring this challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A purchaser directly liable for a tax has standing to challenge an exemption that allegedly burdens its interstate purchases. The adverse assessment supplies the purchaser's own economic injury, caused by the challenged tax rule and redressable through lawful relief from that assessment, whether or not payment has been established.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** General Motors contests a charge imposed on its own purchases. Its injury does not become another seller's claim merely because the tax also affects competing suppliers. Bacchus recognizes a taxed purchaser's ability to challenge a discriminatory exemption; Fulton likewise examines the taxpayer's own liability although the distinction concerns another market participant. Relief from an unlawful assessment would remove the asserted burden. Ohio's seller-only objection therefore fails. Standing establishes access to review, not the truth of the claimed comparison between the sellers. The Court does not find an assessment amount or prior payment and orders no refund on the strength of standing alone.

**Precedent treatment:** Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias supplies purchaser standing; Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner confirms that the claimant may contest its own tax burden without being the disfavored seller. Their merits and remedial boundaries remain.

##### II. This record does not establish the asserted discrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio's exemption for sales by regulated local distribution companies does not violate the Commerce Clause on this record, because their compulsory rate, reliability and service obligations materially distinguish their regulated supply business from independent marketers' commodity sales. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence of those offerings where the record does not establish a distinct industrial-sales category separated from the obligations relied upon; the holding confers no general immunity on utilities or all of their competitive transactions.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Stevens do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must examine the practical competitive relationship, rather than accept a utility label or the fact that local marketers also pay the tax. West Lynn requires examination of an integrated fiscal preference; Carbone requires attention to the actual service market. Here the favored sellers must maintain regulated supply and meet reliability and human-needs obligations rather than select only profitable customers. Those duties materially affect their offering. General Motors bought virtually all its gas from interstate marketers and often obtained local delivery, but the record gives little detail about the industrial market and does not establish the asserted unregulated negotiated LDC sales during the assessment period. The common gas commodity does not by itself remove the established service differences.

The objection that duties in a captive market cannot justify preference in a separate competitive market would require considering such a supported separation. This record does not establish it. The Court neither finds that every industrial sale funds a particular public duty nor presumes that any alternative would endanger service. The 1996 enactment cannot prove the character of 1986–1990 purchases. Oregon Waste and Fulton continue to require demanding justification and actual tax equivalence once discrimination is established; no compensatory defense is adopted here. A materially different proved sales category remains for its own case. No independent incidental-burden balancing claim or congressional-authorization theory is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** West Lynn's integrated-operation rule and Carbone's practical service-market inquiry apply without categorical utility immunity. Oregon Waste's discrimination-and-justification rule and Fulton's cumulative compensatory conditions remain unchanged; the Court does not reach justification after rejecting the asserted comparison.

##### III. The economic classification has a rational basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio may distinguish regulated local distribution companies from independent natural-gas marketers under ordinary economic equal-protection review because the companies' actual rate and service duties rationally relate to the tax classification. Competitive overlap does not eliminate that legitimate distinction, and this rational-basis holding does not reduce the independent Commerce Clause requirements.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Stevens reserve this question on their proposed Commerce Clause dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits an ordinary economic classification when a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis supports it, without requiring perfect fit or formal legislative findings. Ohio's rate, reliability and service obligations supply such a basis here. An industrial buyer's opportunity to purchase from either class does not make it irrational to distinguish the regulated service business from independent marketers. The Court does not equate rationality with proof that utility taxes exactly offset the challenged charge. Nor does the Court attribute this analysis to the Ohio court, which treated equal protection as submerged in its Commerce Clause discussion. The ordinary economic challenge fails on this separate ground; independent constitutional prohibitions retain their force.

**Precedent treatment:** FCC v. Beach Communications supplies the ordinary economic-classification rule and its limits; it does not displace Commerce Clause scrutiny.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias: applied to the assessed purchaser's own standing; local-product tax preferences remain subject to its substantive rule.
- West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy: its examination of integrated fiscal operation remains controlling; the present record differs in the established obligations of the compared service businesses.
- C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown: its practical service-market comparison is applied; neither a nominal utility classification nor equal taxation of local outsiders is sufficient by itself.
- Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality: the discrimination trigger and demanding justification survive unchanged; no new public-service exception is created.
- Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner: preserves all three cumulative compensatory-tax requirements and lawful fact-dependent relief; no exact compensation or payment is found here.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc.: supplies ordinary economic rational-basis review, separately from the Commerce Clause.

#### Law After Decision

The regulated service differences defeat the particular Commerce Clause comparison established here; no universal utility exemption or new evidentiary presumption follows. The economic classification independently survives equal protection. A taxpayer may challenge its own assessment even when the alleged competitive discrimination affects suppliers. The existing discrimination, compensation and conditional tax-remedy rules remain in force, and a materially different proved industrial-sales category is unresolved.


### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's creation of roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise concern affirmative conduct. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver. Neither lower-court disagreement nor absence of identical facts independently establishes immunity. Collins, decided after Uhlrig's death, and later Elder and Pinder govern present analysis but cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court leaves constitutional culpability, the affirmative-danger merits and the Tenth Circuit's five-part test undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald supplies objective qualified immunity and remains intact.
- Anderson v. Creighton supplies particularized apparent unlawfulness without requiring an identical prior case.
- Elder v. Holloway is applied to require examination of all relevant conduct-time authority while preserving the favorable factual account and ordinary claim/evidence limits; it supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Pinder v. Johnson is applied to distinguish an affirmative deprivation from omitted protection and to keep notice separate from merits. Its March 1989 result does not resolve this later conduct automatically, and its substantive reservation remains open.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights retains its no-general-workplace-duty rule and its separate, bounded arbitrariness holding. It does not reject every affirmative-danger claim or supply notice before its February 26, 1992 decision.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County and Daniels v. Williams preserve the distinctions between a general protective duty, an affirmative deprivation and negligence; the Court does not convert them into a complete constitutional answer here.
- Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are considered as conduct-time circuit authority at their actual scopes, without converting their formulations into Supreme Court holdings.

#### Law After Decision

The decision establishes only the particularized immunity result for the five administrators on this reviewed claim. It supplies no general affirmative-danger test, categorical public-employment bar, mandatory deceit or assurance requirement, no-awareness finding or constitutional approval of budget choices. The distinction between a general workplace-safety duty and an attributable affirmative deprivation remains. A different official act, record or conduct-time legal landscape requires its own analysis.


### 1997-02-19 - Maryland v. Wilson; No. 95-1268; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Record](../records/Maryland_v_Wilson_merits_1997-02-19.md).

#### Holdings

#### Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** During a lawful traffic stop, an officer may direct a passenger to leave the vehicle without additional passenger-specific suspicion as a limited safety measure incident to the stop. This authority concerns the exit direction itself; it does not establish categorical forcible detention for the entire stop, permission to frisk or search, or power to prolong detention after the lawful traffic business ends.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, counting its author within that six-Justice coalition; six direct rationale votes control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court extends Mimms's comparison of officer safety and incremental intrusion to the passenger's immediate relocation during an independently lawful traffic stop. A passenger may conceal a weapon and threaten an officer even though the passenger committed no traffic offense. The safety risk does not disappear when the potentially dangerous occupant sits beside the driver.

The passenger has a stronger initial liberty argument because the driver's offense supplies the reason for the stop. But the direction changes the passenger's location during an interruption already lawfully occasioned by the vehicle's stop. At that limited scope, the safety interest outweighs the additional intrusion. Terry and Brignoni-Ponce require attention to the nature of the intrusion and its justification; their particularized-ground rules for different intrusions remain. The Court does not resolve whether every passenger was already seized at the instant the vehicle stopped or whether a passenger may be forcibly held for its entire duration.

Whren permits an objectively justified ordinary initial stop while preserving execution limits. Robinette likewise separates lawful traffic measures from continued detention after their justification ends. Neither supplies automatic search or frisk authority. References in Michigan v. Long to occupants leaving cars and in the Rakas concurrence do not constitute an earlier controlling passenger-exit holding; the Court decides that question here. Because Maryland presented only categorical authority, the Court neither relies on the observed movements as a distinct justification nor eliminates preservation limits. The contrary categorical federal premise is reversed, leaving ordinary further proceedings rather than a final evidence-admission order.

**Precedent treatment:** Mimms's safety/incremental-intrusion rule extends to this passenger-exit direction. Terry, Brignoni-Ponce, Whren and Robinette retain their distinct predicates and limits; no frisk, search or prolonged-detention rule is displaced.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Pennsylvania v. Mimms supplies the safety and incremental-intrusion comparison and is extended from drivers to the limited passenger-exit direction.
- Terry v. Ohio and United States v. Brignoni-Ponce retain their requirements for the distinct seizures and protective intrusions they govern; this decision does not make every additional police measure categorical.
- Whren v. United States preserves objective authority for the ordinary initial traffic stop and independent constraints on manner, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette preserves the distinction between measures during lawful traffic business and unsupported continuation after it ends, together with genuinely independent state law.
- Michigan v. Long and the Rakas concurrence are not treated as having already decided the passenger-exit question. Ornelas retains its independent ultimate-suspicion/probable-cause review and protection of historical facts without becoming a universal review rule.

#### Law After Decision

Federal law permits the limited passenger-exit command during a lawful traffic stop without an additional passenger-specific reason. The decision adds that authority while leaving other seizure, duration, search, frisk and independent-state-law questions at their existing scopes. It does not authorize every subsequent command merely because the vehicle was lawfully stopped.


### 1997-03-10 — Nabozny v. Podlesny; Seventh Circuit No. 95-3634, 92 F.3d 446; no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Nabozny_v_Podlesny_merits_1997-03-10.md).

#### Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. That direct common coalition controls each holding below.

###### Intentionally unequal school protection because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials who intentionally withhold ordinarily available protection because a student is male must establish that the sex distinction substantially serves an important governmental objective. The supported evidence permits a finding of purposeful unequal administration without a sufficient objective, so the sex claim independently survives summary judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Yick Wo forbids deliberately unequal administration of an existing protection. Feeney requires purposeful selection because of the adverse effect, and Arlington Heights permits that purpose to be shown through statements, departures and context. Hogan and Virginia supply the demanding important-objective and substantial-relationship inquiry for sex discrimination. Officials need not give identical discipline in every case, and negligence, unequal outcomes or violation of a state policy alone does not establish this claim. Here the alleged responses to male-on-male abuse, admitted ordinary enforcement and differing treatment of male-on-female abuse permit the requisite finding. Missing names and dates in some comparator examples affect proof but do not erase the direct evidence. Defendants may establish their contrary account at trial; credibility cannot be resolved against Nabozny now.

###### Intentionally unequal protection because of sexual orientation

**Holding and operative rule:** Intentional denial of ordinarily available school protection because of sexual orientation violates equal protection when the distinction has no rational relation to a legitimate objective. The orientation claim separately survives on the supported account without assignment of a heightened scrutiny tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer preserves ordinary rational-basis review, including conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit, while enforcing Moreno’s and Cleburne’s prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice. Walmer applies that inquiry in its particular military setting; its service-related rationale supplies no reason to deny this student ordinary protection against battery. Wright likewise permits reasonable operational predictions but supplies no school justification and expressly reserves discriminatory administration; its marriage threshold does not govern this equality claim. Bowers’s specified-conduct holding does not authorize private violence or discriminatory nonprotection. The alleged statements and enforcement departures support purposeful selection for disadvantage because Nabozny was openly gay, and no legitimate disciplinary objective explains that treatment on the favorable account. Knowledge, deliberate indifference without discriminatory purpose and inconsistent enforcement alone do not suffice. Virginia establishes no sexual-orientation tier. Neither Curtis nor Brown school excuses intentional discrimination merely because it occurs in an educational setting.

###### Conduct-time equality law defeats individual immunity on these facts

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual officials receive no qualified immunity when the supported facts show purposeful, irrational withholding of an ordinary protective policy whose unlawfulness was apparent from the relevant law when they acted; a factually identical harassment case is unnecessary. The clearly established sex-equality ground independently defeats immunity, and the narrow irrational-discrimination ground also supports denial on the orientation claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson require objectively apparent unlawfulness at the particularized level of the conduct, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant conduct-time authority. Before the conduct began in 1988, Reed, Weinberger and Hogan prohibited arbitrary sex distinctions; Yick Wo, Feeney and Arlington Heights supplied the purposeful-administration rule; Moreno and Cleburne rejected bare harm and irrational prejudice. Bohen provided relevant circuit notice concerning intentional sex discrimination, although its setting was employment. This is alleged deliberate discrimination in an existing protection, not notice of a novel affirmative-danger duty. Pinder’s contrary immunity result concerned that distinct due-process uncertainty as of its conduct date. DeShaney’s later equality reservation can confirm the distinction only for conduct after its date and is unnecessary to pre-1989 notice. Romer, Virginia and later decisions furnish no retroactive notice. The school’s policy is evidence of practice and knowledge, not an independent federal cause or automatic immunity defeat. Each individual’s actual participation, purpose and causation remain disputed; denial of legal immunity on assumed facts does not decide liability.

###### The preserved due-process theories fail on their own actor-specific record

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that sufficiently culpable official creation or increase of danger, or an attributable policy affirmatively encouraging private injury, can establish a constitutional deprivation, the preserved failure-to-punish and harmful-policy theories do not establish that contribution here. Their dismissal therefore stands without a categorical rule against all affirmative-danger claims or a due-process immunity holding.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a general governmental duty to protect from private violence. Pinder separates omission of public protection from affirmative deprivation of available protection; Uhlrig leaves the merits of the latter theory open. Lack of custody alone cannot defeat that distinct claim, and no assurance/reliance chain is made indispensable.

The Court examines the favorable account beyond mere silence. Promises, stereotyped responses, counseling for leaving after the mock assault and return to the ordinary schedule support the equality claim but do not establish a separate act directing a new assault or depriving available protection. Davis and Blauert referred Nabozny for a protective schedule change; the later special-education placement alongside assailants does not identify a remaining defendant’s deliberate decision or establish its increased-danger contribution to a particular later injury. Social Services’ return order is not attributed to these defendants or a District policy. Crawford’s discouragement of charges followed the completed beating, cannot have caused it, and establishes no later injury or attributable District policy. He is not a remaining defendant. Repeated nonenforcement does not become affirmative injury merely by being called a policy. The Court neither adjudicates a new Crawford claim nor rules out liability for proved obstruction, culpable placement or other materially different affirmative conduct.

The Court decides no sexual-orientation scrutiny tier, general school-safety entitlement, complete affirmative-danger test, municipal policy, actual liability or measure of damages.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Yick Wo v. Hopkins: applied to purposeful unequal administration; absence of a general protective duty does not remove equality constraints.
- Personnel Administrator v. Feeney: applied for discriminatory purpose rather than adverse effect alone.
- Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp.: applied to evidence of purpose through statements, departures and context.
- Reed v. Reed: supplies conduct-time notice against arbitrary sex distinctions.
- Weinberger v. Wiesenfeld: supplies conduct-time notice against sex stereotypes in allocating governmental benefits.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan: supplies the sex-classification justification rule and conduct-time notice.
- United States v. Virginia: applies demanding intermediate review to sex discrimination without assigning an orientation tier or furnishing retroactive notice.
- Romer v. Evans: applies the ordinary-rationality floor against unexplained class disadvantage; heightened scrutiny remains unresolved.
- Department of Agriculture v. Moreno: applied against bare harm as a governmental objective, including in conduct-time notice.
- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center: applied against irrational prejudice, including in conduct-time notice.
- Walmer v. Department of Defense: its military rationale is distinguished; its ordinary-review limits remain intact.
- Bowers v. Hardwick: its specified-conduct holding is retained and supplies no authority for discriminatory denial of protection.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: the received qualified-immunity defense remains governing.
- Anderson v. Creighton: particularized apparent unlawfulness is applied without requiring a factual twin.
- Elder v. Holloway: all relevant contemporaneous authority must be considered; later cases do not supply notice retroactively.
- Pinder v. Johnson: its omission/affirmative-deprivation distinction is applied and its conduct-time immunity result distinguished; its reserved danger merits remain reserved.
- Uhlrig v. Harder: personal attribution and conduct-time limits are preserved; its immunity decision supplies no complete danger test.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services: its no-general-duty rule is applied without erasing equal protection or all affirmative-deprivation claims.
- Johnson v. Jones: favorable-facts discipline is preserved; the interlocutory factual-sufficiency restriction does not bar this final-judgment review.
- Behrens v. Pelletier: legal immunity and factual disputes remain distinct; no collateral-appeal restriction is imported here.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services: municipal policy, custom or final-policymaker attribution and causation remain necessary.
- Owen v. City of Independence: the District receives no qualified immunity.
- Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth: its optional-program holding does not decide intentional discriminatory protection.
- Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.: its actual presentation and Title IX holdings create no general school immunity.
- Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.: limited discretion concerning included abandoned arguments does not compel revival of the disavowed special-relationship argument or supply facts.
- Wright v. MetroHealth Medical Center: its actual operational rationality holding preserves the bare-harm limit; its marriage threshold and flight-team facts do not decide intentional school discrimination or furnish conduct-time notice.

#### Law After Decision

Schools remain free to make legitimate disciplinary judgments, but the protection they provide may not be intentionally withheld on the prohibited sex or irrational orientation grounds established here. The two equality claims and the officials’ conduct-time notice are distinct sufficient paths; orientation scrutiny is unchanged. The decision establishes no constitutional insurance against peer violence, no categorical school immunity and no general affirmative-danger framework. Individual liability and municipal attribution retain their separate proof requirements.


### 1997-03-19 — Bennett v. Spear; No. 95-813.

Authority: [Record](../records/Bennett_v_Spear_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is directly joined by all nine Justices. That common coalition controls every proposition below.

###### Petitioners adequately plead their own constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Irrigators alleging concrete loss of their project water from a Biological Opinion that the operating agency has announced it will follow adequately plead injury, fair traceability and likely redress from removal of the challenged constraint. They must support standing with the evidence required at each later litigation stage; statutory permission alone never replaces Article III injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires a concrete personal interest and proof appropriate to the stage, but its three-Justice absent-agency discussion supplies no categorical rule defeating this claim. The alleged loss is the petitioners’ own, and the Bureau’s announced compliance links the Service’s completed action to that threatened injury. The Bureau’s operational authority does not make its response unpredictable as a matter of law or require certainty that every eventual choice will change. Setting aside the challenged constraint can afford concrete relief. Humane Society’s missing connection between a later permit amendment and an already completed transfer is absent here. Tracy recognizes cognizable economic injury without making every economic claimant eligible to sue. Arizonans likewise requires an actual continuing controversy; no later fact eliminating this alleged injury is established. Merits uncertainty about the biological conclusion is not a pleading-stage standing defect.

###### The citizen-suit language is broad within its actual channels

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1540(g)’s authorization to any person removes an additional species-preservation zone-of-interests restriction for claims that its particular channels authorize. Section (g)(1)(C) permits the alleged failure to perform a nondiscretionary §1533 duty, including the pleaded failure to consider economic effects of an asserted de facto habitat designation; §(g)(1)(A) does not authorize general supervision of the Secretary’s alleged §1536 maladministration.

**Controlling explanation:** The breadth of the plaintiff language cannot be restricted by an unwritten environmental preference, but it does not erase the enacted division among person-in-violation actions, emergency enforcement and Secretary-duty suits. Treating every alleged administrative error as a violation under (A) would disregard (C)’s specific limit to nondiscretionary §1533 acts. Petitioners may pursue the asserted §1533(b)(2) consideration duty through (C) at this threshold stage; the Court does not find that a designation occurred or triggered a duty. The §1536 consultation claims require a different review route.

When designation or revision occurs, §1533(b)(2) requires consideration of economic and other relevant impacts. Exclusion of an area is discretionary when exclusion benefits outweigh inclusion benefits, and remains forbidden if the best scientific and commercial data show that failure to designate will result in extinction. Reviewability commands neither a particular exclusion nor wholesale economic balancing during §1536 consultation. The (B) emergency channel is not invoked. Article III remains independently necessary.

**Notice and enforcement limits:** The (A) route generally requires 60 days’ written notice to the Secretary and alleged violator; a commenced civil-penalty action or a commenced, diligently prosecuted United States criminal action addressing the violation bars it. The (B) route separately requires 60 days’ notice explaining the claimed emergency and is barred by the Secretary’s commenced diligent emergency-determination action. The (C) route requires 60 days’ notice to the Secretary, except that suit concerning an emergency posing significant risk to the well-being of a fish, wildlife or plant species may follow notification immediately. No actual compliance, applicable emergency or enforcement bar is found or waived here.

###### The consultation claims have an APA route and a provision-specific interest

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed §1536 consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels provide no other adequate judicial remedy for these administrative claims. Petitioners’ alleged injury from restrictions unsupported by the best scientific and commercial data is at least arguably within the interests protected by §1536(a)(2)’s data requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** Review must concern the provision sued upon, not an assumption that every ESA provision protects only species or that every economically affected person may enforce every conservation duty. Requiring the best available scientific and commercial data serves sound decisionmaking and protects affected interests against restrictions unsupported by that information. The Court does not replace agency scientific judgment with independent judicial biology or find the data claim correct. Section 1540(g)(5) preserves other available rights but does not itself establish every APA prerequisite. Dalton’s indivisible base-closure package, deadlines and confined review scheme justified preclusion there; no comparable structure is shown here. Its noncontrolling recommendation-finality theory is not a general bar to agency review.

**Retained APA limits:** Section 702 addresses relief other than money damages and preserves other legal and equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. Any mandatory or injunctive decree must identify by name or title the responsible federal officers and their successors. Section 704 requires final action and no other adequate court remedy. Preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, otherwise final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal does not postpone finality unless an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. This case grants no decree and establishes no unproved exhaustion or review prerequisite.

###### The Biological Opinion is completed action with legal consequences

**Holding and operative rule:** This Biological Opinion is final agency action because it completes the Service’s consultation determination and its incidental-take statement carries legal consequences: compliance with its terms changes prohibited-taking liability under §1536(o)(2). The Bureau’s retained operational discretion does not convert that completed and consequential action into tentative advice.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin requires examination of a completed subordinate agency determination even though a later governmental step remains. The Service has completed its assigned consultation here, and the statement conditions protection from taking liability on compliance with its requirements. Practical influence and announced Bureau compliance support causation, but legal consequences supply the distinct finality ground. The Court does not hold every advisory report, prediction or preliminary analysis final, nor does it decide Franklin’s reserved presidential-status question.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Section 1536(a)(2) requires avoidance of likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat using the best scientific and commercial data available, unless the Committee has granted an exemption under (h); none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must conclude that the action will not violate (a)(2), or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed not to violate it, and conclude that incidental taking will not violate that subsection. For a marine mammal, taking also requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The resulting statement specifies the taking’s impact, reasonable and prudent minimization measures, any required marine-mammal measures, and implementing terms including reporting. Section (o)(2) protects only taking that complies with those terms; it is not unconditional immunity from every ESA duty. The separate exemption-based protection in (o)(1) is not the source of this statement’s effect. No marine-mammal finding is needed for these fish.

The Court reserves the statutory and scientific merits, whether a designation or nondiscretionary duty was actually triggered, the applicable standard of review’s merits application, any properly remaining threshold issue and final relief. The NEPA dismissal remains outside the reviewed questions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: applies personal injury, causation, redressability and litigation-stage proof; its citizen-suit limit remains, and the noncontrolling absent-agency application is not expanded.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts: applies its controlling reviewability of completed subordinate agency action despite a later governmental step; no presidential-status question is decided.
- Dalton v. Specter: its statutory-scheme preclusion holding is distinguished; the four-Justice recommendation-finality theory supplies no binding rule.
- Humane Society of the United States v. Babbitt: its missing causal and remedial link is distinguished by the pleaded Bureau response and continuing water constraint here.
- General Motors Corp. v. Tracy: preserves cognizable personal economic injury without dispensing with the remaining standing requirements.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona: requires an actual continuing controversy and a legally available claim and remedy; no analogous loss of this alleged injury is established.

#### Law After Decision

Economic interests do not disqualify an otherwise injured plaintiff from authorized ESA citizen suits. Each claim must still fit its actual statutory channel; the §1536 consultation claims use the APA and its provision-specific interests and finality requirements. The completed Biological Opinion’s conditional taking consequences establish finality here. These reviewability holdings establish no biological error or entitlement to particular water operations, and leave Article III proof, statutory notice and all retained review limits intact.


### 1997-03-31 — United States v. Lanier; No. 95-1717.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Lanier_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted

**Rule:** Section 242, alone or as authoritatively construed, must make the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; a novel or unforeseeable criminal application is forbidden. Willful bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws supplies the statute's willfulness and definite-right limitations. Vagueness, lenity and the prohibition on unexpected retrospective judicial enlargement protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. The courts interpret Congress's criminal enactment rather than create common-law crimes. The reprehensibility or state criminality of the alleged conduct does not substitute for identification of a federal right and adequate notice of its application. Current decisions govern the reviewing court's legal method at their actual scopes; a decision issued after the conduct cannot itself supply earlier notice.

##### Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions

**Rule:** Fair warning under §242 may be supplied by the relevant pre-existing legal landscape, including lower-court authority; it does not categorically require a Supreme Court decision on the particular application. Neither one lower-court decision nor a fixed numerical consensus is automatically sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws did not prescribe the Sixth Circuit's Supreme-Court-only restriction. Elder supplies the examination of all relevant authority in assessing clear constitutional law; actual conflicts, source authority and their relation to the charged acts matter. Excluding a whole relevant class of decisions can wrongly convert established law into unknowable law. Conversely, source breadth does not eliminate the demand for sufficient clarity in the particular context.

##### Obvious applications need no factual twin

**Rule:** Existing constitutional law can give fair warning of an obviously covered application without an earlier case involving fundamentally similar facts. A broad invocation of liberty or bodily security is insufficient where the particular application remains unsettled, including where an earlier decision expressly leaves it open.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson supplies a particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness, not a demand for repetition of every factual detail. Officials cannot insist upon a decision matching their office, victim and precise method when established law applies with obvious clarity. At the same time, Elder and the actual Pinder and Uhlrig decisions preserve attention to unsettled applications and personal responsibility. Those distinctions prevent an abstract right from automatically resolving each charged assault, official nexus or notice question. The Court rejects the categorical similarity prerequisite while leaving those count-specific determinations below.

##### The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact

**Rule:** Section 242 fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: constitutional unlawfulness must be apparent in light of pre-existing law. That common protection supplies neither automatic criminal liability nor a substitute for constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the Government's criminal burden of proof and every other statutory element.

**Controlling explanation:** Due process does not demand a categorically clearer legal rule in the criminal case than the clarity protection furnished in the civil inquiry. But a conclusion about civil damages immunity cannot itself convict anyone. Screws's willfulness requirement performs separate work, and judicial title alone does not establish that each act occurred under color of state law. The Sixth Circuit must keep legal notice and proof of each criminal predicate distinct, rather than use either to erase the other.

##### Review the counts without the rejected legal shortcuts

**Rule:** The appellate court must reconsider the case without a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite and without categorical exclusion of due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody from §242. The judgment resolves no count's ultimate constitutional basis, fair warning, official nexus, proof or sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws and United States v. Price foreclose excluding rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process wholesale. DeShaney's limits on affirmative protection against private violence do not immunize an official's own assault merely because the victim was outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendment. Actual Collins, Pinder, Uhlrig and Nabozny preserve the distinction among personal official wrongdoing, omitted protection and supervisory attribution. These are legal directions for review, not new findings of assault, automatic restoration of the verdicts, a universal constitutional-assault formula or an order for a new evidentiary hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Screws v. United States:** Applied definite-right and willfulness protections; rejected a supposed Supreme-Court-only source restriction and categorical exclusion of due-process rights.
- **United States v. Price:** Preserved §242's reach to protected due-process rights without deciding the elements of each charged count.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied particularized apparent unlawfulness; no factual-twin requirement follows.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Applied consideration of relevant existing authority while preserving temporal limits.
- **Pinder v. Johnson and Uhlrig v. Harder:** Preserved their particularized notice, unsettled-application and personal-attribution boundaries; later holdings cannot create earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Kept constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation and supervisory theories distinct; their later dates cannot establish notice for earlier conduct.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserved the difference between a constitutional deprivation and every injurious official act; no broad federal tort rule is created.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Distinguished limits on protection from private violence from the official's own asserted assault.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied the specific-provision rule when a particular Amendment governs, without treating the Fourth and Eighth Amendments as exhaustive of every abusive official act.

#### Law After Decision

Section 242 has no Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite. Pre-existing law must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness apparent, using the same legal-clarity protection as clearly-established-law analysis while preserving every distinct criminal element and burden. The Court creates no new substantive right or count-specific liability holding.


### 1997-04-15 — Chandler v. Miller, No. 96-126

Authority: [Record](../records/Chandler_v_Miller_merits_1997-04-15.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification below.

**1. Candidacy does not waive bodily privacy.** Government-required urine collection and chemical analysis are Fourth Amendment searches. Choosing to seek public office does not waive that protection; candidacy's public setting may inform reasonableness but does not itself authorize a compelled bodily search.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner establishes the search character of compelled urinalysis, and Vernonia preserves that conclusion even where participation in an activity is voluntary. A candidate's exposure to public scrutiny of qualifications is different from consent to bodily collection and medical analysis. Georgia's use of a personal physician and initial disclosure to the candidate reduce the intrusion, but do not eliminate the protected interest. The Court evaluates those actual safeguards and attributes no unsupported public disclosure, observed collection, police use or unrelated medical testing to the program.

**2. Georgia has not supplied a special need sufficient to justify this candidate-testing program.** A suspicionless search justified by a need beyond ordinary law enforcement must be reasonable in light of the actual governmental need, the program's operation, and the affected privacy interests. General desires for drug-free officials and public confidence do not supply the substantial, concrete need required for this broad advance-scheduled candidate program; its reduced intrusion cannot make up that deficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner and Von Raab permit special-needs searches when the protective or integrity interests and the program's operation justify the departure from individualized suspicion. Vernonia sustains its particular school-athletic program through custodial responsibility, contextual privacy and protective need. Political candidates do not share that setting. Georgia's covered offices have no common duty supplying a comparable protective risk, and the record identifies neither a concrete drug problem nor another substantial operational need connecting this broad class to the search. Sound judgment, integrity and deterrence remain legitimate interests, but do not explain why a negative certificate from one predictable test, scheduled within thirty days before qualification, adequately answers the asserted concern about future service. Advance scheduling limits what the certificate establishes; it does not make every scheduled test useless or unconstitutional. Nor must a State wait for a catastrophe, quantify efficacy, exhaust every alternative, or show safety-sensitive duties in every conceivable valid program. The holding rests on this program's combined deficiencies, not a mandatory checklist of missing facts. Georgia's strongest argument—the importance of trustworthy officials and the modest intrusion—therefore fails because a modest bodily search still needs an adequate governmental justification.

**3. The live prospective challenge requires relief against enforcement, without altering past elections.** Chandler's stated and uncontested intention to run again preserves the controversy despite completion of the 1994 election. The judgment sustaining the challenged requirement is reversed, and the case is remanded for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief barring its enforcement.

**Controlling explanation:** The continuing qualification condition affects Chandler's future candidacy, so resolving that live Fourth Amendment dispute does not require setting aside a completed election. The Court prescribes no replacement testing regime or new candidate qualification, and decides no damages, drug use, test result or individualized disqualification. The holding leaves materially different programs to their own supported special-needs justification. Independent First Amendment or state-law qualification claims, medical-fitness examinations, financial disclosure, particularized-suspicion testing, security screening tied to an actual safety risk, and private-sector testing are not decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association: Applies the rule that compelled urine collection and analysis are searches and its attention to substantiated protective need and actual privacy safeguards.
- Treasury Employees v. Von Raab: Preserves contextual special-needs analysis, including integrity interests; the doctrine is not confined to preventing a physical catastrophe.
- Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton: Applies the search and no-waiver holding and distinguishes the actual school-athletic merits holding on custodial, privacy and protective facts. Its five-Justice program rule, eight-Justice facial-prescription ruling and unanimous search rule remain distinct and unchanged.
- New Jersey v. T.L.O.: Its school-responsibility rationale does not transfer wholesale to adult political candidates. No earlier special-needs decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Georgia's challenged candidate-testing condition violates the Fourth Amendment. Suspicionless testing remains available under the existing contextual special-needs doctrine when a materially different program has an adequate supported justification. Public prominence and reduced intrusion alone do not provide it; neither a proven catastrophe nor exhaustion of all alternatives becomes a universal condition.


### 1997-04-21 — Blessing v. Freestone, No. 95-1441

Authority: [Record](../records/Blessing_v_Freestone_merits_1997-04-21.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each following proposition has nine direct votes. Scalia and Kennedy also state a separate, noncontrolling reservation about the original meaning of §1983; it changes none of these joins or the mandate.

**1. A §1983 statutory claim must identify an individual right.** A claimant must identify the particular statutory provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding rather than merely precatory obligation, and defining the asserted individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than leaving it vague or amorphous. The text, statutory setting and actual entitlement asserted govern; violation of a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish the right.

**Controlling explanation:** Wright and Wilder recognize defined individual entitlements within funding statutes; they do not make an entire program an indivisible promise. Livadas separately identifies the federal right, the §1983 channel, congressional foreclosure, defendant and remedy. Suter likewise preserves provision-specific adjudication rather than one answer for all state-plan provisions. The families must therefore identify what each particular command entitles them to demand. Program importance, mandatory wording and remedial usefulness cannot take the place of that inquiry; qualitative language is not universally unenforceable merely because applying it requires judgment.

**2. Title IV-D supplies no individual right to general administrative adequacy.** The federal substantial-compliance measure does not give each beneficiary a personal right to compel systemwide compliance. The same general theory fails when restated as an entitlement to sufficient agency staffing or satisfactory computerized administration as such; rejection of those theories does not decide whether a distinct, actually asserted provision creates a particular individual right.

**Controlling explanation:** The substantial-compliance measure evaluates aggregate performance for federal oversight and funding consequences. A State may meet that benchmark while failing one family; aggregate success cannot extinguish an independently existing personal right. Section 654(3) directs a single, separate organizational unit meeting prescribed staffing requirements, but identifies no beneficiary's right to a particular staff complement, caseload or assigned worker. Former and amended §654(16), their associated requirements and §654a's management and performance functions similarly organize program administration. Their technical detail does not make the system itself an individual entitlement. Mandatory duties important to effective collections therefore do not sustain the general claim recognized below. Alleged shortages, backlogs and accounting defects may still bear on a proved violation or fitted remedy, but create no right by themselves. No separately pleaded claim to each standardized computer requirement, confidentiality protection or individual transmission deadline is established here. The Court adjudicates no individual clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 and announces no categorical rule against regulatory elaboration of an established statutory right. A genuinely distinct claim must receive its own provision-specific inquiry, rather than automatic approval or rejection because it appears in an administrative section.

**3. General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983.** Once a provision creates an individual federal right, that right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes the channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Title IV-D's federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions do not alone foreclose every otherwise existing individual claim; any specific incompatible remedy must be assessed for the actual provision and claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Livadas separates enforceability from whether a right exists. Funding sanctions regulate the federal-state relationship and offer no general administrative forum for a family to obtain case-specific relief. Their detail and practical importance do not create the incompatibility found in the different remedial arrangements of Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson. Wright and Wilder likewise reject automatic foreclosure from federal administrative enforcement. The District Court's chapterwide preclusion ground cannot support dismissal of every possible concrete right. This holding does not establish a particular right, excuse ordinary defendant or relief requirements, or resolve a hypothetical provision-specific remedy not identified here.

**4. State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 prohibit rejecting enforceability merely because a Social Security Act provision appears among requirements for a state plan or its contents. They do not dispense with the individual-right inquiry or alter the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not enforceable in a private action.

**Controlling explanation:** These provisions apply to actions pending on their respective October 20 and October 31, 1994 enactment dates and actions brought thereafter, including this action. Their text leaves other established private-action grounds intact except for overturning any state-plan ground applied in Suter but not in earlier enforcement decisions, and expressly saves the §671(a)(15) result. The Court applies Suter's actual provision-specific rights and separate implied-action holdings, including its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) case-plan claim. No broader state-plan immunity is imported through the statutory reference, and no every-provision entitlement follows from removing that categorical obstacle.

**5. Madrid's concrete distribution allegation and the operative law require separate examination.** A broad prayer for programwide relief does not erase Madrid's allegation that collected support was not distributed under former §657(b)(1). The District Court must identify each asserted provision, conduct, remedy, governing version and effective date before deciding right, violation and relief; neither a superseded duty nor a duty not yet effective may silently support an injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** Former §657(b)(1) is materially more specific than an aggregate administrative benchmark, but specificity does not decide Madrid's unadjudicated eligibility, payment facts, legal right, defendant or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not promise a fresh $50 for each installment regardless of timing. Subsection (b) covers collections under an approved plan in fiscal years beginning after September 30, 1976, subject to subsection (d)'s distinct foster-care rules. Those rules first reimburse current foster-care maintenance, direct qualifying excess up to ordered support to the placement agency for the child's interests, then reimburse unrepaid past foster-care or family-assistance payments from the remainder, with the federal financing share reimbursed appropriately and any balance going to that agency for the child's interests. The agency may reserve its payments for future needs or make them available to the person meeting the child's daily needs. No relevant exception or payment fact is established for Madrid. The 1996 changes likewise have separate operative dates and transitions; compilation together does not make all duties presently applicable. The court below must apply them to identified claims and dates, without inferring Arizona implementation or default.

**Statutory timing limits on the remand:**

- Pub. L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.
- Under §395(b), the applicable date is the later of the otherwise specified date or the effective date of implementing state legislation, but no later than the first day of the first calendar quarter after the close of the first regular legislative session beginning after August 22, 1996. Each year of a two-year session counts separately. Under §395(c), a State unable to comply without amending its constitution is protected from a noncompliance finding until the earlier of one year after the necessary amendment takes effect or five years after August 22, 1996. Neither rule establishes an Arizona event or compliance fact here.
- The former §654(24) operational-computer deadline was changed from October 1, 1995 to October 1, 1997 by the October 12, 1995 amendment; the old date establishes no present default. The 1996 text separately requires compliance by October 1, 1997 with requirements enacted on or before October 13, 1988, and by October 1, 2000 with requirements enacted on or before August 22, 1996. The latter deadline extends one day for every day the Secretary misses the final-regulations deadline of two years after August 22, 1996. Implementation deadlines remain distinct from enactment and general effective dates.
- Section 654a(g), added by §312(c), takes effect October 1, 1998. Section 312(d)(2) separately permits a State processing receipts through local courts on August 22, 1996 to continue that receipt processing through September 30, 1999, at its option, through the courts already processing those payments by enactment. That narrow exception concerns §654b(b)(1)'s unit receipt-handling requirement; it is not a wholesale extension of every computer-system obligation. No Arizona qualification for it is found, and these future duties are not April 1997 mandates merely because they appear in the compilation.

**6. Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation.** On remand, the District Court must identify and assess the actual individual claims, then any §1983 foreclosure, proper defendant, breach, causation and lawful relief. Neither general administrative failure nor rejection of the programwide theory authorizes an undifferentiated substantial-compliance injunction, blanket dismissal, specified payment, damages award or prescribed staffing or computer redesign.

**Controlling explanation:** General administrative shortcomings can be evidence relevant to an established right and a remedy fitted to its violation. They are not a general commission to supervise Arizona's program. The Court decides no directly implied Title IV-D action through the §1983 inquiry. The Eleventh Amendment argument and request to overrule Maine v. Thiboutot were neither presented below nor included in the certiorari questions and are not reached; that preservation limit does not resolve every future defendant-specific question. The Ninth Circuit's undifferentiated right cannot stand, but the State's requested dismissal of every possible particular claim does not follow.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Suter v. Artist M.: Preserves its actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights and separate implied-action holdings and its unanimous preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim for specific adjudication. State-plan placement is not a chapterwide answer.
- Livadas v. Bradshaw: Applies the separate inquiries into individual federal right, §1983, congressional foreclosure, defendant and relief; statutory duty or preemption alone is insufficient.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority: Preserves its defined rent-and-utility entitlement and independent foreclosure analysis; no programwide Title IV-D right follows.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association: Preserves its identified reimbursement obligation and rejection of automatic foreclosure through funding withdrawal; it does not resolve the unparsed claims here.
- Pennhurst: Applies the need for clear notice of an asserted funding obligation without categorical immunity for state-plan provisions.
- Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association and Smith v. Robinson: Preserve foreclosure through comprehensive incompatible remedies; Title IV-D's general audits and funding measures alone do not supply that incompatibility.
- Maine v. Thiboutot: Remains undisturbed; the unpreserved request to overrule it is not reached.

#### Law After Decision

Title IV-D's aggregate compliance measure and general administrative machinery create no individual entitlement to systemwide adequacy. Specific actual claims remain subject to the individual-right test and a separate congressional-foreclosure inquiry. General federal oversight does not alone displace §1983, and state-plan location does not alone defeat enforcement. Remedy must fit an established right and violation under the applicable statutory version; no individual distribution right or statewide structural remedy is newly established.


### 1996-11-18 — Ohio v. Robinette; No. 95-891

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He would vacate the Ohio judgment and remand. ### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment. He would reach the constitutional merits under Collins's workplace-duty boundary and a proposed rule for the reserved affirmative-danger claim. He would require an official's affirmative creation or material increase of a specific foreseeable danger, or knowing obstruction of available protection, with conscious disregard of a known serious risk when time and practical opportunity permit a response, and a causal connection to the injury. Assurances, reliance and imminence may bear on that inquiry but are not universally necessary elements; he would add no undefined independent conscience-shocking requirement after those conditions are proved.

On this record, closing the unit and delegating placement were affirmative policy acts, but the warnings, file access and clinical process do not demonstrate the personal culpable contribution his rule requires. No named defendant is shown personally to have selected Waddell's destination, given Uhlrig a concrete assurance inducing abandonment of available protection, concealed a known immediate threat or personally overridden clinical safeguards to direct her into the encounter. He does not find that all officials lacked awareness or that the decision was sound policy. The gravity of the harm and possible negligence cannot alone make senior administrators constitutional insurers.

He would preserve liability questions involving concealment of an imminent threat, an affirmative direction into a uniquely dangerous encounter after a safety assurance, or a personal override of safeguards with deliberate indifference. Independently pleaded federal rights and genuinely different compulsory or intentionally harmful conduct remain outside his no-general-duty reasoning. Because his merits inquiry finds no constitutional violation, he does not decide qualified immunity. His proposed framework and application are noncontrolling.


### 1997-02-19 - Maryland v. Wilson; No. 95-1268; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Record](../records/Maryland_v_Wilson_merits_1997-02-19.md).

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justice Stevens.** Stevens and Kennedy would affirm because the passenger's added restraint needs an objective circumstance connected to the actual stop. Mimms's driver committed the traffic offense and controlled the vehicle; a passenger did neither. Aggregate danger does not justify an otherwise arbitrary command to every innocent passenger. Officers retain practical latitude to act on supported safety circumstances, but an explanation tied to those circumstances must precede the exercise of additional control rather than depend on the officer's grace after categorical authority is conferred. The movements and nervousness are not denied; the State did not preserve them as an independent justification, so they supply no basis to concur in reversal.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would permit an exit direction when any objective circumstance reasonably supports it for safety, facilitating a lawful search or conducting the stop. The showing is modest and may arise from movements, numbers, location, conduct or other practical facts; neither probable cause nor a formal suspicion label is required. Mimms's driver-offense predicate does not by itself justify an added command to a passenger. Whren's ordinary stop authority should therefore remain subject to a reason connected to the actual stop, rather than general statistics or administrative ease. These observations might have supplied such a reason if properly preserved, but the Court cannot replace the State's categorical case. He would leave suppression in force and order no remand for a new justification. He reserves commands to remain in a vehicle and preserves lawful-search control and arrest on probable cause.
