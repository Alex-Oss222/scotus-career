## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent legal review, contemporaneous notice and preservation limits: Elder v. Holloway — Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue, 1994-02-23. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue.

## Standards and Tests selection: No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

### No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

**Governing question:** Whether sovereign consent itself permits constitutional damages against a federal agency.

**Current rule:** A waiver removes sovereign immunity but does not create constitutional damages liability. Bivens’s individual-officer action does not extend directly to a federal agency: that extension would create a new defendant and public obligation. Independently, even assuming compensation and deterrence benefits, the expansion of direct federal fiscal liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that agency action. Existing individual Bivens, Davis and Carlson actions and genuinely authorized statutory remedies retain their own scope; no adequate substitute remedy is presumed.

**Authority by component:**

- Separation of waiver from agency cause: FDIC v. Meyer — Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action, 1994-02-23. The cause-of-action portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the agency award.
- Independent fiscal-and-remedial reason against extension: FDIC v. Meyer — Independent reason to leave this fiscal and remedial extension to Congress, 1994-02-23. The independent fiscal-and-remedial portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative ground.

**Present operation effective:** FDIC v. Meyer, 1994-02-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action.

## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal, 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

- Successive-stage review and preserved mandate limits: Behrens — Holding 1, February 21, 1996; Ginsburg Part I for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and herself (seven).
- Conditional assumed-fact rule: Behrens — Holding 2, February 21, 1996; unanimous Part II. Claim-specific effect of another surviving claim: Holding 3, Part III for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join. Stevens and Breyer join these conditional rules while opposing authorization of the successive appeal.

**Present operation effective:** Behrens, February 21, 1996; Johnson and Kimberlin retain their original component authority.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

**Holdings navigation:** Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal; Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule; Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299.

## Standards and Tests selection: Announcement before dwelling entry

### Announcement before dwelling entry

**Governing question:** The role of knock-and-announce in Fourth Amendment reasonableness.

**Current rule:** Common-law announcement of identity and authority forms part of the reasonableness inquiry when officers enter a dwelling. Supported countervailing circumstances, including danger, futility, escape or threatened evidence destruction, may justify entry without announcement. Justification depends on the actual circumstances; the rule is not an inflexible requirement and does not itself decide exclusion or another remedy.

**Authority by component:**

- Announcement and reasonable home entry: Announcement and reasonable home entry, 1995-05-22. Thomas's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-22; the component authorities identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Announcement and reasonable home entry.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

#### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### Do protective assurances or custody of a private assailant establish a custodial duty to protect the threatened family?

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. An alleged affirmative deprivation of otherwise available private protection remains analytically distinct from an omission of public protection; absence of custody of the threatened persons does not itself resolve that different claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale. Part I's separate appellate holding appears under Federal Courts, Qualified-immunity appeals and presentation.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a protection duty founded merely on official knowledge and assurances, but its discussion of increased vulnerability does not fully resolve this alleged affirmative course of conduct. The substantive-due-process inquiry concerns the official's own deprivation and the culpability attached to that deprivation, not simply the making of a statement. Daniels excludes mere negligence; the Court does not decide its reserved questions about recklessness or gross negligence. Pinder alleged that Johnson's assurance induced her to return to work rather than provide or arrange private protection. That favorable account must remain distinct from a general right to police protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court establishes no general constitutional duty to detain an assailant, warn a victim, or fulfill every protective promise. It does not hold that custody is indispensable to every affirmative-danger claim, or decide the constitutional merits of the assumed affirmative-deprivation theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- DeShaney rejects knowledge-and-promise protection duties without deciding this entire affirmative-increased-vulnerability theory.
- Daniels excludes merely negligent deprivation while leaving its reserved culpability questions unresolved here.

##### Was Johnson's alleged conduct sufficiently established as unconstitutional by March 10, 1989 to defeat qualified immunity?

**Holding:** Qualified immunity turns on whether all relevant law existing when the officer acted made the particular conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, without demanding a factual twin. On the favorable allegations here, law existing March 10, 1989 did not sufficiently establish that this noncoercive undertaking, release of the assailant, and uncorrected exposure constituted Johnson's own substantive-due-process deprivation; he therefore receives individual immunity on the reviewed claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II's notice standard joined by all nine Justices; Part III's application and affirmance joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice majority. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from the immunity disposition; their substantive theory is not controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not identical prior facts, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant law. DeShaney had rejected an omission-based duty while leaving the distinct affirmative theory incomplete. Bowers discussed active official wrongdoing but rejected release-based liability; Archie discussed obstruction of private aid in a materially different culpability setting and reached an adverse outcome; Jensen's discussion of a distinct danger did not preserve a knowledge-and-promise special relationship after DeShaney. Those sources did not sufficiently identify the assumed noncoercive interference here as the officer's own deprivation. A judge's disagreement, the number of judges on each side, or an officer's subjective belief in legality does not independently establish immunity. Later cases cannot retroactively supply the missing conduct-time notice.

**Material application:** The Court assumes that the assurance caused the mother to return to work and forgo alternative protection and that the remaining favorable allegations are true. It does not award immunity because actual reliance was absent, choose disputed facts against Pinder, or reject constitutional liability on the merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is notice-specific and claim-specific. Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets do not supply retroactive notice; Love establishes no mandatory merits-first sequence. Neither broader affirmative-danger liability nor the ultimate culpability and causation merits is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's reviewed individual-immunity judgment for Johnson is affirmed, 5-4. No new constitutional merits judgment or damages award is entered. Official-capacity and municipal claims, equal protection, section 1985, state wrongful-death and survival claims, and other excluded matters remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Anderson supplies the objective, particularized notice inquiry without an exact-facts requirement.
- Elder requires review of all relevant contemporaneous authority rather than an isolated citation or subjective belief.
- DeShaney supplies the rejection of a general protective duty but does not itself resolve the assumed affirmative-deprivation merits.
- Bowers, Archie and Jensen are considered as conduct-time circuit-law notice sources; their distinct facts, outcomes and culpability limits are preserved, and they are not converted into Supreme Court holdings.
- Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets cannot establish notice for conduct preceding those decisions; Love does not impose mandatory sequencing.

#### FDIC v. Meyer, 510 U.S. 471

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-741.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### FTCA exclusivity does not encompass this federal constitutional claim

**Holding:** Section 2679(a) makes FTCA relief exclusive as against an agency's sue-and-be-sued authority only for claims cognizable under the whole of §1346(b), including its private-person and state-law requirements. Meyer's federal constitutional tort is outside that category, so §2679(a) does not bar this suit in the agency's own name; this statutory conclusion neither supplies a cause of action nor guarantees recovery.

**Proposition-level authority:** The statutory-exclusivity portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this separate threshold holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The §1346(b) category requires a claim against the United States for money damages for property injury or loss, personal injury or death caused by an employee's negligent or wrongful act or omission within employment scope, under circumstances in which a private person would be liable under the law of the place. That last requirement means state substantive law; §2679(b)(1)'s differently worded employee rule is not imported.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Richards v. United States, 369 U.S. 1: applies the FTCA's state-law source and private-person limitation.
- Miree v. DeKalb County, 433 U.S. 25: applies state law as the substantive source under the law-of-the-place provision.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: preserves FTCA coordination while specifying that §2679(a) refers to cognizability under §1346(b), not everything described as tortious.
- United States v. Smith, 499 U.S. 160: distinguishes employee exclusivity under §2679(b)(1) and its treatment of the foreign-country exception from the differently worded agency provision.
- Hubsch v. United States, 338 U.S. 440: preserves cognizability despite failure of proof on an element; distinguishes a claim that never falls within §1346(b)'s category.

##### Former FSLIC's sue-and-be-sued clause waives immunity

**Holding:** Former 12 U.S.C. §1725(c)(4) waives FSLIC's sovereign immunity for this constitutional tort claim, even though a private entity ordinarily would not bear that constitutional duty. An implied restriction on its broad sue-and-be-sued authority requires a clear showing that the suit is inconsistent with the statutory or constitutional scheme, that restriction is necessary to avoid grave interference with governmental functions, or that Congress plainly intended the clause in a narrow sense; FDIC establishes none here.

**Proposition-level authority:** The waiver portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt the specific waiver and its stated limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FHA v. Burr, 309 U.S. 242: applies liberal construction and all three grounds for a clearly established implied restriction; mere absence of private constitutional liability does not establish one.
- Loeffler v. Frank, 486 U.S. 549: applies the broad waiver rule while keeping separate statutory limits distinct.
- Franchise Tax Board v. United States Postal Service, 467 U.S. 512: preserves private-business liability as support for the incidents of suit recognized there, without turning it into an outer boundary for every waiver.
- United States v. Nordic Village, February 25, 1992: preserves the requirement to identify the particular sovereign consent; the ambiguous bankruptcy provision there does not narrow this express agency clause.

##### Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action

**Holding:** A waiver of sovereign immunity removes a defense to suit but does not itself authorize constitutional damages; Bivens does not supply Meyer a direct damages cause of action against FSLIC. Extending an individual-officer action to the federal agency would create a distinct defendant and public obligation, rather than merely provide relief within an existing agency action.

**Proposition-level authority:** The cause-of-action portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the agency award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Mitchell, 463 U.S. 206: applies the analytical separation of sovereign consent and a substantive source authorizing relief.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: retains its individual-officer action and declines extension directly to a federal agency.
- Davis v. Passman, 442 U.S. 228: preserves the individual employment-discrimination action; it does not supply an agency defendant here.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves individual-officer liability and its deterrent rationale without making every useful compensatory arrangement an authorized agency action.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992: preserves appropriate relief within an established private action; distinguishes creation of a new action and defendant.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming that a direct agency remedy could advance the compensatory and deterrent purposes of Bivens, the resulting expansion of direct federal financial liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that remedy. Those institutional considerations independently require rejection of this implied agency damages action; the Court does not find that Meyer received an adequate substitute remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The independent fiscal-and-remedial portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The $130,000 agency award cannot stand for want of an agency cause. Pattullo's separate immunity judgment is outside review and remains undisturbed. The Court does not decide Meyer's property entitlement, the underlying due-process violation, entitlement under another source, a universal federal waiver, or elimination of existing individual Bivens remedies. Former §1725(c)(4), rather than a silently substituted successor clause, governs this 1982 agency claim. Section 2679(b)(1)'s separate employee rule is not rewritten.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: applies its special-factors limitation to the proposed direct public liability, independently of the individual-defendant distinction.
- United States v. Standard Oil Co. of California, 332 U.S. 301: applies the concern against judicial determination of a new federal fiscal obligation.
- Bush v. Lucas, 462 U.S. 367: applies institutional restraint concerning a new damages remedy; does not find that Bush's civil-service scheme governed or compensated Meyer.
- Schweiker v. Chilicky, 487 U.S. 412: applies caution about remedy creation despite incomplete compensation, without importing its Social Security system as Meyer's actual remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The agency damages judgment is reversed and the $130,000 award cannot stand for want of a direct agency cause. Pattullo's separate, unreviewed immunity judgment remains undisturbed. No constitutional merits finding or new damages trial is ordered.

#### Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1244.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### A prior appeal does not categorically exhaust legal-immunity review

**Holding:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later summary-judgment appeal presenting a separable legal immunity question that otherwise satisfies the collateral-order requirements. The later appeal may address legal immunity on properly assumed facts; this rule authorizes neither factual-sufficiency review nor repeated appeals of an unchanged legal ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen's cumulative conditions and Mitchell's legal-immunity category apply. Digital Equipment's importance and category discipline remains; Johnson supplies no numerical ceiling and retains its factual boundary.

##### An authorized appeal uses assumed facts and excludes factual sufficiency

**Holding:** Within an otherwise authorized qualified-immunity appeal, the appellate court may decide whether conduct on the District Court's properly assumed or sufficiently supported facts violates clearly established law, but may not choose which factual account is true or reweigh whether evidence supports it. If the relevant assumptions are unstated, the court may identify the likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant in the manner Johnson permits, without deciding truth or evidentiary sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, unanimous. Stevens and Breyer condition agreement on an otherwise authorized appeal and do not find successive authorization here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson and Kimberlin preserve assumed-fact review without factual selection. Elder requires relevant conduct-date law but creates no jurisdiction or retrospective notice. Swint rejects convenience-based expansion; Doe's factual-sufficiency exclusion remains.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No immunity, constitutional violation, property interest, factual truth, evidentiary sufficiency or damages issue is decided.

##### A different surviving claim does not itself defeat a qualifying immunity appeal

**Holding:** The fact that an official must still defend another claim does not, by itself, eliminate an otherwise authorized immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity denial on a different claim. That claim-specific principle neither supplies missing appellate authorization nor makes the other claim reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join; Stevens and Breyer continue to oppose successive appeal as of right.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitchell protects against defending the claim to which immunity applies; Swint prevents a proper appeal from carrying unrelated matters and leaves its necessary-connected-review reservation open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the categorical Ninth Circuit dismissal and remand to identify proper assumptions favorable to the nonmovant and any actually reviewable legal issue, dismissing factual-sufficiency and otherwise unavailable issues. The summary-judgment denial is not reversed, immunity is not awarded, and neither the separate United States negligence claim nor earlier substitution ruling is reopened. No stay or permission is granted.

#### Johnson v. Jones

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-455.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

##### Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal

**Holding:** A denial of qualified immunity is not immediately appealable under §1291 insofar as it determines only whether the pretrial evidence presents a genuine factual dispute about the official's participation in the alleged conduct. Mitchell's immediate appeal remains available for the separable legal question whether the conduct, on the relevant assumed facts, violated clearly established law; the present excessive-force participation dispute does not meet that condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A proper legal immunity appeal uses the district court's assumed facts. If unstated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bootstrap factual review. Genuine necessary connected review and future mixed-order questions remain at their reserved scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949): applies separateness as a cumulative collateral-order condition.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511 (1985): retains immediate legal immunity review on assumed facts, bounded against ordinary factual-sufficiency appeals.
- Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct (June 6, 1994): applies category and cumulative-condition discipline; public immunity importance does not erase separateness.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission (March 1, 1995): applies rejection of convenience-based appellate expansion; genuine necessary connected review remains at its reserved scope.
- Elder v. Holloway (February 23, 1994): preserves review of all relevant date-eligible legal authority on an authorized immunity issue; supplies no fact-reweighing jurisdiction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the reviewed immediate excessive-force factual-sufficiency appeal. The circuit's separate false-arrest immunity reversal remains undisturbed. No participation, beating, evidence-sufficiency, liability, state-law claim or other defendant's liability is adjudicated.

#### Soldal v. Cook County, 506 U.S. 56 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-6516.

**Decided:** December 8, 1992.

##### A seizure of a home protects possession independently of privacy

**Holding:** Meaningful government-attributable interference with possession of a home is a seizure under the Fourth Amendment even without a search, invasion of privacy or liberty, or criminal investigation. The family’s alleged forcible dispossession meets that threshold, but coverage alone does not establish unreasonableness or every element of §1983 liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas; nine Justices adopt the same rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109 — applied; possession and privacy remain independently protected interests.
- United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696 — applied; seizure scrutiny does not require an accompanying search.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321 — applied; plain-view seizure remains subject to the Fourth Amendment.
- Adickes v. S. H. Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144 — applied to the summary-judgment joint-action inference, without a final conspiracy finding.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 — preserved; seizure coverage does not establish municipal liability.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992 — preserved; attribution does not settle immunity or a private merits defense.

##### A due-process characterization does not displace the specific seizure claim

**Holding:** A court may not reject an otherwise applicable Fourth Amendment claim merely because the same conduct also concerns a deprivation of property under the Due Process Clause. Each applicable constitutional protection must be examined on its own terms; this disposition does not adjudicate the family’s separate substantive-due-process claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 — applied; the specific Fourth Amendment source governs its covered conduct and is not displaced by a dominant-character theory.
- Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 — distinguished; postdeprivation procedural remedies do not answer the distinct seizure claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517 — confined to its prison setting for the relevant coverage proposition; no rule excluding possession of a home follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Private action alone is insufficient. Ultimate reasonableness, municipal policy, causation, immunity, damages and the separate substantive-due-process claim remain undecided. The holding addresses a home, without deciding coverage of every property category.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse adverse summary judgment and remand the seizure claim; no final finding of unreasonableness or award of damages.

### 1996-11-18 — Ohio v. Robinette; No. 95-891

Authority: [Record](../records/Ohio_v_Robinette_merits_1996-11-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Review of the interwoven federal and state grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** When a state judgment fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven with federal law and does not clearly state an independently sufficient state ground, the Court may review its federal basis. Ohio's intertwined constitutional explanation supplies no clear independent ground barring review here; Ohio remains free to supply greater protection on genuinely independent state-law grounds.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by six direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees to exercise review without joining this explanation; Ginsburg and Stevens accept review in their separate writings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's reference to its own Constitution does not end the jurisdictional inquiry. Michigan v. Long supplies the rule for opinions that rest on federal law or interweave federal and state law without a clear independent basis. Arizona v. Evans retains that rule. The Ohio opinion invokes both provisions and develops the suppression analysis with federal authorities; it does not announce that an independent Ohio ground alone sustains the judgment. Federal review therefore lies. Deciding the federal question neither erases the Ohio provision nor prevents the Ohio courts from adopting a clearly independent state rule in further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Long and Evans are applied without changing the independent-state-ground rule or creating an exception to the ordinary final-judgment requirement.

##### II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer's uncommunicated investigative motive or private decision to issue only a warning does not invalidate an objectively lawful traffic stop or an exit order while lawful traffic business is being completed. Once that justification ends, further detention requires independent legal grounds; the court must examine the objective circumstances to determine whether the person could terminate the encounter, and return of a license is neither an automatic end of seizure nor an automatic license to continue it.

**Authority:** Part II.A's rule concerning motive and the exit order has six direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Part II.B's distinct continued-detention rule has those six plus Stone-Zsela, seven. Stone-Zsela supplies no additional reason for Part II.A.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an objectively justified beginning and a lawful continuation are separate questions. Whren rejects subjective purpose as an extra condition for an ordinary probable-cause traffic stop while preserving limits on manner, scope and duration. Mimms permits an exit order during a lawful traffic stop without a separate justification for that direction. The Ohio court could not deem the direction unlawful merely because the deputy privately intended a warning or hoped to ask unrelated questions.

That correction does not establish that detention lawfully continued through the later consent request. Bostick requires an objective assessment of whether the person could decline the request or terminate the encounter. The returned license and completed warning bear on that inquiry, but so do the immediacy and actual wording of further questioning, tone, positioning and continuity of official authority. Neither the officer's secret willingness to permit refusal nor Robinette's subjective impression alone decides the seizure question. Supported trial findings matter; the Court does not replace the record with a categorical conclusion in either direction. Ornelas's distinction between historical fact and ultimate constitutional sufficiency remains applicable within its decided scope; this decision announces no universal appellate-review rule for every mixed or consent question. The encounter's classification remains for proper application below.

**Precedent treatment:** Whren's objective initial-stop rule and independent duration limits remain intact; Mimms is confined to lawful traffic business. Bostick's actual-circumstances inquiry is applied. Ornelas is not extended into a general rule for all consent issues.

##### III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not invariably require an officer to give a free-to-go warning before seeking consent to search. Consent must be voluntary under all the circumstances, and when an unlawful detention preceded or accompanied it, voluntariness alone does not establish that the consent was sufficiently independent of that illegality to permit use of the evidence.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by seven direct joins. Ginsburg and Stevens separately reject an indispensable federal script; neither additional agreement is needed to establish the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a useful warning is not automatically an indispensable constitutional condition. Schneckloth requires voluntary consent under the total circumstances and treats knowledge of the right to refuse as relevant without making advice of that right universally mandatory. A fixed release formula would replace that inquiry rather than perform it. The absence of a clear release signal nevertheless remains important evidence; rejecting a script does not make every post-warning request consensual.

The supported trial findings concerning the manner, wording and positions of the parties, Robinette's education and the conclusion of traffic business must be considered with the returned license and actual continuation words. His testimony includes both an initial belief that he could leave and a later feeling that he could not refuse. Neither fragment decides voluntariness by itself. The Court holds neither that this consent was valid nor that it was invalid.

Royer and Brown keep consent's voluntariness separate from its causal independence of unlawful detention. If the court finds an unsupported continued seizure, it must address that additional question; voluntary consent does not automatically erase the illegality, and illegality does not establish that every later consent is incurably tainted. Suppression remains available when the applicable inquiry fails. Nothing in federal law prevents Ohio from requiring clearer warnings under independent state law or police policy.

**Precedent treatment:** Schneckloth is applied without a new warning element. Royer and Brown preserve the separate inquiry into the effect of prior illegality; no automatic cure or automatic suppression rule is created.

##### IV. The remedy corrects the federal rules without deciding admissibility

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the warning rule and the motive-based exit-order premise requires further proceedings on the actual objective detention, voluntary consent and any necessary attenuation question. It does not itself establish the search's validity, require admission of the evidence or reinstate the conviction.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer directly join Part IV; Stone-Zsela joins its stated inquiries and limits while selecting vacatur instead of reversal. Ginsburg separately supports reversal with the same unresolved-application boundary. Seven Justices support the Court's formal reversal and remand; the holdings do not merge Stone-Zsela's different remedy into that coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's two grounds must be addressed separately. The federal script requirement fails, and the detention ruling cannot rest on undisclosed motives during lawful traffic business. Yet those errors do not decide whether the later encounter was consensual, lawfully justified or unlawfully extended, or whether consent was both voluntary and independent of any illegality. The state courts must apply the rules to the supported findings and complete encounter. They may consider genuinely independent state law. A new hearing is not commanded merely by this remand, and the Court enters no final evidence-admission or conviction order.

**Precedent treatment:** The rule/application distinction retained in Evans and the separate consent and causation inquiries in Schneckloth, Royer and Brown govern the scope of remand without enlargement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Michigan v. Long:** Applies the presumption permitting review of interwoven federal grounds absent a plain independent-state-law basis.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Retains that jurisdictional rule and the distinction between correcting a federal rule and deciding its unresolved factual application; its court-record-error exclusion rule is not extended here.
- **Whren v. United States:** Applies objective legality to the initial traffic business and preserves independent limits on manner, scope and duration.
- **Pennsylvania v. Mimms:** Applies the permission to order a driver out during a lawful stop; no indefinite post-stop detention authority follows.
- **Florida v. Bostick:** Requires assessment of the actual objective capacity to decline requests or terminate the encounter, without a categorical seizure or release rule.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Preserves its historical-fact/ultimate-sufficiency distinction within its actual scope; no general review rule for all consent questions is added.
- **Schneckloth v. Bustamonte:** Applies total-circumstances voluntariness and treats knowledge as relevant rather than an indispensable warning requirement.
- **Florida v. Royer:** Preserves the need to consider whether consent follows or remains a product of unlawful detention.
- **Brown v. Illinois:** Preserves attenuation as distinct from voluntariness; neither a voluntary response nor the occurrence of illegality alone answers the remedial question.

#### Law After Decision

Federal law imposes no universal release script, but the objective seizure, voluntary-consent and necessary attenuation inquiries remain distinct and enforceable. The decision supplies no categorical rule that a returned license ends a seizure, that a traffic offense authorizes later unrelated detention, or that a polite request ensures admissibility. The actual search result remains unresolved under the corrected federal standards, and genuinely independent Ohio protection remains available.

### 1997-02-19 - Maryland v. Wilson; No. 95-1268; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Record](../records/Maryland_v_Wilson_merits_1997-02-19.md).

#### Holdings

#### Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** During a lawful traffic stop, an officer may direct a passenger to leave the vehicle without additional passenger-specific suspicion as a limited safety measure incident to the stop. This authority concerns the exit direction itself; it does not establish categorical forcible detention for the entire stop, permission to frisk or search, or power to prolong detention after the lawful traffic business ends.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, counting its author within that six-Justice coalition; six direct rationale votes control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court extends Mimms's comparison of officer safety and incremental intrusion to the passenger's immediate relocation during an independently lawful traffic stop. A passenger may conceal a weapon and threaten an officer even though the passenger committed no traffic offense. The safety risk does not disappear when the potentially dangerous occupant sits beside the driver.

The passenger has a stronger initial liberty argument because the driver's offense supplies the reason for the stop. But the direction changes the passenger's location during an interruption already lawfully occasioned by the vehicle's stop. At that limited scope, the safety interest outweighs the additional intrusion. Terry and Brignoni-Ponce require attention to the nature of the intrusion and its justification; their particularized-ground rules for different intrusions remain. The Court does not resolve whether every passenger was already seized at the instant the vehicle stopped or whether a passenger may be forcibly held for its entire duration.

Whren permits an objectively justified ordinary initial stop while preserving execution limits. Robinette likewise separates lawful traffic measures from continued detention after their justification ends. Neither supplies automatic search or frisk authority. References in Michigan v. Long to occupants leaving cars and in the Rakas concurrence do not constitute an earlier controlling passenger-exit holding; the Court decides that question here. Because Maryland presented only categorical authority, the Court neither relies on the observed movements as a distinct justification nor eliminates preservation limits. The contrary categorical federal premise is reversed, leaving ordinary further proceedings rather than a final evidence-admission order.

**Precedent treatment:** Mimms's safety/incremental-intrusion rule extends to this passenger-exit direction. Terry, Brignoni-Ponce, Whren and Robinette retain their distinct predicates and limits; no frisk, search or prolonged-detention rule is displaced.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Pennsylvania v. Mimms supplies the safety and incremental-intrusion comparison and is extended from drivers to the limited passenger-exit direction.
- Terry v. Ohio and United States v. Brignoni-Ponce retain their requirements for the distinct seizures and protective intrusions they govern; this decision does not make every additional police measure categorical.
- Whren v. United States preserves objective authority for the ordinary initial traffic stop and independent constraints on manner, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette preserves the distinction between measures during lawful traffic business and unsupported continuation after it ends, together with genuinely independent state law.
- Michigan v. Long and the Rakas concurrence are not treated as having already decided the passenger-exit question. Ornelas retains its independent ultimate-suspicion/probable-cause review and protection of historical facts without becoming a universal review rule.

#### Law After Decision

Federal law permits the limited passenger-exit command during a lawful traffic stop without an additional passenger-specific reason. The decision adds that authority while leaving other seizure, duration, search, frisk and independent-state-law questions at their existing scopes. It does not authorize every subsequent command merely because the vehicle was lawfully stopped.

### 1997-03-31 — United States v. Lanier; No. 95-1717.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Lanier_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted

**Rule:** Section 242, alone or as authoritatively construed, must make the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; a novel or unforeseeable criminal application is forbidden. Willful bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws supplies the statute's willfulness and definite-right limitations. Vagueness, lenity and the prohibition on unexpected retrospective judicial enlargement protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. The courts interpret Congress's criminal enactment rather than create common-law crimes. The reprehensibility or state criminality of the alleged conduct does not substitute for identification of a federal right and adequate notice of its application. Current decisions govern the reviewing court's legal method at their actual scopes; a decision issued after the conduct cannot itself supply earlier notice.

##### Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions

**Rule:** Fair warning under §242 may be supplied by the relevant pre-existing legal landscape, including lower-court authority; it does not categorically require a Supreme Court decision on the particular application. Neither one lower-court decision nor a fixed numerical consensus is automatically sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws did not prescribe the Sixth Circuit's Supreme-Court-only restriction. Elder supplies the examination of all relevant authority in assessing clear constitutional law; actual conflicts, source authority and their relation to the charged acts matter. Excluding a whole relevant class of decisions can wrongly convert established law into unknowable law. Conversely, source breadth does not eliminate the demand for sufficient clarity in the particular context.

##### Obvious applications need no factual twin

**Rule:** Existing constitutional law can give fair warning of an obviously covered application without an earlier case involving fundamentally similar facts. A broad invocation of liberty or bodily security is insufficient where the particular application remains unsettled, including where an earlier decision expressly leaves it open.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson supplies a particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness, not a demand for repetition of every factual detail. Officials cannot insist upon a decision matching their office, victim and precise method when established law applies with obvious clarity. At the same time, Elder and the actual Pinder and Uhlrig decisions preserve attention to unsettled applications and personal responsibility. Those distinctions prevent an abstract right from automatically resolving each charged assault, official nexus or notice question. The Court rejects the categorical similarity prerequisite while leaving those count-specific determinations below.

##### The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact

**Rule:** Section 242 fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: constitutional unlawfulness must be apparent in light of pre-existing law. That common protection supplies neither automatic criminal liability nor a substitute for constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the Government's criminal burden of proof and every other statutory element.

**Controlling explanation:** Due process does not demand a categorically clearer legal rule in the criminal case than the clarity protection furnished in the civil inquiry. But a conclusion about civil damages immunity cannot itself convict anyone. Screws's willfulness requirement performs separate work, and judicial title alone does not establish that each act occurred under color of state law. The Sixth Circuit must keep legal notice and proof of each criminal predicate distinct, rather than use either to erase the other.

##### Review the counts without the rejected legal shortcuts

**Rule:** The appellate court must reconsider the case without a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite and without categorical exclusion of due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody from §242. The judgment resolves no count's ultimate constitutional basis, fair warning, official nexus, proof or sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws and United States v. Price foreclose excluding rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process wholesale. DeShaney's limits on affirmative protection against private violence do not immunize an official's own assault merely because the victim was outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendment. Actual Collins, Pinder, Uhlrig and Nabozny preserve the distinction among personal official wrongdoing, omitted protection and supervisory attribution. These are legal directions for review, not new findings of assault, automatic restoration of the verdicts, a universal constitutional-assault formula or an order for a new evidentiary hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Screws v. United States:** Applied definite-right and willfulness protections; rejected a supposed Supreme-Court-only source restriction and categorical exclusion of due-process rights.
- **United States v. Price:** Preserved §242's reach to protected due-process rights without deciding the elements of each charged count.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied particularized apparent unlawfulness; no factual-twin requirement follows.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Applied consideration of relevant existing authority while preserving temporal limits.
- **Pinder v. Johnson and Uhlrig v. Harder:** Preserved their particularized notice, unsettled-application and personal-attribution boundaries; later holdings cannot create earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Kept constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation and supervisory theories distinct; their later dates cannot establish notice for earlier conduct.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserved the difference between a constitutional deprivation and every injurious official act; no broad federal tort rule is created.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Distinguished limits on protection from private violence from the official's own asserted assault.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied the specific-provision rule when a particular Amendment governs, without treating the Fourth and Eighth Amendments as exhaustive of every abusive official act.

#### Law After Decision

Section 242 has no Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite. Pre-existing law must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness apparent, using the same legal-clarity protection as clearly-established-law analysis while preserving every distinct criminal element and burden. The Court creates no new substantive right or count-specific liability holding.

### 1997-04-28 — Richards v. Wisconsin; No. 96-5955.

Authority: [Record](../records/Richards_v_Wisconsin_merits_1997-04-28.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The felony-drug category cannot replace the required execution inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not permit officers to dispense with announcement solely because a warrant seeks evidence of felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver. A valid search warrant authorizes the search but does not itself establish that an unannounced forcible entry is reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine in Parts I, II and IV; Part III has the distinct seven-Justice coalition specified below.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson v. Arkansas makes announcement of identity and authority part of reasonable dwelling entry while preserving supported exceptions. Wisconsin's category assumes danger and threatened destruction in every included case despite differences in occupants, evidence and execution. General associations among drugs, guns and easy disposal cannot do the work of a particular justification. Announcement protects voluntary admission, property, privacy and occupant safety even when officers hold a warrant. Buie rejects an automatic offense premise for a broader protective sweep; its permission to inspect immediately adjoining attack spaces rests on a different immediate risk. Maryland v. Wilson likewise permits a limited passenger-exit direction through its specific safety and incremental-intrusion comparison, not a general license for surprise dwelling entry. The Court therefore rejects this category without declaring every categorical Fourth Amendment rule invalid. Chandler's attention to the actual justification in its different testing context is consistent with, but does not enlarge, this holding.

##### II. Reasonable suspicion governs a supported execution exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers must identify particular circumstances and rational inferences supporting reasonable suspicion that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including by allowing destruction of evidence. The threshold is below probable cause but requires more than the offense label or an unsupported hunch, and the relevant facts must be known when the challenged force begins.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson preserves announcement exceptions but leaves their precise quantum open. Buie's use of articulable suspicion for a limited protective step supports a practical threshold by analogy: execution of an independently authorized search may require a prompt response without awaiting probable cause of imminent destruction. This rule supplies no authority to search without a warrant or another lawful basis and does not transfer every protective-sweep rule to evidence searches. For the destruction branch, suspicion must connect delay for announcement and voluntary admission to a threat to preservation of the evidence. Officers need not hear destruction or wait until evidence is lost. A drug label or unsupported general weapons claim remains insufficient, and later discovery or flight cannot retroactively justify earlier force. The standard applies to the identified execution exceptions; the Court fixes no universal waiting time and does not equate seeing a uniform with knowing the officers' purpose. The independent useless-gesture contention need not be decided here.

##### III. Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles

**Holding and operative rule:** In reviewing this execution under the reasonable-suspicion standard, the historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, with grounded experience given real weight; the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of those facts is independently assessed. That allocation permits review of the supported record without manufacturing new facts.

**Authority:** Breyer, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven direct joins. Scalia withholds this method; Stone-Zsela joins the substantive holdings and application without an additional position on it.

**Controlling explanation:** Ornelas separates historical factfinding from ultimate reasonable suspicion in its stop and warrantless-search setting. The same functional distinction applies here: the trial court decides what officers observed and whose testimony to credit, while the constitutional execution threshold remains a legal question. Ornelas did not previously decide every warrant-execution question; this is its reasoned application to the present one. Robinette demonstrates that correcting a general rule does not invariably decide its application or require remand. Here the original hearing already supplies the material observations and credibility determination. The Court respects those findings while testing their constitutional significance, and need not supplement them with assumed purpose knowledge or later events.

##### IV. The credited contemporaneous facts justified this entry

**Holding and operative rule:** The credited look toward a nearby uniformed officer and surprised reaction, combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, supplied particularized reasonable suspicion of destruction risk before the first attempt to enter; the subsequent rapid closure and locking strengthened that justification for the later force. The entry was therefore reasonable despite the invalid categorical rule and the judge's advance refusal of no-knock authorization.

**Controlling explanation:** Pharo's maintenance ruse brought Richards to the door. The trial judge rejected the material portion of Richards's contrary account and inferred awareness of the uniformed officer from the credited observations. Recognition is an inference on disputed evidence, not a concession. The look toward the uniform and surprise preceded the first attempted entry and supported the inference that the ruse had exposed the police; rapid closure and locking strengthened it for later force. Combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, these facts supplied more than a general drug assumption. Closing against a supposed late-night maintenance intruder is a possible innocent account, but it does not eliminate reasonable suspicion from the credited police-recognition response. Neither intended destruction nor certainty of recognition had to be proved. Identity was shouted two or three seconds after closure, possibly while force was already underway; purpose was recalled as announced when the door opened. A belated announcement cannot cure prior unlawful force, but a destruction exception already justified this execution. Later window flight, drugs found above ceiling tiles, unproved destruction noises and an unproved weapon play no part in supplying pre-force justification. The original hearing suffices without deciding the objection to supplemental testimony. Advance refusal required attention to new facts, not disregard of the judge's ruling on the same general premise; the doorway developments supplied those new facts. The ruse was not consent to forced entry.

The Court does not decide exclusion after a violation, an independently sufficient futility ground, the cross-petition objection concerning the alternative remedy argument, or a general doctrine for every police-created exigency. A valid warrant alone is not declared an independent evidentiary source or proof of inevitable lawful discovery.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Wilson v. Arkansas is applied to reject the offense category and given a reasonable-suspicion execution threshold; its constitutional exclusion question remains open.
- Maryland v. Buie supplies a practicable articulable-suspicion analogy and distinguishes a justified limited category from an automatic offense premise for a greater intrusion.
- Ornelas is applied by functional analogy to the review allocation in Part III, with the existing factual protections intact.
- Ohio v. Robinette supports review of interwoven federal/state reasoning and separates correction of a rule from application and remedy; it imposes no obligatory remand on an adequate record.
- Michigan v. Long supplies the independent-state-ground inquiry without displacing genuinely independent Wisconsin law.
- Maryland v. Wilson retains its limited passenger-exit holding, distinguished from forcible dwelling entry.
- Chandler retains its actual-justification analysis in the different bodily-testing setting and supplies no universal prohibition of categories.

#### Law After Decision

Wisconsin may no longer rely on this felony-drug category alone. Unannounced execution requires reviewable reasonable suspicion grounded in the particular circumstances, and the time of the challenged force controls the justification. The Court applies a distinct factual/legal appellate allocation by seven direct joins. The existing conviction stands on the destruction-risk ground; no new constitutional exclusion rule, automatic valid-warrant immunity or universal waiting period follows.
