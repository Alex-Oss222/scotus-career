## Event

Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc.; Fourth Circuit No. 95-2476; judgment below reported at 88 F.3d 258. Argued and decided February 26, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). The questions concern responsibility under Title VII for harassment aided by delegated supervisory authority without higher-management notice, the distinction between agency attribution and negligence, and the required connection between workplace authority and misconduct. The jury awarded $25,000; the District Court entered renewed judgment as a matter of law for the employer, and the Court of Appeals affirmed. Constructive discharge is outside the grant.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment on hostile-environment employer attribution and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Vacated in the reviewed part and remanded, 9-0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** none. The employer-attribution issue returns for application of the correct agency rule under Rule 50. Neither reinstatement of the $25,000 verdict nor a new trial is automatic. Constructive discharge remains undisturbed.

## Holdings

All holdings below are in Justice Breyer's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices. Each is the holding of the Court, not an aggregation of separate reasons.

### Employer responsibility through delegated authority

**Operative rule.** An employer may be responsible under Title VII for a supervisor-created hostile environment when actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the creation or maintenance of that environment, even without actual or constructive notice to higher management. A supervisory title or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; the employee must establish the connection between delegated authority and the misconduct, while negligent failure to correct known or reasonably knowable harassment remains a distinct route to liability.

**Controlling explanation.** Meritor directs courts to agency principles and rejects both automatic imputation of every supervisor's misconduct and a universal defense based on lack of notice. The statutory employer-agent relationship gives legal significance to authority the employer actually entrusted to the wrongdoer. Hiring, firing, discipline and threatened employment action may establish the necessary connection. An express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; daily control of working conditions can also aid unlawful conduct. The employer's argument that personal misconduct necessarily falls outside its responsibility would eliminate the agency inquiry Meritor preserves. Conversely, the employee cannot establish attribution simply by pointing to the administrator's rank. Harris and DeAngelis define an actionable hostile environment without supplying this separate attribution predicate. The administrator's control and hiring and firing authority therefore require examination, even though he reported to a corporate officer. The Court does not adopt a general liability rule for every supervisor's private act or a new affirmative defense.

### Corrected review of the employer's judgment

**Operative rule.** A Rule 50 judgment resting on the legal premise that higher management's lack of notice necessarily defeats supervisor-agency attribution must be reconsidered under the proper agency standard, with the evidence and permissible inferences viewed in the verdict winner's favor. Vacatur does not itself establish attribution, restore the verdict or require a new trial; preserved alternative grounds, evidentiary sufficiency and genuine instruction or preservation questions remain for the lower court.

**Controlling explanation.** The jury's $25,000 award does not answer whether the correct attribution question was submitted or whether the record legally supports the verdict. The lower courts foreclosed the agency route because the administrator was not a proprietor, partner or corporate officer and higher management lacked notice. That categorical barrier cannot stand under Meritor. The proper response is to return the preserved attribution issue for review under the corrected rule, not to treat every allegation as a finding or assume that no other preserved ground can matter. Constructive discharge was separately rejected and lies outside the granted questions; this remand does not reopen it.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied to require an actual agency inquiry; neither supervisor status nor absence of higher-management notice supplies a categorical answer.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Preserved as the objective-and-subjective hostile-environment standard without mandatory psychological injury; it does not establish employer attribution.
- **DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers Association:** Preserved for the severity-or-pervasiveness inquiry and its context; proof of an unlawful environment does not dispense with the separate attribution requirement.

No precedent is overruled. The Court resolves neither constructive discharge nor a general affirmative-defense regime.

## Law After Decision

Title VII retains distinct routes through agency attribution and employer negligence. Courts must examine whether delegated authority actually enabled the supervisor's harassment; higher management's lack of notice alone cannot foreclose that route. The hostile-environment threshold remains separate. The legal rule changes the analysis of the employer's Rule 50 judgment, while leaving factual sufficiency, preserved alternative grounds and the appropriate next proceeding to the lower court.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit judgment is vacated on the hostile-environment attribution issue and the case is remanded for application of the corrected agency rule in the proper Rule 50 posture. The damages verdict is not reinstated by this mandate. No new trial is automatically ordered, and the constructive-discharge disposition remains undisturbed. The lower court must decide the preserved issue and any properly remaining grounds; no further Supreme Court filing or retained proceeding is ordered.

## Event

In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer; Seventh Circuit No. 94-3912; 51 F.3d 1293. Argued and decided February 26, 1997, on writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit. The questions concern nationwide issue certification under differing state laws, predominance and superiority including jury protections and settlement pressure, and the exceptional conditions for mandamus review. Manufacturers challenged a common-negligence issue class for hemophiliacs allegedly infected with HIV through blood-clotting concentrates. The Court of Appeals ordered decertification by mandamus; no common verdict had been returned. Review concerns that extraordinary writ, not an ordinary immediate appeal from certification.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the mandamus judgment and remands with instructions to deny the writ petition.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The appellate decertification command falls; the District Court retains ordinary certification authority subject to the substantive and jury limits stated below.

| Writing | Author | Joins | Scope |

## Holdings

All propositions below are adopted in Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight participating Justices. The judgment and each stated controlling proposition are unanimous.

### Extraordinary review of this revisable certification

**Holding and operative rule.** Mandamus requires a clear and indisputable entitlement, no adequate ordinary means of relief and circumstances warranting the extraordinary writ. Serious possible certification error, aggregate exposure and ordinary settlement pressure do not establish those conditions where the trial arrangements remain revisable and eventual named-plaintiff final judgment and appellate review are available, without a demonstrated otherwise-unavoidable loss of jury rights.

**Controlling explanation.** Allied Chemical and Kerr require more than reversible error; Blodgett requires examination of actual ordinary avenues rather than assuming their inadequacy. The appellate court expressly assumed that final judgment for the named plaintiffs would permit review. The certification and trial questions remain open to correction before trial. Digital Equipment and Swint concern ordinary appellate routes, not a categorical bar on mandamus, but reject litigation burdens as a general entitlement to immediate review. Coopers & Lybrand applies that finality discipline to class certification. Estimated exposure and predicted settlement cannot make every consequential class action extraordinary. Twelve earlier defense verdicts neither adjudicate these remaining claims nor establish that the earlier cases had identical law and proof. The manufacturers' strongest objection is the jury right, not expense. Beacon Theatres and Dairy Queen permit protection before a jury entitlement is destroyed. Here, however, no jury has returned a common verdict and no fixed unavoidable sequence establishes the feared deprivation. A definitively ordered inseparable scheme with no adequate ordinary protection could present a different case; parties need not invariably suffer a completed violation before requesting focused relief.

### Certification and jury limits remain binding

**Holding and operative rule.** Reversing this writ does not approve the nationwide negligence plan: any continued issue certification must satisfy the applicable Rule 23 requirements under the actual governing state laws and preserve genuine defenses and jury functions. Material legal equivalence, lawful groupings or genuinely separable issues must be demonstrated; management convenience cannot supply an average national negligence rule or make a later federal jury redecide facts committed to the first.

**Controlling explanation.** Castano requires a concrete account of governing law and anticipated proof, while rejecting tort novelty and ordinary aggregation pressure as independent prohibitions. Rule 23(c)(4)(A) permits appropriate treatment of particular issues, but the Rules Enabling Act does not permit alteration of substantive rights. Erie, Klaxon and O'Melveny require the governing state-law inquiry, and Gasperini preserves substantive state rules together with lawful federal allocation. Foreseeability, professional custom and comparative fault may materially change what a common negligence answer means. Gasoline Products requires distinct issues where separate federal juries are used; a label separating common negligence from individual causation or comparative fault cannot establish that distinction. Genuinely independent historical facts may remain suitable for common treatment. Matsushita and Richards separately preserve adequate representation and a lawful basis for binding absent persons. The Court supplies no replacement trial plan, approves no existing instruction and decides neither the negligence claims nor the full relationship between action-wide predominance and issue certification reserved in Castano. The appellate command is reversed because the extraordinary-remedy showing fails, not because these continuing restrictions disappear.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Kerr v. United States District Court; Allied Chemical Corp. v. Daiflon; and Mallard v. United States District Court:** Applied to preserve the extraordinary writ's clear-entitlement, inadequate-alternative and discretionary requirements.
- **In re Blodgett:** Applied to require concrete examination of ordinary relief; no universal reconsideration prerequisite or unproved omission is inferred.
- **Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay; Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct; and Swint v. Chambers County Commission:** Their finality rules remain intact; settlement pressure and litigation burdens do not create ordinary immediate appeal, and Swint's distinct mandamus reservation remains operative.
- **Beacon Theatres v. Westover and Dairy Queen v. Wood:** Preserved for protection of an established jury entitlement before its otherwise-unavoidable loss; not confined to completed violations.
- **Gasoline Products v. Champlin Refining:** Applied to require genuinely separable successive-jury issues.
- **Castano v. American Tobacco Co.:** Its demonstrated law-and-proof requirements and rejection of categorical tort-maturation and settlement-pressure barriers remain binding; its reserved action-wide-predominance question remains open.
- **Erie Railroad v. Tompkins; Klaxon v. Stentor; and O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC:** Applied against replacing governing state duties with a synthetic federal negligence standard.
- **Gasperini v. Center for Humanities:** Its distinction between state substantive rules and lawful federal judicial allocation is preserved; its excessiveness holding is not converted into a class-trial rule.
- **Matsushita Electric Industrial v. Epstein and Richards v. Jefferson County:** Their independent representation and binding-authority protections remain applicable; notice does not substitute for adequate representation.

No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

A revisable class-certification plan remains subject to rigorous Rule 23, state-law and jury requirements. Those requirements do not themselves eliminate the distinct extraordinary-writ threshold. This decision creates neither a general class-action immunity from mandamus nor an immediate-review entitlement based on aggregate stakes; focused protection remains possible when an otherwise-unavoidable jury deprivation is actually demonstrated.

## Procedure After Action

The Seventh Circuit's mandamus judgment is reversed. On remand it must deny this writ petition, removing its command to decertify. The District Court retains its ordinary authority and duty to revise certification and trial arrangements consistently with Rule 23, governing state law and the jury right. No class is independently certified here, no negligence claim or individual judgment is adjudicated, and no common verdict or damages award exists to reinstate. No automatic stay, replacement plan, preclusion ruling or new filing deadline is ordered.
