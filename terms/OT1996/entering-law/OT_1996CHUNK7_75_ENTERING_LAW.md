## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. As of this date the Court has not adopted a general rule governing remote historical movement in every statute.

**Authority by component:**

- Architecture; connection before magnitude; recognition of necessary inclusion; no-insertion rule: United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.
- Economic vehicle-taking class and enacted vehicle nexus: United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995; O’Connor’s eight-Justice commerce rationale. Stone concurs in the judgment on the narrower direct-instrumentality/vehicle-specific nexus ground.
- Direct interference with an interstate service market: American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995; Kennedy Part I, unanimous.
- Application of Harris without new doctrine: United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, February 5, 1996; O’Connor Part II, unanimous.
- Anti-evasion/material-impairment rule for the post-1986 machinegun regime: United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996; O’Connor Part I, unanimous.

**Present operation effective:** Hale, February 12, 1996, for the latest application and the anti-evasion/material-impairment branch; the controlling architecture dates to Lopez, April 26, 1995.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. The Court has not yet adopted a general material-interstate-externality doctrine. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Hale preserves § 922(o)'s government-related channels and prior-lawful pre-ban stock. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Material development:**

- April 26, 1995 — Lopez: ADDED / CHANGED. Added the controlling decision sequence of regulated class → interstate mechanism → magnitude/aggregation → fit/boundary. Changed the prior substantial-effects law only to the extent necessary to reject aggregation of generalized downstream consequences as a substitute for the missing Commerce connection. Preserved Wickard and Perez within their genuine economic-class settings. Reserved fuller Necessary-and-Proper inclusion questions.
- April 27, 1995 — Harris: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that Lopez does not disable direct regulation of an economic vehicle-taking class connected to an interstate motor-vehicle regime when the statute separately requires a particular-vehicle interstate nexus. Stone’s narrower concurrence emphasizes direct protection of the transportation instrumentality. No general remote-movement rule is added.
- November 13, 1995 — American Life League: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that intentional obstruction directed at provision or receipt of services can be regulated when it directly interferes with an actual interstate service market. This is not downstream crime-cost aggregation.
- February 5, 1996 — Bishop/Stokes: APPLIED ONLY. Applied Harris. No new Commerce rule.
- February 12, 1996 — Hale: ADDED. Added the first operational anti-evasion/material-impairment doctrine. Intrastate possession may be included where excluding it would materially undermine or permit evasion of a valid federal interstate market regime. This development uses the Necessary and Proper Clause together with Commerce authority. Hale does not create a general firearm possession power.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Lopez; United States v. Harris; American Life League v. Reno; United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, judgment below 66 F.3d 569; United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016.

### Commerce power and armed vehicle taking

#### United States v. Harris

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-297.
**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Criminal Procedure — Armed vehicle taking and cumulative firearm punishment (Knowing participation and intentional assistance give the original offense definite content; Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking; Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution).

##### The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection

**Holding:** Congress may regulate the armed forcible acquisition of the covered vehicle proved here as an economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme, while original §2119's enacted requirement that the particular vehicle have been transported, shipped, or received in interstate or foreign commerce must be proved. Neither remote movement alone nor generalized crime, insurance, or productivity costs independently supplies this holding's constitutional ground. The decision creates no rival commerce test and no automatic instrumentality rule beyond these facts and the enacted nexus.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's commerce portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a narrower ground: he would sustain the application because the offense directly attacks possession and control of a covered motor vehicle functioning as an interstate transportation instrumentality or thing in commerce, and because the statute requires proof of the particular vehicle's interstate connection. He does not rely on remote historical movement alone.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: applies its controlling connection-before-magnitude architecture for the first time, in a direct vehicle/instrumentality and economic vehicle-taking setting; the economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme supplies the connection, and Lopez's inadequate stand-alone possession/downstream-effects chain is distinguished; no missing element is supplied.
- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111 (1942): applies aggregation within a regulated economic class rather than demanding a substantial effect from each individual taking.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146 (1971): applies Congress's authority over the economic class despite an individual instance's local character.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: preserves its distinction between actual commercial operation and remote object history; prior manufacture alone is not the constitutional ground here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both convictions and each defendant's forty-one-month Count One term plus consecutive sixty-month Count Two term are affirmed on all three presented challenges. No merger, vacatur, concurrent conversion, sentence increase, resentencing, new trial or exploratory remand is ordered.

### Commerce power and school-zone firearm possession

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. The interstate-externality reservation is unchanged.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

### Federal regulation of economic activity

#### United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, judgment below 66 F.3d 569

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit Nos. 94-5321 and 94-5387; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** February 5, 1996.

##### Harris sustains application of original §2119 to both defendants

**Holding:** Congress may apply original §2119 to the armed vehicle taking at issue as regulation of the economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme. The separately enacted requirement that the particular vehicle have been transported, shipped or received in interstate or foreign commerce remains necessary; remote vehicle history alone and generalized crime, insurance or productivity costs do not supply this holding's constitutional ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic vehicle-instrumentality theory, new Commerce Clause test or unpresented sufficiency determination is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris is applied within its economic-class and enacted-element limits without creating a new Commerce Clause rule; the decision does not enlarge Lopez or the instrumentality route. Lopez retains its connection-before-magnitude architecture and its rejection of the remote downstream-effects theory; Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope; American Life League's distinct direct market-interference ground does not change §2119's text.

#### United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** February 12, 1996.

##### Congress may include possession in this regulation of the machinegun market

**Holding:** Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market, subject to the subsection's government-related and prior-lawful-possession exceptions, when possession and transfer are sufficiently integrated that excluding possession would permit acquisition, retention, concealment, or continued circulation of the prohibited stock and thereby materially impair enforcement of the valid transfer/stock regime. The relationship is one of anti-evasion and material impairment under the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, and it defeats the presented commerce challenge without a transaction-specific interstate element. General gun violence, fear, insurance cost, marketability alone, or remote interstate history does not supply the ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 922(o)(2) excepts transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions; it separately excepts lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Earlier manufacture alone does not satisfy that second exception. No count is newly found within either exception.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lopez is clarified and developed: Lopez reserved intrastate inclusion genuinely necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime, and this decision supplies the first operational rule for that route. Wickard and Perez are preserved for economic-class aggregation. Harris, Bishop/Stokes and American Life League rest on distinct mechanisms; none is converted into the source of Hale's anti-evasion rule, and no other statute's interstate element is transferred into §922(o).

### RICO enterprise commerce

#### United States v. Robertson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-251.
**Decided:** May 1, 1995.

##### The mine’s own interstate operations satisfy the enacted enterprise-commerce condition

**Holding:** An enterprise is engaged in interstate commerce under §1962(a) when it directly participates in interstate acquisition, production or distribution of goods or services. This mine’s actual cross-border procurement, repeated interstate recruitment and mine-work movement, together with its proprietor’s carriage out of Alaska of approximately $30,000 of its own output, establish that connection without a separate showing that this individual mine substantially affected interstate commerce.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. American Building Maintenance Industries, 422 U.S. 271, 283–286 (1975): applies its distinction between direct interstate goods or services transactions and local purchases after interstate movement has ended; its Clayton Act setting does not erase RICO’s separate affecting-commerce alternative.
- Gulf Oil Corp. v. Copp Paving Co., 419 U.S. 186, 195 (1974): applies its direct-engagement distinction; local economic effects and direct participation remain different inquiries.
- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: distinguishes the stand-alone school-possession offense and generalized social-cost chain from this proved interstate enterprise connection; its controlling connection-before-magnitude architecture remains intact.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: preserves attention to actual multistate commercial operation and its reserved constitutional ceiling; the FAA’s broader involving-commerce wording is not substituted for §1962(a).
- United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995: preserves the economic-class vehicle-taking holding and refusal to make remote vehicle history alone conclusive; this decision instead concerns the enterprise’s own present interstate operations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The cumulative ground makes an independently sufficient procurement-only holding unnecessary. The Court does not decide whether a purely intrastate enterprise with only remote economic consequences satisfies §1962(a), establish a universal owner-activity attribution rule, or make a jurisdictional phrase substitute for proof. Predicate drug commerce alone is not enterprise commerce. Neither an out-of-state nugget sale, a precise output percentage, productive use of the wrecked Cadillac, mine use of the airplane, nor traced downstream refinery shipment is necessary or found. The Court decides no other RICO appellate claim, Guidelines issue or claim arising under a different RICO subsection.

Section 1962(a)’s separate income, participation and investment conditions remain intact. Its securities qualification continues to exclude an open-market investment purchase made without intent to control or participate in control of the issuer or assist another in doing so, when the purchaser’s, immediate family’s and racketeering or unlawful-debt accomplices’ aggregate holdings after purchase are less than one percent of any outstanding class and confer no power, in law or fact, to elect a director. That qualification is not implicated by ownership and operation of this mine.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's commerce-based reversal of Count Six is reversed and remanded. Reinstatement remains subject to preserved special-verdict, venue, severance, Agent Devetko testimony, Cadillac-owner-location funding and vagueness claims, plus the unresolved post-November-1-1987 Guidelines argument. Counts One–Four convictions and their separate actual-resentencing mandate remain undisturbed, as do the acquittals on Five and Eight and forfeiture dismissal on Seven. No unconditional RICO conviction or sentence reinstatement is directed.

### 1997-02-18 - General Motors Corp. v. Tracy; No. 95-1232.

Authority: [Record](../records/General_Motors_v_Tracy_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The assessed purchaser may bring this challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A purchaser directly liable for a tax has standing to challenge an exemption that allegedly burdens its interstate purchases. The adverse assessment supplies the purchaser's own economic injury, caused by the challenged tax rule and redressable through lawful relief from that assessment, whether or not payment has been established.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** General Motors contests a charge imposed on its own purchases. Its injury does not become another seller's claim merely because the tax also affects competing suppliers. Bacchus recognizes a taxed purchaser's ability to challenge a discriminatory exemption; Fulton likewise examines the taxpayer's own liability although the distinction concerns another market participant. Relief from an unlawful assessment would remove the asserted burden. Ohio's seller-only objection therefore fails. Standing establishes access to review, not the truth of the claimed comparison between the sellers. The Court does not find an assessment amount or prior payment and orders no refund on the strength of standing alone.

**Precedent treatment:** Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias supplies purchaser standing; Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner confirms that the claimant may contest its own tax burden without being the disfavored seller. Their merits and remedial boundaries remain.

##### II. This record does not establish the asserted discrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio's exemption for sales by regulated local distribution companies does not violate the Commerce Clause on this record, because their compulsory rate, reliability and service obligations materially distinguish their regulated supply business from independent marketers' commodity sales. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence of those offerings where the record does not establish a distinct industrial-sales category separated from the obligations relied upon; the holding confers no general immunity on utilities or all of their competitive transactions.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Stevens do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must examine the practical competitive relationship, rather than accept a utility label or the fact that local marketers also pay the tax. West Lynn requires examination of an integrated fiscal preference; Carbone requires attention to the actual service market. Here the favored sellers must maintain regulated supply and meet reliability and human-needs obligations rather than select only profitable customers. Those duties materially affect their offering. General Motors bought virtually all its gas from interstate marketers and often obtained local delivery, but the record gives little detail about the industrial market and does not establish the asserted unregulated negotiated LDC sales during the assessment period. The common gas commodity does not by itself remove the established service differences.

The objection that duties in a captive market cannot justify preference in a separate competitive market would require considering such a supported separation. This record does not establish it. The Court neither finds that every industrial sale funds a particular public duty nor presumes that any alternative would endanger service. The 1996 enactment cannot prove the character of 1986–1990 purchases. Oregon Waste and Fulton continue to require demanding justification and actual tax equivalence once discrimination is established; no compensatory defense is adopted here. A materially different proved sales category remains for its own case. No independent incidental-burden balancing claim or congressional-authorization theory is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** West Lynn's integrated-operation rule and Carbone's practical service-market inquiry apply without categorical utility immunity. Oregon Waste's discrimination-and-justification rule and Fulton's cumulative compensatory conditions remain unchanged; the Court does not reach justification after rejecting the asserted comparison.

##### III. The economic classification has a rational basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio may distinguish regulated local distribution companies from independent natural-gas marketers under ordinary economic equal-protection review because the companies' actual rate and service duties rationally relate to the tax classification. Competitive overlap does not eliminate that legitimate distinction, and this rational-basis holding does not reduce the independent Commerce Clause requirements.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Stevens reserve this question on their proposed Commerce Clause dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits an ordinary economic classification when a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis supports it, without requiring perfect fit or formal legislative findings. Ohio's rate, reliability and service obligations supply such a basis here. An industrial buyer's opportunity to purchase from either class does not make it irrational to distinguish the regulated service business from independent marketers. The Court does not equate rationality with proof that utility taxes exactly offset the challenged charge. Nor does the Court attribute this analysis to the Ohio court, which treated equal protection as submerged in its Commerce Clause discussion. The ordinary economic challenge fails on this separate ground; independent constitutional prohibitions retain their force.

**Precedent treatment:** FCC v. Beach Communications supplies the ordinary economic-classification rule and its limits; it does not displace Commerce Clause scrutiny.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias: applied to the assessed purchaser's own standing; local-product tax preferences remain subject to its substantive rule.
- West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy: its examination of integrated fiscal operation remains controlling; the present record differs in the established obligations of the compared service businesses.
- C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown: its practical service-market comparison is applied; neither a nominal utility classification nor equal taxation of local outsiders is sufficient by itself.
- Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality: the discrimination trigger and demanding justification survive unchanged; no new public-service exception is created.
- Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner: preserves all three cumulative compensatory-tax requirements and lawful fact-dependent relief; no exact compensation or payment is found here.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc.: supplies ordinary economic rational-basis review, separately from the Commerce Clause.

#### Law After Decision

The regulated service differences defeat the particular Commerce Clause comparison established here; no universal utility exemption or new evidentiary presumption follows. The economic classification independently survives equal protection. A taxpayer may challenge its own assessment even when the alleged competitive discrimination affects suppliers. The existing discrimination, compensation and conditional tax-remedy rules remain in force, and a materially different proved industrial-sales category is unresolved.


### 1997-03-19 — Bennett v. Spear; No. 95-813.

Authority: [Record](../records/Bennett_v_Spear_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is directly joined by all nine Justices. That common coalition controls every proposition below.

###### Petitioners adequately plead their own constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Irrigators alleging concrete loss of their project water from a Biological Opinion that the operating agency has announced it will follow adequately plead injury, fair traceability and likely redress from removal of the challenged constraint. They must support standing with the evidence required at each later litigation stage; statutory permission alone never replaces Article III injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires a concrete personal interest and proof appropriate to the stage, but its three-Justice absent-agency discussion supplies no categorical rule defeating this claim. The alleged loss is the petitioners’ own, and the Bureau’s announced compliance links the Service’s completed action to that threatened injury. The Bureau’s operational authority does not make its response unpredictable as a matter of law or require certainty that every eventual choice will change. Setting aside the challenged constraint can afford concrete relief. Humane Society’s missing connection between a later permit amendment and an already completed transfer is absent here. Tracy recognizes cognizable economic injury without making every economic claimant eligible to sue. Arizonans likewise requires an actual continuing controversy; no later fact eliminating this alleged injury is established. Merits uncertainty about the biological conclusion is not a pleading-stage standing defect.

###### The citizen-suit language is broad within its actual channels

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1540(g)’s authorization to any person removes an additional species-preservation zone-of-interests restriction for claims that its particular channels authorize. Section (g)(1)(C) permits the alleged failure to perform a nondiscretionary §1533 duty, including the pleaded failure to consider economic effects of an asserted de facto habitat designation; §(g)(1)(A) does not authorize general supervision of the Secretary’s alleged §1536 maladministration.

**Controlling explanation:** The breadth of the plaintiff language cannot be restricted by an unwritten environmental preference, but it does not erase the enacted division among person-in-violation actions, emergency enforcement and Secretary-duty suits. Treating every alleged administrative error as a violation under (A) would disregard (C)’s specific limit to nondiscretionary §1533 acts. Petitioners may pursue the asserted §1533(b)(2) consideration duty through (C) at this threshold stage; the Court does not find that a designation occurred or triggered a duty. The §1536 consultation claims require a different review route.

When designation or revision occurs, §1533(b)(2) requires consideration of economic and other relevant impacts. Exclusion of an area is discretionary when exclusion benefits outweigh inclusion benefits, and remains forbidden if the best scientific and commercial data show that failure to designate will result in extinction. Reviewability commands neither a particular exclusion nor wholesale economic balancing during §1536 consultation. The (B) emergency channel is not invoked. Article III remains independently necessary.

**Notice and enforcement limits:** The (A) route generally requires 60 days’ written notice to the Secretary and alleged violator; a commenced civil-penalty action or a commenced, diligently prosecuted United States criminal action addressing the violation bars it. The (B) route separately requires 60 days’ notice explaining the claimed emergency and is barred by the Secretary’s commenced diligent emergency-determination action. The (C) route requires 60 days’ notice to the Secretary, except that suit concerning an emergency posing significant risk to the well-being of a fish, wildlife or plant species may follow notification immediately. No actual compliance, applicable emergency or enforcement bar is found or waived here.

###### The consultation claims have an APA route and a provision-specific interest

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed §1536 consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels provide no other adequate judicial remedy for these administrative claims. Petitioners’ alleged injury from restrictions unsupported by the best scientific and commercial data is at least arguably within the interests protected by §1536(a)(2)’s data requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** Review must concern the provision sued upon, not an assumption that every ESA provision protects only species or that every economically affected person may enforce every conservation duty. Requiring the best available scientific and commercial data serves sound decisionmaking and protects affected interests against restrictions unsupported by that information. The Court does not replace agency scientific judgment with independent judicial biology or find the data claim correct. Section 1540(g)(5) preserves other available rights but does not itself establish every APA prerequisite. Dalton’s indivisible base-closure package, deadlines and confined review scheme justified preclusion there; no comparable structure is shown here. Its noncontrolling recommendation-finality theory is not a general bar to agency review.

**Retained APA limits:** Section 702 addresses relief other than money damages and preserves other legal and equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. Any mandatory or injunctive decree must identify by name or title the responsible federal officers and their successors. Section 704 requires final action and no other adequate court remedy. Preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, otherwise final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal does not postpone finality unless an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. This case grants no decree and establishes no unproved exhaustion or review prerequisite.

###### The Biological Opinion is completed action with legal consequences

**Holding and operative rule:** This Biological Opinion is final agency action because it completes the Service’s consultation determination and its incidental-take statement carries legal consequences: compliance with its terms changes prohibited-taking liability under §1536(o)(2). The Bureau’s retained operational discretion does not convert that completed and consequential action into tentative advice.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin requires examination of a completed subordinate agency determination even though a later governmental step remains. The Service has completed its assigned consultation here, and the statement conditions protection from taking liability on compliance with its requirements. Practical influence and announced Bureau compliance support causation, but legal consequences supply the distinct finality ground. The Court does not hold every advisory report, prediction or preliminary analysis final, nor does it decide Franklin’s reserved presidential-status question.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Section 1536(a)(2) requires avoidance of likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat using the best scientific and commercial data available, unless the Committee has granted an exemption under (h); none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must conclude that the action will not violate (a)(2), or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed not to violate it, and conclude that incidental taking will not violate that subsection. For a marine mammal, taking also requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The resulting statement specifies the taking’s impact, reasonable and prudent minimization measures, any required marine-mammal measures, and implementing terms including reporting. Section (o)(2) protects only taking that complies with those terms; it is not unconditional immunity from every ESA duty. The separate exemption-based protection in (o)(1) is not the source of this statement’s effect. No marine-mammal finding is needed for these fish.

The Court reserves the statutory and scientific merits, whether a designation or nondiscretionary duty was actually triggered, the applicable standard of review’s merits application, any properly remaining threshold issue and final relief. The NEPA dismissal remains outside the reviewed questions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: applies personal injury, causation, redressability and litigation-stage proof; its citizen-suit limit remains, and the noncontrolling absent-agency application is not expanded.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts: applies its controlling reviewability of completed subordinate agency action despite a later governmental step; no presidential-status question is decided.
- Dalton v. Specter: its statutory-scheme preclusion holding is distinguished; the four-Justice recommendation-finality theory supplies no binding rule.
- Humane Society of the United States v. Babbitt: its missing causal and remedial link is distinguished by the pleaded Bureau response and continuing water constraint here.
- General Motors Corp. v. Tracy: preserves cognizable personal economic injury without dispensing with the remaining standing requirements.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona: requires an actual continuing controversy and a legally available claim and remedy; no analogous loss of this alleged injury is established.

#### Law After Decision

Economic interests do not disqualify an otherwise injured plaintiff from authorized ESA citizen suits. Each claim must still fit its actual statutory channel; the §1536 consultation claims use the APA and its provision-specific interests and finality requirements. The completed Biological Opinion’s conditional taking consequences establish finality here. These reviewability holdings establish no biological error or entitlement to particular water operations, and leave Article III proof, statutory notice and all retained review limits intact.


### 1997-02-18 - General Motors Corp. v. Tracy; No. 95-1232.

Authority: [Record](../records/General_Motors_v_Tracy_merits_1997-02-18.md).

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Part I on General Motors's own assessment injury. He would reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the tax on the competitive commodity-gas purchases and remand for nondiscriminatory treatment. His comparison focuses on the transaction bought by a noncaptive industrial customer: LDCs and interstate marketers can compete to supply that commodity even though LDCs also serve captive customers. West Lynn's examination of the actual fiscal preference and Oregon Waste's rule against unjustified discrimination require a connection between the favored transaction and the service burden said to justify it. In his view, a blanket seller exemption lacks that connection on this record. This is a disagreement over the legal significance of the established competitive commodity purchase, not a finding that particular unregulated special contracts existed. He would preserve local-distribution regulation and fitted support for documented backup, low-income, credit or distribution obligations through nondiscriminatory means, including a neutral subsidy, cost-based credit or exemption confined to regulated bundled service. State courts would select lawful consequent tax relief, including a refund only where actual payment and governing remedial law support it, or a lawful neutral adjustment. He would not decide equal protection.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Part I but would vacate and remand the Commerce Clause merits for an actual industrial-market comparison. West Lynn requires examination of how the linked fiscal scheme operates; Carbone prevents equal treatment of local and interstate outsiders from curing a preference for a favored local service. Regulated obligations are material, but in his view do not justify closing the inquiry into sales competing for noncaptive buyers. He would not find unregulated contracts, calculate cross-subsidies or conclusively invalidate the exemption on this sparse record. If the comparison establishes discrimination, Ohio must satisfy the existing justification rule; any compensatory defense must meet Fulton's actual-burden, approximate-equivalence and equivalent-events requirements. He would reserve equal protection and order neither a particular subsidy nor a predetermined refund.

## Prior public continuity

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, dissents. Congress could rationally find that the class of school gun possession substantially affects national commerce through disrupted education, workforce preparation and travel. Wickard and Perez require attention to the class rather than treating one defendant’s effect as independently substantial. The dissent would retain judicial review, a substantial class relationship and reasonable means, while deferring to the legislative judgment presented here. It rejects a universal findings prerequisite and does not equate every imaginable consequence with a substantial commercial effect. In its view, the Court discounts the practical national connection by treating the local and noncommercial features of possession as insufficiently connected to the class-wide effects. Safe schools’ desirability alone and the gun’s remote interstate manufacture are not its grounds. The dissent reads the original offense without a new nexus element; on that reading the indictment omits no required commerce allegation. It would reverse the constitutional dismissal and remand for the unresolved supervised-release and special-assessment objections, not approve the entire sentence. Its application is noncontrolling.

### 18 U.S.C. 1301 (1994)

Whoever brings into the United States for the purpose of disposing of the same, or knowingly deposits with any express company or other common carrier for carriage, or carries in interstate or foreign commerce any paper, certificate, or instrument purporting to be or to represent a ticket, chance, share, or interest in or dependent upon the event of a lottery, gift enterprise, or similar scheme, offering prizes dependent in whole or in part upon lot or chance, or any advertisement of, or list of the prizes drawn or awarded by means of, any such lottery, gift, enterprise, or similar scheme; or, being engaged in the business of procuring for a person in 1 State such a ticket, chance, share, or interest in a lottery, gift, enterprise or similar scheme conducted by another State (unless that business is permitted under an agreement between the States in question or appropriate authorities of those States), knowingly transmits in interstate or foreign commerce information to be used for the purpose of procuring such a ticket, chance, share, or interest; or knowingly takes or receives any such paper, certificate, instrument, advertisement, or list so brought, deposited, or transported, shall be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than two years, or both.
