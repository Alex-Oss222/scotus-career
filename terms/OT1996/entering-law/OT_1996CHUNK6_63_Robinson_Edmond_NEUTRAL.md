### Issue 1: Christian cross in the official city seal

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687 (1994), prohibits special allocation of civil authority by religious identity. Capitol Square v. Pinette and Chabad-Lubavitch protect clearly private religious expression in genuinely open forums, while emphasizing attribution, control, ownership, and context.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Private religious expression in an open government forum does not govern a symbol the government selected and uses as its own. Religion-neutral allocation of civil authority and the attribution standards confirm that governmental authorship and control are central.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause; County of Allegheny v. ACLU; Lemon v. Kurtzman to the extent surviving; municipal government-speech principles.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Application of private-attribution limits

Pinette’s seven-Justice access holding rejects categorical exclusion of a genuinely private display; its comprehensive contextual explanation had only four votes. Chabad separately has a five-Justice rule that actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference, and obscured sponsorship matter to attribution. Edmond selected the cross as part of the City’s own seal and repeatedly used it to authenticate official acts. That government adoption distinguishes this record from access for a privately identified speaker, without turning Kiryas Joel’s allocation-of-civil-authority rule into a general seal test. Rosenberger expressly leaves Lemon in force.

## G. RECONCILED QUESTIONS, LAW, AND AVAILABLE PATHS

The challenged emblem is selected and used by the City to authenticate its own action. This differs from the private-access holdings in Good News, Chabad and Pinette; Pinette’s broader contextual formulation lacked a majority, while Chabad’s five-Justice holding uses actual attribution. Rosenberger’s preservation of Lemon and private-expression holding supplies no immunity for government’s own religious identifier. Historical origin, the secular quadrants, purpose and an informed observer’s context all inform meaning, but none automatically neutralizes a recognizable Christian cross used pervasively as an official seal. The City seeks civic-history treatment; the residents challenge official religious identification, not incidental exposure to private religious speech.
