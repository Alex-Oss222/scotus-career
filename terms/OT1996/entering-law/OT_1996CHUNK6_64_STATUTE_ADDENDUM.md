# Turner II — exact statutory qualifications

Neutral statutory supplement for the March 31, 1997 event. Read with `OT_1996CHUNK6_64_Turner_II_NEUTRAL.md`; this supplies enacted terms, not a constitutional disposition or a resolution of the separately documented appeal-scope dispute.

**Sources and time:** Complete 1996 United States Code text and notes for [47 U.S.C. §534](../sources/chunk6-neutral-control/TURNER_534_1996.txt) and [§535](../sources/chunk6-neutral-control/TURNER_535_1996.txt), copied from the [official 1996 Title 47](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1996-title47/html/USCODE-1996-title47.htm). Both sections originally became effective 60 days after October 5, 1992. Section 534's market-definition and request-decision provisions were amended February 8, 1996; the amendment expressly applies to requests pending then and requests filed afterward. These sources establish statutory commands and permissions, not the status of individual FCC proceedings, implementing regulations, or any appellant's compliance.

## Capacity and compulsory carriage

The commercial and noncommercial educational obligations are distinct: §535(a) applies **in addition to** §534. One-third is not a combined ceiling on both kinds of carriage. Every row remains subject to its section's eligibility and other qualifications below.

| Provision | Operative requirement and qualification |
|---|---|
| §534(b)(1)(A), 12 or fewer usable activated channels | Carry **at least three** local commercial television stations. A system with **300 or fewer subscribers** is exempt from **all requirements of §534 only so long as it does not delete any broadcast television signal from carriage**. This is not an unconditional small-system exemption and is not an exemption from §535. |
| §534(b)(1)(B), more than 12 usable activated channels | Carry local commercial signals **up to one-third of the aggregate usable activated channels**. It is not a command to fill one-third with ineligible stations or a prohibition on voluntary additional carriage. |
| §534(c), insufficient full-power local commercial signals to fill the subsection (b) channels | A system with **35 or fewer** usable activated channels must carry **one qualified low-power station**; one with **more than 35** must carry **two**. This additional requirement depends on the stated full-power shortfall and low-power qualification. Additional low-power carriage beyond the first may use otherwise unused public, educational, or governmental channels with franchising-authority approval. |
| §535(b)(1), general educational rule | Carry **each qualified local noncommercial educational station requesting carriage**, subject to the small- and intermediate-system rules and subsection (e). |
| §535(b)(2), 12 or fewer usable activated channels | Carry **one** qualified local educational station, subject to the continued-carriage rule in subsection (c); additional carriage is permitted. Beyond the presence of any qualified local station, import **one** qualified educational station chosen by the operator. For that importation, the operator need not remove another programming service actually provided on **March 29, 1990**, but **must use the first channel available**. |
| §535(b)(3), 13–36 usable activated channels | Carry **at least one** qualified local educational station; the operator need not carry **more than three**, subject to subsection (c), and may carry more. If none is local, import at least one qualified educational station. If capacity rises above 36 on or after March 29, 1990, the system must carry each requesting qualified local station under the section's other provisions, including subsection (e). |
| §535(b)(1), (e), more than 36 usable activated channels | The general each-requesting-qualified-local-station rule applies. A system required to carry three such stations need not carry **additional** stations whose programming substantially duplicates another qualified local educational station **requesting carriage**. This is not a general three-station cap. |

**Continued educational carriage:** Notwithstanding the other provisions of §535, subsection (c) requires continued carriage of **all qualified local educational stations carried as of March 29, 1990**. Waiver for a particular operator and station requires **both parties' written consent**. The capacity numbers alone do not authorize deletion of those stations. Separately, §535(g)(4) states, also notwithstanding the section's other provisions, that carriage is not required without delivery of a good-quality or baseband video signal to the principal headend. Preserve both enacted clauses; do not silently discard the signal condition or invent an adjudicated conflict between them.

## Selection and duplication are different rules

Under §534(b)(2), when eligible local commercial stations exceed the required maximum, the operator selects among them, but cannot substitute a qualified low-power station for a local commercial station. When it elects a network affiliate, it must carry that network's affiliate whose city-of-license reference point is closest to the principal headend.

Section 534(b)(5) does **not require** carriage of a local commercial signal substantially duplicating another local commercial signal **already carried**, or more than one affiliate of a particular network. Either form of additional carriage remains permitted, and **all voluntarily carried duplicate signals or multiple affiliates count toward the paragraph (1) number**. This permission to omit is not a prohibition on carriage.

Educational duplication is narrower and differentiated. For **13–36-channel systems**, §535(b)(3)(C) permits omission of an additional affiliate of the **same State public television network only if** its programming substantially duplicates that of the affiliate receiving carriage. For **over-36-channel systems**, §535(e) has the separate additional-station rule in the table; its reference is another qualified local educational station **requesting carriage**, not an invented already-carried limitation. FCC's definition of substantial duplication must promote access to distinctive educational services. Under §535(f), a carried local educational station cannot use network nonduplication rights to require deletion of programs on another carried local educational station.

## Eligibility and the reserved components

Commercial eligibility under §534(h)(1) concerns full-power, noneducational stations regularly licensed to their community in the system's television market. Low-power stations, translators, and passive repeaters are excluded from that definition. A distant signal is excluded unless the station agrees to indemnify increased copyright liability. A station below the specified principal-headend levels (**−45 dBm UHF; −49 dBm VHF**) is excluded if it does not agree to bear the costs of delivering a good-quality or baseband signal.

**Market modification is not low-power qualification.** Under §534(h)(1)(C)(i), the Commission determines markets by regulation or order using available commercial publications describing viewing patterns. On written request, it **may** include or exclude communities for a particular station to effectuate the section and may place a community in more than one market. It must pay particular attention to localism, considering historical carriage; the station's coverage or other local service; **whether any other eligible television station supplies community-concern news or community-interest sports and other events**; and cable and noncable viewing patterns. The third factor concerns **another eligible station**, not a statutory requirement that the requesting station itself provide that specified programming. The exact text controls over the shorthand descriptions in the lower opinion and advocacy excerpts. During a market proceeding an operator may not delete a commercial signal. The Commission must grant or deny a request within 120 days of filing, or 120 days after February 8, 1996 if later. The 1996 amendment replaced the former market-definition reference and the earlier general expedited-consideration clause; do not present those superseded provisions as the current text.

**Qualified low-power status**, §534(h)(2), requires all the listed conditions: minimum operating hours; compliance with the specified nonentertainment, political/election/controversial-issue/editorial/personal-attack, children's programming and equal-employment obligations; and an FCC determination that the station's programming would address local news and information needs inadequately served by full-power stations **because of their geographic distance**. It also requires interference compliance consistent with secondary status; location within 35 miles of the headend and a good-quality over-the-air signal; both community and franchise area outside the 160 largest metropolitan statistical areas as ranked June 30, 1990 and community population no greater than 35,000 then; and no full-power station licensed to any community in the county or other state political subdivision served. Secondary status remains unchanged. The news-and-information qualification cannot be transferred wholesale into the distinct market-modification factor.

Educational qualification follows §535(l)(1)'s license/ownership and public-broadcasting-grant-eligibility route or its municipal-ownership/predominantly-educational-programming route, with the specified translator and reserved-channel inclusions. A qualified station is **local** if its principal-community reference point is within 50 miles of the principal headend **or** its Grade B contour encompasses that headend, §535(l)(2). Under §535(i)(2), an operator need not **add** a distant educational signal absent copyright-cost indemnification, but this exception expressly excludes stations **already carried under subsection (c)**; it does not erase continued carriage.

## Other terms bearing on the burden

- **What is carried:** Section 534(b)(3) requires the entire primary video, accompanying audio, and line-21 captions, plus technically feasible program-related blanking-interval or subcarrier material; other material is discretionary. Where appropriate and feasible, enhancements may be removed and used at the headend. The entire program schedule is required unless the identified exclusivity regulations prohibit the particular programming and authorize substitution. Section 535(g)(1) separately requires primary video/audio/captions and technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education, or language purposes; other ancillary material is discretionary. Neither section merely reserves an empty channel.
- **Quality and placement:** Commercial signals cannot be materially degraded and receive the statutory technically feasible quality comparison, §534(b)(4). Educational signals receive commercial-equivalent bandwidth/technical capacity without material degradation, §535(g)(2). Commercial stations may elect their over-the-air, July 19, 1985, or January 1, 1992 channel; educational stations have the over-the-air or July 19, 1985 choices. Both permit an agreed alternative and FCC dispute resolution. Do not import the commercial January 1992 option into §535.
- **Availability and changes:** Commercial required signals reach every subscriber under §534(b)(7)'s receiver-connection provisions; its limited subscriber-installed-connection situation requires converter information and an offer of sale or lease. Educational required signals reach every subscriber on the lowest-priced tier including local commercial broadcast signals. Commercial deletion/repositioning requires 30 days' written station notice and cannot occur during major audience-measurement periods. Educational repositioning, including deletion, requires 30 days' written notice to the station **and all subscribers**. Notice cannot evade carriage or positioning obligations. Additional educational stations may use unused public, educational, or governmental channels with franchising approval.
- **Payment:** Section 534(b)(10) bars requesting or accepting compensation for mandatory commercial carriage or positioning, subject to delivery costs, distant-signal copyright indemnity, and continuation of a pre-June 26, 1990 agreement only until its expiration. Section 535(i) separately bars accepting carriage compensation except delivery costs and supplies the distinct distant-signal qualification above.
- **Enforcement:** Commercial stations first give written notice; the operator must respond within 30 days. FCC complaint review provides an opportunity to respond and a determination within 120 days; a carriage order requires at least 12 months' carriage. Section 535(j) allows an educational station to complain to FCC, provides an operator response opportunity and a 120-day determination, and requires particular findings and necessary remedial action. It does not copy the commercial preliminary-notice or 12-month-remedy terms. Compliance requires dismissal under either section.
- **Conditional provisions:** Section 534(e) eliminates a requirement to supply input-selector switches or information about them. Section 534(g)'s sales-presentation rule applies **pending** the specified FCC proceeding; its outcome governs qualification. This statutory text alone does not establish that the proceeding remained pending in March 1997 or that every such station was exempt then. Similarly, §534(b)(4)(B)'s advanced-television proceeding is triggered by modified broadcast standards; no completed proceeding is inferred from the command.

**Effect on the neutral premises:** The existing general description of limited compulsory carriage is confirmed but was insufficient to state these exceptions. No contrary categorical capacity rule was identified in the clean packet. The market-factor distinction is material precision if that reserved component is reached: earlier source accounts must not be substituted for the enacted **other-station** factor. Nothing here resolves preservation, the scope of the actual grant, constitutional scrutiny or fit, any party's injury, or the modeled judgment. Those questions remain within the already identified paths on the actual record and entering law.
