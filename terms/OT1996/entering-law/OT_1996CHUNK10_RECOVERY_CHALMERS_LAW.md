## Court, participation, allotments and standing practice


### 1997-03-17 — Brown v. Polk County; Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313, 61 F.3d 650 (en banc); no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Polk_County_merits_1997-03-17.md).

#### Holdings

All eight Associate Justices directly join Parts I–IV of the Court’s opinion. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also joins Parts I, II and IV. Part III’s same-decision and closed-liability reasons therefore command eight votes; the other holdings command nine. No proposition rests on aggregation of separate writings.

###### Part I — Religion-specific commands exceed the demonstrated justification

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer may enforce lawful controls on work, resources, coercion and official religious activity, but a religion-specific prohibition extending beyond those concerns into private religious expression and personal religious possession must satisfy the constitutional justification its discrimination demands. The undifferentiated witnessing, proselytizing and counseling command and removal of all religious items, including a private desk Bible, fail that inquiry at their demonstrated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith preserves neutral rules, while Lukumi requires justification and tailoring for religious targeting. Preventing official worship, supervisory pressure, favoritism and diversion of paid resources supplies legitimate and potentially compelling concerns. Those concerns do not justify suppressing religious possession without an established operational consequence or every act described in religious terms. Narrower lawful controls cannot save the overreaching directives actually issued.

The supervisor setting matters. Voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayers are not automatically private, and nominal voluntariness cannot erase coercive pressure or government adoption. The Court need not recognize a general constitutional right to organize worship during department meetings to decide the orders’ excess. Pickering, Connick and Givhan retain the actual employment-speech inquiry. NTEU addresses broad burdens on protected citizen expression without converting supervisory instructions into citizen speech; Waters concerns genuine reliance on a disciplinable account, not blanket religious suppression. Pinette establishes no comprehensive majority test, and Chabad and Good News do not make this office a forum. The free-exercise ground suffices; no new equal-protection tier or constitutional discharge rule is adopted. A Title VII violation does not automatically establish a First Amendment violation.

###### Part II — Accommodation turns on the actual practice and supported burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §2000e(j), the employee must establish the sincere practice, employment conflict, employer knowledge and adverse treatment implicated by the claim; the employer must demonstrate effective reasonable accommodation or inability to accommodate without more than a de minimis hardship under the governing law. Concrete noncash burdens and supported professional predictions count without realized injury, an experiment or an actual complaint; an effective accommodation need not be preferred, and no independent duty to negotiate or try every alternative remains after effective accommodation or sufficient hardship is shown.

**Controlling explanation:** Hardison, Ansonia and Beadle require attention to the practice and actual operational justification. Diverting a paid secretary to Bible-study work supplies a sufficient resource burden. The particular prework office use likewise establishes no protected access: the County treats arrival as starting work and prohibits personal resource use, and no public or limited forum or comparable allowed personal use was shown. That conclusion is not a categorical prohibition of off-clock prayer.

The department-meeting prayers and one Christian work-ethic reference require separate assessment. The testimony described voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayer, no compelled participation, and a single reference. Indirect reports, absence of direct complaints or observed discomfort, testimony denying favoritism and accounts attributing disputes to organization did not connect those practices to the asserted polarization or favoritism burden. Supervisor status remains relevant but is not conclusive proof of coercion. Infrequency is no immunity, and realized disruption is not indispensable; the Court rejects those broader suggestions below and sustains the result because the actual proof fails under the correct predictive standard. The proved cost of secretarial work cannot be exported to every religious act. Knowledge and the absence of attempted accommodation alone do not decide liability, and no new opportunity to invent coercion or retry the completed hardship question is ordered.

###### Part III — The original mixed-motive defense fails on this completed record

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the original Price Waterhouse rule applicable to this pre-1991 conduct, direct decisional evidence that protected religion was a substantial factor actually relied on, proved by a preponderance, shifts to the employer the burden to prove by a preponderance that it would have made the same decision without that consideration; success defeats liability, not merely relief. That narrow trigger is met here, the County did not carry its defense on the completed trial record, and statutory religious-discharge liability remains resolved without another same-decision trial or evidentiary opportunity.

**Controlling explanation:** The decisionmaker’s acknowledgment that the religious reprimand played a role, together with the connected testimony and protected activities encompassed by the reprimand, meets O’Connor’s narrower Price Waterhouse concurrence as well as the plurality. Mere religious awareness, stray remarks or unprotected resource use would not suffice; the Court settles no other disagreement among those writings.

The District Court treated inadequate performance and religious causation as mutually exclusive. Performance evaluations, delays, restructuring, budget concerns and the computer investigation nevertheless remain genuine evidence of legitimate reasons. Such reasons can justify discharge without proving that this discharge would have occurred on those reasons alone. On the whole authenticated trial account, including the administrator’s inability to say whether discharge would have followed without the religious reprimand and the insufficient contrary proof, the County failed its counterfactual burden. Uncertainty is not a universal conclusive admission; neither a sole legitimate cause, special objective-evidence category nor formulaic answer is required.

The alternative of returning the existing record for a factual finding respects Rule 52’s ordinary protection of permissible choices. Here the Court sustains the appellate determination that the completed record cannot carry the defense, rather than announcing power to replace any disputed trial finding. Reopening evidence is different again: the five-day trial afforded the opportunity, no relevant exclusion or newly announced burden is shown, and the August rehearing refusal preserves closure. Warner-Jenkinson’s contextual treatment of a newly express patent burden supplies no reset under this preexisting rule. Landgraf’s temporal principle preserves the original defense; the 1991 Act’s altered consequences would change the legal effect of completed conduct and no express retrospective command is supplied.

###### Part IV — The remedial tracks remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Remand addresses appropriate relief on the two constitutional restriction claims and, separately, backpay and reinstatement on the established statutory religious-discharge claims. The constitutional claims require their own lawful compensatory or declaratory predicates, while the statutory remand concerns amount, mitigation and equitable conditions and does not reopen liability or guarantee reinstatement.

**Controlling explanation:** Brown actually requested a declaration and compensatory damages for the constitutional restrictions. Complaint language suggested constitutional discharge, but no argument supported it at trial closing or in appellate argument or briefs; the Court adds neither that theory nor constitutional backpay or reinstatement. No independent constitutional injunction request is established. The lower court’s coordinated Iowa disposition is preserved without an independent construction of Iowa law; the affirmed statutory race judgment is outside the religious grant.

Landgraf and Rivers preclude retrospective use of §102’s new Title VII compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury election. They do not eliminate preexisting statutory employment relief or independently available §1983 compensation. Constitutional injury, causation, personal involvement and capacity, municipal responsibility and any properly preserved individual immunity remain distinct; no new defense or chance to undo settled invalidity is conferred. Later merits law does not furnish earlier conduct-time notice. Arizonans creates no automatic mootness where actual compensation claims against the County and identified officials remain; prospective declaratory relief still requires its own continuing controversy. Nabozny preserves those defendant-specific and remedial distinctions without reopening this completed trial.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Employment Division v. Smith: neutral work and resource rules remain permissible; the challenged orders target religion.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah: its targeting and tailoring requirements invalidate the demonstrated religion-specific excess.
- Trans World Airlines v. Hardison: the governing more-than-de-minimis hardship rule is applied, including real operational and third-party burdens.
- Ansonia Board of Education v. Philbrook: effective reasonable accommodation suffices without the employee’s preferred alternative or proof against every option.
- Beadle v. City of Tampa: requires practice-specific hardship and permits supported professional prediction and concrete noncash cost without an accident or negotiation duty after sufficient accommodation or hardship.
- Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins: applies the original liability-stage defense at the narrow direct/substantial-reliance trigger, with the employer’s preponderance burden; no later mixed-motive regime is substituted.
- Pickering v. Board of Education: preserves actual public-employee speech balancing rather than an undifferentiated religious ban.
- Connick v. Myers: preserves public-concern and operational-context inquiries.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District: preserves private expression and attention to manner, time and place.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: broad prospective restrictions require justification for their actual protected-citizen-speech reach; supervisory expression does not automatically fall within that category.
- Waters v. Churchill: its limited genuine-reliance and factual-error holdings remain; no constitutional termination claim is added here.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: supplies no comprehensive majority establishment test or automatic workplace attribution.
- Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller: private access and government adoption remain contextual; no office forum is established.
- Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue: its actual private-use arrangements do not establish an entitlement to official workplace worship.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products: its temporal principle preserves the preenactment liability regime, separately from its bar on retrospective §102 damages and dependent jury relief.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express: the retained temporal distinction supplies no elimination of preexisting relief or independent constitutional compensation.
- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.: its contextual new patent burden creates no right to reopen this existing and fully tried employment defense.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona: preserves an actual live controversy and available remedy without automatically extinguishing these compensation claims.
- Nabozny v. Podlesny: individual responsibility, municipal attribution, conduct-time notice and relief remain separate; its summary-judgment disposition supplies no new Brown liability trial.

#### Law After Decision

A public employer retains lawful controls on resources, coercion, harassment and official religious activity, while religion-specific commands must fit their constitutional justification. Statutory accommodation requires the actual practice and supported burden to be assessed separately; predictions may suffice, but the County’s proof did not establish the asserted burden for these meeting practices. The original mixed-motive defense has been resolved on the completed record. Constitutional compensation and declaration questions and statutory backpay/reinstatement questions remain distinct and do not reopen the adjudicated liability.


### 1997-04-07 — Opuku-Boateng v. California; Ninth Circuit No. 94-16542, 95 F.3d 1461; separate Supreme Court docket unassigned

Authority: [Record](../records/Opuku_Boateng_v_California_merits_1997-04-07.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification stated below.

**1. Applicants receive the same statutory protection.** Title VII's prohibition on refusing to hire because of religion and its definition of religion require reasonable accommodation of an applicant's known sincere religious practice on the same terms that protect an employee. Public employment creates neither an exemption from that duty nor an enhanced duty; prior temporary service is not a prerequisite.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 2000e-2(a)(1) and 2000e(j) expressly connect refusal to hire with religious observance and the employer's accommodation defense. Opuku-Boateng disclosed the Sabbath conflict, and the conceded prima facie case brings California's refusal to complete his appointment within that inquiry. The State's operational concerns therefore go to accommodation and hardship, not to whether an applicant is protected. The Court decides no separate constitutional claim.

**2. Supported predictions may establish hardship, but good-faith discussions are not themselves an accommodation.** An employer must offer an effective reasonable accommodation or show that accommodation would impose more than a de minimis hardship under the governing Hardison rule. When no effective accommodation has been supplied, that showing must address supported materially distinct proposals in the actual operation; it may rest on concrete noncash costs and reasonable, evidence-supported operational predictions without a trial arrangement, realized accident, complaint, preferred accommodation or exhaustion of every conceivable alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Hardison preserves protected seniority rights and legitimate costs or transferred burdens; Beadle and Brown require a practice-specific connection between the proposed arrangement and those burdens while allowing supported professional predictions. Ansonia ends the inquiry when an effective reasonable accommodation has already been offered. California discussed the conflict in good faith for a week, but those discussions produced no effective accommodation. Good faith is relevant conduct, not a substitute for either statutory answer. Nor does Title VII demand an experiment or make negotiation a freestanding prerequisite to an otherwise sufficient hardship defense. Noncontractual coworker burdens, necessary coverage, efficiency and security can count; hostility to religious practice and abstract future requests do not by themselves carry the burden.

**3. California's completed proof does not sustain its hardship defense.** On this record, neutral Saturday rotation and the asserted morale and operational concerns do not establish the required hardship from supported combinations of other undesirable shifts and voluntary trades. This conclusion neither declares every proposed arrangement feasible nor treats all shifts as equally burdensome to every employee.

**Controlling explanation:** Opuku-Boateng offered nights, Sundays, holidays and other undesirable work. The station had no established collective-bargaining or contractual seniority restriction, but that absence alone does not make all coworker interests immaterial. The State failed to identify the material burden transferred by the offered combinations. The poll's occurrence was admitted; its reported results were expressly not admitted for their truth and cannot establish collective unwillingness. Lozano addressed a mistakenly understood three-day absence, while Whitacre could not recall the question or number surveyed. Those limitations do not prove that every employee misunderstood, but leave the cited evidence insufficient to establish that adequate voluntary combinations were unavailable. The testimony favoring varied schedules to prevent collusion did not connect this limited Sabbath absence, with variation on other days, to a concrete security loss. Existing flexibility for courses and a minister's Sunday mornings reinforces the need for that specific comparison without creating an unlimited exemption. Rejecting one permanent substitute did not answer the supported combinations. The absence of an implemented experiment is not independently decisive.

**4. Clear-error review preserves permissible findings, not the use of excluded evidence or an incorrect legal rule.** Rule 52 protects supported trial findings and reasonable operational inferences. It does not require sustaining a hardship conclusion that depends on using evidence for a purpose expressly excluded, a materially mistaken description of the requested absence, or equating neutral rotation with adequate accommodation; the remaining completed proof here cannot carry the defense under the correct predictive standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The strongest argument for restoring the District Court's judgment is its experience with the station's needs and Hardison's low hardship threshold. Those considerations would support an evidence-based finding about scheduling, coverage, security or transferred burdens. They do not remedy the particular evidentiary and legal defects identified here. Beadle permits remand where materially distinct proposals remain for proper assessment; Brown permits preserving resolved liability when a completed record cannot sustain the existing defense. California had its trial opportunity under that defense, and no new burden or exclusion preventing its proof is established. The Court therefore affirms the Ninth Circuit's judgment without endorsing a compulsory experiment, exhaustive negotiation or realized-disruption rule, and does not reopen 1982 liability for a new hardship case.

**5. Relief remains source-authorized and dependent on the facts relevant to remedy.** The remand determines lawful employment and equitable relief, backpay and mitigation, any present basis for prospective relief, and fees and costs under their ordinary predicates. Liability does not guarantee appointment, a transfer vacancy, a particular schedule, a fixed award or a continuing injunction; the employer may choose an effective accommodation, and a permanent schedule may be prescribed only if lawful and necessary after that opportunity.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII relief should place Opuku-Boateng as nearly as lawful and practicable in the position he would have occupied absent discrimination. Current staffing or hiring conditions may affect placement and prospective relief without reopening the failed defense to the original refusal to hire. The 1991 Act's new compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury entitlement do not apply retrospectively to the 1982 conduct, as Brown's temporal remedial distinction confirms. Ordinary mitigation, equitable discretion and fee predicates remain. No Free Exercise, individual constitutional-liability or mixed-motive question is decided, and no general right to a preferred shift is created.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hardison: The then-governing more-than-de-minimis threshold, bona fide seniority protection and legitimate third-party burdens remain in force; the absence of a protected seniority restriction here does not make every noncontractual coworker burden irrelevant.
- Ansonia Board of Education v. Philbrook: An effective reasonable accommodation satisfies the employer's obligation even if another arrangement is preferred; that stopping rule does not resolve this case because no effective accommodation was supplied.
- Beadle v. City of Tampa: Applies its allowance for supported prospective and noncash hardship proof and its requirement to address materially distinct supported proposals; no universal experimental schedule or every-alternative rule is added.
- Brown v. Polk County: Applies its practice-specific statutory standard, completed-record liability distinction and conduct-time remedial limit. Its Free Exercise and mixed-motive holdings do not enlarge this grant.

#### Law After Decision

Applicants for public employment receive the same Title VII accommodation protection as employees. Existing accommodation and hardship doctrine remains intact, including supported predictions and legitimate third-party burdens. On the completed record here, the employer's failed defense leaves liability resolved while lawful relief remains for the District Court. A materially different record may establish hardship; no general preferred-shift entitlement follows.


### 1997-03-17 — Brown v. Polk County; Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313, 61 F.3d 650 (en banc); no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Polk_County_merits_1997-03-17.md).

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins Parts I, II and IV and supports Part III’s disposition without adopting its reasons. He additionally explains that public-employer control should turn on attribution and actual function: the County may control its own supervisory expression and restrict coercive or resource-dependent activity, while genuinely private citizen speech on public concern receives Pickering balancing against actual or reasonably forecast operational disruption. Under NTEU, a broad prospective ban needs justification commensurate with its actual protected-speech burden.

He would examine authorship, voluntariness, time, location, control, resource use and supervisory pressure to distinguish private expression from government worship. Objectively grounded Establishment concerns can justify tailored limits, especially for supervisor-led prayer at mandatory meetings; offense or abstract fear cannot establish those concerns by itself. Employee-initiated voluntary prayer, private conversation and personal items cannot all be treated as official directives, but nominal voluntariness does not immunize coercion. His account preserves neutral resource limits, forbids treating every Title VII violation as automatically constitutional, and leaves genuinely unresolved applications to the proper remedial proceedings. It adds no constitutional discharge claim, mandatory workplace forum or reopening of the completed statutory liability determination. No other Justice joins this additional framework.


### 1997-03-17 — Brown v. Polk County; Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313, 61 F.3d 650 (en banc); no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Polk_County_merits_1997-03-17.md).

The en banc judgment is affirmed insofar as reviewed. Its remands proceed for appropriate relief on the actually argued constitutional restrictions and the established statutory religious-discharge claims, applying their separate conditions. Statutory same-decision liability and the completed hardship determination are not reopened. No fixed award, automatic reinstatement, new liability trial, independent constitutional injunction, constitutional termination judgment or new Iowa-law construction is ordered. The statutory race judgment remains undisturbed.


### 1997-04-07 — Opuku-Boateng v. California; Ninth Circuit No. 94-16542, 95 F.3d 1461; separate Supreme Court docket unassigned

Authority: [Record](../records/Opuku_Boateng_v_California_merits_1997-04-07.md).

The case returns for the appropriate relief contemplated by the Ninth Circuit's remand. The District Court must apply ordinary Title VII remedy predicates, mitigation and current facts relevant to prospective relief, allow the employer to choose among effective accommodations, and preserve the determination of liability. No new evidentiary opportunity to retry the original hardship defense is ordered.
