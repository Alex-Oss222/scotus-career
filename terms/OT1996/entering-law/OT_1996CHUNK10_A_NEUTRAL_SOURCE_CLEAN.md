# 108. Doe v. District of Columbia

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Doe v. District of Columbia
- **Citation or Docket:** 93 F.3d 861 (D.C. Cir. 1996); court-of-appeals No. 95-7077
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Procedural Category:** Simulation-assigned appellate merits
- **Lower-Court Event Date:** 1996-08-27; this is a source date, not an assigned simulation event.

Jane Doe, then a young child living with her mother's friend, allegedly remained unprotected after relatives reported neglect to assigned social workers. She suffered severe scalding injuries before entering District shelter custody. Through a next friend she sued the District and two employees for damages, alleging CAPTA and local child-protection violations and procedural due process.

The district court dismissed the federal claims and found the local negligence claims barred by statutory notice requirements. The circuit resolved the federal questions but certified the unsettled local notice issue to the District of Columbia Court of Appeals. Thus local certification remains pending for this packet. The federal review is of Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal: well-pleaded allegations are assumed, not established as trial findings. The timing and adequacy of reports, official response, causation, and damages are unresolved.

### Questions Presented

1. Whether the Child Abuse Prevention and Treatment Act's requirement that participating jurisdictions provide for prompt investigation of suspected child abuse creates an individually enforceable federal right under 42 U.S.C. §1983.
2. Whether mandatory obligations imposed upon recipients of federal funding create enforceable rights for intended beneficiaries, or instead constitute conditions enforceable only through the federal government's supervision of the funding program.
3. Whether a child-protection statute establishing mandatory investigative procedures creates a liberty interest protected by procedural due process when the child remains outside government custody.

**Provision-specific statutory enforcement.** Simulated *Suter v. Artist M.* rejects the particular §671(a)(15) reasonable-efforts and §671(a)(9) reporting rights asserted there, and separately rejects the implied causes of action. It does not make all funding or state-plan conditions unenforceable, does not treat administrative funding withdrawal as automatic foreclosure, and leaves other provisions for individual examination. Its remand of §671(a)(16) is not a holding that the right exists. Here CAPTA's then-applicable §5106a(b)(2) directs prompt initiation of an investigation after an abuse report and immediate protection following a finding. The question is whether that language creates an individual entitlement or requires a funded jurisdiction to establish a system.

Pre-divergence *Wright*, *Wilder*, and *Thiboutot* remain operative: identify the beneficiary, a binding sufficiently determinate obligation, and a cognizable right, then ask whether Congress foreclosed §1983. Simulated *Livadas v. Bradshaw* applies this separation to a definite federal labor right and refuses to turn Suter into a universal enforcement bar. A remedy cannot follow from compassionate purpose alone; equally, the absence of a freestanding implied action is not dispositive where enacted §1983 supplies the asserted channel. Funding notice and the actual statutory version matter.

**Procedural liberty is separate from statutory enforceability.** Simulated *Sandin v. Conner* preserves genuine substantive constraints and meaningful protected interests while rejecting mandatory procedural wording alone. Its sufficient prison-liberty rule is not automatically a universal child-protection entitlement, and historical Sandin's replacement hardship test does not govern. *DeShaney* rejects a general affirmative constitutional duty to prevent private violence outside custody but does not erase every independently created statutory entitlement. Simulated *Pinder v. Johnson* preserves the distinction between mere omission and an official's own affirmative deprivation without declaring every increased-risk theory established. The pleaded failure to investigate cannot be relabeled custodial abuse based on custody acquired only after the injury.

**Remedy and defendant.** Even if a federal right exists, §1983 requires personal or lawful institutional attribution, causation, and appropriate damages; municipal employer status alone is insufficient. The local notice statute and the certified question have their own sources and cannot be decided through an assumed federal merits conclusion.

## C. LITIGATION POSITIONS, LAWFUL PATHS, AND UNCERTAINTY

Doe argues that the report-triggered duty identifies endangered children and concrete required action, and that an alleged complete failure to begin investigation is judicially manageable even without a universal hour-by-hour deadline. The District emphasizes the funding eligibility structure, administrative implementation, flexible investigatory content, and the absence of a guarantee against private violence.

The Court may affirm the federal dismissals, distinguish CAPTA from Suter and remand the statutory claim, or require a more precise right/foreclosure analysis. It may separately reject a due-process claim founded only on investigative procedures without declaring all noncustodial entitlements impossible. Local certification should remain intact; the Supreme Court simulation does not answer unresolved D.C. statutory notice law or invent a state-court response. Recognizing enforceability would not establish that prompt investigation would have prevented these injuries or automatically support damages against every defendant.

# 109. Bracy v. Gramley

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Bracy v. Gramley
- **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 899 (1997); No. 96-6133
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued federal habeas merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 9, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Bracy showed good cause under Habeas Corpus Rule 6 for discovery supporting his claim that a corrupt trial judge displayed compensatory bias against defendants who did not bribe him.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the particular allegations and public corruption record require discovery into actual judicial bias in Bracy's capital trial.
- **Legal Field:** Federal habeas corpus, due process, judicial bias, and discovery.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit affirmed denial of habeas relief and discovery, reasoning that Bracy had not first produced evidence showing bias in his own case.

- **Material Facts and Record:** Bracy was convicted and sentenced to death by Illinois Judge Thomas Maloney, later federally convicted for accepting bribes to fix criminal cases. Bracy alleges that Maloney harshly handled unbribed cases to conceal corruption. He points to counsel's prior association with Maloney, unusually rapid preparation and penalty proceedings, and the placement of Bracy's trial between murder trials identified in the federal proffer as fixed. He has not yet proved that Maloney was biased in this case.
- **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The grant concerns discovery, not final habeas relief. Rule 6 requires good cause and leaves properly supported scope to the district court. Allegations of compensatory bias in Bracy's own trial must connect the requested discovery to a cognizable claim; corruption in other cases does not itself establish bias here.

### Issue 1: Good cause for discovery on the judicial-bias claim

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Due process requires a neutral judge and forbids adjudication by a judge with actual bias or a personal interest in the outcome. Discovery must develop the pleaded constitutional bias connection under its own elements, while ultimate bias and remedy remain undecided.

- **Case Name:** Gilbert v. Homar
- **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 924 (1997); No. 96-651

- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** June 9, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether due process required a hearing before a state university suspended a tenured police officer without pay immediately after his felony arrest, and whether the later hearing was sufficiently prompt.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the pre-suspension process and timing of the post-suspension hearing satisfied the Fourteenth Amendment.
- **Legal Field:** Procedural due process, public employment, emergency suspension, and timing.

# 111. Johnson v. Fankell

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Johnson v. Fankell
* **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 911 (1997); No. 96-292
* **October Term:** 1996
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued § 1983 procedure case reviewing whether federal law requires a state appellate court to provide an immediate appeal from denial of qualified immunity when state procedure postpones review until final judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 9, 1997 (historical)
* **Question Presented:** Does 42 U.S.C. § 1983, the Supremacy Clause, or federal qualified-immunity doctrine require a State to provide an interlocutory appeal from denial of qualified immunity, notwithstanding a neutral state final-judgment rule?
* **Question Granted:** Whether Idaho’s refusal to entertain an immediate appeal from denial of qualified immunity impermissibly burdened the federal immunity.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Idaho Supreme Court dismissed the interlocutory appeal because Idaho procedure did not treat the order as immediately appealable. Fankell v. Johnson, state judgment reported below.
* **Relief Requested:** The state officials seek reversal and an immediate appellate forum. Johnson seeks affirmance and continuation of the state action subject to ordinary final review.
* **Material Record:** Johnson filed a § 1983 action in Idaho state court against state officials. The trial court denied the officials’ qualified-immunity motion. Federal courts would ordinarily permit an immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity question under the collateral-order doctrine and 28 U.S.C. § 1291, but Idaho follows its own final-judgment rules and dismissed the officials’ appeal as premature. The federal claim remains pending in a competent state trial court.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court must distinguish the substantive federal immunity from the federal appellate statute and procedural doctrine that make certain federal orders immediately appealable. State courts must enforce the immunity but ordinarily follow neutral state procedural rules unless federal law makes a contrary procedure indispensable.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Idaho judgment preceded the petition; argument occurred March 24, 1997; the Supreme Court event date is June 9, 1997.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None identified as a separate matter requiring adjudication.
- **Participation Matter:** Official opinion checked: no historical case-specific nonparticipation is recorded. The term-effective simulation roster applies with Stone as Chief Justice; no case-specific exclusion or vote is inferred beyond the controlling participation record.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** State courts are competent to enforce federal rights and defenses. The Supremacy Clause prevents discrimination or nullification, not every procedural difference between sovereign court systems.

### Issue 1: State procedural autonomy and federal qualified immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated Elder, Johnson v. Jones, Behrens, and Swint define federal qualified-immunity appellate practice under federal jurisdiction. None states that § 1983 itself commands an interlocutory state appeal.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Keep substantive immunity, federal appellate jurisdiction, and state procedure separate. A neutral state rule may govern the timing of review unless it discriminates against federal claims or defeats a substantive federal right.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Supremacy Clause; 28 U.S.C. § 1291; Mitchell v. Forsyth; Howlett v. Rose; Felder v. Casey; Testa v. Katt.
* **Material Other Authority:** Idaho appellate rules, trial order, state treatment of comparable state and federal defenses, and availability of final review.
* **Open Point:** Whether a discriminatory or functionally destructive state procedure would be preempted, and whether the officials ultimately have qualified immunity.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Immediate appeal

* **Reasoning Below:** The Idaho Supreme Court applied its final-judgment rule and dismissed the appeal without deciding immunity.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Qualified immunity includes protection from trial, so delayed review destroys the federal entitlement and must yield under the Supremacy Clause.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** The immunity remains enforceable in the trial court and on final appeal. Immediate federal review comes from § 1291 and the federal collateral-order doctrine, not from § 1983.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** A state rule that singles out federal immunities or leaves no meaningful review could present a different preemption question.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Affirmance does not authorize a state court to deny or dilute the substantive immunity.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance is available if immediate federal appeal rests on §1291 and Idaho's neutral final-judgment rule leaves the substantive immunity meaningfully enforceable. Reversal would require an identified federal right to immediate state appellate review or a showing that the state procedure discriminates against or defeats the governing federal right. Either disposition concerns timing of appellate review and does not determine the officials' immunity or underlying liability.
