## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

### Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

**Governing question:** Allocation of claim meaning and factual infringement work in a patent damages action.

**Current rule:** A patent damages action carries the Seventh Amendment jury right, while the court determines the public claim’s operative legal meaning using the claim, specification and prosecution history. Judicial construction includes terms of art and genuinely disputed technical usage or expert credibility embedded in that interpretive task, evaluated for consistency with the whole instrument. The jury retains disputed infringement, product operation, external identity and other factual questions outside construction. Technical complexity alone neither removes protected facts from the jury nor makes all patent issues legal.

**Authority by component:**

- Markman — Part I, legal meaning and retained factual work, 1996-04-23; Souter for all nine.
- Markman — Part II, technical and credibility disputes within construction, 1996-04-23; Souter and all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela’s contrary embedded-fact limitation is noncontrolling.

**Present operation effective:** Markman, April 23, 1996.

**Open question:** Trial allocation does not settle appellate review of every subsidiary finding. No universal pretrial construction hearing, exclusion of all extrinsic evidence, allocation for other instruments or comprehensive means-plus-function rule is prescribed.

**Holdings navigation:** Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370.

#### Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-26.
**Decided:** April 23, 1996.

##### Judge and jury responsibilities in a patent damages action

**Holding:** A patent infringement action seeking legal damages carries the Seventh Amendment jury right. The court states the public claim's legally operative meaning; the jury retains disputed infringement facts, external product identity, and other properly factual work under that construction. The action's jury status does not assign every subsidiary task to the jury, and technical complexity alone does not remove otherwise protected facts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, unanimous.

##### Genuine technical and credibility disputes within claim construction

**Holding:** Judicial claim construction includes terms of art informed by extrinsic evidence and resolution of genuine technical-usage or expert-credibility disputes embedded in determining claim meaning. The judge evaluates that evidence against the claim, specification, prosecution history, and the instrument's coherence. This trial allocation neither makes every evidentiary proposition intrinsically legal nor determines appellate review of every subsidiary finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join. His concurrence cannot narrow this direct majority holding; no *Marks* synthesis applies.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Useful technical evidence remains available, and factual infringement work outside construction remains with its proper factfinder. No universal de novo subsidiary review, mandatory separate construction hearing, allocation for every other instrument, or general means-plus-function rule is decided. Uniformity corroborates the historical allocation but cannot alone override a protected jury function.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Tull*, *Terry*, and *Wooddell* distinguish a legal action's jury protection from allocation of particular functions. *Silsby*, *Bischoff*, *Heald*, *Singer*, and *Winans* retain distinctions among instrument construction, technical explanation, comparison of external inventions, and infringement; they do not require a jury for every input into claim meaning. *Gaudin*'s criminal-element holding and *Thompson*'s independent custody review concern different tasks and remain unchanged.

##### Construction of claims 1 and 10 and the independent sufficiency ground

**Holding:** The claim, specification, and prosecution history require clothing as part of the patented inventory. Cash totals or invoices alone cannot satisfy the integrated article-related detection and localization function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court opinion, unanimous.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even crediting petitioners' supported technical evidence favorably and assuming their proposed jury allocation, no reasonable cash-only interpretation performs that function. Ordinary judgment-as-a-matter-of-law principles independently sustain noninfringement because the parties concede noninfringement under a clothing-inclusive construction.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III, unanimous; this independent record-specific ground does not narrow Part II.

**Material application:** General cash/invoice usage, advertising, and technical testimony do not erase this instrument's article requirement. No adverse credibility choice is necessary; subjective intended coverage and legal opinion cannot expand the public grant. At least one scanner and possible batch or transaction processing do not remove the clothing component or require a particular scanning count, permanent item-by-item architecture, or universal tracking technology.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm noninfringement judgment as a matter of law on claims 1 and 10, 9–0. No construction hearing, new trial, restored infringement verdict, or damages award follows. Claim 14's adverse verdict is not reopened; validity, damages, and other unreviewed matters retain their actual status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general rule makes any intrinsic evidence defeat any expert account, permits disbelieving a witness on judgment as a matter of law, or presumes noninfringement. No validity, damages, or general §112 paragraph 6 question is decided.

### 1997-03-03 — Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.; No. 95-728.

Authority: [Record](../records/Warner_Jenkinson_Co_v_Hilton_Davis_Chemical_Co_merits_1997-03-03.md).

#### Holdings

Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins all propositions except the statement that bad intent is not an element, which therefore has eight direct joins; he reserves that issue. Every other proposition below, including the contextual treatment of older prosecution records, has nine direct joins.

##### Equivalence survives but cannot erase a claim element

**Holding and operative rule.** The doctrine of equivalents survives the 1952 Patent Act, but nonliteral infringement must be assessed against every material claim element in its patent and technical context; overall similarity cannot eliminate a limitation. Function, way and result may assist the substantial-difference inquiry without serving as an exclusive mechanical formula, and bad intent is not an element of infringement under this doctrine.

**Controlling explanation.** Graver Tank preserves protection against insubstantial substitutions that would evade a lawful claim while requiring attention to the actual invention and technical evidence. Nothing in the 1952 Act displaces that doctrine. The claim nevertheless defines the public grant: similarity in a process's ultimate result cannot replace proof concerning an omitted element. Known interchangeability and relevant technical evidence can inform whether the difference is substantial. Here the patent, as amended, claims dye ultrafiltration at approximately pH 6.0 to 9.0, while Warner-Jenkinson uses pH 5.0; Hilton Davis concedes the absence of literal infringement. The lower pH boundary therefore cannot be ignored under an overall-equivalence assessment. Deuel likewise requires attention to actual claims and supported technical evidence, though its obviousness burden is a different inquiry. The Court neither abolishes equivalents nor decides whether this particular process infringes. It adopts no categorical rule barring every foreseeable but unclaimed substitute absent a narrowing amendment.

##### The patentee must explain an otherwise unexplained narrowing amendment

**Holding and operative rule.** Prosecution-history estoppel prevents recapture of subject matter surrendered through narrowing to meet a patentability objection. When the record does not disclose a legally sufficient reason for a required narrowing amendment, the patentee seeking the disputed equivalent bears the burden of supplying one; after fair consideration, an inadequate explanation supports a presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the affected limitation.

**Controlling explanation.** Exhibit Supply preserves the actual subject matter surrendered to obtain a patent; it does not make every amendment a universal bar to equivalents. The claim and prosecution record protect public notice as well as the patentee's legitimate coverage. An unexplained narrowing cannot simply disappear whenever infringement by an equivalent is alleged, and the patentee bears the explanatory burden rather than forcing the accused infringer to reconstruct the omission. But the presumption is rebuttable. The court must evaluate a supported nonpatentability explanation and its legal effect rather than treat every amendment as conclusive or extinguish equivalents unrelated to the surrender. The reason for this lower pH limit remains unestablished; no finding supplies a particular examiner demand for pH 6.0. The lower court must consider whether a reason was previously offered and whether a further opportunity is proper, recognizing that old prosecution files were completed without the newly express burden. It must not demand nonexistent contemporaneous magic words or treat historical silence as misconduct. This contextual consideration neither transfers the burden nor creates an automatic right to reopen evidence, a mandatory evidentiary hearing or endless opportunities to repair failed proof. The remand requires decision of the unresolved amendment question without predetermining the adequacy of any explanation.

##### Legal claim boundaries precede unresolved infringement facts

**Holding and operative rule.** The court must determine claim meaning and enforce the legal effect of prosecution surrender before an asserted equivalent may be used to establish infringement. The judgment must be reversed and remanded for that determination; if the equivalent remains legally available, genuine disputed factual equivalence remains for the jury under ordinary procedures, without a final infringement decision or automatic new trial here.

**Controlling explanation.** Markman assigns legally operative claim meaning to the judge, including genuine technical-usage and credibility disputes embedded in construction under its controlling eight-Justice holding. It also preserves external infringement facts and does not permit a jury to erase an element of the construed claim. The unexplained amendment presents a legal gate to the equivalence claim that the Federal Circuit did not fully resolve. Once the court determines the permitted boundary, any remaining properly factual infringement work retains its ordinary place. Neither this remand nor Markman answers the complete judge-jury allocation for every equivalents issue or every appellate review standard. The Court orders neither patent invalidation nor an automatic new trial and makes no final comparison of the accused process with a legally available equivalent.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Graver Tank & Manufacturing v. Linde Air Products**: Retained as authority for equivalents after the 1952 Act, with technical context and public claim boundaries preserved; the doctrine does not authorize deleting a material element.
- **Exhibit Supply Co. v. Ace Patents Corp.**: Applied to prohibit recapture of subject matter actually surrendered to obtain the patent; it supplies no universal bar from every amendment.
- **Markman v. Westview Instruments**: Applied for judicial construction, including its controlling embedded-technical-dispute allocation, while preserving external infringement facts; no universal appellate standard or complete equivalents allocation is inferred.
- **In re Deuel**: Its claim-specific and evidentiary discipline remains intact. The Office's initial obviousness burden is not transferred into this different patentee explanation burden.

No earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Equivalents remains available within each material claim element and the legally permitted prosecution boundary. The patentee must explain an otherwise unexplained narrowing amendment; the court applies the rebuttable consequence in context rather than treating old silence as either irrelevant or irrebuttable surrender. Construction and surrender limits do not eliminate ordinary factual infringement work.

### 1997-03-03 — Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.; No. 95-728.

Authority: [Record](../records/Warner_Jenkinson_Co_v_Hilton_Davis_Chemical_Co_merits_1997-03-03.md).

The Federal Circuit's judgment affirming infringement and the associated injunction is reversed, and the case is remanded for element-specific analysis and determination of the pH amendment's legal effect under the patentee's explanatory burden. The court must consider the adequacy of any supported reason and whether further opportunity is proper in the circumstances. If an asserted equivalent remains legally available, remaining factual infringement issues proceed under ordinary procedures. The mandate neither finally finds infringement or noninfringement, invalidates the patent, presumes an examiner's actual reason nor automatically orders a hearing or new trial.
