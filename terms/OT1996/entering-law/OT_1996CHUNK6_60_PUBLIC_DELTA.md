## Event

**Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.**, Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510, 83 F.3d 118. Merits decision, March 19, 1997. On review of the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of dismissal on a categorical same-sex-harassment ground. Merits hearing and decision: March 19, 1997 under the established Court calendar; no separate argument date is supplied.

The questions are whether Title VII excludes harassment when offender and employee share a sex, whether sexual desire is necessary, and whether an all-male workplace precludes actionable sex discrimination.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment insofar as it rests on the categorical same-sex exclusion and remands the affected claims.

The independently grounded dismissal of Pippen and Johnson as not statutory employers remains undisturbed outside the grant; the Court adopts no new rule of individual immunity. ## Holdings

**Common authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

### Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII

**Rule:** Title VII does not exclude discriminatory harassment merely because the offender and employee share a sex. The claimant must plead and ultimately prove that the challenged employment conditions were imposed because of sex and must satisfy the remaining requirements of the asserted claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor establishes that abusive working conditions can constitute statutory discrimination. The enacted protection concerns the individual employee's treatment, not an unstated requirement that employee and offender belong to different sex categories. A categorical exclusion therefore cannot support this dismissal. Rejecting that exclusion does not prove any allegation, create liability for all objectionable conduct, or adopt a free-standing dignity cause of action. The independent statutory-employer ruling outside the grant remains untouched.

### Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element

**Rule:** Discrimination because of sex may be established through competent direct or circumstantial evidence without proving sexual desire, a particular sexual orientation, or the presence of an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish discriminatory causation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hicks preserves the claimant's ultimate persuasion burden without making one evidentiary route compulsory. Sex-directed hostility, selective conduct, discriminatory statements, threats and the circumstances can support a causal inference; this illustrative list is neither exclusive nor a checklist. Desire can be relevant, but making it indispensable would exclude sex-based mistreatment for reasons the statute does not recognize. The trier must evaluate the actual inference and competing explanations. Ordinary interpersonal hostility unconnected to sex remains outside this cause, and no orientation identity is found here.

### Apply Harris in the actual workplace context

**Rule:** A hostile-environment claim requires sex-based conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively hostile or abusive environment in context, which the claimant actually perceived as abusive. An all-male workforce supplies context, not categorical immunity; neither psychiatric injury nor measurable productivity loss is indispensable.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires an objective and subjective inquiry into the circumstances and supplies the severe-or-pervasive threshold. Context helps distinguish actionable abuse from ordinary offensive banter; it cannot excuse forced physical humiliation or rape threats simply by labeling them rough camaraderie. The alleged conduct may satisfy the threshold, but allegations are not findings and gravity does not substitute for unwelcome treatment, discriminatory causation or employer attribution. No single factor, diagnosis, treatment, universal reasonable-woman standard or medical proof is made compulsory.

### Attribution, other claims and relief remain distinct on remand

**Rule:** If employer attribution is reached, Andrade requires examination whether actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the hostile environment; job title alone is insufficient, and absence of higher-management notice alone does not defeat that agency route. Negligence and any preserved quid-pro-quo claim remain distinct inquiries, and this reversal decides neither their factual predicates nor relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Andrade supplies the governing agency rule, including that employment action or economic loss is not invariably necessary for its authority-aided-environment route. A quid-pro-quo theory separately requires the alleged demand, use of employment power and employment-condition consequence to be identified; coverage does not collapse the two theories. The lower courts must apply the correct rules to the affected claims and any properly presented independent defenses in ordinary course. The Court does not reinstate an unchallenged coworker-employer theory, decide Lyons's status, find constructive discharge, order discovery or trial, or award reinstatement, backpay or punitive damages. Remedy questions for the 1991 conduct remain open.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied the recognition of discriminatory abusive working conditions, extending its statutory application to employees and offenders who share a sex without eliminating any element.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Applied its contextual severe-or-pervasive, objective and subjective inquiry; no psychiatric injury or measurable economic harm is added.
- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks:** Preserved ultimate proof of discrimination and the availability of circumstantial inference; no compulsory comparator or desire showing is created.
- **Andrade v. Mayfair Management:** Applied the actual-authority/material-aid employer-attribution rule, keeping negligence and employment consequences distinct.
- **Brown v. Polk County and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Their separate statutory and constitutional grounds do not replace Title VII's requirements or decide a sexual-orientation coverage question here.
- **Garcia's categorical same-sex exclusion:** The lower-court rule cannot govern Title VII after this decision.

## Law After Decision

Title VII reaches discriminatory harassment without an opposite-sex requirement. Desire and workforce composition create no categorical gates or immunity. The employee still must establish sex causation and every element of the applicable harassment theory; Harris and Andrade retain their independent force. No sexual-orientation coverage holding or automatic liability rule is adopted.
