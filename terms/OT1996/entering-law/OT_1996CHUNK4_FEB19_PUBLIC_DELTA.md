# Auer v. Robbins; No. 95-897.

Authority: [Public decision](../records/Auer_v_Robbins_merits_1997-02-19.md).

## Event

Auer v. Robbins, No. 95-897. Argued December 10, 1996; decided February 19, 1997. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit. St. Louis police sergeants and a lieutenant challenge their treatment as exempt executive or administrative employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act. The questions concern the salary-basis rule's validity for public employees, practical exposure to impermissible disciplinary deductions and correction of an improper deduction. The Board also asserts that it is an arm of Missouri. Salary amount and the appellate duties determination are uncontested predicates; the latter is not independently reconsidered.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms, subject to the correction conditions stated in its mandate.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed subject to actual correction, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Before disposing of the affected salary-basis claim on a corrected-exemption ground, the lower courts must give effect to both reimbursement of Guzy's improper deduction and a promise of future compliance. Neither is adjudged complete. Without satisfaction, the employer cannot rely on restored exemption, and the applicable compensation consequences remain for lawful determination.

The Board's state-arm defense is rejected. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with that threshold resolution without adopting its ground. The Court awards no fixed overtime sum, restores no part of the $9,284 pre-appeal district award and orders no general reopening of application evidence or the uncontested duties ruling.

## Holdings

### The Board's local relationship does not make it an arm of Missouri

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board is not an arm of Missouri entitled to the State's immunity in these actions because its combined financial, budgetary and operational relationship is local. State creation, statutory regulation and gubernatorial appointments do not alone make a separate public body the State; treasury exposure carries substantial weight alongside institutional control rather than serving as an indispensable sole condition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice agrees with the threshold resolution only.

**Controlling explanation:** Hess requires attention to the actual entity rather than its governmental label, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes local public bodies from the State itself. Missouri created and regulates the Board and the governor appoints four members, with the mayor serving as the fifth. Those controls matter, but the record's municipal financing, city budget and appropriation role, limits requiring city approval and local operational relationship establish the relevant character of this Board. No single appointment or financial fact substitutes for that combined inquiry. Because the Board is not a state arm, the Court need not decide whether litigation conduct effected an authorized waiver, whether Congress validly abrogated state immunity for this claim, or whether an officer route would permit the requested relief. Seminole Tribe supplies no categorical Article I answer to those unnecessary questions.

### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Fixed salary reasonably distinguishes exempt compensation from pay varying with work quality or quantity. Congress's delegation includes public employment. Disciplinary needs do not make the criterion unreasonable or establish an exception. Major-safety rules prevent serious danger to the plant or other employees; residency does not qualify. Specified absences and furloughs differ from general discipline. No rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith major-safety penalties retain the narrow scope stated above.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

### The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds

**Holding and operative rule:** The responsible Secretary's fair and considered interpretation of the salary regulation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Presentation in an amicus brief does not alone disqualify that interpretation, but supplies no automatic authority to an agency lawyer, power to amend operative text or deference to new factual assertions about this employer.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice proposes a different interpretive rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Thomas Jefferson University applies Seminole Rock's bounded own-regulation rule; Stinson preserves the difference between interpreting a rule and contradicting or amending it. Martin requires attention to the statutory allocation of administrative responsibility, while Bowen excludes unsupported litigation rationalizations. The Secretary's position here interprets the actual salary and correction text and is consistent with the supported administrative treatment, including the correction letter. It is a fair and considered legal construction, not an employer-specific factual determination resulting from a Department investigation. Its amicus form does not make it an impermissible invention or itself establish its correctness. The canon favoring narrow construction of exemptions does not prevent the Secretary from resolving actual regulatory ambiguity within the congressional delegation. Courts still determine whether the reading fits the text and lawful authority; they do not accept the agency's characterization of unproved facts merely because its legal construction controls.

### Practical exposure requires an actual practice or an effective particular policy

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The salary text concerns real susceptibility to reductions, not only deductions already made and not every abstract possibility listed in a broad disciplinary code. An effective policy can alter compensation arrangements before the first deduction; an undifferentiated range of sanctions governing exempt and nonexempt workers does not itself communicate that these salaried officers will receive impermissible deductions. Guzy's negotiated two-day residency suspension instead of termination was treated below as an exceptional event. The later rule made termination the exclusive residency sanction; it did not repeal every disciplinary authorization. That episode alone does not establish recurring deductions or a general significant likelihood.

The officers' argument that the manual itself defeats any salary agreement rests on the broader legal construction the Court rejects. Their additional examples, witness-understanding contentions and related evidentiary-burden submission remain disputed submissions, not grounds for a new blanket factual or forfeiture finding. The Court adopts no numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or universal employer-compliance presumption, and directs no general factual reopening. Ordinary preservation and mandate rules retain their force.

### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The text separates two eligibility alternatives from two cumulative acts of correction. Intentionality does not defeat the other-than-lack-of-work alternative. Nor does the text require payment immediately upon discovery. It nevertheless requires actual reimbursement and a promise; eligibility alone accomplishes neither. The correction mechanism does not convert a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. Deductions generally made when work is unavailable indicate no intention to pay on a salary basis and defeat exemption during that period.

The lower opinion quoted both conditions and required reimbursement, but its formal disposition did not expressly establish a remand implementation proceeding. This Court therefore states the operative condition in its own mandate: on return, the lower courts must secure both required acts before disposing of the affected claim on the ground of corrected exemption. Neither completed payment nor a completed promise is found. If the employer does not perform, it cannot rely on restored exemption, and lawful compensation consequences remain to be determined. No new correction condition, fixed compliance timetable, automatic overtime amount or damages award is added.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. and Mt. Healthy are applied to the Board's actual local relationship; treasury exposure remains important without becoming the exclusive condition of immunity.
- Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida remains limited to its actual holdings and unresolved State-amenability rationale. No waiver, abrogation or retrospective officer-remedy rule is added.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala and Bowles v. Seminole Rock retain controlling weight for a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation, with text, consistency, lawful authority and actual-ground limits intact.
- Stinson v. United States preserves authoritative interpretation within, rather than amendment of, the operative rule; its Sentencing Commission setting is not mechanically transferred to factual assertions here.
- Martin v. OSHRC supplies attention to actual interpretive responsibility and reasonableness; Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital preserves the exclusion of unsupported litigation rationalizations.

## Law After Decision

The Board may be subjected to these FLSA proceedings without deciding the State's own immunity. Salary basis remains a valid component of the public executive/administrative exemption. Practical exposure, actual deductions and correction are distinct inquiries, and every stated regulatory exception retains its conditions. The own-regulation controlling-weight rule remains law; the Chief Justice's proposed replacement receives no additional join. A correction-dependent exemption requires both reimbursement and the future promise, secured through the present mandate. Duties and the former district award are not reopened.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins validity, practical exposure and correction, but would replace the controlling-weight component of Thomas Jefferson University and the corresponding general Seminole Rock rule. He acknowledges that he joined TJU and that it already protects plain text, consistency, explanation and the actual agency ground. His disagreement concerns the remaining proposition that the administering agency's selection among permissible meanings controls because it wrote and administers the regulation.

He would have courts determine meaning independently using ordinary interpretive tools. If genuine uncertainty remains, an authoritative and considered agency interpretation should receive special persuasive weight when it is consistent or adequately explained, fairly noticed, within delegated authority, grounded in relevant expertise and more than a restatement of the statute. Weight would follow those reasons, rather than agency authorship alone. An interpretation may neither enlarge statute or regulation nor impose liability without the notice required by law. Agency amicus participation neither makes its reading controlling nor automatically disqualifies it as litigation invention.

In his view, delegation to prescribe an exemption differs from the judicial responsibility to determine the adopted rule's meaning. Expertise may persuade, and an actual delegation may assign policy discretion, without every ambiguity transferring interpretive authority. He accepts the Secretary's practical-exposure reading independently because it fits the text, salary concept and the distinction between an abstract sanction, an effective policy and an actual practice. He likewise accepts the correction provision's alternatives and mandatory reimbursement and future promise. He does not reach the separately asserted affirmative-burden contention because he regards it as inadequately preserved.

His proposed change would preserve lawful delegated policy choices, operative regulatory text, ordinary reasoned-decisionmaking requirements, review of the agency's actual ground and the earlier decisions' independent substantive holdings. He would not reopen TJU's completed reimbursement judgment or declare its actual construction substantively wrong. Statute-specific allocations of authority require separate examination. Concrete reliance involving settled transactions, compensation arrangements and fairly communicated obligations deserves weight; an administrative preference for controlling deference is not itself that reliance. This record identifies no concrete reliance requiring a different result where independent interpretation reaches the same salary construction and correction mechanism. He makes no finding about reliance across other programs, authorizes no general reopening, and leaves distinct retrospective-liability claims to their applicable notice, finality and remedial rules. This proposed doctrinal change is noncontrolling.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment is affirmed with this Court's enforceable correction condition. On return, the lower courts must give effect to actual reimbursement and a future-compliance promise before disposing of the affected salary-basis claim on a corrected-exemption ground. Without performance that ground remains unavailable, and compensation consequences require lawful determination. No completed cure, express earlier remand or implementation schedule is presumed. The uncontested duties ruling remains intact; the Court neither restores the former $9,284 district award nor orders a general new evidentiary inquiry.

# Maryland v. Wilson; No. 95-1268; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Public decision](../records/Maryland_v_Wilson_merits_1997-02-19.md).

## Event

Maryland v. Wilson, No. 95-1268. Argued December 11, 1996; decided February 19, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Maryland Court of Special Appeals, which affirmed suppression of cocaine that fell when Wilson obeyed an order to leave a lawfully stopped vehicle. The question is whether Mimms's categorical driver-exit authority extends to passengers. The State presents that categorical theory; the delayed stop, passenger movements and nervousness are not a preserved independent ground for the order.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment and remands. Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.** Supporting: O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Kennedy, who would affirm suppression on the presented theory. Reversal removes the federal premise that a passenger-exit direction always needs an additional individualized justification; it neither orders admission of the cocaine nor reinstates a conviction. The State receives no new decision on its unpreserved alternative theory.

## Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Kennedy files a dissent, joined by Stevens. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela files a separate dissent, joined by no other Justice. No dissenting writing supplies a join to the Court's rule.

## Holdings

### Passenger-exit directions during a lawful traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** During a lawful traffic stop, an officer may direct a passenger to leave the vehicle without additional passenger-specific suspicion as a limited safety measure incident to the stop. This authority concerns the exit direction itself; it does not establish categorical forcible detention for the entire stop, permission to frisk or search, or power to prolong detention after the lawful traffic business ends.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, counting its author within that six-Justice coalition; six direct rationale votes control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court extends Mimms's comparison of officer safety and incremental intrusion to the passenger's immediate relocation during an independently lawful traffic stop. A passenger may conceal a weapon and threaten an officer even though the passenger committed no traffic offense. The safety risk does not disappear when the potentially dangerous occupant sits beside the driver.

The passenger has a stronger initial liberty argument because the driver's offense supplies the reason for the stop. But the direction changes the passenger's location during an interruption already lawfully occasioned by the vehicle's stop. At that limited scope, the safety interest outweighs the additional intrusion. Terry and Brignoni-Ponce require attention to the nature of the intrusion and its justification; their particularized-ground rules for different intrusions remain. The Court does not resolve whether every passenger was already seized at the instant the vehicle stopped or whether a passenger may be forcibly held for its entire duration.

Whren permits an objectively justified ordinary initial stop while preserving execution limits. Robinette likewise separates lawful traffic measures from continued detention after their justification ends. Neither supplies automatic search or frisk authority. References in Michigan v. Long to occupants leaving cars and in the Rakas concurrence do not constitute an earlier controlling passenger-exit holding; the Court decides that question here. Because Maryland presented only categorical authority, the Court neither relies on the observed movements as a distinct justification nor eliminates preservation limits. The contrary categorical federal premise is reversed, leaving ordinary further proceedings rather than a final evidence-admission order.

**Precedent treatment:** Mimms's safety/incremental-intrusion rule extends to this passenger-exit direction. Terry, Brignoni-Ponce, Whren and Robinette retain their distinct predicates and limits; no frisk, search or prolonged-detention rule is displaced.

## Precedent Treatment

- Pennsylvania v. Mimms supplies the safety and incremental-intrusion comparison and is extended from drivers to the limited passenger-exit direction.
- Terry v. Ohio and United States v. Brignoni-Ponce retain their requirements for the distinct seizures and protective intrusions they govern; this decision does not make every additional police measure categorical.
- Whren v. United States preserves objective authority for the ordinary initial traffic stop and independent constraints on manner, scope and duration.
- Ohio v. Robinette preserves the distinction between measures during lawful traffic business and unsupported continuation after it ends, together with genuinely independent state law.
- Michigan v. Long and the Rakas concurrence are not treated as having already decided the passenger-exit question. Ornelas retains its independent ultimate-suspicion/probable-cause review and protection of historical facts without becoming a universal review rule.

## Law After Decision

Federal law permits the limited passenger-exit command during a lawful traffic stop without an additional passenger-specific reason. The decision adds that authority while leaving other seizure, duration, search, frisk and independent-state-law questions at their existing scopes. It does not authorize every subsequent command merely because the vehicle was lawfully stopped.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justice Stevens.** Stevens and Kennedy would affirm because the passenger's added restraint needs an objective circumstance connected to the actual stop. Mimms's driver committed the traffic offense and controlled the vehicle; a passenger did neither. Aggregate danger does not justify an otherwise arbitrary command to every innocent passenger. Officers retain practical latitude to act on supported safety circumstances, but an explanation tied to those circumstances must precede the exercise of additional control rather than depend on the officer's grace after categorical authority is conferred. The movements and nervousness are not denied; the State did not preserve them as an independent justification, so they supply no basis to concur in reversal.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would permit an exit direction when any objective circumstance reasonably supports it for safety, facilitating a lawful search or conducting the stop. The showing is modest and may arise from movements, numbers, location, conduct or other practical facts; neither probable cause nor a formal suspicion label is required. Mimms's driver-offense predicate does not by itself justify an added command to a passenger. Whren's ordinary stop authority should therefore remain subject to a reason connected to the actual stop, rather than general statistics or administrative ease. These observations might have supplied such a reason if properly preserved, but the Court cannot replace the State's categorical case. He would leave suppression in force and order no remand for a new justification. He reserves commands to remain in a vehicle and preserves lawful-search control and arrest on probable cause.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Maryland courts for proceedings consistent with the limited federal rule. No new hearing, final admissibility determination, conviction reinstatement or extended detention is ordered. Genuinely independent Maryland protections remain available within ordinary procedure.

The observed movement and nervousness remain part of the account; the decision does not adjudicate their sufficiency on an independent theory.

# Regents of the University of California v. Doe; No. 95-1694; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Public decision](../records/Regents_v_Doe_merits_1997-02-19.md).

## Event

Regents of the University of California v. Doe, No. 95-1694. Argued December 2, 1996; decided February 19, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing its September 11, 1995 judgment, 65 F.3d 771. Doe alleged breach of an agreement to employ him at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory after the University determined that he could not obtain the required security clearance. The question is whether the Department of Energy's contractual reimbursement of litigation costs and qualifying judgments removes the University's immunity, assuming it otherwise qualifies as an arm of California.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remands. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the unanimous opinion.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The indemnification agreement supplies no basis for setting aside the District Court's immunity disposition to the extent that disposition rests on the accepted premise that the University is an arm of California. Any independently open challenge to that premise, waiver, abrogation, prospective or individual-defendant relief, and the underlying contract merits remain unresolved for lawful further proceedings; the Court does not dismiss every claim on a new ground.

## Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate opinion is filed.

## Holdings

### Collateral indemnification does not alter an assumed state arm's immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A third party's contractual promise to reimburse litigation expense or an adverse judgment does not by itself divest an otherwise immune state instrumentality of Eleventh Amendment immunity. The instrumentality remains the defendant and primary legal obligor; collateral reimbursement does not replace the complete inquiry into its legal, financial and institutional relationship with the State or make structural financing irrelevant to antecedent arm status.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine participating Justices join the rule, explanation and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Hess's distinction between an entity's actual institutional relationship with the State and a single financial fact. Hess denied immunity to PATH because its compact and self-supporting structure did not make the States responsible for its employee judgments. It did not reduce arm status to identifying the final source of every dollar spent.

The University remains the party against which judgment would run here. DOE's promise allocates reimbursement between the contracting parties; it neither substitutes the United States as defendant nor extinguishes the University's legal obligation to Doe. An otherwise immune arm does not lose its constitutional identity whenever it acquires insurance or a contractual right to repayment. The Ninth Circuit discussed several institutional factors, but treated federal reimbursement as the decisive difference from its accepted University precedent. That collateral difference cannot do the work assigned to it.

Doe's strongest argument is that assured federal payment eliminates the treasury concern central to immunity. Treasury exposure remains highly probative, but Hess makes it neither the sole consideration nor categorically indispensable. Mt. Healthy and Lake Country Estates require attention to the entity that is actually sued and its governing structure. A financing arrangement embedded in that structure may bear on arm status; this decision concerns a reimbursement promise on the express assumption that arm status otherwise exists.

The grant does not authorize deciding the University's status from first principles or the independent grounds for other relief. The Court reverses the indemnity-based premise and remands within those limits. It decides neither the employment agreement's merits nor whether any separate individual or prospective claim may proceed.

**Precedent treatment:** Hess's complete entity inquiry is applied and extended to the distinct collateral-indemnity question without a sole-treasury test. Mt. Healthy and Lake Country Estates retain their distinction between an arm of a State and an independent public entity.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. remains an entity-specific compact decision; its highly probative but nonexclusive treasury consideration does not make collateral repayment decisive.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle preserves the distinction between a separate public body and an arm of the State; a reimbursement contract does not itself change that identity.
- Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency retains attention to the actual creating structure rather than governmental function or funding labels alone.

## Law After Decision

An assumed state arm retains its immunity despite a third party's collateral promise to reimburse litigation costs or judgments. Initial arm status still requires the governing institutional inquiry; this decision adds no general funding immunity, sole-treasury rule, waiver or abrogation doctrine. The University's status is assumed only for the question answered.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for proceedings consistent with the rule that collateral indemnification cannot remove the assumed immunity. The District Court's accepted-arm immunity disposition cannot be displaced on that ground; independently open claims and defenses retain their lawful posture. No employment order, damages award or decision on prospective relief is entered.

## Holdings

### Fixed access restrictions satisfy the governing injunction standard on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** A content-neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve its corresponding significant interest. These fixed fifteen-foot demonstration restrictions at identified doorways, driveway entrances and parking-access points of covered facilities—all facilities where abortions are performed in the Western District of New York—with nonthreatening initial counseling by no more than two counselors per approached person or group, satisfy that requirement on the supported obstruction and crowding record; fifteen feet is not a universal safe harbor.

The injunction binds responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert; shared views, association or independent advocacy alone do not establish that relationship. This coverage boundary neither adjudicates new nonparty liability nor determines whether a particular speech restriction is sufficiently tailored.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's fixed-access sufficiency holding has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas additionally join the responsible-party, notice and actual-concert coverage boundary, giving that distinct boundary nine direct joins despite their dissent from the fixed restrictions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Madsen's provision-specific fit inquiry. Stable, identifiable access corridors protect entry and exit where crowding and obstruction occurred while leaving visible and audible advocacy nearby. The counseling exception permits limited conversational approach inside the otherwise cleared area. The decree regulates demonstrating, not mere presence, and the exception applies per person or group approached rather than to two counselors in an entire zone.

The strongest objection is that existing conduct prohibitions and substantial compliance with the temporary order make further speech restraints unnecessary. Those matters must be considered, but they do not erase the supported exceptions, repeated access interference and pre-order conduct. Madsen does not require proof that every conceivable narrower command failed or that a wholly speech-neutral predecessor was first disobeyed. The established access problem, fixed geography and retained communication together support this limited arrangement. The prior temporary order already contained personal-distance and counseling restrictions; the Court does not describe it as an obstruction-only order.

Carroll requires precision in speech relief, and Claiborne Hardware ties relief to supported misconduct and responsible actors. Their demands are met at this qualified fixed scope, without making general medical distress or disagreement with a message sufficient. The injunction binds responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert; shared views, association or independent advocacy alone do not establish that relationship. The Court neither adjudicates new nonparty liability nor validates a larger or differently supported exclusion.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's heightened injunction-fit rule is applied without turning its earlier dimensions into safe harbors. Carroll's precision requirement and Claiborne Hardware's misconduct/coverage limits remain controlling.

### The fixed-zone counseling exception may end after a communicated refusal

**Holding and operative rule:** Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, the allowance for nonthreatening counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates verbally or nonverbally that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; the condition establishes neither silence everywhere nor a mobile personal radius beyond the fixed zone.

**Authority:** The same six Justices directly join Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's fixed-zone counseling holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court treats the counseling provision as an exception to valid access relief. It permits an initial peaceful approach without advance invitation. Its communicated-refusal condition keeps that exception from becoming the crowding or continued pursuit the valid corridor is designed to prevent. Unexpressed unwillingness does not trigger the condition.

Madsen rejected an advance-permission rule that obstructed peaceful initial advocacy across a broad area. This condition allows that initial encounter within a more confined, independently justified fixed zone, then ends the exception after a clear refusal. The distinction does not rest on a general right to avoid unwelcome ideas on a public street. Protected signs, leaflets, speech and approaches remain available outside the fixed corridor within lawful limits.

The contrary view treats the listener's refusal as an impermissible veto on protected speech. Refusal does not suppress the message here; it terminates the privilege to continue this close encounter inside a valid access restriction. The Court's holding rests on that remedial and geographic premise. It neither permits an unwilling listener to move a fifteen-foot boundary through otherwise available public space nor decides every independently drafted cessation rule.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's protection of peaceful initial approach is preserved, and its qualified access-remedy principle is applied to the limited counseling exception. No broader captive-audience rule is adopted.

### The moving-person restriction burdens more speech than the record warrants

**Holding and operative rule:** The injunction's fifteen-foot demonstration boundary moving with each person seeking access to or leaving a covered facility burdens more protected speech than necessary under Madsen and must be struck, even with its initial counseling exception and communicated-refusal qualification. This record-specific holding does not create a right to threaten, touch, obstruct, crowd or coercively pursue anyone, or invalidate every precisely supported separation rule.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's holding has eight direct rationale joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Breyer opposes present invalidation on the separately stated procedural ground.

**Controlling explanation:** A boundary that moves with a listener restricts ordinary conversational approach, passing leaflets and stationary advocacy outside stable access points. A speaker may enter it through another person's movement, and multiple moving zones make compliance uncertain. The initial counseling exception and refusal trigger reduce some burdens but do not cure the remaining uncertainty or protect every peaceful interaction nominally permitted.

The clinics rely on persistent pursuit, crowding and medical access needs. Those are substantial interests, and the existing decree directly forbids obstruction and physical interference. Madsen nevertheless requires a separate fit for the additional moving exclusion; supported misconduct does not authorize suppressing unrelated peaceful advocacy. The retained fixed corridors and independently lawful conduct provisions address the established access problems without this mobile speech boundary.

The lower court left the distance a zone could travel from the clinic unresolved and preserved a possible application concerning a different willing listener. The Court invents neither a travel cap nor an overlap prohibition to sustain the actual restraint. Carroll's precision principle bears special force where contempt enforces the boundary. The Court invalidates this provision at its established operation while leaving genuinely different and precisely supported lawful conduct relief open.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen supplies the distinct fit requirement and protection of peaceful initial approach; Carroll supplies precision. Claiborne Hardware preserves the connection between responsible misconduct and speech burdens.

### The moving-vehicle restriction independently fails

**Holding and operative rule:** The fifteen-foot demonstration radius moving with vehicles seeking access to or leaving covered facilities likewise burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be struck. The decision preserves traffic safety and fixed access protection without deciding the validity of a materially different, precisely supported vehicle restriction.

**Authority:** The same eight Justices directly join Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's vehicle-specific holding.

**Controlling explanation:** A moving vehicle radius can displace peaceful speakers on the sidewalk or curb as the vehicle changes position. The need to protect a driveway or parking entrance does not by itself establish a need for that further mobile exclusion. The valid fixed access corridors address the identified ingress and egress points, and independently lawful prohibitions on blocking or physical interference remain available.

Madsen requires examination of the burden added by this particular provision. General traffic safety is a significant interest, but it does not establish that all peaceful demonstration within fifteen feet of a moving vehicle must be forbidden. The Court neither denies that vehicles may require particular protection nor substitutes an untested smaller radius. This provision fails on its demonstrated fit, with any different proposed restriction requiring its own lawful basis.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's provision-specific tailoring is applied separately to vehicles; its fixed-access holding supplies no automatic mobile-zone permission.

### Removal of the moving predicate also removes the dependent retreat distance

**Holding and operative rule:** Once paragraph 1(b)'s moving-person and moving-vehicle restrictions are invalidated, paragraph 1(c)'s cross-reference cannot recreate an automatic fifteen-foot moving distance outside valid fixed zones. That dependent operation must be removed on remand; independently lawful conduct prohibitions remain, and the Court neither commands a substitute distance nor decides every cessation condition considered apart from the invalidated predicate.

**Authority:** The same eight Justices directly join Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's dependency and conforming-remedy holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Madsen requires removal of an invalidated injunction predicate and forbids restoring it through another clause. The retreat cross-reference derives its moving distance from the provision that now falls. Retaining the same mobile exclusion under a different label would leave the unconstitutional burden in place.

That consequence is distinct from sustaining the counseling condition inside an independently valid fixed zone. It is also distinct from deciding every conduct-only cessation rule that might be proposed. The lower court must conform the decree to the actual rulings, preserving only independently lawful unreviewed terms. The Court orders no replacement personal zone, travel endpoint or new contempt finding. Underlying state-law entitlement and other ungranted claims retain their ordinary unresolved posture.

**Precedent treatment:** Madsen's invalidated-predicate and no-evasion holdings directly govern the cross-reference; its refusal to require a personal-zone substitute remains intact.

## Precedent Treatment

- Madsen v. Women's Health Center supplies the content-neutral-injunction standard, provision-specific remedial fit, actual-concert coverage and removal of invalidated predicates. Its particular distances are applications; its rejection of broad advance permission does not prohibit every lawful response to pursuit, and its remand commands no substitute personal zone.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism retains its ordinary rule for generally applicable neutral regulation; the more demanding Madsen standard governs this injunction.
- Carroll v. President and Commissioners of Princess Anne supplies the need for precision where judicial restraints burden expression and contempt enforces them.
- NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co. preserves protected peaceful advocacy and confines relief to supported misconduct and responsible parties; viewpoint or association alone does not make a nonparty bound.
- Boos v. Barry preserves the distinction between neutral physical harm and offense at communicative content; no general unwilling-listener veto is recognized.

## Law After Decision

Madsen remains the governing standard. These qualified fixed access zones and their in-zone counseling condition survive, while the moving-person and moving-vehicle restraints and their dependent outside-zone distance fall. Stable geography and a retained initial counseling exception do not create a universal valid buffer size. Removal of a moving zone may not be evaded through a cross-reference. No general personal-space right, complete cessation prohibition, mandatory substitute injunction or newly adjudicated state-law remedial entitlement follows.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's fixed/mobile distinction and removal of the automatic retreat. In his view the supported repeated close pursuit permits a narrower provision on remand: preserve an initial peaceful approach, but after an objectively clear verbal or nonverbal refusal require the enjoined defendants to stop following and maintain a modest, precisely defined separation supported by the record. Direct conduct rules can address obstruction, touching, threats and harassment without making every speaker calculate an automatic moving fifteen-foot boundary. He would permit, not require, that carefully fitted provision. It would restrict neither nonparties, willing conversation, the initial peaceful approach, stationary advocacy nor speech addressed generally, and would create no undefined personal-space right or general listener's veto.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas.** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the Court's independent conclusion that the moving restraints fail Madsen and that their dependent distance must fall. They would invalidate the fixed expressive restrictions as well. Their stricter approach requires a legally supported remedial predicate and direct justification for restraining protected expression. The existing conduct prohibitions and substantial TRO compliance make the added expressive exclusion doubtful; appellate assumptions about what access relief might justify cannot replace the trial court's actual assessment. In particular, treating cessation as a discretionary counseling privilege does not establish its actual necessity when the lower rationale relies on unwilling listeners' personal space. General safety interests cannot by themselves establish entitlement to a private judicial remedy. They preserve lawful obstruction and physical-interference restraints and do not claim a right to threaten or coerce. Their disagreement with fixed relief is consistent with joining the sufficient Madsen ground against the moving restraints.

**Justice Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the qualified fixed access and counseling rulings but would leave the lower judgment intact pending the District Court's interpretation and possible modification of the disputed mobile terms. The lower court expressly left their geographic reach and particular applications unresolved and retained an ordinary route for clarification, including problems arising from escorts or overlapping encounters. In his view contextual construction and first-instance administration should precede invalidating the disputed operation. He does not approve an unlimited radius, supply a travel cap himself or conclude that every literal floating zone is constitutional. His disagreement concerns the Court's decision to invalidate now on this posture.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for a conforming preliminary decree. The qualified fixed access restrictions and communicated-refusal condition within them may remain. The person and vehicle moving zones and the cross-referenced automatic moving distance outside valid fixed zones must be removed. Independently lawful conduct provisions and unresolved underlying claims proceed under ordinary law; no replacement distance, new damages or contempt adjudication, final permanent injunction or particular filing date is ordered.
