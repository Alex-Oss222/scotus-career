## Standards and Tests selection: Modification of institutional consent decrees

### Modification of institutional consent decrees

**Governing question:** When Rule 60(b)(5) permits modification of a continuing institutional consent decree and how the change must be framed.

**Current rule:** The party seeking modification must prove a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable. An unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary, but actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief. Dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice. Once the threshold is met, the modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite the decree to the constitutional minimum. Practical administration and cost may inform the selection of lawful relief but cannot substitute for the threshold showing or authorize unconstitutional conditions. A clarification that the Constitution permits an alternative practice does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted stricter obligation.

**Authority by component:**

- Significant change, inequity, movant's burden, and significance of accepted risk: Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail — Threshold for modifying an institutional consent decree, January 15, 1992.
- Responsive modification, preservation of remaining purposes, and constitutional and financial limits: Rufo — Fit and limits of the proposed modification, January 15, 1992. Both components are controlled by Stone's opinion joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; O'Connor's remand vote does not join that rationale.

**Present operation effective:** Rufo, January 15, 1992; additional covered-prison-relief conditions from PLRA, April 26, 1996, as applied in Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996.


**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The decree remains binding until lawfully modified; a request for modification does not itself suspend its obligations.

**Holdings navigation:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail; Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367 (1992) — Prospective equitable relief; Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996).

**Exceptions and limits:** For covered prison-conditions prospective relief, the original §3626 conditions and separate termination/stay mechanisms apply alongside this general rule; see Prison-conditions prospective relief and Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays. PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text (§802, April 26, 1996) and Lewis v. Casey — Holdings IV–VI (June 24, 1996) preserve that additional statutory operation. A pending Rule 60 request alone is not substituted for the statute's actual motion and timing requirements. Modification under Rule 60(b)(5) concerns the continuing prospective operation of an existing institutional decree following a demonstrated significant change in fact or law. It does not establish a legislative power to compel reinstatement of a completed private damages or limitations judgment, nor does it decide whether a separate valid statutory remedy may provide collateral relief from such a judgment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

### Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

**Governing question:** Whether an omitted Teague defense must be entertained when first raised in Supreme Court merits briefing.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. When the State omitted it below and in its certiorari opposition and first asserted it in merits briefing, the Court may decline consideration. The permission is discretionary, not a mandatory waiver rule. A consistently preserved subsidiary defense limiting the very constitutional rule and habeas relief under review presents the distinct fair-inclusion setting addressed in Caspari; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Discretion to decline the late nonjurisdictional defense: Schiro v. Farley — Late presentation of nonretroactivity, 1994-01-19. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens also agree with merits reach, but their agreement is unnecessary to the opinion's controlling authority.
- Preserved subsidiary-defense distinction: Caspari v. Bohlen — The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here, 1994-02-23. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Present operation effective:** Schiro v. Farley, 1994-01-19, for discretionary treatment of the late defense. Caspari, 1994-02-23, supplies the distinct preserved-defense comparison, not a later change to that discretion.

**Related current rules:** “Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review” supplies the question-presentation boundary. Neither decision changes Teague’s new-rule inquiry or either contemporary exception.

**Holdings navigation:** Late presentation of nonretroactivity; The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here.

## Standards and Tests selection: Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

### Parole ineligibility as an answer to future-danger evidence

**Governing question:** When a capital defendant must be allowed to explain the only lawful alternative to death.

**Current rule:** When the State puts future dangerousness in issue and the only lawful alternative to death is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity to inform the jury of that ineligibility, through defense presentation or an adequate instruction. No single verbal formula is required. Speculative clemency does not make ordinary parole available. On direct review, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that denying the explanation did not contribute to the jury’s selection of death; where it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is required if it continues to seek death. The error is not classified as structural.

**Authority by component:**

- Trigger, effective means and error remedy: Simmons v. South Carolina — Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case; The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued, 1994-06-17. Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary. Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.

**Present operation effective:** Simmons v. South Carolina, 1994-06-17.

**Open question:** The decision does not establish the instruction requirement when the State has not raised future danger or when the legally available alternatives materially differ.

**Holdings navigation:** Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case; The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued.

## Standards and Tests selection: Civil forfeiture and criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy

### Civil forfeiture and criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy

**Governing question:** Classification of forfeiture under §§881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A) for successive-punishment purposes.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress intended civil proceedings. Civil intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. Neither a civil label nor proceeding against property supplies an irrebuttable exemption. Deterrence, burden on an owner, connection to crime and owner defenses alone do not establish the demanding contrary showing. Forfeiture’s nonpunitive functions include removing facilitating property, discouraging unlawful use and recovering actual illicit proceeds; it is not universally limited to reimbursing Government costs under Halper’s method for fixed personal civil penalties. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy criminal punishment; Austin’s distinct protection remains.

**Authority by component:**

- Civil-intent/clearest-proof test and separate clause classification: I. These civil forfeiture proceedings do not constitute a second criminal punishment; II. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy punishment, 1996-06-24; O’Connor with Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Ursery and United States Currency, 1996-06-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** Halper’s own setting and Kurth Ranch’s extraordinary-tax rule survive. Statutory nexus, provenance, ownership, owner defenses and excessiveness remain independent. Whether punitive civil proceedings place a person in jeopardy, offense identity under Blockburger/retained Grady and coordinated-proceeding treatment remain unadjudicated.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency, 518 U.S. 267 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

### Continued psychiatric confinement after an insanity-acquittal predicate ends

**Governing question:** Whether a State may continue psychiatric confinement under insanity-acquittee authority after conceding that the acquittee is no longer mentally ill.

**Current rule:** The State may not continue that confinement solely because the acquittee cannot prove harmlessness. The acquittal supplies neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric authority after the illness predicate disappears. Continued psychiatric custody requires constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing its own proper grounds; ordinary civil commitment requires current mental illness and dangerousness proved by clear and convincing evidence, and additional procedure cannot supply a missing substantive ground. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure an indefinite psychiatric regime requiring a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee to prove nondangerousness. Any independently lawful restraint must rest on its own authority and procedures; the holding does not prescribe a universal preventive-detention burden or scrutiny tier.

**Authority by component:**
- Ended predicate and absence of punitive authority: Foucha v. Louisiana, “1. The insanity judgment no longer authorizes this psychiatric confinement”, May 18, 1992; White Part II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).
- Appropriate new grounds and procedures; ordinary civil-commitment burden: Foucha, “2. Psychiatric custody requires appropriate grounds and procedures,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (five). Addington's existing burden is retained within that channel.
- Limits of the asserted preventive alternative: Foucha, “3. Public safety does not sustain this unbounded dangerousness regime,” May 18, 1992; White Part II-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter (six).

**Present operation effective:** Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992.

**Open question:** Materially narrower preventive regimes, maximum duration of an otherwise lawful commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses remain undecided. The equal-protection plurality supplies no controlling rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992) — Psychiatric confinement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

### Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern equal protection of commitment proof and family participation?

**Current rule:** Kentucky's clear-and-convincing burden for adult intellectual-disability commitment, compared with reasonable-doubt proof for mental illness, fails rational-basis review because the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not justify the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Lawful equalization and severability must be addressed; Addington's federal minimum is unchanged and no national reasonable-doubt requirement or automatic release follows. Separately, continuing family knowledge rationally supports guardians' and immediate relatives' party participation for lifelong developmental disability. Such participation is not categorically unfair where the adult has counsel and effective opportunity to contest evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives cannot replace statutory grounds, control decision or suppress the adult's position. Heightened scrutiny remains undecided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

### Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

**Governing question:** Whether a later law may withdraw mandatory discretionary-parole reconsideration for an earlier offender by doubling hearing intervals or permitting individualized postponement for up to three years.

**Current rule:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining intervening review is established. That binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence and ultimate release discretion; the prisoner need not prove the omitted hearing would have produced release. Relief restores the consideration opportunity, not an entitlement to parole. An individualized initial prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that prediction do not preserve later mandatory opportunities to present rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken forecast. Where the actual three-year deferral removes meaningful annual consideration while parole is legally available and no ordinary substitute is established, it increases punishment. The prisoner must show the earlier entitlement, retrospective actual withdrawal and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient. Once shown, the State must substantiate a substitute it invokes through its legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide that consideration. The prisoner retains the ultimate burden of proving an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory annual-to-biennial withdrawal, proof and relief boundary: Cavanaugh v. Roller — The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record, 1993-11-30. Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, its application to Roller, and the prospective remedy. Ginsburg's conditional agreement in her separate opinion adds no vote to this controlling coalition.

- The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here: The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here, 1995-04-25. Part II of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the same rule, application and relief.
- The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes: The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes, 1995-04-25. Part III of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices expressly adopt this bounded evidentiary direction and its application. Recognition of a source limitation in the dissent is not an additional join.

**Present operation effective:** Morales, April 25, 1995, for the bounded individualized-deferral application and claimant/substitute substantiation rule; Cavanaugh retains its original scope.

**Open question:** Materially different individualized arrangements and demonstrated interim review remain unresolved. No automatic ban on every frequency reduction, universal probability formula, numerical response deadline or general presumption shifting ultimate constitutional proof is adopted. Changed suitability or voting rules, parole abolition and extraordinary clemency are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record; The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here; The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

### Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

**Governing question:** Viewpoint-neutral access for private religious expression and the distinct establishment inquiry in the forums and benefits actually opened.

**Current rule:** When a public school admits private family-life and child-rearing discussion to an after-hours forum, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible presentation solely for its religious viewpoint, even assuming a nonpublic forum. Neutral restrictions outside the forum's actual subjects remain possible. Equal access to voluntary, publicly accessible after-hours films without school sponsorship has a secular forum purpose, does not primarily advance religion as governmental activity and causes no excessive entanglement. That establishment holding concerns private speech, not an official devotional program; it supplies no replacement for the inherited establishment framework or universal ruling on worship restrictions. A State may not categorically exclude an otherwise eligible private unattended religious display from a forum actually opened to comparable expression merely because its message is religious or its location adjoins or lies within a capitol. Pinette’s access holding includes the proposal’s offered private-attribution safeguard; it establishes neither adequacy of a later sign nor a comprehensive establishment test. Chabad separately holds that an interior capitol location does not establish religion when actual context makes the expression clearly private. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can alter that conclusion; no forum label or unspecified sign decides attribution. In a generally available, privately edited student-publication program, a religious editorial viewpoint cannot exclude an otherwise eligible publication from printing support. Its secular expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent attribution and controlled vendor payments together permit that benefit; neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing or governmental worship.

## Standards and Tests selection: Program-wide athletic opportunity and independent sex-classification review

### Program-wide athletic opportunity and independent sex-classification review

**Governing question:** Title IX accommodation and constitutional review during public-university athletic restructuring.

**Current rule:** Title IX does not guarantee a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity. Effective accommodation may be shown by any one of substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex’s interests and abilities; other regulatory duties remain independently binding. A public university’s sex-conscious restructuring separately must serve an actual important objective and be substantially related to it. Genuine fiscal or programmatic change may preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality, but ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient. Stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Authority by component:**

- Program-wide participation opportunity: Program-wide participation opportunity, 1995-05-22. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.
- Independent constitutional scrutiny: Independent constitutional scrutiny, 1995-05-22. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-22; the component authorities identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Program-wide participation opportunity; Independent constitutional scrutiny.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prospective officer enforcement of IGRA negotiations

### Prospective officer enforcement of IGRA negotiations

**Governing question:** The available officer route and complete remedial progression under §2710(d).

**Current rule:** Ex parte Young permits a prospective official-capacity suit against an officer connected to an ongoing violation of IGRA's good-faith negotiating duty, within IGRA's complete sequence and remedial limits. State-directed language and remedial detail do not exclude that route under this statute. A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers good-faith negotiation, not acceptance of preferred compact terms; a failure-to-negotiate action under §2710(d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's burden to prove good faith. Nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith. An adverse finding triggers an order to conclude a compact within sixty days. The court may consider public interest, public safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming; it shall consider a demand for direct taxation of the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith.

If that period passes without agreement, both sides submit last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the one best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the court's findings and order. Submission of that selection to both parties begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent makes it an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe, consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion in mediator selection is not repeated verbatim in the Secretary provision. Nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal, and failure to agree does not itself authorize contempt or an added order compelling agreement.

**Authority by component:**

- Connected officer action and enacted sequence: Seminole Tribe — Holdings II–III, March 27, 1996; Souter for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, Breyer and himself (five).

**Present operation effective:** Seminole Tribe, March 27, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No freestanding broader injunction, enlarged negotiating duty or retrospective Treasury compensation follows. Incorporating state gaming law into the federal duty does not itself turn the claim into a Pennhurst state-law action. Section (d)(4) grants no new taxing power, but preserves regulatory-cost assessments agreed under (d)(3)(C)(iii); this differs from the mandatory evidentiary treatment of direct-tax demands. Lack of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation. The effective-compact action under (A)(ii), Secretary enforcement under (A)(iii) and an asserted substitute administrative route remain distinct. Section 2721 severability satisfies none of the statutory predicates.

**Controlling construction:** Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain independent. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA violations, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction produces approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA; Federal Register publication remains necessary.

**Open question:** No general rule that every detailed federal statute preserves officer equity is adopted, and no rule for every separate obstruction sanction or automatic Secretary bypass is decided. Immunity removal proves neither bad faith nor the remaining merits.

**Holdings navigation:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44.

## Standards and Tests selection: Boundary-preserving conditions for artificial coastal extension

### Boundary-preserving conditions for artificial coastal extension

**Governing question:** The Corps' authority under Rivers and Harbors Act §10, 33 U.S.C. §403, to protect federal submerged-land rights when permitting an artificial coastal extension.

**Current rule:** Section 10 permits consideration of an artificial extension's prospective effect on federal submerged-land rights and permits conditioning approval on preservation of the existing federal-state boundary for additional entitlement attributable to the project. The Submerged Lands Act does not compel unconditional approval or invalidate such a condition when existing state property and claims remain intact. The condition must address the same boundary harm supporting withholding of approval; this holding supplies no power to demand unrelated existing territory or approve different conditions.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory power, attributable-extension limit and preserved state interests: United States v. Alaska, April 21, 1992, “Statutory authority for the boundary-preserving condition,” White's Part II for all nine Justices. The source adopts this construction through the Nome condition's stated limits; it does not grant unconditional approval to all future disclaimers.

**Present operation effective:** Alaska, April 21, 1992.

**Open question:** Natural accretion, compulsory surrender of existing state territory, different permit conditions and a general rule governing States' constitutional takings remedies remain undecided.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Alaska, 503 U.S. 569 (1992) — Federal and state submerged-land boundaries.

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

##### Falconer announces a new rule for this collateral proceeding

**Holding:** Falconer’s specific requirement governing the interaction of these murder and voluntary-manslaughter instructions was not dictated by precedent at Taylor’s finality and is therefore a new rule under Teague. The State’s concession of error under Falconer does not waive its preserved retroactivity objection, and this conclusion does not approve the charge on direct review or exclude all defense-instruction errors from due process.

#### Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1500.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here

**Holding:** In this habeas case, Missouri's consistently asserted Teague defense is fairly included as a subsidiary limit on applying the very constitutional rule and granting the writ challenged by the petition, although the stated question names the double-jeopardy issue rather than Teague. The Court therefore considers that nonjurisdictional defense; preservation, disclosure in the petition's body or logical priority alone does not make every independent omitted question reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido: applied to distinguish subsidiary legal arguments from distinct questions; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate requirements.
- Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.: preserved; an omitted independent question does not become reviewable merely through notice, logical priority or ordinary economy. This confined same-rule habeas limitation is distinguished from its separate intervention determination.
- Schiro v. Farley: distinguished on presentation; the defense there first appeared at merits briefing after earlier omissions. Its discretionary treatment of a nonjurisdictional defense remains intact.

##### The requested extension was a new rule at finality

**Holding:** Bohlen's conviction and sentence became final on January 2, 1986, and the rule forbidding renewed proof of persistent-offender status in this noncapital proceeding was not dictated by the precedent then in force. Federal habeas relief therefore depends on a recognized Teague exception; the Court does not decide whether the proposed double-jeopardy protection should govern a case eligible for a decision on the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt the finality-specific new-rule determination, not a prospective rule permitting every noncapital sentencing rehearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applied to the date at which the state judgment became final and the distinction between dictated applications and new rules.
- Graham v. Collins: applied in its controlling form; a dictated application does not become new merely because the facts differ or courts disagree.
- Gilmore v. Taylor: applied to preserve the possibility of a genuinely new noncapital extension after substantive comparison.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; their extension to this noncapital status proceeding was not dictated at finality, and their ultimate reach is not decided.
- United States v. DiFrancesco: applied as prefinality support for distinguishing sentencing determinations from offense acquittals.
- Griffith v. Kentucky: used for the finality convention; direct review was exhausted and the certiorari period expired on January 2, with January 1 a legal holiday.
- United States v. Dixon: its retention of Grady remains in force but does not answer whether this sentencing-status proceeding has the necessary jeopardy character.

##### Neither Teague exception permits this writ

**Holding:** The proposed rule falls within neither the exception for rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, nor the exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Its protection against renewed litigation of offender status does not remove the robberies or these authorized sentences from lawful punishment and does not supply a watershed accuracy procedure; the habeas grant on this new rule must therefore be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices apply both exceptions as they exist at this decision's date. Neither exception is eliminated or prospectively narrowed out of existence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applies both contemporary exceptions after finding a new rule; neither is abolished.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applies the substantive exception's protection against punishment beyond the State's constitutional power; this claim does not satisfy it.
- Sawyer v. Smith: applies the distinction between an important procedural protection and the exceptional accuracy-and-fairness requirement.
- Bullington v. Missouri: preserved; refusing retroactive extension does not overrule its established capital-acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both contemporary Teague exceptions remain. The State's consistently presented defense is subsidiary here; preservation, mention in the petition body and logical priority do not automatically make an omitted independent question reviewable. The ultimate constitutional reach of double jeopardy to the noncapital status hearing is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas grant on this claim is reversed, 8–1, under Teague. No new State proof opportunity, replacement sentence or release deadline is prescribed, and unrelated properly preserved claims are not disturbed.

#### Simmons v. South Carolina, 512 U.S. 154

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-9059.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

##### Parole ineligibility must be available to answer the State's future-danger case

**Holding:** When the State places future dangerousness at issue in a capital sentencing proceeding and the only legally available noncapital sentence is life imprisonment without ordinary parole, due process requires an effective opportunity for the defendant to inform the jury of that ineligibility through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. A direction to disregard parole and give life imprisonment its ordinary meaning does not satisfy that requirement on this record; the rule neither requires a particular script nor promises immunity from clemency or future legislation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Seven Justices directly adopt this Fourteenth Amendment rule and its application; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The operative alternative was life without ordinary parole under §24-21-640's second-or-subsequent violent-conviction rule following separate sentencing for the prior conviction; offenses within one day or one continuous course count as one. The eligibility premise is uncontested here, not a rule that every life sentence excludes parole. The State may still prove danger to inmates or guards and offer truthful information about commutation or pardon.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gardner v. Florida, 430 U.S. 349 (1977): its opportunity-to-answer principle supplies the due-process foundation for responding to material sentencing information.
- Skipper v. South Carolina, 476 U.S. 1 (1986): its future-danger rebuttal principle is applied to a fixed legal sentencing alternative; its independent mitigation rule remains intact.
- California v. Ramos, 463 U.S. 992 (1983): distinguished; permission to give truthful commutation information does not authorize withholding this responsive fact about ordinary parole.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993, and Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: their effective-mitigation rules remain controlling within their scope; neither is treated as having already decided this separate legal-information question.

##### The error is not harmless on this record and requires a new penalty proceeding if death is pursued

**Holding:** Under Chapman's direct-review standard, the State cannot establish beyond a reasonable doubt that barring the parole-ineligibility explanation did not contribute to this jury's selection of death, given the State's future-danger argument, the complete exclusion of the legal answer and the jury's express parole inquiry. The death-sustaining judgment is reversed and the case remanded for a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; a lawful noncapital disposition remains available, and the convictions and separate sentences are undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt this record-specific harmlessness determination and mandatory further-hearing direction. O'Connor and Kennedy join reversal but reserve the further remedial determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court adopts no independently sufficient Eighth Amendment instruction rule. It does not determine an entitlement to parole information where the State has not raised future danger, settle a disputed parole-eligibility question, require a fixed form of words, guarantee against clemency or future legislation, or announce automatic reversal for every omission. The State may pursue a lawful noncapital disposition instead of another capital hearing. No guilt retrial, acquittal, release, invalidation of the separate sentences or general prohibition of capital punishment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applied; the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, and fails on this record.
- Romano v. Oklahoma, June 13, 1994: distinguished as an affirmative disclosure of another jury's death decision; this decision neither supplies a missing majority for its further-remedy positions nor changes that case's mandate.
- Johnson v. Texas, June 24, 1993: preserves the distinction between correcting a capital sentencing defect and disturbing guilt; no automatic noncapital sentence follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The death-sustaining judgment is reversed, 7–2, and the case remanded. Five Justices require a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death; the State may instead pursue a lawful noncapital disposition. The existing death sentence cannot be retained by a further harmlessness finding on this omission. Guilt and the separate life and thirty-day sentences remain undisturbed.

#### Allridge v. Scott

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 93-9137; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 41 F.3d 213.
**Decided:** January 15, 1996.

##### Ordinary parole availability and a proposed release-prediction instruction

**Holding:** Simmons's right to explain legal parole ineligibility does not require a favorable prediction of eventual release when the noncapital alternative permits ordinary parole. The claimed prediction entitlement fails; this conclusion neither categorically bars truthful parole evidence nor permits a misleading account of the lawful alternative.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I-A, unanimous.

**Independent alternative holding:** Habeas relief on the proposed favorable parole-improbability instruction is independently unavailable because no basis establishes that this different rule was dictated when the conviction became final before Penry in 1989 or that either then-existing Teague exception applies: substantive rules excluding conduct, classes of persons or punishments from state power, or watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's Part I-B; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional ground.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Affirm rejection, unanimous in judgment, without reaching harmlessness. The information rule neither removes conduct, a class or punishment from state power nor is established as watershed merely because useful against future-danger evidence. A fixed statutory-minimum claim would require separate presentation and retroactivity inquiry and is not decided. Simmons is distinguished on ordinary parole availability without a new prison-danger exception; Teague, Gilmore and Caspari retain their claim-specific finality rule and both exceptions.

##### Effective consideration of actually admitted capital mitigation

**Holding:** A preserved mitigation claim cannot be rejected merely because admitted evidence fails to establish a uniquely severe permanent disability to which the crime is conclusively attributable. Where actual evidence supports material culpability mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger, the reviewing court must determine from the charge, evidence and trial as a whole whether there is a reasonable likelihood that jurors could not give that mitigation effect while answering the special issues truthfully; mere possibility is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The father's admitted lay testimony concerning prison abuse and alleged mental illness was rejected under an unlawful categorical severe-disability/crime-linkage requirement. Lack of diagnostic expertise limits what it proves; it does not erase admitted lay evidence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no psychiatric diagnosis, credibility, causation, material independent mitigating force, inadequate sentencing route or actual instructional violation. The evidence may remain weak, unsupported or adequately usable under the actual charge; the jury need not give favorable weight. No expert-only gate, categorical additional special issue or facial invalidation of Texas's scheme is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate rejection of only the admitted-evidence mitigation component and return it for the functional inquiry and the bounded further review stated below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham's independent-culpability, background, youth and prefinality rules apply; its fractured positive-character component is not enlarged. Johnson and Boyde supply whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review. Jurek's conditional approval survives. Lockett and Eddings require effective mitigating use, not compelled favorable weight. Tuggle separates an insufficient reviewing rationale from present entitlement to a writ.

##### Instruction claims resting on unoffered medical evidence

**Holding:** A defendant cannot obtain a mitigation instruction based on the assumed content of medical evidence never presented merely because offering it might also support future dangerousness. That independent instruction claim fails; a supported challenge to actual unconstitutional exclusion or a legal impediment remains distinct and is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the component's rejection, without a ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Strategic difficulty does not establish an absent diagnosis, admissibility or mitigating significance. The rule does not demand formal presentation of evidence unconstitutionally excluded, create an expert-only requirement or adverse inference from silence, reduce the State's aggravation burden, or decide ineffective assistance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of this component, unanimous in judgment; the admitted-evidence inquiry remains separate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Delo's supporting-evidence predicate applies without displacing Graham's protection of supported mitigation or foreclosing a supported unconstitutional-exclusion claim.

##### Review correction and eventual punishment-only habeas relief

**Holding:** Vacating the admitted-mitigation review ruling establishes neither an available violation nor entitlement to relief. That component requires review of the actual evidence, full charge, prefinality law, genuinely available defenses and any necessary error-effect inquiry; if a defect ultimately requires correction, the relief concerns punishment through adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, leaving guilt intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The instruction request establishes no exhaustion or preservation of every reformulated theory. Applicable factual presumptions and genuinely available defenses remain; no forfeited defense revives or settled issue reopens. Graham's established rule cannot be called new merely because another defendant invokes it, but a materially new extension requires its own Teague inquiry. Whether a distinct error-effect inquiry is required, its standard, actual effect and relief are reserved; the full charge, testimony, claim-specific rulings and properly preserved submissions are necessary before a writ.

**Operative remedy or transition:** No present writ, sentencing hearing, release, stay, deadline or execution authorization. Brady, lesser-offense and other unreviewed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Tuggle supplies the review/relief distinction without transferring its Ake subject. Brecht and O'Neal retain their category-specific Chapman holdings; neither they nor Chapman establish the proposed capital-mitigation extension here.

#### California Department of Corrections v. Morales

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1462.
**Decided:** April 25, 1995.

##### The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here

**Holding:** Applying the 1981 California hearing-deferral provision to Morales’s 1980 offense violates the Ex Post Facto Clause because the actual three-year deferral withdraws offense-date mandatory annual consideration during a period when parole is legally available, without an established ordinary substitute for the meaningful intervening opportunities. The separately reasoned prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that initial prediction do not preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances, or a materially mistaken forecast; relief restores consideration, not release.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: applies its opportunity rule to this reserved individualized setting; its original holding and rejection of proof of the omitted hearing’s outcome remain in force.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies protection of a legally available avenue to reduced custody and the distinction between guaranteed benefits and discretionary alternatives; it supplies no right to parole.
- Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987): applies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged outer limits and a possibly identical result.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990): preserves the punishment boundary; no free-standing rule against every retrospective procedural disadvantage is adopted.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): accepts its separate-reasons construction, permitted factual overlap and initial review; its state-court federal characterization does not displace Cavanaugh or establish ordinary later reopening.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1981 amendment retains annual review as the default. It permits a next hearing no later than three years after denial only for a prisoner convicted, in the same or different proceedings, of more than one offense involving the taking of a life, when the Board finds that parole cannot reasonably be expected at a hearing during the intervening years and states its bases. Jackson requires reasons separately directed to postponement, permits their supporting facts to overlap the present-unsuitability reasons, and establishes review of the initial choice. It establishes no generally available later reopening procedure. The rule concerns Morales's actual maximum deferral, not a proved cohort-wide practice; his eligibility arose during the postponed interval.

##### The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes

**Holding:** A prisoner challenging retrospective withdrawal of a parole-consideration opportunity must show the earlier legal entitlement, its retrospective actual withdrawal, and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and proof that the omitted hearing would actually have produced parole is unnecessary. After that showing, the State must substantiate any replacement mechanism it invokes by identifying its legal basis and establishing its practical availability and timely capacity to provide the relevant consideration; the prisoner retains the ultimate burden of establishing an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: preserves meaningful opportunity loss and the absence of an actual-release prerequisite; this decision expressly clarifies substantiation of an asserted replacement.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies its refusal to assume discretionary alternatives compensate for guaranteed benefits; it did not itself prescribe this parole evidentiary direction.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): confines review and conceivable advancement to what the opinion establishes; initial review does not become later reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns Morales’s demonstrated meaningful loss under the challenged 1981 provision. It does not invalidate every individualized postponement, require an interim hearing in every possible individualized scheme, or constitutionalize every scheduling detail. Maximum deferral is permission, not a statutory command or a proved near-uniform cohort practice. The Court neither requires wholly different facts for the two decisions nor treats the initial forecast as unreviewable.

No universal probability threshold, general presumption transferring constitutional proof to the State, numerical response deadline, mandatory filing procedure, suitability finding, release date, damages award, or right to favorable parole is adopted. Adequacy of a demonstrated materially different arrangement, later amendments and their transition or mootness effects are not decided. The false-information and sentence claims are not reopened. Unresolved credit and matrix calculations establish neither release entitlement nor legal impossibility of earlier release.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's hearing-frequency judgment is affirmed, 5–4, preserving its remand for offense-date annual consideration. Lawful suitability standards and release discretion remain; no parole, release date or damages are ordered. Rejected false-information and sentence claims remain undisturbed.

#### United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency, 518 U.S. 267 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-345 and 95-346.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether the reviewed civil forfeitures impose a second criminal punishment

**Holding:** For forfeiture under sections 881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A), the court first determines whether Congress intended civil proceedings; that intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. These schemes and the grounds established here do not meet that demanding contrary-proof standard, so the challenged forfeitures supply no double-jeopardy bar to the respective later proceeding; neither a civil label nor an action against property creates an irrebuttable exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. Scalia and Thomas join only the judgments; additional judgment votes do not enlarge the rationale.

**Material application:** Qualified incorporation of customs procedures and the civil statutory mechanisms establish civil intent. Removing facilitating property, discouraging unlawful use and recovering illicit proceeds supply nonpunitive functions beyond reimbursing enforcement costs. Loss, deterrence, a crime connection and an owner defense alone do not satisfy clearest proof.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither each asset’s nexus, provenance or ownership nor excessiveness, attachment of personal jeopardy, offense identity under Blockburger and retained Grady, coordination of proceedings or settlement waiver. Distinct statutory defenses remain: §§881(a)(6)/(7) protect an owner’s interest upon proof that the relevant act or omission occurred without knowledge or consent; §981(a)(2) protects an owner or lienholder upon proof of lack of knowledge. Section 981(a)(1)(A)’s §5313(a) exception bars seizure/forfeiture for that reporting violation by a federally supervised domestic financial institution, an SEC-regulated institution, or their partner, director or employee. Customs procedures apply only insofar as applicable and consistent with the forfeiture statute.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ursery reversed and remanded, 7–2 (Stone-Zsela and Stevens would affirm); Currency reversed and remanded, 8–1 (Stone-Zsela favors vacatur and a narrower remand). All nine reject Currency’s blanket bar, but no unanimous rationale or mandate follows. Properly remaining criminal, probable-cause, excessive-fines and property-authority objections remain; no automatic transfer or new sentence.

##### Whether Excessive Fines coverage itself establishes Double Jeopardy punishment

**Holding:** A forfeiture's coverage by the Excessive Fines Clause does not, without the separate civil-intent and clearest-proof inquiry, establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy purposes. Austin's protection and any properly presented excessiveness objection remain effective; today's classification decides neither excessiveness nor an exclusive test for measuring it.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** Austin’s partly punitive coverage protects against excessive exactions; it does not replace the separate criminal-classification inquiry. Halper’s fixed personal civil-penalty accounting and Kurth Ranch’s combined-operation tax holding retain their distinct fields.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No instrumentality-only fines rule or finding that these forfeitures are nonexcessive. James Daniel Good’s prior-process protection and demonstrated-exigency exception, Bennis’s innocent-owner protection, and Libretti’s statutory and third-party limits remain independently operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242:** Applies legislative-intent and demanding contrary-proof analysis; civil designation is relevant but not conclusive.
- **United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S. 354:** Applies the civil-forfeiture classification test, including the clearest-proof exception, rather than categorical immunity for proceedings against property.
- **Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez, 372 U.S. 144:** Preserves consideration of punitive purpose and effect; no individual indicator is conclusive and no new mandatory exhaustive checklist is announced.
- **United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435:** Preserves its exceptional fixed-personal-civil-penalty accounting rule, including reasonable remedial approximation, while distinguishing forfeiture's broader nonpunitive functions. It is not overruled or converted into universal asset-value accounting.
- **Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, June 6, 1994:** Preserves the combined-operation punishment holding for the extraordinary arrest-linked assessment on confiscated contraband; no universal compensation ceiling follows.
- **Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993:** Preserves Excessive Fines coverage and separate excessiveness review. Its partly punitive coverage rule does not automatically answer the Double Jeopardy question.
- **Witte v. United States, June 14, 1995:** Preserves the distinction between consideration of conduct and an independent punishment, and its separate coordination limits; coordination cannot cure an otherwise established successive-prosecution bar.
- **Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299; Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508; United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992; and United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993:** Preserve necessary-offense and essential-conduct rules and the distinct-agreement limitation. The Court does not reach their application here and does not replace retained Grady with an elements-only rule.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, December 13, 1993:** Preserves notice and meaningful opportunity before civil seizure of real property; exigency requires showing that less restrictive measures would not prevent sale, destruction or continued unlawful use, with appropriate subsequent process.
- **Bennis v. Michigan, March 4, 1996:** Preserves its stated innocent-owner protection; no qualifying innocent-owner finding is established here.
- **Libretti v. United States, November 7, 1995:** Preserves statutory authority and third-party limits despite agreement; it supplies no settlement waiver in these cases.

No precedent is overruled. The separate writings' broader or contrary theories are not the Court's treatment of precedent.

#### Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-5844.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether the insanity acquittal continues to authorize psychiatric custody after the State concedes the end of mental illness

**Holding:** When the State concedes that an insanity acquittee is no longer mentally ill, it may not continue his psychiatric confinement under the existing insanity-acquittee authority solely because he cannot prove that he will be harmless. The original acquittal supports neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric custody after its illness predicate disappears.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-A, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this proposition and application.

**Material application:** The conceded absence of present mental illness is not cured by antisocial personality or a doctor’s inability to guarantee safety. An insanity acquittal is not a conviction authorizing punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the continuing illness-and-danger basis of insanity commitment; initial verdict-based commitment and rejection of an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling remain intact.
- O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563 (1975): applied to the requirement that confinement end when its authorizing basis disappears; the Court does not equate a conditional-release recommendation with a finding of harmlessness.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the required relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose.

##### Whether Louisiana may preserve psychiatric custody without new adequate grounds and proceedings

**Holding:** Once the insanity-acquittal basis for holding Foucha as a mental patient has ended, Louisiana cannot preserve that psychiatric custody without constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing the grounds required for it. If the State invokes ordinary civil commitment, it must establish current mental illness and dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence; additional procedure cannot supply an absent substantive ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: five of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone does not join this section's separate Vitek-based procedural characterization.

**Material application:** A counseled hearing about danger alone cannot supply the absent illness predicate. Ordinary civil commitment requires clear and convincing proof of present illness and dangerousness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Vitek v. Jones, 445 U.S. 480 (1980): applied to psychiatric institutionalization's distinct liberty deprivation and need for appropriate grounds and procedures.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the need for an independently authorized commitment proceeding after the initial basis expires.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418 (1979): applied unchanged to the clear-and-convincing burden for ordinary civil commitment; that burden is not made universal for every preventive custody channel.
- Baxstrom v. Herold, 383 U.S. 107 (1966): relied on for the ordinary protections attending a new civil commitment after the prior detention basis ends; no separate majority equal-protection holding is created here.

##### Whether indefinite psychiatric confinement may rest on failure to prove nondangerousness after mental illness ends

**Holding:** Louisiana's scheme permitting indefinite psychiatric confinement of a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee because he has not proved nondangerousness violates due process. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure the absence of a carefully limited substantive authority; the Court leaves materially narrower preventive regimes undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-C, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient rejection of Louisiana's asserted alternative preventive justification. The holding adopts no new general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness proof standard.

**Material application:** Periodic review does not cure an indefinite scheme without carefully limited substantive authority. Salerno’s focused pretrial regime does not justify this post-acquittal confinement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. #### Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-783.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Program-wide participation opportunity

**Holding:** Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Lawful separate teams remain subject to equal opportunity; effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, with any one route sufficient and distinct regulatory requirements independently binding.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Separate teams are permitted for competitive-skill selection or contact sports while equal opportunity remains required. When a noncontact team is offered only to one sex, the excluded sex must receive tryouts if its athletic opportunities previously were limited. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Proportionality is an alternative safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Men's participation remained above substantial proportionality after the cut; complete compliance for women and independently regulated facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits are not decided or excused.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applies lawful delegated interpretation; authorizes no quota contrary to statute.
- Martin v. Occupational Safety & Health Review Commission, 499 U.S. 144: applies reasonable regulatory interpretation within its terms.

##### Independent constitutional scrutiny

**Holding:** A public university may consider sex during genuine fiscal or programmatic restructuring to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient; stereotypes, pretext and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The record includes demonstrated earlier denied opportunities, persistent inequality and preservation of actual women's swimming places during genuine restructuring, not percentages alone. The rule operates symmetrically for a demonstrably disadvantaged sex. Sexual orientation, gender identity, transgender status and individual team eligibility are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applies intermediate scrutiny to sex-conscious treatment.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applies genuine-objective and connected-disadvantage requirements.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547: its racial-treatment analysis supplies no changed sex standard or federal-compliance immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm summary judgment for the University on the distinct Title IX and equal-protection claims. No reinstatement, damages or remand is ordered, and no broad section 1983 preclusion rule is adopted.

#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. **Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. **Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

The engineering alternative requires three Mary Baldwin years plus two at Washington University at private tuition without the proposed final-two-year public discount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 reversed, 7–1; monitoring cannot cure a substantively inadequate design.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

#### Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail; Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-954 and 90-1004.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Rufo and Rapone are consolidated petitions presenting the same modification request; both judgments are vacated.

##### What change justifies modification of an institutional consent decree under Rule 60(b)(5)

**Holding:** Under Rule 60(b)(5), the party seeking to modify an institutional consent decree must prove a significant change in fact or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable; an unforeseeable catastrophe is not required. Actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief, and dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Current-force modification:** The Rule 60(b)(5) holding survives, but PLRA §802 supplies independent routes for federal-law civil prison-conditions relief, excluding habeas challenges to the fact or duration of confinement and expressly including prospective relief granted before enactment. Under §3626(b)(1), relief is terminable on a party's or intervener's motion two years after grant or approval, one year after denial of termination under that paragraph, or, for an order issued on or before enactment, two years after enactment. Section 3626(b)(2) entitles a defendant or intervener to immediate termination of prospective relief granted without findings that it is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means. The original §3626(b)(3) prevents termination when record-based written findings establish that relief remains necessary to correct a current or ongoing federal violation, is no broader than necessary, and is narrowly drawn and least intrusive. This current-or-ongoing formulation is preserved as enacted. Under original §3626(e)(2), relief subject to a pending motion is automatically stayed from day 30 for a (b)(1) or (b)(2) motion, or day 180 for a motion under other law, until final ruling. Admission establishes no motion, missing finding, termination or elapsed stay period; relief entered by a state court solely on state-law claims is outside §3626's limitations. The former equitable rule cannot alone foreclose these independent statutory avenues.

**Material application:** Officials reaffirmed the single-occupancy obligation in 1985 after the original 1979 decree. The centrality of that term and acceptance of known risks matter, but the lower court must assess inequity without treating unforeseeability as an absolute condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Population growth, expense, and the need for the proposed design are asserted grounds, not Supreme Court findings satisfying the burden. Modification under Rule 60(b)(5) concerns the continuing prospective operation of an existing institutional decree following a demonstrated significant change in fact or law. It does not establish a legislative power to compel reinstatement of a completed private damages or limitations judgment, nor does it decide whether a separate valid statutory remedy may provide collateral relief from such a judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both First Circuit judgments are vacated and the cases remanded for the district court to determine whether proven changed circumstances make continued enforcement inequitable and whether the proposed modification responds suitably to those changes. The existing decree remains operative until lawfully modified.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Swift & Co.*, 286 U.S. 106 — its disposition is preserved; its demanding factual formulation is confined to its setting rather than imposed universally.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — applied to preserve equitable power to adjust continuing obligations to changed conditions.
- *United States v. United Shoe Machinery Corp.*, 391 U.S. 244 — applied to connect modification to the continuing decree's purpose and operation.
- *Board of Education v. Dowell*, 498 U.S. 237 — reinforces contextual treatment of institutional equitable relief; no school-desegregation termination rule is imported.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, independent §3626(b)(1)–(2) termination, (b)(3) limitation, (d) state-law exception, original (e)(2) stay and §802(b)(1) enactment transition. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

##### What fit and protections a justified consent-decree modification must preserve

**Holding:** A justified modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite a consent decree to the constitutional minimum. Bell's clarification of constitutional limits does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted single-cell obligation, and financial pressure cannot authorize unconstitutional confinement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Current-force modification:** Rufo's equitable tailoring rule remains controlling where its premise is otherwise lawful. For covered prison-conditions relief, PLRA §802 additionally requires findings before entry or approval of a consent decree that the prospective relief is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular plaintiff's federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means, with substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violating state or local law, additionally requires that federal law permit that contrary relief, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation and that no other remedy correct the violation. The original current-or-ongoing-violation limitation on statutory termination appears in the preceding question. A consensual obligation exceeding the constitutional minimum does not by itself defeat these statutory conditions. Private settlements not judicially enforceable beyond reinstatement of the settled proceeding remain distinct, and their breach may be pursued through available state-law remedies; relief entered by a state court solely under state law retains §3626(d)'s exception. No decree is dissolved by this source admission.

**Material application:** The district court may consider practical administration and cost in selecting lawful relief, while exercising its own equitable judgment; these considerations cannot replace proof of a qualifying change. Bell does not dissolve the officials' independently accepted single-cell promise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves no double-cell design or number of cells and makes no determination that the proposed arrangement is constitutional or that remaining safeguards are adequate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Local No. 93, International Association of Firefighters v. Cleveland*, 478 U.S. 501 — consent obligations may exceed what constitutional liability alone would require.
- *Bell v. Wolfish*, 441 U.S. 520 — its constitutional treatment of double-celling does not erase a consensual single-cell requirement.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — preserves adjustment for changed circumstances while maintaining judicial control of lawful relief.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, consent-decree limits, private-settlement distinction and surviving equitable rule. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

The findings/order criterion expressly appears in selection under (iv), not verbatim in the Secretary clause (vii).

Pennhurst is not triggered merely because the federal obligation incorporates state gaming law. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not supply this action. #### Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue

##### Religious instruction on an admitted moral-development subject

**Holding:** A school opening an after-school period to comparable private moral- and character-development instruction and activities may not exclude an otherwise eligible club because its approach is religious. ### 1996-12-16 — United States v. Sahhar; Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-50186 and 94-50356; separate Supreme Court docket unassigned

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Sahhar_merits_1996-12-16.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. A hypothetical criminal maximum does not end otherwise lawful civil custody

**Holding and operative rule:** A separately lawful §4246 commitment does not end solely because the incompetency-related prosecution has been dismissed or custody has exceeded the maximum sentence that a conviction could have carried. Continued psychiatric custody must remain supported by current mental disease or defect causing the statutory danger, an applicable civil-commitment basis and effective review, release and state-placement safeguards; a former charge, incompetence alone, danger without qualifying illness or a stale finding cannot substitute.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins. The distinct statutory commitment and discharge rules are stated in Parts IV–V at their own join scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a possible punishment does not measure recovery from a present disorder. Section 4246 supplies a civil basis distinct from prosecution for the former threat offense. Jones rejects an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling, but its initial inference from an insanity acquittal cannot be transferred to someone never adjudicated guilty of criminal conduct. Independent civil grounds must do the work here.

Jackson requires a relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose. Foucha requires current lawful psychiatric predicates and prevents a past custody basis from supporting indefinite restraint after its justification ends. Addington supplies ordinary civil commitment's adequate proof protection, while Baxstrom prevents an expired prior basis from dispensing with the new route's safeguards. Those rules reject both automatic release at five years and unlimited retention based on an old charge. A civil label or public-safety interest alone is insufficient.

The District Court's clinical findings belong to its proceeding; they are not new findings of present illness and danger as of this decision. The remand requires contemporaneous application of the lawful predicates and placement duties. The unappealed indictment dismissal remains undisturbed. No distinct enumerated-power challenge is decided because its preservation is not established, and no freestanding federal police power is recognized.

**Precedent treatment:** Jones's duration principle is applied without its acquittal-based initial presumption. Jackson, Foucha, Addington and Baxstrom preserve independent current grounds and adequate procedures; none makes criminal punishment the measure of civil treatment.

#### II. The sentence-based classification objection does not require release

**Holding and operative rule:** Equal protection does not require release merely because §4246 custody outlasts the maximum possible sentence for the former federal charge. The lawful federal-custody origin and limited transition toward appropriate state responsibility relate to this civil class, while sentence severity and the duration of present illness-caused danger address different questions; this holding selects no universal scrutiny tier and does not merge equal protection with due process.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the objection through his separate even-under-intermediate-scrutiny reasoning and does not join Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual disparity must be examined. Heller requires a relationship between the classification and the disadvantage imposed; it does not make every different commitment rule invalid or impose a universal heightened tier. Section 4246 concerns safe disposition of persons within specified federal custody channels, with treatment, present danger requirements, release routes and a continuing preference for state responsibility. The possible sentence addresses punishment after conviction, not the persistence of those civil conditions. Sahhar's lack of a conviction makes independent civil grounds necessary, but does not itself make five years a constitutional medical endpoint. This conclusion leaves each safeguard operative. Passing this equality inquiry does not establish that any individual confinement satisfies due process. Current grounds and meaningful procedures require separate examination under Part I and the statutory rules below.

**Precedent treatment:** Heller's actual-disadvantage inquiry is applied without restoring its rejected proof disparity or selecting a new tier. Foucha's noncontrolling equality discussion remains noncontrolling.

#### III. State placement requires actual and periodically renewed efforts

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §4246(d), the Attorney General must make all reasonable efforts, continued periodically, to secure the domicile or trial State's assumption of custody, care and treatment, and must release the person to the appropriate state official when an eligible State assumes that responsibility. If neither State assumes responsibility despite those efforts, suitable federal hospitalization may continue only until state assumption or a condition permitting safe unconditional or prescribed-regimen release, whichever occurs first.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the state-placement requirement must operate in practice. The statute commands all reasonable efforts and their periodic renewal; it does not make a single unsuccessful request permanently sufficient. Actual acceptance requires transfer. The alternative federal hospitalization branch operates only while its own predicates and the statute's earliest endpoint remain unsatisfied.

Arizona had not been asked to assume responsibility after dismissal of the indictment. Earlier conditional placement ended after an assault injured another inmate. That episode establishes neither present acceptance nor present refusal and cannot replace the required contemporary inquiry. The lower courts must determine Arizona's actual response, the Attorney General's efforts and the current lawful basis for custody. The Court affirms the Ninth Circuit's remand without ordering final placement, finding acceptance or directing unconditional release. Foucha and Jackson reinforce that continuing custody must retain its actual lawful basis; absence of a state placement cannot cure missing psychiatric grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Foucha and Jackson support continuing lawful justification; the concrete transfer and periodic-efforts commands come from §4246(d), not a new judicial review schedule.

#### IV. The statutory commitment categories and the unrelated-dismissal route remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4246(a) applies to a person whose sentence is about to expire, one committed under §4241(d), or one whose charges were dismissed solely for reasons related to mental condition, upon the required director's certification of present illness-caused statutory danger and unavailable suitable state arrangements. A dismissal for reasons not related to mental condition instead triggers §4246(g)'s state referral and, if neither eligible State assumes responsibility, release upon refusal notice and no later than ten days after the director's certification; it cannot be converted into subsection (d)'s continuing-hospitalization branch.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this distinct statutory-category explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that former federal status alone is not a commitment category. The specified §4246(a) certification concerns mental disease or defect causing a substantial risk of bodily injury to another person or serious damage to another's property, together with unavailable suitable state custody and care. Filing stays release pending the statutory procedures and requires a hearing. Section 4246(d) requires clear and convincing evidence of that illness-caused danger for commitment. A free-standing danger-to-self formula is not its test.

The distinct subsection (g) branch prevents the general civil-hospitalization account from swallowing a dismissal unrelated to mental condition. Its state referral concerns institution of state civil-commitment proceedings, followed by its own release deadline when neither relevant State assumes responsibility. The supplied history concerns incompetency-connected custody and dismissal; it establishes no separate unrelated-dismissal ground. The Court neither finds that all dismissals are mental-condition-related nor resolves an unsupported new trigger here. Greenwood supplies no authority in this case for a general post-dismissal federal commitment power; the separate enumerated-power question remains outside review.

**Precedent treatment:** Greenwood's connection to the federal criminal process is not enlarged into a general police power. Addington's ordinary commitment protection is consistent with subsection (d)'s initial clear-and-convincing requirement; no universal discharge burden is inferred.

#### V. Discharge, conditional release and review retain their separate requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4246(e)'s sufficient-recovery determination after the prescribed hearing uses a preponderance of the evidence, distinct from subsection (d)'s commitment burden; safe unconditional release requires immediate discharge, while safe conditional release requires an individually prepared treatment regimen certified appropriate by the director, found appropriate by the court and imposed with an express compliance condition. Counsel, adversarial participation, annual reports, habeas and the independent discharge-motion route remain operative; neither annual reporting nor the 180-day restriction on repeated discharge motions creates an automatic judicial hearing schedule.

**Authority:** Part V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this discharge-burden and detailed procedural formulation. His concurrence preserves his current clear-and-convincing proof requirement and release when the statutory route requires it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that commitment and discharge provisions cannot be collapsed. A director finding sufficient recovery must promptly certify it. Under §4246(e), the court orders discharge or conducts the hearing authorized on the Government's motion or its own motion. After that hearing the statutory recovery finding uses preponderance, with unconditional or prescribed-regimen release as the finding warrants. After a hearing on the same criteria the court may modify or eliminate the regimen. The Court decides no unpresented constitutional attack on that discharge provision.

Section 4246(f) permits action on notice of conditional-release noncompliance or other probable cause, including possible arrest and prompt presentation, followed by a hearing on whether noncompliance makes continued release create the statutory danger. It is no general authority to confine everyone once federally charged.

Section 4247(d) supplies counsel, appointed when financially necessary, and opportunities to testify, present evidence, subpoena witnesses, confront and cross-examine. Section 4247(e) requires annual reports with continued-hospitalization recommendations. Section 4247(g) preserves habeas. Section 4247(h) permits counsel or a guardian to seek discharge without the director's certificate, but bars another such motion within 180 days of a determination that hospitalization should continue. A restriction on repeat motions is not a mandatory hearing every 180 days. These actual safeguards, alongside the continuing predicates and placement duty, require lawful current application on remand; they establish no present medical or release finding.

**Precedent treatment:** Addington and Foucha preserve appropriate commitment grounds and proof without replacing §4246(e)'s separate discharge text. Jackson's relationship between custody and lawful purpose remains enforceable through the actual statutory channels.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354:** Applies rejection of the hypothetical-sentence ceiling; does not treat an incompetent defendant as an insanity acquittee or import an acquittal-based presumption.
- **Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715:** Requires the nature and duration of confinement to remain connected to its lawful purpose; incompetence alone is insufficient for indefinite custody.
- **Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992:** Applies current psychiatric predicates and independent civil authority; its separate ordinary-commitment burden and unresolved preventive regimes remain at their stated scope.
- **O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563:** Preserves the requirement that confinement end when its supporting basis disappears; the Court makes no finding of harmlessness here.
- **Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418:** Preserves clear-and-convincing proof for ordinary commitment without substituting that burden for §4246(e)'s discharge text.
- **Baxstrom v. Herold, 383 U.S. 107:** Requires the ordinary protections of a new civil route when a prior detention basis ends; no sentence-based automatic release rule follows.
- **Heller v. Doe, June 24, 1993:** Applies the required relation to the actual procedural disadvantage, while retaining separate equality and due-process inquiries and no universal heightened tier.
- **Greenwood v. United States, 350 U.S. 366:** Does not decide the unpreserved general enumerated-power challenge or establish unlimited commitment authority after any former federal charge.

#### Law After Decision

Effective December 16, 1996, otherwise lawful §4246 commitment is not capped by the former offense's possible sentence, but present civil grounds, genuine statutory release and review, and recurring reasonable state-placement efforts remain essential. The separate initial clear-and-convincing and discharge-preponderance standards retain their own triggers; the unrelated-dismissal referral and ten-day outside limit remain distinct. No new scrutiny tier, recurring judicial hearing schedule, general federal police power or current clinical/placement fact is established. Stone-Zsela's separate proof formulation does not enlarge the eight-Justice statutory discharge rule.

### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's creation of roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise concern affirmative conduct. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver. Neither lower-court disagreement nor absence of identical facts independently establishes immunity. Collins, decided after Uhlrig's death, and later Elder and Pinder govern present analysis but cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court leaves constitutional culpability, the affirmative-danger merits and the Tenth Circuit's five-part test undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald supplies objective qualified immunity and remains intact.
- Anderson v. Creighton supplies particularized apparent unlawfulness without requiring an identical prior case.
- Elder v. Holloway is applied to require examination of all relevant conduct-time authority while preserving the favorable factual account and ordinary claim/evidence limits; it supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Pinder v. Johnson is applied to distinguish an affirmative deprivation from omitted protection and to keep notice separate from merits. Its March 1989 result does not resolve this later conduct automatically, and its substantive reservation remains open.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights retains its no-general-workplace-duty rule and its separate, bounded arbitrariness holding. It does not reject every affirmative-danger claim or supply notice before its February 26, 1992 decision.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County and Daniels v. Williams preserve the distinctions between a general protective duty, an affirmative deprivation and negligence; the Court does not convert them into a complete constitutional answer here.
- Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are considered as conduct-time circuit authority at their actual scopes, without converting their formulations into Supreme Court holdings.

#### Law After Decision

The decision establishes only the particularized immunity result for the five administrators on this reviewed claim. It supplies no general affirmative-danger test, categorical public-employment bar, mandatory deceit or assurance requirement, no-awareness finding or constitutional approval of budget choices. The distinction between a general workplace-safety duty and an attributable affirmative deprivation remains. A different official act, record or conduct-time legal landscape requires its own analysis.

### 1997-02-19 - Lynce v. Mathis, No. 95-7452; October Term 1996, chunk 4, inventory No. 41.

Authority: [Record](../records/Lynce_v_Mathis_threshold_1997-02-19.md).

#### Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** Before a favorable habeas merits adjudication on Lynce's sole Ex Post Facto claim, the lower courts must determine the actual exhaustion predicate and the scope and legal effect of the State's qualified futility concession. The election to pursue only the cancellation claim neither creates a mixed petition from hypothetical additional theories nor by itself establishes exhaustion, express waiver, or the absence or ineffectiveness of available state corrective process.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight Justices adopt the same threshold rule, explanation and limited remand. The Chief Justice joins the judgment only and writes separately. No aggregation of distinct rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2254 requires exhaustion or an established absence or ineffectiveness of state corrective process before habeas relief may be granted. Granberry v. Greer establishes that exhaustion does not remove appellate power, but its discretionary holding concerned a State that omitted the district-court defense. Florida expressly raised that defense and refused waiver. Duckworth v. Serrano prevents clear constitutional error or efficiency alone from supplying the missing statutory predicate.

The State nevertheless affirmatively accepted futility for the common cancellation issue concerning prisoners still in custody, distinguished Lynce's prior release as potentially creating additional reincarceration claims, and later confirmed that he pursued only the Ex Post Facto claim. This supports a substantial claim-specific exception argument. But the district answer already identified that sole claim while retaining its custody qualification, available-procedure account and nonwaiver position. The later election does not conclusively resolve those limits, and counsel's legal characterization cannot alter the statute.

California Department of Corrections v. Morales supplies a concrete intervening reason to reconsider the older Florida decisions that treated overcrowding credits as merely procedural. Morales protects a demonstrated offense-date opportunity for meaningful release consideration; it rejects the procedural label as sufficient while retaining the prisoner's ultimate punishment-increase burden. The older adverse precedents therefore do not conclusively establish that state consideration would now be ineffective. The remaining comparison between Florida's offense-date and later credit laws also may give state proceedings meaningful work. This does not establish that relief in state court is necessarily available or that each accounting detail requires separate exhaustion.

Rose v. Lundy concerns claims actually asserted, so conceivable additional theories cannot manufacture a mixed petition. The Court returns the actual statutory question for decision without resolving the constitutional merits. The lower courts may accept a legally sufficient claim-specific exception; they must not order a state-court detour regardless of what the qualified concession means.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional violation, statutory continuity, protected-credit amount, release date or entitlement to restoration is decided. Neither a Florida procedural default nor cause and prejudice is established. The State's assertion that every other possible theory is foreclosed remains an argument, not a preclusion holding. The decision does not decide the 1996 amendment's general application to this already-pending proceeding: it rests on no relevant difference between versions and adopts no implied-waiver theory. Permission to deny a claim despite nonexhaustion does not authorize a favorable grant on an assumed predicate. The Court neither requires new filings nor treats the source record as missing litigation conduct.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Granberry v. Greer, 481 U.S. 129 (1987): applies its distinction between appellate power and exhaustion, while distinguishing its omitted district defense; it does not authorize automatic waiver or unrestricted disregard of an expressly maintained defense.
- Duckworth v. Serrano, 454 U.S. 1 (1981): applies the rejection of merits clarity and judicial economy as independent excuses; unavailable or ineffective state process remains a distinct statutory possibility.
- Rose v. Lundy, 455 U.S. 509 (1982): preserves its actual mixed-petition rule without creating claims the prisoner did not pursue or treating election as exhaustion of the remaining claim.
- California Department of Corrections v. Morales, decided April 25, 1995: recognizes its binding meaningful-opportunity protection as intervening law relevant to claimed futility. Its merits rule, claimant burden, limited substitute-substantiation requirement and remedy remain unchanged; it does not itself decide Florida credit continuity or excuse exhaustion.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, decided November 30, 1993: retains its meaningful-opportunity protection and opportunity-only remedy. Its civil-rights route does not remove habeas exhaustion from a demand for restored credits and shorter custody.
- Engle v. Isaac, 456 U.S. 107 (1982): its refusal to equate predicted futility with cause for procedural default cautions against automatic reliance on adverse precedent; no default or cause-and-prejudice holding is made here.
- Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984): the cited account of a lower court's futility conclusion supplies no universal Supreme Court exception for first presentation after adverse precedent.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): no new Court application to Florida's canceled credits is made; its merits relevance appears only in the Chief Justice's noncontrolling concurrence.

#### Law After Decision

The governing change in this proceeding is a required determination of the actual exhaustion predicate and the qualified concession before a favorable merits adjudication. No general adverse-precedent exception, automatic waiver, mandatory rejection of concession-based exceptions or requirement to pursue hypothetical additional claims is created. The existing Ex Post Facto rules remain unchanged. The Court establishes neither continuity between the 1983, 1987 and 1988 Florida credit systems nor a protected number of days. The Chief Justice's conditional views do not supply an alternative Court holding.

### 1997-03-19 — Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District; court-of-appeals No. 94-60631.

Authority: [Record](../records/Ingebretsen_v_Jackson_Public_School_District_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Imminent personal exposure permits this challenge

**Rule and authority:** A student facing a concrete, imminent application of an official school-event mechanism may seek prospective relief before the threatened exposure occurs. Part I has all nine joins and controls; standing does not establish the threatened conduct's illegality.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires personal injury, a causal connection and redressability, rather than abstract disagreement with a statute. These enrolled students face impending events under a mandatory permission mechanism administered by school officials, and the hearing record supports that concrete prospect. Their parents may represent their children's interests through the proper representative route. The absence of a completed local policy does not make the impending exposure remote, while the availability of lawful applications remains relevant to the merits and remedy. This holding establishes neither general taxpayer standing nor a right for an abstract objector elsewhere.

##### II. Official school worship cannot be insulated by student delivery or a disclaimer

**Rule and authority:** A school violates the Establishment Clause when it organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event; student initiation or delivery and a statutory disclaimer do not alone make that exercise private. Part II controls through Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, independently of a separate quantified-pressure showing.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee supplies the prohibition on official sponsorship of school worship. The mandatory event permission and religious-content eligibility terms give officials an operational role that must be examined, rather than disregarded because the speaker is a student. Section 1(2) permits student-initiated, nonsectarian, nonproselytizing prayer at compulsory and noncompulsory events; the State may not administer those conditions through official devotional organization or religious supervision. Section 1(4)'s disclaimer does not erase actual official conduct. Section 2, by contrast, denies schools authority to prescribe prayer's form or content. That is a statutory command, not a factual finding that it is always obeyed or always violated. Pinette and Chabad require attention to attribution, while Rosenberger protects neutral support for independently edited private speech; none makes every school religious statement official. The supported prospective mechanism warrants bounded interim protection, not a final holding against every permissible application.

##### III. School-directed worship in a pressured setting is independently forbidden

**Rule and authority:** School direction of religious exercise in compulsory or comparably pressured school settings violates the Establishment Clause when the official setting places actual pressure on students to participate. Part III independently controls through Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; mere offense at another student's private viewpoint does not suffice.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee's distinct school-pressure holding supplies this ground. Official control, students' attendance and participation expectations, and the setting's practical demands matter even without a legal punishment for declining prayer. The State cannot dispose of that inquiry merely by describing participation as voluntary or substituting a student speaker. These circumstances support interim protection against the official mechanism. The Court does not presume coercion from hearing private religious speech, require a quantified social-pressure measurement, or hold every voluntary gathering coercive. This is an alternative ground with its own six-Justice coalition, not an aggregation with Part II.

##### IV. Correct the graduation exception and preserve lawful expression

**Rule and authority:** Preliminary relief must restrain supported unlawful official worship while preserving independently lawful statutory provisions and genuinely private expression. Part IV's legal limit has all nine joins; its continued restraint, removal of graduation's categorical exemption and remand have Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee does not exempt graduation merely because students initiate and deliver prayer; the same official attribution and pressure inquiries apply. A genuinely independent student's religious viewpoint remains protected. The lower court must therefore remove its automatic graduation safe harbor and frame the interim order around the actual official mechanism. Section 1(3)'s protection of other voluntary expression, Section 2's separate voluntary-prayer provision and no-prescription command, and Section 1(5)'s severability direction must each receive effect. The supported threat of official worship establishes the relevant interim constitutional injury; narrowing protects the countervailing private-speech interests. The Court neither compels a new forum nor deletes the religious filters to create a broader statutory access entitlement. State-law construction and the precise severed decree remain below. Scalia and Thomas agree with the private-expression limit but would remove the retained restraint too.

##### V-A. Actual adequate representation defeats intervention as of right here

**Rule and authority:** Under Rule 24(a)(2), a timely applicant with a protectable interest potentially impaired by litigation is entitled to intervene if existing representation may be inadequate; a shared desired outcome or the State's official identity does not conclusively establish adequacy. All eight Associates join Part V-A and hold the actual representation adequate here, affirming denial without deciding unnecessary alternative elements.

**Controlling explanation:** Trbovich supplies the minimal burden for showing possible inadequacy and recognizes that governmental and private interests may differ. The Attorney General here actually advanced the proposed intervenors' private-expression interests, and they identify no relevant interest omitted from this statutory-validity controversy. The required narrowed decree protects those independent interests. Their private status alone therefore does not establish a representational gap on this record. Amicus participation could not replace an otherwise established right to intervene. Materially changed interests may be considered on a proper later application; this judgment orders neither a renewed hearing nor automatic future intervention.

##### V-B. A common question permits but does not compel permissive intervention

**Rule and authority:** Rule 24(b) permits intervention where its common-question condition is met, subject to the court's discretion and consideration of undue delay or prejudice to the original parties; it does not require admission merely because participation would be relevant. All eight Associates join Part V-B and affirm the denial as within discretion on this record.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court's duplication and delay assessment is permissible in this narrow statutory proceeding. The opportunity to make amicus submissions helps explain why excluding duplicative party participation caused no demonstrated prejudice, but does not defeat intervention as of right. The Court adopts neither an absolute rule against applicants without novel legal arguments nor an unreviewable discretion or universal plain-error requirement. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without adding a ground.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Applied its controlling concrete-injury, causation and redressability requirements to imminent personal exposure; no nonmajority redressability rationale becomes law.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Applied both independently sufficient majorities, with their distinct joins, to official school worship and pressure. Student delivery creates no categorical graduation exception.
- **Capitol Square Review Board v. Pinette:** Preserved protection for independently attributable private religious access; neither a forum label nor a disclaimer settles every setting.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio:** Applied contextual attribution; the surrounding governmental role remains material.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors:** Distinguished neutral assistance to independently edited private expression from official devotional organization.
- **Lamb's Chapel and Good News Club:** Preserved genuine private religious expression without converting every school event into an opened private forum.
- **Brown v. Polk County:** Applied the distinction between lawful government control and overbroad suppression of independent religious expression, without importing employment burdens into school-prayer adjudication.
- **Trbovich v. United Mine Workers:** Applied its minimal possible-inadequacy burden and individualized representation inquiry; no conclusive presumption of governmental adequacy is adopted.
- **Jones II:** Rejected the lower-court graduation criteria as a categorical safe harbor inconsistent with governing Lee; lawful private graduation speech remains distinct.

#### Law After Decision

The school-religion holdings apply existing sponsorship and pressure prohibitions while clarifying that student delivery and graduation do not create categorical exceptions. Both grounds control independently. Genuine private expression and independently lawful provisions remain protected. The intervention holdings apply ordinary Rule 24 to actual representation and discretionary case management, establishing no universal entitlement or governmental-adequacy rule.

### 1997-03-31 — Robinson v. City of Edmond; lower-court Nos. 94-6237 and 95-6008.

Authority: [Record](../records/Robinson_v_City_of_Edmond_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. A recurring personal municipal communication supplies standing here

**Rule and authority:** A person who actually and repeatedly receives the City's own allegedly sectarian civic identification may seek prospective relief against that continuing use when the asserted personal injury is attributable to the City and redressable by the requested relief. All eight Associate Justices join Part I; Stone-Zsela supports reaching the merits without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires concrete personal injury, causation, redressability and proof appropriate to the stage of litigation; Valley Forge excludes an abstract demand for constitutional compliance. Feldman's supported recurring receipt of the cross-bearing City water bill, together with his direct encounter and personal objection, provides the required individual relationship to the continuing use. His 1988 encounter alone would not establish present prospective injury. Residence, work, taxation, general awareness and the City's decision not to contest standing are not substitutes for that relationship. The filed account of monthly receipt is accepted as an accurate account of this litigated record, considered with the actual posttrial findings and established continuing municipal use; it is not a new sworn exhibit. The Court assigns no unnamed lower finding of changed behavior to Feldman and imposes no universal avoidance-expense requirement. Because his claim supports the shared prospective relief, the Court need not separately decide the other three merits plaintiffs' standing for that same relief or any distinct personal remedy. Orza's dismissed merits claim is not before the Court.

##### II. The adopted municipal seal is the City's own expression

**Rule and authority:** A symbol selected and adopted as the municipality's official seal and used to authenticate its own communications, personnel and property is attributable to the municipality, even if a private person originally designed it. Part II has all nine joins; attribution alone does not establish that every governmental religious acknowledgment is unconstitutional.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinette and Chabad distinguish official adoption from private religious expression admitted through neutral access; Rosenberger protects neutral support for independently edited private speech. Those protections do not convert the City's own identifier into a private message. Edmond selected the seal through its authorized design process, registered it for municipal services and uses it in ordinary official business. No neutral forum, independent speaker or disclaimer separates that continuing civic identification from the City. The actual adopted function, rather than the designer's private status or beliefs, resolves attribution. The constitutional character of that expression requires the further inquiries below.

##### III. Context establishes an impermissible official religious affiliation

**Rule and authority:** Government violates the Establishment Clause when the whole context of its own continuing civic expression identifies it with a particular faith; a secular historical purpose and surrounding secular images do not by themselves cure that effect. Part III controls through Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon supplies the independent inquiry into religious effect, and Allegheny requires consideration of the expression's actual context. The unmistakable Christian cross occupies its own identifying quadrant in the seal carried throughout the City's official operations. The Court considers the composite design, its history, municipal uses and absence of church control. Assuming the district court's historical-purpose finding, the other three quadrants do not make this religious identifier neutral, and age is neither a constitutional safe harbor nor proof of affiliation by itself. Favorable witnesses' reactions do not displace that contextual inquiry. The rule requires neither a majority-offense poll nor coercion; it also creates no automatic rule against an isolated graphic regardless of setting. Purpose and entanglement need not be independently decided. No new credibility finding or universal rule for reviewing constitutional facts supplies the result.

##### IV. Making a faith's badge the City's permanent civil identifier is independently forbidden

**Rule and authority:** A city may not deliberately and pervasively use a recognizable symbol of a particular faith as a permanent identifying part of its own badge of present civil authority. Part IV has all nine direct joins and independently supports the judgment on the combined adoption, institutional-identification and continuing-use facts established here.

**Controlling explanation:** Kennedy's separate writing in Allegheny identifies a boundary between permissible acknowledgment and permanent official religious identification. The Court now directly adopts that bounded distinction on the institutional-use facts here. Edmond crosses that boundary by placing this freestanding Christian cross in its selected civic seal and carrying the seal through its ordinary official business. This is the government's own institutional identity, rather than another speaker's religious message or an exhibit about a particular historical artifact. The conclusion does not require a proved evangelical motive, church control, an added proselytizing inscription, a compulsory profession, denial of a benefit or visual dominance over every secular quadrant. It does require the actual combination present here; government ownership or religious content alone is insufficient. Religious place names, specific historical buildings, inherited coats of arms, holiday acknowledgments, historical exhibits and private symbols retain their own contexts. The Court announces no categorical approval or invalidation of them. This ground has nine direct votes; its authority does not depend on combining incompatible rationales.

##### V. Relief must end the present official use and preserve the distinction from history

**Rule and authority:** Prospective relief must end the unconstitutional use of the cross-bearing seal as the City's current official identifier, with a reasonable implementation period fixed below; accurate contextual preservation of historical records and displays remains permissible. All nine join PartV.

**Controlling explanation:** The violation concerns the City's continuing institutional identification. An order directed to that use remedies it without rewriting history or suppressing private expression. The district court may set the practical implementation period, but longevity does not authorize continued official adoption without end. The Court supplies no replacement design and commands no destruction of artifacts. Personal liability, immunity, nominal damages, unappealed Free Exercise and state-law claims, the production dispute and independent fees or costs remain outside this merits judgment. Existing proceedings for appropriate relief and fees may continue without a new fee award from this Court.

### 1997-05-12 — Lambrix v. Singletary; No. 96-5658; OT1996, chunk 7, matter 83.

Authority: [Record](../records/Lambrix_v_Singletary_merits_1997-05-12.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Procedural default and Teague nonretroactivity are distinct, nonjurisdictional limits, with no inflexible rule requiring one always to precede the other. In this case the Court decides the accepted Teague issue while reserving the disputed state-default predicates; that choice neither excuses default nor decides the constitutional merits.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support from Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stone-Zsela and O'Connor disagree with reaching Teague before the default inquiry in this case.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court ordinarily respects the independent state ground before granting federal relief, but these two defenses do not establish jurisdictional priority. Stringer rejected a Teague ground and remanded an unresolved default question without creating a universal order. Schiro confirms that nonretroactivity is nonjurisdictional; Caspari distinguishes proper presentation of a preserved subsidiary defense from other omitted questions. Here Teague is expressly accepted for review.

The State repeatedly asserted default, so the Court does not proceed on a supposed wholesale waiver. The present dispute concerns the adequacy and operation of the actual state rule and any legally sufficient excuse, questions not resolved by the lower appellate disposition. Addressing the accepted finality issue now does not decide those unsettled predicates or establish an advisory constitutional rule: it resolves a distinct restriction on this pending claim. The strongest alternative is to return immediately for default because a sufficient, preserved bar could end the case. That route is lawful, but is not obligatory before this accepted nonjurisdictional question. No established jurisdictional defect is bypassed, and no mandatory Teague-first rule is announced.

##### II. This claim invokes an old guidance rule at November 24, 1986 finality

**Holding and operative rule:** For Lambrix's judgment final on November 24, 1986, the claim that inadequate aggravator guidance in Florida's legally consequential capital jury recommendation can infect the final death sentence invokes an already dictated rule. Teague therefore does not bar this claim as new merely because the judge separately must exercise independent judgment; the ruling establishes no actual instructional defect, harmful influence or failed cure on this record.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support from Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices. O'Connor affirmatively joins this holding once Teague is reached, while maintaining her objection to that sequence. Stone-Zsela takes no position on the new-rule question. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject the holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Godfrey already required meaningful guidance of capital aggravation. Teague and Caspari require comparison of the precise application with law at finality, not merely citation of a broad principle. Stringer applies Godfrey's existing protection to consequential weighing; Gray likewise distinguishes preserving an established protection's real function from creating a new procedural entitlement. Those current decisions govern the inquiry without being recast as cases available in 1986.

Prefinality Florida law establishes the function at issue. LeDuc gave a death recommendation operative weight if all relevant data were considered, without making it binding. Messer treated an informed jury contribution as a distinct statutory safeguard: later judicial consideration did not replace wrongly excluded accomplice-sentence and proposed psychiatric mitigation before the jury. Godfrey supplies the federal duty; the state safeguard alone does not.

Ross supplies the strongest contrary argument. It requires the judge's independent reasoned judgment and recognizes judicial experience as protection against lay notions of heinousness. Spaziano and Baldwin likewise preserve judicial sentencing authority. But Ross simultaneously retains the recommendation's weight and LeDuc's qualification. Its remand corrected excessive judicial deference, not defective jury guidance. Eutzy preserves weight and independence together, and rejected the instruction challenge only because the jury recommended life; it did not decide the instructions' merits.

The independent judge therefore adds a protection without making the consequential jury contribution disappear. Applying meaningful guidance to that contribution preserves Godfrey's existing function rather than creating a right to jury-imposed sentencing. Neither identical power nor equal weight for life and death recommendations is required. The later date of Espinosa, a reserved question or reasonable disagreement asserted without a substantive distinction cannot alone establish novelty. Actual cure remains a separate inquiry. LeDuc's affirmance, Messer's particular new-jury remedy, Ross's judicial remand and Eutzy's favorable-life reasoning do not mechanically select a remedy here.

##### III-A. Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support at this exact scope from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. The further binding old-rule instruction rests separately on Part III-B's five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals disposed of the claim without resolving the State's asserted procedural bar. The actual operation of the rule and the State's litigation conduct bear on independence, adequacy, preservation and an applicable excuse. Those genuine unfinished inquiries require a bounded remand; neither an unestablished procedural premise nor a generalized waiver assertion supplies their answer.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial. Florida's identified ground concerns his failure to pursue that issue on direct appeal, not failure to object at trial. Its separate successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and alternative merits rejection do not establish a federal cause determination. The lower court must assess the actual state materials and any properly available cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice contention. It may not treat a proposed exception as already established or a present reservation as a finding that the bar is inadequate.

The common direction sets aside the reviewed appellate disposition and requires default to be addressed before any available constitutional merits and relief. It does not establish a universal order for nonjurisdictional defenses. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, declares no waiver or exception and creates no present right to a new penalty proceeding. The Justices' different positions on Teague do not erase their agreement on this operative remand task; those differences are preserved in Parts I–II, Part III-B and the separate writings.

##### III-B. The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by the same five Justices as Part II. They directly adopt this additional binding direction. Stone-Zsela joins Part III-A's common vacatur and default remand, but not the instruction deciding Teague now or foreclosing its consideration if default fails; his distinct mandate is stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on a Teague conclusion the Court rejects. Vacatur therefore removes that judgment and binds further proceedings to the old-rule determination; it is not an invitation to repeat the rejected ground. The accepted claim includes the heinous, atrocious or cruel and cold, calculated and premeditated instruction issues. The court of appeals assumed inadequate guidance for nonretroactivity analysis, which establishes neither a violation nor its effect.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial; Florida relied on failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. That is not an unpreserved trial objection. Coleman and Ford require attention to the actual independent state ground, its adequacy when compliance was required, preservation and an applicable excuse. The separate state treatment of successive appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and its alternative merits rejection are not federal findings of cause. Ordinary cause and prejudice remain distinct from Sawyer's clear-and-convincing requirement that no reasonable juror would find death eligibility absent constitutional error; a selection defect alone does not meet that gateway.

If an available violation is established, Espinosa and Stringer require genuine authorized reweighing or Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt addressing the recommendation's transmitted influence. Remaining valid aggravators, bare proportionality review or an independent-judge label alone supplies neither cure. If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required. None is presently ordered.

Gray preserves any genuinely applicable, unresolved statutory condition before an operative writ. This decision settles neither chapter 153's temporal reach, amended §2254(d)'s application, chapter 154 qualification nor an exhaustion waiver or statutory exception. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, conditional writ or revived deadline, and no guilt retrial, life sentence, release, stay or execution authorization. Unrelated assistance, double-jeopardy, testimony and guilt rulings remain untouched.

### 1997-05-27 — Church on the Rock v. City of Albuquerque; Tenth Circuit No. 95-2009, 84 F.3d 1273; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned. OT1996, chunk 8, inventory No. 91.

Authority: [Record](../records/Church_on_the_Rock_v_City_of_Albuquerque_merits_1997-05-27.md).

#### Holdings

####### I. Religious advocacy is a viewpoint within this admitted program

**Holding and operative rule:** A city that permits private movies and instruction on religion, morality, history, literature and philosophy may not exclude an otherwise eligible presentation because it affirmatively teaches a faith, invites conversion or prayer, or offers religious literature. This rule governs the film and literature within this actual senior-center program, while preserving genuine, reasonable and viewpoint-neutral subject and activity limits.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Lamb's Chapel supplies the distinction between a religious perspective on an admitted subject and a subject genuinely outside a forum. Good News/Good Sports applies that distinction where prayer and Bible instruction are the means of addressing an admitted subject. Here the City's broad private movie and class program includes nondevotional treatments of the Bible and religion. The excluded invitation to faith is material to the presentation, but does not transform this otherwise admitted film-and-literature event into a different activity solely because it urges belief. Staff's undefined sectarian line confirms that affirmation, rather than a neutral class of movies or instruction, prompted the denial. This conclusion does not erase every distinction between worship and discussion in every limited forum. Stevens joins at this actual movie-and-literature scope; the different extracurricular activity boundary he identified in Good News and the institutional symbol setting addressed in Chabad remain distinct.

####### II. This private access does not establish religion

**Holding and operative rule:** Allowing the requested privately sponsored presentation on the actual voluntary, nonresidential adult-program terms does not violate the Establishment Clause. Public ownership and devotional content do not alone convert the speaker's expression into governmental worship; actual official selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured sponsorship could produce a different case.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices for this concrete conclusion. The complete contextual rationale in Part III has the separate six-Justice authority stated there.

**Controlling explanation:** The adults may attend or leave, the presenter and materials remain private, and the City neither produces the message nor conducts the prayer as its own. Equal access therefore admits private speech rather than creating a municipal religious program. Lamb's Chapel and Good News preserve that distinction in actual private-use arrangements. Lee v. Weisman and Ingebretsen concern official organization and pressure, and Robinson concerns the government's own institutional religious identification; voluntary attendance alone would not cure such official action. The combined private sponsorship, adult setting and absence of governmental adoption support this result. Neither the forum's label nor a hypothetical disclaimer establishes categorical immunity from an establishment claim.

####### III. Context satisfies the retained purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Lemon, equal private access here serves the secular purpose of the City's broad activity forum, does not primarily advance religion as governmental expression, and creates no excessive entanglement. Actual private attribution and the program's operation, considered together, establish that conclusion without a universal disclaimer, monitoring duty or devotional-financing entitlement.

**Authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six direct joins control; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this complete methodology. No Marks combination is used.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon retains its purpose, primary-effect and entanglement inquiries. Good News and Chabad require real examination of the private-use arrangements, and Pinette supplies no comprehensive majority exemption for every public forum. This program's ordinary private lectures, classes and films have a secular access purpose. The actual live presenter and adult program identify the film and literature as the Church's message, without the uncertain attribution of an isolated government-building display. Admitting the event does not require City officials to approve theology; ending an undefined sectarian-content line avoids the asserted need to supervise devotional content. A neutral identification notice remains available but is not newly made a condition of the injunction. Rosenberger's bounded printing benefit preserves the same need to distinguish private expression from governmental religious activity, and establishes no right to compulsory devotional aid.

####### IV. The funding defense does not defeat bounded as-applied relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The invoked Older Americans Act funding condition does not justify this unconstitutional denial of equal private access. The as-applied injunction and appellate fee entitlement are affirmed, with fee amount left below and ordinary neutral facility administration preserved.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices directly.

**Controlling explanation:** A funding condition cannot furnish a defense to the viewpoint exclusion established here. Enjoining that denial does not require the City to produce worship or finance devotional materials, and does not decide the Act's facial validity or federal grant administration. The existing injunction fits the proven film-and-literature denial. Neutral eligibility, scheduling, safety, capacity and identification rules, and genuinely prospective viewpoint-neutral redefinition of the forum, remain possible. The Court awards no damages or fixed fees, orders no withdrawal of federal funds and grants no relief against the United States.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lamb's Chapel: applies the admitted-subject/religious-viewpoint distinction and the concrete private-access establishment result.
- Good News/Good Sports Club: applies its speech rule to the actual film and literature and its contextual establishment analysis; its reservation concerning different standalone worship settings survives.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains and applies purpose, primary effect and entanglement; no replacement test is adopted.

The City must provide equal access for this otherwise eligible religious film and literature within its existing private adult program. ### 1997-05-27 — Kinman v. Omaha Public School District; 94 F.3d 463 (8th Cir. 1996); Eighth Circuit No. 95-2809

Authority: [Record](../records/Kinman_v_Omaha_Public_School_District_merits_1997-05-27.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The sex of the harasser does not exclude a Title IX claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Same-sex teacher harassment may constitute discrimination because of sex under Title IX; the claimant must still prove sex-based causation, unwelcome treatment, the required educational harm, recipient responsibility and causal injury. Sexual conduct alone does not establish those separate elements, and no opposite-sex comparator is invariably required.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion has all nine direct joins. Part IIA has five direct joins—Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas—on its recipient rule, although four of those Justices oppose remand. Parts IIB–III have only Stone-Zsela's rationale and a different five-Justice remand judgment. Justice Ginsburg's Part IV has all eight Associate joins and Part V has all nine. The rule coalitions are direct joins, not a combination of opposing rationales under Marks.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1681 protects persons from discrimination on the basis of sex in covered education programs and contains no opposite-sex condition. Brown preserves sex-based causation; Oncale's analogous statutory analysis confirms that a same-sex relationship is no categorical bar without importing Title VII's attribution rule. The reported sex-directed selection of Kinman and lack of comparable attentions to male students support the causation issue at this stage, but supply neither a universal comparator requirement nor a finding of every defendant's intent. No sexual-orientation coverage rule is decided, and no supported fact triggers a statutory exception.

##### II. Participation does not establish welcome, and educational harm is contextual

**Holding and operative rule:** Voluntary participation and postgraduation continuation do not conclusively establish that earlier teacher advances were welcome. Actionable harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive; the full educational context controls without a psychiatric-injury, measurable-grade-loss or mandatory-factor requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor separates participation from welcome. Kinman's reported initial objection, the teacher's authority and the surrounding evidence leave a factual dispute that cannot be resolved by choosing credibility at summary judgment. Harris supplies the contextual severity analogy: frequency, gravity, coercion, humiliation, educational authority and interference matter, but no one circumstance is indispensable. Neither graduation nor later participation eliminates an otherwise proved earlier violation; neither makes every later private act actionable. The school's policy extending to welcome conduct, off-duty or off-campus acts and two years after departure is not itself the federal liability test.

##### III. Recipient compensation requires its own culpable response

**Holding and operative rule:** Damages against a funding recipient for teacher harassment require actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial risk, followed by deliberate and unreasonable disregard of the known danger causing injury or continuation. Constructive notice, the teacher's status or ordinary authority-assisted access, policy breach, and the wrongdoer's knowledge of her own misconduct alone do not satisfy that recipient standard; a reasonable response does not become unlawful merely because harm continues.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional discrimination but expressly leaves recipient attribution open. The recipient's responsibility must therefore be established separately from the teacher's intentional wrong. Title IX's recipient obligation differs from Title VII's express employer-agent relationship; Andrade does not automatically supply the same attribution route here. Section 1682 addresses the recipient's own compliance and gives notice and an opportunity for voluntary correction in federal administrative enforcement. That structure supports attention to the recipient's actual culpable response, but its agency notice, hearing, congressional-report and thirty-day provisions are not literal prerequisites to this private damages action. A delegated teacher relationship may connect conduct to the program without establishing the separate corrective official's awareness and disregard. Administrative and injunctive standards remain governed by their own sources and are not decided. The recipient receives no personal qualified immunity.

**Application and limit:** Five Justices retain the Title IX remand, but no majority adopts a common application rationale. Stone-Zsela would obtain further recipient-specific review under this threshold; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would remand under their distinct agency and negligent-correction grounds; the four other threshold joiners would restore summary judgment. The directly supported threshold governs further proceedings. Stone-Zsela's additional circumstantial-awareness formulation is separately stated and has no other joiners; no inference or presumption beyond the common rule is attributed to the Court.

##### IV. This supervisory record does not establish the constitutional wrong

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Doe, personal supervisory liability requires the particular official's actual knowledge of abuse or a substantial risk, deliberate and unreasonable disregard despite protective authority and opportunity, and causal contribution. Even assuming sufficient awareness for Whitehouse and Mackiel separately, the supported response evidence does not establish deliberate disregard; their reviewed §1983 summary judgments are affirmed on the merits without a new immunity ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Doe distinguishes knowing acquiescence from negligent supervision and keeps Caplinger's conduct-date immunity ruling separate from constitutional merits. Whitehouse's student, staff and mother reports are not pooled with Mackiel's knowledge from Grosse's October 16 report. Assuming the relevant awareness, the interviews, attempted trace, testing and confrontation, followed by authentication and termination efforts after journal, cards and photographs arrived, show an imperfect response rather than the necessary deliberate refusal to protect. The transfer refusal, delay, nonsuspension and asymmetry remain adverse evidence; they do not on this record establish the constitutional fault. An alleged wrong-line trace is not itself evidence of a deliberately selected error. Denials and polygraphs are not conclusive truth, conclusive corroboration was not required before all action, and the mere existence of an investigation does not invariably defeat liability. The Court resolves this supported record without demanding a confession or treating the later 1992 evidence as proof of earlier awareness. Four Justices' statutory negligence theory remains distinct from the more demanding constitutional rule.

##### V. The District's constitutional attribution is separately absent

**Holding and operative rule:** A school district is responsible under §1983 only for an unconstitutional policy or custom, or a decision attributable to an official with final policymaking authority, that causes the injury. This record establishes neither that policy/custom nor an attributable final decision; ordinary supervisory rank and the teacher's conduct do not supply the missing institutional link.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell bars ordinary vicarious municipal liability while requiring the actual institutional source and causal relationship. The evidence establishes no persistent District practice of permitting abuse, no policy directing it and no final policymaking authority in either supervisor for the asserted decision. Absence of a broader pattern is not declared fatal to every claim based on one final policymaker's decision; that distinct route fails here because its attribution predicate is unestablished. The District does not receive Harlow immunity. Nabozny preserves the distinction between individual wrongs and institutional attribution, and supplies no new selective-enforcement claim. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no additional merits rule.

The Court determines no damages amount, teacher default, direct-teacher merits disposition, unappealed Nebraska tort claim, universal postgraduation coverage or administrative or injunctive enforcement rule. The received Harlow–Anderson immunity framework remains in force; later Doe, Farmer, Lanier and Nabozny supply no earlier conduct-date notice.
