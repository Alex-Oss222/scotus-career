## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

### Unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing

**Governing question:** What instructions govern unequal opportunity to compete and associational standing?

**Current rule:** A governmental barrier preventing persons able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms supplies injury without proof that they would have won a contract. An association may sue when a member has individual standing, the interest is germane to its purpose, and, absent clear congressional displacement, neither the claim nor relief requires individual-member participation. That third Hunt condition is prudential; WARN §2104(a)(5) displaces it by authorizing the exclusive employee representative to enforce represented employees’ back-pay and benefit claims. The representative enforces the employees’ entitlement without becoming its beneficiary. Individual proof remains necessary as applicable; its necessity alone does not bar the authorized action. Concrete injury, traceability and redressability for the represented members remain constitutional requirements. Accepted allegations suffice only at the appropriate stage; genuinely disputed elements require stage-appropriate proof. Replacing an ordinance does not moot the dispute when the successor continues the challenged exclusion from particular competitions; a replacement actually ending that injury may have a different effect.

**Authority by component:**

- Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- The replacement continues the controversy: Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville — The replacement continues the controversy, 1993-06-14. Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, The replacement continues the controversy portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.
- Brown Group — Parts I–II, express WARN authority and prudential third condition, 1996-05-13; Souter for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Brown Group, May 13, 1996, for congressional displacement of the third condition; Northeastern Florida’s competitive-injury and mootness rules survive.

**Holdings navigation:** Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656 (1993); United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc., 517 U.S. 544.

**Exceptions and limits:** The union receives no right to its own damages or the local-government penalty; employee eligibility, ordinary defenses, individual amounts and WARN’s exclusive statutory remedies retain their requirements. No closing or layoff injunction is authorized by WARN. Standing germaneness does not itself establish adequate representation or binding effect, and no mandatory certification, joinder or jury procedure follows.

**Open question:** Whether Hunt’s germaneness condition is prudential and displaceable, or a sufficiently simplified damages claim could satisfy the third condition without abrogation, remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Review preclusion for the approved 1990 base-closure package

### Review preclusion for the approved 1990 base-closure package

**Governing question:** Whether implementation litigation may reopen the approved package under the 1990 base-closure statute.

**Current rule:** The 1990 base-closure statute’s integrated selection, recommendation, presidential approval and congressional-review process, with its constrained choices and strict deadlines, precludes an APA challenge to the approved closure selection through litigation nominally directed at implementation and antecedent selection defects. The holding is tied to that completed statutory package. It does not establish a categorical rule that every presidential recommendation is nonfinal, or resolve presidential APA status as a controlling rationale.

**Authority by component:**

- Scheme-specific preclusion of the completed selection challenge: Dalton v. Specter — The approved closure selection cannot be reopened through this implementation challenge, 1994-05-23. Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. The statutory-preclusion ground alone commands the Court; a four-Justice concurrence on recommendation finality is not controlling.

**Present operation effective:** Dalton v. Specter, 1994-05-23.

**Open question:** Distinct timely claims to compel an unperformed statutory duty, constitutional claims and genuinely ultra vires conduct remain outside the holding. The broader four-Justice rationale is not governing law.

**Holdings navigation:** The approved closure selection cannot be reopened through this implementation challenge.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals

### Private settlement rights and collateral-order appeals

**Governing question:** Whether rejection of a private settlement defense permits an immediate collateral-order appeal.

**Current rule:** Ordinary private settlement rights do not have the public importance required for immediate appeal under the collateral-order doctrine. Independently, final-judgment review adequately vindicates this category of contractual rights despite the expense of the intervening litigation. Describing the bargain as a right not to stand trial does not establish appealability. These independently sufficient limits govern the category presented, not every conceivable private agreement; other authorized interlocutory routes, including §1292 certification when its conditions are met, remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for affirmance.

Desktop Direct, Inc — Independently, final review adequately vindicates this category of settlement rights, 1994-06-06. Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative; Stone-Zsela does not join Part II.

**Present operation effective:** Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc, 1994-06-06.

**Holdings navigation:** A private settlement does not supply the required collateral-order importance.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-law incidents of claims acquired by the FDIC

### State-law incidents of claims acquired by the FDIC

**Governing question:** Whether FDIC receivership creates a federal rule displacing state imputation defenses to acquired state tort claims.

**Current rule:** When the FDIC acquires an institution’s state-law tort claims, state law ordinarily supplies the associated imputation rules and defenses. Federal financial interests or a preference for uniformity alone do not justify new federal common law; a significant conflict with an identified federal policy is required. For pre-FIRREA receivership claims the ordinary rule applies independently of that Act. If FIRREA applies, §1821’s succession to the institution’s rights carries their legal incidents subject to Congress’s enacted exceptions; it supplies no additional categorical anti-imputation rule.

**Authority by component:**

- State-law rule and federal-common-law limit: O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC — These acquired California claims carry California imputation law, 1994-06-13. Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same pre-FIRREA ground, independently sufficient to reject the proposed rule without deciding FIRREA's temporal reach.
- Independent conditional FIRREA ground: O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC — FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule, 1994-06-13. Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this conditional statutory construction and its temporal reservation. It is not a finding that FIRREA applies retrospectively.

**Present operation effective:** O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC, 1994-06-13.

**Open question:** The decision leaves FIRREA’s temporal applicability and the eventual operation of the particular state-law defense for the lower courts.

**Holdings navigation:** These acquired California claims carry California imputation law; FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal, 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

- Successive-stage review and preserved mandate limits: Behrens — Holding 1, February 21, 1996; Ginsburg Part I for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and herself (seven).
- Conditional assumed-fact rule: Behrens — Holding 2, February 21, 1996; unanimous Part II. Claim-specific effect of another surviving claim: Holding 3, Part III for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join. Stevens and Breyer join these conditional rules while opposing authorization of the successive appeal.

**Present operation effective:** Behrens, February 21, 1996; Johnson and Kimberlin retain their original component authority.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

**Holdings navigation:** Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal; Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule; Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299.

## Standards and Tests selection: Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

### Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

**Governing question:** A state class-settlement judgment releasing claims within exclusive federal adjudicatory jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** Section 1738 first requires the effect the rendering State gives its judgment; class form, consent disposition and exclusive federal jurisdiction alone create no categorical exception. Approval of an authorized representative release differs from adjudicating the released federal liability. Exchange Act §27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 for this judgment category. An incorporated, affirmed Delaware release has its ordinary state-law effect subject to Delaware's identical-factual-predicate and representative limits; alleged ordinary state-law error does not itself reopen that judgment. Recognition remains subject to constitutional authority to bind absent persons and does not confer state jurisdiction over federal securities liability or authorize coercive interference with federal litigation.

**Authority by component:**

- State-law-first framework: Matsushita — Section 1738 governs this kind of judgment, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part I.
- Ordinary Delaware recognition: the same Record, “This judgment receives ordinary Delaware preclusive effect,” Part II, seven Justices (all except Stevens and Ginsburg).
- Section 27 nonrepeal: the same Record, “Section 27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 here,” Part III, eight Justices (all except Ginsburg).

**Present operation effective:** Matsushita, February 27, 1996.

**Open question:** Actual constitutional adequacy, lawful binding and the complete permissible scope of collateral review, including effect of a full and fair prior adequacy determination, remain unresolved. No universal same-transaction release rule or fiction of individually negotiated consent arises from failure to opt out; valid opt-outs remain outside the release.

**Related current rules:** Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies the independent constitutional minimum.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367.

## Standards and Tests selection: Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

### Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

**Governing question:** The due-process prerequisite to claim or issue preclusion against a nonparty.

**Current rule:** Identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources, public importance and fiscal reliance alone do not authorize binding absent persons' own legal rights. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process. Constitutionally inadequate representation is not independently cured by notice, opportunities to object or opt out; notice alone imposes no general duty on strangers to intervene. Issue preclusion, like claim preclusion, requires a lawful basis to bind the person. Genuine representative public-right adjudication and use of precedent in a later case remain distinct from extinguishing a stranger's personal claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Representation independent of notice and exclusion rights: Matsushita — Constitutional representation and unresolved review questions limit the remand, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part IV-A; eight Associates reserve complete collateral-review scope in IV-B.
- Nonparty binding and direct personal tax claims, including the issue-preclusion alternative: Richards — Holdings II–IV, June 10, 1996; Stone-Zsela for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Richards, June 10, 1996; Matsushita supplies the retained representation component.

**Open question:** The Court assumes without deciding whether adequate representation may cure absent notice in some representative proceedings. It neither requires individual notice in every such action nor constitutionalizes every federal class-action requirement. The broader adequacy of officials' representation of constituents, ordinary state-law-error review and Matsushita's collateral-review boundary are not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367; Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793.

## Standards and Tests selection: State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

### State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

**Governing question:** The measure and allocation of compensatory-excessiveness review in a New York diversity action.

**Current rule:** The district court applies CPLR §5501(c)'s material-deviation-from-reasonable-compensation measure through Rule 59, retaining federal new-trial procedure and allocation of functions. Pertinent state decisions guide compensation, without fixed prices or an imported state appellate hierarchy. The court of appeals reviews for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment; the Seventh Amendment permits that bounded review but forbids a first-instance appellate valuation of jury-tried compensation. A remittitur must offer the plaintiff reduced recovery or a new jury trial, not simply impose a substituted amount; federal additur remains forbidden.

**Authority by component:**

- State standard and coexistence with Rule 59: Gasperini — Holding I, June 24, 1996; Souter Part I for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer and himself (seven).
- Trial/appellate allocation and lawful remittitur: the same Record, Holding II; Souter Part II for the same coalition except Stevens (six).

**Present operation effective:** Gasperini, June 24, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** This does not transplant every outcome-affecting state procedure, adopt a fixed damages cap or authorize a conditional increase under state practice. No damages amount follows from the rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc., 518 U.S. 415 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Individual-capacity liability for official conduct under §1983

### Individual-capacity liability for official conduct under §1983

**Governing question:** Whether use of official authority prevents a state officer from being a §1983 person when sued individually.

**Current rule:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority; sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by the officer personally. The plaintiff must prove the officer's own federal violation under color of law, and applicable personal immunities remain available. The capacity in which the officer is sued, rather than whether the conduct was official, distinguishes this action from a claim against the State or an official-capacity damages claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Personhood, capacity distinction, personal-payment immunity limit, and retained defenses: Hafer v. Melo — Individual-capacity §1983 coverage of official conduct, November 5, 1991; O'Connor's unanimous participating opinion.

**Present operation effective:** *Hafer*, November 5, 1991.

**Holdings navigation:** Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991) — Individual-capacity liability.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

### Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern equal protection of commitment proof and family participation?

**Current rule:** Kentucky's clear-and-convincing burden for adult intellectual-disability commitment, compared with reasonable-doubt proof for mental illness, fails rational-basis review because the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not justify the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Separately, continuing family knowledge rationally supports guardians' and immediate relatives' party participation for lifelong developmental disability. ## Standards and Tests selection: Effective correction of sex-based educational exclusion

### Effective correction of sex-based educational exclusion

**Governing question:** The adequacy of a proposed alternative remedy for an unconstitutional exclusion from a public educational opportunity.

**Current rule:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, considering material tangible and intangible benefits. A shared general purpose or equal annual public subsidy alone does not establish adequacy. Government must promptly eliminate the denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, retaining initial responsibility for lawful design under judicial supervision. Continued provision of the same public opportunity without an adequate lawful alternative requires consideration of qualified applicants without the unconstitutional categorical sex bar; promises of improvement do not permit indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive remedial adequacy and lawful government choice: Part II — Adequacy of the proposed alternative and Part III — Effective relief and lawful State choice, 1996-06-26; O’Connor, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven); Thomas did not participate.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Virginia / Virginia v. United States, 1996-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate qualifications, privacy, physical training and operational accommodations remain available. No identical campus, numerical resource ratio, expenditure minimum, guaranteed career outcome, quota or categorical prohibition of all separate education is prescribed. Admission scrutiny remains demanding intermediate review: a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means; neither litigation timing alone nor lack of an original written rationale establishes pretext.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective and subjective requirements, totality and absence of an injury floor: Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc — Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Present operation effective:** Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc, 1993-11-09.

**Open question:** A sex-specific reasonable-person formulation, constructive discharge and employer-attribution questions are not resolved by Harris.

**Holdings navigation:** Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account

### Public-employee speech and the employer’s mistaken factual account

**Governing question:** Which employer beliefs about employee speech protect a disciplinary decision, and which do not.

**Current rule:** An employer acting on a genuine, reasonable account of speech that would be disciplinable under the governing public-employee-speech rules is protected even if that factual account is mistaken. Conversely, an unreasonable and incorrect account does not defeat a claim when the employee actually spoke protected speech and that speech caused the adverse action. The employee must show protected speech was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may establish by a preponderance that it would have taken the same action absent the protected speech. These limited propositions do not impose liability for every investigation defect or adopt the plurality’s complete investigative procedure.

**Authority by component:**

- Seven-Justice genuine-and-reasonable-account safe harbor: Waters v. Churchill — A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor, 1994-05-31. O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.
- Six-Justice unreasonable-and-false-account limit: Waters v. Churchill — An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech, 1994-05-31. O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.
- Motive and same-decision proof: Waters v. Churchill — The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment, 1994-05-31. O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Present operation effective:** Waters v. Churchill, 1994-05-31.

**Open question:** Unreasonable belief where the actual speech was unprotected, and a universal investigation checklist, are not resolved. The four-Justice complete reasonableness framework is noncontrolling.

**Holdings navigation:** A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor; An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech; The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Religious accommodation and demonstrated operational hardship

### Religious accommodation and demonstrated operational hardship

**Governing question:** An unresolved work conflict with religious observance under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Under §2000e(j), the employer must reasonably accommodate religious practice unless it demonstrates inability without undue hardship. The governing Hardison threshold permits refusal of more than de minimis cost, including concrete lost efficiency, impaired training, safety burdens and protected seniority interests. Neutral scheduling and absence of hostility do not discharge this separate duty. If an effective reasonable accommodation resolves the conflict, the employer need not supply the employee’s preferred alternative or disprove every other possibility. If the conflict remains, hardship must address supported, materially distinct proposals and actual operations; a concession about one proposal does not dispose of materially different ones.

**Authority by component:**

- Beadle — both accommodation holdings, 1996-02-05; Stevens and all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Present operation effective:** Beadle, February 5, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** Professional evidence and reasonable operational inferences may establish hardship without waiting for an accident or monetizing every burden. No independent negotiation duty arises when effective accommodation or adequate hardship already exists. General safety concerns create no occupational exemption, and this rule establishes no particular proposal’s feasibility or entitlement.

**Holdings navigation:** Beadle v. City of Tampa, judgment below 42 F.3d 633.

## Standards and Tests selection: Reviewability of the Secretary's completed census determination

### Reviewability of the Secretary's completed census determination

**Governing question:** APA review of a completed population count followed by presidential transmission of apportionment figures.

**Current rule:** The Secretary's completed census determination is final agency action under §704 despite the President's later statutory transmission of the resulting apportionment. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit counting entirely to unreviewable discretion: the obligation to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice. General supervision and possible correction do not make this completed decision tentative; the prescribed transmission differs from a statutorily independent presidential policy judgment.

**Authority by component:**

- Finality and reviewability: Franklin v. Massachusetts, “The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable”, June 26, 1992; Stone's Part IV joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter.

**Present operation effective:** *Franklin*, June 26, 1992.

**Open question:** The President's APA status and susceptibility to coercive judicial relief are left undecided. This rule does not turn remaining agency discretion into entitlement to a selected outcome.
