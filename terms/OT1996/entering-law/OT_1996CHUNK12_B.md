## Standards and Tests selection: Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

### Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

**Governing question:** A state class-settlement judgment releasing claims within exclusive federal adjudicatory jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** Section 1738 first requires the effect the rendering State gives its judgment; class form, consent disposition and exclusive federal jurisdiction alone create no categorical exception. Approval of an authorized representative release differs from adjudicating the released federal liability. Exchange Act §27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 for this judgment category. An incorporated, affirmed Delaware release has its ordinary state-law effect subject to Delaware's identical-factual-predicate and representative limits; alleged ordinary state-law error does not itself reopen that judgment. Recognition remains subject to constitutional authority to bind absent persons and does not confer state jurisdiction over federal securities liability or authorize coercive interference with federal litigation.

**Authority by component:**

- State-law-first framework: Matsushita — Section 1738 governs this kind of judgment, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part I.
- Ordinary Delaware recognition: the same Record, “This judgment receives ordinary Delaware preclusive effect,” Part II, seven Justices (all except Stevens and Ginsburg).
- Section 27 nonrepeal: the same Record, “Section 27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 here,” Part III, eight Justices (all except Ginsburg).

**Present operation effective:** Matsushita, February 27, 1996.

**Open question:** Actual constitutional adequacy, lawful binding and the complete permissible scope of collateral review, including effect of a full and fair prior adequacy determination, remain unresolved. No universal same-transaction release rule or fiction of individually negotiated consent arises from failure to opt out; valid opt-outs remain outside the release.

**Related current rules:** Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies the independent constitutional minimum.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367.

## Standards and Tests selection: Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

### Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

**Governing question:** The due-process prerequisite to claim or issue preclusion against a nonparty.

**Current rule:** Identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources, public importance and fiscal reliance alone do not authorize binding absent persons' own legal rights. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process. Constitutionally inadequate representation is not independently cured by notice, opportunities to object or opt out; notice alone imposes no general duty on strangers to intervene. Issue preclusion, like claim preclusion, requires a lawful basis to bind the person. Genuine representative public-right adjudication and use of precedent in a later case remain distinct from extinguishing a stranger's personal claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Representation independent of notice and exclusion rights: Matsushita — Constitutional representation and unresolved review questions limit the remand, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part IV-A; eight Associates reserve complete collateral-review scope in IV-B.
- Nonparty binding and direct personal tax claims, including the issue-preclusion alternative: Richards — Holdings II–IV, June 10, 1996; Stone-Zsela for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Richards, June 10, 1996; Matsushita supplies the retained representation component.

**Open question:** The Court assumes without deciding whether adequate representation may cure absent notice in some representative proceedings. It neither requires individual notice in every such action nor constitutionalizes every federal class-action requirement. The broader adequacy of officials' representation of constituents, ordinary state-law-error review and Matsushita's collateral-review boundary are not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367; Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793.

## Standards and Tests selection: RFRA review of prospective enforcement

### RFRA review of prospective enforcement

**Governing question:** Application of original RFRA to threatened enforcement, including preexisting laws and commercial religious exercise.

**Current rule:** The claimant must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise. The government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The inquiry applies to threatened future implementation of a preexisting law. Commercial participation does not forfeit its protection, and labeling a law antidiscrimination does not itself justify its application. Direct transactional and dignitary injury and legally material alternatives or exemptions may be considered; availability of alternative housing is not an automatic answer to direct injury.

RFRA also governs later federal enactments unless Congress expressly excludes application by reference to RFRA; FACE contains no such exclusion.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

### Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions?

**Current rule:** For truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech about lawful activity, the government must establish real harms, material advancement and a reasonable fit between its chosen restriction and its interests. Where commercial and newspaper racks cause the same physical harms, the commercial category requires justification tied to those harms; benign objectives alone do not make content-selected regulation content neutral. Incremental regulation is not invalid merely because its reduction is small, but alternatives bear on fit without a least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement. In professional solicitation the government must demonstrate advancement in the relevant professional setting, without an invariable empirical-study requirement. Deceptive or coercive solicitation remains regulable. Central Hudson and Fox remain the framework; no general equality of commercial and noncommercial speech or categorical vice exception follows. When the State otherwise makes records available, a condition denying access and prohibiting use because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation receives this same review. Absence of a general right to every government record does not remove a speech-selected condition from First Amendment scrutiny; neutral access, retrieval-fee and privacy-redaction rules retain their lawful scope.

## Standards and Tests selection: Timely state redistricting and superseding state judgments

### Timely state redistricting and superseding state judgments

**Governing question:** What instructions govern timely state redistricting and superseding state judgments?

**Current rule:** Federal courts must defer redistricting while a State's legislature or judiciary is timely undertaking it unless the record shows no lawful plan will be produced in time for election. A reasonable deadline and retained jurisdiction may safeguard federal relief, but preference for another plan cannot displace or obstruct timely state work; every state appeal need not finish first. A final state judgment replacing the challenged plan must receive legal effect, not treatment as an optional proposal. The challenge to the old plan is moot unless it also reaches the replacement; old-plan allegations alone do not establish a new-plan violation. This supplies no broader party-preclusion rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Culpable affirmative assistance to a securities fraud

### Culpable affirmative assistance to a securities fraud

**Governing question:** When the received private §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 action reaches a person assisting another’s fraud.

**Current rule:** A person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct can be privately liable. The defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the activity’s fraudulent character and its conduct’s assisting role. Severe recklessness is an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it. An independent disclosure duty is unnecessary for affirmative assistance. The plaintiff must prove the underlying manipulation or deception, qualifying assistance and culpability, legally sufficient reliance on the underlying fraud, causation and recoverable loss by the ordinary civil preponderance standard. Investors need not have known of or relied on concealed assistance itself; no reliance presumption follows merely from this rule.

No fractured-decision synthesis is necessary. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; all five adopt this confined mental-state and affirmative-assistance rule, not a universal recklessness rule for every securities claim. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the record application, preserved proof requirements and limited affirmance and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A, 1994-04-19.

## Standards and Tests selection: Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

**Authority by component:**

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. ## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

#### Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1809.

**Decided:** February 27, 1996.

##### Whether §1738 reaches an incorporated class-settlement release of exclusively federal claims

**Holding:** A state judicial judgment incorporating a class settlement falls within §1738 even when it releases claims subject to exclusive federal adjudicatory jurisdiction. Recognition begins with rendering-state law; class form, consent disposition and exclusive jurisdiction alone create no categorical exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A representative's authority to bind absent members differs from an owner's personal settlement capacity. Silence after notice is not individual contractual consent; release wording supplies no missing constitutional representation. State courts acquire no power to adjudicate Exchange Act liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marrese requires state-law inquiry and preserves its distinct settlement-release reservation; its adjudicated-claim competency limit is not mechanically transferred to releases. Kokkonen's incorporation distinction does not make representative authority self-validating.

##### The ordinary Delaware effect of this judgment

**Holding:** The affirmed Delaware judgment's incorporated release receives ordinary Delaware preclusive effect and cannot lose that effect merely because a federal litigant alleges state-law error in settlement approval, subject to unresolved federal constitutional binding limits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nottingham's identical-factual-predicate and representative limits and Prezant's Rule 23 and express adequacy requirements remain. No same-acquisition universal release rule, finding about counsel incentives or full-and-fair adequacy determination is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marrese supplies the inquiry, not an inflexible first-instance remand. Kremer preserves finality subject to due process and the actual scope of matters determined.

##### Whether Exchange Act §27 impliedly displaces §1738 for this category

**Holding:** Section 27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 for the state settlement judgments at issue. Exclusive federal adjudication of Exchange Act liability is compatible with a state court's approval of an authorized representative release within an action over which it has jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices. Ginsburg reserves this further determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Claim valuation is distinct from adjudicating federal liability. Recognition does not approve every state injunction or contempt remedy interfering with a pending federal action; an independently preserved coercive-operation objection retains its own lawful treatment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marrese and Kremer require a supported basis to displace recognition; uniform federal adjudication does not establish statutory incompatibility here.

##### Constitutional representation and the reserved scope of collateral review

**Holding:** Section 1738 cannot bind an absent class member through constitutionally inadequate representation; notice, objection opportunities and opt-out rights do not independently cure that defect. Actual constitutional adequacy and binding effect remain undecided, so immediate dismissal is not directed.

Conversely, notice, objections or opt-out opportunities do not independently cure inadequate representation, and mere notice of another person's suit supplies neither representative authority nor a general intervention duty.

Ordinary state preclusion and valid finality remain. Correction of this extreme application supplies no general retrial of ordinary state-law errors and decides none of Matsushita's full collateral-review reservation. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422, supplies the protected status of an otherwise available individual claim, or chose in action, reinforcing the same hearing right; it creates no new cause of action or universal state-remedy scheme. **Decided:** December 4, 1991.

##### When a continuing general objector's disputed compulsory bar dues must be protected

**Holding:** Once a compulsory-bar member makes a continuing objection to financing nongermane ideological activity, the bar may not require repeated objections to each announced position or leave the reasonably disputed share available for use until each new objection arrives. The bar must provide adequate advance explanation and protect the disputed share before use through a reasonable reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow; it retains the burden to justify chargeability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas; eight of nine participants.

**Material application:** Annual collection preceded twice-monthly lobbying announcements, and protection began only after position-specific objections. Budget estimates and protection of a reasonably sufficient amount can address uncertainty without exact advance prediction; repeated 45-day waivers cannot shift the monitoring burden back to a known dissenter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Keller applies compelled-dues safeguards to integrated bars. Hudson requires explanation, prompt review, and protection of disputed funds. Ellis rejects later repayment with interest as a cure for temporary ideological use. **Holding:** Membership in the compulsory bar alone does not establish constitutional bias in every arbitrator selected through a procedure giving the bar and objector one selection each and requiring a mutually selected or judicially appointed chair. Actual bias, a direct stake, or obstructive delay remains subject to review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion joined on this proposition by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices.

**Material application:** Neither party controls the chair, and judicial appointment is available if agreement fails. The shared indirect incidence of bar dues does not itself establish a direct stake in a particular award.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling does not approve an actually biased panel, indefinite delay in constituting it, or a procedure ineffective in operation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The rejection of the facial panel-membership challenge is affirmed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hudson's demand for reasonably prompt impartial review is applied; Bar membership alone does not defeat that requirement under this selection arrangement.

**Holding:** Even under ordinary *Central Hudson* review and treating temperance as substantial, Rhode Island has not demonstrated that these truthful-price restrictions directly and materially reduce consumption. Constitutional sufficiency is independently assessed without turning appellate economic inference into a new trial finding.

Concrete direct price and tax alternatives expose inadequately justified fit; they are neither mandatory replacements nor a least-restrictive-means rule.

**Holding:** A political party's own campaign advertisement that is genuinely independent of a candidate may not be subjected to the Senate-election expenditure ceiling in 2 U.S.C. §441a(d)(3) merely because its speaker is an official political party. #### Growe v. Emison, 507 U.S. 25 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1420.

##### Timely state redistricting includes the State's courts

**Holding:** When a State's legislature or judiciary is timely undertaking redistricting, a federal court must defer its own redistricting action unless the record shows that the State will not produce a lawful plan in time for the election. The federal court may set a reasonable deadline and retain jurisdiction as a safeguard, but may neither obstruct timely state work nor displace it merely because it prefers another plan; completion of every state appeal before the election is not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion on state priority, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt this rule. The separate eight-Justice dismissal direction concerns this completed record, not the general availability of retained jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Scott v. Germano, 381 U.S. 407 (1965): applied to both legislative and congressional redistricting through state courts; its timely-action condition and reserve federal jurisdiction remain.
- Reynolds v. Sims, 377 U.S. 533 (1964): applied for primary state responsibility and the obligation to remedy unconstitutional apportionment; population-equality requirements are unchanged.

##### A superseding state judgment must receive legal effect

**Holding:** Once a state court's final judgment replaces the challenged legislative plan, the federal court must give the replacement legal effect rather than treat it as one optional proposal. A challenge to the old plan becomes moot unless it also reaches the superseding plan; allegations against the old plan alone do not establish a violation in the replacement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion on the live-plan issue, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the stated rule. No party-preclusion rule beyond that necessary legal effect is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Atlantic Coast Line Railroad Co. v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 398 U.S. 281 (1970): applied for respect for state judgments and the federal-state judicial relationship; no enlarged preclusion rule adopted.
- Wise v. Lipscomb, 437 U.S. 535 (1978): applied for the need to evaluate the legally operative replacement before federal remedial displacement.

##### Fragmentation claims require proof of the causal voting conditions

**Holding:** A §2 claim that single-member districts fragment a minority group's potential electoral majority requires proof that the group is sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a majority in a single-member district, is politically cohesive, and faces majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate. Without the required cohesion and majority-bloc evidence, the court may not order a minority district by adding distinct groups' populations; the Court assumes without deciding that a supported coalition claim is permissible and leaves influence-only claims and the population denominator unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's statutory opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the stated single-member extension and record application. No fractured-opinion or Marks inference is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): extended from multimember dilution to the single-member fragmentation claim at issue; its causal prerequisites and totality inquiry remain distinct, and its influence-only reservation remains open.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gingles prerequisites are necessary, not sufficient; the totality inquiry remains. No proportional representation, mandatory statistical method, filing-date priority or enlarged party-preclusion rule is adopted. Coalition permissibility is assumed; influence claims and population denominator remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the federal redistricting judgment. Eight Justices—White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—direct dismissal of this action; Stone-Zsela supports reversal but would allow conditional retention. ##### Racial predominance alone does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding:** Predominant racial consideration in drawing a common territorial constituency, even when ordinary districting criteria are subordinated, does not by itself establish the asserted equal-protection deprivation or trigger strict scrutiny. **Holding:** A status or participation injury can be complete when government itself imposes unequal racial treatment; no additional monetary loss, electoral defeat or later denial of service is necessary. **Holding:** A State’s otherwise lawful consideration of racial conditions to improve electoral opportunity is not unconstitutional merely because no prior adjudication or federal statute requires the precise district chosen. This negative proposition leaves every independent constitutional and statutory restriction intact and creates neither a duty to maximize majority-minority districts nor a right to proportional officeholding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes for the limited permission-versus-compulsion proposition. Only Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer conclude that this plan survives the asserted constitutional challenge.

Direct and circumstantial evidence may establish the facts material to the particular claim, subject to its own elements and proof requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-A, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes at this evidentiary level; they disagree about the substantive effect of proved predominance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purpose evidence can include shape, demographics, statements, instructions, departures from ordinary criteria and actual operation, with supported alternatives considered. No universal compactness cutoff, expert report, commissioned study or replacement map is required; any comparator or proof required by the actual claim remains necessary.

- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: treats geography as evidence of an operative constitutional wrong, not a universal shape test.
- Wright v. Rockefeller: distinguished because this case accepts predominant racial purpose rather than finding it unproved.
- Shaw v. Reno: preserves ordinary pleading and claim-specific proof without an aesthetic threshold.

**Holding:** Trial findings concerning districting purpose receive ordinary clear-error review, while the legal consequence of those findings is decided separately. The supported finding that race predominated and traditional criteria were subordinated is accepted here; no supported basis for setting it aside is supplied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part V-B, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes on acceptance and review of the finding; the constitutional consequence divides 5–4 as stated above.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Acceptance of racial predominance does not establish every constitutional element. The conditional statutory-compliance defense discussion, although joined on its hypothesis by all nine, is nonessential to the judgment and supplies no independent alternative holding, scrutiny trigger or invalidation of DOJ action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Anderson v. Bessemer City: applies ordinary clear-error review to the supported factual finding.
- Shaw v. Reno: controls the distinct legal consequence; changing an assumed predicate into an accepted finding does not itself displace the rule.

##### The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance

**Holding:** The established private action under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 permits recovery from a person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct, subject to proof of that fraud, the defendant’s qualifying participation, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable loss. ### 1997-01-14 - Castano v. American Tobacco Co.; Fifth Circuit No. 95-30725; 84 F.3d 734; interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b).

Authority: [Record](../records/Castano_v_American_Tobacco_Co_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. All four holdings command nine votes.

##### I. Certification requires a demonstrated legal and evidentiary basis

**Holding and operative rule.** Before certifying a Rule 23(b)(3) class, a court must determine from the actual claims, governing law and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and class adjudication is superior to the available alternatives, together with satisfaction of Rule 23(a). Conditional certification does not permit postponing that showing, and a claim's novelty neither dispenses with rigorous analysis nor categorically bars certification until individual verdicts exist.

**Controlling explanation.** Falcon requires rigorous examination of the Rule's prerequisites. Eisen forbids awarding or denying certification merely because the court predicts success on the merits; it does not make the inquiry pleadings-only when the substantive elements determine whether a common trial can work. The proponents must identify what the class proceeding would decide and how that work connects to remaining claims and defenses. An untested liability theory makes that explanation especially necessary, but the Rule contains no tort-maturation requirement. Common evidence of manufacturer conduct cannot itself establish that the resulting questions predominate over the unresolved legal and individual work. Administrative convenience cannot replace a prerequisite. Matsushita preserves constitutionally adequate representation as distinct from notice or opt-out rights, and Richards preserves the lawful basis needed to bind absent persons; neither supplies a finding of actual adequacy or conflict in this case. A renewed proposal must satisfy the representation requirements as well as predominance and superiority.

##### II. The present core-liability class cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule.** This nationwide core-liability certification fails Rule 23(b)(3) because the court deferred the material state-law inquiry and did not show how common issues, individual addiction, reliance, causation and defenses would fit into lawful, manageable adjudication. Differences among state laws do not automatically prohibit every multistate class, but proponents must demonstrate materially common answers or workable groupings rather than assume uniformity or rely on subclass labels.

**Controlling explanation.** Shutts prevents applying a single State's law to materially disconnected claims merely for convenience; it does not select the governing law here. The pleaded fraud, misrepresentation, negligence, warranty and consumer-protection claims require examination of their actual duties, defenses and burdens. The District Court certified core conduct while excluding injury, causation, reliance, defenses and individual compensatory damages. Without explaining how the common answers would materially resolve the legally varied claims, the court could not establish predominance or make a grounded superiority comparison. The proponents' strongest point is that common manufacturer evidence is substantial and individual litigation expensive. But an imagined future trial structure cannot supply the missing showing for the order entered. Workable legal groupings or genuinely separable common issues may matter on a different proposal. The Court requires a credible account of claims and proof, not a universal separate document bearing the title trial plan. It does not decide the full relationship between Rule 23(b)(3) and (c)(4), including whether issue certification may ever proceed when an entire cause of action fails an action-wide predominance inquiry.

##### III. The punitive component and superiority finding also fail

**Holding and operative rule.** The certified punitive-ratio component fails because its state-law predicates, burdens, relation to liability and coordination with individual proceedings were not demonstrated; those same gaps defeat the present superiority finding. Settlement pressure from a procedure that bypasses defenses or detaches punitive assessment from established claims may reinforce concrete certification defects, but aggregate size or ordinary settlement pressure alone is not a categorical bar.

**Controlling explanation.** The certification order incorporated important elements of the proposed plan, including its punitive-ratio arrangement, without adopting that plan in full. The court did not establish what one jury could lawfully decide about punishment before the remaining individualized liability work, or how later proceedings would use that decision consistently with each governing law. Predicted savings from millions of trials cannot substitute for identifying the work the common trial would actually accomplish. The manufacturers' concern about repeated jury determinations underscores the need for that account, but repetition of evidence is not automatically reexamination of a decided fact. The Court rests on Rule 23 and does not decide the constitutionality of every punitive ratio, issue class or bifurcated trial. The proposed Liggett settlement does not establish a completed release or make the certification controversy moot. No manufacturer liability, individual recovery, punitive amount, Article III severance question, new Seventh Amendment rule or complete resolution of every asserted Erie objection is decided.

##### IV. Decertification does not require a categorical ban on reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The rejection of the present core-liability and punitive certification is affirmed, but the mandate is vacated insofar as it requires dismissal of the class pleading and bars reconsideration merely because the tort has not matured through individual trials. The case is remanded for ordinary proceedings on individual claims or consideration of a materially narrower, supported class proposal that independently satisfies all applicable Rule 23 requirements; the existing class remains uncertified.

**Controlling explanation.** The lower court identified decisive defects in this proposal, then treated the asserted novelty of the tort as making correction on remand categorically unavailable. The Rule requires a present showing, not a quota of prior trials or an existing crisis in individual litigation. Rejecting an unsupported class does not decide whether a materially different proposal can establish its prerequisites. The proponents retain their burden; remand creates no presumption of certification, discovery entitlement, preferred trial plan or deadline. It adjudicates no individual claim on the merits. The separate denial of Rule 23(b)(2) equitable and medical-monitoring certification was not cross-appealed and is not reopened.

#### Precedent Treatment

- General Telephone Co. of Southwest v. Falcon, 457 U.S. 147: applied to require rigorous determination of the actual certification prerequisites, including where the inquiry overlaps substantive issues.
- Eisen v. Carlisle & Jacquelin, 417 U.S. 156: preserved as forbidding certification based merely on a preliminary prediction of merits success; it does not forbid the substantive inquiry needed to determine whether class adjudication can work.
- Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts, 472 U.S. 797: applied against assuming one State's substantive law for materially disconnected claims; no particular choice-of-law result is directed.
- Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996: its adequate-representation requirement remains distinct from notice and opt-out rights; its judgment-recognition holding does not itself decide prospective certification or the actual adequacy of this class.
- Richards v. Jefferson County, June 10, 1996: preserved for the lawful basis necessary to bind absent persons; it does not constitutionalize every Rule 23 defect or resolve the reserved certification questions.

#### Law After Decision

Rule 23 requires the concrete law-and-proof showing before certification. Neither novelty nor ordinary aggregation pressure creates an independent exclusion, and differing state laws require demonstrated analysis rather than an automatic national-class prohibition. The present certification is defeated by its own missing prerequisites. The partial vacatur preserves lawful reconsideration without reinstating or presuming any class, modifying individual substantive burdens, or creating a federal tobacco tort.

### 1997-03-19 — Bennett v. Spear; No. 95-813.

Authority: [Record](../records/Bennett_v_Spear_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is directly joined by all nine Justices. That common coalition controls every proposition below.

###### Petitioners adequately plead their own constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Irrigators alleging concrete loss of their project water from a Biological Opinion that the operating agency has announced it will follow adequately plead injury, fair traceability and likely redress from removal of the challenged constraint. They must support standing with the evidence required at each later litigation stage; statutory permission alone never replaces Article III injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires a concrete personal interest and proof appropriate to the stage, but its three-Justice absent-agency discussion supplies no categorical rule defeating this claim. The alleged loss is the petitioners’ own, and the Bureau’s announced compliance links the Service’s completed action to that threatened injury. The Bureau’s operational authority does not make its response unpredictable as a matter of law or require certainty that every eventual choice will change. Setting aside the challenged constraint can afford concrete relief. Humane Society’s missing connection between a later permit amendment and an already completed transfer is absent here. Tracy recognizes cognizable economic injury without making every economic claimant eligible to sue. Arizonans likewise requires an actual continuing controversy; no later fact eliminating this alleged injury is established. Merits uncertainty about the biological conclusion is not a pleading-stage standing defect.

###### The citizen-suit language is broad within its actual channels

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1540(g)’s authorization to any person removes an additional species-preservation zone-of-interests restriction for claims that its particular channels authorize. Section (g)(1)(C) permits the alleged failure to perform a nondiscretionary §1533 duty, including the pleaded failure to consider economic effects of an asserted de facto habitat designation; §(g)(1)(A) does not authorize general supervision of the Secretary’s alleged §1536 maladministration.

**Controlling explanation:** The breadth of the plaintiff language cannot be restricted by an unwritten environmental preference, but it does not erase the enacted division among person-in-violation actions, emergency enforcement and Secretary-duty suits. Treating every alleged administrative error as a violation under (A) would disregard (C)’s specific limit to nondiscretionary §1533 acts. Petitioners may pursue the asserted §1533(b)(2) consideration duty through (C) at this threshold stage; the Court does not find that a designation occurred or triggered a duty. The §1536 consultation claims require a different review route.

When designation or revision occurs, §1533(b)(2) requires consideration of economic and other relevant impacts. Exclusion of an area is discretionary when exclusion benefits outweigh inclusion benefits, and remains forbidden if the best scientific and commercial data show that failure to designate will result in extinction. Reviewability commands neither a particular exclusion nor wholesale economic balancing during §1536 consultation. The (B) emergency channel is not invoked. Article III remains independently necessary.

**Notice and enforcement limits:** The (A) route generally requires 60 days’ written notice to the Secretary and alleged violator; a commenced civil-penalty action or a commenced, diligently prosecuted United States criminal action addressing the violation bars it. The (B) route separately requires 60 days’ notice explaining the claimed emergency and is barred by the Secretary’s commenced diligent emergency-determination action. The (C) route requires 60 days’ notice to the Secretary, except that suit concerning an emergency posing significant risk to the well-being of a fish, wildlife or plant species may follow notification immediately. No actual compliance, applicable emergency or enforcement bar is found or waived here.

###### The consultation claims have an APA route and a provision-specific interest

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed §1536 consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels provide no other adequate judicial remedy for these administrative claims. Petitioners’ alleged injury from restrictions unsupported by the best scientific and commercial data is at least arguably within the interests protected by §1536(a)(2)’s data requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** Review must concern the provision sued upon, not an assumption that every ESA provision protects only species or that every economically affected person may enforce every conservation duty. Requiring the best available scientific and commercial data serves sound decisionmaking and protects affected interests against restrictions unsupported by that information. The Court does not replace agency scientific judgment with independent judicial biology or find the data claim correct. Section 1540(g)(5) preserves other available rights but does not itself establish every APA prerequisite. Dalton’s indivisible base-closure package, deadlines and confined review scheme justified preclusion there; no comparable structure is shown here. Its noncontrolling recommendation-finality theory is not a general bar to agency review.

**Retained APA limits:** Section 702 addresses relief other than money damages and preserves other legal and equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. Any mandatory or injunctive decree must identify by name or title the responsible federal officers and their successors. Section 704 requires final action and no other adequate court remedy. Preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, otherwise final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal does not postpone finality unless an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. This case grants no decree and establishes no unproved exhaustion or review prerequisite.

###### The Biological Opinion is completed action with legal consequences

**Holding and operative rule:** This Biological Opinion is final agency action because it completes the Service’s consultation determination and its incidental-take statement carries legal consequences: compliance with its terms changes prohibited-taking liability under §1536(o)(2). The Bureau’s retained operational discretion does not convert that completed and consequential action into tentative advice.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin requires examination of a completed subordinate agency determination even though a later governmental step remains. The Service has completed its assigned consultation here, and the statement conditions protection from taking liability on compliance with its requirements. Practical influence and announced Bureau compliance support causation, but legal consequences supply the distinct finality ground. The Court does not hold every advisory report, prediction or preliminary analysis final, nor does it decide Franklin’s reserved presidential-status question.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Section 1536(a)(2) requires avoidance of likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat using the best scientific and commercial data available, unless the Committee has granted an exemption under (h); none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must conclude that the action will not violate (a)(2), or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed not to violate it, and conclude that incidental taking will not violate that subsection. For a marine mammal, taking also requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The resulting statement specifies the taking’s impact, reasonable and prudent minimization measures, any required marine-mammal measures, and implementing terms including reporting. Section (o)(2) protects only taking that complies with those terms; it is not unconditional immunity from every ESA duty. The separate exemption-based protection in (o)(1) is not the source of this statement’s effect. No marine-mammal finding is needed for these fish.

The Court reserves the statutory and scientific merits, whether a designation or nondiscretionary duty was actually triggered, the applicable standard of review’s merits application, any properly remaining threshold issue and final relief. The NEPA dismissal remains outside the reviewed questions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: applies personal injury, causation, redressability and litigation-stage proof; its citizen-suit limit remains, and the noncontrolling absent-agency application is not expanded.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts: applies its controlling reviewability of completed subordinate agency action despite a later governmental step; no presidential-status question is decided.
- Dalton v. Specter: its statutory-scheme preclusion holding is distinguished; the four-Justice recommendation-finality theory supplies no binding rule.
- Humane Society of the United States v. Babbitt: its missing causal and remedial link is distinguished by the pleaded Bureau response and continuing water constraint here.
- General Motors Corp. v. Tracy: preserves cognizable personal economic injury without dispensing with the remaining standing requirements.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona: requires an actual continuing controversy and a legally available claim and remedy; no analogous loss of this alleged injury is established.

#### Law After Decision

Economic interests do not disqualify an otherwise injured plaintiff from authorized ESA citizen suits. Each claim must still fit its actual statutory channel; the §1536 consultation claims use the APA and its provision-specific interests and finality requirements. The completed Biological Opinion’s conditional taking consequences establish finality here. These reviewability holdings establish no biological error or entitlement to particular water operations, and leave Article III proof, statutory notice and all retained review limits intact.

### 1997-03-31 — Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission; No. 95-992.

Authority: [Record](../records/Turner_Broadcasting_System_Inc_v_FCC_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The granted question does not decide the reserved classifications

**Rule and authority:** Review of whether the general full-power scheme satisfies its prescribed intermediate standard does not adjudicate the separate content classification of low-power eligibility under §534(h)(2)(B) or the market-modification factor under §534(h)(1)(C)(ii)(III). All nine join Part I and decline to expand this expressly limited review.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner I classified the full-power core but reserved those different provisions. A former remand does not permanently enlarge a subsequent limited appellate grant. Time Warner actually presses both challenges; the Government's identified preservation objection concerns low power. Merits briefing alone does not change the granted question, and neither classification is a necessary subsidiary step in reviewing the core's record support and fit. The 1995 Rules 18.3, 14.1(a) and 24.1(a) preserve that question boundary and permit, rather than require, consideration of otherwise cognizable plain error. The Court declines that discretionary expansion given the distinct operations, injury and relief questions. It adopts no categorical standing bar, abandonment finding or judgment that either criterion is neutral. The district court's grouping of a market factor with low power does not merge them, and viewpoint neutrality or benevolent motive alone does not answer content classification. Their constitutional validity remains outside this judgment.

##### II. The developed record supports preservation of independent broadcast sources

**Rule and authority:** The general full-power carriage duties materially advance the substantial interests in preserving free local broadcasting and a multiplicity of independently available information sources on this developed record. Part II controls through Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer under the existing intermediate standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner I recognizes ordinary cable editorial and programmer speech protection and permits respect for supported legislative predictions while requiring independent judicial scrutiny. The developed evidence links cable bottleneck control, incentives to favor affiliated programming and advertising, drops or refusals and adverse repositioning to diminished broadcast audience, revenue and continued service. Those circumstances support preventive protection of significant local sources without proof that the entire industry will collapse or that every carried station individually faces failure. Contrary growth and profit evidence matters but does not negate every supported local threat. The sufficient justification is preservation and diversity, not an independently sufficient ruling that the Act is tailored against economic predation. Breyer shares that common rationale directly: bottleneck choices can restrict audience options without predatory intent. Congressional prediction is not conclusive, and a desirable broadcasting policy alone would not suffice.

##### III. The present burden and fit satisfy intermediate review

**Rule and authority:** On this developed facial record, the reviewed duties do not burden substantially more cable editorial discretion than necessary to advance those interests; intermediate review requires adequate fit, not the least restrictive conceivable arrangement. Part III has the same five direct joins and permits final affirmance without a further trial here.

**Controlling explanation:** O'Brien and Ward supply the intermediate-fit inquiry. The capacity categories, commercial nonduplication provisions and separate noncommercial rules described above confine the legal obligation; existing voluntary carriage counts without extinguishing editorial freedom to choose differently. The evidence that many systems added no station and that additions occupied 1.2 percent of all channels bears on incremental burden. That statistic does not measure every legally reserved channel, displaced program or foregone choice. Expanding capacity and the remaining editorial space also matter, alongside the burdens on operators, cable programmers and viewers. The district court considered regulated leased access, A/B reception switches, narrower former FCC-type rules and subsidies, and the developed record supports its conclusion that these alternatives would not protect the accepted interests as effectively. Alternatives are relevant without becoming a least-restrictive-means command. The predictive sufficiency question can be decided on this record despite conflicting forecasts; no general exemption of legislative facts from summary-judgment rules follows. The ruling neither preapproves a more burdensome future law nor forecloses a supported as-applied displacement challenge.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC (1994):** Applied the majority full-power classification, intermediate standard and limited predictive-deference principle; did not convert its four-Justice former insufficiency explanation into a holding or decide the reserved classifications.
- **United States v. O'Brien:** Applied the requirement of a substantial interest unrelated to speech suppression and a sufficiently fitted burden.
- **Ward v. Rock Against Racism:** Applied adequate tailoring without demanding the least restrictive imaginable alternative; concrete alternatives remain relevant evidence of fit.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium v. FCC:** Preserved ordinary cable speech protection, separate statutory components and independent justification/means review. Its contextual rationales and separate strict-content position supply no universal replacement standard here.
- **Broadcast spectrum-scarcity doctrine:** Not extended to cable or used to reduce review to rational-basis scrutiny.

#### Law After Decision

The developed record sustains the general full-power core under existing content-neutral intermediate scrutiny through a directly shared preservation/diversity rationale. Congress retains room for supported preventive judgments; courts retain independent review of actual advancement, editorial burdens and fit. No independent economic-predation application, new general cable test, approval of the two reserved classifications or immunity from supported as-applied review is established.

### 1997-05-12 — Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board; Price v. Bossier Parish School Board; Nos. 95-1455 and 95-1508.

Authority: [Record](../records/Reno_v_Bossier_Parish_School_Board_merits_1997-05-12.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Distinct statutory inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5's effect inquiry compares the proposed voting change with the operative benchmark for retrogression; a violation of §2 does not itself establish prohibited §5 effect. A proved §2 violation is also not an independent ground for withholding §5 preclearance, but clearance leaves a separate §2 challenge available.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I. All nine join the effect-benchmark and independent-action propositions. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply six direct votes for the complete nonincorporation rule; Stone-Zsela does not join that additional proposition, and Stevens and Souter oppose it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court keeps the two statutory tasks distinct. *Beer* supplies the retrogression inquiry, and *Lockhart* preserves comparison with the operative voting practice. Section 2 separately asks whether minority voters have equal electoral opportunity. *De Grandy* requires the statutory predicates and totality inquiry and rejects a command to maximize majority-minority districts. The objection that Congress cannot permit clearance of an unlawful plan mistakes clearance for a determination that the plan is lawful for every purpose. Section 2 enforcement remains available, with its own standards and burdens. Neither the stipulated lack of retrogression nor the possibility of two additional majority-Black districts decides §2 liability. The Court makes no original §2 finding and requires no proportional representation.

##### II. Relevant evidence remains relevant to purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** Evidence bearing on racial dilution may also bear on the purpose of a voting change and may not be categorically excluded from §5 purpose review merely because it supports a §2 theory. When such an exclusion may have affected the purpose determination, the ultimate preclearance judgment must be vacated for consideration of the relevant evidence without treating it as conclusive proof or altering the jurisdiction's existing burden to establish absence of prohibited purpose.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by all nine Justices, controls directly.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that evidentiary relevance and substantive liability are different questions. *Arlington Heights* makes impact, historical background, the sequence of events, departures from ordinary procedure or substantive criteria, contemporaneous explanations and possible pretext relevant to intent. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* preserve purpose as an inquiry distinct from nonretrogressive effects. The Board's selection of the Police Jury plan, rejection of the alternative, parish history and disputed explanations therefore require consideration for what they show about its actual purpose. They are not presumptive proof. The contrary argument—that evidence of a permissible result cannot show an impermissible purpose—does not justify excluding evidence capable of supporting the required inference. The district court's apparent categorical separation leaves uncertainty whether it applied the correct inquiry. The Court remands rather than finds purpose itself and creates neither a same-decision defense nor a new causation rule or burden shift.

##### III. Unconstitutional purposeful racial dilution is not confined to intended retrogression

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5 prohibits preclearance of a covered voting change adopted with an unconstitutional purpose to dilute voting opportunity on account of race, even when that purpose does not include worsening the existing benchmark. Nonretrogressive effect therefore does not resolve that purpose inquiry; the court must determine actual purpose, preserving the existing preclearance burden and without treating every consideration of race or rejection of a feasible alternative as unlawful.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, has five direct votes and controls the remand. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not decide this larger scope question.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the independent purpose prohibition its own work. The statutory bar addresses a purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race, not merely a purpose to worsen the existing arrangement. *Beer* defines effect through retrogression; it does not replace the separate purpose language. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* confirm that benign comparative effects do not dispose of unlawful intent. Intentional unconstitutional dilution consequently cannot obtain clearance simply because the resulting plan does not make an already deficient arrangement worse. This holding does not incorporate every §2 results violation. *Shaw* preserves the distinction between purposeful racial dilution and racial consideration in territorial design alone; *De Grandy* rejects automatic liability from the feasibility of additional districts. The Board's actual purpose remains for the factfinder. No same-decision defense, altered allocation of proof, maximization duty or proportional-representation guarantee follows.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Beer v. United States*: Retains the retrogression benchmark for §5 effect; the separate purpose rule does not overrule it.
- *City of Lockhart v. United States*: Applies its comparison with the operative voting practice, without merging §2 liability into §5.
- *Johnson v. De Grandy*: Preserves separate statutory predicates, totality proof and rejection of maximization; feasible alternative districts are evidence, not liability by themselves.
- *Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp.*: Applies its contextual intent evidence; no new same-decision or burden rule is adopted here.
- *City of Pleasant Grove v. United States* and *City of Richmond v. United States*: Apply their separation of purpose and effect to require an independent purpose inquiry.
- *Shaw v. Reno*: Preserves the distinction between purposeful deprivation and racial territorial design alone.
- *Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia* and *Young v. Fordice*: Preserve the designated preclearance process, independent enforcement and distinct coverage, benchmark, purpose and effect tasks; neither previously resolved this purpose-scope question.

#### Law After Decision

Effective May 12, 1997, §5 clearance does not depend on separate §2 compliance, but cannot be granted for a change adopted with unconstitutional purposeful racial vote dilution merely because retrogression was neither intended nor achieved. Dilution evidence must receive its proper evidentiary consideration. The effect benchmark, independent §2 standards, existing allocation of the preclearance burden and designated review channels remain unchanged. Actual liability and purpose in this case remain unresolved.

### 1997-06-02 — Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.; Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136, 90 F.3d 1237.

Authority: [Record](../records/Citizens_for_a_Better_Environment_v_Steel_Co_merits_1997-06-02.md).

#### Holdings

##### 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question

**Rule and authority.** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim; §11046(c)'s enforcement-and-penalty language does not turn every element of statutory liability into a jurisdictional fact. Part I controls by the agreement of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, with O'Connor and Kennedy expressly reserving additional legitimate exceptional threshold sequences.

**Controlling explanation.** Bell v. Hood separates a substantial federal claim from success on that claim; Young applies that distinction to a constitutional defense within established statutory jurisdiction. Section 11046(c) specifies relief in actions brought under subsection (a). Whether that action reaches reports filed late but before suit is a substantial dispute about statutory liability, not authority to render a binding merits judgment while constitutional power is assumed. CBE's reliance on the express jurisdictional wording does not erase this distinction.

Norton involved an appellate-route question when a contemporaneous decision already controlled the merits; Avrech concerned review of a court-martial after an intervening merits decision. Neither supplies a merits answer to this contested EPCRA question. The Court preserves their actual settings and does not claim an exhaustive order among every possible nonmerits threshold. It therefore determines redressability and leaves EPCRA's temporal reach undecided.

##### 2. None of the requested remedies redresses this completed reporting deprivation

**Rule and authority.** Assuming concrete past informational injury and traceability, a plaintiff lacks Article III standing when the requested relief neither repairs that deprivation nor prevents a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. Part II controls by the agreement of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; citizen-suit authorization and litigation expenses alone cannot supply the missing remedial connection.

**Controlling explanation.** Lujan requires likely personal redress, while Church of Scientology permits meaningful partial relief when it remains possible. Brown Group's authorization of representative enforcement preserves that requirement; Bennett likewise requires a supported link between pleaded injury and relief.

The reports were current before this suit. A declaration of their past lateness neither restores timely information nor resolves an alleged ongoing legal consequence. Treasury penalties do not compensate CBE or its members. Deterrence can prevent a threatened injury, but no continuing or imminent reporting violation is alleged. Annual duties alone do not supply that allegation. This is absence of standing at commencement, not cessation of a live controversy during suit.

Periodic facility or records access and future report copies likewise cannot repair the lost timeliness on these allegations. Section 11022(f)'s fire-department inspection right creates no automatic inspection entitlement for CBE. The scope of monitoring in a properly live action remains open.

Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party litigation costs when a final order issues; it creates no independent compensation claim for information-gathering losses. Expenses incurred to litigate cannot manufacture the controversy needed to litigate. A lawful claim compensating independent replacement-information expenses could present a different question; none is supplied. Which prefiling litigation expenses might be recoverable in a proper action is also left open.

The Court does not reject informational injury generally, decide associational requirements, or hold that sovereign-payable penalties never redress threatened injury. No separate compensation claim, withheld report, supported recurrence or amendment entitlement supplies another route here. Under Arizonans, corrective vacatur and dismissal are proper without deciding the statutory merits.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

#### Law After Decision

The Court establishes the qualified jurisdiction-first rule for this disputed federal merits question and the absence of redress through the remedies requested for this completed reporting deprivation. Injury and traceability are assumed, not adjudicated adversely. EPCRA's authorization of citizen actions for fully corrected reporting defaults remains undecided by a controlling majority.

The Company's reporting duties and governmental enforcement remain intact. The statutory concurrence is not an alternative Court holding; neither it nor Stone's contrary conditional construction changes the statute's controlling interpretation.

### 1997-06-23 — Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton; Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

Authority: [Record](../records/Agostini_v_Felton_merits_1997-06-23.md).

#### Holdings

All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding and operative rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Precedent treatment:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding and operative rule:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

##### III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding and operative rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

##### IV. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reinstate that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

Aguilar no longer supplies a categorical constitutional bar to the separately administered, safeguarded public Title I service described in Holding II, and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale has no contrary force at that scope. Ball's completed judgment, Community Education and unaddressed holdings remain undisturbed. Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

For this continuing injunction, the Court's substantive change satisfies Rule 60(b)(5)'s legal-change requirement. Removal of the now-unsupported onsite prohibition must follow through a tailored district-court order. Rufo's other requirements and Rodriguez de Quijas's rule for lower courts remain. Permission to provide the service is distinct from a statutory duty or funding entitlement. Absence of reported misconduct is not proof that every possible defect has been disproved.

The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Rosenberger remains a permissible financing arrangement on its actual controlled vendor-funded publication terms, not merely an access decision, and religious editorial viewpoint there is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Kiryas Joel, Pinette and Chabad retain their actual holdings. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. Zobrest remains an antecedent-law remand without an Establishment Clause merits holding. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.
