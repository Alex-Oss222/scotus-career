## Source verification and bounded legal alternatives

### 108. Doe v. District of Columbia

The controlling current-term addition is Blessing (April 21): identify a particular intended beneficiary, binding obligation and judicially determinate individual entitlement; general administrative duties alone are insufficient. A right, if present, is presumptively enforceable through ?1983 unless Congress forecloses it. Funding oversight alone is not foreclosure. Its state-plan-location statutes concern the Social Security Act, and do not automatically amend CAPTA. Suter's reporting and reasonable-efforts holdings remain provision-specific; its rejection of those rights is material but not a categorical bar.

The source statute is the 1994 official Code, ?5106a, saved at `../entering-law/neutral-a-sources/capta-statute.html` and `.txt`. Its amendment history leaves subsection (b)(2) unchanged from the provision applicable to the 1989 conduct. The post-1992 grant-development and plan provisions must not be silently applied to the conduct. The operative provision requires prompt initiation after a report, then immediate protective steps after a finding of abuse or neglect. These are distinct triggers. No finding of abuse before the injury is established. The then-subsection (c), later (d), waiver machinery may permit limited waivers upon Secretary findings; none for the District is established. The right question concerns the report-triggered prompt-investigation duty, not an unconditional guarantee of protection against all private abuse.

The D.C. Circuit opinion, 93 F.3d 861, is primary lower-court authority available at https://openjurist.org/93/f3d/861. Its federal disposition and local certification agree with the supplied packet. Its competing majority and Rogers analyses identify the live fork: an institutional grant obligation versus a specific, report-triggered entitlement. The complaint's reports and causal account are allegations. The opinion also shows a later reply theory attacking loss of the local tort remedy; because the notice issue is certified, this distinct theory is reserved and cannot be collapsed into the presented investigative-liberty theory. The supplied review does not decide the certified question.

Path A: affirm federal dismissal because the statutory instruction requires a responsive protective system without giving this child an individual federal entitlement to a particular investigative response. Its strongest objection is the text's discrete report trigger and mandatory prompt initiation, which is more specific than aggregate staffing adequacy. A valid affirmance must address that language and cannot rely solely on plan location or funding withdrawal.

Path B: recognize the limited ?1983 right to prompt initiation of an appropriate investigation after a report identifying the child, reverse only the statutory dismissal and remand for defendant attribution, breach, causation, immunity and relief. Its strongest objection is that a binding governmental duty is not always an individual right and the investigative content remains discretionary. A valid version does not promise removal, a fixed universal deadline or successful prevention of harm.

The independent due-process inquiry concerns an identifiable substantive liberty or property entitlement, not compliance with procedures as such. Sandin preserves substantive constraints but rejects mandatory procedural words alone. DeShaney leaves the noncustodial private-violence boundary intact; Pinder and Uhlrig preserve separate affirmative-deprivation questions without converting this alleged omission into one. Either statutory path can coexist with affirmance of the investigative-procedure due-process dismissal. No state custody preceded these injuries. Local tort-law entitlement and any later reply theory remain separate.

### 109. Bracy v. Gramley

Rule 6 and Harris v. Nelson, 394 U.S. 286, supply the discovery source and good-cause inquiry. Specific allegations must give reason to believe developed facts could establish entitlement; they need not already prove the claim. Tumey, Ward and Aetna preserve the due-process requirement of a neutral judge. Liteky's ?455(a) appearance standard does not become the constitutional habeas standard. Armstrong supplies no Rule 6 limitation, automatic comparator requirement or established corruption exception to ordinary proof.

The primary lower opinion is 81 F.3d 684, https://openjurist.org/81/f3d/684, April 12, 1996. It identifies the rejected request to investigate whether Maloney's corruption produced bias in the petitioner's trial. The original packet supplies the independently extracted trial-specific allegations: counsel's association, short preparation and the trial's relation to fixed cases. Those remain allegations, and the finding of corruption elsewhere remains distinct from proof of bias here. No independent review of the original federal proffer is claimed.

Path A: reverse the discovery denial and remand for focused Rule 6 discovery because corroborated corruption plus these trial links supplies good cause; retain district-court control over scope and leave ultimate bias, evidentiary hearing and habeas relief open. The strongest objection is that trial outcomes or aggressive scheduling might have innocent explanations and the theory could become unbounded inquiry into every case.

Path B: affirm for insufficient case-specific connection, preserving good-cause discretion. The strongest objection is that requiring completed proof of concealed bias before the discovery that could expose it converts the preliminary threshold into the merits burden. The choice must turn on the cumulative concrete allegations, not automatic guilt or innocence of the trial judge in this case.

### 110. Gilbert v. Homar

Correct the packet's lower-decision date to July 18, 1996, as shown in the primary opinion at https://openjurist.org/89/f3d/1009 (89 F.3d 1009). The June 9 Supreme Court event and March 24 argument remain unchanged. The complaint and undisputed sequence establish an immediate unpaid suspension after a felony arrest, charges dismissed September 1, first opportunity to respond September 18, and later demotion. The lower opinion records a September 11 police report containing disputed attributed admissions; no admission by Homar is found here. The later retroactive groundskeeper pay does not by itself cure an earlier procedural violation.

Mathews and Loudermill supply the governing private-interest/error-risk/government-interest inquiry. Stevens's unanimous FDIC v. Mallen, 486 U.S. 230 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/486/230), permits immediate provisional suspension supported by an independent criminal accusation and requires justification of the ensuing delay. Its precise banking statute and ongoing indictment differ from this police arrest followed by dismissal. Barry v. Barchi requires prompt effective review after justified initial action. Young v. Harper confirms the distinction between permissible provisional action and adequate process for a continuing deprivation; its parole-specific procedures are not imported.

Path A: reject a categorical pre-suspension-hearing rule; a serious independent arrest can provide enough assurance for immediate unpaid suspension from sensitive police duties under Mathews, provided a prompt adequate opportunity follows. Remand for the justification and adequacy of continued deprivation, particularly after dismissal of charges. Strongest objection: duty removal could protect the University while pay continued, and an arrest does not adjudicate employment misconduct.

Path B: sustain a prehearing-pay requirement on this record while allowing immediate removal, because the additional wage loss lacks the urgency supporting removal. Strongest objection: Mathews is a contextual balance and Mallen permits temporary livelihood deprivation without first making the employer pay throughout an urgent investigation. Neither path may declare the entire 23-day period automatically constitutional, impose an unexplained fixed deadline, award damages, decide immunity, or uphold the final demotion.

### 111. Johnson v. Fankell

Control supplied an objective official-reporter correction: the argument was February 26, 1997, not March 24. June 9 remains the event date. This receipt does not independently claim a new original-source reading of that metadata. The neutral packet establishes Idaho's nondiscriminatory final-judgment rule and available later review; no different treatment of comparable state immunity is proved.

Johnson v. Jones, Swint and Behrens locate immediate federal appeals in ?1291 and its collateral-order rules, preserving substantive immunity while rejecting unlimited review. Howlett v. Rose, 496 U.S. 356 (Stevens for a unanimous Court; https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/496/356), forbids state immunity and jurisdiction rules that nullify or discriminate against federal rights. Felder v. Casey concerns a state condition incompatible with the federal claim. Neither source makes every federal procedural incident binding on state appellate courts.

Path A: affirm Idaho's dismissal of the immediate appeal; ?1983 and qualified immunity require enforcement of the substantive defense but do not transplant ?1291. Ordinary final review remains available. Strongest objection: immunity from trial loses some benefit when appellate error cannot be corrected until afterward. The answer must address that cost without relabeling immunity a mere damages defense.

Path B: require immediate state review as indispensable to immunity from trial. Strongest objection: it makes the substantive defense generate appellate jurisdiction independent of the statute that supplies that jurisdiction in federal court, and would strain the Court's own bounded federal-appeal cases. A discriminatory or deliberately destructive state procedure is reserved, not established. The merits of immunity and the underlying ?1983 claim are outside this disposition.
