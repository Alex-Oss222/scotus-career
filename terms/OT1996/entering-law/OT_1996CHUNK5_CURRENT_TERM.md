### Standing Practices

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the opening Standing State and the OT1993 Term Continuity Note. Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.

### 1996-12-09 — Humane Society v. Babbitt; D.C. Circuit No. 93-5339; District of Columbia No. 92-cv-0952; no Supreme Court docket assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Humane_Society_v_Babbitt_merits_1996-12-09.md).

#### Holdings

#### Independent failure of traceability

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming sufficient personal aesthetic injury from Lota's absence, the Society has not shown that the challenged August 1991 ESA certificate amendment caused the lost zoo access. The independent earlier donation produced that loss, and this record establishes no mechanism connecting the later amendment to its continuation.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient jurisdictional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires support for the plaintiff's own injury and its connection to the challenged action at the litigation stage presented. A person can have a concrete aesthetic interest in observing a particular animal; species-wide depletion, ownership and a scientific profession are not prerequisites. The Court nevertheless assumes actual injury here rather than finding that the hypothetical regular observer described in the question exists in the evidence. The zoo's donation preceded the amendment, which governed later uses by Hawthorn. The Society abandoned its separate appeal concerning the original transfer and did not challenge the CITES certificate. Neither action can be restored to the case by calling this a challenge to continued absence. Government permission can be causally effective through supported, predictable private conduct; a government command is not invariably necessary. But this record supplies no such link between the later amendment and the lost Milwaukee observation. Sweet Home's treatment of actual injury to individual animals under a habitat regulation does not supply the missing human standing link for this different transportation permission.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the actual members' affidavits establish injury in fact. It adopts no categorical rule against third-party causation, no purchased-ticket requirement, and no general prohibition on standing to observe one animal.

#### Independent failure of likely beneficial relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Society also fails independently to show that suspending the challenged certificate or declaring the transportation interpretation unlawful would likely benefit a member's impaired observation. Complete restoration is unnecessary, but a regulatory change must offer some likely concrete improvement to the member's interest.

**Authority:** Part II of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices independently adopt this remedial standing ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Invalidating the amendment would leave Hawthorn able to retain Lota at its Illinois premises. It would not annul the donation, compel the zoo to receive her, or make renewed Milwaukee access substantially likely. No supported alternative benefit to an established observer appears in the affidavits or requested relief. Church of Scientology permits meaningful partial relief even when full restoration is impossible, but changing another person's legal options is not beneficial relief to a member by definition. Even with favorable reasonable inferences from the existing materials and assumed injury, the missing connection remains. The Winikoff letters were considered because the objection to their unsworn form was not preserved; considering them does not supply absent facts. The Owens letter addresses the Society's access, not its authors' personal observation. A remand to create a new return mechanism or observer would not correct an established error in this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical conclusion is reached about remedies in another properly supported action. The Court orders no return, certificate cancellation, damages or other substantive relief.

#### Procedural and representative claims retain the concrete-interest connection

**Holding and operative rule:** An asserted ESA procedure does not cure the missing connection between this challenged amendment and a represented member's concrete interest. Assuming that the asserted conservation-permit procedure exists, a claimant need not prove that proper procedure certainly changes the ultimate agency decision, but must still show the threatened personal interest that the procedure protects; representative litigation does not dispense with that constitutional requirement.

**Authority:** Part III of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices reject these proposed ways around the causal and remedial defects.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan preserves procedural protection for threatened concrete interests without requiring certainty of a different final result. It does not authorize general judicial supervision based on an interest in lawful conservation administration. No individual entitlement to Lota's Milwaukee residence or separate informational right is established here. Assuming the asserted section 1539 procedure for this analysis does not convert that section's permission into a found statutory command. Brown Group preserves concrete injury, traceability and redressability for represented members; its displacement of Hunt's prudential participation limit through WARN's express authorization supplies no ESA exemption. The procedure asserted in this case does not connect the later amendment to the completed transfer or establish likely benefit to the zoo observer. The Court therefore need not construe the commercial-activity provision or the asserted permit duty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether section 1539 actually imposes the claimed procedure, the commercial-activity interpretation, the unchallenged CITES certificate, the abandoned transfer claim and Hawthorn's Chaney objection remain undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife (June 12, 1992): applies its controlling elements, stage-specific evidence and concrete-interest/procedural-right rules while preserving reasonable inferences and cognizable personal aesthetic use. Its three-Justice absent-agency discussion is not made controlling.
- Church of Scientology of California v. United States (November 16, 1992): applies the sufficiency of meaningful partial relief; distinguishes a constraint on a third party that supplies no demonstrated benefit to the claimant.
- United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group (May 13, 1996): preserves represented members' constitutional standing requirements; its WARN-specific displacement of Hunt's prudential participation condition is not extended to an ESA exemption.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333: retains the need for a member with constitutional standing.
- Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter (June 29, 1995): its actual-injury and statutory-source distinctions remain; its habitat-harm holding and separate deference rationale do not decide transportation permission or human standing here.

#### Law After Decision

The Court applies constitutional causation, redressability and procedural-standing rules to this certificate challenge. Actual injury is assumed and unresolved; the discussion of observing a particular animal does not find an injured member or independently decide the suit. No ESA commercial-activity or permit-procedure construction is adopted. Jurisdictional dismissal does not decide a genuinely different claim supported by adequate standing facts and does not reopen the abandoned transfer count.

### 1997-01-06 — DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association; Fifth Circuit No. 93-8700, 51 F.3d 591; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

Authority: [Record](../records/DeAngelis_v_El_Paso_Association_merits_1997-01-06.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. The credited course of publication does not meet Title VII's objective threshold

**Holding and operative rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when the complete circumstances establish conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as abusive; no physical advance, superior-subordinate relationship, psychological injury or measurable work loss is indispensable. Crediting this verdict record and favorable reasonable inferences, the supported course of newsletter commentary falls below that objective threshold, so the Association's hostile-environment judgment is affirmed.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; six direct votes support this rule and application. Stone-Zsela's judgment vote supplies no additional statutory join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Harris's all-circumstances inquiry, which preserves an objective boundary while rejecting injury and productivity prerequisites. Meritor supplies the requirement that discriminatory harassment alter working conditions. The ten reproduced columns must be considered together, including general comments about women, the references associated with DeAngelis, extensive workplace circulation, humiliation and alleged interference with command. General comments are not excluded merely because they do not name her.

The jury's favorable inferences receive their proper force. Even on that account, the irregular sequence, degree, limited direct targeting, editorial setting and actual workplace responses establish offensive intermittent commentary rather than the required abusive alteration. Broad circulation and repeated ridicule matter, but do not eliminate that statutory distinction. Good ratings, upheld discipline and supportive responses are contextual evidence, not indispensable defenses. Neither an invitation to respond nor the membership's protection of the author's anonymity creates consent or a numerical safe harbor.

The lower opinion's suggestions that equal opportunity must be destroyed or work performance palpably deteriorate are disapproved. Citation to Harris alone would not cure those suggestions. The Court instead independently applies the correct standard and sustains the judgment on that ground. It neither disbelieves DeAngelis's testimony nor requires physical threats, advances or supervisory harassment. Because the statutory ground is sufficient, the Court does not decide the preserved First Amendment defense. It adopts no newsletter immunity and no general rule that offensive expression cannot constitute harassment.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris is applied without adding a prerequisite; Meritor's alteration-of-conditions boundary remains controlling. No constitutional speech precedent is extended by this holding.

#### II. The complaint-related publications establish no actionable retaliatory consequence here

**Holding and operative rule:** Protected activity and hostile commentary do not alone establish Title VII retaliation without a cognizable adverse consequence. The mocking EEOC reference, routine minutes reporting the complaint and announced intention to sue if the action proved groundless establish no denial of an employment or Association benefit, concrete imposed penalty or comparably supported material disadvantage on this record; judgment for the Association is therefore affirmed.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes opposition to a discrimination complaint from a proved retaliatory consequence. The publications reveal disparagement and contemplated conditional litigation, but do not establish lost pay, promotion, assignment, authority, membership, representation or a comparable imposed disadvantage caused by the complaint. No actual retaliatory lawsuit or unreported sanction is found. The same record can raise distinct hostile-environment and retaliation questions; disagreement on the former does not supply the missing consequence for the latter.

This record-specific conclusion does not confine all actionable retaliation to ultimate employment decisions. It does not hold that threats, harassment or public shaming can never establish a materially adverse consequence on stronger facts, and it decides no comprehensive standard for every retaliation claim. The Court does not infer failure from missing research materials or convert the opportunity to answer the newsletter into a condition of protection.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's distinction between offensive treatment and legally sufficient discrimination is preserved at its hostile-environment scope; its injury rule is not converted into a comprehensive retaliation test.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harris v. Forklift Systems: applies the objective and subjective totality inquiry and rejects mandatory work-loss, injury, advance and hierarchy requirements; the present application adds no such requirement.
- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson: applies the requirement of discriminatory alteration of employment conditions; extreme examples remain illustrations.
- Wisconsin v. Mitchell, Hurley and R. A. V.: their existing holdings remain intact. The Court does not reach the expressive defense, adopt R. A. V.'s separate content-selection rationale, or decide a general immunity for association publications.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 6, 1997, the particular newsletter record is insufficient under Harris even with testimony and reasonable verdict-favorable inferences credited. Title VII retains its contextual severe-or-pervasive test, with no numerical publication threshold or mandatory tangible injury. Retaliation fails here for lack of a supported material consequence; the decision leaves broader retaliation categories open. No controlling First Amendment rule is added. Stone-Zsela's expressive ground and Stevens and Ginsburg's proposed hostile-environment remand remain noncontrolling.

### 1997-01-13 — Walmer v. Department of Defense; Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377, 52 F.3d 851; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

Authority: [Record](../records/Walmer_v_Department_of_Defense_merits_1997-01-13.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Ordinary rationality sustains this preliminary denial on the stipulated conduct record

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged conduct-based military personnel rule receives ordinary rational-basis review, which permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable predictions and imperfect fit while requiring a relationship to the actual disadvantage and forbidding bare harm or irrational prejudice. On this preliminary record of admitted homosexual acts and stipulated ineligibility for the retention exceptions, discipline, morale and workable military service relationships supply a permissible predictive relationship, so denial of equal-protection preliminary relief is affirmed without a final ruling on another or materially developed record.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins to this ground and the preliminary disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits conceivable legitimate reasons without formal findings or empirical certainty. Heller requires a relation to the specific disadvantage and also preserves relevant classwide generalizations. Romer applies those limits to a blanket civil disability; its ordinary-review rule neither assigns an orientation scrutiny tier nor makes every conduct-based personnel classification invalid. The Court examines this military rule's connection to discipline, morale and workable service relationships instead of accepting the military label as an answer.

The stipulated acts and unavailable retention exceptions define this application. Reasonable military predictions may address service relationships classwide without proof that this officer's performance has already impaired a unit. Goldman and Rostker preserve substantial respect for professional military judgments while leaving constitutional review intact. A more individualized policy is not constitutionally required merely because it is possible. Bare private hostility would not be a legitimate objective, but the identified personnel relationship supplies a distinct predictive ground here.

Rich's fraudulent-entry facts are not this record, and its suggestion about satisfying a hypothetical stricter standard is unnecessary. The Court sustains the preliminary denial through direct ordinary-review analysis, not factual identity with Rich or automatic foreclosure by it. Bowers's specified-conduct holding does not itself decide the equal-protection tier or application. Kelley and Virginia concern sex classifications and supply no transferred burden here.

Walmer's preliminary showing does not establish the requisite merits basis for relief under the inquiry applied below. The Court finds none of the other three equitable factors satisfied merely because the court of appeals assumed them. It orders no discharge and decides neither an orientation-only inference, later statements legislation nor another claimant's exception eligibility.

**Precedent treatment:** Beach, both relevant Heller propositions and Romer are applied at their actual ordinary-review scope; Goldman and Rostker supply deference without immunity. Bowers, Kelley and Virginia are distinguished at the questions they decided.

#### II. The attainder prohibition does not extend to this executive military regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** The Bill of Attainder Clause restrains legislative punishment, and the challenged executive Army regulation does not satisfy that legislative-action predicate. The attainder ground therefore fails without deciding whether the separation would constitute punishment if the required legislative predicate existed; other constitutional, statutory and administrative restraints on executive action remain distinct.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books identify legislative action as an antecedent condition distinct from identification and punishment. Walmer does not contend that this measure is legislation; she asks to extend the prohibition to executive military regulation. The constitutional allocation does not support that extension. Other sources of law may constrain executive abuse without changing the Clause's institutional target.

The district court also discussed punishment, but the court of appeals did not reach it and the Court has no need to do so. An honorable-separation label is therefore not held conclusively nonpunitive, and no hypothetical legislative separation measure is adjudicated. The abandoned APA theory is not revived; neither an independent due-process claim nor an unpresented statutory challenge is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books supply and preserve the legislative-action prerequisite. Their distinct punishment inquiries are neither displaced nor answered for this separation.

#### Precedent Treatment

- FCC v. Beach Communications: applies conceivable legitimate grounds and tolerance for imperfect fit without formal findings, subject to the prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice.
- Heller v. Doe: preserves the need for a relationship to the actual disadvantage and the separate allowance for relevant family-information generalizations; no heightened scrutiny follows from either holding.
- Romer v. Evans: applies ordinary rational-basis limits and distinguishes its blanket withdrawal and foreclosure of civil protection from this service-specific conduct rule. It is neither overruled nor converted into a Government actual-objective burden.
- Goldman v. Weinberger and Rostker v. Goldberg: preserve substantial military deference without constitutional immunity; an established right's separate scrutiny rule is not imported into this ordinary equality inquiry.
- Bowers v. Hardwick: remains effective at its specified-conduct question and does not automatically settle this equal-protection tier or application.
- Kelley and United States v. Virginia: retain their sex-classification requirements; their burdens are not transferred to a different classification by analogy alone.
- Nixon v. Administrator of General Services, Selective Service System v. Minnesota Public Interest Research Group, and Ambassador Books: preserve legislative action as necessary to an attainder claim, separately from punishment. No new ruling on the punishment character of separation is entered.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 13, 1997, ordinary rational-basis review governs this preliminary, stipulated-conduct challenge to the earlier Army regulation, and the identified military personnel relationship sustains denial of interim relief. The Court creates no heightened sexual-orientation tier, Government actual-purpose proof burden, individualized-impairment prerequisite, least-restrictive-means rule or constitutional right to military service. Reasonable military judgments remain reviewable, and bare hostility remains insufficient. The executive regulation cannot be challenged as a bill of attainder on the presented extension theory, but other legal restraints are not displaced. The retention exceptions remain part of the regulation; their complete criteria, validity and application to another claimant are not adjudicated.

### 1997-01-14 — Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, No. 95-928.

Authority: [Record](../records/Atherton_v_FDIC_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Review of the source-of-law argument

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court may consider the argument that no federal common-law duty exists because it concerns the same restored federal claims and is included within the presented dispute over §1821(k)'s displacement or preservation of those claims. Both sides have addressed that legal question on the existing record; consideration introduces no separate claim, additional factual finding or different requested relief.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that the merits should be reached but does not join this procedural explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must distinguish a supporting legal argument from a separate question. Yee permits new arguments for a preserved claim but retains the independent limit imposed by the question presented. United States National Bank permits consideration of necessary governing law after the parties have a fair opportunity to address it. Lebron permits, without requiring, consideration of an included legal argument on an established record. Here the existence of the asserted duty is part of deciding what federal claim survives the statute. Izumi is different: its separate adverse intervention judgment had to be displaced before the presented question could be reached. Merits briefing alone does not enlarge the grant, and the withdrawal of the state claims remains a separate procedural matter. This confined exercise of review creates no universal exception for antecedent questions.

**Precedent treatment:** Yee's argument/question distinction, United States National Bank's governing-law authority and Lebron's discretionary included-argument rule are applied within their limits; Izumi's separate-judgment limit is distinguished and preserved.

#### II. State law supplies the ordinary corporate duty

**Holding and operative rule:** State law supplies the ordinary standard of care for these acquired corporate-governance claims against officers and directors of a federally chartered savings association unless enacted federal law or a specifically demonstrated significant conflict with an identified federal policy displaces it. Federal chartering, insurance, receivership recovery and general administrative uniformity do not establish that conflict here.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** O'Melveny applies Erie's rejection of general federal common law and Wallis's requirement of a particular significant conflict with federal policy. Its independently sufficient pre-FIRREA ground does not depend on applying the later statute to earlier conduct. The asserted duties concern lending decisions, fiduciary conduct and corporate governance, subjects ordinarily governed by state law. The receiver identifies no charter command or operational obligation requiring a different rule. Kimbell Foods distinguishes rights created by federal programs and rejects automatic national uniformity even there. A desire to increase recoveries cannot supply the missing conflict. New Jersey law therefore supplies the ordinary duty for any properly available state theory, subject to federal displacement actually established. The Court neither selects New Jersey's precise standard or business-judgment rule nor decides breach, causation, defenses or liability. Congress or a valid federal regulation may prescribe a specific duty; federal enforcement of distinct statutory obligations is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** O'Melveny's acquired-claim and specific-conflict rules are applied to these corporate duties; Erie and Wallis retain their general-law and conflict limits; Boyle's exceptional-displacement requirements remain available; Kimbell Foods supplies no automatic uniform federal rule.

#### III. Section 1821(k) establishes a federal minimum and preserves other rights

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1821(k), when applicable, permits personal monetary liability of a director or officer of an insured depository institution, whether federally or state chartered, for gross negligence, similar conduct or conduct demonstrating a greater disregard of duty, including intentional tortious conduct, with those culpability terms defined under applicable state law; the action must be by, on behalf of, or at the request or direction of the Corporation, prosecuted wholly or partly for its benefit, with the Corporation acting as receiver or conservator, on a claim purchased from, assigned by or otherwise conveyed by such a receiver or conservator, or on a claim purchased from, assigned by or otherwise conveyed wholly or partly by an insured institution or its affiliate in connection with §1823 assistance. That authorization defeats a more protective state liability shield but preserves rights actually available under other applicable law, including a state duty permitting recovery for less culpable conduct: gross negligence is a federal floor, not a ceiling.

**Authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins to the textual construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted provision describes insured institutions without excluding federal charters. Its final sentence preserves other applicable rights; restricting that sentence to other provisions of the same federal enactment would deny its ordinary breadth. State law imposing an ordinary-negligence duty can therefore remain effective. Preservation of other law does not manufacture a federal common-law action or restore a withdrawn pleading. The section's authorization, the three routes by which the Corporation acts, the state-law definitions of culpability and the savings sentence each retain their separate work. Permission to impose liability does not establish breach, causation, the absence of defenses or an amount due. The text resolves the construction without reliance on legislative history. O'Melveny's rejection of an additional receiver preference supplies the source-of-law boundary; it did not previously decide this provision's minimum or savings rule. The section's temporal application is unnecessary to resolve the independently defective federal-common-law premise and remains open.

**Precedent treatment:** O'Melveny's conditional FIRREA analysis is preserved without converting it into a retroactivity decision; its source-of-law rule supplies no additional federal action. Landgraf's provision-specific temporal inquiry remains available when application to earlier conduct must actually be decided.

#### IV. Vacatur requires further proceedings under the correct sources

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit judgment is vacated because it restored the asserted general federal-common-law theories and excluded federally chartered institutions from §1821(k). On remand the lower courts must apply the state-law starting point and the federal provision at its lawful scope, resolve properly available claims and defenses, and determine any liability and relief without treating this decision as reinstatement of withdrawn state claims.

**Authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate court reserved the content of its proposed federal standard, including ordinary versus gross negligence. Its judgment is not an adjudication of ordinary-negligence liability. The statutory pleading remains for ordinary adjudication. State claims were treated as withdrawn below; the effect of that treatment, permissible amendment and any properly available state theory remain for the lower courts. Neither the savings sentence nor correction of the federal source resurrects those claims. O'Melveny distinguishes correction of an erroneous federal rule from automatic reinstatement of an earlier judgment. Its same distinction supports this bounded remand. The Court decides neither the precise New Jersey standard nor FIRREA's temporal application, and awards no damages.

**Precedent treatment:** O'Melveny's separation of the governing-law correction from final relief is applied; no liability or temporal rule is added by the mandate.

#### Precedent Treatment

- O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: its state-law starting point, specific-conflict requirement and separation of source correction from ultimate relief govern; the §1821(k) floor and savings construction is decided here. Its conditional FIRREA analysis remains conditional.
- Erie Railroad v. Tompkins: the absence of general federal common law remains the starting point.
- Wallis v. Pan American Petroleum Corp. and Boyle v. United Technologies Corp.: exceptional displacement still requires the identified federal interest and significant conflict; no such conflict is established here.
- United States v. Kimbell Foods, Inc.: federal program rights are distinguished, and general uniformity alone remains insufficient.
- Yee v. City of Escondido: supporting arguments and separate questions remain distinct.
- United States National Bank of Oregon v. Independent Insurance Agents: necessary governing law may be considered after a fair opportunity to address it.
- Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.: the Court applies its discretionary included-argument rule without erasing the question-presented limit.
- Izumi Seimitsu v. U.S. Philips Corp.: the separate intervention judgment there distinguishes it from this included source argument.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products: its provision-specific temporal inquiry remains unchanged; retrospective application is not decided.

#### Law After Decision

The ordinary duty rules for these federally chartered institutions remain state supplied unless valid federal displacement is shown. Section 1821(k) applies to insured institutions of either charter and preserves stricter applicable state duties while establishing its own minimum authorization. Federal chartering and receivership do not alone create a general federal negligence action. The content and procedural availability of New Jersey claims remain open.

### 1997-01-14 — Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc., No. 95-259; Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc., No. 95-779.

Authority: [Record](../records/Walters_v_Metropolitan_Educational_Enterprises_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Count genuine employment relationships on each working day

**Holding and operative rule:** For Title VII's requirement of fifteen or more employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year, count an individual on every working day during an existing employment relationship, whether or not that person worked or was paid that day. Payroll ordinarily evidences that relationship but is not conclusive: a person not yet hired, already separated, an independent contractor or within an express statutory exclusion does not become an employee merely by appearing on a list.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute counts employees the employer has, not daily units of compensated labor. Employment continues across ordinary unpaid days, part-time schedules and leave. The daily requirement still does real work: an employee must have the relationship on each pertinent working day, so days before hiring or after separation do not count. Metropolitan's alternative counted paid leave; it is therefore a compensation rule, not simply an actual-work rule. But compensation on a particular day does not follow from Congress's choice of the employment relationship. Darden supplies the common-law agency approach to a circular employee definition, considering all relevant incidents without a single decisive factor. Town & Country's compatible-service and actual-control analysis supports attention to the real relationship within each statute's terms. Neither case had already decided Title VII's daily-counting language. Payroll offers ordinary administrable evidence without displacing legal status. The Court adopts no exclusion or worker-status dispute absent from this stipulated record, and does not decide whether numerosity is jurisdictional.

**Qualifications:** The industry-affecting-commerce requirement and employer-agent language remain. Employer exclusions remain for the United States, wholly United States-owned corporations, Indian tribes, District of Columbia departments or agencies subject by statute to competitive-service procedures, and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations exempt under §501(c). The first-year-after-March-24-1972 transition for employers with fewer than twenty-five employees does not govern this period. The separate employee exclusions remain for elected state or local public officers, their chosen personal staff, policymaking appointees and immediate advisers concerning the office's constitutional or legal powers; those exclusions do not cover employees subject to applicable state or local civil-service laws. **Precedent treatment:** Darden's genuine-relationship framework is applied by analogy at its common-law scope; Town & Country's attention to compatible employment and enacted exclusions is preserved without transplanting NLRA exclusions into Title VII.

#### II. The stipulated minimum satisfies the 1990 numerical requirement

**Holding and operative rule:** Under relationship counting, the stipulated 1990 record establishes at least thirty-eight weeks in which Metropolitan had the required employees on each working day, exceeding the twenty-week requirement. That minimum resolves the 1990 numerical objection to the retaliatory-discharge claim without a remand to recompute an exact total or litigate an unsupported worker-status dispute.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The pertinent years for the retaliatory-discharge claim are 1990 and 1989. The stipulated failure to reach the threshold in 1989 remains, but success in 1990 suffices. The original count identified forty-seven weekly payroll counts without ensuring presence on every working day in each week. Even excluding all nine weeks potentially affected by midweek employment changes leaves forty-seven minus nine, or thirty-eight, qualifying weeks. No larger exact total is needed, and the Court does not decide whether an excluded week might also qualify. The separate nine-week figure under the rejected compensation method describes a different calculation; the record does not identify those as the same weeks. Metropolitan disputed the relationship criterion rather than the use of payroll evidence to establish the stipulated relationships. The legal possibility that payroll can be rebutted elsewhere therefore supplies no present factual contest. This application does not classify every possible payroll entry or establish continuous threshold employment throughout all fifty-two weeks.

**Precedent treatment:** Darden and Town & Country preserve the distinction between legal status and an evidentiary list; neither requires relitigation of these stipulated relationships.

#### III. The unpresented promotion-year issue receives no relief

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory current-or-preceding-year formula identifies 1988 and 1989 for the September 1989 promotion claim. The District Court instead used 1989 and 1990. That mismatch is a separate claim-specific issue; the supported omission from both petition and merits brief is an actual filing omission, not an inference from unavailable papers. Yee preserves the independent question-presented limit. Williams permits review of a question actually passed upon in its particular circumstances; the district court's choice of years does not establish such appellate consideration. Zafiro shows that declining review may leave a judgment undisturbed without a merits rejection or intentional-waiver finding. United States National Bank and Lebron permit consideration of included legal questions on established records, but do not require it or authorize automatic restoration of a claim after the Court declines review. The retaliation counting question can be answered without a 1988 finding. The Court neither treats the missing count as evidence against Walters nor uses its absence to reopen the promotion judgment. It does not hold that 1990 is a proper year for that claim, that the promotion was lawful or that review is jurisdictionally barred.

**Precedent treatment:** Yee and Zafiro support bounded nonreview and its undisturbed-judgment consequence; Williams, United States National Bank and Lebron retain their permissive, circumstance-specific scope and supply no mandatory promotion remand.

**Holding and operative rule:** The compensation-based judgment is reversed within the retaliatory-discharge dispute in the consolidated cases, and that dispute is remanded for unresolved liability, genuinely remaining defenses and appropriate relief under the correct counting rule. The 1990 numerical objection is resolved, while the promotion dismissal remains undisturbed and this mandate supplies no authority to reopen its calendar years, preservation or unknown 1988 count.

**Authority:** Part IV of Souter's opinion has nine direct joins concerning reversal and further retaliation proceedings; its promotion-disposition explanation has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part III, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only to that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Meeting the statutory count establishes no retaliation, discrimination, causation, damages or ultimate liability. Those questions remain for ordinary adjudication. Conversely, the established minimum requires no remand merely to determine whether thirty-eight exceeds twenty. The mandate must respect the claim-specific limit stated in Part III: nonreview leaves an existing dismissal in place without a new affirmance of its substantive correctness. The Court expresses no view about an independently available procedure outside this mandate. The lower courts' description of dismissal as jurisdictional remains procedural history, not a new Supreme Court classification of numerosity.

**Precedent treatment:** Zafiro's distinction between nonreview and merits adjudication governs the promotion limit; Darden and Town & Country provide no automatic liability or remedial award from employee classification.

### 1997-01-14 - Castano v. American Tobacco Co.; Fifth Circuit No. 95-30725; 84 F.3d 734; interlocutory appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b).

Authority: [Record](../records/Castano_v_American_Tobacco_Co_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. All four holdings command nine votes.

##### I. Certification requires a demonstrated legal and evidentiary basis

**Holding and operative rule.** Before certifying a Rule 23(b)(3) class, a court must determine from the actual claims, governing law and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and class adjudication is superior to the available alternatives, together with satisfaction of Rule 23(a). Conditional certification does not permit postponing that showing, and a claim's novelty neither dispenses with rigorous analysis nor categorically bars certification until individual verdicts exist.

**Controlling explanation.** Falcon requires rigorous examination of the Rule's prerequisites. Eisen forbids awarding or denying certification merely because the court predicts success on the merits; it does not make the inquiry pleadings-only when the substantive elements determine whether a common trial can work. The proponents must identify what the class proceeding would decide and how that work connects to remaining claims and defenses. An untested liability theory makes that explanation especially necessary, but the Rule contains no tort-maturation requirement. Common evidence of manufacturer conduct cannot itself establish that the resulting questions predominate over the unresolved legal and individual work. Administrative convenience cannot replace a prerequisite. Matsushita preserves constitutionally adequate representation as distinct from notice or opt-out rights, and Richards preserves the lawful basis needed to bind absent persons; neither supplies a finding of actual adequacy or conflict in this case. A renewed proposal must satisfy the representation requirements as well as predominance and superiority.

##### II. The present core-liability class cannot stand

**Holding and operative rule.** This nationwide core-liability certification fails Rule 23(b)(3) because the court deferred the material state-law inquiry and did not show how common issues, individual addiction, reliance, causation and defenses would fit into lawful, manageable adjudication. Differences among state laws do not automatically prohibit every multistate class, but proponents must demonstrate materially common answers or workable groupings rather than assume uniformity or rely on subclass labels.

**Controlling explanation.** Shutts prevents applying a single State's law to materially disconnected claims merely for convenience; it does not select the governing law here. The pleaded fraud, misrepresentation, negligence, warranty and consumer-protection claims require examination of their actual duties, defenses and burdens. The District Court certified core conduct while excluding injury, causation, reliance, defenses and individual compensatory damages. Without explaining how the common answers would materially resolve the legally varied claims, the court could not establish predominance or make a grounded superiority comparison. The proponents' strongest point is that common manufacturer evidence is substantial and individual litigation expensive. But an imagined future trial structure cannot supply the missing showing for the order entered. Workable legal groupings or genuinely separable common issues may matter on a different proposal. The Court requires a credible account of claims and proof, not a universal separate document bearing the title trial plan. It does not decide the full relationship between Rule 23(b)(3) and (c)(4), including whether issue certification may ever proceed when an entire cause of action fails an action-wide predominance inquiry.

##### III. The punitive component and superiority finding also fail

**Holding and operative rule.** The certified punitive-ratio component fails because its state-law predicates, burdens, relation to liability and coordination with individual proceedings were not demonstrated; those same gaps defeat the present superiority finding. Settlement pressure from a procedure that bypasses defenses or detaches punitive assessment from established claims may reinforce concrete certification defects, but aggregate size or ordinary settlement pressure alone is not a categorical bar.

**Controlling explanation.** The certification order incorporated important elements of the proposed plan, including its punitive-ratio arrangement, without adopting that plan in full. The court did not establish what one jury could lawfully decide about punishment before the remaining individualized liability work, or how later proceedings would use that decision consistently with each governing law. Predicted savings from millions of trials cannot substitute for identifying the work the common trial would actually accomplish. The manufacturers' concern about repeated jury determinations underscores the need for that account, but repetition of evidence is not automatically reexamination of a decided fact. The Court rests on Rule 23 and does not decide the constitutionality of every punitive ratio, issue class or bifurcated trial. The proposed Liggett settlement does not establish a completed release or make the certification controversy moot. No manufacturer liability, individual recovery, punitive amount, Article III severance question, new Seventh Amendment rule or complete resolution of every asserted Erie objection is decided.

##### IV. Decertification does not require a categorical ban on reconsideration

**Holding and operative rule.** The rejection of the present core-liability and punitive certification is affirmed, but the mandate is vacated insofar as it requires dismissal of the class pleading and bars reconsideration merely because the tort has not matured through individual trials. The case is remanded for ordinary proceedings on individual claims or consideration of a materially narrower, supported class proposal that independently satisfies all applicable Rule 23 requirements; the existing class remains uncertified.

**Controlling explanation.** The lower court identified decisive defects in this proposal, then treated the asserted novelty of the tort as making correction on remand categorically unavailable. The Rule requires a present showing, not a quota of prior trials or an existing crisis in individual litigation. Rejecting an unsupported class does not decide whether a materially different proposal can establish its prerequisites. The proponents retain their burden; remand creates no presumption of certification, discovery entitlement, preferred trial plan or deadline. It adjudicates no individual claim on the merits. The separate denial of Rule 23(b)(2) equitable and medical-monitoring certification was not cross-appealed and is not reopened.

#### Precedent Treatment

- General Telephone Co. of Southwest v. Falcon, 457 U.S. 147: applied to require rigorous determination of the actual certification prerequisites, including where the inquiry overlaps substantive issues.
- Eisen v. Carlisle & Jacquelin, 417 U.S. 156: preserved as forbidding certification based merely on a preliminary prediction of merits success; it does not forbid the substantive inquiry needed to determine whether class adjudication can work.
- Phillips Petroleum Co. v. Shutts, 472 U.S. 797: applied against assuming one State's substantive law for materially disconnected claims; no particular choice-of-law result is directed.
- Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996: its adequate-representation requirement remains distinct from notice and opt-out rights; its judgment-recognition holding does not itself decide prospective certification or the actual adequacy of this class.
- Richards v. Jefferson County, June 10, 1996: preserved for the lawful basis necessary to bind absent persons; it does not constitutionalize every Rule 23 defect or resolve the reserved certification questions.

#### Law After Decision

Rule 23 requires the concrete law-and-proof showing before certification. Neither novelty nor ordinary aggregation pressure creates an independent exclusion, and differing state laws require demonstrated analysis rather than an automatic national-class prohibition. The present certification is defeated by its own missing prerequisites. The partial vacatur preserves lawful reconsideration without reinstating or presuming any class, modifying individual substantive burdens, or creating a federal tobacco tort.

### 1997-01-14 - In re Deuel; Federal Circuit No. 94-1202; 51 F.3d 1552; application 07/542,232; no Supreme Court docket number assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/In_re_Deuel_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. Every rule and each claim-group disposition below commands nine votes.

##### I. Product obviousness turns on supported ordinary skill, not exact prior disclosure

**Holding and operative rule.** Under §103(a), the claimed subject matter as a whole is compared with the prior art and the capabilities of a person of ordinary skill when the invention was made; absence of the product's exact nucleotide sequence from the art is not a safe harbor. A known method may establish obviousness with the relevant art when evidence connects a sufficiently identified target, appropriate source material and a predictably effective route to the claimed product, but a useful research objective and a familiar general technique alone do not establish that connection.

**Controlling explanation.** Graham requires consideration of the prior art, differences from the claim, ordinary skill and relevant objective evidence. Bonito Boats preserves nonobviousness as a threshold beyond literal novelty. Section 103 expressly addresses inventions not identically disclosed under §102, so requiring the art to print the precise product would collapse that distinction. Conversely, the availability of cloning does not establish that every product ultimately obtained by it was within ordinary skill. The Office must support the route from the actual art to the actual subject matter; the applicant's success cannot retrospectively supply that evidence. The manner in which an invention was made does not itself defeat patentability. Predictable-method evidence is therefore relevant and may be sufficient with the necessary supporting art, while generalized encouragement to investigate is not enough. Genetic-code redundancy bears on what the art disclosed and what a worker could predict; it does not establish nonobviousness by arithmetic alone. The Court rejects the appellate opinion's categorical separation of familiar methods from product obviousness, without finding that the Office supplied the missing technical proof here.

##### II. The rejection of the specific sequences lacks the necessary evidentiary connection

**Holding and operative rule.** The reversal of the §103 rejection of claims 5 and 7, which recite the particular human placental and bovine uterine cDNA sequences, is affirmed because the agency did not support the connection between Bohlen's incomplete protein disclosure, Maniatis's general screening method and these claimed products. A label of routine cloning and the applicants' failure to disprove unsupported agency assumptions do not discharge the Office's initial burden.

**Controlling explanation.** Bohlen supplies the first nineteen amino acids of brain mitogens, not complete protein sequences or the claimed DNA. Maniatis supplies general library screening rather than the target-specific library selection and probe design needed here. The agency assumed identity between the disclosed proteins and the claimed growth factors and treated tissue and species differences as immaterial without adequate support. A motivation to clone a useful protein's gene leaves those gaps unresolved. Graham's ordinary-skill inquiry permits proven laboratory capabilities to matter; it does not authorize the Court or litigation counsel to invent the missing factual bridge. Even under the correct noncategorical standard, the grounds actually given fail. No findings of commercial success, unexpected results or others' failed research are supplied to create a different basis for decision.

##### III. The encoding genera require their own analysis and fail on the agency's stated grounds

**Holding and operative rule.** The reversal of the §103 rejection of claims 4 and 6, covering DNA encoding the identified 168-amino-acid proteins, is separately affirmed because the agency articulated no independently supported genus ground and left unresolved the same target-identification and recovery assumptions. A genus does not become nonobvious merely because every member was not previously identified: a supported obvious species may matter, and the Court does not declare these genera nonobvious against all possible art.

**Controlling explanation.** The broader claims cannot automatically receive every defense applicable to the two particular sequences. Their actual scope may make a rejection easier to establish, but scope cannot substitute for a supported rejection. The Office supplied no independent explanation showing that a product within these genera would have been obvious on this record. New technical assertions by counsel cannot cure that omission. Markman requires attention to the operative claim boundary while retaining the distinction between construction and factual work; it supplies no DNA-obviousness test and decides no standard for all PTO fact review. Separate examination of the broad claims therefore leads to the same disposition for an independently stated reason, without converting them into narrow sequence claims or declaring the entire genus patentable.

##### IV. Affirmance removes these rejections without ordering allowance

**Holding and operative rule.** The Federal Circuit's judgment reversing the existing §103 rejections of claims 4–7 is affirmed on the grounds and record presented; no patent issuance, new rejection or mandatory reopening to rehabilitate the unsupported grounds is ordered. Independently proper further examination remains available under ordinary notice and procedure, and §101 eligibility, §112 disclosure or enablement, priority and infringement are not decided.

**Controlling explanation.** Correcting an erroneous categorical legal rule may warrant remand when a necessary factual determination remains blocked by it. Here the agency's unsupported premises independently fail for both claim groups, so correcting the appellate overstatement does not restore the rejections. Any different statutory defect needs its own ground, notice and record. The 1995 amendment retained the product rule in §103(a) and added qualified treatment of biotechnological process claims; the claims here are products, and no process-claim election is assumed. The Court does not turn the amendment into a product exception. The judgment respects lawful examination without awarding exclusivity for research effort or deciding a reserved question.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Graham v. John Deere Co., 383 U.S. 1: applied for the prior-art, claim-difference, ordinary-skill and relevant objective-evidence inquiry; the Court adds no exact-sequence prerequisite or biotechnology exception.
- Bonito Boats, Inc. v. Thunder Craft Boats, Inc., 489 U.S. 141: applied for the distinction between novelty and nonobviousness, including what ordinary skill can deduce.
- Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., April 23, 1996: applied only for preserving actual claim boundaries and the separation of construction from factual work. Its embedded-construction allocation remains intact; the decision supplies no obviousness rule, universal PTO fact-review standard or mandatory construction hearing.

The Federal Circuit's categorical suggestion that familiar methods cannot establish product obviousness without advance exact structural disclosure is rejected. Its reversal of the particular rejections is affirmed on the record-specific grounds stated here. No earlier Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Section 103(a) governs these product claims without a special DNA safe harbor or presumption against biological inventions. Supported evidence of a predictive route can establish obviousness even without an advance printed sequence; a research objective plus general technique cannot alone carry the Office's burden. The present sequence and genus rejections fail independently on the agency grounds supplied. That result grants no patent and does not foreclose a different, properly supported examination ground with ordinary procedure.

### 1997-01-21 - Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth; historical petition identifier No. 95-617; no separate merits docket assigned; OT1996.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Every holding below has nine direct rationale votes.

##### I. The optional program does not establish the claimed parental-liberty infringement

**Holding and operative rule:** The described school condom-availability program does not violate parental liberty or familial privacy merely because parents cannot require the school to exclude their children or notify them of participation. Program entry is optional, abstention carries no educational or disciplinary consequence, and the program does not prohibit parental teaching, alter custody, compel treatment or sexual conduct, or require assent to a message; the junior-high counseling condition applies only after a student elects to request condoms.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Meyer and Pierce, which protect parental direction of upbringing and education, together with Yoder's protection against the particular compulsory educational interference with religious family life and Prince's recognition that parental authority does not exclude every public role concerning children. Those decisions do not establish the particular school-enforced veto sought here.

The school provides the item directly, not merely information about it. That difference matters, but it does not itself displace parental authority. Parents remain free to teach against participation and instruct their children to abstain. Students may comply without losing instruction, grades, benefits, or standing at school. Compulsory school attendance does not turn every separable optional resource into compelled participation.

A junior-high student requesting condoms must receive counseling before receipt. The Court does not erase that condition or approve unknown counseling content. Nothing in the stated condition requires entry into the program, a prescribed affirmation, sexual activity, or a medical procedure. No independent coercive-counseling claim is established here.

A. St. P. C. protects against a complete, potentially indefinite parental-contact cutoff; Baby Richard protects against serious avoidable harm in the manner of compulsory custody transfer; M.L.B. protects necessary-record access in a provided first appeal from permanent parental termination. None of those deprivations occurs here. Practical difficulty in securing a child's adherence to parental teaching does not, on these facts, establish a constitutional right to compel the school to enforce the requested refusal.

**Limits:** This holding does not decide confidential medical treatment, prescription drugs, compelled sex education, distribution to a child unable to consent, or a program using penalty or deception to undermine parental authority. It neither denies parental liberty nor recognizes an independent constitutional entitlement to receive condoms.

##### II. The stated program imposes no established constitutional burden on religious exercise

**Holding and operative rule:** A program that students may decline without sacrificing ordinary education or another benefit, and that requires neither religiously prohibited participation nor assent, does not burden these families' free exercise merely by making the described condoms available contrary to their religious teaching. The stated program establishes no religious targeting, unequal treatment, penalty for religious refusal, or comparable official pressure to participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Lyng and Bowen v. Roy distinguish sincere conflict with government activity from interference with protected religious exercise; they do not place all government services beyond constitutional review. Here the school demands no endorsement of contraception or premarital sex, no use of condoms, and no surrender of worship or religious instruction. The junior-high receipt condition does not compel anyone to enter the program and supplies no established contrary religious affirmation.

The parents' concerns about peer influence and compulsory attendance deserve consideration. Lee's treatment of substantial official pressure at school-directed graduation prayer remains intact. Students here may avoid the separable resource without giving up the educational occasion or apparently joining a state-directed religious exercise. The asserted generalized peer influence does not establish comparable official pressure under these program terms. The holding therefore does not depend on a universal rule that only formal punishment can coerce.

The claim fails at its constitutional burden threshold. The Court need not decide whether Smith would independently defeat a claim involving an actual burden under a neutral law. Invoking parental liberty and free exercise together supplies neither the missing infringement nor automatic strict scrutiny. Lukumi's religious-targeting protections and Settle's prohibition on supported religious exclusion remain fully applicable to materially different facts.

**Limits:** The Court does not approve religious targeting, compelled participation in sexual instruction, denial of an exemption from a mandatory practice, or benefits conditioned on conduct forbidden by faith. The separate statutory inquiry under RFRA is not decided.

No injunction, new evidentiary proceeding, or prescribed parental procedure follows.

**Controlling explanation:** An exemption from compelled conduct differs from a parental right to bar a child's voluntary access. The child can already decline this program without consequence; the claimed school-enforced veto requires a substantive constitutional source that the presented parental and religious claims do not supply. Casey permitted a qualified parental-consent arrangement for abortion while preserving an independent judicial route; permission for such a statute does not command parental control over every preventive item.

The parents' expert affidavits concerning possible adverse consequences are not findings of harm or harmlessness. Under the adopted no-infringement grounds, disputed policy wisdom and comparative health effects do not create a material constitutional fact requiring trial. The judgment does not establish that the program is medically necessary, effective, harmless, or the least restrictive policy.

**Limits:** The Court does not prohibit voluntary parental accommodation, resolve different consent or disclosure laws, or decide an independent RFRA claim. Unargued state statutory claims concerning statutory-rape accomplice liability, abuse reporting, and moral education, and unpresented state constitutional claims, remain outside review. No finding that a medical-consent claim was waived is made.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205, and Prince v. Massachusetts, 321 U.S. 158:** Preserve their distinct treatment of family religious direction and public authority concerning children; neither compels the requested veto over this optional resource.

### 1997-02-18 - General Motors Corp. v. Tracy; No. 95-1232.

Authority: [Record](../records/General_Motors_v_Tracy_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The assessed purchaser may bring this challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A purchaser directly liable for a tax has standing to challenge an exemption that allegedly burdens its interstate purchases. The adverse assessment supplies the purchaser's own economic injury, caused by the challenged tax rule and redressable through lawful relief from that assessment, whether or not payment has been established.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** General Motors contests a charge imposed on its own purchases. Its injury does not become another seller's claim merely because the tax also affects competing suppliers. Bacchus recognizes a taxed purchaser's ability to challenge a discriminatory exemption; Fulton likewise examines the taxpayer's own liability although the distinction concerns another market participant. Relief from an unlawful assessment would remove the asserted burden. Ohio's seller-only objection therefore fails. Standing establishes access to review, not the truth of the claimed comparison between the sellers. The Court does not find an assessment amount or prior payment and orders no refund on the strength of standing alone.

**Precedent treatment:** Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias supplies purchaser standing; Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner confirms that the claimant may contest its own tax burden without being the disfavored seller. Their merits and remedial boundaries remain.

##### II. This record does not establish the asserted discrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio's exemption for sales by regulated local distribution companies does not violate the Commerce Clause on this record, because their compulsory rate, reliability and service obligations materially distinguish their regulated supply business from independent marketers' commodity sales. Commodity overlap alone does not establish equivalence of those offerings where the record does not establish a distinct industrial-sales category separated from the obligations relied upon; the holding confers no general immunity on utilities or all of their competitive transactions.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela and Stevens do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court must examine the practical competitive relationship, rather than accept a utility label or the fact that local marketers also pay the tax. West Lynn requires examination of an integrated fiscal preference; Carbone requires attention to the actual service market. Here the favored sellers must maintain regulated supply and meet reliability and human-needs obligations rather than select only profitable customers. Those duties materially affect their offering. General Motors bought virtually all its gas from interstate marketers and often obtained local delivery, but the record gives little detail about the industrial market and does not establish the asserted unregulated negotiated LDC sales during the assessment period. The common gas commodity does not by itself remove the established service differences.

The objection that duties in a captive market cannot justify preference in a separate competitive market would require considering such a supported separation. This record does not establish it. The Court neither finds that every industrial sale funds a particular public duty nor presumes that any alternative would endanger service. The 1996 enactment cannot prove the character of 1986–1990 purchases. Oregon Waste and Fulton continue to require demanding justification and actual tax equivalence once discrimination is established; no compensatory defense is adopted here. A materially different proved sales category remains for its own case. No independent incidental-burden balancing claim or congressional-authorization theory is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** West Lynn's integrated-operation rule and Carbone's practical service-market inquiry apply without categorical utility immunity. Oregon Waste's discrimination-and-justification rule and Fulton's cumulative compensatory conditions remain unchanged; the Court does not reach justification after rejecting the asserted comparison.

##### III. The economic classification has a rational basis

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

### 1997-02-19 - Auer v. Robbins; No. 95-897.

Authority: [Record](../records/Auer_v_Robbins_merits_1997-02-19.md).

#### Holdings

#### The Board's local relationship does not make it an arm of Missouri

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board is not an arm of Missouri entitled to the State's immunity in these actions because its combined financial, budgetary and operational relationship is local. State creation, statutory regulation and gubernatorial appointments do not alone make a separate public body the State; treasury exposure carries substantial weight alongside institutional control rather than serving as an indispensable sole condition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice agrees with the threshold resolution only.

**Controlling explanation:** Hess requires attention to the actual entity rather than its governmental label, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes local public bodies from the State itself. Missouri created and regulates the Board and the governor appoints four members, with the mayor serving as the fifth. Those controls matter, but the record's municipal financing, city budget and appropriation role, limits requiring city approval and local operational relationship establish the relevant character of this Board. No single appointment or financial fact substitutes for that combined inquiry. Because the Board is not a state arm, the Court need not decide whether litigation conduct effected an authorized waiver, whether Congress validly abrogated state immunity for this claim, or whether an officer route would permit the requested relief. Seminole Tribe supplies no categorical Article I answer to those unnecessary questions.

#### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Fixed salary reasonably distinguishes exempt compensation from pay varying with work quality or quantity. Congress's delegation includes public employment. Disciplinary needs do not make the criterion unreasonable or establish an exception. Major-safety rules prevent serious danger to the plant or other employees; residency does not qualify. Specified absences and furloughs differ from general discipline. No rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith major-safety penalties retain the narrow scope stated above.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

#### The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds

**Holding and operative rule:** The responsible Secretary's fair and considered interpretation of the salary regulation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Presentation in an amicus brief does not alone disqualify that interpretation, but supplies no automatic authority to an agency lawyer, power to amend operative text or deference to new factual assertions about this employer.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice proposes a different interpretive rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Thomas Jefferson University applies Seminole Rock's bounded own-regulation rule; Stinson preserves the difference between interpreting a rule and contradicting or amending it. Martin requires attention to the statutory allocation of administrative responsibility, while Bowen excludes unsupported litigation rationalizations. The Secretary's position here interprets the actual salary and correction text and is consistent with the supported administrative treatment, including the correction letter. It is a fair and considered legal construction, not an employer-specific factual determination resulting from a Department investigation. Its amicus form does not make it an impermissible invention or itself establish its correctness. The canon favoring narrow construction of exemptions does not prevent the Secretary from resolving actual regulatory ambiguity within the congressional delegation. Courts still determine whether the reading fits the text and lawful authority; they do not accept the agency's characterization of unproved facts merely because its legal construction controls.

#### Practical exposure requires an actual practice or an effective particular policy

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The salary text concerns real susceptibility to reductions, not only deductions already made and not every abstract possibility listed in a broad disciplinary code. An effective policy can alter compensation arrangements before the first deduction; an undifferentiated range of sanctions governing exempt and nonexempt workers does not itself communicate that these salaried officers will receive impermissible deductions. Guzy's negotiated two-day residency suspension instead of termination was treated below as an exceptional event. The later rule made termination the exclusive residency sanction; it did not repeal every disciplinary authorization. That episode alone does not establish recurring deductions or a general significant likelihood.

The officers' argument that the manual itself defeats any salary agreement rests on the broader legal construction the Court rejects. Their additional examples, witness-understanding contentions and related evidentiary-burden submission remain disputed submissions, not grounds for a new blanket factual or forfeiture finding. The Court adopts no numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or universal employer-compliance presumption, and directs no general factual reopening. Ordinary preservation and mandate rules retain their force.

#### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The text separates two eligibility alternatives from two cumulative acts of correction. Intentionality does not defeat the other-than-lack-of-work alternative. Nor does the text require payment immediately upon discovery. It nevertheless requires actual reimbursement and a promise; eligibility alone accomplishes neither. The correction mechanism does not convert a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. Deductions generally made when work is unavailable indicate no intention to pay on a salary basis and defeat exemption during that period.

The lower opinion quoted both conditions and required reimbursement, but its formal disposition did not expressly establish a remand implementation proceeding. This Court therefore states the operative condition in its own mandate: on return, the lower courts must secure both required acts before disposing of the affected claim on the ground of corrected exemption. Neither completed payment nor a completed promise is found. If the employer does not perform, it cannot rely on restored exemption, and lawful compensation consequences remain to be determined. No new correction condition, fixed compliance timetable, automatic overtime amount or damages award is added.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. and Mt. Healthy are applied to the Board's actual local relationship; treasury exposure remains important without becoming the exclusive condition of immunity.
- Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida remains limited to its actual holdings and unresolved State-amenability rationale. No waiver, abrogation or retrospective officer-remedy rule is added.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala and Bowles v. Seminole Rock retain controlling weight for a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation, with text, consistency, lawful authority and actual-ground limits intact.
- Stinson v. United States preserves authoritative interpretation within, rather than amendment of, the operative rule; its Sentencing Commission setting is not mechanically transferred to factual assertions here.
- Martin v. OSHRC supplies attention to actual interpretive responsibility and reasonableness; Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital preserves the exclusion of unsupported litigation rationalizations.

#### Law After Decision

The Board may be subjected to these FLSA proceedings without deciding the State's own immunity. Salary basis remains a valid component of the public executive/administrative exemption. Practical exposure, actual deductions and correction are distinct inquiries, and every stated regulatory exception retains its conditions. The own-regulation controlling-weight rule remains law; the Chief Justice's proposed replacement receives no additional join. A correction-dependent exemption requires both reimbursement and the future promise, secured through the present mandate. Duties and the former district award are not reopened.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined on each of the three granted holdings by every other Justice, giving each proposition nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Meyer and Pierce protect parental direction of education and upbringing. They do not make the public school a forum in which every parent may prescribe the particular lessons a child receives. The parents remain free to choose lawful educational alternatives and to instruct their children concerning the ideas encountered at school. Public-school enrollment does not waive that continuing role.

The Court acknowledges the difference from Curtis: attendance here was required, while Curtis concerned a separable optional resource and expressly reserved compulsory instruction. Its result cannot automatically decide this case. The particular compulsion alleged here is exposure to a presentation, not displacement of parental custody or instruction, compelled profession or personal performance by these plaintiffs. Those limits support the present conclusion without treating attendance as voluntary.

Settle requires a genuine pedagogical basis; calling an event curriculum is insufficient. The Court neither endorses the alleged crude conduct as sound teaching nor holds that any presentation bearing an educational label is constitutional. **Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory exposure to the alleged presentation and subsequent coarse imitation does not, on this complaint, establish the students' asserted decisional-privacy or bodily-integrity deprivation or extreme abuse of official power. **Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this holding in the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** A constitutional privacy or bodily-integrity claim requires an invasion of the protected interest, not simply exposure to ideas or language the listener finds offensive. These students were required to attend. They were not alleged to have been selected for a skit, required to affirm the message, disclose intimate information, engage in sexual or religious conduct, or undergo bodily intrusion. The Court considers that account together with the alleged weeks of coarse imitation; it does not discard the aftermath or convert unfavorable facts into trial findings.

The parents' strongest objection is that the school's compulsory authority gave the presentation an intrusive character beyond ordinary offensive speech. That circumstance matters, but it does not establish the particular personal deprivation pleaded. The alleged program, taken as a whole and with favorable inferences, also does not establish the extreme official abuse asserted here. Curtis supplies no categorical answer because it reserved compulsory instruction. Settle likewise does not license religious exclusion, pretextual pedagogy or arbitrary conduct merely because it occurs in school. The present holding leaves protections against compelled profession or participation, bodily intrusion, religious establishment or targeting, and independently established discrimination intact. **Holding and operative rule:** Title IX requires discrimination, exclusion or a hostile educational condition imposed because of sex; sexual subject matter and offense alone do not establish that relationship. **Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this sufficient statutory ground. No severity, attribution or constitutional speech-defense holding supplies an alternative basis.

Neither fact, alone or in their stated context, supplies the missing connection between the treatment of these plaintiffs and their sex. They allege disagreement with the program's message and ensuing hostility on that basis. The program's delivery to an audience containing both sexes is contextual evidence, not a categorical defense to sex discrimination.

Harris requires a contextual inquiry and rejects psychological injury as a prerequisite; DeAngelis likewise rejects mandatory deterioration in performance, physical advances or similar added thresholds. Those employment decisions inform the analysis by analogy, but the Court does not transplant a complete Title VII attribution formula into Title IX. Personal targeting, touching, measurable academic loss and psychological injury are not indispensable elements announced here. Nor does the Court hold that one episode can never suffice. The decision leaves questions involving sex-specific humiliation, school-authorized sexual targeting and deliberate indifference to known sex-based harassment for cases that present them, without prescribing their complete liability rules.

Compulsory attendance remains a material fact, but these allegations establish neither the asserted parental or personal constitutional deprivation nor discrimination because of sex. The Court adopts no general curricular immunity, categorical exemption for a single offensive episode, added injury prerequisite or comprehensive Title IX recipient rule. The separate ungranted constitutional and statutory dismissals and the state-law dispute receive no new controlling merits determination.

#### Off-exchange foreign-currency options are within the Treasury Amendment's exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Options to buy or sell foreign currency are transactions in foreign currency within the Treasury Amendment, so these off-exchange trades are excluded from the Commodity Exchange Act's coverage. The statutory proviso restores coverage only for transactions involving a sale for future delivery **and** conducted on a board of trade; no bank-only or dealer-only restriction is added to the exclusion.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join the rule, explanation and limited remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** A currency option is a transaction whose object is the underlying currency. The contract derives its substance from the currency and its fluctuating value; the statutory words do not postpone that relationship until exercise. The CFTC's distinction between trading currency and trading a right to transact in currency is too narrow. Applied consistently, that distinction would also unsettle the recognized treatment of futures and deprive the express future-delivery proviso of its natural work.

Congress separately listed foreign currency, security warrants, security rights, resales of installment loan contracts, repurchase options, government securities, and mortgages and mortgage purchase commitments. It then stated a particular exception. The Court applies that language and structure without adding a counterparty condition or deciding every listed category's boundary. Both future delivery and trading on a board of trade are required by the proviso; the off-exchange transactions here do not satisfy it.

The distinct regulatory trade-option exemption does not define this statutory exclusion. Its quoted conditions require the offeror reasonably to believe the option is offered to a producer, processor, commercial user or merchant solely for purposes of its business as such. The CFTC's representation below that interbank options qualify under that exemption is preserved at its stated scope; it establishes neither universal regulatory coverage nor qualification of every trade here. A permissible regulatory interpretation cannot create authority withheld by the statute. Auer's own-regulation decision concerns a different inquiry, and the Court need not decide the competing deference contentions to apply this clear statutory boundary.

The alleged fraud cannot supply absent authority. Exclusion from this Act does not protect fraud under other law. Because this is an interlocutory receiver appeal, reversal removes the rejected coverage premise and requires implementation below, without a final liability determination or automatic rejection of genuinely independent grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** The Second Circuit's American Board of Trade option/exercise distinction is rejected at this statutory scope; Salomon Forex's contrary inclusion of off-exchange currency options accords with the rule adopted here. Auer remains confined to its own-regulation setting and supplies no expansion of the enacted exclusion.

#### Precedent Treatment

- CFTC v. American Board of Trade, Inc., 803 F.2d 1242, cannot sustain the contrary distinction between an option transaction and its exercise under this exclusion.
- Salomon Forex, Inc. v. Tauber, 8 F.3d 966, is consistent with inclusion of off-exchange foreign-currency options; the Court does not adopt every statement concerning other transactions or counterparties.
- Auer v. Robbins governs permissible interpretation of an agency's own regulation within lawful authority. The present statutory holding neither changes that rule nor decides a broader agency-deference dispute.

#### Law After Decision

The Treasury Amendment excludes these off-exchange currency options. Coverage under its proviso requires both future delivery and conduct on a board of trade. The statute and the separate trade-option regulation remain distinct. Enforcement need alone cannot expand the CFTC's statutory authority, and other antifraud laws retain their independent reach.
