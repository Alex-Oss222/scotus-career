### Martin v. District of Columbia Court of Appeals et al., No. 92-5584, and Martin v. McDermott et al., No. 92-5618

Fee motions, petition management, and proposed prospective restriction, November 2, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided November 2, 1992, without oral argument; no separate submission date is verified. In No. 92-5584, Martin seeks certiorari under §1254(1) to the D.C. Circuit, No. 91-7176. On May 8, 1992, that court summarily affirmed the District Court for the District of Columbia’s October 4, 1991 jurisdictional dismissal under *District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman*, which distinguishes general bar-rule challenges from review of individual admission judgments. Rehearing orders followed July 24. The petition presents questions concerning general bar rules, delay, the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and Civil Rights Act, jurisdiction, prospective relief, and immunity.

In No. 92-5618, Martin seeks certiorari under §1257(a) to the Delaware Supreme Court’s May 26, 1992 judgment in No. 448,1991; rehearing was denied June 5. That court sustained a jurisdictional dismissal because the attempted appeal for a new determination changed the parties and claims. It separately rejected the recusal claim as meritless and moot. The petition challenges the judge’s law-school affiliations as a constitutionally disqualifying interest. In each docket, Martin seeks leave to proceed in forma pauperis—without prepaying the filing fee—and relief from the judgment below. No question is granted.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas participate in the paper consideration and decision. The Rule 39 motions reach the Court through its ordinary filing channel, not an interim-application referral.

#### Public Action and Judgment

The Court grants leave to proceed without prepayment in No. 92-5584 and denies certiorari. It denies leave in No. 92-5618, allowing payment and a petition in the required form by November 23, 1992. It declines any prospective filing restriction. A per curiam opinion—an unsigned opinion of the Court—explains the fee and prospective-restriction decisions. Stevens dissents from the companion fee denial, joined by Stone-Zsela and Blackmun. No dissent from denial of the lead petition is published.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### No. 92-5584: an arguable current petition is not defeated by filing history

**Controlling proposition:** Rule 39.8 permits denial of leave to file without prepayment only upon a finding that the particular petition is frivolous or malicious; earlier unsuccessful filings cannot substitute for that finding. No. 92-5584's distinct general-rule and prospective statutory arguments clear the nonfrivolousness threshold on this record, so the Court grants leave without deciding that the arguments warrant certiorari or would succeed on the merits.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine participating Justices adopt this filing-specific application and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Martin's history does not dispose of the paper now before it. Zatko permits discretionary fee denial for identified frivolous submissions in an extreme pattern of abuse; it does not make previous denials conclusive. Feldman distinguishes a challenge to a general bar rule from review of an individual admission judgment. Martin invokes that distinction and asserts prospective statutory grounds. His personal grievance, extensive collateral litigation and uncertain statutory theories present substantial obstacles, but those obstacles do not establish that every distinct question is frivolous. Denton preserves the difference between an arguable claim and a clearly baseless allegation; its statutory screening rule does not mechanically govern every feature of this Court's filing practice. The Court neither credits the disputed medical allegations nor finds that the asserted general classification actually exists. It decides no question of statutory coverage, retroactivity, preservation, immunity or ultimate jurisdiction. The motion is granted; the petition remains subject to ordinary discretionary review.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zatko v. California, 502 U.S. 16 (November 4, 1991): applied as the existing discretionary, particular-submission rule; its extreme-abuse finding does not establish the frivolousness of a different current petition.
- District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman, 460 U.S. 462 (1983): its general-rule/individual-judgment distinction supplies an arguable legal question here; the Court does not decide which side of that distinction the complete complaint occupies.
- Denton v. Hernandez, 504 U.S. 25 (May 4, 1992): its distinction between arguability and clearly baseless factual allegations informs this limited assessment; its statutory review and remedial rules remain unchanged and are not imported wholesale into Rule 39.

##### No. 92-5618: particular frivolousness and the available paid route

**Controlling proposition:** Where the current recusal petition identifies no concrete constitutionally disqualifying interest and develops no federal challenge to the independent state jurisdictional ground that defeats the action, those defects together establish frivolousness for this Rule 39.8 motion. In light of Martin's established extreme repetitive filing abuse, the Court exercises its discretion to deny this motion only, allowing payment of the Rule 38 fee and submission in Rule 33 form through November 23, 1992; the petition itself is not denied by this order.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six of nine control the motion, its stated ground and the compliance opportunity. Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Stevens dissent from this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this motion must be judged from the McDermott petition itself. Zatko permits a targeted fee denial when a present frivolous submission accompanies established extreme abuse. The Delaware judgment rests on the state rule that an appeal from the Justice of the Peace court retain the same action and parties. Martin's petition develops no federal objection to that jurisdictional ground. It instead attributes unconstitutional bias to professional ties, appearances and related allegations without identifying a concrete disqualifying interest comparable to the personal financial stake in Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Lavoie. A genuine constitutional defect in the decisionmaker cannot be dismissed merely by calling another ground jurisdictional. Here, however, the undeveloped constitutional premise and the unaddressed jurisdictional obstacle operate together. Neither filing volume, the state court's characterization, nor an affiliation alone supplies the finding. Denying this fee privilege leaves paid, compliant filing available. The Court decides no general rule insulating judicial affiliations from scrutiny and enters no merits judgment on the recusal claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Zatko v. California, 502 U.S. 16 (November 4, 1991): applied to this distinct submission; fee denial remains discretionary and separate from disposition of certiorari.
- Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Lavoie, 475 U.S. 813 (1986): distinguished because this petition establishes no comparable concrete personal financial stake; other constitutionally disqualifying interests are not categorically excluded.

##### No prospective filing restriction on this record

**Controlling proposition:** The Court declines to impose a prospective fee-and-form condition on Martin's unidentified future civil-certiorari petitions because the established record does not show that the available treatment of particular filings is inadequate. This case-specific refusal decides neither that a prior warning is invariably required nor that prospective restrictions are categorically unlawful, and imposes no condition on future civil or criminal filings.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine adopt the sufficiency of this narrower, record-specific ground without resolving the Court's power on a different record.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the remedy should remain confined to the filings before it. McDonald and Sindram permit measures directed at concrete abuse of extraordinary writs; Sindram also identifies the special risk created by petitions that have no filing deadline. Those decisions do not compel a restriction on all future ordinary civil petitions. Zatko denied the identified motions and preserved a paid route. This record establishes no subsequent warning, breach of that order or sequence of intervening adjudications demonstrating that its narrower response failed. Moreover, one present petition raises distinct arguments sufficient to permit filing. The established repetitive history supports close examination and, in the companion matter, fee denial, but does not demonstrate a need to prejudge unknown papers. The Court therefore gives the Clerk no prospective exclusion instruction. Its conclusion rests on this record and available remedies, without deciding a universal notice requirement, a numerical threshold, or the legality of every possible future restriction.

**Precedent treatment:**

- In re McDonald, 489 U.S. 180 (1989): preserved as authority for targeted protection against extraordinary-writ abuse; no extension to all future civil-certiorari petitions is made here.
- In re Sindram, 498 U.S. 177 (1991): its extraordinary-writ setting and preservation of other filing categories are distinguished; its existing force is unchanged.
- Zatko v. California, 502 U.S. 16 (November 4, 1991): its limited remedy is preserved; no warning, compliance default or further adjudication is inferred from that order.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The denial of certiorari in No. 92-5584 creates no merits holding and approves no reasoning below. No certiorari disposition is entered in No. 92-5618. The Court does not resolve ADA coverage or retroactivity, Civil Rights Act coverage, the full complaint’s jurisdictional status, judicial immunity, the truth of either petition’s allegations, or the merits of the recusal claim.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Effective November 2, 1992, the Court applies the existing discretionary, filing-specific Rule 39.8 rule of *Zatko*, which permits targeted fee denial for a particular frivolous submission in the established abuse setting. No. 92-5584 clears the low filing threshold; the distinct defects in No. 92-5618 support denial of its fee motion. No replacement rule, numerical test, categorical ban, mandatory warning prerequisite, or constitutional right to unrestricted fee waiver is created. The refusal of prospective relief is confined to this record. *Denton*’s §1915(d) rules for screening factual allegations and appellate review remain unchanged. No precedent is overruled. Neither underlying judgment is adopted as this Court’s merits holding, and denial of certiorari establishes no bar-admission, statutory, immunity, or recusal merits precedent.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stevens dissents from Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela and Blackmun. They would grant leave in No. 92-5618 and deny that petition in the ordinary course. The judicial-disqualification question is a separate claim requiring its own assessment; it cannot be rejected merely as another aspect of the bar-admission litigation. The petition’s failure to establish a basis for Supreme Court review does not, for these Justices, warrant withholding access without prepayment. They accept no recusal allegation as true and decide no statutory or constitutional merits question. Their disagreement concerns the fee disposition and its resulting paid-filing condition, not an entitlement to certiorari. They join Parts I and III, including the grant of leave in No. 92-5584 and the refusal of any prospective restriction. This dissent supplies no controlling proposition and leaves in force *Zatko*’s discretionary rule for particular submissions.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The November 2 order grants leave to proceed without prepayment and denies certiorari in No. 92-5584. That petition proceeding is closed; the D.C. Circuit judgment remains undisturbed without approval of its merits.

In No. 92-5618, only the fee motion is decided. Martin may pay the Rule 38 docketing fee and submit a petition complying with Rule 33 by November 23, 1992. Ordinary petition processing requires that compliance. The order neither denies the petition nor commands an automatic later denial. No payment, default, extension, or subsequent Court action is established. The Delaware judgment is not vacated, affirmed on the merits, or remanded. No prospective filing restriction issues, and the Clerk receives no instruction excluding future filings.

### Attwood v. Singletary, No. 95-6710

Financial leave and certiorari action, January 22, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 297. The Court considers financial leave for the present certiorari petition, a proposed prospective noncriminal filing condition, and disposition of the petition. Attwood challenges Florida counsel-signature restrictions while alleging inability to obtain counsel. The attached state order records extensive abusive litigation; his claims about the restrictions' effects on custody and mistreatment remedies remain allegations.

#### Public Action

The motion for leave to proceed without prepayment of fees is granted. The petition for a writ of certiorari is denied. No new prospective filing condition is imposed. The Court announces no petition poll.

The present petition is accepted without prepayment and denied. No payment interval is necessary, no future noncriminal fee-and-form condition issues, and no judgment on the underlying federal or state claims is entered. Denial of certiorari does not affirm the state court's reasoning or approve its counsel-signature requirement.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to action | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Financial leave granted; proposed prospective control declined | Present-application and prospective-control grounds. |
| Concurrence in the result | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Agrees with the result | Separate explanation; does not join the Court's reasoning. |

The certiorari denial supplies no merits opinion or disclosed poll.

#### Holdings

##### This particular application supports financial leave

**Operative rule:** Supported inability to prepay and an arguable present petition warrant financial leave on this record; prior volume and state abuse do not themselves establish the particular frivolousness or malice needed for the proposed Rule 39.8 denial. Granting leave does not establish the truth of the allegations, entitlement to certiorari, or future financial eligibility.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. The same eight Justices adopt the following prospective-control holding. Stone-Zsela agrees with the result on his separately stated grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The signed poverty statement and accompanying financial answers support this application despite partly illegible account details. The Court adopts no precise asset balance. The petition raises a concrete concern that mandatory counsel signatures, coupled with an alleged inability to obtain counsel, impede access including claimed custody-related relief.

Martin distinguishes an arguable current submission from one actually established as frivolous. Anderson likewise separates financial leave for an access claim from obstacles to obtaining the requested writ. The state's findings of extensive abuse remain relevant, but do not decide this distinct challenge to the restriction's practical operation. Zatko continues to permit denial of a particular filing upon the required showing. The Court accepts no general right to unlimited fee-free litigation and decides no claim of constitutional invalidity here.

##### The proposed future condition lacks its separate supporting showing

**Operative rule:** A prospective fee-and-form condition on a category of future filings requires support tied to the applicant, that category, and the inadequacy of treating individual submissions. Extensive state litigation alone does not establish that showing for the proposed condition on future noncriminal certiorari petitions in this Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The restriction would preserve paid and criminal routes. That limited scope matters, but does not prove its necessity or fit. The established state abuse does not itself demonstrate abuse of this Court's relevant category or why particular-filing treatment is inadequate. Martin, Day, Sassower, and Anderson require those distinctions rather than a prediction that every unwritten claim will be frivolous.

The Court declines the condition on this record. It establishes no universal warning prerequisite, numerical threshold, or obligation to exhaust every conceivable lesser measure. McDonald and Sindram retain their authority for properly supported targeted controls. Ordinary neutral filing rules and sanctions for proved misconduct remain available.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Zatko v. California:** Preserves supported denial of a particular frivolous or malicious filing; prior losses alone do not make this challenge frivolous.
- **Martin v. District of Columbia:** Applied to separate the current petition's arguability from the independent justification for prospective control.
- **In re Anderson:** Applied to distinguish supported financial leave and actual access concerns from entitlement to a writ or future control.
- **Day and Sassower:** Their individualized, category-specific prospective-control requirements remain and are applied here.
- **In re McDonald, 489 U.S. 180, and In re Sindram, 498 U.S. 177:** Targeted protection of the judicial process remains available on an adequate record; neither dictates the proposed order without its supporting predicates.

#### Law After Decision

The filing ruling applies the existing current-submission and prospective-control requirements. It creates no categorical immunity from supported controls and no right to discretionary Supreme Court review. The certiorari denial decides none of the access, habeas, counsel-availability, or mistreatment questions.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the result.** He favors granting leave upon the established financial showing and promptly disposing of the present petition without attaching a disability to future litigation. In his view, equal access to federal process cannot be traded away merely for docket convenience; volume warrants efficient scrutiny of each paper, not a presumption that every unwritten claim is frivolous. Rule 39.8 permits a fee denial when justified but does not require that intermediate step before ordinary certiorari disposition.

His constitutional position seeks stronger protection against predictive, identity-based exclusion than the Court's record-specific ground establishes. His preference for direct screening is an institutional judgment, not an empirical finding about administrative cost. He preserves ordinary neutral rules, jurisdiction, sanctions for proved misconduct, and valid finality doctrines. No other Justice joins this explanation, and it does not turn denial of the petition into a ruling on the underlying claims.

#### Source Notes

The [actual petition, affidavit, and attached Florida order](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0788) supply the present financial showing, access allegations, and state abuse findings. The [reported docket identification](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-297.pdf) supplies the case, docket, and date. The partly illegible account explanation is not an exact financial finding. The Florida Supreme Court counsel-signature condition is distinct from the paid-filing alternative described for another state appellate court.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

This petition is concluded. No payment deadline, additional Court warning, counsel appointment, release order, or new prospective restriction follows. Any different later filing remains subject to the ordinary rules and governing law.

**End of entry: Attwood v. Singletary, Financial leave and certiorari action, January 22, 1996.**

### Jones v. ABC-TV, No. 95-7186, 516 U.S. 363

Filing-management order, February 26, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

The Court considers the financial-leave motion accompanying Jones's petition for certiorari and a proposed condition on future noncriminal certiorari filings. The petition seeks review of the Eighth Circuit's August 8, 1995 summary affirmance of the district court's June 14 dismissal of a media-related civil action. Certiorari itself is not decided by this order.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participate. Justice Breyer takes no part; no reason is stated.

#### Public Action

All eight participating Justices support keeping the financial-leave motion pending, reserving financial eligibility, declining a present denial under Rule 39.8, and refusing the proposed future noncriminal-certiorari fee-and-form condition. This is neither a grant nor a denial of financial leave or certiorari. The motion and petition remain pending; no petition poll is announced.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas throughout; Stone-Zsela in part | Supports pending financial treatment and refusal of present and prospective restrictions | Eight Justices adopt the financial and current-paper propositions. Stone-Zsela joins pending financial treatment and the present Rule 39.8 showing; seven Justices adopt the record-specific prospective-control ground. |
| Concurrence in part and in the result concerning prospective control | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Agrees that no future condition should issue | Joins the financial/current-paper explanation; rests prospective refusal on a broader individual-consideration ground. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Financial eligibility remains reserved

**Holding and authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined on this proposition by all seven other participating Justices, keeps the financial motion pending without finding eligibility or ineligibility.

Rule 39.1 requires the prescribed motion and notarized affidavit or declaration under 28 U.S.C. §1746, unless a qualifying appointed-counsel exception applies; no such exception is established here. References to monthly benefits, copying loans and an accompanying affidavit do not establish the required financial particulars or the affidavit's contents. An incomplete evidentiary showing also does not establish that Jones can pay, failed to submit a required document or made a false statement. Pending treatment adds no refiling duty, deadline or default consequence. A later financial ruling requires its own supported basis.

##### The present showing does not establish frivolousness or malice under Rule 39.8

**Holding and authority:** The same eight Justices decline a present Rule 39.8 denial. Martin, Anderson and Attwood require assessment of the particular paper; ordinary pleading failure is not automatically frivolousness.

The media-agreement, compulsory-publication and derivative §1986 theories have substantial defects. Formal assignment and actual appellate review contradict broad allegations of self-assignment and no review. But the petition also identifies distinct objections to actual leave requirements affecting paid filings by litigants representing themselves and to denial of requested appellate briefing. Defects in the media theories do not dispose of those objections. The petition and its appended June 14 district memorandum, transfer order and August 8 Eighth Circuit order distinguish those questions. The alleged proof in nineteen separate appendix volumes is not established by the record described here.

The Court finds neither that the access allegations are true nor that the lower restrictions, recusal ruling or appellate procedure were unlawful. A supported Rule 39.8 denial remains available; the Rule permits rather than compels it. Refusal of this proposed denial establishes neither indigence nor entitlement to review.

##### The proposed future condition lacks independent support

**Holding and authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, applies Martin, Day, Sassower, Anderson and Attwood to require support tied to the applicant, the relevant filing category and the inadequacy of individual treatment before imposing a prospective condition.

The lower courts' abuse findings do not themselves establish those predicates for future noncriminal certiorari submissions here. Preserving paid and criminal routes narrows the proposal but supplies no missing justification. The Court declines the condition on this record. It does not categorically invalidate all prospective controls, require a warning in every case, fix a numerical threshold or demand exhaustion of every conceivable lesser measure.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Martin's distinction between the present paper and the independent justification for prospective restrictions is applied with Anderson, Day, Sassower and Attwood. Zatko continues to permit supported denial of a particular frivolous or malicious filing. McDonald and Sindram retain their bounded authority for targeted protection of the judicial process. No prior filing-control holding is overruled or enlarged into categorical immunity.

Assessment of the current paper, financial eligibility, justification for future controls and certiorari remain separate inquiries. The order applies existing filing law and creates no merits holding concerning the underlying suit or its procedural restrictions. The governing provisions are the Rules effective October 2, 1995, and 28 U.S.C. §1915 as then in force. The Court does not adopt the concurrence's broader future-filing rule.

#### Separate Position

Stone-Zsela concurs in the financial and current-paper treatment and in refusal of the prospective condition. He would require individual consideration of future ordinary civil-certiorari submissions by an otherwise financially eligible litigant. Rule 39.8 addresses a frivolous or malicious submission; past abuse does not establish the contents of an unwritten petition. Automatic fee denial based on identity and history risks excluding a later legitimate claim solely because its author cannot pay. Inherent docket authority should implement the Rules' current-paper inquiry while permitting prompt rejection of a submission actually shown defective.

His position goes beyond the Court's record-specific ground. It concerns ordinary civil-certiorari fee treatment, does not decide every extraordinary-writ restriction, vacate unreviewed orders or establish unlimited fee-free litigation in every court, and preserves jurisdictional, timeliness, form and current-paper controls. Individual consideration requires no merits opinion, hearing or elaborate explanation for every filing. Financial eligibility must still be established; once it is established and current frivolousness or malice is not shown, he would grant financial leave and consider certiorari separately. Missing financial proof is not itself ineligibility, and pending treatment grants neither review nor relief against respondents.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The financial-leave motion and certiorari petition remain pending. Eligibility is reserved for a supported later determination, with no date set. No direction to refile, payment interval, new warning, sanction, Rule 33.1 payment/form condition, merits remand, terminal petition action or alteration of an existing lawful filing order follows. Future submissions remain subject to ordinary rules and governing law.

**End of entry: Jones v. ABC-TV, filing-management order, February 26, 1996.**
