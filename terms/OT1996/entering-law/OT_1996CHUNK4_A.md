## Standards and Tests selection: Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties

### Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties

**Governing question:** Whether a voluntary public-employment relationship creates a general substantive-due-process duty to provide workplace safety.

**Current rule:** Voluntary municipal employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care, and substantive due process imposes no independent general obligation to provide such employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks. A state-law safety duty, even assuming it creates a protected liberty interest, does not convert its breach alone into a substantive constitutional wrong. The underlying federal right and constitutional arbitrariness remain distinct from state-law entitlement and municipal attribution.

**Authority by component:**

- No employment-based general safety duty or custody: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — Voluntary public employment creates no general constitutional safety duty, February 26, 1992; Part B of Stevens's unanimous opinion.
- State entitlement does not alone establish arbitrary deprivation: Collins — These pleaded omissions do not establish constitutional arbitrariness, February 26, 1992; Part C joined by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas with Stevens. Stone's distinct concurrence is not part of this component's authority.

**Present operation effective:** *Collins*, February 26, 1992.

**Open question:** Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, procedural due process, and a properly alleged affirmative-danger claim are not resolved. *Collins* creates neither a general affirmative-danger test nor a categorical rejection of one.

**Holdings navigation:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992) — Municipal liability and public employment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. ## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

**Authority by component:**

- Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal: Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal, 1995-06-12. Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this bounded rule.
- Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule: Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer; all nine support this legal-review proposition.

- Successive-stage review and preserved mandate limits: Behrens — Holding 1, February 21, 1996; Ginsburg Part I for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and herself (seven).
- Conditional assumed-fact rule: Behrens — Holding 2, February 21, 1996; unanimous Part II. Claim-specific effect of another surviving claim: Holding 3, Part III for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join. Stevens and Breyer join these conditional rules while opposing authorization of the successive appeal.

**Present operation effective:** Behrens, February 21, 1996; Johnson and Kimberlin retain their original component authority.

**Open question:** Genuinely necessary connected review and a categorical rule for every mixed order remain unresolved. Johnson and Kimberlin use their common entering baseline; neither is treated as earlier same-day law for the other.

**Holdings navigation:** Factual sufficiency is not an immediate qualified-immunity appeal; Separable legal review of the categorical evidence rule; Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nexus and rough proportionality for individualized land dedications

### Nexus and rough proportionality for individualized land dedications

**Governing question:** What a government must establish when it conditions a development permit on dedication of property interests.

**Current rule:** For an individualized permit condition demanding a real-property dedication, the government must establish an essential nexus to a legitimate regulatory interest and rough proportionality between the nature and extent of the demanded dedication and the proposed development’s impact. An individualized determination must justify each demand; a generalized possibility of benefit is insufficient. Reasonable estimates may suffice, without exact mathematical precision or proof that this property is uniquely suited. Public access must itself be justified when a less intrusive restriction would address the impact.

## Standards and Tests selection: Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

### Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

**Governing question:** Whether a later law may withdraw mandatory discretionary-parole reconsideration for an earlier offender by doubling hearing intervals or permitting individualized postponement for up to three years.

**Current rule:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining intervening review is established. That binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence and ultimate release discretion; the prisoner need not prove the omitted hearing would have produced release. Relief restores the consideration opportunity, not an entitlement to parole. An individualized initial prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that prediction do not preserve later mandatory opportunities to present rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken forecast. Where the actual three-year deferral removes meaningful annual consideration while parole is legally available and no ordinary substitute is established, it increases punishment. The prisoner must show the earlier entitlement, retrospective actual withdrawal and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient. Once shown, the State must substantiate a substitute it invokes through its legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide that consideration. The prisoner retains the ultimate burden of proving an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory annual-to-biennial withdrawal, proof and relief boundary: Cavanaugh v. Roller — The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record, 1993-11-30. Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, its application to Roller, and the prospective remedy. Ginsburg's conditional agreement in her separate opinion adds no vote to this controlling coalition.

- The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here: The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here, 1995-04-25. Part II of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the same rule, application and relief.
- The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes: The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes, 1995-04-25. Part III of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices expressly adopt this bounded evidentiary direction and its application. Recognition of a source limitation in the dissent is not an additional join.

**Present operation effective:** Morales, April 25, 1995, for the bounded individualized-deferral application and claimant/substitute substantiation rule; Cavanaugh retains its original scope.

**Open question:** Materially different individualized arrangements and demonstrated interim review remain unresolved. No automatic ban on every frequency reduction, universal probability formula, numerical response deadline or general presumption shifting ultimate constitutional proof is adopted. Changed suitability or voting rules, parole abolition and extraordinary clemency are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record; The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here; The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes.

## Standards and Tests selection: Notice-date selection of the Tax Court refund lookback

### Notice-date selection of the Tax Court refund lookback

**Governing question:** The payment window under 26 U.S.C. §6512(b)(3)(B) for an ordinary refund claim.

**Current rule:** The hypothetical claim filed on the deficiency-notice date both selects and measures the incorporated §6511(b)(2) lookback. Ordinarily, §6511(a) permits a claim within three years after a return or two years after payment, whichever ends later; without a return, two years after payment applies. A claim within the return-based three-year period reaches payments within three years plus an applicable filing extension; otherwise the payment lookback is two years. The notice-date hypothetical supplies a claim, not a return: a later-filed return cannot retrospectively select the three-year window or bring earlier payments inside it.

**Authority by component:**

- Lundy — notice-date selection and measurement, 1996-01-17; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Lundy, January 17, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** Deficiency jurisdiction remains distinct from refund authority. Section 6512(b)(3)(A)’s postnotice payments, subsection (C)’s qualifying actual prenotice claims, assessment-extension agreements and special statutory periods retain their separate operation. No universal jurisdictional characterization of all tax deadlines follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Commissioner v. Lundy, 516 U.S. 235.

## Standards and Tests selection: Service timing in Suits in Admiralty Act actions

### Service timing in Suits in Admiralty Act actions

**Governing question:** Whether §742 supplies a separate jurisdictional or procedural deadline alongside Rule 4.

**Current rule:** Section 742's forthwith-service timetable is a procedural direction implementing existing consent, not an independent jurisdictional condition. In a timely commenced domestic SAA action, Congress's later enactment of former Rule 4(j) displaces the inconsistent shorter timetable; the applicable Rule 4 timing scheme and authorized enlargement govern without enlarging substantive consent or removing designated-recipient requirements. Former Rule 4(j) required dismissal without prejudice after notice when service exceeded 120 days without good cause, except foreign service under former subdivision (i). Amended Rule 4(m) requires, after notice, dismissal without prejudice or an order for service within a specified time; good cause requires extension, while enlargement without good cause is permitted but not compelled. Foreign service under (f) and (j)(1) is excepted; a limitations bar may support discretion but does not mandate relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Procedural classification, later-enactment displacement and distinct Rule schemes: Henderson — Holdings I–III, May 20, 1996; Scalia for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer and himself (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Henderson, May 20, 1996.

**Effective transition:** Amended Rule 4 took effect December 1, 1993 for new cases and, insofar as just and practicable, proceedings then pending. It does not automatically relabel completed 1993 service acts or validate a particular enlargement.

**Exceptions and limits:** The two-year commencement limit and substantive waiver remain. This is no general rule displacing every service statute and no independently sufficient Court-promulgation ground under §2072; the operative conflict is with Congress's direct enactment.

**Holdings navigation:** Henderson v. United States, 517 U.S. 654.

## 3. Standing Practices

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. **Holding:** Applying the 1981 California hearing-deferral provision to Morales’s 1980 offense violates the Ex Post Facto Clause because the actual three-year deferral withdraws offense-date mandatory annual consideration during a period when parole is legally available, without an established ordinary substitute for the meaningful intervening opportunities. ##### The amended escheat provision still takes the presented succession interests without compensation

**Holding and operative rule:** Amended §207 of the Indian Land Consolidation Act effects an uncompensated taking as applied to these fractional trust-land interests: it eliminates intestate succession and confines devise to existing co-owners of the same parcel, leaving these owners unable to transmit valuable interests to their chosen descendants even though each devise preserves an existing fraction. Penn Central supplies the examination of economic impact, investment-backed expectations, and the character of the governmental action. Low annual income does not establish that these interests lack value: their reported aggregate value is $1,239. Expectations of investment return are not strong, but that consideration does not answer the extraordinary destruction of succession.

Subsection (b) permits devise to another owner of an undivided interest in the same parcel. ### 1997-01-14 - Chamber of Commerce v. Reich; D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; 74 F.3d 1322; no Supreme Court docket number assigned.

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. Each proposition below therefore has nine votes; judgment agreement and reasoning agreement coincide.

##### I. Review of the implementing officer

**Holding and operative rule.** A person injured by an officer's implementation of a restriction outside delegated statutory authority may seek prospective equitable restraint under federal-question jurisdiction unless Congress has displaced that remedy. Section 702 waives sovereign immunity for this nonmonetary official-capacity suit against the implementing Secretary even without a pleaded APA review cause, while preserving other legal and equitable review limits and relief prohibitions in another consent-to-suit statute; any injunction must identify the officer or successor responsible for compliance.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 1331 supplies jurisdiction; traditional officer-directed equity supplies the action; §702 addresses sovereign immunity. None independently creates a damages claim or a cause for every statutory error. McAnnulty, Stark and Harmon establish that the absence of an express review provision does not itself eliminate prospective officer relief. Larson and Dugan support the confined distinction between authorized action and action beyond delegated power, without making every allegation of error sufficient. The employers challenge an operative disqualification imposed by the Secretary, not merely the wisdom of an authorized purchase. Their claim presents a judicially manageable boundary between two federal statutes. Dalton bars reopening its particular completed base-closure package; the Procurement Act contains no comparable structure foreclosing this action. Franklin preserves review of a completed subordinate act despite later presidential involvement. The origin of this restriction in a presidential order does not answer whether its implementing officer has statutory authority. The Court neither decides the President's APA status nor authorizes coercive relief against him.

##### II. The procurement delegation does not authorize this labor-policy sanction

**Holding and operative rule.** The Procurement Act's general economy-and-efficiency delegation does not authorize Executive Order 12954's government-wide disqualification based on permanent replacement of economic strikers, because that sanction alters the bargaining choices Congress preserved under the NLRA. This statutory holding leaves Congress free to change those choices and preserves ordinary review of materially different proprietary conditions and lawful centralized purchasing standards.

**Controlling explanation.** Mackay Radio recognizes the use of permanent replacements for economic strikers within the NLRA's remaining protections; it does not equate those strikers with unfair-labor-practice strikers. Insurance Agents and Machinists identify the economic weapons Congress left to the bargaining parties. A general purchasing delegation must operate with that allocation, not silently displace it. Boston Harbor protects a lawful bargain justified by the public purchaser's project interests; a procurement label alone does not turn general labor policy into proprietary conduct. This order makes replacement itself the disqualifying event and reaches affiliates capable of supplying the contract, even when the striking workers do not perform that contract. The Government's interest in dependable supply and retained experience is legitimate, but the policy's actual operation goes beyond a supported performance bargain. Its threshold and discretionary exceptions do not cure that statutory mismatch. The Court neither imposes a universal one-project rule nor invalidates purchasing standards because of their size. It does not guarantee any employer government business. The ground is construction of the federal enactments together, not mechanical application of state-preemption labels or a constitutional limit on Congress.

**Operative qualifications.** The order concerns contracts above $100,000. After finding permanent replacement of lawfully striking workers, the Secretary may find termination for convenience appropriate unless the contracting agency head objects; termination is not mandatory. Debarment retains exceptions when the precipitating dispute has been resolved as determined by the Secretary or the agency head finds a compelling reason to lift it. Debarment normally reaches the organizational units found to have used permanent replacements; the regulations define a unit to include the responsible prime-contracting division or element and any affiliate capable of supplying the contracted goods or services. In deciding whether a dispute ended, the regulations consider a formal settlement or agreed dispute-resolution procedure, informal resolution, the strikers' return, and other factors tending to show the dispute has ended. The Court does not adopt the lower court's reading of those factors as a universal requirement of union approval or find that every application compels unlawful recognition.

##### III. The relief follows the statutory defect

**Holding and operative rule.** The judgment reversing dismissal and rejecting the challenged implementation is affirmed, and the case is remanded for appropriate prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against the implementing Secretary confined to the unlawful procurement restriction. No injunction against the President, damages, contract award, striker reinstatement or employee-specific bargaining remedy is ordered.

**Controlling explanation.** The reviewable defect concerns the Secretary's implementation of an unauthorized restriction. Prospective officer relief addresses that defect without transferring purchasing decisions to the courts or deciding individual labor rights. The lower court must formulate the appropriate decree consistently with §702's responsible-officer requirement and retained legal and equitable limits. No independent nondelegation ruling, mandatory procurement-findings procedure or decision on a materially different proprietary measure is necessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

- McAnnulty, 187 U.S. 94: applied for prospective review of officer action beyond delegated statutory authority despite the absence of an express review provision.
- Stark v. Wickard, 321 U.S. 288: applied for the availability of judicial protection against officer action exceeding statutory limits.
- Harmon v. Brucker, 355 U.S. 579: applied for judicial determination of an officer's statutory authority before accepting a claim of unreviewable discretion.
- Larson v. Domestic & Foreign Commerce Corp., 337 U.S. 682: applied at its confined officer-action scope; statutory error is not converted into a universal immunity exception.
- Dugan v. Rank, 372 U.S. 609: applied for the corresponding distinction between sovereign relief and action outside lawful officer authority.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992: its review of completed subordinate action is preserved; presidential APA status and coercive presidential relief remain reserved.
- Dalton v. Specter, May 23, 1994: distinguished because its completed base-closure scheme supplies preclusion absent here; its rule is not general executive immunity.
- NLRB v. Mackay Radio & Telegraph Co., 304 U.S. 333: applied for permanent replacement of economic strikers within the NLRA's retained protections, without changing the protection of unfair-labor-practice strikers.
- NLRB v. Insurance Agents' International Union, 361 U.S. 477: applied for Congress's preservation of economic pressure in bargaining.
- Machinists v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Commission, 427 U.S. 132: its account of the economic choices Congress left unregulated informs the statutory construction; it is not mechanically transferred from state regulation to every federal enactment.
- Boston Harbor, March 8, 1993: distinguished on the actual proprietary bargain; its protection of lawful project purchasing and its rejection of procurement labels as sufficient remain intact. No universal one-project restriction is added.

#### Law After Decision

Federal courts may hear this confined prospective challenge to the implementing Secretary. Executive Order 12954's challenged replacement-based restriction cannot operate under the general procurement delegation. Lawful proprietary measures and centralized purchasing standards remain subject to their own statutory justification; no entitlement to a government contract follows. The Court adds this statute-specific limit without changing Dalton's actual preclusion rule or deciding presidential APA status.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1197.
**Decided:** November 14, 1994.

##### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Holding:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 440 U.S. 391 (1979), applies its entity-specific compact inquiry: exercising state power does not itself make a bistate corporation the States, and the compact’s actual structure matters.
- *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), preserves the distinction between a separate public entity and an arm of a State; a governmental function alone supplies no immunity.
- *Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), is distinguished as deciding federal-forum consent while assuming, rather than deciding, PATH’s arm status. Its consent ruling is not an earlier holding that PATH is immune.
- *Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 502 U.S. 197 (1991), preserves state-court FELA coverage without constructive waiver of federal-forum immunity; no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged here.
- *Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc.*, 506 U.S. 139 (1993), preserves immediate review of a qualifying immunity denial without establishing that a particular entity is an immune arm. Its appealability holding does not decide PATH’s status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both the Hess and Walsh immunity dismissals are reversed and remanded. The Eleventh Amendment bar is removed; ordinary nonimmunity defenses and the separate actions' merits remain undecided.

#### Behrens v. Pelletier, 516 U.S. 299

##### A prior appeal does not categorically exhaust legal-immunity review

**Holding:** An earlier pleading-stage qualified-immunity appeal does not, merely because it occurred, bar a later summary-judgment appeal presenting a separable legal immunity question that otherwise satisfies the collateral-order requirements. The later appeal may address legal immunity on properly assumed facts; this rule authorizes neither factual-sufficiency review nor repeated appeals of an unchanged legal ruling or matters resolved by the first mandate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen's cumulative conditions and Mitchell's legal-immunity category apply. Digital Equipment's importance and category discipline remains; Johnson supplies no numerical ceiling and retains its factual boundary.

##### An authorized appeal uses assumed facts and excludes factual sufficiency

**Holding:** Within an otherwise authorized qualified-immunity appeal, the appellate court may decide whether conduct on the District Court's properly assumed or sufficiently supported facts violates clearly established law, but may not choose which factual account is true or reweigh whether evidence supports it. If the relevant assumptions are unstated, the court may identify the likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant in the manner Johnson permits, without deciding truth or evidentiary sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, unanimous. Stevens and Breyer condition agreement on an otherwise authorized appeal and do not find successive authorization here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Johnson and Kimberlin preserve assumed-fact review without factual selection. Elder requires relevant conduct-date law but creates no jurisdiction or retrospective notice. Swint rejects convenience-based expansion; Doe's factual-sufficiency exclusion remains.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No immunity, constitutional violation, property interest, factual truth, evidentiary sufficiency or damages issue is decided.

##### A different surviving claim does not itself defeat a qualifying immunity appeal

**Holding:** The fact that an official must still defend another claim does not, by itself, eliminate an otherwise authorized immediate appeal of a separable legal immunity denial on a different claim. That claim-specific principle neither supplies missing appellate authorization nor makes the other claim reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join; Stevens and Breyer continue to oppose successive appeal as of right.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitchell protects against defending the claim to which immunity applies; Swint prevents a proper appeal from carrying unrelated matters and leaves its necessary-connected-review reservation open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the categorical Ninth Circuit dismissal and remand to identify proper assumptions favorable to the nonmovant and any actually reviewable legal issue, dismissing factual-sufficiency and otherwise unavailable issues. The summary-judgment denial is not reversed, immunity is not awarded, and neither the separate United States negligence claim nor earlier substitution ruling is reopened. No stay or permission is granted.
