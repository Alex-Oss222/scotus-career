# 1997-06-16 — Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey; 95 F.3d 1422 (9th Cir. 1996); Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-16248 and 94-16313; district court No. CV-86-05042-CAL

Authority: [Record](../records/Del_Monte_Dunes_at_Monterey_Ltd_v_City_of_Monterey_merits_1997-06-16.md).

## Event

Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey; Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-16248 and 94-16313, 95 F.3d 1422; district court No. CV-86-05042-CAL. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Certiorari granted January 13, 1997. Submitted and decided June 16, 1997.

Following repeated proposals to develop 37.6 oceanfront acres and the City's final denial, the owners obtained separate takings and equal-protection liability verdicts supporting one $1,450,000 award. The Ninth Circuit's September 13, 1996 judgment sustained the taking claim and the rejection of post-trial relief; it did not reach the City's preserved equal-protection objections. Further appellate and rehearing proceedings were held in abeyance pending this review by a separate appellate order. The grant itself did not remove appellate jurisdiction. The adverse substantive-due-process ruling was not appealed. The owners waived their additional-damages cross-appeal only if the judgment was affirmed.

The questions are whether this §1983 damages action carries a Seventh Amendment jury right on the disputed taking facts; whether the repeated denials can constitute a taking through complete loss of economically viable use or the then-governing substantial-advancement formulation; and whether damages are available through §1983 rather than only prospective or administrative relief. No separate equal-protection merits question is presented.

## Participation

At submission and decision, all nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No Justice is disqualified or otherwise absent. The Court has a quorum.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment on the three questions presented and preserves the existing compensatory award.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The $1,450,000 award and denial of the City's new-trial motion stand within the questions reviewed. Affirmance activates the owners' conditional waiver of additional damages. No new award, permit, equal-protection merits judgment or reinstatement of the unappealed substantive-due-process claim is ordered. The opposing Justices would vacate and remand for independent judicial taking findings, with lawful use of the existing record and further consideration of preserved equal-protection objections if necessary to sustain the common award.

## Holdings

### I. The final decision and the scope of review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definitive municipal decision fixing the permitted use of the parcel satisfies the final-decision requirement; later valuation does not make that decision provisional. This claim may proceed on the established absence of an adequate state compensation route for the challenged period, without eliminating any compensation prerequisite in other cases.

**Authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, I–III, is joined by all nine Justices; that common coalition controls Holdings I–III.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson County requires a final application of land-use rules and an adequate-compensation inquiry. Suitum separates a definitive use decision from valuation and ultimate liability. The City's fixed position and the earlier finality proceedings establish the former here; the case proceeded on the stated compensation-route predicate. Neither finality nor the futility of another proposal proves a taking. The grant presents the taking, jury and damages questions, while the City's preserved equal-protection objections and the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling retain their distinct status.

### II. A permit denial is not subject to Dolan's dedication burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The government must establish nexus and individualized rough proportionality when it demands conveyance of identified property interests as a permit condition. That special burden does not govern this ordinary permit-denial claim; the Ninth Circuit's contrary proportionality discussion supplies no independent ground to disturb the judgment because it was not part of the jury instructions and was unnecessary to verdict review.

**Controlling explanation:** Dolan protects the owner's interest against compelled dedication; it does not impose a government justification burden upon every unsuccessful development proposal. Prior dedication discussions do not change the action actually challenged. The jury instead received the Agins-based reasonable-relationship instruction and the complete-economic-deprivation alternative. Lucas requires proof of complete loss of economically beneficial use, after which the government must substantiate a background property or nuisance limitation that independently excluded the use; a newly declared environmental harm is insufficient by itself. Penn Central remains applicable to partial deprivation. Legitimate environmental aims, repeated rejection and a later sale price are relevant without conclusively proving or defeating either submitted taking theory. The Court does not replace Agins's received formulation with a new general rule.

### III. Compensation remains available through the established municipal action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 permits compensatory damages for a proved taking attributable to a municipality's own qualifying policy or final decision, subject to the action's constitutional and procedural predicates. Recovery is limited to the legally attributable extent and duration of the deprivation, accounting for sale proceeds or other recovery to prevent duplication; prospective relief alone does not extinguish a completed compensable injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell requires the municipality's own policy or final decision and causation, rather than liability solely for employing an official. McMillian preserves the function-specific inquiry into the institution represented by a final policymaker; it supplies no new finding about an unidentified Monterey official. First English permits compensation for the actual period of a temporary taking. Franklin distinguishes available relief in an established action from creation of a new action or proof of loss. Those principles defeat a categorical limit to prospective or administrative relief without guaranteeing anticipated profits, treating ordinary permit processing as compensable, or dispensing with proof. The questions reviewed establish no basis for a second damages amount or a construction order.

### IV. The statutory wording alone does not resolve jury entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983's provision for legal, equitable and other proper redress does not by itself confer a statutory jury right. Any jury entitlement in the present action must therefore be established under the Seventh Amendment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this statutory ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lorillard's inference from another statute depended on incorporated enforcement procedures as well as the availability of legal relief. Section 1983's remedial wording alone does not provide that answer. This conclusion leaves the damages action available and requires the separate constitutional inquiry; it neither decides that inquiry against the owner nor converts legal compensation into equitable relief.

### V. This legal §1983 action carries the constitutional jury right

**Holding and operative rule:** A §1983 action against a municipality seeking compensatory legal damages for violation of federally protected rights sounds in tort and falls within the legal actions protected by the Seventh Amendment. Its legal character does not disappear because the particular federal right concerns property or because an inverse-condemnation remedy could otherwise exist; equitable-only claims, sovereign immunity and allocation of subsidiary tasks remain separate questions.

**Authority:** Parts V–VII of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supply five direct joins for Holdings V–VII. No vote from a separate rationale is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Imbler, Carey and Memphis Community School District identify the tort character and compensatory purpose of §1983 relief. Curtis protects the jury in statutory tort actions seeking legal relief; Tull and Terry direct attention to the actual action and remedy. The present action seeks compensation for the municipality's alleged completed federal violation and proceeds on the absence of an adequate state compensation route. Calling the underlying injury a taking does not remove the statutory action's legal character. The contrary comparison to ordinary condemnation does not overcome that character here. The holding does not settle every inverse-condemnation proceeding, authorize damages against an immune sovereign, dispense with an underlying violation, or assign every subsidiary legal issue to a jury.

### VI. The disputed tasks submitted here belong to the jury

**Holding and operative rule:** Under legal standards stated by the judge, the jury may decide whether this parcel retained economically viable use and whether this particular final denial, in its development history, bore the required relationship to the City's specified legitimate purposes. The judge determines governing law, the relevant property interest, legal legitimacy of the purposes and evidentiary sufficiency; the jury may not choose a constitutional standard or revise general land-use policy.

**Controlling explanation:** Markman separately examines the action's jury status and allocation of its tasks. Its assignment of patent meaning, including embedded technical and credibility issues, to the judge remains intact. This permit denial's practical effects and highly particularized application of the stated purposes present different work. The actual instructions use the received Agins reasonable-relationship formulation, not Dolan's proportionality burden. The absence of objection to that wording does not waive the City's allocation objection, which the Court rejects on its merits. The decision does not comprehensively decide the full validity or elements of every substantial-advancement formulation, general zoning challenges, all as-applied regulations or all condemnation proceedings.

### VII. The supported taking verdict sustains the single award

**Holding and operative rule:** The City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law or a new trial on the reviewed grounds because sufficient evidence supports both undifferentiated taking alternatives under the instructions actually given. The taking verdict therefore sustains the existing single $1,450,000 compensatory award without decision of the separate equal-protection theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The owners offered evidence that the combined beach/access, viewshed and habitat requirements left no feasible development area and that further proposals could not satisfy the City's fixed position. Their evidence also addressed compliance with the fifteen conditions, prior grading/restoration approval, favorable habitat evidence and the City's role in the access difficulty. Crediting that evidence and reasonable favorable inferences supports the complete-deprivation and reasonable-relationship findings without reweighing credibility. No substantiated antecedent title limitation independently requires the entire deprivation found. The sale to California for $800,000 above the purchase price is relevant to value and compensation, but does not conclusively establish a productive use during the earlier period or payment for its loss. Neither denial of the preferred housing project alone nor disagreement among environmental experts would suffice. Support for both taking alternatives avoids any need to decide the preserved equal-protection objections; the award is not sustained by assuming those objections were abandoned. Affirmance gives effect to the owners' conditional additional-damages waiver and leaves the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling untouched.

## Law After Decision

Municipal §1983 compensation claims of the kind presented carry a constitutional jury right, with law and legally legitimate purposes supplied by the judge and these concrete factual applications entrusted to the jury. The existing verdict and award stand under the actual instructions. The statutory language alone establishes no jury right. Ordinary exactions, complete deprivation, partial deprivation, finality, municipal attribution and compensation remain distinct. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer.** The closer analogue is the constitutional compensation proceeding in direct condemnation, which has no constitutional jury entitlement merely because money is sought. Section 1983 supplies a remedy for that right without changing the specific analogue. Markman requires a task-specific inquiry even within a legal action; these Justices would require the judge to determine independently whether a compensable taking occurred. They do not make disputed historical facts intrinsically legal, foreclose an advisory jury or require a new evidentiary trial where the existing record permits lawful findings and conclusions. They join the Court's ordinary-denial limit on Dolan, its other common-law propositions and the statutory-language ruling. Dolan's unnecessary appellate discussion is not an independent ground for their remand. They would preserve the separate equal-protection verdict but require resolution of the City's outstanding objections if necessary to sustain the common award. Their disposition would not activate the additional-damages waiver, order a permit or reinstate substantive due process; nor would it automatically extinguish the award.

# 1997-06-16 — Fletcher v. Kalina; 93 F.3d 653 (9th Cir. 1996); Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129

Authority: [Record](../records/Fletcher_v_Kalina_merits_1997-06-16.md).

## Event

Fletcher v. Kalina; Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129, 93 F.3d 653. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Submitted and decided June 16, 1997.

Deputy prosecutor Lynne Kalina prepared an information, a motion for an arrest warrant and a sworn probable-cause certification accusing Rodney Fletcher of a school burglary. Fletcher alleges that the certification wrongly denied his legitimate school access, although he had installed glass carrying his fingerprints, and incorrectly asserted an identification despite unsuccessful photographic identifications described in police reports. A warrant issued; charges were later dismissed. These are allegations in an immunity appeal, not findings of intentional falsity.

The district court denied absolute immunity and left qualified immunity unresolved on disputed facts. The Ninth Circuit's August 22, 1996 decision sustained the denial. The questions concern absolute immunity for a prosecutor's personal factual certification, the complaining-witness analogy, the distinction between legal preparation/presentation and personal swearing, and possible personal liability for material falsehoods or omissions. The fourth question does not convert this interlocutory appeal into a final liability determination.

## Participation

At submission and decision, all nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. There is no nonparticipation and a quorum is present.

## Public Action

The Court affirms denial of absolute prosecutorial immunity for Kalina's personally sworn certification, clarifies the protection of distinct advocacy and returns the case for the unresolved proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed and remanded, 9–0, within the personal-certification scope.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower courts must separate claims resting on the personal oath from claims resting only on protected preparation or presentation. No finding of intentional falsehood, constitutional liability, loss of qualified immunity or damages is entered. No automatic trial is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Full affirmance/remand | Complete functional-immunity and remaining-defense rationale |
| Same opinion, personal oath and bounded remand | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela | Full judgment through his express current-law alternative | Joins the oath classification, protection for ordinary distinct advocacy, remaining liability elements and mandate; does not join protection for knowing material falsification in an otherwise immune function or endorse ordinary qualified immunity as his proposed rule |
| Concurrence | Scalia | Thomas | Joins the Court in full | Common-law categories, stare decisis and the limited warrant-procurement analogy |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins the specified grounds and accepts the current-law remand | Proposes a knowing-material-falsification exception and a narrower reasonable-reliance defense |

The oath rule and actual mandate have nine direct votes. The complete rules preserving advocacy immunity despite alleged dishonesty and ordinary qualified-immunity doctrine have eight Associate rationale votes.

## Holdings

### I. Personal factual attestation is a witness function

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness, and absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect that personal certification. The actual function controls regardless of whether the statement ultimately proves true or false; applying for a warrant without personally attesting facts does not itself make an advocate a witness.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Court opinion, joined on this proposition by all nine Justices, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley requires classification of the particular act, preserving advocacy while withholding absolute protection from ordinary evidence gathering. Antoine requires a historically and functionally justified immunity rather than protection based only on importance to adjudication. Malley's warrant-affiant rule supplies the close comparison here. Kalina did more than evaluate another person's evidence or argue that it established probable cause: she personally supplied its sworn truth assurance. That undertaking is distinct even when performed alongside initiation of prosecution. The whole-transaction argument would extend advocacy immunity to an evidentiary function because of its setting. The Court instead preserves protection for each genuine advocacy function and denies it only for the separately assumed personal oath. The holding neither decides ordinary courtroom witness immunity nor makes every sworn statement actionable.

### II. Distinct preparation and presentation retain their protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Preparing and filing the information and warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions and presenting the State's legal case retain absolute prosecutorial immunity when performed in the advocate's capacity. Alleged bad purpose or false evidence does not itself remove that function's protection, and a separate unprotected personal certification does not strip immunity from all other acts in the charging transaction.

**Authority:** Kennedy and the other seven Associate Justices join the complete rule: eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela joins protection for ordinary distinct advocacy but proposes an exception for knowing material falsification and does not join the rule to that extent.

**Controlling explanation:** Imbler protects initiation and presentation of a prosecution; Burns distinguishes protected courtroom advocacy from other functions. Buckley preserves genuine advocacy preparation and rejects a single classification for all acts before or after a charge. A witness's personal oath is not the same function as preparing that witness's evidence for legal presentation. Claims must therefore connect the alleged injury to the actual challenged act. The contrary effort to make every document in the charging package unprotected would disregard the same functional method that denies immunity for the oath. The Court adopts no general dishonesty exception to otherwise absolute immunity and changes no separate constitutional privilege.

### III. Denial of absolute immunity leaves liability and personal defenses unresolved

**Holding and operative rule:** Fletcher must still prove an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; denial of absolute immunity for the oath establishes none of them. Kalina may invoke conduct-date qualified immunity and other available defenses, and the remand does not require a damages judgment or an automatic trial.

**Authority:** All nine join the remaining-elements requirements and actual remand. The eight Associates adopt ordinary qualified-immunity doctrine as the governing rationale; Stone-Zsela accepts its application on remand through his independently sufficient alternative while proposing a different defense.

**Controlling explanation:** Hafer permits a personal-capacity §1983 action while preserving applicable personal immunities. Malley does not treat a judicial warrant as an automatic break in causation when material deception procured it. The assessment must account for corrected false assertions and supplied material omissions; a truthful account may still establish probable cause. The alleged lawful school access and failed identifications could matter, but their constitutional significance and the required culpability are unresolved. Elder requires informed independent consideration of relevant legal authority on qualified immunity, while Lanier preserves fair warning measured when the official acted. Neither later decision retrospectively supplies notice. Disputed historical facts remain for lawful treatment below; the legal immunity question belongs to the court. This interlocutory review establishes no actual deceit, corrected absence of probable cause, damages or entitlement to judgment on the merits.

## Precedent Treatment

- Imbler is preserved for genuine prosecutorial advocacy, including the inherited protection despite alleged dishonesty; the Court adopts no general falsification exception.
- Burns supplies the distinction between courtroom advocacy and different functions; institutional importance alone does not control.
- Buckley is applied to the actual function while preserving genuine preparation and rejecting categorical pre-charge/post-charge classification.
- Antoine supplies the historical/functional justification requirement and rejects indispensability as sufficient for absolute immunity.
- Malley supplies the warrant-affiant comparison and preserves the separate constitutional-liability and causation requirements.
- Hafer preserves personal-capacity liability and applicable personal defenses without converting the action into a claim against the State.
- Elder requires consideration of relevant precedent on the preserved immunity question and leaves conduct-date notice and ordinary factual treatment intact.
- Lanier supplies the conduct-date fair-warning distinction, without providing retrospective notice or resolving qualified immunity here.

## Law After Decision

Prosecutors must distinguish their personal evidentiary oath from preparing and presenting the State's legal case. The former has no absolute prosecutorial immunity; the latter retains its functional protection. The claim may proceed only through the remaining constitutional and personal-defense requirements. No precedent is overruled, no general witness-immunity exception is adopted and the Court's qualified-immunity standard remains unchanged.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring, joined by Thomas.** The functional doctrine does not reproduce every category of common-law tort immunity exactly. The privilege for witness testimony historically concerned defamation, whereas a complaining witness could incur malicious-prosecution liability for procuring proceedings without testifying personally. Scalia's Burns analysis distinguishes those functions; Imbler and the Court's functional precedents remain binding on stare decisis grounds. Malley's warrant-affiant treatment supports the result here without implying that every sworn statement defeats witness immunity or that ordinary defamation becomes a constitutional tort. Scalia and Thomas join the entire Court opinion and add no new liability element.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The personal oath is an evidentiary undertaking for which §1983 requires accountability, while distinct ordinary advocacy retains its protection. Beyond that common ground, he would exclude knowingly false material factual assertions or deliberate evidentiary fabrication causing an independently actionable deprivation from absolute immunity, including when committed through advocacy or testimony. The proposed exception would not reach honest error, adverse credibility findings, disputed inferences or merely mistaken legal judgment; unrelated advocacy and any independently sourced constitutional privilege would keep their protection. To the extent Imbler shields such knowing falsification, he would depart from that rule because intentional corruption of the factual predicate defeats §1983 accountability and carries no legitimate reliance interest.

For personal damages he would decide the merits first and place on the official the burden of establishing genuine reasonable reliance on binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the actual conduct. General office authority, lack of an identical precedent and a warrant obtained through the official's own knowing material falsehood would not suffice. Those proposals command no additional join. He therefore accepts denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath and remand under the Court's existing qualified-immunity law as an independently sufficient alternative, without finding falsity or awarding damages.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for the remaining merits and personal defenses. The lower courts must preserve immunity for protected advocacy and evaluate liability only for an actionable unprotected function. Qualified immunity is neither granted nor denied; additional factfinding or trial is required only if ordinary adjudication warrants it. The Court orders no damages and no renewed prosecution.

The [Ninth Circuit opinion, 93 F.3d 653](https://openjurist.org/93/f3d/653/fletcher-v-kalina) supplies the interlocutory posture and alleged certification errors. Its isolated reference to a search warrant does not change the described arrest-warrant transaction. The alleged inaccuracies are not adjudicated findings of deliberate falsehood. Submitted June 16, 1997; no separate oral-argument date is supplied. No later adjudication of Kalina's conduct supplies the law or facts of this decision.

# 1997-06-16 — Piscataway Township Board of Education v. Taxman; 91 F.3d 1547 (3d Cir. 1996) (en banc); Third Circuit Nos. 94-5090 and 94-5112

Authority: [Record](../records/Piscataway_Township_Board_of_Education_v_Taxman_merits_1997-06-16.md).

## Event

Piscataway Township Board of Education v. Taxman; Third Circuit Nos. 94-5090 and 94-5112, 91 F.3d 1547 (en banc). On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Submitted and decided June 16, 1997.

A necessary reduction eliminated one business-department teaching position. Sharon Taxman and Debra Williams had equivalent qualifications and seniority. To retain Williams, the department's only Black teacher, the Board used race instead of its previously used lottery. The Board expressly relied on departmental diversity rather than a demonstrated remedial imbalance or its own past discrimination. The en banc judgment of August 8, 1996, amended August 21, sustained liability and relief under Title VII and the New Jersey Law Against Discrimination.

The questions are whether Title VII permits this diversity-based racial choice between equally qualified and senior employees; whether voluntary affirmative-action permission requires the remedial connection described in Weber and Johnson; whether the discharge unnecessarily burdens the employee's protected interests; and whether educational constitutional diversity principles supply statutory employment permission. No constitutional claim was before the lower court. Taxman had been rehired before the damages trial, so reinstatement is not at issue.

## Participation

At submission and decision, all nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No nonparticipation is established; a quorum is present.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the reviewed judgment on two independently sufficient statutory grounds and decides no equal-protection question.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Both the absence of a permissible remedial premise and the unnecessary burden of this layoff independently sustain federal liability. The $134,014.62 federal award for backpay, benefits and prejudgment interest, the $10,000 state-law emotional-suffering award and restored seniority remain in place. There is no new reinstatement order. The denials of a broader prospective injunction and punitive damages are undisturbed. The opposing Justices would vacate federal liability and dependent relief and remand for application of a statutory rule allowing consideration of a genuinely supported educational-integration purpose. They would not decide that this layoff was lawful. The independent NJLAD ground would remain for identification of the relief it supports; neither automatic cancellation of all relief nor unconditional preservation of relief dependent solely on the vacated federal determination would follow.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, I | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Common statutory/constitutional distinction | Intentional racial selection, actual Hopwood authority and separate statutory permission; no constitutional decision |
| Same opinion, II–III | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Two independent grounds for affirmance | Remedial boundary and unnecessary trammeling |
| Same opinion, IV | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in full; Stone-Zsela as to federal relief and the judgment, with the state-component limit below | Implements affirmance | Actual awards, seniority and nonreach; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join only the general federal/state separation principle |
| Concurrence | Scalia | No other Justice | Joins the Court in full | Textual objection and reservation concerning Weber's interpretation |
| Concurrence | Thomas | No other Justice | Joins the Court in full | Individual statutory protection without endorsement of broader racial-remedy theories |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Would vacate federal liability and dependent relief and remand | Possible educational-integration justification and actual statutory application; no present no-liability ruling; independent NJLAD treatment preserved |

Stone-Zsela concurs in the disposition of the independent NJLAD liability and $10,000 state-law award without joining an additional state-law rationale. All five members of the federal majority directly join each independently sufficient Title VII holding. No combination of separate writings supplies Court law.

## Holdings

### I. Statutory permission and constitutional permission remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Selecting which equally qualified and equally senior employee loses an existing position because of race requires permission under Title VII; the employment tie does not remove the discrimination question. Constitutional recognition of a diversity interest does not itself create an exception to that employment statute, and this statutory case decides no equal-protection question.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Court opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII protects individuals against covered employment decisions because of race. Weber and Johnson recognize limited voluntary affirmative action without requiring an adjudication that the employer itself violated the statute. That accommodation must be examined on its own terms. Hopwood now affirmatively holds, by six direct votes, that concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse public-university student body can supply a compelling interest on a supported record. It requires substantiation of the educational objective and narrow fit of the actual means; a diversity label, racial balance or assumed racial viewpoint is insufficient. Its unanimous invalidation of the separate admissions machinery illustrates that an available interest does not establish lawful operation.

Hopwood expressly reserved race-conscious employment and other educational levels. Its university holding neither amends Title VII nor establishes the educational benefit or statutory justification for this faculty layoff. Nor is that reservation a constitutional holding against faculty diversity. Hopwood preserved Bakke's actual five-Justice competitive-consideration proposition and adopted its own more specific affirmative rule; the Court does not reduce the current rule to unresolved Bakke discussion. Adarand's distinct federal constitutional standard supplies no local employment exemption. Hopwood's remedial-duty discussion supplies no unasserted continuing violation or imbalance here; its Title VI intent and admissions same-decision/remedy rules do not replace the distinct employment inquiries. No constitutional claim is decided and no admissions proof burden is imported.

### II. This nonremedial layoff lies outside the recognized accommodation

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VII's voluntary-affirmative-action accommodation does not authorize choosing an incumbent for discharge because of race solely to preserve departmental diversity, without the remedial connection to a manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category described in Weber and Johnson. A qualifying remedial plan need not rest on proof that this employer committed an actionable past violation; diversity alone does not supply the missing remedial premise here.

**Authority:** Parts II–III of O'Connor's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supply five direct joins for this holding and the independent holding below.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber permits a bounded effort to open traditionally segregated employment, and Johnson permits voluntary action addressing manifest imbalance while preserving individual interests. Neither makes every beneficial racial objective sufficient to justify a race-selected discharge. The Board claims no remedial imbalance under those standards. Its strongest argument combines the equal employment tie with a continuing educational benefit and Hopwood's recognition that a supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available. Hopwood's employment reservation does not decide this statutory question against the Board, but its affirmative university rule does not enact the requested employment exception. Equal competence does not supply the statutory connection that distinguishes the recognized accommodation from a general desirable-purpose exception. Preserving one department's existing racial composition cannot alone carry that burden. The Court preserves Weber and Johnson and does not decide that only judicially proved discrimination can justify voluntary action or that every remedial layoff arrangement is forbidden.

### III. This discharge independently imposes an unnecessary burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming a diversity purpose could qualify under Title VII, this nonremedial policy unnecessarily trammels the displaced employee's protected interests by assigning her the loss of an existing tenured job because of race, without a remedial limiting goal and despite an available nonracial tie procedure. This record-specific burden independently establishes the violation; it does not invalidate every race-conscious arrangement involving some burden or every remedial layoff plan.

**Controlling explanation:** Weber and Johnson require attention to the interests of employees who bear a voluntary plan's costs. Losing an existing job is a concentrated injury, not merely exclusion from one competitive opportunity. The staff reduction makes one loss necessary, but it does not make selecting the person by race necessary. The Board had previously used a lottery to resolve an equal-seniority tie and could use a nonracial procedure here. Equal qualifications and seniority do not erase Taxman's interest in that equal chance. Hopwood likewise distinguishes a protected opportunity interest from entitlement to ultimate success; that distinction informs the comparison without itself deciding the statutory burden here. Unlike a remedial plan with a limiting objective tied to employment exclusion, the stated departmental-diversity policy supplies no such limiting goal. The Court does not hold that all hiring preferences are permissible or that every layoff arrangement is impermissible; the necessity and justification of this burden are assessed in this actual policy and record.

### IV. The actual relief follows the distinct federal and state judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** Affirmance preserves the reviewed federal liability and $134,014.62 backpay, benefits and prejudgment-interest award, the independent NJLAD liability and $10,000 emotional-suffering award, and restored seniority. Taxman's earlier rehiring makes a new reinstatement order unnecessary; the denials of a broader injunction and punitive damages remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** The five judgment supporters sustain this disposition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join its complete rationale; Stone-Zsela joins the federal rationale and concurs only in the disposition of the independent state-law component. All eight Associates independently join the principle that federal and state liability and remedial sources must remain distinct; no new NJLAD merits rule is announced.

**Controlling explanation:** The record identifies separate statutory sources and separate damage components. Neither the federal violation nor its review turns the state emotional-suffering award into federal damages. Because the federal judgment is affirmed, no reassessment based on loss of that federal premise is required. The Court grants no additional recovery and decides no unpresented interest-rate, punitive-damages or state-law construction question. Treating the already rehired employee as requiring a new reinstatement command would misstate both the judgment and the available relief.

## Precedent Treatment

- United Steelworkers v. Weber remains binding and supplies the remedial accommodation and protection of affected employees; the Court declines to extend it to this nonremedial discharge.
- Johnson v. Transportation Agency remains binding, including manifest-imbalance permission without proof of the employer's own actionable violation and the no-unnecessary-trammeling limit. Neither independent holding overrules it.
- Hopwood v. Texas, November 25, 1996, retains its affirmative six-Justice rule permitting a concretely supported university educational-diversity interest, its strict scrutiny of actual means and burdens, and its unanimous invalidation of the separate admissions machinery. Its express employment reservation is respected without treating it as a prohibition. Its Fordice, Title VI, admissions burden and remedy components supply no substitute statutory predicate, burden or remedy here.
- Bakke retains the actual five-Justice Part V-C competitive-consideration proposition recognized in Hopwood; neither all of Powell's reasoning nor Hopwood's later affirmative rule is attributed to an earlier majority.
- Adarand's rule for the supported federal constitutional arrangement supplies no local employment exemption or amendment to Title VII. Its actual federal holding remains intact.
- Wygant's attention to concrete burdens informs the competing positions, while its constitutional questions and separate writings are not treated as a controlling Title VII disposition.

## Law After Decision

A public employer cannot invoke the voluntary-affirmative-action accommodation to select an equally qualified incumbent for discharge solely to preserve departmental diversity on this nonremedial record. The absence of the recognized remedial connection and the unnecessary burden independently sustain the judgment. Manifest-imbalance remediation remains available under Weber and Johnson, without a requirement to confess or prove the employer's own violation. The Court adopts neither a universal prohibition on remedial layoff plans nor a constitutional rule against diversity. Hopwood's affirmative public-university educational-interest holding and actual-means requirements remain intact; this employment decision does not extend its constitutional rule into Title VII or import its admissions burdens and remedies. Federal and state relief retain their separate sources.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring.** Title VII's individual prohibition on discharge because of race is sufficient to reject the proposed diversity extension. His Johnson dissent explains why desirable representational objectives cannot replace the enacted text. He recognizes Hopwood's binding affirmative university rule while preserving the contrary position stated in Section A of his separate writing there; that opposition is not Court law. Hopwood expressly reserved employment, and its equal-opportunity holding also confirms that an exact tie does not erase the individual's protected interest. Scalia would reconsider Weber's textual departure in an appropriate case, but joins both present grounds as sufficient applications of existing law. No overruling, constitutional decision or punitive-culpability finding is needed.

**Thomas, concurring.** The statutory protection belongs to the employee facing discharge. The asserted departmental benefit does not itself answer why this employee may bear a racial job loss, and the absence even of Weber and Johnson's recognized premise suffices. Thomas retains his objection to nonremedial preferences from Hopwood's Section A without denying the binding force of the Court's different university rule. His join in Hopwood's bounded remedial-duty holding distinguishes him from Scalia on that separate issue: he does not reject every supported remedial measure or an established dismantlement duty. No such predicate is asserted here. He joins the complete Court opinion, and his broader reservation supplies no new constitutional holding or overruling.

**Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** Weber and Johnson identify sufficient remedial purposes without excluding every forward-looking educational justification. Stevens's Johnson concurrence and faculty-specific Wygant dissent support allowing a public educational employer to offer a genuinely supported integration purpose. Hopwood's affirmative university-interest holding reinforces that possibility by analogy, while its actual-process invalidity and attention to individual burdens show why an available purpose does not establish that a particular practice is lawful. Its employment reservation leaves the Title VII question open; it does not answer it for either party.

The dissent would reject both categorical exclusion of the proposed nonremedial purpose and a rule treating this layoff burden as dispositive without the full statutory application. Equal qualifications and seniority, the necessary reduction and preservation of the department's sole Black teacher are relevant, but establish neither absence of a protected employee interest nor the lawfulness of the Board's choice. The proper purpose-and-burden inquiry would consider the actual educational benefit and connection to the practice, the serious loss of a tenured job, racial selection, the available nonracial tie procedure and whether the measure serves confined integration or racial representation for its own sake. The dissent adopts no general diversity exemption, racial-viewpoint assumption, imported strict-scrutiny or admissions burden, mandatory study, numerical test or automatic termination date. A continuing educational purpose cannot excuse a perpetual racial quota.

These Justices would vacate federal liability and dependent relief and remand for application of that Title VII construction to the actual record. They would not declare the Board's conduct lawful or require a new evidentiary hearing; ordinary proceedings could still yield liability. The independent NJLAD ground and $10,000 state award would not be reversed merely because the federal rule changed. The lower courts would identify which monetary and seniority relief that ground independently sustains, neither cancelling all relief automatically nor preserving relief dependent solely on a vacated federal determination unconditionally. No new reinstatement, punitive award, broader injunction or constitutional claim would follow.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed judgment and existing awards and seniority relief stand. Taxman's prior rehiring is not altered by a new reinstatement command. No broader injunction, punitive damages, additional damages or constitutional ruling issues. The mandate implements affirmance.

# 1997-06-16 — Salazar-Haro v. INS; 95 F.3d 309 (3d Cir. 1996); Third Circuit No. 96-3007

Authority: [Record](../records/Salazar_Haro_v_INS_merits_1997-06-16.md).

## Event

Salazar-Haro v. INS; Third Circuit No. 96-3007, 95 F.3d 309. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Submitted and decided June 16, 1997.

Pedro Alejandro Salazar-Haro, a Peruvian citizen who entered in 1978 and obtained permanent-resident status in 1983, was convicted in June 1993 of cocaine-distribution conspiracy and aiding and abetting and received forty-two months' imprisonment. His wife and codefendant was deported in 1994; their three sons were United States citizens. The immigration judge considered rehabilitation, remorse, education, employment, care for the children and cooperation with the Government, but denied discretionary relief in light of sustained and renewed trafficking and the admission that he sold more than ten kilograms of cocaine. The Board of Immigration Appeals rejected the appeal.

The petition for judicial review was filed January 17, 1996, before AEDPA's April 24 enactment. On September 13, 1996, the Third Circuit dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. Its observation that it would have denied relief on the merits if it had jurisdiction was hypothetical, not a merits judgment. The final administrative order predates September 30, 1996.

The questions are whether §440(a) withdraws jurisdiction over this pending qualifying deportation petition, whether withdrawal of ordinary review violates due process or constitutional limits on jurisdiction, and what review outside that channel remains available when the Constitution requires it. Those questions are addressed within this record, which identifies no distinct legal defect apart from disagreement with the discretionary balance.

## Participation

At submission and decision, all nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No nonparticipation is established; a quorum is present.

## Public Action

The Court affirms dismissal for lack of ordinary direct-review jurisdiction. The judgment does not approve the agency's discretionary balance or determine an unfiled collateral proceeding.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 9–0, solely on direct-review jurisdiction.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The ordinary-review proceeding ends. No waiver, reopening, new administrative procedure, habeas relief, transfer, stay or direction to execute removal is ordered. The Third Circuit's hypothetical merits footnote is not adopted. The Court leaves the availability and requirements of an unfiled collateral proceeding unresolved; Stone-Zsela would expressly recognize qualifying §2241 review on the terms of his separate writing.

## Holdings

### I. IIRIRA's transition provisions do not decide AEDPA's separate restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** The saved former procedure governs this old deportation proceeding, subject to applicable transition exceptions, while §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not itself withdraw review of the completed-adjudication claims presented here. That conclusion preserves no exemption from AEDPA §440(a), whose separate temporal operation must be decided.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, has eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this transition disposition and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Yang supplies the April 1, 1997 commencement rule; Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto apply the corrected §§306 and 309 and distinguish the three identified executive decisions or actions—commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing orders—from review of a completed legal adjudication. Corrected §309(c)(1) preserves proceedings begun before the new scheme's commencement subject to the particular exceptions. Paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only when the evidentiary hearing has not commenced, with notice at least thirty days before it; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; this earlier order does not meet that trigger. Section 306(c)(2) protects specified existing §106 judgments and orders from invalidation or required reconsideration under paragraph (1), but creates no jurisdiction. The date of this Court's consideration does not apply the permanent scheme wholesale, and these transition savings do not nullify AEDPA's independent bar.

### II. Section 440(a) withdraws this pending ordinary-review channel

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA §440(a) applies to this qualifying final deportation order despite the January 17, 1996 petition because withdrawal of future ordinary appellate review attaches no new legal disability to a protected completed event established by this record. Filing, prior conviction, family hardship and an expectation of favorable review alone establish no such disability; the agency had already considered discretionary waiver under the former law.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf first asks whether Congress prescribed temporal reach and otherwise examines the actual new legal consequence attached to a completed event; a jurisdictional label does not substitute for that inquiry. Rivers preserves the provision-specific method. Section 440(f)'s express timing rule concerns subsection (e), not a saving clause for subsection (a). Hincapie-Nieto applies §440(a) to pending ordinary review where filing and expectation alone identify no protected completed choice. Reyes-Hernandez differs because an uncontested surrender of a colorable defense acquired a new consequence; that is one possible protected predicate, not a universal prerequisite or a rule excluding every other completed event. No such distinguishing event is established here. Past convictions already made Salazar-Haro deportable, and the agency actually considered his waiver request under the earlier law. This is not a new §440(d) eligibility denial, and that subsection's separate operation is not decided. The Court adopts neither a blanket exemption for all pending petitions nor a categorical rule that every jurisdictional amendment is free from retroactivity limits.

### III. The present record does not require deciding an unfiled collateral case

**Holding and operative rule:** This record does not require preservation of ordinary appellate review for another weighing of discretionary equities, and a constitutional label or §1651 alone does not restore that jurisdiction. Affirmance neither approves eliminating every meaningful judicial forum for an actually presented legal or constitutional defect nor decides the availability, scope or custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates of immigration review under §2241.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's Court opinion has eight Associate joins. Stone-Zsela also joins the present-record conclusion against mere discretionary reweighing, while proposing an affirmative §2241 construction rather than joining its reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hincapie-Nieto distinguishes this ordinary channel from a concrete challenge that executive restraint exceeds legal authority. Here family hardship and rehabilitation were considered against repeated serious drug dealing; dissatisfaction with that balance identifies no distinct constitutional or statutory defect. The Court does not decide whether that balance was the best exercise of discretion. Felker sustained a restricted review channel alongside an actual retained original proceeding that received independent consideration. It does not establish the existence or adequacy of an imaginary immigration substitute. Flores preserves actual individual safeguards at its custody and facial-review scope without settling these different collateral questions. Because no developed legal defect demands resolution here, the Court need neither pronounce §2241 available nor declare every other forum foreclosed. Section 1651 assists existing jurisdiction rather than creating a free-standing source. No custody status, collateral filing or adequate substitute is presumed.

### IV. The mandate is jurisdictional, not a waiver judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's ordinary-review dismissal is affirmed without adopting its hypothetical merits footnote or adjudicating an unfiled collateral claim. No waiver, reopening, new hearing, transfer, stay or execution direction follows from this channel-specific decision.

**Authority:** Part IV of Breyer's Court opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The withdrawn jurisdiction cannot support an agency-merits adjudication. A prediction of what the lower court would have done had jurisdiction existed does not become a judgment for this Court to affirm. Nor does an abstract possibility of another remedy establish that a new petition has been filed, that its predicates are met or that relief should issue. Hincapie-Nieto supplies that confined disposition while leaving genuinely presented future claims to their proper statutory and constitutional analysis.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.** The relevant activity is the judicial review now requested, which the present jurisdictional enactment governs. The earlier filing cannot preserve a future ordinary appellate forum. These Justices retain the approach expressed in their Landgraf and Felker writings and their current Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto positions; they do not join the proposition that surrender of an earlier defense can preserve this future channel. **Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The completed-event inquiry permits withdrawal of direct review here: convictions already supplied deportability, the agency considered the waiver and no surrendered defense or comparable completed choice acquired a new substantive disability. But he would construe §440(a), in its INA setting and with §2241, affirmatively to preserve qualifying collateral review of substantial legal or constitutional objections to custody or deportation beyond lawful authority. Section 2241 supplies that source when custody and its other statutory requirements are satisfied; neither free-floating constitutional jurisdiction nor §1651 alone is sufficient. A retained forum must be able to test the actual legal basis of restraint and grant effective relief, rather than review detention conditions alone.

Felker supports attention to an actual retained route but does not itself settle this immigration construction.
