## Standards and Tests selection: Separate due-process and Commerce Clause connections for remote use-tax collection

### Separate due-process and Commerce Clause connections for remote use-tax collection

**Governing question:** A State's imposition of sales-or-use-tax collection duties on a remote seller.

**Current rule:** Purposeful and continuous solicitation of a State's consumers may supply due process's minimum connection without physical presence when the collection obligation is rationally related to benefits received through the market. Bellas Hess's indispensable physical-presence requirement for due process no longer governs this collection duty. The separate Commerce Clause rule remains: a seller whose customer connections are limited to mail or common-carrier commerce without the requisite local presence cannot be required to collect sales or use tax merely because purposeful market access satisfies due process. Complete Auto's substantial-nexus requirement remains distinct from its other requirements. Congress may alter dormant-Commerce constraints through valid legislation but cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Authority by component:**
- Revised due-process connection: Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, “1. Purposeful contacts can satisfy due process without physical presence”, May 26, 1992; Stevens Parts I–III, supported by all eight Associate Justices including the author; Stone does not reach it.
- Surviving specific Commerce collection boundary: Quill, “2. The specific Commerce collection precedent remains controlling,” May 26, 1992; express common narrower ground of Stevens, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Stone (eight), with the record's stated common-ground authority rather than the four-Justice Part IV policy discussion.

**Present operation effective:** Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, May 26, 1992, separating the revised due-process rule from the surviving specific Commerce collection rule.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not govern every state tax, immunize consumers from use tax, set numerical sales or property thresholds, or establish a general software-nexus rule. The particular software application does not supply an additional reusable property rule. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant.

**Holdings navigation:** Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, 504 U.S. 298 (1992) — State-tax collection and interstate commerce.

#### Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, 504 U.S. 298 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-194.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### Whether purposeful solicitation can satisfy due process for use-tax collection without physical presence

**Holding:** A mail-order seller that purposefully and continuously solicits a State’s consumers may have the minimum connection required for a use-tax collection obligation even without physical presence, when the obligation is rationally related to benefits received through that market. Quill’s sustained solicitation and approximately $1 million in annual North Dakota sales satisfy due process; Bellas Hess and earlier holdings are overruled only insofar as they make physical presence indispensable for this collection duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices. Stone expressly does not reach due process and supplies no join.

**Material application:** Sustained solicitation and about $1 million in annual North Dakota sales establish the purposeful connection; the obligation relates rationally to benefits from that market. The distinct Commerce inquiry remains necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consumer use-tax liability, other taxes, a numerical transaction or property-value threshold, a general software or physical-presence rule, and the result under future legislation are not decided. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The collection judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, under the Commerce Clause. The eight Justices reaching due process reject that challenge, but this supplies no collection liability given the Commerce disposition. No tax amount, interest, penalty, retrospective liability or §1983/§1988 entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- National Bellas Hess v. Department of Revenue, 386 U.S. 753 — overruled only to the extent its due-process holding requires physical presence for use-tax collection; its Commerce rule is treated separately below.
- Miller Brothers v. Maryland, 347 U.S. 340 — the minimum-connection requirement remains; any indispensable physical-entry implication is displaced at the stated collection scope.
- International Shoe Co. v. Washington, 326 U.S. 310 — applied for purposeful affiliation and fair connection.
- Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz, 471 U.S. 462 — its nonphysical purposeful-contact reasoning is extended by analogy to the collection obligation; every personal-jurisdiction rule is not imported.
- Shaffer v. Heitner, 433 U.S. 186 — applied by comparable reasoning against a mechanical presence prerequisite.
- Moorman Manufacturing Co. v. Bair, 437 U.S. 267 — the requirement that the tax relate rationally to state-connected values remains.

##### Whether Bellas Hess’s specific Commerce Clause collection rule remains controlling

**Holding:** Bellas Hess’s specific Commerce Clause rule continues to bar imposing a sales-or-use-tax collection duty on a seller whose customer connections are limited to mail or common-carrier commerce without the requisite local presence. Quill’s solicitation and remote deliveries do not themselves displace that rule; Complete Auto’s separate substantial-nexus requirement is not satisfied merely by purposeful market access or the due-process showing.

**Proposition-level authority:** The common, narrower ground expressly supported by Stevens, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter in Part IV, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in their concurrence, and Stone in his concurrence in the judgment; eight Justices retain the particular precedent. The full Part IV substantive policy analysis has four joins and is not an Opinion of the Court. The software application has the separate five-Justice authority identified below.

**Material application:** Complete Auto preserves substantial nexus separately from apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair relation to state services. The specific collection precedent and reliance it created persist; the four-Justice Part IV policy analysis is not imported into the common precedent ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consumer use-tax liability, other taxes, a numerical transaction or property-value threshold, a general software or physical-presence rule, and the result under future legislation are not decided. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The collection judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, under the Commerce Clause. The eight Justices reaching due process reject that challenge, but this supplies no collection liability given the Commerce disposition. No tax amount, interest, penalty, retrospective liability or §1983/§1988 entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- National Bellas Hess v. Department of Revenue, 386 U.S. 753 — retained for its specific Commerce Clause sales-and-use collection rule; no general immunity for interstate commerce is restored.
- Complete Auto Transit v. Brady, 430 U.S. 274 — substantial nexus remains distinct from fair apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair relation to state services; its rejection of Spector’s labeling rule remains.
- National Geographic Society v. California Board of Equalization, 430 U.S. 551, and D.H. Holmes Co. v. McNamara, 486 U.S. 24 — their express distinctions of Bellas Hess preserve reasonable reliance on the particular collection precedent rather than establish its abandonment.
- Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164, and Payne v. Tennessee, 501 U.S. 808 — their congressional-correction and reliance principles support the narrower adherence ground at the stated scope.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 — preserves Congress’s power to authorize otherwise dormant-Commerce burdens; Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

##### Whether the limited licensed-software interests provide sufficient local collection nexus

**Holding:** Quill’s insignificant interests in the licensed ordering software on this record do not supply the local commercial presence required to take its mail-order sales outside the retained Commerce Clause collection rule. This conclusion rejects the State’s asserted alternative nexus for this seller without prescribing a general rule for all software, licenses or in-state property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens, Blackmun, O’Connor and Souter in Part IV, together with Stone’s expressly matching record-specific software ground in his concurrence in the judgment; five Justices adopt this application at the same scope. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support reversal but are not counted as joining this substantive software explanation.

**Material application:** The insignificant software interests facilitate communication with the out-of-state business; they do not establish the local commercial operation or market-maintaining representative activity asserted by the State. No warehouse, employee or agent is found.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consumer use-tax liability, other taxes, a numerical transaction or property-value threshold, a general software or physical-presence rule, and the result under future legislation are not decided. Potential retroactive effects of overruling the Commerce rule were excluded from the grant. Congress cannot authorize a due-process violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The collection judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, under the Commerce Clause. The eight Justices reaching due process reject that challenge, but this supplies no collection liability given the Commerce disposition. No tax amount, interest, penalty, retrospective liability or §1983/§1988 entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- National Bellas Hess v. Department of Revenue, 386 U.S. 753 — applied to this mail-order collection demand after rejection of the asserted distinguishing property interest.
- Tyler Pipe Industries v. Washington Department of Revenue, 483 U.S. 232 — distinguished for actual in-state market-maintaining activity, including representatives; no employee-only nexus requirement is adopted.
- National Geographic Society v. California Board of Equalization, 430 U.S. 551 — Part IV’s additional rejection of a slightest-presence standard supports its four signers’ explanation; Stone’s fifth vote adopts the stated record-specific insufficiency without a whole-opinion join. Its local-office holding remains undisturbed.

### 1997-01-27 - ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg; Seventh Circuit No. 96-1139, 86 F.3d 1447; District Court judgment, 908 F. Supp. 640. No separate Supreme Court docket number assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/ProCD_v_Zeidenberg_merits_1997-01-27.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. All nine directly adopt each holding and its explanation; no synthesis of separate opinions is required.

##### Assent to this packaged-software transaction

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Wisconsin UCC §2-204, a seller may invite acceptance by use after review when the buyer has reasonable advance notice that terms govern, receives the full terms before meaningful use, has a practical opportunity to reject the transaction and obtain a refund, and objectively manifests assent by continuing to use the product. Those conditions establish assent to this noncommercial-use covenant; payment alone did not complete a different bargain that the seller later amended unilaterally.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this agreement arose through the transaction the parties actually made. The package announced restrictions; the manual and program supplied them; operation required an indication of assent; and return for a refund offered a practical means of rejection. Zeidenberg nevertheless used the consumer product to operate a commercial service and made two later purchases. Section 2-204 permits conduct to establish agreement. It does not require every term on the outside of the box, but neither does it permit a seller to insert new obligations after an already completed agreement.

The purchaser's contrary argument treats payment as the necessary final act of assent in every retail transaction. That does not account for this communicated offer and its review-and-return sequence. Lack of individual negotiation also does not itself resolve enforceability. Carnival Cruise Lines v. Shute, 499 U.S. 585 (1991), rejects that automatic objection in its setting but does not decide contested notice or Wisconsin formation here. American Airlines v. Wolens, January 18, 1995, permits enforcement of the actual voluntary bargain under its own preemption clause; it requires identifying the undertaking rather than manufacturing one.

Acceptance of goods under §2-606 is distinct from assent to contract terms. This single-form transaction does not decide §2-207's operation when parties exchange materially different forms. Nor does the Court decide terms first disclosed after irrevocable use, terms without a practical rejection route, or website terms lacking objective assent. Unconscionability, fraud, illegality and other generally applicable defenses remain available when genuinely preserved. No developed defense to this covenant requires additional proceedings merely to search for one.

**Precedent treatment:** Carnival Cruise is applied only against an automatic nonnegotiation objection, leaving its distinct maritime setting and the actual-notice inquiry intact. Wolens supplies the actual-undertaking analogy, not a Wisconsin rule of formation or immunity for every license.

##### Copyright subject matter and the distinct personal promise

**Holding and operative rule:** A fixed factual compilation can fall within §301's copyright subject-matter inquiry even when its facts or particular compilation lack copyright protection; absence of protection alone therefore does not end preemption analysis. Section 301 nevertheless does not preempt enforcement of this genuine, party-specific noncommercial-use promise, because the accepted undertaking changes the right asserted and binds only the parties, rather than imposing an equivalent exclusive right under §106 against persons who made no promise.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that subject matter and equivalence are separate questions. The database's assumed lack of copyright does not remove the compilation from the first inquiry. On the second, this action requires an undertaking governing purchased access, not copying alone. Zeidenberg accepted the consumer transaction on the condition that he would not use it commercially. Enforcing that exchange does not give ProCD ownership of the facts or a claim against an independent acquirer who never assented.

The objection that mass contracts could recreate copyright in unprotected information requires attention to the right actually enforced. A contract label or an additional pleading allegation does not invariably avoid §301. Here the real promise changes the parties' legal relationship; it is not merely a relabeled claim to control copying by the public. Section 301(b)(3) preserves nonequivalent rights, not every right described as contractual. Wolens, January 18, 1995, and Cipollone v. Liggett Group, 505 U.S. 504 (1992), supply clause-specific analogies distinguishing voluntarily assumed obligations from externally prescribed duties. Neither substitutes for §301's enacted equivalence inquiry.

Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone Service, 499 U.S. 340 (1991), denies copyright in facts based merely on collection effort; that limit remains intact. The assumed database premise does not adjudicate the copyrightability of every compilation element or enlarge the distinct program copyright. Nor does a holding under §301 answer whether an independent federal rule forbids a particular covenant. Nominal promises that do not qualitatively change the asserted right, first sale, reverse engineering, fair use, misuse, public policy, competition law and compulsory-license questions are not decided for materially different transactions. This judgment gives no right against nonassenting persons.

**Precedent treatment:** Feist's originality and factual-public-domain limits remain unchanged. Wolens and Cipollone's warranty component are extended by analogy only after independently construing §301; their differently worded preemption clauses create no categorical contract exemption.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Carnival Cruise Lines v. Shute, 499 U.S. 585 (1991):** Its treatment of standard terms supports rejecting invalidity solely from lack of negotiation. It does not replace the notice, rejection and assent inquiry under Wisconsin law.
- **American Airlines v. Wolens, January 18, 1995:** The actual-undertaking distinction is applied by analogy; the airline clause and its limits retain their separate force. Ordinary law must first establish the promise, and courts may not enlarge it through external policy.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, 505 U.S. 504 (1992):** The voluntary-warranty component supplies an analogy about the source of an obligation, not an exemption for all common-law claims or all contracts. Its claim-specific authority and cigarette-statute boundaries remain unchanged.
- **Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone Service, 499 U.S. 340 (1991):** The rule that effort alone supplies no copyright in facts is preserved; enforcing this personal undertaking does not change the originality requirement or create rights against the public.

#### Law After Decision

Wisconsin's ordinary assent rules apply to this form of software transaction, with actual notice, review, meaningful rejection and objective assent doing the work. The Court rejects both a rule that payment necessarily ends formation in every such transaction and a rule validating every enclosed term. Under §301, inclusion within copyright subject matter and equivalence remain distinct inquiries. This accepted promise is enforceable between the contracting parties; neither copyright in facts nor universal immunity for license claims follows. The decision adds these applications as of January 27, 1997, without displacing Feist's originality rule or enlarging Wolens and Cipollone beyond their statutory settings.

### 1997-04-21 — Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Robertson-Ceco Corp.; Second Circuit No. 95-7370; companion petition identifiers 96-306 and 96-472.

Authority: [Record](../records/Metropolitan_Life_v_Robertson_Ceco_merits_1997-04-21.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. A reasonable multiyear inquiry governs the contacts record

**Holding and operative rule:** Whether a corporation's forum business is continuous and systematic may be assessed over a reasonable period preceding commencement, rather than exclusively during the filing year; a ceased earlier activity is relevant to that course but does not by itself establish present amenability. The 1987–1993 discovery period was reasonable on this record, and the discovery limit remains undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion has all nine Justices' support for the reasonable-period rule. Its separate discovery-limit rationale is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supplying eight direct joins including the author. Stone-Zsela supplies a disposition-only vote on that subcomponent; all nine support leaving the discovery order undisturbed.

**Controlling explanation:** International Shoe makes the quality and nature of the actual forum relationship material. Perkins and Helicopteros distinguish a substantial continuing operation from insufficient recurrent contacts; neither requires a filing-year snapshot. The plaintiff was entitled to show a reasonably recent course, while the defendant was entitled to resist unlimited retrospective discovery. Discovery covering 1987–1993 permitted examination of repeated sales, company support, visits, solicitation, and the earlier employee office. The end of that office does not erase the rest of the course, and its past existence alone does not decide current jurisdiction. The Court approves the actual reasonable discovery limit, without prescribing seven years in every case or ordering a new evidentiary hearing.

**Precedent treatment:** International Shoe's quality-and-nature inquiry and Perkins/Helicopteros's general-jurisdiction distinction are applied without a new mandatory temporal formula.

##### II. The credited aggregate contacts satisfy the existing general-jurisdiction standard

**Holding and operative rule:** At the prima-facie stage presented here, Robertson's sustained selling, deliberate solicitation, supported distribution relationships, repeated company visits, and earlier staffed office, considered together, constitute continuous and systematic forum business sufficient for general jurisdiction. This conclusion credits the plaintiff's factual averments after discovery; it neither converts them into final findings nor makes sales, registration, an independent dealer, a former office, or any percentage independently sufficient.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter: seven direct joins. Ginsburg and Breyer disagree with the sufficiency conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** General jurisdiction permits adjudication of unrelated claims only upon substantially greater affiliation than purposeful contact supporting a particular related claim. Perkins supplies the continuous-operation principle; Helicopteros shows why regular purchases and associated training do not necessarily satisfy it. This defendant's own sustained Vermont sales and support course is qualitatively different. The credited record includes approximately $4 million in sales over the discovered multiyear course, $226,319 in 1993, five Star dealers and four Ceco authorized builders, product and trademark support, national and targeted solicitation reaching Vermont architectural firms, more than 150 employee or engineer visits, and an employee office during 1989–1990. The independent outlets, lack of current property or office, and small 1993 share of total sales are considered with those facts. None controls alone. Quill explains purposeful nonphysical affiliation for a related tax duty; it does not turn such affiliation into all-purpose jurisdiction. The Court independently applies the more demanding general standard to the full course. Glater's different selling operation is informative but does not establish a categorical rule that this combination can never suffice. No rule limiting general jurisdiction to incorporation or headquarters, automatic dealer-attribution rule, registration-consent holding, or numerical threshold is adopted.

**Precedent treatment:** Perkins and Helicopteros are applied to the aggregate business course. Quill's due-process holding remains confined to its related collection-duty setting; its distinct Commerce Clause rule is unaffected.

##### III-A. General jurisdiction retains an independent constitutional fairness inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Sufficient continuous and systematic contacts do not end the due-process inquiry: a defendant may still establish that exercising general jurisdiction would be constitutionally unreasonable by making a compelling showing grounded in the actual burden and relevant plaintiff, forum, and judicial-system interests. The absence of a connection between the claim and forum may inform that inquiry, but ordinary unrelatedness cannot alone do decisive work because general jurisdiction exists to reach some unrelated claims.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: six direct joins. Scalia reserves whether a freestanding multifactored inquiry must invariably follow adequate general contacts; Ginsburg and Breyer do not reach this issue after finding those contacts insufficient. The six directly adopt this rule without a fractured-opinion synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** International Shoe protects fair play as well as a sufficient relationship. Burger King requires a compelling case of unreasonableness once purposeful contacts exist, and Asahi's independent fairness holding gives real weight to the defendant's burden and the other relevant interests. That holding is not erased because this case concerns general jurisdiction or domestic parties. Nor does it make the lack of claim-specific forum interests a substitute for demonstrated constitutional unfairness. Sustained voluntary forum business supplies notice and substantially weakens an ordinary objection that suit there is unexpected or inconvenient. The inquiry remains demanding and concrete; serious domestic unfairness is not categorically excluded, and the factors are not restricted to foreign defendants. Litigation convenience and available venue remedies remain distinct from the constitutional limit.

**Precedent treatment:** International Shoe and Burger King's fairness requirements are applied to general jurisdiction. Asahi's eight-Justice independent-fairness holding remains effective; its separate stream-of-commerce plurality is not made controlling.

##### III-B. The offered fairness objection is insufficient on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** The foreign location of the claim-generating conduct, proof and witnesses, the parties' nonresidence, and the slight domestic defense burden shown here, considered together, do not make jurisdiction over this deliberately maintained commercial relationship constitutionally unfair. The Vermont dismissal cannot rest on that reasonableness determination; preserved venue and transfer questions remain for their own rules.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter: seven direct joins. Scalia joins this application assuming the complete separate inquiry applies; the other six also join Part III-A's requirement of that inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The construction dispute concerns a Miami building and conduct in Missouri, Texas, and Florida, rather than Vermont. Met Life and Robertson are both domestic corporations headquartered outside Vermont. Those circumstances favor another trial location, but Burger King's compelling-case requirement demands more than ordinary inconvenience. The lower court described the domestic defense burden as slight. Its conclusion that the forum and plaintiff had little claim-specific interest gave ordinary unrelatedness disproportionate constitutional force. Asahi involved a severe international burden and competing national legal systems not shown here. The Court does not require those international features in every successful fairness objection; it distinguishes their actual contribution to that decision. Keeton also prevents an asserted favorable limitations period from distorting personal jurisdiction. Selection of a forum with such a period is not itself unconstitutional, and the Court decides neither the applicable period nor its expiration. Even taking all relevant interests into account, this record does not establish the required unfairness.

**Precedent treatment:** Burger King's compelling-case rule is applied. Asahi is distinguished in application, not confined to foreign defendants. Keeton preserves the separation of personal jurisdiction from choice of law and limitations.

##### IV. Reversal removes the constitutional dismissal and leaves ordinary adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment affirming the Rule 12(b)(2) dismissal is reversed and the case remanded because the credited contacts are sufficient and the offered fairness ground fails. Remaining properly presented factual, service, limitations, venue, transfer, and merits questions must be decided under their ordinary requirements; the Court directs neither damages, transfer, nor a new evidentiary proceeding on questions the existing record resolves.

**Authority:** Part IV's formal reversal and remand are adopted by O'Connor, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: six Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees with removing the constitutional dismissal and remanding under Parts II–III's limits but would vacate rather than reverse, and joins only the stated proof, defenses, and remand limits within Part IV. Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm for insufficient general contacts.

**Controlling explanation:** Extensive discovery followed by a prima-facie assessment does not decide disputed ultimate facts. The errors identified here remove the present constitutional ground for dismissal; they do not adjudicate construction liability, waive a defense, resolve a limitations question, or make jurisdictional averments binding trial findings. The other defendant's separate summary judgment is outside this appeal. No entitlement to a particular transfer or new evidentiary hearing follows. Preserving ordinary procedure gives effect to the jurisdictional holding without turning it into a merits judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** The mandate applies the jurisdiction, fairness, and choice-of-law distinctions stated above; it creates no independent liability, limitations, or transfer rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- International Shoe Co. v. Washington: the quality and nature of affiliation and the protection of fair play remain controlling; the inquiry is not a count of isolated transactions.
- Perkins v. Benguet Consolidated Mining Co.: the continuous-operation basis for unrelated-claim jurisdiction is applied to the aggregate credited business course.
- Helicopteros Nacionales de Colombia v. Hall: the insufficiency of regular purchases and associated training remains; this supported selling and service course is distinguished.
- Quill Corp. v. North Dakota: purposeful nonphysical affiliation remains sufficient for its related use-tax duty at the scope already decided; neither all-purpose jurisdiction nor an indispensable physical office follows. Its separate Commerce Clause holding is untouched.
- Burger King Corp. v. Rudzewicz: the defendant's compelling-case burden and the difference between constitutional unfairness and venue relief are applied.
- Asahi Metal Industry Co. v. Superior Court: the independent-fairness holding remains law and is applied with attention to the actual international burden there; the stream-of-commerce plurality is not treated as a majority rule, and domestic defendants are not categorically denied fairness protection.
- Keeton v. Hustler Magazine: choice of law and a supposed favorable limitations period remain separate from the personal-jurisdiction determination.
- The lower-court decisions Crane, Pleasant Street, and Glater inform the competing applications at their actual scopes; none is treated as a prior Supreme Court holding.

#### Law After Decision

From April 21, 1997, the Court's general-jurisdiction law expressly permits a reasonable multiyear assessment and requires examination of the cumulative quality and regularity of contacts. The complete credited Robertson course is sufficient at this prima-facie stage. Six Justices adopt an independent, demanding constitutional fairness inquiry after adequate general contacts; seven conclude that this particular combination of ordinary domestic inconvenience and unrelatedness fails it. The Court establishes no numerical threshold, registration-consent rule, mandatory filing-year limit, incorporation-or-headquarters-only rule, automatic attribution of every independent dealer, or categorical immunity from fairness review. Remaining factual adjudication and ordinary venue, transfer, service, limitations, and merits rules retain their work.

### 1997-05-19 — United States v. Thomas; Sixth Circuit Nos. 94-6648 and 94-6649, 74 F.3d 701; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned. OT1996, chunk 8, inventory No. 90.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Thomas_merits_1997-05-19.md).

#### Holdings

####### I. The offense-date statute covers these transmitted images

**Holding and operative rule:** Offense-date §1465 reaches obscene visual image files knowingly transported through interstate computer-and-telephone facilities for sale or distribution. Applying its picture, image and electrical-transcription terms to this means of conveying the charged images does not depend on a later computer-specific amendment; the statutory distribution purpose and applicable culpability requirements remain necessary.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this statutory proposition directly, although Stevens dissents on the constitutional question.

**Controlling explanation:** The objects transmitted were the pictures made available to the approved member, not merely an abstract sequence of numbers divorced from those pictures. The statutory media terms encompass their actual conveyance. United States v. Alpers supports applying comprehensive media language to a method beyond the familiar physical forms; legislative purpose alone would not create an offense. Carlin's treatment of recorded telephone messages does not remove visual images from the enacted terms. United States v. Lanier requires fair warning in the law at the time of the conduct, so neither a later amendment nor a later judicial decision supplies an earlier prohibition. The ordinary visual-media language and the knowing distribution presented here answer the statutory objection. This holding establishes no rule that every new technology necessarily falls within every old criminal statute.

####### II. Receiving-community standards apply to this controlled distribution

**Holding and operative rule:** When operators screen and approve a subscriber, know his receiving community and knowingly authorize delivery there, Miller's geographic community standards may govern prurient appeal and patent offensiveness. Serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value remains Pope's reasonable-person inquiry, independent of local approval; no national cyberspace standard is required on this record.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by the eight Justices identified as its complete joiners. Stevens does not join this proposition. Eight direct votes supply controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** Miller v. California and Hamling v. United States permit the receiving community's standards in the distribution of obscenity; Pope v. Illinois separately protects serious value from local majoritarian judgment. Sable Communications applies the obscenity rule to commercial electronic transmission while preserving the distinction between obscenity and protected indecency. Here membership review, address information, telephone contact and individual passwords gave the operators actual control over this distribution. They knew that they were serving Memphis and could decline the subscription or delivery. The concern that an unknown viewer could expose an uncontrollable global publication to an unexpected locality therefore does not describe these transactions. A recipient's willingness does not remove otherwise unprotected obscenity from the governing distribution doctrine. The Court does not announce a general rule for open websites, anonymous networks, unavoidable routing or publishers unable to identify or control recipients, and does not require knowledge of obscenity law or of the jury's eventual legal classification.

####### III. Venue lies where the authorized interstate transportation ends

**Holding and operative rule:** Article III, the Sixth Amendment and 18 U.S.C. §3237(a) permit trial in the district where the charged continuing interstate transportation reaches the recipient whom the distributor authorized to receive it. The approved Memphis downloads completed that transportation there; known destination supports this application without becoming a universal additional statutory element.

**Authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this bounded venue proposition directly.

**Controlling explanation:** Venue follows the charged conduct's actual beginning and completion. The operators authorized and provided the interstate transfer to this screened subscriber. The inspector's act of downloading was the completion of that offered distribution, not an independent event with no connection to the defendants' conduct. Locating the server in California does not remove the receiving district from a continuing offense under §3237(a). Mere remote effects or an unknown viewer would not, by themselves, establish this holding's premises. The Court decides neither venue in every district traversed by electronic signals nor venue for a passive page addressed to an unknown audience.

####### IV. Scope of affirmance

The judgment affirms only the three granted questions. It does not decide entrapment, selective prosecution, separate evidentiary or counsel objections, sentencing, forfeiture, a new child-pornography theory or the legal effect of uncharged communications. Robert Thomas's Count 11 acquittal remains undisturbed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Alpers: applies its treatment of comprehensive statutory media terms; purpose does not independently create criminal liability.
- Miller v. California: applies the geographic standards for prurient appeal and patent offensiveness to the known receiving community; no new digital-speech tier is created.
- Hamling v. United States: applies the receiving-community distribution principle and preserves the distinction between factual knowledge and knowledge of legal obscenity.
- Pope v. Illinois: preserves the reasonable-person serious-value inquiry; receiving-community sentiment does not control that element.
- Sable Communications of California, Inc. v. FCC: applies its obscenity and audience-control reasoning to this subscription service, while retaining protection for indecency and leaving uncontrolled publication open.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.: preserves its statute-specific distinction between knowledge of regulated facts and knowledge of legal classification; it neither supplies a national obscenity community nor construes every §1465 element.
- United States v. Lanier: applies conduct-time fair warning; no later amendment or decision retroactively supplies the offense.

#### Law After Decision

The offense-date obscenity statute applies to the controlled electronic delivery of the charged visual images. Existing local-standard doctrine and continuing-offense venue rules govern these known-destination transactions, while serious value remains an objective inquiry. The Court establishes no general geographic rule for uncontrolled Internet publication and overrules no precedent.

### 1997-04-21 — Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Robertson-Ceco Corp.; Second Circuit No. 95-7370; companion petition identifiers 96-306 and 96-472.

Authority: [Record](../records/Metropolitan_Life_v_Robertson_Ceco_merits_1997-04-21.md).

Justice Scalia concurs in the sufficient-contacts holding and the reversal and remand. He agrees that the ordinary domestic inconveniences identified here cannot defeat this otherwise sufficient general affiliation even if the complete separate fairness framework applies. He reserves whether a freestanding multifactored inquiry must invariably follow adequate general contacts. His position does not repudiate Asahi's controlling independent-fairness holding or endorse its extension; it leaves that broader question unnecessary to his vote here.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the sufficient-contacts and independent-fairness holdings but would vacate the judgment and remand. The sustained voluntary commercial course makes ordinary claims of surprise or inconvenience weak, while due process retains protection against a concrete compelling burden. He agrees that the absent claim-specific Vermont connection cannot do decisive work alone. His requested remedy preserves the prima-facie posture, remaining defenses, and ordinary further proceedings without requiring a new hearing on matters the existing record resolves. He does not join the Court's formal reversal.

Justice Ginsburg, joined by Justice Breyer, dissents from the removal of dismissal. They agree that a reasonable multiyear course matters and that the discovery limit was adequate, but find the complete credited contacts insufficient for all-purpose jurisdiction. Perkins's substantial operations and Helicopteros's demanding threshold distinguish purposeful sales from the more substantial affiliation needed for every unrelated claim. Ginsburg's Crane analysis separates general, claim-linked, and statutory contacts; Breyer's joined Pleasant Street analysis likewise requires attention to the quality and nature of the actual relationship. Even taking together approximately $4 million of multiyear sales, independent outlets, company support, and intermittent visits, the absence of a continuing office, property, or comparable operational presence leaves this course short of that standard. They do not ignore the former office or rely on a sales percentage alone. Neither proposes an incorporation-or-headquarters-only rule, a fixed amount, or a categorical rule that sales can never suffice. Because contacts fail first, they would affirm without approving or rejecting the Second Circuit's separate fairness weighing.

### 1997-05-19 — United States v. Thomas; Sixth Circuit Nos. 94-6648 and 94-6649, 74 F.3d 701; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned. OT1996, chunk 8, inventory No. 90.

Stevens joins Parts I and III and dissents from the constitutional holding and judgment. Following the objection he joined in Sable Communications, he would not criminally suppress the distribution of obscene material to consenting adults. The difficulty of defining obscenity with adequate precision threatens substantial protected expression, and the legitimate protection of minors does not justify eliminating adult access.
