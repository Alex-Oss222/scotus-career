## Standards and Tests selection: Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

### Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

**Governing question:** What instructions govern prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements?

**Current rule:** Ordinary investigative work to identify a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication; later courtroom use does not retrospectively transform it into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate's role remain protected; neither all pre-indictment nor all post-indictment acts share one classification. Statements to the press announcing an indictment also lack absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern prosecution or cause injury through subsequent proceedings. Qualified immunity, an actionable constitutional deprivation and other liability predicates remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity: Buckley v. Fitzsimmons — Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity, 1993-06-24. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity: Buckley v. Fitzsimmons — A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity, 1993-06-24. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-24; Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity

### Ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity

**Governing question:** What instructions govern ministerial court reporting and absolute judicial immunity?

**Current rule:** Accurate recording and production of a verbatim transcript are ministerial functions and do not carry absolute judicial immunity merely because they are important or integral to adjudication. The claimant of immunity must establish its historical and functional justification; discretionary adjudication supplies a different function. Denial of absolute immunity establishes neither a cause of action nor liability, and other defenses remain open.

**Authority by component:**

- The ministerial transcript function does not carry absolute judicial immunity: Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc. — The ministerial transcript function does not carry absolute judicial immunity, 1993-06-07. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-07; Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., for the components identified above.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent legal review, contemporaneous notice and preservation limits: Elder v. Holloway — Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue, 1994-02-23. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Morgan Stanley's and Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b).

## Standards and Tests selection: Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

### Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar

**Governing question:** The retained original-writ channel and extraordinary showing following §2244(b)(3)(E).

**Current rule:** The statutory ban on ordinary certiorari review of a circuit’s successive-application authorization decision does not repeal §2241’s distinct Supreme Court original habeas authority. An original petition receives independent consideration rather than appellate review of that authorization judgment. Circuit permission to file in district court is not a prerequisite to filing here. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform original-writ discretion; whether they bind it directly remains unresolved. Rule 20 separately requires an explanation for not seeking district relief, how and where state remedies were exhausted or §2254(b) otherwise satisfied, exceptional circumstances warranting the Supreme Court’s discretionary exercise and lack of adequate relief in another form or court. Retained jurisdiction alone does not warrant the writ, further proceedings or an execution stay. Section 1651 aids independently held jurisdiction rather than supplying a freestanding new source.

**Authority by component:**

- Separate jurisdiction and original-writ discretion: The withdrawn review route and retained original jurisdiction; Successive restrictions and original-writ discretion; The separate constitutional innocence claim and original relief, 1996-06-28; Felker Parts I-A, II and IV-B, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Felker, 1996-06-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Felker’s as-applied constitutional rejection rests on actual independent consideration and an adequate traditional original-writ ground, not approval of eliminating every meaningful forum. Constitutional innocence’s universal merits threshold, other supervisory routes, and the constitutionality of all AEDPA restrictions remain open.

**Holdings navigation:** Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Economic-capacity modification of nonscheduled disability awards

### Economic-capacity modification of nonscheduled disability awards

**Governing question:** Modification under LHWCA §22 without a change in physical impairment.

**Current rule:** Section 22 permits modification of a nonscheduled disability award upon a demonstrated change in injury-related earning capacity although physical impairment is unchanged. Modification on the ground of changed conditions or mistake in a determination of fact must be sought or reviewed within one year after the last compensation payment, whether or not a compensation order issued, or within one year after claim rejection. The employer seeking modification must prove the qualifying change. Increased wages are conclusive only insofar as they fairly and reasonably represent capacity under §8(h); otherwise the decisionmaker must consider injury, impairment, usual employment, other capacity circumstances and natural future disability effects. Findings retain statutory substantial-evidence review.

**Authority by component:**

- Economic change can support timely nonscheduled modification: Economic change can support timely nonscheduled modification, 1995-06-12. Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt the construction and bounded reversal/remand.
- Capacity, proof and the remand: Capacity, proof and the remand, 1995-06-12. Justice Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt this application/remedy qualification.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-12; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** True §8(i) settlements are distinct. Scheduled compensation, medical benefits, untimely reopening, future recurrence and nominal-award doctrine remain undecided; no automatic termination or unrestricted revision of final awards follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Economic change can support timely nonscheduled modification; Capacity, proof and the remand.

## Standards and Tests selection: APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures

### APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures

**Governing question:** Who loses when evidence is genuinely balanced in an adjudication governed by APA §556(d).

**Current rule:** Unless a controlling statute otherwise provides, §556(d)’s burden of proof assigns the burden of persuasion to the proponent of the order; in the proceedings governed here, the proponent must establish the claim by a preponderance and loses at genuine equipoise. Burdens of producing evidence, lawful presumptions and separately allocated defenses remain distinct. The Longshore Act’s incorporation of APA adjudication is not displaced by §23’s evidentiary flexibility or its treatment of corroborated declarations of deceased employees. In the black-lung setting, even assuming statutory authority for an otherwise valid regulatory departure, the invoked remedial-policy provision does not establish an ultimate tie-breaking rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Persuasion allocation: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.
- Longshore incorporation despite evidentiary flexibility: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.
- No established black-lung departure: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Present operation effective:** Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 1994-06-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** Transportation Management’s contrary description of the APA burden is disapproved; its distinct mixed-motive defense allocation is not overruled. The full scope of power to create a black-lung exception is left open.

**Holdings navigation:** The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order; The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation; The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA.

## Standards and Tests selection: Direct relation between a civil RICO violation and injury

### Direct relation between a civil RICO violation and injury

**Governing question:** Causation for damages under 18 U.S.C. §1964(c).

**Current rule:** A plaintiff must establish factual causation and proximate cause, including some direct relation between the injury asserted and the alleged §1962 violation. Directness is a contextual requirement informed by attribution difficulties, overlapping recovery and available directly injured plaintiffs; those considerations explain the inherited requirement and are not separate newly required elements. Factual connection, foreseeable loss and an obligation to advance money alone are insufficient. Subrogation changes the claimant's identity without making the underlying injury more direct. The construction imports no antitrust-injury requirement, special racketeering injury or prior-conviction requirement, and creates no exceptionless bar to every third-party injury.

**Authority by component:**
- Factual and proximate cause, contextual directness and preserved limits: Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp., March 24, 1992, “Civil RICO requires a sufficiently direct causal relation,” Part II.
- Subrogation cannot improve the directness of the asserted injury: the same record, “The nonpurchasing customers' derivative losses are too remote,” Part III-A. Souter for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas controls both propositions; Scalia's judgment-only rationale is distinct.

**Present operation effective:** Holmes, March 24, 1992.

**Open question:** Whether securities-fraud RICO plaintiffs must be purchasers or sellers remains undecided; separate opinions rejecting that restriction do not create a Court holding.

**Holdings navigation:** Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp., 503 U.S. 258 (1992) — Civil RICO causation and SIPA remedies.

## Standards and Tests selection: Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

### Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

**Governing question:** Compensation for a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use of the relevant property.

**Current rule:** Complete deprivation effects a taking requiring just compensation unless background state property or nuisance law already excluded the prohibited use from the owner's title. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate a background limitation that independently prohibits the use in the property's circumstances. A new legislative declaration that the use is harmful is insufficient. Apply ordinary adjudicative proof requirements, without a special heightened quantum. Changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance law; legislative labeling cannot substitute for an antecedent title limit. Compensation concerns any actual taking period, not an assumed permanent loss.

**Authority by component:**

- Categorical compensation, background-law exception and allocation: Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, “Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use”, June 29, 1992; Scalia, Stone, White, O'Connor and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lucas*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Penn Central's contextual analysis remains for partial deprivation. No percentage shortcut, parcel-definition rule, automatic valuation, right to anticipated profits or finding that every environmental restriction is invalid follows. Valid authority to regulate remains distinct from responsibility to compensate.

**Holdings navigation:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992) — Complete economic deprivation of land.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nexus and rough proportionality for individualized land dedications

### Nexus and rough proportionality for individualized land dedications

**Governing question:** What a government must establish when it conditions a development permit on dedication of property interests.

**Current rule:** For an individualized permit condition demanding a real-property dedication, the government must establish an essential nexus to a legitimate regulatory interest and rough proportionality between the nature and extent of the demanded dedication and the proposed development’s impact. An individualized determination must justify each demand; a generalized possibility of benefit is insufficient. Reasonable estimates may suffice, without exact mathematical precision or proof that this property is uniquely suited. Public access must itself be justified when a less intrusive restriction would address the impact.

**Authority by component:**

- Nexus, governmental burden and nature-and-extent comparison: Dolan v. City of Tigard — The City must justify the nature and extent of each demanded dedication; The public-greenway dedication is insufficiently justified; The pathway dedication requires more than a possible traffic benefit, 1994-06-24. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt this rule and its government justification burden. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; all five support both the rule and this application. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the application and its estimation allowance.

**Present operation effective:** Dolan v. City of Tigard, 1994-06-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** The decision does not decide legislative exactions, monetary fees or every permit restriction. Failure of the showing does not automatically entitle the applicant to a permit or compensation; lawful reconsideration remains available.

**Holdings navigation:** The City must justify the nature and extent of each demanded dedication; The public-greenway dedication is insufficiently justified; The pathway dedication requires more than a possible traffic benefit.

## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

### Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

**Governing question:** Allocation of claim meaning and factual infringement work in a patent damages action.

**Current rule:** A patent damages action carries the Seventh Amendment jury right, while the court determines the public claim’s operative legal meaning using the claim, specification and prosecution history. Judicial construction includes terms of art and genuinely disputed technical usage or expert credibility embedded in that interpretive task, evaluated for consistency with the whole instrument. The jury retains disputed infringement, product operation, external identity and other factual questions outside construction. Technical complexity alone neither removes protected facts from the jury nor makes all patent issues legal.

**Authority by component:**

- Markman — Part I, legal meaning and retained factual work, 1996-04-23; Souter for all nine.
- Markman — Part II, technical and credibility disputes within construction, 1996-04-23; Souter and all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela’s contrary embedded-fact limitation is noncontrolling.

**Present operation effective:** Markman, April 23, 1996.

**Open question:** Trial allocation does not settle appellate review of every subsidiary finding. No universal pretrial construction hearing, exclusion of all extrinsic evidence, allocation for other instruments or comprehensive means-plus-function rule is prescribed.

**Holdings navigation:** Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370.

#### Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-681.  
**Decided:** November 5, 1991.

##### Whether a state officer sued individually is a section 1983 person for acts undertaken through official authority

**Holding:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under 42 U.S.C. §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority, and state sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by that officer personally. The plaintiff must still prove the officer's own violation under color of law, and the officer retains applicable personal immunities.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The Court of Appeals construed the damages claims as personal claims against Hafer. Her authority to discharge employees supplies the state-law connection without converting the claims into claims against Pennsylvania.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional violations, qualified immunity, damages, and entitlement to reinstatement remain unresolved; no immunity standard changes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. The individual-capacity claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded by this decision.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kentucky v. Graham supplies the distinction between litigation capacities. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police is clarified and preserved for States and official-capacity damages defendants. Scheuer v. Rhodes confirms possible personal liability of executive officers; Monroe v. Pape supplies the under-color-of-law rule for misuse of official power. Ex parte Young's prospective-relief channel is unchanged.

#### Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., 508 U.S. 429 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7604.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### The ministerial transcript function does not carry absolute judicial immunity

**Holding:** Court reporters performing the statutory task of accurately recording or producing a verbatim transcript do not receive absolute judicial immunity merely because their work is important or integral to a judicial proceeding. A claimant must establish the historically and functionally justified immunity; this ministerial task does not involve the discretionary adjudication that supports judicial immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The burden rests on the person claiming immunity. An authorized federal damages action, actionable deprivation, causation, qualified immunity or other defenses and damages remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the absolute-immunity dismissal and remand for the remaining questions through an authorized cause of action; no automatic liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219: functional distinction between adjudication and administration applied to reporting.
- Butz v. Economou, 438 U.S. 478: burden of justifying absolute immunity and functional comparison applied.
- Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478: historical and functional justification required; importance alone is insufficient.
- Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409: immunity for adjudicative functions distinguished from the ministerial transcript task.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992: private-party qualified-immunity holding preserved without extension to the defense decided here.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

## Verbatim Holdings selection

Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`; heading: Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña


#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, without deciding every certification route or conferring a general exemption on measures called benign.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. **Authority.** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute makes eligibility and favorable discretion distinct. It reaches covered entry fraud or misrepresentation, whether willful or innocent. An applicant must be the spouse, parent, son or daughter of a citizen or lawful permanent resident, have possessed an immigrant visa or equivalent document, and otherwise have been admissible at entry. The exceptions for labor-certification and immigrant-documentation grounds under §§1182(a)(5)(A) and (7)(A) apply only when those grounds directly resulted from the fraud. The separate exclusion for assistance in Nazi persecution or participation in genocide remains. An actually granted waiver also reaches entry-inadmissibility grounds directly resulting from the fraud. These provisions define eligibility and the consequence of relief; they do not remove conduct from the discretionary decision.

*Jay v. Boyd* supplies the distinction between statutory eligibility and discretionary selection. *INS v. Errico* construed a mandatory predecessor; its family-preservation concern does not convert the later discretionary provision into an entitlement. *National Center for Immigrants' Rights* supports discretion exercised within the statute while preserving required individual consideration.

The Board accepted eligibility and expressly disregarded the initial entry fraud. It considered the wife's false-identity scheme and sham marital arrangements, the later naturalization fraud, further marital arrangements and lack of candor, together with favorable residence, business, property, family and health considerations and Taiwan connections. Those are the Board's findings and assessments. The Ninth Circuit allowed dishonest testimony to count but wrongly excluded collaboration and naturalization conduct because of their connection with entry. The argument that waiver discretion must remain meaningful therefore does not justify its categorical rule. A continuing pattern distinguishes applicants without making every eligible applicant lose. The Court does not reweigh the balance.

#### III. Permissible factors remain subject to reasoned and consistent administration

**Holding and operative rule.** Statutory permission to consider a factor does not excuse arbitrary decisionmaking, pretext, disregard of a binding rule, or an unexplained departure from a genuinely settled agency policy. Here the Board rationally distinguished continuing immigration fraud from the initial entry fraud it disregarded; the record supplies no policy reversal requiring a new administrative balance.

**Authority.** Part III of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court separates statutory authority from the lawfulness of its exercise. *Smiley*, applying the administrative-change principles preserved from *Good Samaritan*, requires examination of the agency's actual reasons, prior guidance and legitimate reliance. A court cannot supply a litigation explanation for an unexplained change. An agency may revise a policy openly with adequate explanation, subject to binding law; the existence of discretion alone supplies no answer.

The Board retained its initial-fraud distinction and explained the significance of the continuing pattern. Its actual reasons defeat the claim that this decision irrationally abandoned that distinction. The Court neither finds a different binding policy nor commands a new explanation for an unproved reversal. The mandate removes the Ninth Circuit's compelled reweighing based on categorical exclusion. Any genuinely remaining preserved objection is for ordinary proceedings within the record and mandate; the Court directs neither a waiver nor its denial, reopening, a new hearing, or a fresh Board balance. This decision concerns the statutory immigration function presented, not classifications outside it.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Landgraf v. USI Film Products* and *Rivers v. Roadway Express*: applied to the actual congressional timing instructions; their distinct temporal rationales are not combined into a broader unanimous framework.
- *Jay v. Boyd*, 351 U.S. 345: applied for eligibility's separation from discretionary selection; it remains authority for that distinction.
- *INS v. Errico*, 385 U.S. 214: distinguished because its mandatory predecessor differs from the discretionary text here; its own construction is not overruled.
- *INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights* (December 16, 1991): applied by analogy for statutory boundaries and legally required individual consideration; its release-condition holding is not transformed into a waiver decision.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* and *Smiley* (June 3, 1996): applied for reasoned administrative interpretation and explained treatment of settled policy and reliance; statutory discretion does not displace those limits.
- *INS v. Doherty* (January 15, 1992): its fractured disposition supplies no controlling substantive reopening rationale for this waiver question; no separate rationale is promoted to Court law.

#### Law After Decision

Effective November 13, 1996, courts may not exclude a continuing immigration-fraud pattern from former §241(a)(1)(H) discretion merely because its acts are connected with entry. The eligibility conditions, the distinct consequence of a granted waiver, meaningful individual discretion and ordinary review of actual agency reasons remain operative. The Court leaves the minimum-fraud-only individualized case unresolved and permits no universal eligibility-based denial. The specific transition construction preserves review at this event; it decides no post-April §242(g) scope question. No waiver is awarded or denied by this Court, and the agency balance is not judicially redone.

### 1997-02-18 - United States v. Brockamp and United States v. Scott; No. 95-1225, one petition embracing Ninth Circuit judgments reported at 67 F.3d 260 and 70 F.3d 120.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Brockamp_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

#### The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 6511's integrated filing, allowance and amount scheme does not permit the requested nonstatutory equitable extension for mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file. Assuming Irwin's rebuttable presumption and the taxpayers' private-collector analogy apply, the scheme rebuts that presumption; incapacity and absence of prejudice supply no additional period.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; direct unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress separately prescribed when a claim may be filed, when a refund may be allowed and which payments may be returned. For return taxes, §6511(a) uses three years from actual return filing or two years from payment, whichever expires later, and two years from payment if no return was filed. Stamp taxes separately use three years from payment. Section (b)(1) repeats the prohibition on late allowance absent a timely claim, while (b)(2) adds independent monetary limits. This coordinated and repeated structure, reinforced by Congress's particular statutory adjustments, defeats the requested general equitable insertion.

Irwin allows rebuttal from the enacted scheme; an express anti-equity sentence is unnecessary. The Court assumes that refund actions' private-collector history favors the taxpayers. Their strongest claim—actual inability followed by filing and no unfair prejudice—differs from ordinary attorney neglect, but cannot alter this scheme. Suspending both filing and the monetary interval, and adding the taxpayers' proposed matching assessment extension, would change distinct statutory operations. The scale of tax administration corroborates the reading but is not its independent ground. Specific exceptions alone likewise do not carry the decision.

Dalm preserves the connected statutory conditions without itself answering this disability question; Henderson requires this particular text-and-function inquiry. The Court neither finds Scott capable nor decides Brockamp's disputed dementia facts. It does not classify every tax deadline as jurisdictional, adjudicate a separate constitutional disability claim, or decide unpresented fraud, affirmative governmental inducement or distinct recoupment questions.

#### The payment lookback independently bars these tax-payment recoveries

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming an otherwise timely return-based claim under §6511(a), §6511(b)(2)'s separately prescribed payment window is not enlarged by the requested mental-incapacity or extraordinary-inability tolling. These tax payments fall outside the applicable ordinary windows, including any supported return-filing extension; this amount limit independently defeats the ordinary tax-refund recoveries without deciding whether McGill's disputed remittance was instead a deposit.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; an independently sufficient unanimous ground.

**Controlling explanation:** A timely claim does not necessarily entitle the claimant to recover older payments. Under (b)(2)(A), a claim within the three-year filing route reaches payments in the immediately preceding three years plus any extension for filing the return; the stamp-tax proviso uses three years. Subparagraph (B) limits other claims to payments within two years. Subparagraph (C) separately limits an allowance without a claim as though the claim were filed on the allowance date. A return-filing extension is not an assessment-extension agreement under §6511(c).

Lundy supplies the distinction between selecting a claim route and measuring recoverable payments, although its Tax Court hypothetical differs from these actual claims. The Court need not reject a favorable late-return filing assumption to enforce the independent cap. The taxpayers argue that equitable filing relief must carry the amount interval with it to preserve an effective remedy. That reasoning presupposes the very equitable power the integrated text withholds; filing relief cannot itself move the dates of refundable payments. Williams preserves express refund consent with its actual conditions, and O'Gilvie's receipt rule creates no extension.

No qualifying assessment agreement, special-period tax item or mitigation circumstance is established. Their statutory conditions remain intact, but the Court announces no new operative construction of an unused exception. The result rejects the asserted equitable ground without treating McGill's deposit characterization as already decided.

#### Brockamp's separate deposit contention remains for the appellate court

**Holding and operative rule:** Reversal of Brockamp's tolling ground does not dispose of her preserved contention that the remittance was a deposit rather than a tax payment, which the appellate majority did not reach. The remand leaves that contention and its lawful consequences for ordinary appellate consideration, without ordering repayment or unconditional restoration of the district judgment.

**Authority:** Part III's deposit-remand explanation in Justice Breyer's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices; the Chief Justice agrees with that remedial component without joining its reasons.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory ruling governs tax-payment refunds. The district court rejected the distinct classification contention, but the court of appeals left it unresolved after accepting tolling, and the Government acknowledges its preservation. Removing the tolling premise does not decide that independent appellate alternative. The Court neither treats suspense accounting or lack of assessment as conclusive proof of a deposit nor treats the extension form as conclusively disproving one. Those merits and the consequences of any lawful classification remain open. Brockamp's return reporting $427 liability is not a finding that all $7,000 must be refunded.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs retains its rebuttable equitable-tolling presumption. The Court assumes its application and finds statutory rebuttal here; it does not treat established incapacity as ordinary attorney neglect.
- United States v. Dalm retains its connected waiver, administrative-claim and timing conditions and rejection of recoupment as independent refund jurisdiction. It is not redescribed as having decided disability tolling under Irwin.
- Commissioner v. Lundy supplies the distinct filing-route and payment-window functions; its Tax Court hypothetical and separate statutory routes remain confined to their terms.
- United States v. Williams preserves the express refund remedy with its administrative and timing requirements; its payer-eligibility holding supplies no equitable extension.
- Henderson v. United States supplies provision-specific attention to text, function and authorized enlargement; its service holding supplies no general power to extend tax limitations.
- O'Gilvie v. United States retains its payment-receipt trigger and expressly supplies no tolling rule.

#### Law After Decision

The applicable §6511 filing and amount provisions independently reject the asserted nonstatutory incapacity and extraordinary-inability extension. Courts must distinguish actual return filing from a return's due date and filing timeliness from the separate amount recoverable. Express statutory adjustments retain their own conditions; none is established here. The decision supplies no universal jurisdictional classification, no later disability amendment and no adjudication of a separate fraud, inducement, recoupment or constitutional claim.

### 1997-03-31 — United States v. Lanier; No. 95-1717.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Lanier_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted

**Rule:** Section 242, alone or as authoritatively construed, must make the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; a novel or unforeseeable criminal application is forbidden. Willful bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws supplies the statute's willfulness and definite-right limitations. Vagueness, lenity and the prohibition on unexpected retrospective judicial enlargement protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. The courts interpret Congress's criminal enactment rather than create common-law crimes. The reprehensibility or state criminality of the alleged conduct does not substitute for identification of a federal right and adequate notice of its application. Current decisions govern the reviewing court's legal method at their actual scopes; a decision issued after the conduct cannot itself supply earlier notice.

##### Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions

**Rule:** Fair warning under §242 may be supplied by the relevant pre-existing legal landscape, including lower-court authority; it does not categorically require a Supreme Court decision on the particular application. Neither one lower-court decision nor a fixed numerical consensus is automatically sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws did not prescribe the Sixth Circuit's Supreme-Court-only restriction. Elder supplies the examination of all relevant authority in assessing clear constitutional law; actual conflicts, source authority and their relation to the charged acts matter. Excluding a whole relevant class of decisions can wrongly convert established law into unknowable law. Conversely, source breadth does not eliminate the demand for sufficient clarity in the particular context.

##### Obvious applications need no factual twin

**Rule:** Existing constitutional law can give fair warning of an obviously covered application without an earlier case involving fundamentally similar facts. A broad invocation of liberty or bodily security is insufficient where the particular application remains unsettled, including where an earlier decision expressly leaves it open.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson supplies a particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness, not a demand for repetition of every factual detail. Officials cannot insist upon a decision matching their office, victim and precise method when established law applies with obvious clarity. At the same time, Elder and the actual Pinder and Uhlrig decisions preserve attention to unsettled applications and personal responsibility. Those distinctions prevent an abstract right from automatically resolving each charged assault, official nexus or notice question. The Court rejects the categorical similarity prerequisite while leaving those count-specific determinations below.

##### The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact

**Rule:** Section 242 fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: constitutional unlawfulness must be apparent in light of pre-existing law. That common protection supplies neither automatic criminal liability nor a substitute for constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the Government's criminal burden of proof and every other statutory element.

**Controlling explanation:** Due process does not demand a categorically clearer legal rule in the criminal case than the clarity protection furnished in the civil inquiry. But a conclusion about civil damages immunity cannot itself convict anyone. Screws's willfulness requirement performs separate work, and judicial title alone does not establish that each act occurred under color of state law. The Sixth Circuit must keep legal notice and proof of each criminal predicate distinct, rather than use either to erase the other.

##### Review the counts without the rejected legal shortcuts

**Rule:** The appellate court must reconsider the case without a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite and without categorical exclusion of due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody from §242. The judgment resolves no count's ultimate constitutional basis, fair warning, official nexus, proof or sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws and United States v. Price foreclose excluding rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process wholesale. DeShaney's limits on affirmative protection against private violence do not immunize an official's own assault merely because the victim was outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendment. Actual Collins, Pinder, Uhlrig and Nabozny preserve the distinction among personal official wrongdoing, omitted protection and supervisory attribution. These are legal directions for review, not new findings of assault, automatic restoration of the verdicts, a universal constitutional-assault formula or an order for a new evidentiary hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Screws v. United States:** Applied definite-right and willfulness protections; rejected a supposed Supreme-Court-only source restriction and categorical exclusion of due-process rights.
- **United States v. Price:** Preserved §242's reach to protected due-process rights without deciding the elements of each charged count.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied particularized apparent unlawfulness; no factual-twin requirement follows.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Applied consideration of relevant existing authority while preserving temporal limits.
- **Pinder v. Johnson and Uhlrig v. Harder:** Preserved their particularized notice, unsettled-application and personal-attribution boundaries; later holdings cannot create earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Kept constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation and supervisory theories distinct; their later dates cannot establish notice for earlier conduct.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserved the difference between a constitutional deprivation and every injurious official act; no broad federal tort rule is created.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Distinguished limits on protection from private violence from the official's own asserted assault.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied the specific-provision rule when a particular Amendment governs, without treating the Fourth and Eighth Amendments as exhaustive of every abusive official act.

#### Law After Decision

Section 242 has no Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite. Pre-existing law must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness apparent, using the same legal-clarity protection as clearly-established-law analysis while preserving every distinct criminal element and burden. The Court creates no new substantive right or count-specific liability holding.

### 1997-05-12 — Reyes-Hernandez v. INS; Seventh Circuit No. 95-3677, 89 F.3d 490; Supreme Court docket unassigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Reyes_Hernandez_v_INS_merits_1997-05-12.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Section 242(g) does not withdraw review of these completed-adjudication claims

**Holding and operative rule:** After its April 1, 1997 commencement, §242(g) reaches past, pending and future proceedings but withdraws review of the identified executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders, rather than every legal challenge arising from completed adjudication. These challenges to temporal eligibility, the legality of the completed waiver denial and treatment of a supplemental brief remain outside that withdrawal and may use the otherwise saved former §106 review route.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, has eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the threshold result.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Yang*'s April 1 commencement holding and construes the scope it reserved. Corrected §306(c)(1) applies the general amendments through §309 while extending §242(g) across old and new proceedings. Corrected §309(c)(1) preserves earlier proceedings and review subject to the stated transition rules: paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only before the evidentiary hearing has commenced, with notice at least thirty days before that hearing; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. Neither predicate exists after this completed 1995 adjudication. The October 1995 final administrative order does not meet §309(c)(4)'s requirement of an order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; no agency election or later final order is established. Section 306(c)(2) protects existing §106 judgments from invalidation or required reconsideration by paragraph (1), but creates no jurisdiction.

The contrary construction stresses the broad reference to any claim notwithstanding other law and the exception for review provided in §242. But making the three enumerated executive choices encompass every adjudicated legal issue would erase the detailed saving scheme beyond Congress's stated exceptions. Review of a completed legal result differs from challenging the executive action to adjudicate. Former §106 review is not declared to be review textually provided in §242; it survives through §309 only for claims outside the specified withdrawal. This construction does not preserve every discretionary or constitutional claim. *Felker* requires identification of the particular channel withdrawn and retained; neither a hypothetical §2241 remedy nor §1651 supplies the jurisdiction here.

##### II. The two AEDPA restrictions do not attach these new consequences to the completed concession

**Holding and operative rule:** On the uncontested premise that Reyes surrendered a colorable defense to deportability before enactment in order to pursue the then-available waiver-and-review path, §440(d) does not remove eligibility for that original waiver claim, and §440(a) does not eliminate ordinary review of its lawful denial, because neither clearly prescribes those new consequences for that completed choice. A genuine defense is necessary to this lost-option rationale, not a universal element of retroactivity analysis or an exemption for every preenactment conviction, pending case or later reopening request.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Landgraf* separately to each provision: first examine enacted temporal direction, then the new legal consequence attached to the completed act. Section 440(f) directs the reach of subsection (e), not either subsection at issue. Section 440(d) would remove eligibility that formed part of Reyes's completed surrender. Section 440(a) would separately remove the then-available opportunity to correct an unlawful denial through ordinary review after that surrender. Discretionary relief was never guaranteed, but the absence of an entitlement to mercy does not answer whether a real legal option has acquired a new disability.

The strongest objection treats §440(a) solely as regulation of present judicial power. *Landgraf* does not let the jurisdictional label replace examination of the actual consequence. This record supplies a completed concession, more than mere pendency or a general hope that a forum will persist. *Felker* applied its review restriction to authorization proceedings begun after enactment, a different temporal event. The Government did not dispute the colorable-defense predicate; the Court invents no particular defense and orders no factfinding to reopen that accepted premise. An utterly insubstantial defense would not establish this surrendered-option theory. Other temporal predicates remain open, and no universal subjective-reliance requirement, constitutional invalidation, habeas holding or promise of favorable discretion is adopted. The IIRIRA dual-offense technical correction supplies no additional conviction finding.

##### III. Eligibility does not compel a favorable waiver balance

**Holding and operative rule:** An eligible §212(c) applicant remains subject to a reasoned individualized exercise of lawful discretion, and the BIA may adopt an immigration judge's adequate reasons without independently repeating the entire balance. Here consideration of rehabilitation and family hardship against serious, recent and repeated drug offenses lawfully supports denial; the reviewing court may not replace that balance merely because the favorable equities are substantial.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes. The jurisdictional dissenters do not reach this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Yang*'s distinction between eligibility, permissible factors and reasoned administration. Permission to exercise discretion does not permit pretext, disregard of binding rules or an unexplained departure from genuinely settled policy. Those defects are not established here. The immigration judge addressed family hardship and rehabilitation, yet gave greater weight to repeated serious drug violations; the BIA adopted those actual reasons. Adoption of an adequate explanation is not absence of analysis. Reyes's stronger-equities argument asks for a fresh judicial weighing, not correction of a demonstrated legal error. The Court sustains this denial without creating a presumption against every eligible drug offender or foreclosing review of an actual policy departure.

##### IV. A supplemental appellate brief did not require original factfinding or automatic reopening

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the BIA could require the established motion to supplement, remand or reopen before considering new post-hearing evidence offered in an appellate brief, rather than treating that brief as a different motion or conducting original appellate factfinding. The decision does not permit disregard of material evidence already admitted or decide the eligibility, timeliness, merits or disposition of any later motion.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes refusing to receive new evidence through an improper vehicle from ignoring evidence already in the record. The supplemental brief described later employment, sobriety and volunteer work, but the agency had not admitted that material. It required the established procedural route and expressly identified reopening as remaining possible. *Abudu* supplies the governing reopening framework; *Doherty*'s fractured result supplies no controlling general rationale. The argument that several years of developments could affect the equities shows why an evidentiary route matters, not why the Board had to convert a merits brief into a new proceeding. On the actual record that requirement was lawful. The Court neither guarantees a successful motion nor decides a later proceeding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang*: Applies its April 1 commencement holding, decides the previously reserved §242(g) scope at the stated boundary, and applies its separation of eligibility from reasoned discretion.
- *Landgraf v. USI Film Products*: Applies its provision-specific temporal inquiry to the completed surrender of an actual legal option; neither pendency nor a jurisdictional label controls by itself.
- *Felker v. Turpin*: Preserves channel-specific review and distinguishes its post-enactment authorization proceedings; no presumed alternative remedy replaces the actual preserved route.
- *INS v. Abudu*: Preserves the existing reopening framework and distinction between record review and presentation of new material.
- *INS v. Doherty*: Its fractured disposition still supplies no general controlling reopening rationale; no separate writing is elevated into law.

Effective May 12, 1997, §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not eliminate these legal challenges to a completed adjudication within the saved former review scheme. The separate AEDPA review and eligibility restrictions do not attach the specified retrospective consequences to this uncontested surrender of a colorable defense. That limited temporal protection supplies review and eligibility, not a waiver, automatic reopening or a guarantee that agency discretion will favor the applicant. The completed waiver denial and ordinary new-evidence procedure stand. Other convictions, temporal predicates, later motions, actual habeas scope and constitutional review requirements remain unresolved.

### 1997-05-27 — Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency; No. 96-243. OT1996, chunk 8, inventory No. 93.

#### Holdings

####### The final land-use decision does not depend on selling transferable rights

**Holding and operative rule:** When the responsible land-use agency has definitively prohibited development of the claimant's parcel and no further land-use procedure can change that decision, the final-decision requirement is satisfied. The owner need not first sell transferable development rights that cannot alter the parcel's permitted use; uncertainty about their value or ultimate constitutional significance does not make that completed regulatory determination provisional.

**Authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices directly. The Court adopts this common finality ground without an alternative inquiry treating the rights' saleability, award or value as potentially decisive to finality. No Marks synthesis is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank requires a final decision identifying how the regulation applies to the property. MacDonald, Sommer & Frates v. Yolo County likewise requires a definitive account of the development the agency will allow, rather than review of a hypothetical restriction. Here the permit denial and completed governing-board appeal settle that institutional question: Suitum may not develop this parcel. A sale of rights usable elsewhere cannot revise the answer. The Agency's objection that economic injury cannot be measured without more information concerns the later assessment of the restriction, not an unmade land-use decision. Lucas preserves ordinary finality while distinguishing reviewability from proof of complete deprivation and the ultimate compensation inquiry. The Court neither requires a market transaction as a condition of finality nor values the rights, fixes their role in taking liability or compensation, or rules that they are irrelevant to the merits. Those issues can be addressed under the applicable law below.

**Limits and remedy:** The Ninth Circuit's finality dismissal is vacated and the case remanded without the attempted-sale prerequisite. Any independently applicable state-compensation condition, the relevant parcel, the governing takings framework, liability, valuation, defenses and remedy remain open. The Court orders neither compensation nor a building permit and finds no complete economic deprivation.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank: applies the final-decision inquiry to the permitted use of the regulated parcel; the independent state-compensation requirement is not decided.
- MacDonald, Sommer & Frates v. Yolo County: distinguishes remaining agency discretion over development from this completed prohibition; no futile market sale becomes a new land-use application.
- Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council: preserves regulatory finality and the distinction between reviewability, proven loss and compensation; its complete-deprivation rule is not applied as a merits finding here.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City: leaves its contextual merits framework intact and the rights' ultimate role unresolved. The separate concurrence's proposed distinction of its transferable-rights discussion is not a holding of the Court.

#### Law After Decision

A definitive parcel-use prohibition satisfies regulatory finality even though the owner has not marketed rights permitting development elsewhere. The Court removes the attempted-sale barrier and leaves the substantive takings and compensation questions open. No precedent is overruled and no transfer-right valuation rule is adopted.

### 1997-06-02 — McMillian v. Monroe County; No. 96-542; 88 F.3d 1573 (11th Cir. 1996)

Authority: [Record](../records/McMillian_v_Monroe_County_merits_1997-06-02.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Final authority and the government represented are distinct inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Monell and Jett, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, the official who speaks with final policymaking authority for the particular governmental entity concerning the challenged action. Final operational discretion does not alone identify that entity; office labels, election, territory, funding and absence of commission control are each insufficient standing alone.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer, for five direct joins; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept Part I but reject the County application and resulting judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell requires the government's own policy or custom, not liability merely because it employs the wrongdoer. Pembaur permits an authorized final policymaker's single decision; repetition is not invariably required. Its four-Justice Part II-B is not the whole Court's reasoning. Praprotnik's relevant plurality emphasizes lawful allocation while preserving legally effective custom, genuine delegation and ratification. Jett Part IV supplies the later majority instruction to identify who speaks finally for the entity on the particular subject. These sources distinguish authority to decide an act, final policymaking on that subject, and the governmental entity represented.

Collins separates a federal deprivation from municipal responsibility. Kinman likewise preserves policy/custom and the attributable single-final-decision route; its failure of proof on a school record does not decide the identity of an Alabama sheriff's authority. Nor do its separate personal-supervisor and Title IX rules replace Monell. Swint decided appellate reach rather than this attribution question. The later permitted §1292(b) appeal presents the issue through a lawful route. The court must respect Alabama's actual allocation without allowing a label alone to eliminate federal responsibility or assuming that only a county commission can constitute County government.

##### II. The sheriff's independent local office supplies County law-enforcement authority

**Holding and operative rule:** An Alabama sheriff represents the County when setting and executing the law-enforcement policy involved in these criminal-investigation acts. The independently constituted final local office supplies that County authority without a delegation from, or power of review in, the county commission; the holding does not classify every sheriff function or every act as County policy.

**Controlling explanation:** County electors choose the sheriff, who exercises independent final law-enforcement responsibility within the county. The County treasury pays his salary, and the commission must furnish quarters, equipment, vehicles and related necessities reasonably needed for the office. Those features together support the identity of the local institution; payment or geography alone would not. A County need not place every final policy decision in a single commission. Independence from that commission therefore does not necessarily make the sheriff a State policymaker.

The strongest contrary evidence is article V's placement of sheriffs in the State executive department, the immunity classifications in Hereford and Parker v. Amerson, duties to courts and prosecutors, and §36-22-5's requirement to investigate on written direction of the district attorney, attorney general or governor and report to that officer. Those provisions deserve weight, but do not displace the sheriff's autonomous local policymaking in the function presented. Enforcing statewide criminal law does not itself answer which government owns the local policy. State-law immunity and vicarious-liability classifications inform the structure without deciding federal attribution.

The Court neither assumes an actual State instruction in this investigation nor treats County financing as power to direct it. It relies on the sheriff's own final local office. Genuine County policies, customs, delegation and ratification remain governed by Monell and Jett. Other functions, including jail administration, and other States' arrangements require their own analysis. The decision does not determine State damages liability, immunity or every form of State policymaking.

##### III. The County claim returns for the remaining liability elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The County-attribution dismissal is reversed and the claim based on the sheriff's final law-enforcement policymaking is reinstated for further proceedings. McMillian must still establish a federal violation and an attributable County policy or final decision that was the moving force behind his injury; the sheriff's institutional status establishes neither the allegations' truth nor liability for every act.

**Controlling explanation:** Collins's separation of deprivation and responsibility remains essential. The reversal removes the legal conclusion that this office cannot supply County policy for the challenged function. It does not decide whether Tate exercised that final authority in the alleged misconduct, whether a constitutional injury occurred, or whether the required policy caused it. Treating institutional identity as the whole liability case would reinstate the employer liability Monell rejects. Kinman's single-final-decision route preserves the claim's possible legal basis without guaranteeing recovery.

The personal claims and immunity, unreviewed evidentiary and summary-judgment rulings, independent County customs, State liability and immunity, and other sheriff functions are outside this granted disposition. No missing custom, State directive or litigant default is supplied. Bryan County remains pending for reargument and contributes no hiring, fault, causation or remedial rule. The mandate orders neither a liability finding nor damages or a trial.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Monell is applied to the County’s own policy, custom or attributable final decision and continues to reject ordinary employer liability.
- Pembaur preserves the single-authorized-final-decision route at the Court portions’ scope; its Part II-B plurality is not treated as a complete majority rule.
- Praprotnik’s plurality is read with its legally effective custom, genuine delegation and ratification qualifications; its formal allocation inquiry does not make labels conclusive.
- Jett Part IV supplies the controlling majority method of identifying the official speaking finally for the entity on the challenged subject.
- Collins preserves the separate inquiries into a federal deprivation and institutional responsibility.
- Kinman preserves the attributable single-final-policymaker route while its different school-record application and personal-supervisor rules remain distinct.
- Swint is distinguished as an appellate-jurisdiction decision leaving a properly authorized later merits route open; it did not decide sheriff attribution.
- Hereford supplies evidence of Alabama’s structure through its state-law classification, without determining federal County attribution.
- Parker v. Amerson likewise informs State structure and immunity rather than conclusively resolving §1983 attribution.
- Parker v. Williams concerns jail-personnel authority and is not extended into an all-functions sheriff rule; Stone uses it only as a separate analogy.
- Hess appears only as a structural Eleventh Amendment analogy in Stone’s concurrence; it supplies no County-attribution holding here.

#### Law After Decision

For the law-enforcement function presented, the Alabama sheriff can supply County policy in his own right, although the commission does not direct him. A plaintiff must still prove an underlying federal injury, an attributable policy or final policy decision, and moving-force causation. Other functions and States, genuine independent County customs, individual liability and State immunity retain their separate rules. A State designation does not alone defeat federal attribution, and County employment or financing does not alone establish it.
