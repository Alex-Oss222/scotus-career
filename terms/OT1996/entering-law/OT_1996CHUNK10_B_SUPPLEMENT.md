#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203

**Holding:** Section 3203 survives this facial challenge under the prospective construction that compliance with the Act cannot create a significant threat to the patient's life or health. Its good-faith clinical standard permits prompt treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset, without waiting for catastrophic deterioration; the ruling does not adjudicate every other postviability restriction or every degree of clinical risk.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3203 defines medical emergency through the physician's good-faith clinical judgment: a condition necessitating immediate abortion to avert death, or one in which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The significant-threat interpretation and application to the three obstetric conditions are the Third Circuit's construction, not additional statutory words or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court interpretation. Section 3205(c)'s separate disclosure defense is not an emergency-delay exception. A materially different authoritative construction or enforcement remains challengeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Facial relief against the emergency definition is denied under the prospective protective construction. No separate vagueness judgment is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services: applies its attention to a medical rule's workable, prudent construction; does not adopt every part of its fractured abortion analysis.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton: their retained necessary-care protection is stated separately at its own proposition-level coalition; this unanimous statutory construction does not create unanimous adherence to their entire reasoning.

##### Emergency explanation under §3205(b)

**Holding:** Section 3205(b) requires an explanation of the medical indications supporting emergency abortion before the procedure only if possible. It does not condition necessary immediate care on completing an explanation or obtaining advance permission, and it survives the facial challenge on that limited operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** When medical emergency compels an abortion, the physician shall inform the woman, prior to abortion if possible, of the medical indications supporting the judgment that abortion is necessary to avert death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The statutory text appears in the appendix at 505 U.S. 903–904.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The explanation requirement survives with its express if-possible qualification; it creates no advance-permission or treatment-delay condition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permission for appropriate informed-consent administration while keeping interference with the treatment decision distinct.

##### The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c)

**Holding:** A physician is protected from liability for failing to furnish §3205(a)'s information if the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would have a severely adverse effect on the patient's physical or mental health. This statutory disclosure defense does not supply a general waiting-period waiver, authorize delay of necessary emergency treatment or replace §3203's emergency protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The paragraph identified by the District Court and Third Circuit as §3205(c) protects a physician from liability for failure to furnish subsection (a)'s information when the physician proves, by a preponderance of the evidence, a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. The defense concerns the physician's reasonable belief, not proof that feared injury actually occurred. Its enacted preponderance burden does not displace the constitutional justification allocation. It does not excuse every unfavorable reaction or inconvenience.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The enacted disclosure defense remains available without becoming a general waiting waiver or a substitute for prompt emergency treatment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: ordinary informed-consent administration remains permissible subject to the actual statutory and constitutional limits; no general proof rule is derived from it.

##### Accurate material medical information

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(1)(i)–(iii)'s accurate, medically material information concerning the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks may be required for informed consent. Content is sustained independently of the separately invalidated exclusive routine-delivery and fixed waiting requirements, with the statutory disclosure-health defense preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Material application:** Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter additionally apply the complete framework: separated from the invalid interval and exclusive routine-speaker condition, the bounded medical information supplied during the existing encounter establishes no demonstrated meaningful additional access burden on this record. Accuracy alone would not answer a concrete burden from materially different delivery, timing or implementation. The other four Justices join the bounded medical-content permission without adopting the complete framework.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. False or exaggerated risk descriptions and ideological assent are outside the sustained content. Concrete misleading implementation remains challengeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The content ruling is affirmed separately from invalid timing and routine-delivery exclusivity. Accurate, material content and appropriate clinical responsibility survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to ordinary voluntary and informed consent.
- Akron and Thornburgh: their protection against misleading or coercive communication survives; they do not forbid this isolated accurate medical content.

##### Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(2)(iii) may require accurate notice that the father is liable to assist in supporting the child even if he offered to pay for an abortion; the statute provides, “In the case of rape, this information may be omitted.” No police report is required. Legal liability cannot be represented as guaranteed payment, and the omission permission changes no support obligation. This holding governs the disclosure alone; a married patient's separate question of notifying her spouse of the pregnancy is governed by the spousal-notice holding below, not by this provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. ##### Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials

**Holding:** Section 3208's publication duties and section 3205(a)(3)'s provision of a copy when the patient chooses to review it are sustained at their objective, accurate and nonjudgmental statutory scope. The patient need not accept, read or endorse the materials, and the ruling does not establish that every publication or listed agency actually complies.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3208 requires easily comprehensible English, Spanish and Vietnamese materials within sixty days after the chapter becomes law, annual updates and legible type. A geographically indexed directory identifies public and private pregnancy, childbirth, dependent-child and adoption services and contacts, including phone numbers; the Department may instead provide a toll-free twenty-four-hour number supplying that list and local service information. Fetal information covers probable anatomy and physiology in two-week increments from fertilization to full term and relevant survival information; realistic pictures state dimensions and match the patient's stage. Information must be objective, nonjudgmental and scientifically accurate, including common abortion methods and risks, possible detrimental psychological effects and childbirth risks. Free copies in appropriate numbers are available to any person, facility or hospital requesting them. The materials state that coercion is unlawful, that abortion without informed consent or a private medical consultation may expose the physician to civil damages, that paternal support liability remains despite an offer to pay for abortion, and that adoptive parents may pay prenatal, childbirth and neonatal costs. Financial statements do not guarantee availability or collection. Findings of directory defects and misinformation by some organizations do not establish universal falsity or compliance.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Publication and provision of a copy if chosen are sustained. Acceptance, reading and ideological assent are not required, and clinicians may supplement or criticize the material.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: supports information serving voluntary choice.
- Thornburgh: distinguished at voluntary publication/receipt scope; misleading or coercive implementation remains reviewable.

##### Certification of lawful receipt

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(4) may document actual receipt of lawful information without ideological assent or certification of unchosen reading. Its particular mandatory ancillary, timing and physician-delivery links stand or fall with the separately counted duties; a nine-Justice agreement on basic documentation is not a nine-Justice vote for every retained prerequisite.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by the other eight Justices: 9 direct rationale joins including the author. The basic receipt principle is unanimous. Ancillary dependencies survive 8–1, with Blackmun dissenting; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter join the five-Justice ancillary rationale, while White, Scalia and Thomas concur in those judgments. Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter sever both timing and exclusive-delivery dependencies, each 5–4.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The patient certifies in writing before the procedure that required lawful information was supplied. Receipt is not agreement; no certificate may demand unchosen reading, compliance with invalid timing or an exclusive routine speaker. Particular duty coalitions remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Retain accurate documentation of surviving information duties. Remove timing-dependent and exclusive-speaker-dependent forms and sanctions, each on the five-Justice invalidation coalition. Ancillary dependencies survive on their separately stated five-Justice rationale and eight-vote judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Danforth: applied to written consent documentation, with receipt distinguished from assent.

##### Physician delivery of medical information

**Holding:** Section 3205(a)(1) is invalid insofar as it excludes qualified personnel from communicating routine consent information under appropriate physician responsibility. Accurate content remains required; individualized diagnosis, clinical assessment and professional responsibility are not eliminated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from invalidation. The judgment is 5–4.

**Material application:** The record establishes meaningful added demands from physician-only delivery of information trained personnel can competently communicate. Qualified communication with physician availability and responsibility is a concrete practicable alternative the State has not adequately answered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever exclusive routine physician delivery and its dependent certification and sanctions; preserve accurate content, individualized diagnosis, clinical assessment and applicable professional qualifications.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Akron: its protection against unnecessary exclusive physician delivery is retained and applied.
- Ohio v. Akron: parental notification does not independently resolve adult clinical counseling.
- Danforth: permits informed consent without validating every staffing restriction.

##### The mandatory 24-hour interval

**Holding:** The compulsory twenty-four-hour interval in §3205(a)(1)–(2) is invalid under the meaningful-interference branch. The State has not substantiated the justification and fit of a uniform interval or adequately answered practical, substantially less burdensome means of securing informed and voluntary consent. Timing-dependent certification and enforcement are severed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. The judgment is 5–4; no finding that every application is a substantial obstacle is required for this distinct ground.

**Material application:** The demonstrated additional delay, travel, expense and scheduling burdens are meaningful interference. Individual counseling, questions and additional time when needed or requested supply practical alternatives the State has not adequately answered. An emergency exception does not justify burdens on patients outside its terms, and voluntary waiting does not justify universal compulsion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the fixed interval and timing-dependent certifications and enforcement; preserve lawful consent, voluntary reflection and time required by genuine clinical need or unresolved consent. No finding that every application is a substantial obstacle is necessary to this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Akron: its fixed-waiting protection is retained and applied.
- Hodgson and Ohio v. Akron: parental-involvement decisions do not supply a justification for universal adult reconsideration.
- Roe and Doe: the protected decision and necessary timely care remain independently binding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the compulsory twenty-four-hour interval creates a substantial obstacle to the practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population, it is independently invalid. No compelling-interest assertion can rescue an established obstacle.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; the same five directly adopt this obstacle ground and the complete framework. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from invalidating the interval.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains controlling at the stated substantial-obstacle scope.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The Court does not find that every application of the interval creates a substantial obstacle. Invalidation of the compulsory interval as a whole rests independently on the meaningful-interference ground above; this ground applies where the additional obstacle condition is established. It requires no further or different relief beyond removal of the interval and dependent certification and enforcement. Roe and Doe preserve the protected decision and timely necessary care; Akron's fixed-waiting protection remains applied.

##### Confidential referring-source identification

**Holding:** The referring-source component of §3214(a)(1) is invalid insofar as it demands identification solely because a physician, agency or service made a referral. The remaining identification duties and otherwise lawful inquiries based on actual clinical responsibility are preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent. The judgment is 5–4.

**Material application:** Evidence shows referrers would stop providing referral information if identified even under accepted confidentiality. ##### Public facility and affiliation reports

**Holding:** Section 3207(b)'s compulsory public-release condition is unconstitutional as applied to the plaintiff facilities and their covered reports. **Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. **Material application:** Supported risks of harassment and withdrawal from medical-assistance participation establish meaningful interference for these plaintiffs. **Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3207(b)'s release trigger uses State-appropriated funds received in the twelve calendar months before a request to inspect or copy. **Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse public-release approval as applied to plaintiff facilities and remand to identify and protect their covered reports and conform the injunction, including dependent enforcement. 1. Identify the enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances, separating actual commands from assumed administration. ### 1997-03-17 — Brown v. Polk County; Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313, 61 F.3d 650 (en banc); no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Polk_County_merits_1997-03-17.md).

#### Holdings

All eight Associate Justices directly join Parts I–IV of the Court’s opinion. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also joins Parts I, II and IV. Part III’s same-decision and closed-liability reasons therefore command eight votes; the other holdings command nine. No proposition rests on aggregation of separate writings.

###### Part I — Religion-specific commands exceed the demonstrated justification

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer may enforce lawful controls on work, resources, coercion and official religious activity, but a religion-specific prohibition extending beyond those concerns into private religious expression and personal religious possession must satisfy the constitutional justification its discrimination demands. The undifferentiated witnessing, proselytizing and counseling command and removal of all religious items, including a private desk Bible, fail that inquiry at their demonstrated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith preserves neutral rules, while Lukumi requires justification and tailoring for religious targeting. Preventing official worship, supervisory pressure, favoritism and diversion of paid resources supplies legitimate and potentially compelling concerns. Those concerns do not justify suppressing religious possession without an established operational consequence or every act described in religious terms. Narrower lawful controls cannot save the overreaching directives actually issued.

The supervisor setting matters. Voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayers are not automatically private, and nominal voluntariness cannot erase coercive pressure or government adoption. The Court need not recognize a general constitutional right to organize worship during department meetings to decide the orders’ excess. Pickering, Connick and Givhan retain the actual employment-speech inquiry. NTEU addresses broad burdens on protected citizen expression without converting supervisory instructions into citizen speech; Waters concerns genuine reliance on a disciplinable account, not blanket religious suppression. Pinette establishes no comprehensive majority test, and Chabad and Good News do not make this office a forum. The free-exercise ground suffices; no new equal-protection tier or constitutional discharge rule is adopted. A Title VII violation does not automatically establish a First Amendment violation.

###### Part II — Accommodation turns on the actual practice and supported burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §2000e(j), the employee must establish the sincere practice, employment conflict, employer knowledge and adverse treatment implicated by the claim; the employer must demonstrate effective reasonable accommodation or inability to accommodate without more than a de minimis hardship under the governing law. Concrete noncash burdens and supported professional predictions count without realized injury, an experiment or an actual complaint; an effective accommodation need not be preferred, and no independent duty to negotiate or try every alternative remains after effective accommodation or sufficient hardship is shown.

**Controlling explanation:** Hardison, Ansonia and Beadle require attention to the practice and actual operational justification. Diverting a paid secretary to Bible-study work supplies a sufficient resource burden. The particular prework office use likewise establishes no protected access: the County treats arrival as starting work and prohibits personal resource use, and no public or limited forum or comparable allowed personal use was shown. That conclusion is not a categorical prohibition of off-clock prayer.

The department-meeting prayers and one Christian work-ethic reference require separate assessment. The testimony described voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayer, no compelled participation, and a single reference. Indirect reports, absence of direct complaints or observed discomfort, testimony denying favoritism and accounts attributing disputes to organization did not connect those practices to the asserted polarization or favoritism burden. Supervisor status remains relevant but is not conclusive proof of coercion. Infrequency is no immunity, and realized disruption is not indispensable; the Court rejects those broader suggestions below and sustains the result because the actual proof fails under the correct predictive standard. The proved cost of secretarial work cannot be exported to every religious act. Knowledge and the absence of attempted accommodation alone do not decide liability, and no new opportunity to invent coercion or retry the completed hardship question is ordered.

###### Part III — The original mixed-motive defense fails on this completed record

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the original Price Waterhouse rule applicable to this pre-1991 conduct, direct decisional evidence that protected religion was a substantial factor actually relied on, proved by a preponderance, shifts to the employer the burden to prove by a preponderance that it would have made the same decision without that consideration; success defeats liability, not merely relief. That narrow trigger is met here, the County did not carry its defense on the completed trial record, and statutory religious-discharge liability remains resolved without another same-decision trial or evidentiary opportunity.

**Controlling explanation:** The decisionmaker’s acknowledgment that the religious reprimand played a role, together with the connected testimony and protected activities encompassed by the reprimand, meets O’Connor’s narrower Price Waterhouse concurrence as well as the plurality. Mere religious awareness, stray remarks or unprotected resource use would not suffice; the Court settles no other disagreement among those writings.

The District Court treated inadequate performance and religious causation as mutually exclusive. Performance evaluations, delays, restructuring, budget concerns and the computer investigation nevertheless remain genuine evidence of legitimate reasons. Such reasons can justify discharge without proving that this discharge would have occurred on those reasons alone. On the whole authenticated trial account, including the administrator’s inability to say whether discharge would have followed without the religious reprimand and the insufficient contrary proof, the County failed its counterfactual burden. Uncertainty is not a universal conclusive admission; neither a sole legitimate cause, special objective-evidence category nor formulaic answer is required.

The alternative of returning the existing record for a factual finding respects Rule 52’s ordinary protection of permissible choices. Here the Court sustains the appellate determination that the completed record cannot carry the defense, rather than announcing power to replace any disputed trial finding. Reopening evidence is different again: the five-day trial afforded the opportunity, no relevant exclusion or newly announced burden is shown, and the August rehearing refusal preserves closure. Warner-Jenkinson’s contextual treatment of a newly express patent burden supplies no reset under this preexisting rule. No independent constitutional injunction request is established. The lower court’s coordinated Iowa disposition is preserved without an independent construction of Iowa law; the affirmed statutory race judgment is outside the religious grant.

Constitutional injury, causation, personal involvement and capacity, municipal responsibility and any properly preserved individual immunity remain distinct; no new defense or chance to undo settled invalidity is conferred. Later merits law does not furnish earlier conduct-time notice. ### 1997-04-07 — Opuku-Boateng v. California; Ninth Circuit No. 94-16542, 95 F.3d 1461; separate Supreme Court docket unassigned

Authority: [Record](../records/Opuku_Boateng_v_California_merits_1997-04-07.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification stated below.

**1. Applicants receive the same statutory protection.** Title VII's prohibition on refusing to hire because of religion and its definition of religion require reasonable accommodation of an applicant's known sincere religious practice on the same terms that protect an employee. Public employment creates neither an exemption from that duty nor an enhanced duty; prior temporary service is not a prerequisite.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 2000e-2(a)(1) and 2000e(j) expressly connect refusal to hire with religious observance and the employer's accommodation defense. Opuku-Boateng disclosed the Sabbath conflict, and the conceded prima facie case brings California's refusal to complete his appointment within that inquiry. The State's operational concerns therefore go to accommodation and hardship, not to whether an applicant is protected. The Court decides no separate constitutional claim.

**2. Supported predictions may establish hardship, but good-faith discussions are not themselves an accommodation.** An employer must offer an effective reasonable accommodation or show that accommodation would impose more than a de minimis hardship under the governing Hardison rule. When no effective accommodation has been supplied, that showing must address supported materially distinct proposals in the actual operation; it may rest on concrete noncash costs and reasonable, evidence-supported operational predictions without a trial arrangement, realized accident, complaint, preferred accommodation or exhaustion of every conceivable alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Hardison preserves protected seniority rights and legitimate costs or transferred burdens; Beadle and Brown require a practice-specific connection between the proposed arrangement and those burdens while allowing supported professional predictions. Ansonia ends the inquiry when an effective reasonable accommodation has already been offered. California discussed the conflict in good faith for a week, but those discussions produced no effective accommodation. Good faith is relevant conduct, not a substitute for either statutory answer. Nor does Title VII demand an experiment or make negotiation a freestanding prerequisite to an otherwise sufficient hardship defense. Noncontractual coworker burdens, necessary coverage, efficiency and security can count; hostility to religious practice and abstract future requests do not by themselves carry the burden.

**3. California's completed proof does not sustain its hardship defense.** On this record, neutral Saturday rotation and the asserted morale and operational concerns do not establish the required hardship from supported combinations of other undesirable shifts and voluntary trades. This conclusion neither declares every proposed arrangement feasible nor treats all shifts as equally burdensome to every employee.

**Controlling explanation:** Opuku-Boateng offered nights, Sundays, holidays and other undesirable work. The station had no established collective-bargaining or contractual seniority restriction, but that absence alone does not make all coworker interests immaterial. The State failed to identify the material burden transferred by the offered combinations. The poll's occurrence was admitted; its reported results were expressly not admitted for their truth and cannot establish collective unwillingness. Lozano addressed a mistakenly understood three-day absence, while Whitacre could not recall the question or number surveyed. Those limitations do not prove that every employee misunderstood, but leave the cited evidence insufficient to establish that adequate voluntary combinations were unavailable. The testimony favoring varied schedules to prevent collusion did not connect this limited Sabbath absence, with variation on other days, to a concrete security loss. Existing flexibility for courses and a minister's Sunday mornings reinforces the need for that specific comparison without creating an unlimited exemption. Rejecting one permanent substitute did not answer the supported combinations. The absence of an implemented experiment is not independently decisive.

**4. Clear-error review preserves permissible findings, not the use of excluded evidence or an incorrect legal rule.** Rule 52 protects supported trial findings and reasonable operational inferences. It does not require sustaining a hardship conclusion that depends on using evidence for a purpose expressly excluded, a materially mistaken description of the requested absence, or equating neutral rotation with adequate accommodation; the remaining completed proof here cannot carry the defense under the correct predictive standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The strongest argument for restoring the District Court's judgment is its experience with the station's needs and Hardison's low hardship threshold. Those considerations would support an evidence-based finding about scheduling, coverage, security or transferred burdens. They do not remedy the particular evidentiary and legal defects identified here. Beadle permits remand where materially distinct proposals remain for proper assessment; Brown permits preserving resolved liability when a completed record cannot sustain the existing defense. California had its trial opportunity under that defense, and no new burden or exclusion preventing its proof is established. The Court therefore affirms the Ninth Circuit's judgment without endorsing a compulsory experiment, exhaustive negotiation or realized-disruption rule, and does not reopen 1982 liability for a new hardship case.

Liability does not guarantee appointment, a transfer vacancy, a particular schedule, a fixed award or a continuing injunction; the employer may choose an effective accommodation, and a permanent schedule may be prescribed only if lawful and necessary after that opportunity.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII relief should place Opuku-Boateng as nearly as lawful and practicable in the position he would have occupied absent discrimination. Current staffing or hiring conditions may affect placement and prospective relief without reopening the failed defense to the original refusal to hire. The 1991 Act's new compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury entitlement do not apply retrospectively to the 1982 conduct, as Brown's temporal remedial distinction confirms. Ordinary mitigation, equitable discretion and fee predicates remain. No Free Exercise, individual constitutional-liability or mixed-motive question is decided, and no general right to a preferred shift is created.

#### Denton v. Hernandez, 504 U.S. 25 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1846.
**Decided:** May 4, 1992.

##### When a complaint filed without prepayment of fees may be dismissed as factually frivolous

**Holding:** A court may dismiss a claim brought in forma pauperis (without paying filing fees first) as factually frivolous under 28 U.S.C. §1915(d) when its allegations are clearly baseless—irrational or wholly incredible—even without judicially noticeable facts contradicting them. The initial assessment must favor the plaintiff; mere improbability, extraordinary circumstances, or ordinary disbelief does not suffice, and screening cannot resolve genuine factual disputes.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. These seven Justices adopt the rule and the judgment-supporting explanation. Stevens’s dissent, joined by Blackmun, agrees with this factual-screening standard but disagrees with the additional appellate remand; their agreement is not needed to establish controlling authority.

**Current-force modification:** The clearly-baseless factual-screening rule remains the holding; the original permission to dismiss states former §1915(d). PLRA §804 replaces the dismissal text with §1915(e)(2), which requires dismissal at any time, notwithstanding payment of all or part of a filing fee, if the poverty allegation is untrue or the action or appeal is frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim, or seeks monetary relief from an immune defendant. Admission of that command neither finds any claim frivolous nor supplies a new judicial construction of its categories; disputed application to pending proceedings remains unresolved.

**Material application:** The magistrate relied on the complaints’ combined pattern while acknowledging that each was not necessarily frivolous. Particular supporting affidavits must be considered without deciding truth from the pattern alone.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular district dismissal is affirmed, no allegation is found true or false, and no liability or damages are determined. The circuit’s categorical notice-and-amendment rule, an absolute right to amend and a universal preclusion rule for later fee applications remain undecided. Availability of an affidavit is not a condition of an arguable claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate judgment is vacated and remanded, 7–2, for review of Nos. CIV S-83-0645, CIV S-83-1348 and CIV S-85-0084 under the proper standards. Review must consider the Esquer affidavit, proposed Pierce affidavit and amendment, pro se status and aggregate-pattern rationale. The two unappealed dismissals and due-process and mattress claims outside the grant are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319 (1989): applied and clarified; its clearly-baseless category reaches irrational or wholly incredible allegations without requiring a judicially noticeable contradiction, while preserving the distinction from Rule 12(b)(6).
- Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S. 519 (1972): applied; liberal treatment of pleadings filed without counsel remains part of the screening inquiry and does not adjudicate their truth.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §804(a), replacement mandatory dismissal provision §1915(e)(2). This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

##### How appellate courts review a §1915(d) dismissal and account for its consequences

**Holding:** A dismissal under section 1915(d) is reviewed for abuse of discretion; an erroneous legal premise remains independently correctable as law. Review must address relevant circumstances, including pro se status, resolution of genuine factual disputes, legal errors, the adequacy of the stated reasons, prejudice, and whether more specific pleading could cure the defect; the dismissal is not a merits adjudication barring a paid complaint, although its possible effect on later in forma pauperis frivolousness determinations makes prejudice and curability material.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Seven participating Justices adopt this review rule and its remand instructions. No separate proposition establishes a categorical preclusion rule for every later fee application or an absolute right to amend.

**Current-force modification:** The review and nonmerits-consequence holding governs the former §1915(d) dismissal adjudicated. PLRA §804 replaces that discretionary dismissal provision with the mandatory grounds described in the preceding question. The enactment itself does not settle the appellate standard, amendment opportunity or preclusive consequences under the new text. Its separate §1915(g) condition bars a prisoner from bringing a civil action or appealing a judgment in a civil action or proceeding under §1915 after the prisoner, on three or more prior occasions while incarcerated or detained in any facility, brought a federal action or appeal that was dismissed as frivolous, malicious or failing to state a claim, unless the prisoner is under imminent danger of serious physical injury; it does not establish that any Denton dismissal qualifies or bar a paid complaint automatically. Pending-case application remains unresolved.

**Material application:** Abuse-of-discretion review does not insulate legal error. Screening does not adjudicate the merits or bar a paid complaint; possible effects on later fee applications make prejudice and curability relevant.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular district dismissal is affirmed, no allegation is found true or false, and no liability or damages are determined. The circuit’s categorical notice-and-amendment rule, an absolute right to amend and a universal preclusion rule for later fee applications remain undecided. Availability of an affidavit is not a condition of an arguable claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The appellate judgment is vacated and remanded, 7–2, for review of Nos. CIV S-83-0645, CIV S-83-1348 and CIV S-85-0084 under the proper standards. Review must consider the Esquer affidavit, proposed Pierce affidavit and amendment, pro se status and aggregate-pattern rationale. The two unappealed dismissals and due-process and mattress claims outside the grant are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Boag v. MacDougall, 454 U.S. 364 (1982): applied; statutory discretion does not preserve a dismissal resting on legal error, and reasons must permit intelligent appellate review.
- Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S. 519 (1972): applied to review of pleadings filed without counsel and the significance of potentially curable deficiencies.
- Neitzke v. Williams, 490 U.S. 319 (1989): applied; section 1915(d)’s screening function remains distinct from disposition of the merits under ordinary pleading rules.

**Holding:** The creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance. Subsection (A) excludes statements about the debtor's or insider's financial condition; (B) separately requires a materially false written statement of that kind, reasonable reliance and debtor-caused making or publication with intent to deceive. Fraud, reliance, justifiability, debt obtained by the conduct and the extent of that debt remain separate. Only actual fraud is decided, not a distinct false-pretense or representation-only theory or the nondischargeable amount.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darden's established-common-law-term method applies by analogy without its employment factors. Grogan preserves proof by a preponderance. Restatement §§537 and 540–541 distinguish justifiability from universal prudent-person investigation, using general common law rather than any State's tort rules.

##### Correct-standard review before a final nondischargeability determination

**Holding:** An adverse actual-fraud discharge ruling resting on the broader objective prudent-person investigation duty does not stand merely because its findings include individualized facts relevant to justifiability. The proper standard must be applied to existing findings, supplemented if necessary, with separate determination of properly remaining obtained-by, extent and other statutory issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct votes. Scalia and Breyer would affirm on the existing findings.

**Material application:** The bankruptcy court expressly required prudent-person investigation, including possible legal consultation despite accepting lack of sophistication. Findings of actual reliance, detriment and effective extension of credit survive, but do not establish that every dollar of the original note was obtained by later concealment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7–2, through the First Circuit for correct-standard application and needed findings. No immediate nondischargeability judgment on the whole debt is ordered; ordinary preservation rules remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The common-law standard and unchanged Grogan burden govern further adjudication without supplying automatic nondischargeability.


## Verbatim further bankruptcy authority

#### United States v. Noland, 517 U.S. 535

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-323.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Administrative penalties after conversion and susceptibility to equitable subordination

**Holding:** Allowed §503(b)(1)(C) postpetition tax penalties retain case-era §507(a)(1) administrative priority after conversion because §348(d) expressly excepts §503(b) claims from its ordinary treatment of post-order, preconversion claims. Priority does not immunize them from lawful §510(c) equitable subordination: §726 makes distribution subject to §510.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 726(a)(4)'s fourth rank applies to covered fines, penalties, forfeitures, and multiple, exemplary, or punitive damages arising before the earlier of the order for relief or appointment of a trustee, and only insofar as they do not compensate actual pecuniary loss. It does not demote every noncompensatory penalty. Section 510(c), after notice and a hearing, permits all-or-part distributional subordination of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, and transfer to the estate of a lien securing the subordinated claim. Both priority and the express exception retain effect; their coexistence establishes no conflicting congressional choices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No §510(a) agreement, §510(b) securities ground, or lien-transfer basis is established here. Susceptibility to equitable subordination does not prove a sufficient ground for its exercise or freeze equitable principles into a closed catalogue.

##### Insufficiency of the lower courts' class-based reasons

**Holding:** Section 510(c) does not authorize subordinating this statutory penalty class merely because the penalties compensate no pecuniary loss and payment reduces recovery for creditors who supplied value. The lower courts' stated reasons are legally inadequate even though they expressly disclaimed automatic subordination of every penalty. Lawful claim-specific equity remains available, but the Court establishes no other sufficient ground on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Noncompensation, insufficient assets, losses to value-giving creditors, and retained priority for tax and interest recur whenever this class competes with compensatory claims in an insolvent estate. Those reasons express the category-wide preference Congress's administrative classification rejects; calling them individualized balancing does not supply a different equitable circumstance. Statutory susceptibility and legislative statements about continued equitable development do not create a replacement ranking. Innocent-creditor and financing concerns cannot alone displace priority or bypass separate financing conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither requires misconduct universally nor establishes an affirmative entitlement to subordination without it. Permissible development is not frozen at 1978; particular inequitable conduct or another recognized claim-specific ground remains addressable when lawfully presented and proved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, 9–0. The same class reasons cannot preserve subordination under a new label. Any other consideration depends on the record and preservation; no factual reopening, misconduct hearing, lien transfer, distribution schedule, or payment amount is ordered. Penalty allowance and tax-and-interest priority remain unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ahlers* requires equity within the Code without itself construing §510(c) or prohibiting all equity. *Taylor v. Standard Gas*, *Pepper*, and *Comstock* preserve their misconduct-based applications without establishing a universal prerequisite. *Simonson*'s former statutory penalty prohibition supplies no present ranking. *Boteler* and *Nicholas* preserve postpetition penalties' distinct treatment and compliance function. *Things Remembered* supports giving compatible statutes separate effect; its remand-review ruling and separate equitable-remand theory do not authorize a new distribution rank.
