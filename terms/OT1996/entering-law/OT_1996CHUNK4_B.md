## Standards and Tests selection: State immunity of bistate compact entities

### State immunity of bistate compact entities

**Governing question:** When a bistate compact entity shares its constituent States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity.

**Current rule:** Exercise of governmental power alone does not confer the States’ immunity. The entity’s actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must give good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred. Exposure of the state treasuries is highly probative but not categorically indispensable; a separate, self-supporting enterprise responsible for its own obligations cannot claim immunity merely from its public functions.

**Authority by component:**

- PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity: PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity, 1994-11-14. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-14; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This compact-entity inquiry does not establish a universal sole-treasury test, decide abrogation or consent, or equate immunity with governmental status for constitutional-rights purposes.

**Holdings navigation:** PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

### Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

**Governing question:** How a court tailors a content-neutral injunction regulating speech-related conduct.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to serve the significant governmental interest supporting the remedy. This inquiry is more demanding than the ordinary content-neutral legislative time, place and manner rule, without becoming a universal least-restrictive-means test. Each restriction needs a remedial connection to the established interest and conduct. Public access, medical quiet and residential privacy may support relief, but neither those interests nor prior misconduct justifies territorial restrictions or advance-permission conditions broader than necessary. Removing an invalid restriction also removes its operative predicate; it cannot be restored by relabeling the same conduct under another clause.

**Authority by component:**

- Content classification and five-Justice injunction standard: Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — The decree as confined is not categorically content based; A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary, 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part II-A(2), joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this application while accepting the separate actual-concert principle. O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.
- Removal of invalidated predicates and lawful remedial discretion: Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — Relief removes invalidated predicates; Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone, 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part IV-B, joined by all eight other Justices: nine at the invalidated-predicate and no-evasion level. This agreement does not settle the permissible content of a new decree. O'Connor's Part IV-C, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight. Stone-Zsela dissents from the refusal to require his bounded post-refusal replacement.

- Coverage boundary retained in applying the speech-injunction rule: Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — An injunction binds responsible parties and actual concert, not a viewpoint, 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part II-A(1), joined by all eight other Justices: nine. The three dissenters on operational neutrality join only this distinct coverage principle.

**Present operation effective:** Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc, 1994-06-30.

**Exceptions and limits:** The particular distances and hours adjudicated are applications, not universal safe harbors. The decision does not require a personal buffer zone, decide the reserved independent conduct-language claims, or transplant Casey’s abortion-regulation framework into the advocacy inquiry. Coverage remains confined to responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert or participation; viewpoint, association or independent advocacy alone does not establish concert.

**Holdings navigation:** An injunction binds responsible parties and actual concert, not a viewpoint; Relief removes invalidated predicates; Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone; The decree as confined is not categorically content based; A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary.

## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

**Authority by component:**

The reasoned dismissal establishes this question-presentation ruling, not the merits of the judgment below.
- Confined subsidiary Teague defense: Caspari v. Bohlen — The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here, 1994-02-23. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).
- Geographic inclusion and independently justified exceptional review: Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc — The limited scope of geographic and paragraph (4) review; The independent conduct-language claims remain undecided, 1994-06-30. O'Connor's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight. Stevens declines these distinct merits questions. O'Connor's Part IV-A, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight on the Rule 14 ground. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the nonreach disposition for these independently disputed terms and applications and adds no ground.

- The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment: The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment, 1994-11-29. Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this reach ruling. Stone-Zsela joins only the dispositions on the two alternatives and adds no reach ground. Scalia and Thomas accept the legal availability of such defenses but do not decide either alternative because their independent ground would sustain the appellate judgment.

**Exceptions and limits:** Madsen’s separate noise-and-images review rested together on a continuing categorical restraint, direct connection to the clinic remedy, existing medical record, adversarial presentation and severable legal correction. It declined further independent conduct-language questions despite briefing. Those circumstances are not a universal mandatory checklist or permission to review every related clause.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

### Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause

**Governing question:** Appellate review of ultimate reasonable suspicion and probable cause after a nonwarrant search.

**Current rule:** Appellate courts independently decide whether established circumstances, objectively considered in their totality, supply reasonable suspicion for the stop and probable cause for the nonwarrant search. Ultimate sufficiency needs no clear-error finding. Historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review. Grounded contextual inferences of resident judges and experienced local officers receive genuine due weight, without controlling ultimate constitutional sufficiency. Experience does not establish an unproved observation, and no police-credibility presumption, numerical weight or intermediate standard follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Review allocation, objective totality and due weight: II. Ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently reviewed, with factual protection and genuine due weight, 1996-05-28; O’Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Ornelas, 1996-05-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gates’s substantial-basis review of an issuing magistrate remains distinct. Not every mixed question is independently reviewed. The rule creates no new hearing, remedy, preservation exception or immediate conditional-plea withdrawal right.

**Holdings navigation:** Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690.

## Standards and Tests selection: Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

### Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop

**Governing question:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause despite another investigative purpose.

**Current rule:** Objective probable cause of a traffic violation supports an ordinary initial stop notwithstanding an unrelated investigative aim. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce that violation absent that aim, or on compliance with customary assignment and enforcement policies. Manner, scope and duration remain independently constrained: a materially greater detention or search requires independent grounds. This rule does not remove purpose-specific conditions from inventory and administrative exceptions; inventories retain standardized criteria or established routine and genuine inventory purposes rather than investigatory rummaging. Purposeful racial selection remains independently prohibited by Equal Protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective initial-stop rule: Objective traffic probable cause supports this ordinary initial stop; independent execution and discrimination limits: Policy, execution and discrimination limits, 1996-06-10; Thomas’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Whren, 1996-06-10.

**Open question:** No discovery entitlement, immunity, suppression consequence or damages remedy for discriminatory selection is decided. Armstrong’s federal-charging threshold is not transferred to stop claims; traffic probable cause does not authorize every later intrusion.

**Holdings navigation:** Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

### Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

**Governing question:** Whether remedial silence or Spending Clause notice bars compensatory damages in the established Title IX private action.

**Current rule:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing relief does not itself create a new cause of action. Title IX's established private action permits compensatory damages for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation, and compensable loss. The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar that compensation for intentional breach of Title IX's express nondiscrimination condition.

**Authority by component:**

- Appropriate relief in an existing action and availability of Title IX compensation: Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools — Compensatory relief in the established Title IX action, February 26, 1992.
- Rejection of the categorical funding-notice bar: Franklin — Notice does not bar damages for the pleaded intentional violation, February 26, 1992. Both holdings rest on White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; Scalia and Thomas supply judgment votes only on a narrower statutory ground.

**Present operation effective:** *Franklin*, February 26, 1992.

**Open question:** The decision supplies no final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, liability for unintended discriminatory effects, or resolution of genuinely ambiguous funding conditions.

**Holdings navigation:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992) — Statutory discrimination remedies.

## Standards and Tests selection: Genuine pedagogy and religious perspectives in graded assignments

### Genuine pedagogy and religious perspectives in graded assignments

**Governing question:** A student’s challenge to rejection of a religious research topic within a teacher-assessed curricular assignment.

**Current rule:** Teachers may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns; religious conviction creates no exemption. But categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal ban on academic religious subjects, is not legitimate pedagogy merely because so labeled. Schools retain authority to select knowledge, method or perspective taught and to distinguish objective inquiry from testimony or devotional advocacy; no universal curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule follows. Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible academic justification where supported evidence permits a material inference of impermissible religious causation. The student must establish protected conduct and a substantial or motivating role; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent it.

**Authority by component:**

- Legitimate-pedagogy boundary and actual causal proof: I. Curricular authority must rest on genuine, legitimate pedagogy and II. The affirmative record leaves a material causal dispute, 1996-06-25; Souter’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Settle, 1996-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary genuine factual or evaluative errors may remain within pedagogical discretion; the rule creates no classroom forum, automatic topic approval, compelled grade, independent investigative duty or conclusive liability from an impermissible factor where the same-decision defense is proved.

**Holdings navigation:** Settle v. Dickson County School Board.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective and subjective requirements, totality and absence of an injury floor: Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc — Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury, 1993-11-09. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Present operation effective:** Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc, 1993-11-09.

**Open question:** A sex-specific reasonable-person formulation, constructive discharge and employer-attribution questions are not resolved by Harris.

**Holdings navigation:** Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-11; Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, for the components identified above.

#### United States v. Gaudin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. Statutory interpretation and threshold evidentiary sufficiency remain judicial functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship: applies proof beyond a reasonable doubt to facts establishing guilt.
- Sinclair v. United States: overruled to the extent it assigns a criminal pertinency element to the judge; unrelated propositions are not displaced.
- Kungys v. United States: distinguishes civil denaturalization and its appellate allocation.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: preserves the actual existing constitutional jury/burden holding; this case creates no universal omitted-element error classification.
- Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed false-statement convictions. Lawful retrial submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury remains available; no acquittal, disposition of the separate equity-skimming conviction or sentence calculation follows.


## Verbatim Holdings selection — Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. The reviewing court must nevertheless respect what occurred, the trial court's credibility role, local context and grounded police experience. Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane support allocating mixed questions by function rather than a single label; First Options preserves separate legal and factual review. Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White supply the substantive totality inquiry but do not decide who finally applies it on appeal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision concerns a different institutional task. Mere acknowledgment of an officer's inference is insufficient due weight, but experience cannot manufacture an unproved fact or entitle the officer's ultimate conclusion to a presumption of correctness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** There is no numerical weighting formula, intermediate standard, new evidentiary fact or police-correctness presumption. Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain deference for their task-specific discretionary determinations; Salve Regina's treatment of legal review remains. The Court imports neither habeas burdens from Thompson nor a general solution to every mixed question; Markman's reserved appellate issue remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane supply functional allocation of legal and factual review; Thompson's habeas setting does not transfer its burdens here.
- First Options preserves independent legal review and protection of historical findings.
- Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White retain objective totality-of-circumstances substantive standards.
- Gates's warrant-issuance substantial-basis rule is distinguished from independent review of a warrantless search.
- Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain their task-specific deference; Salve Regina supports independent legal review.
- Markman does not decide the reserved appellate-review question by analogy alone.

##### What follows for these two appeals and the district court's suppression findings?

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated and reconsidered under this allocation, without automatically ordering suppression or reopening the evidentiary record. Under section 636(b)(1)(B), the district judge independently determines specifically objected-to portions of the magistrate's recommendation and may receive evidence or recommit; that duty does not invariably require a new hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part III, joined by the same eight-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court accepted the credited loose-panel finding; rust was rejected, scratches were inconclusive, and actual NADDIS identification was unproved. Independent legal review cannot improve those facts by invoking experience. Raddatz permits the district judge's specified independent determination without an invariably repeated hearing; its concern about rejecting credibility findings is not triggered by the acceptance here. Section 636(b)(1)(A) does not set the ultimate suppression standard or the appellate allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the circuit's limited-consent premise: consent did not authorize dismantling the panel. It adopts no general consent rule and does not revive waived inevitable discovery. The allocation also governs authorized Government suppression appeals, but section 3731's actual conditions remain: the statutory restriction after jeopardy attaches and before verdict, required certification of no delay and substantial material proof, the thirty-day and diligence requirements, and double-jeopardy limits. No independent Government appeal is authorized here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Seventh Circuit judgments, reported at 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, are vacated and remanded, 8-1. The Court orders reconsideration of both constitutional thresholds, not suppression, plea withdrawal, release, a new evidentiary hearing or a particular ultimate result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Raddatz preserves the distinction between a required independent judicial determination and an invariably new hearing; its reserved credibility-rejection question remains untriggered.


## Verbatim Holdings selection — Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

#### Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5841.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Investigative motive and customary enforcement in an ordinary traffic stop

**Holding:** An ordinary initial traffic stop supported by objective probable cause of a traffic violation does not become unreasonable under the Fourth Amendment because officers also investigate an unrelated offense. Validity does not additionally depend on whether reasonable officers ordinarily would enforce the violation absent that other aim. Independent constitutional limits on manner, scope, and duration remain; detention or search materially exceeding what the traffic violation reasonably justifies requires independent grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous, including the application and qualifications.

**Material application:** The accepted observations of waiting unusually long at a stop sign, failing to signal, and unreasonable speed supply traffic probable cause. Petitioners' proposed objective “would-have” test receives the same rejection as a subjective-motive prerequisite: variable local priorities, uniforms, assignments, markings, and enforcement customs do not change constitutional authority for the known violation. A separate balance of ordinary traffic-safety gains against plainclothes-intervention anxiety supplies no additional requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inventory and administrative-inspection authority retain their genuine-purpose and standardized or other source-specific constraints; motive is not irrelevant to every constitutional question. General Order 303.1 compliance, its immediate-safety-threat exception for otherwise unauthorized plainclothes or unmarked enforcement, and its extreme-circumstances oral-warning exception are not adjudicated. Neither speed nor missing execution details establish an exception or constitutional execution finding. The later search is not independently presented.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Scott* applies objective assessment of justified action; *Robinson*, *Gustafson*, and *Villamonte-Marquez* retain their distinct lawful-arrest, search, and customs-inspection predicates without creating new power. *Prouse* distinguishes suspicionless discretionary stops; *Martinez-Fuerte* and *Brignoni-Ponce* do not require a further balance for this ordinary probable-cause stop. *Bertine* and *Wells* preserve inventory constraints and forbid investigatory ruses; *Burger* preserves purpose-limited administrative inspection. *Abel* and *Bannister*'s regular-procedure and no-pretext observations do not establish the proposed customary-enforcement condition. *Wilson* preserves reasonable-execution review with its supported danger, futility, escape, and evidence-destruction qualifications, leaving suspicion quantum and automatic suppression open. *Garner*, *Welsh*, and *Winston* retain their distinct deadly-force, home-entry, and bodily-intrusion limits.

##### Review of the accepted predicate and separation of discriminatory selection

**Holding:** The accepted traffic observations permit resolution under *Ornelas*: historical facts, credibility, and genuinely factual subsidiary findings or inferences receive clear-error review, with genuine due weight to grounded contextual inferences; the legal standard and ultimate constitutional sufficiency properly at issue receive independent judgment. No new factual inquiry is required to decide this legal-rule challenge.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Due weight means substantive consideration of supported trial and local-officer context, not a numerical weight, officer-credibility presumption, or intermediate standard insulating constitutional conclusions. **Holding:** A content-neutral injunction burdening protected expression must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve the corresponding significant governmental interest. ##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. - McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: special religious disabilities distinguished from neutral regulation.
- Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693: the individualized-exemption discussion supports requiring a compelling reason for denying religious hardship treatment where comparable secular reasons are assessed.

**Holding:** A law is not generally applicable where it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct threatening the interests invoked to burden religious exercise. **Holding:** A restriction of religious practice that is nonneutral or not generally applicable must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. **Independent alternative holding:** Where the government burdens protected religious conduct but leaves appreciable comparable harm unregulated despite feasible controls, it has not established that its asserted interest justifies the selective restriction at the highest constitutional level. Hialeah’s substantial underinclusion independently defeats its compelling-interest showing for this scheme; the decision does not declare animal welfare or public health inherently unimportant.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Material application:** Ordinance87-40 incorporates Florida’s unnecessary-or-cruel-killing rule as construed to reject religious necessity while accepting comparable secular reasons. Ordinance87-52 prohibits owning, keeping, possessing, sacrificing or slaughtering enumerated animals or any other animal with intent to use it for food, and limits application to killing in any ritual regardless of whether flesh or blood will actually be consumed; its separate exception allows licensed establishments to slaughter animals specifically raised for food where properly zoned or permitted under state/local law and Agriculture Department rules. Ordinance87-71 defines sacrifice as unnecessary killing, tormenting, torturing or mutilating in a ritual or ceremony not primarily for food consumption and prohibits it citywide. Ordinance87-72 requires food slaughter in a properly zoned slaughterhouse meeting health, safety and sanitation codes, but separately exempts persons or organizations slaughtering or processing for sale small numbers of hogs or cattle weekly in accordance with the state-law exemption. The distinct exclusions, comparable secular harms and available neutral controls support the holdings; 87-72’s independent validity in another setting remains reserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enactment-history discussion lacks a majority. No exemption regime replaces Smith, no universal general-applicability definition is adopted, and exemption from a genuinely evenhanded anti-cruelty law remains undecided. Narrow tailoring and the compelling-interest failure independently sustain invalidation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief against ordinances 87-40, 87-52, 87-71 and 87-72 as presented. No damages or immunity ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Star v. B.J.F., 491 U.S. 524: substantial unregulated comparable harm undermines the asserted exceptional justification; no broader rule is imported from a separate writing.
- Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: the asserted interest must justify the particular restriction at the required level.

### 1996-11-04 — California v. Roy, No. 95-2025; October Term 1996, chunk 1.

Authority: [Record](../records/California_v_Roy_summary_review_1996-11-04.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The existing official appellate party may seek review

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1254(1) requires a petitioner who was a party in the court of appeals; on this record, that court's repeated identification of the Attorney General as an official-capacity respondent and the timely petition expressly naming the incumbent in that capacity satisfy the requirement. The Court accepts the existing official party's identity despite the contrary earlier custodial pleadings; it neither adds a party nor holds that a caption, counsel's participation or a State's interest invariably establishes party status.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejecting the objection and reaching the case, without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the official litigant identified by the court of appeals may seek review through the incumbent Attorney General. Scofield requires a party below; Perini keeps that statutory requirement distinct from injury. California's interest in its conviction alone would not suffice.

The amended habeas petition names Gomez and Borg, the answer identifies them as proper respondents, and the subsequent order substitutes Merkle for Borg. Those documents establish custodial participation and replacement. They do not supply an appellate ruling removing the Attorney General's office. The June 1995 panel opinion, September rehearing order and April 1996 en banc opinion repeatedly name Van de Kamp as a respondent. The Court accepts that appellate identification on these competing documents. Lungren's timely, expressly official petition identifies the incumbent of the same office.

FRAP 43(c)(2) and FRCP 25(d)(2) permit designation of an existing official-capacity party by title, subject to the court's authority to require a name. Their corresponding automatic-substitution provisions operate when an existing official-capacity party leaves office during the respective appellate proceeding or action. They disregard an immaterial misnomer, and omission of a formal substitution order does not defeat substitution. These rules continue an existing party; they do not create one. The Court does not assume that the change of Attorney General occurred during this appeal or this Court's proceeding. The appellate identification is the decisive predicate, not an imagined intervention or succession order.

Smith v. Barry supports reading a timely document for the litigant it actually identifies, while retaining mandatory party information. No custodian is added to the petition under Rule 12.4, and Rule 35.3 does not establish original party status below. Whether the People separately qualify is unnecessary to decide. The Attorney General's appearance as counsel does not independently establish party status.

**Precedent treatment:** Scofield and Perini retain their actual-party requirement; Smith is applied to identification in a timely filing without dispensing with that requirement.

##### II. This personal-intent omission permits harmless-error review under Chapman

**Holding and operative rule:** The preserved omission of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery is a constitutional trial error subject to harmlessness review, not an error requiring automatic reversal merely because an element was omitted. For this personal-intent violation properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict; neither collateral posture nor absence of accompanying prosecutorial argument substitutes a weaker burden.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously. Agreement on this category and burden does not establish agreement on the method or result of harmlessness review.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the jury's responsibility to determine the defendant's required intent remains protected, while the effect of this instructional omission may be examined. Gaudin requires jury determination of an offense element but does not classify every failure to submit an element as structural. Sullivan addresses a different defect: an unconstitutional reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings leaves no valid reasonable-doubt verdict to examine. The omission here does not destroy that standard throughout the charge.

O'Neal requires the State's Chapman showing for its preserved personal-intent category. That case involved combined instructions and prosecutorial argument permitting conviction without the defendant's own required intent. This charge permits the same constitutional failure by omitting the purpose to facilitate robbery. An additional prosecutorial statement is not the basis of the protection and supplies no reason to withhold it here. Knowledge of the perpetrator's unlawful purpose and affirmative assistance were required, so mere presence was insufficient; neither knowledge nor assistance is legally identical to the omitted purpose.

The State retains the burden. Roy need not prove innocence, and the reviewing court may not supply the missing intent itself. This decision does not adopt Chapman for every constitutional habeas claim, reopen a resolved exhaustion issue, or decide the temporal operation of AEDPA.

**Precedent treatment:** Gaudin's jury-allocation rule is applied and its structural-error reservation respected; Sullivan's rule remains intact at its stated scope; O'Neal's protected personal-intent category applies to the omission without accompanying argument; Chapman's actual-contribution burden remains the governing burden.

##### III. Necessary equivalence is sufficient, but not the exclusive permissible inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Chapman, the State may establish that this omission did not contribute to the actual verdict through supported whole-record inferences as well as findings necessarily embracing the omitted purpose; it may not substitute evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction, equate knowledge plus assistance with purpose, or infer purpose from general mental capacity. Under Yates, the court must examine whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting the proposed inference; if the omission kept the relevant personal-purpose question from the jury so that the inference cannot reliably address its actual verdict, strong evidence alone does not carry the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, controls by five direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports the remand on his separately stated whole-record Chapman ground but does not join Part III's further analysis. Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg dissent from the remand. No combination of separate opinions or narrower-ground synthesis supplies this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the inquiry concerns what the error did to the verdict actually returned. Yates requires examination of the evidence the instructions put before the jury and distinguishes actual contribution from proof sufficient to convict. Necessary equivalence between a finding and the omitted purpose is one sufficient route. It is not invariably the only way to determine the effect of this knowledge-and-assistance charge.

The Ninth Circuit expressly found the omission not harmless under Chapman before applying a separate collateral-review analysis. Remand is warranted because its necessary-findings restriction foreclosed the qualified actual-effect inquiry, not because Chapman was never considered. The charge submitted knowledge, affirmative aid and related mental-state questions. That permits examination of their connection to the actual verdict, without assuming the jury considered the omitted purpose. The receiving court must address the possibility that the omission itself prevented the relevant consideration; inability to establish a reliable connection defeats the State's showing.

The record requires more than an overwhelming-evidence label. Possession of property, wet clothing, assistance testimony and reported statements must be assessed with the verdicts and the conflicts in those accounts. The clothing allocated to McHargue in the lower opinions cannot be treated as undisputed property in Roy's backpack. Hudspeth's impeachment and rebuttal and Hall's reported statements and retraction matter together; no verdict establishes wholesale acceptance or rejection of every witness. The possible inference that Roy helped a companion without intending to facilitate robbery is an inference to assess, not a newly found event. The State's and district court's functional-equivalence conclusions are legal assessments, not express jury findings. These matters remain for the proper inquiry, and the Ninth Circuit may again conclude that the State fails its burden.

**Precedent treatment:** Chapman and Yates are applied together to permit a supported actual-effect inquiry while preserving the jury's role; neither sufficiency alone nor a hypothetical verdict satisfies them. O'Neal's whole-record command remains subject to that actual-verdict requirement.

##### IV. The limited remand preserves the record, argument and remedial boundaries

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is vacated for a qualified Chapman review of the preserved personal-intent claim affecting the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions, with District Court proceedings if otherwise proper; no present harmlessness finding follows. If an available violation is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, conditional relief must allow the State a reasonable period to retry those convictions; proper harmlessness permits denial, and neither this remand nor the special-circumstance argument restores reversed punishment, affects the Clark conviction or orders immediate release.

**Authority:** Part III's remand instructions have the five direct joins stated above; Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote and agrees separately with the Chapman and reasonable-retrial remedy. The five-Justice rationale itself controls without enlarging Stone-Zsela's join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that renewed review must apply the proper method to the supported record and respect genuine preservation limits. O'Neal distinguishes a corrected harmlessness inquiry from an automatic writ and requires a reasonable retrial opportunity if an available violation warrants relief. No fixed retrial deadline is set.

Yee distinguishes an additional argument about a preserved question from a new constitutional claim. The special-circumstance argument concerns this preserved harmlessness question, but Roy's objection to its first-instance use still requires consideration. The asserted oral concession is not established, and the objection alone creates no forfeiture.

The instructions must be read together. CALJIC 8.80's intentional-aid requirement applies conditionally to a defendant who was not the actual killer; it is not an unconditional finding of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery. Absence of personal knife use does not itself eliminate every actual-killer theory. CALJIC 8.81.17's robbery-related purpose of the killing differs from Roy's own aiding purpose. The state reversal prevents restoration of the special-circumstance punishment; it does not automatically determine every evidentiary implication of the findings. General specific-intent or diminished-capacity instructions do not silently insert the omitted purpose clause.

The Court therefore adopts neither an automatic special-circumstance cure nor a categorical exclusion of the findings from consideration. It supplies no new trial finding, admissibility ruling on a juror declaration, exhaustion bar, or factual resolution from an unavailable transcript passage. Preservation, independent habeas requirements and any event-date statutory transition remain distinct. This remand addresses the identified method defect, without reviving a resolved gate or ordering new factfinding merely because a source is unavailable.

**Precedent treatment:** Yee preserves the distinction between a new argument and a new claim without compelling disputed first-instance application; O'Neal supplies the conditional-relief and reasonable-retrial rule, without automatic release.

### 1996-11-18 — Ohio v. Robinette; No. 95-891

Authority: [Record](../records/Ohio_v_Robinette_merits_1996-11-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Review of the interwoven federal and state grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** When a state judgment fairly appears primarily federal or interwoven with federal law and does not clearly state an independently sufficient state ground, the Court may review its federal basis. Ohio's intertwined constitutional explanation supplies no clear independent ground barring review here; Ohio remains free to supply greater protection on genuinely independent state-law grounds.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by six direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees to exercise review without joining this explanation; Ginsburg and Stevens accept review in their separate writings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's reference to its own Constitution does not end the jurisdictional inquiry. Michigan v. Long supplies the rule for opinions that rest on federal law or interweave federal and state law without a clear independent basis. Arizona v. Evans retains that rule. The Ohio opinion invokes both provisions and develops the suppression analysis with federal authorities; it does not announce that an independent Ohio ground alone sustains the judgment. Federal review therefore lies. Deciding the federal question neither erases the Ohio provision nor prevents the Ohio courts from adopting a clearly independent state rule in further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Long and Evans are applied without changing the independent-state-ground rule or creating an exception to the ordinary final-judgment requirement.

##### II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer's uncommunicated investigative motive or private decision to issue only a warning does not invalidate an objectively lawful traffic stop or an exit order while lawful traffic business is being completed. Once that justification ends, further detention requires independent legal grounds; the court must examine the objective circumstances to determine whether the person could terminate the encounter, and return of a license is neither an automatic end of seizure nor an automatic license to continue it.

**Authority:** Part II.A's rule concerning motive and the exit order has six direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Part II.B's distinct continued-detention rule has those six plus Stone-Zsela, seven. Stone-Zsela supplies no additional reason for Part II.A.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an objectively justified beginning and a lawful continuation are separate questions. Whren rejects subjective purpose as an extra condition for an ordinary probable-cause traffic stop while preserving limits on manner, scope and duration. Mimms permits an exit order during a lawful traffic stop without a separate justification for that direction. The Ohio court could not deem the direction unlawful merely because the deputy privately intended a warning or hoped to ask unrelated questions.

That correction does not establish that detention lawfully continued through the later consent request. Bostick requires an objective assessment of whether the person could decline the request or terminate the encounter. The returned license and completed warning bear on that inquiry, but so do the immediacy and actual wording of further questioning, tone, positioning and continuity of official authority. Neither the officer's secret willingness to permit refusal nor Robinette's subjective impression alone decides the seizure question. Supported trial findings matter; the Court does not replace the record with a categorical conclusion in either direction. Ornelas's distinction between historical fact and ultimate constitutional sufficiency remains applicable within its decided scope; this decision announces no universal appellate-review rule for every mixed or consent question. The encounter's classification remains for proper application below.

**Precedent treatment:** Whren's objective initial-stop rule and independent duration limits remain intact; Mimms is confined to lawful traffic business. Bostick's actual-circumstances inquiry is applied. Ornelas is not extended into a general rule for all consent issues.

##### III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not invariably require an officer to give a free-to-go warning before seeking consent to search. Consent must be voluntary under all the circumstances, and when an unlawful detention preceded or accompanied it, voluntariness alone does not establish that the consent was sufficiently independent of that illegality to permit use of the evidence.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls by seven direct joins. Ginsburg and Stevens separately reject an indispensable federal script; neither additional agreement is needed to establish the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a useful warning is not automatically an indispensable constitutional condition. Schneckloth requires voluntary consent under the total circumstances and treats knowledge of the right to refuse as relevant without making advice of that right universally mandatory. A fixed release formula would replace that inquiry rather than perform it. The absence of a clear release signal nevertheless remains important evidence; rejecting a script does not make every post-warning request consensual.

The supported trial findings concerning the manner, wording and positions of the parties, Robinette's education and the conclusion of traffic business must be considered with the returned license and actual continuation words. His testimony includes both an initial belief that he could leave and a later feeling that he could not refuse. Neither fragment decides voluntariness by itself. The Court holds neither that this consent was valid nor that it was invalid.

Royer and Brown keep consent's voluntariness separate from its causal independence of unlawful detention. If the court finds an unsupported continued seizure, it must address that additional question; voluntary consent does not automatically erase the illegality, and illegality does not establish that every later consent is incurably tainted. Suppression remains available when the applicable inquiry fails. Nothing in federal law prevents Ohio from requiring clearer warnings under independent state law or police policy.

**Precedent treatment:** Schneckloth is applied without a new warning element. Royer and Brown preserve the separate inquiry into the effect of prior illegality; no automatic cure or automatic suppression rule is created.

##### IV. The remedy corrects the federal rules without deciding admissibility

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the warning rule and the motive-based exit-order premise requires further proceedings on the actual objective detention, voluntary consent and any necessary attenuation question. It does not itself establish the search's validity, require admission of the evidence or reinstate the conviction.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer directly join Part IV; Stone-Zsela joins its stated inquiries and limits while selecting vacatur instead of reversal. Ginsburg separately supports reversal with the same unresolved-application boundary. Seven Justices support the Court's formal reversal and remand; the holdings do not merge Stone-Zsela's different remedy into that coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ohio's two grounds must be addressed separately. The federal script requirement fails, and the detention ruling cannot rest on undisclosed motives during lawful traffic business. Yet those errors do not decide whether the later encounter was consensual, lawfully justified or unlawfully extended, or whether consent was both voluntary and independent of any illegality. The state courts must apply the rules to the supported findings and complete encounter. They may consider genuinely independent state law. A new hearing is not commanded merely by this remand, and the Court enters no final evidence-admission or conviction order.

**Precedent treatment:** The rule/application distinction retained in Evans and the separate consent and causation inquiries in Schneckloth, Royer and Brown govern the scope of remand without enlargement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Michigan v. Long:** Applies the presumption permitting review of interwoven federal grounds absent a plain independent-state-law basis.
- **Arizona v. Evans:** Retains that jurisdictional rule and the distinction between correcting a federal rule and deciding its unresolved factual application; its court-record-error exclusion rule is not extended here.
- **Whren v. United States:** Applies objective legality to the initial traffic business and preserves independent limits on manner, scope and duration.
- **Pennsylvania v. Mimms:** Applies the permission to order a driver out during a lawful stop; no indefinite post-stop detention authority follows.
- **Florida v. Bostick:** Requires assessment of the actual objective capacity to decline requests or terminate the encounter, without a categorical seizure or release rule.
- **Ornelas v. United States:** Preserves its historical-fact/ultimate-sufficiency distinction within its actual scope; no general review rule for all consent questions is added.
- **Schneckloth v. Bustamonte:** Applies total-circumstances voluntariness and treats knowledge as relevant rather than an indispensable warning requirement.
- **Florida v. Royer:** Preserves the need to consider whether consent follows or remains a product of unlawful detention.
- **Brown v. Illinois:** Preserves attenuation as distinct from voluntariness; neither a voluntary response nor the occurrence of illegality alone answers the remedial question.

#### Law After Decision

Federal law imposes no universal release script, but the objective seizure, voluntary-consent and necessary attenuation inquiries remain distinct and enforceable. The decision supplies no categorical rule that a returned license ends a seizure, that a traffic offense authorizes later unrelated detention, or that a polite request ensures admissibility. The actual search result remains unresolved under the corrected federal standards, and genuinely independent Ohio protection remains available.

### 1997-01-06 — DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association; Fifth Circuit No. 93-8700, 51 F.3d 591; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

#### Holdings

#### I. The credited course of publication does not meet Title VII's objective threshold

**Holding and operative rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when the complete circumstances establish conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as abusive; no physical advance, superior-subordinate relationship, psychological injury or measurable work loss is indispensable. Crediting this verdict record and favorable reasonable inferences, the supported course of newsletter commentary falls below that objective threshold, so the Association's hostile-environment judgment is affirmed.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; six direct votes support this rule and application. Stone-Zsela's judgment vote supplies no additional statutory join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Harris's all-circumstances inquiry, which preserves an objective boundary while rejecting injury and productivity prerequisites. Meritor supplies the requirement that discriminatory harassment alter working conditions. The ten reproduced columns must be considered together, including general comments about women, the references associated with DeAngelis, extensive workplace circulation, humiliation and alleged interference with command. General comments are not excluded merely because they do not name her.

The jury's favorable inferences receive their proper force. Even on that account, the irregular sequence, degree, limited direct targeting, editorial setting and actual workplace responses establish offensive intermittent commentary rather than the required abusive alteration. Broad circulation and repeated ridicule matter, but do not eliminate that statutory distinction. Good ratings, upheld discipline and supportive responses are contextual evidence, not indispensable defenses. Neither an invitation to respond nor the membership's protection of the author's anonymity creates consent or a numerical safe harbor.

The lower opinion's suggestions that equal opportunity must be destroyed or work performance palpably deteriorate are disapproved. Citation to Harris alone would not cure those suggestions. The Court instead independently applies the correct standard and sustains the judgment on that ground. It neither disbelieves DeAngelis's testimony nor requires physical threats, advances or supervisory harassment. Because the statutory ground is sufficient, the Court does not decide the preserved First Amendment defense. It adopts no newsletter immunity and no general rule that offensive expression cannot constitute harassment.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris is applied without adding a prerequisite; Meritor's alteration-of-conditions boundary remains controlling. No constitutional speech precedent is extended by this holding.

#### II. The complaint-related publications establish no actionable retaliatory consequence here

**Holding and operative rule:** Protected activity and hostile commentary do not alone establish Title VII retaliation without a cognizable adverse consequence. The mocking EEOC reference, routine minutes reporting the complaint and announced intention to sue if the action proved groundless establish no denial of an employment or Association benefit, concrete imposed penalty or comparably supported material disadvantage on this record; judgment for the Association is therefore affirmed.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes opposition to a discrimination complaint from a proved retaliatory consequence. The publications reveal disparagement and contemplated conditional litigation, but do not establish lost pay, promotion, assignment, authority, membership, representation or a comparable imposed disadvantage caused by the complaint. No actual retaliatory lawsuit or unreported sanction is found. The same record can raise distinct hostile-environment and retaliation questions; disagreement on the former does not supply the missing consequence for the latter.

This record-specific conclusion does not confine all actionable retaliation to ultimate employment decisions. It does not hold that threats, harassment or public shaming can never establish a materially adverse consequence on stronger facts, and it decides no comprehensive standard for every retaliation claim. The Court does not infer failure from missing research materials or convert the opportunity to answer the newsletter into a condition of protection.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's distinction between offensive treatment and legally sufficient discrimination is preserved at its hostile-environment scope; its injury rule is not converted into a comprehensive retaliation test.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harris v. Forklift Systems: applies the objective and subjective totality inquiry and rejects mandatory work-loss, injury, advance and hierarchy requirements; the present application adds no such requirement.
- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson: applies the requirement of discriminatory alteration of employment conditions; extreme examples remain illustrations.
- Wisconsin v. Mitchell, Hurley and R. A. V.: their existing holdings remain intact. The Court does not reach the expressive defense, adopt R. A. V.'s separate content-selection rationale, or decide a general immunity for association publications.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 6, 1997, the particular newsletter record is insufficient under Harris even with testimony and reasonable verdict-favorable inferences credited. Title VII retains its contextual severe-or-pervasive test, with no numerical publication threshold or mandatory tangible injury. Retaliation fails here for lack of a supported material consequence; the decision leaves broader retaliation categories open. No controlling First Amendment rule is added. Stone-Zsela's expressive ground and Stevens and Ginsburg's proposed hostile-environment remand remain noncontrolling.

### 1997-01-21 - Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth; historical petition identifier No. 95-617; no separate merits docket assigned; OT1996.

Authority: [Record](../records/Curtis_v_School_Committee_of_Falmouth_merits_1997-01-21.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Every holding below has nine direct rationale votes.

##### I. The optional program does not establish the claimed parental-liberty infringement

**Holding and operative rule:** The described school condom-availability program does not violate parental liberty or familial privacy merely because parents cannot require the school to exclude their children or notify them of participation. Program entry is optional, abstention carries no educational or disciplinary consequence, and the program does not prohibit parental teaching, alter custody, compel treatment or sexual conduct, or require assent to a message; the junior-high counseling condition applies only after a student elects to request condoms.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Meyer and Pierce, which protect parental direction of upbringing and education, together with Yoder's protection against the particular compulsory educational interference with religious family life and Prince's recognition that parental authority does not exclude every public role concerning children. Those decisions do not establish the particular school-enforced veto sought here.

The school provides the item directly, not merely information about it. That difference matters, but it does not itself displace parental authority. Parents remain free to teach against participation and instruct their children to abstain. Students may comply without losing instruction, grades, benefits, or standing at school. Compulsory school attendance does not turn every separable optional resource into compelled participation.

A junior-high student requesting condoms must receive counseling before receipt. The Court does not erase that condition or approve unknown counseling content. Nothing in the stated condition requires entry into the program, a prescribed affirmation, sexual activity, or a medical procedure. No independent coercive-counseling claim is established here.

A. St. P. C. protects against a complete, potentially indefinite parental-contact cutoff; Baby Richard protects against serious avoidable harm in the manner of compulsory custody transfer; M.L.B. protects necessary-record access in a provided first appeal from permanent parental termination. None of those deprivations occurs here. Practical difficulty in securing a child's adherence to parental teaching does not, on these facts, establish a constitutional right to compel the school to enforce the requested refusal.

**Limits:** This holding does not decide confidential medical treatment, prescription drugs, compelled sex education, distribution to a child unable to consent, or a program using penalty or deception to undermine parental authority. It neither denies parental liberty nor recognizes an independent constitutional entitlement to receive condoms.

##### II. The stated program imposes no established constitutional burden on religious exercise

**Holding and operative rule:** A program that students may decline without sacrificing ordinary education or another benefit, and that requires neither religiously prohibited participation nor assent, does not burden these families' free exercise merely by making the described condoms available contrary to their religious teaching. The stated program establishes no religious targeting, unequal treatment, penalty for religious refusal, or comparable official pressure to participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Lyng and Bowen v. Roy distinguish sincere conflict with government activity from interference with protected religious exercise; they do not place all government services beyond constitutional review. Here the school demands no endorsement of contraception or premarital sex, no use of condoms, and no surrender of worship or religious instruction. The junior-high receipt condition does not compel anyone to enter the program and supplies no established contrary religious affirmation.

The parents' concerns about peer influence and compulsory attendance deserve consideration. Lee's treatment of substantial official pressure at school-directed graduation prayer remains intact. Students here may avoid the separable resource without giving up the educational occasion or apparently joining a state-directed religious exercise. The asserted generalized peer influence does not establish comparable official pressure under these program terms. The holding therefore does not depend on a universal rule that only formal punishment can coerce.

The claim fails at its constitutional burden threshold. The Court need not decide whether Smith would independently defeat a claim involving an actual burden under a neutral law. Invoking parental liberty and free exercise together supplies neither the missing infringement nor automatic strict scrutiny. Lukumi's religious-targeting protections and Settle's prohibition on supported religious exclusion remain fully applicable to materially different facts.

**Limits:** The Court does not approve religious targeting, compelled participation in sexual instruction, denial of an exemption from a mandatory practice, or benefits conditioned on conduct forbidden by faith. The separate statutory inquiry under RFRA is not decided.

##### III. Neither parental exclusion nor notification is constitutionally required on these grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** Because neither claimed constitutional deprivation is established by this program's stated operation, the Constitution does not require the requested parental exclusion or notification mechanism, and summary judgment for the school defendants stands on the granted constitutional claims. No injunction, new evidentiary proceeding, or prescribed parental procedure follows.

**Controlling explanation:** An exemption from compelled conduct differs from a parental right to bar a child's voluntary access. The child can already decline this program without consequence; the claimed school-enforced veto requires a substantive constitutional source that the presented parental and religious claims do not supply. Casey permitted a qualified parental-consent arrangement for abortion while preserving an independent judicial route; permission for such a statute does not command parental control over every preventive item.

The parents' expert affidavits concerning possible adverse consequences are not findings of harm or harmlessness. Under the adopted no-infringement grounds, disputed policy wisdom and comparative health effects do not create a material constitutional fact requiring trial. The judgment does not establish that the program is medically necessary, effective, harmless, or the least restrictive policy.

**Limits:** The Court does not prohibit voluntary parental accommodation, resolve different consent or disclosure laws, or decide an independent RFRA claim. Unargued state statutory claims concerning statutory-rape accomplice liability, abuse reporting, and moral education, and unpresented state constitutional claims, remain outside review. No finding that a medical-consent claim was waived is made.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meyer v. Nebraska, 262 U.S. 390, and Pierce v. Society of Sisters, 268 U.S. 510:** Apply the protected parental role without extending it to compulsory school enforcement of the refusal sought here.
- **Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205, and Prince v. Massachusetts, 321 U.S. 158:** Preserve their distinct treatment of family religious direction and public authority concerning children; neither compels the requested veto over this optional resource.
- **Lyng v. Northwest Indian Cemetery Protective Association, 485 U.S. 439, and Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693:** Apply their distinction between conflict with belief and interference with protected exercise, without creating immunity for all school services.
- **Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872:** Does not supply an additional neutral-law holding; pleading combined interests does not itself establish an infringement. Its application to a genuinely burdening program is unnecessary to decide.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992:** Preserves the rule against substantial official participation pressure in the school-directed religious exercise there. The separable optional resource here does not present the same state-created pressure; formal voluntariness is not universally conclusive.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah, June 11, 1993, and Settle v. Dickson County School Board, June 25, 1996:** Preserve protection against religious targeting and supported religious exclusion; no such act is established by this program's stated terms.
- **Planned Parenthood v. Casey, June 29, 1992:** Distinguishes permission for the abortion-consent statute from a constitutional command for parental control over this item. Outside its medical-emergency exception, the sustained statute ordinarily requires the minor's and one parent's informed consent, allows family substitutions, and permits independent court authorization without first approaching a parent, on maturity and actual informed consent or alternatively best interests. Nothing here modifies that statute's safeguards or makes parental involvement an absolute veto.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C., November 20, 1995; Baby Richard v. Kirchner, June 21, 1996; and M.L.B. v. S.L.J., December 16, 1996:** Distinguish their respective complete-contact cutoff, compulsory custody-transition injury, and first-appellate-review termination barriers. Their rules remain unchanged.
- **Church of Scientology v. Clearwater, December 5, 1994; Swanner, March 27, 1995; and American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995:** Preserve RFRA's separate person-and-application burden and justification inquiry. None supplies a constitutional rule that neutral administration can never burden religion; no statutory RFRA claim is resolved here.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 21, 1997, the described optional school program requires neither the requested parental exclusion nor notification mechanism under the presented parental-liberty and free-exercise theories. The decision establishes that confined constitutional boundary; it creates neither general school-health immunity nor a child's constitutional entitlement to the service. Existing family-status protections, actual-pressure and religious-targeting rules, qualified abortion-consent law, and RFRA's separate statutory inquiry retain their prior force.

### RFRA review of prospective enforcement

**Governing question:** Application of original RFRA to threatened enforcement, including preexisting laws and commercial religious exercise.

**Current rule:** The claimant must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise. The government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The inquiry applies to threatened future implementation of a preexisting law. Commercial participation does not forfeit its protection, and labeling a law antidiscrimination does not itself justify its application. Direct transactional and dignitary injury and legally material alternatives or exemptions may be considered; availability of alternative housing is not an automatic answer to direct injury.

RFRA also governs later federal enactments unless Congress expressly excludes application by reference to RFRA; FACE contains no such exclusion.

**Authority by component:**

- RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication: RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication, 1994-12-05. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that RFRA governs, but does not join the opinion or its disposition of these adverse determinations.
- RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws: RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws, 1995-03-27. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five direct rationale and judgment votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer dissent from the vacatur. Their agreement that RFRA governs does not turn their distinct application into Court law.

- Subsequent federal enactments and separate prospective-enforcement inquiry: RFRA requires further adjudication of the prospective-enforcement claim, 1995-11-13; O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five). The claimant’s substantial-burden threshold and government production/persuasion sequence remain unchanged; the full own-minor FACE remedy exclusion must be considered as enacted, without presuming it is comparable to an outsider blockade or that an equivalent state remedy exists.

**Present operation effective:** Church of Scientology v. Clearwater, December 5, 1994; Swanner, March 27, 1995, clarifies the commercial-participation and application-specific limits without changing the burden sequence; the component authorities identified above.

**Current wording authority:** American Life League, 1995-11-13, preserves the later-enactment condition and applies the existing person-specific inquiry; it changes no burden sequence.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary standing and appropriate-relief limits remain. RFRA protects religious belief and preserves the Establishment Clause; this statutory inquiry does not replace the constitutional free-exercise or establishment standards. A remand under it establishes neither an exemption nor failure of the government’s eventual showing.

**Holdings navigation:** RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication; RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws; American Life League v. Reno.

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.
