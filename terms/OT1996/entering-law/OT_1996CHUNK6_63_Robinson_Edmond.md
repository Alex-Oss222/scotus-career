#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

### Religious neutrality in public school government

#### Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-517, 93-527 and 93-539.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### The State may not select a denomination as the recipient of general governmental authority

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause forbids a State deliberately to allocate general civil authority to a community selected as a religious community, even when the immediate recipients are elected public lay officials rather than clergy. Chapter 748 violates that rule because its design and context use denominational membership as the organizing basis of the special school district; religious homogeneity or coincidence with an existing village line alone would not establish the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II introduction and II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg: five Justices. This is an independently sufficient ground. O'Connor does not join that portion, and Kennedy's separate judgment rationale is not counted as a join.

**Material application:** Chapter 748 grants all powers of a union-free school district through a board of five to nine members elected by village voters for terms not exceeding five years. It contains no special-education-only limitation, sunset, religious voter or office qualification, or rabbinical appointment. The secular public program and professional staffing do not narrow that general statutory grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Larkin v. Grendel's Den, Inc., 459 U.S. 116: applies its civil-authority boundary by structural analogy to deliberate denominational allocation through a lay public board; it does not treat this board as a church or clergy veto.
- Larson v. Valente, 456 U.S. 228: applies denominational neutrality to the State's choice of recipients of general public authority; no general disability on religious communities follows.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: preserves equal political citizenship and distinguishes religious officeholders from governmental allocation by denomination.

**Independent alternative holding:** Chapter 748 independently violates religious neutrality by conferring an anomalously particular benefit of general school-government power on this selected denominational community without neutral terms or dependable assurance of equal treatment for comparable communities. The defect follows from this enactment's design and context, not a rule that every special act is invalid, every general statute is neutral, or an unproved finding that another religious community has already been refused.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This is an independently sufficient alternative ground; Kennedy expressly declines it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Larson v. Valente: applies the prohibition on denominational preference to the particular governmental benefit and its allocation.
- Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos, 483 U.S. 327: distinguishes its general nonprofit religious-employer accommodation from a special denominational franchise; its permission to accommodate remains effective.

##### Accommodation preserves educational access but does not cure this allocation

**Holding:** A State may accommodate religiously grounded needs beyond what free exercise independently requires, but accommodation does not authorize this denominational allocation of general governmental power. Affirmance leaves the children's statutory special-education rights and lawful neutral local public services intact, without commanding a particular placement, validating the adequacy of earlier services or prescribing a replacement district.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II-C and the implementing limits in Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This holding rejects the accommodation defense and defines the remedy's limits; Kennedy reaches the judgment on his separate ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos: preserves permissible accommodation beyond free-exercise compulsion, with its denominational-neutrality and application limits.
- Zorach v. Clauson, 343 U.S. 306: preserves lawful accommodation of private religious needs without authorizing this governmental franchise.
- Wolman v. Walter, 433 U.S. 229: preserves the possibility of properly administered neutral-site services; no particular replacement is approved.
- Board of Education v. Wieder, 72 N.Y.2d 174, 527 N.E.2d 767: respects the state court's recognition of local educational discretion without turning permission into a compulsory separate school.
- Aguilar v. Felton, 473 U.S. 402, and School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball, 473 U.S. 373: neither is overruled, modified or used to order restoration of the terminated annex program.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602: remains governing law; this bounded neutrality decision neither replaces its framework nor imposes a new universal analytical sequence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling rationale is synthesized under Marks from Kennedy's different religious-boundary theory or other separate writings. The Court does not replace Lemon, overrule Aguilar or Ball, create an aid-merits holding in Zobrest, revise Shaw, decide the state constitutional issue, or find religious voter qualifications, religious instruction, mandatory assimilation, a denied request by another faith, permanent religious demographics or a free-exercise entitlement to a separate district. It neither selects individual placements nor requires elimination of culturally responsive secular instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Federal invalidation of Chapter 748 is affirmed, 7–2, in all three dockets. Implementation must preserve statutory educational duties and lawful neutral services. The Court prescribes no replacement district, individual placement, transfer date, damages or repayment, and does not invalidate the village, close private schools, restore the discontinued annex program or resolve the reserved state constitutional question.

### Private religious displays in public forums

#### Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-780.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

For the other questions in Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette, decided June 29, 1995, No. 94-780. see Federal Courts — Mootness and seasonal permits (The expired permit dispute remains reviewable).

##### Ohio’s categorical Establishment Clause defense cannot sustain this exclusion

**Holding:** Ohio may not exclude this genuinely private religious display from Capitol Square’s actual, generally available forum on the categorical ground that its religious message and proximity to the Statehouse necessarily establish religion. The judgment concerns the proposal presented, including its offered private-attribution safeguard; it neither finds the later sign adequate nor creates an unconditional right to every unattended display.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Parts II–III, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: seven votes for this bounded speech and access holding. Those seven do not adopt one comprehensive Establishment Clause test.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The offered readable clarification of private sponsorship had negotiable wording; it is distinct from the later actual sign and the injunction's terms. No finding establishes approved final wording or the sign's adequacy. Government-sponsored displays, actual religious favoritism and future rules remain open. Parts II–III have seven direct joins; Part IV's four-vote contextual explanation and the three-vote private-access rationale supply no controlling comprehensive test or Marks subset. No categorical forum immunity or universal disclaimer requirement is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Widmar v. Vincent: applies protection of religious expression in a genuinely open forum, while preserving examination of an actual establishment defense.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District: applies its controlling protection against exclusion of a religious perspective from comparable private access; its contextual reasoning is not converted into categorical forum immunity.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman and County of Allegheny v. ACLU: not overruled; the Court does not replace existing establishment law with a comprehensive test in this case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit at the original scope of the Count One merits judgment: the December 21–24, 1993 permit period remains expired. No new permit, extension, sign condition, removal order, damages or remand is entered. Unappealed rally, damages and other matters remain outside review.

#### Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1047.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

For the other questions in Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller, decided June 29, 1995, No. 93-1047. see Federal Courts — Mootness and seasonal permits (The recurring access dispute remains live, and only the Rotunda claim is reviewed).

##### Georgia’s categorical defense cannot sustain summary judgment excluding the private religious display

**Holding:** A State that actually opens a designated forum to comparable private unattended expression may not exclude an otherwise eligible religious viewpoint merely by asserting that its Capitol location necessarily establishes religion. On this record that categorical defense does not justify judgment for Georgia; lawful access must be provided on the neutral terms governing comparable private expression, with the actual form of relief left for proceedings below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part II, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight votes for this bounded judgment and speech holding; Stevens dissents from its application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The indoor designated forum actually admits comparable sizeable unattended exhibits for several days. The Court does not endorse all the en banc reasoning or guarantee an unconditional display, and comparable secular access cannot be retained while excluding the otherwise eligible religious viewpoint.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: applies the actual designated-forum status and preserves lawful neutral management and prospective forum changes.
- Widmar v. Vincent and Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District: apply protection of comparable private religious expression and the prohibition on religious viewpoint exclusion.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: applies the bounded rejection of a categorical establishment defense, not a universal forum exemption.

##### Interior location does not itself make clearly private expression governmental religion

**Holding:** Neutral access does not establish religion merely because a genuine private-expression forum lies inside a state capitol, when the actual context makes clear that the religious expression remains private rather than governmental. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preferential treatment or obscured private sponsorship can change that conclusion; neither a forum label nor any unspecified sign automatically resolves attribution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part III, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes for this contextual rule. The additional decree-specific inquiry proposed in Part IV has only O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; it is not a five-Justice mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The identifying greeting names Chabad but does not expressly disclaim state sponsorship; an offered disclaimer is not a finding of an effective completed safeguard. The four-Justice additional decree-specific attribution inquiry is not part of the mandate. Neither a universal sign duty, preferential religious access nor a duty to keep the forum open is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- County of Allegheny v. ACLU: preserves contextual examination of apparent governmental affiliation, rather than title alone.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: preserves its shared access holding and distinct attribution rationales; no categorical immunity is drawn from its separate opinions.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman: remains in force; no comprehensive replacement establishment test is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit reversal of State summary judgment on Count II for further proceedings and lawful relief on neutral terms. No Supreme Court permit, retrospective 1991 display, fixed sign, permanent seasonal entitlement, damages amount or compulsory forum closure is ordered. Count I remains undisturbed; lawful physical, safety, scheduling and identification rules and genuinely prospective viewpoint-neutral forum closure or redefinition remain possible.

### Private student publications and religious viewpoints

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.
- Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen: apply neutral-benefit and private-direction distinctions within the actual program, not neutrality alone.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet: distinguishes denominational governmental authority and retains religious-neutrality limits.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.
- Lee v. Weisman: preserves limits on governmental religious participation and coercion; this private printing benefit compels no student worship.
- Church of Scientology v. Clearwater: distinguishes RFRA burdens and exemptions from constitutional financing authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the religious-viewpoint exclusion under otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms. Examine any properly available independent Virginia funding defense consistently with the federal viewpoint holding, without declaring applicability, validity or revival of forfeited defenses. No full payment, damages, immunity, fee or compelled-fee refund is determined.

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

Interior location does not itself make clearly private expression governmental religion; Religious viewpoint in the private publication program; Permissible printing support on the actual program terms.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lemon remains governing law. No universal disclaimer duty, unconditional right to unattended displays, obligation to fund every religious activity, duty to keep a forum open or automatic immunity for nominal private sponsorship is created. Neutral identification rules may be considered; they are not ordered. The four-Justice additional attribution inquiry in Chabad is not a majority mandate.

### 1997-03-17 — Brown v. Polk County; Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313, 61 F.3d 650 (en banc); no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Polk_County_merits_1997-03-17.md).

#### Holdings

All eight Associate Justices directly join Parts I–IV of the Court’s opinion. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also joins Parts I, II and IV. Part III’s same-decision and closed-liability reasons therefore command eight votes; the other holdings command nine. No proposition rests on aggregation of separate writings.

###### Part I — Religion-specific commands exceed the demonstrated justification

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer may enforce lawful controls on work, resources, coercion and official religious activity, but a religion-specific prohibition extending beyond those concerns into private religious expression and personal religious possession must satisfy the constitutional justification its discrimination demands. The undifferentiated witnessing, proselytizing and counseling command and removal of all religious items, including a private desk Bible, fail that inquiry at their demonstrated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith preserves neutral rules, while Lukumi requires justification and tailoring for religious targeting. Preventing official worship, supervisory pressure, favoritism and diversion of paid resources supplies legitimate and potentially compelling concerns. Those concerns do not justify suppressing religious possession without an established operational consequence or every act described in religious terms. Narrower lawful controls cannot save the overreaching directives actually issued.

The supervisor setting matters. Voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayers are not automatically private, and nominal voluntariness cannot erase coercive pressure or government adoption. The Court need not recognize a general constitutional right to organize worship during department meetings to decide the orders’ excess. Pickering, Connick and Givhan retain the actual employment-speech inquiry. NTEU addresses broad burdens on protected citizen expression without converting supervisory instructions into citizen speech; Waters concerns genuine reliance on a disciplinable account, not blanket religious suppression. Pinette establishes no comprehensive majority test, and Chabad and Good News do not make this office a forum. The free-exercise ground suffices; no new equal-protection tier or constitutional discharge rule is adopted. A Title VII violation does not automatically establish a First Amendment violation.

###### Part II — Accommodation turns on the actual practice and supported burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §2000e(j), the employee must establish the sincere practice, employment conflict, employer knowledge and adverse treatment implicated by the claim; the employer must demonstrate effective reasonable accommodation or inability to accommodate without more than a de minimis hardship under the governing law. Concrete noncash burdens and supported professional predictions count without realized injury, an experiment or an actual complaint; an effective accommodation need not be preferred, and no independent duty to negotiate or try every alternative remains after effective accommodation or sufficient hardship is shown.

**Controlling explanation:** Hardison, Ansonia and Beadle require attention to the practice and actual operational justification. Diverting a paid secretary to Bible-study work supplies a sufficient resource burden. The particular prework office use likewise establishes no protected access: the County treats arrival as starting work and prohibits personal resource use, and no public or limited forum or comparable allowed personal use was shown. That conclusion is not a categorical prohibition of off-clock prayer.

The department-meeting prayers and one Christian work-ethic reference require separate assessment. The testimony described voluntary, spontaneous and irregular prayer, no compelled participation, and a single reference. Indirect reports, absence of direct complaints or observed discomfort, testimony denying favoritism and accounts attributing disputes to organization did not connect those practices to the asserted polarization or favoritism burden. Supervisor status remains relevant but is not conclusive proof of coercion. Infrequency is no immunity, and realized disruption is not indispensable; the Court rejects those broader suggestions below and sustains the result because the actual proof fails under the correct predictive standard. The proved cost of secretarial work cannot be exported to every religious act. Knowledge and the absence of attempted accommodation alone do not decide liability, and no new opportunity to invent coercion or retry the completed hardship question is ordered.

###### Part III — The original mixed-motive defense fails on this completed record

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the original Price Waterhouse rule applicable to this pre-1991 conduct, direct decisional evidence that protected religion was a substantial factor actually relied on, proved by a preponderance, shifts to the employer the burden to prove by a preponderance that it would have made the same decision without that consideration; success defeats liability, not merely relief. That narrow trigger is met here, the County did not carry its defense on the completed trial record, and statutory religious-discharge liability remains resolved without another same-decision trial or evidentiary opportunity.

**Controlling explanation:** The decisionmaker’s acknowledgment that the religious reprimand played a role, together with the connected testimony and protected activities encompassed by the reprimand, meets O’Connor’s narrower Price Waterhouse concurrence as well as the plurality. Mere religious awareness, stray remarks or unprotected resource use would not suffice; the Court settles no other disagreement among those writings.

The District Court treated inadequate performance and religious causation as mutually exclusive. Performance evaluations, delays, restructuring, budget concerns and the computer investigation nevertheless remain genuine evidence of legitimate reasons. Such reasons can justify discharge without proving that this discharge would have occurred on those reasons alone. On the whole authenticated trial account, including the administrator’s inability to say whether discharge would have followed without the religious reprimand and the insufficient contrary proof, the County failed its counterfactual burden. Uncertainty is not a universal conclusive admission; neither a sole legitimate cause, special objective-evidence category nor formulaic answer is required.

The alternative of returning the existing record for a factual finding respects Rule 52’s ordinary protection of permissible choices. Here the Court sustains the appellate determination that the completed record cannot carry the defense, rather than announcing power to replace any disputed trial finding. Reopening evidence is different again: the five-day trial afforded the opportunity, no relevant exclusion or newly announced burden is shown, and the August rehearing refusal preserves closure. Warner-Jenkinson’s contextual treatment of a newly express patent burden supplies no reset under this preexisting rule. Landgraf’s temporal principle preserves the original defense; the 1991 Act’s altered consequences would change the legal effect of completed conduct and no express retrospective command is supplied.

###### Part IV — The remedial tracks remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Remand addresses appropriate relief on the two constitutional restriction claims and, separately, backpay and reinstatement on the established statutory religious-discharge claims. The constitutional claims require their own lawful compensatory or declaratory predicates, while the statutory remand concerns amount, mitigation and equitable conditions and does not reopen liability or guarantee reinstatement.

**Controlling explanation:** Brown actually requested a declaration and compensatory damages for the constitutional restrictions. Complaint language suggested constitutional discharge, but no argument supported it at trial closing or in appellate argument or briefs; the Court adds neither that theory nor constitutional backpay or reinstatement. No independent constitutional injunction request is established. The lower court’s coordinated Iowa disposition is preserved without an independent construction of Iowa law; the affirmed statutory race judgment is outside the religious grant.

Landgraf and Rivers preclude retrospective use of §102’s new Title VII compensatory and punitive damages and dependent jury election. They do not eliminate preexisting statutory employment relief or independently available §1983 compensation. Constitutional injury, causation, personal involvement and capacity, municipal responsibility and any properly preserved individual immunity remain distinct; no new defense or chance to undo settled invalidity is conferred. Later merits law does not furnish earlier conduct-time notice. Arizonans creates no automatic mootness where actual compensation claims against the County and identified officials remain; prospective declaratory relief still requires its own continuing controversy. Nabozny preserves those defendant-specific and remedial distinctions without reopening this completed trial.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Employment Division v. Smith: neutral work and resource rules remain permissible; the challenged orders target religion.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah: its targeting and tailoring requirements invalidate the demonstrated religion-specific excess.
- Trans World Airlines v. Hardison: the governing more-than-de-minimis hardship rule is applied, including real operational and third-party burdens.
- Ansonia Board of Education v. Philbrook: effective reasonable accommodation suffices without the employee’s preferred alternative or proof against every option.
- Beadle v. City of Tampa: requires practice-specific hardship and permits supported professional prediction and concrete noncash cost without an accident or negotiation duty after sufficient accommodation or hardship.
- Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins: applies the original liability-stage defense at the narrow direct/substantial-reliance trigger, with the employer’s preponderance burden; no later mixed-motive regime is substituted.
- Pickering v. Board of Education: preserves actual public-employee speech balancing rather than an undifferentiated religious ban.
- Connick v. Myers: preserves public-concern and operational-context inquiries.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District: preserves private expression and attention to manner, time and place.
- United States v. National Treasury Employees Union: broad prospective restrictions require justification for their actual protected-citizen-speech reach; supervisory expression does not automatically fall within that category.
- Waters v. Churchill: its limited genuine-reliance and factual-error holdings remain; no constitutional termination claim is added here.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: supplies no comprehensive majority establishment test or automatic workplace attribution.
- Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller: private access and government adoption remain contextual; no office forum is established.
- Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue: its actual private-use arrangements do not establish an entitlement to official workplace worship.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products: its temporal principle preserves the preenactment liability regime, separately from its bar on retrospective §102 damages and dependent jury relief.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express: the retained temporal distinction supplies no elimination of preexisting relief or independent constitutional compensation.
- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.: its contextual new patent burden creates no right to reopen this existing and fully tried employment defense.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona: preserves an actual live controversy and available remedy without automatically extinguishing these compensation claims.
- Nabozny v. Podlesny: individual responsibility, municipal attribution, conduct-time notice and relief remain separate; its summary-judgment disposition supplies no new Brown liability trial.

#### Law After Decision

A public employer retains lawful controls on resources, coercion, harassment and official religious activity, while religion-specific commands must fit their constitutional justification. Statutory accommodation requires the actual practice and supported burden to be assessed separately; predictions may suffice, but the County’s proof did not establish the asserted burden for these meeting practices. The original mixed-motive defense has been resolved on the completed record. Constitutional compensation and declaration questions and statutory backpay/reinstatement questions remain distinct and do not reopen the adjudicated liability.

### 1997-03-17 — Brown v. Polk County; Eighth Circuit No. 93-3313, 61 F.3d 650 (en banc); no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Polk_County_merits_1997-03-17.md).

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins Parts I, II and IV and supports Part III’s disposition without adopting its reasons. He additionally explains that public-employer control should turn on attribution and actual function: the County may control its own supervisory expression and restrict coercive or resource-dependent activity, while genuinely private citizen speech on public concern receives Pickering balancing against actual or reasonably forecast operational disruption. Under NTEU, a broad prospective ban needs justification commensurate with its actual protected-speech burden.

He would examine authorship, voluntariness, time, location, control, resource use and supervisory pressure to distinguish private expression from government worship. Objectively grounded Establishment concerns can justify tailored limits, especially for supervisor-led prayer at mandatory meetings; offense or abstract fear cannot establish those concerns by itself. Employee-initiated voluntary prayer, private conversation and personal items cannot all be treated as official directives, but nominal voluntariness does not immunize coercion. His account preserves neutral resource limits, forbids treating every Title VII violation as automatically constitutional, and leaves genuinely unresolved applications to the proper remedial proceedings. It adds no constitutional discharge claim, mandatory workplace forum or reopening of the completed statutory liability determination. No other Justice joins this additional framework.
