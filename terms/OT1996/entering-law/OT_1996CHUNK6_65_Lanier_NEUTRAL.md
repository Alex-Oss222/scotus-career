## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** United States v. Lanier
* **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 259 (1997); No. 95-1717
* **October Term:** 1996
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued criminal merits case reviewing reversal of convictions under 18 U.S.C. § 242 on constitutional fair-warning grounds.
* **Simulated Event Date:** March 31, 1997 (historical)
* **Question Presented:** Whether criminal liability under 18 U.S.C. § 242 requires a prior Supreme Court decision applying the constitutional right to fundamentally similar facts, or whether pre-existing law gives adequate fair warning when the unlawfulness of the charged official conduct is apparent.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the Sixth Circuit imposed an unduly restrictive fair-warning rule by requiring a factually similar Supreme Court precedent before § 242 could reach a state judge’s sexual assaults under color of law.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The en banc Sixth Circuit set aside Lanier’s convictions, holding that § 242 supplied insufficient notice because no Supreme Court case had applied the asserted constitutional right to a fundamentally similar factual situation. United States v. Lanier, 73 F.3d 1380 (6th Cir. 1996) (en banc).
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks vacatur and remand for application of the proper fair-warning standard. Lanier seeks affirmance of the judgment setting aside his convictions.
* **Material Record:** While serving as a Tennessee state judge, David Lanier was charged with sexually assaulting women through the authority and circumstances of his office. A jury convicted him on seven § 242 counts after being instructed that the Government had to prove willful deprivation of the victims’ Fourteenth Amendment liberty right to be free from sexually motivated physical assaults and coerced sexual battery. He received consecutive sentences totaling twenty-five years. A Sixth Circuit panel affirmed, but the en banc court vacated for lack of fair notice under a rule requiring this Court to have recognized the right in fundamentally similar facts.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court reviews the legal fair-warning standard, not whether the full trial record ultimately satisfies every element. Section 242’s willfulness requirement, the clarity of the underlying constitutional right, and due-process notice must be analyzed together. Civil qualified-immunity doctrine is an analogy, not a source that automatically defines criminal liability.

### Issue 1: Fair warning under § 242

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied simulated holding squarely defines § 242 fair warning. Simulated Elder v. Holloway establishes that clearly established law is assessed from the complete materially relevant body of law, but civil qualified immunity remains analytically distinct from criminal notice.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Criminal law must give fair warning through statutory text, authoritative construction, and clearly established constitutional law. Vagueness, rule of lenity, and due process do not require a prior case with identical facts when general law makes the prohibition apparent.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. § 242; Due Process Clause; Screws v. United States; Bouie v. City of Columbia; United States v. Harriss; civil clearly-established-law cases as limited analogy.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Required specificity of precedent

* **Reasoning Below:** The Sixth Circuit required a Supreme Court decision identifying the constitutional right and applying it to fundamentally similar facts.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Fair warning can arise from general constitutional rules whose application is obvious; the Constitution does not require a fact clone or exclusive Supreme Court precedent.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** Criminal punishment under § 242 demands especially precise notice, and the broad substantive-due-process formulations available before the conduct did not clearly criminalize the charged acts under federal law.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** The Court may vacate the categorical rule and remand for the Sixth Circuit to evaluate the pre-existing legal landscape count by count.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Rejecting a fact-identical-precedent requirement does not permit conviction on a novel or unforeseeable constitutional theory.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacatur and remand follow if fair warning does not demand a fundamentally similar Supreme Court case and the appellate court must instead ask whether pre-existing law made the unlawfulness apparent. Affirmance would require accepting the Sixth Circuit’s categorical source and factual-similarity constraints or concluding that no reasonable application of the correct standard could supply notice. A narrow remand avoids deciding the ultimate sufficiency of the legal landscape for every count.
