## Standards and Tests selection: Official dissemination of intimate marital and family information

### Official dissemination of intimate marital and family information

**Governing question:** Intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination of objectively intimate information entrusted for a limited criminal investigation.

**Current rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects this confined intimate marital and family interest against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination without sufficient governmental justification. An assurance of confidentiality informs the objective expectation but does not alone create a federal right; consent to investigative use is not consent to general publication. Ownership or authorship is not indispensable to a person's own intimate interest. Public inspectability weighs against secrecy but does not automatically establish prior dissemination, voluntary exposure or the lawfulness of distinct official facilitation of publication. The protection concerns intimate personal information, not reputation alone.

**Authority by component:**

- Confined protected interest and justification: I. Unjustified official dissemination of this intimate material violates protected personal liberty, 1996-04-29; Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Sheets, 1996-04-29.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate investigation and retention, lawful accountability and legally required disclosure retain their separate rules. There is no universal strict-scrutiny or least-intrusive-means test for personal, financial or business information, general deletion right, inherited privacy claim, liability for every state confidentiality breach or restriction on the private author’s speech. Independent municipal attribution and conduct-date qualified immunity remain. The unobjected trial intent instruction does not establish a universal new mental-state rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Sheets v. Salt Lake County.

#### Hodge v. Jones

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-1182; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** October 23, 1995.

##### Damages immunity for the preserved confidential-record retention claim

**Holding:** The individual officials have qualified immunity for the challenged 1989–1990 retention of the cleared, confidential child-protection investigation report because the asserted federal disclosure, expunction and procedural-treatment duties were not then clearly established. Whether the preserved conduct violated a constitutional or state-created substantive right remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgment on a noncontrolling merits ground.

**Material application:** The retained report recorded clearance, not parental dangerousness. The preserved claim establishes no removal, interference with a particular parental decision, public publication, benefit or employment loss, or revoked clearance. Statutory access by specified recipients was limited, not absolute secrecy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal tangible-loss requirement, general authorization of confidential records, inaccurate entries or disclosure is adopted. The distinct erroneous AMF sexual-abuse code was not pleaded. The lower court's characterization of AMF as the statutory central registry is neither an enacted command nor resolved here. Constitutional informational privacy, any disputed substantive statutory entitlement, broader disclosure and fees remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm judgment for the individual defendants, unanimous in judgment. Prospective mootness is a distinct holding under Federal Courts, Hodge, October 23, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 93-1182.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Elder governs particularized historical notice; Whalen concerns personal information and confidentiality; Paul separates reputation from protected deprivation; Roth and Olim separate substantive interests from procedure alone. Collins preserves the distinction between state duty and federal injury. Albright's fractured views impose no controlling confinement-or-monetary-loss rule. Doe's physical-abuse holding neither decides informational retention nor supplies retrospective notice.

#### Sheets v. Salt Lake County

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

Competing testimony concerning the assurance and notice is not replaced with appellate findings. No necessary criterion of the Court's confined rule lacks factual support; declining the circuit's broader compelling-interest/least-intrusive-means formula therefore requires no new trial.

### Sheets v. Salt Lake County

Merits decision, April 29, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

In October Term 1995, the Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the Tenth Circuit's January 10, 1995 judgment, 45 F.3d 1383, in appellate Nos. 93-4128 and 93-4134. The question is whether officials violated Gary Sheets's own constitutional confidentiality interest by giving a private author access to intimate material entrusted for a murder investigation.

After Kathy Sheets died in a 1985 bombing, Gary supplied her diary to investigators. Farnsworth testified to a confidentiality assurance and its communication to investigators; Bell could not recall an assurance, and George's notice was disputed. The diary described Gary's intimate marital and personal life. After the investigation ended in 1987, the investigative file, including the diary, was archived and publicly inspectable.

Investigator Michael George allowed his friend, author Lindsey, to examine his file box; other authors received answers without comparable access. George had copied intimate entries and kept detailed notes, which he destroyed before Lindsey's book appeared in August 1988. The jury inferred that George disclosed the material and caused its publication. The County's unwritten practice permitted discussion of closed cases with the press subject to evenhanded treatment. Gary seeks redress for his own injury, not his wife's surviving personality, copyright or ownership alone. All nine Justices participated at consideration and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2, within the reviewed constitutional-confidentiality question.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Justices Scalia and Thomas.

The constitutional ruling does not independently settle or reopen limitations, evidence, municipal policy and causation, remittitur, the amount of fees or personal immunity. Any actually preserved independent defense retains its governing law and procedural posture; none is presumed unresolved. Recognition of this right today supplies no automatic notice at the earlier conduct date, and personal immunity is distinct from County liability.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivers the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–II, joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All seven directly adopt both controlling propositions, their explanations and the bounded affirmance. Justice Scalia dissents, joined throughout by Justice Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Unjustified official dissemination of this intimate material violates protected personal liberty

**Controlling proposition:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects a person's objectively intimate marital and family information against intentional official disclosure causing public dissemination when the information was entrusted for a limited criminal investigation and the disclosure lacks sufficient governmental justification on this record. A confidentiality assurance informs the objective expectation but does not alone create the federal right; consent to investigative use does not surrender the information to general publication.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by the seven Justices identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Whalen distinguishes avoiding disclosure of personal matters from autonomy in important decisions. It upheld safeguarded collection and reserved unwarranted dissemination. Nixon considered legitimate personal privacy independently of ownership while sustaining protected archival screening. Those decisions supply the foundation, but neither resolved this disclosure. The Court now protects this confined intimate interest; it does not attribute today's application to an already complete confidentiality test.

Gary asserts exposure of his own marriage and personal life. Kathy's authorship does not make her the only person affected. Paul rejected reputation injury from an official arrest record; this claim concerns intimate information, not unfavorable statements alone. Collins's refusal to turn state duties into a federal workplace guarantee likewise does not decide this affirmative disclosure. Neither a promise nor a breach of Utah's confidentiality rule would suffice without the independently protected interest.

The file became publicly inspectable after 1987. That fact weighs against an expectation of secrecy, but does not itself establish prior public dissemination of these passages, Gary's voluntary exposure or the lawfulness of George's distinct facilitation of their publication. The Court does not invalidate the archive-access regime. Access by legitimate investigators also differs from transferring intimate material for a private author's use.

Trial counsel invoked public interest in the sensational crime and its subjects, although no compelling disclosure interest was advanced on appeal. General interest in the crime does not establish a sufficient governmental connection to publishing these intimate passages. Legitimate investigation, lawful retention, public accountability and legally required disclosure remain distinct; none supplies the missing justification for this transfer on the supported record. The decision regulates the official conduct at issue, not the private author's speech. It creates no general right to deletion or inherited claim, and imposes no universal strict-scrutiny, least-intrusive-means or fixed balancing test for every personal, business or financial record.

##### II. Independent legal review and supported trial findings establish this application

**Controlling proposition:** The Court independently determines constitutional coverage and legal sufficiency while reviewing the denial of Rule 50 relief under the ordinary standard favorable to the prevailing party's supported evidence and reasonable inferences. The objective instructions and supported findings establish the confined right's application here; the unobjected instruction requires intentional acts causing deprivation, not specific intent to the author's ultimate publication or knowledge that the acts violate the Constitution.

**Authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Constitutional protection does not arise whenever a plaintiff wishes to keep something private. The jury was instructed on intimate home, marriage and family information, the expectation of a reasonable person in Sheets's position and his preponderance burden. Those objective criteria and the supported intimate subject permit the Court to decide legal coverage without adopting a wholly subjective preference. Markman's patent-construction allocation does not transfer these historical facts and credibility choices to appellate judges, or surrender the governing constitutional rule to the jury.

Farnsworth's testimony supports an assurance and its communication; Bell's failure to recall it and disputed notice to George do not become contrary appellate findings. Lindsey identified possible official sources. Farnsworth denied disclosure; Cannon lacked the diary and detailed notes; George acknowledged detailed excerpts and access to his files. Only Lindsey's book quoted the diary. Taken favorably to the verdict, that circumstantial evidence supports intentional disclosure by George and resulting publication. A direct admission or eyewitness account was unnecessary. Excluded writings unconnected to these passages do not prove that the diary's intimate contents were already public.

The defendants did not object to the intent instruction. The Court neither adds a purpose to violate a known right nor requires a newly found specific desire for Lindsey's eventual publication. It also does not treat lawful investigative sharing itself as the deprivation. The protected interest and supported causal disclosure distinguish this case from confidential retention.

No additional necessary criterion adopted here lacks factual support. A new trial is therefore unnecessary merely because the Court declines to adopt the circuit's comprehensive compelling-interest and least-intrusive-disclosure formulation. The judgment stands within the reviewed question; independent municipal requirements, conduct-date immunity and unreviewed award issues remain distinct.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

- Whalen v. Roe, 429 U.S. 589: develops its confidentiality foundation into the present actual-disclosure protection while preserving its approval of safeguarded collection and acknowledging its express reservation. Brennan's compelling-interest concurrence is not adopted as a universal rule.
- Nixon v. Administrator of General Services, 433 U.S. 425: applies the significance of legitimate personal privacy independently of ownership and distinguishes safeguarded archival screening from this unjustified dissemination; its governmental-custody holding remains intact.
- Paul v. Davis, 424 U.S. 693: preserves its reputation/arrest-record holding and distinguishes the exposure of intimate marital information; no universal tangible-economic-loss prerequisite follows.
- Collins v. Harker Heights, February 26, 1992: preserves the distinction between state obligations and a federal injury, and between that injury and municipal attribution. Its workplace-omission holding does not foreclose the present affirmative-disclosure claim.
- Albright v. Oliver, January 24, 1994: its separate constitutional-source, prosecution/remedy and additional-injury rationales remain distinct; no universal displacement, state-tort cure or tangible-loss rule is derived from a judgment without a controlling rationale.
- Hodge v. Jones, October 23, 1995: its confidential-retention immunity and mootness holdings remain intact. The broader disclosure merits it reserved are decided only at the present confined scope.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date precedent on a properly preserved immunity defense; today's recognition neither manufactures earlier notice nor opens an unpreserved defense.
- Markman v. Westview Instruments, April 23, 1996: its patent-construction allocation remains at its actual scope and supplies no general power to retry historical facts in this constitutional action.

No precedent is overruled. The Court adopts neither a general informational-privacy framework nor a new compensatory-damages or fee standard.

Effective April 29, 1996, the confined protection recognized here distinguishes supported official acts causing publication from ordinary investigative use or public archival availability. Official possession, a state assurance or subjective preference alone does not establish the right, and public inspectability does not automatically extinguish the claim or prove prior dissemination. Legitimate investigation and retention, lawful accountability and legally required disclosure remain subject to their own rules.

The decision establishes no deletion entitlement, inherited privacy claim, copyright right, liability for every state confidentiality breach or restriction on the private author's speech. It imposes no universal scrutiny formula, heightened civil proof burden or new publication-purpose requirement. Independent municipal attribution, historical individual notice and ordinary judgment and fee requirements remain intact.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents. He accepts for purposes of review the supported intentional disclosure, causation and Gary's own intimate injury, but finds no established constitutional guarantee of the asserted kind. Whalen deliberately reserved unwarranted dissemination, and Nixon sustained protected governmental screening rather than recognizing this damages entitlement. Decisional-autonomy precedents do not themselves establish general control over information held by government. Paul's refusal to constitutionalize the arrest-record claim reinforces the need to identify the actual federal guarantee, without making every intimate disclosure identical to reputation harm.

The dissent therefore finds the majority's extension unsupported even though the conduct may violate a promise, state duties or ordinary privacy expectations. Scalia's Albright source objection and Thomas's distinct insistence on a demonstrated substantive guarantee support that conclusion; they do not establish a general rule that state tort remedies cure an otherwise recognized substantive violation. Hodge left this merits question open. The dissent does not infer prior publication from archive availability, deny the accepted intimate injury, make Kathy's authorship dispositive or require tangible economic loss.

The two would reverse the reviewed constitutional ruling, vacate the federal judgment dependent on this right and remand for disposition consistent with rejection of the §1983 theory. Consequential prevailing-party fee treatment would follow under governing law. They direct no new factual trial, state-tort adjudication, refund amount or independent fee rule, and make no new ruling on personal immunity or the reasonableness of the awards.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Tenth Circuit's judgment remains effective within the reviewed constitutional question. The existing $650,000 compensatory judgment against Salt Lake County and George and the distinct $77,896.93 attorney-fee judgment remain undisturbed. No punitive award is established or reviewed. The Court makes no new award and orders no retrial, damages reassessment or immunity proceeding.

Supreme Court merits review is complete. Any actually preserved independent matter retains its lawful posture, without a finding that such a matter remains pending. Ordinary mandate procedures apply; no special issuance date or further Supreme Court event is prescribed.

### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's creation of roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise concern affirmative conduct. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver. Neither lower-court disagreement nor absence of identical facts independently establishes immunity. Collins, decided after Uhlrig's death, and later Elder and Pinder govern present analysis but cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court leaves constitutional culpability, the affirmative-danger merits and the Tenth Circuit's five-part test undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald supplies objective qualified immunity and remains intact.
- Anderson v. Creighton supplies particularized apparent unlawfulness without requiring an identical prior case.
- Elder v. Holloway is applied to require examination of all relevant conduct-time authority while preserving the favorable factual account and ordinary claim/evidence limits; it supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Pinder v. Johnson is applied to distinguish an affirmative deprivation from omitted protection and to keep notice separate from merits. Its March 1989 result does not resolve this later conduct automatically, and its substantive reservation remains open.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights retains its no-general-workplace-duty rule and its separate, bounded arbitrariness holding. It does not reject every affirmative-danger claim or supply notice before its February 26, 1992 decision.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County and Daniels v. Williams preserve the distinctions between a general protective duty, an affirmative deprivation and negligence; the Court does not convert them into a complete constitutional answer here.
- Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are considered as conduct-time circuit authority at their actual scopes, without converting their formulations into Supreme Court holdings.

#### Law After Decision

The decision establishes only the particularized immunity result for the five administrators on this reviewed claim. It supplies no general affirmative-danger test, categorical public-employment bar, mandatory deceit or assurance requirement, no-awareness finding or constitutional approval of budget choices. The distinction between a general workplace-safety duty and an attributable affirmative deprivation remains. A different official act, record or conduct-time legal landscape requires its own analysis.


### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment. He would reach the constitutional merits under Collins's workplace-duty boundary and a proposed rule for the reserved affirmative-danger claim. Because his merits inquiry finds no constitutional violation, he does not decide qualified immunity. His proposed framework and application are noncontrolling.

### 1997-04-15 — Chandler v. Miller, No. 96-126

Authority: [Record](../records/Chandler_v_Miller_merits_1997-04-15.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification below.

**1. Candidacy does not waive bodily privacy.** Government-required urine collection and chemical analysis are Fourth Amendment searches. Choosing to seek public office does not waive that protection; candidacy's public setting may inform reasonableness but does not itself authorize a compelled bodily search.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner establishes the search character of compelled urinalysis, and Vernonia preserves that conclusion even where participation in an activity is voluntary. A candidate's exposure to public scrutiny of qualifications is different from consent to bodily collection and medical analysis. Georgia's use of a personal physician and initial disclosure to the candidate reduce the intrusion, but do not eliminate the protected interest. The Court evaluates those actual safeguards and attributes no unsupported public disclosure, observed collection, police use or unrelated medical testing to the program.

**2. Georgia has not supplied a special need sufficient to justify this candidate-testing program.** A suspicionless search justified by a need beyond ordinary law enforcement must be reasonable in light of the actual governmental need, the program's operation, and the affected privacy interests. General desires for drug-free officials and public confidence do not supply the substantial, concrete need required for this broad advance-scheduled candidate program; its reduced intrusion cannot make up that deficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner and Von Raab permit special-needs searches when the protective or integrity interests and the program's operation justify the departure from individualized suspicion. Vernonia sustains its particular school-athletic program through custodial responsibility, contextual privacy and protective need. Political candidates do not share that setting. Georgia's covered offices have no common duty supplying a comparable protective risk, and the record identifies neither a concrete drug problem nor another substantial operational need connecting this broad class to the search. Sound judgment, integrity and deterrence remain legitimate interests, but do not explain why a negative certificate from one predictable test, scheduled within thirty days before qualification, adequately answers the asserted concern about future service. Advance scheduling limits what the certificate establishes; it does not make every scheduled test useless or unconstitutional. Nor must a State wait for a catastrophe, quantify efficacy, exhaust every alternative, or show safety-sensitive duties in every conceivable valid program. The holding rests on this program's combined deficiencies, not a mandatory checklist of missing facts. Georgia's strongest argument—the importance of trustworthy officials and the modest intrusion—therefore fails because a modest bodily search still needs an adequate governmental justification.

**3. The live prospective challenge requires relief against enforcement, without altering past elections.** Chandler's stated and uncontested intention to run again preserves the controversy despite completion of the 1994 election. The judgment sustaining the challenged requirement is reversed, and the case is remanded for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief barring its enforcement.

**Controlling explanation:** The continuing qualification condition affects Chandler's future candidacy, so resolving that live Fourth Amendment dispute does not require setting aside a completed election. The Court prescribes no replacement testing regime or new candidate qualification, and decides no damages, drug use, test result or individualized disqualification. The holding leaves materially different programs to their own supported special-needs justification. Independent First Amendment or state-law qualification claims, medical-fitness examinations, financial disclosure, particularized-suspicion testing, security screening tied to an actual safety risk, and private-sector testing are not decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association: Applies the rule that compelled urine collection and analysis are searches and its attention to substantiated protective need and actual privacy safeguards.
- Treasury Employees v. Von Raab: Preserves contextual special-needs analysis, including integrity interests; the doctrine is not confined to preventing a physical catastrophe.
- Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton: Applies the search and no-waiver holding and distinguishes the actual school-athletic merits holding on custodial, privacy and protective facts. Its five-Justice program rule, eight-Justice facial-prescription ruling and unanimous search rule remain distinct and unchanged.
- New Jersey v. T.L.O.: Its school-responsibility rationale does not transfer wholesale to adult political candidates. No earlier special-needs decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Georgia's challenged candidate-testing condition violates the Fourth Amendment. Suspicionless testing remains available under the existing contextual special-needs doctrine when a materially different program has an adequate supported justification. Public prominence and reduced intrusion alone do not provide it; neither a proven catastrophe nor exhaustion of all alternatives becomes a universal condition.
