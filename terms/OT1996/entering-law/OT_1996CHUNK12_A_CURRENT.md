### 1997-01-27 - ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg; Seventh Circuit No. 96-1139, 86 F.3d 1447; District Court judgment, 908 F. Supp. 640. No separate Supreme Court docket number assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/ProCD_v_Zeidenberg_merits_1997-01-27.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. All nine directly adopt each holding and its explanation; no synthesis of separate opinions is required.

##### Assent to this packaged-software transaction

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Wisconsin UCC §2-204, a seller may invite acceptance by use after review when the buyer has reasonable advance notice that terms govern, receives the full terms before meaningful use, has a practical opportunity to reject the transaction and obtain a refund, and objectively manifests assent by continuing to use the product. Those conditions establish assent to this noncommercial-use covenant; payment alone did not complete a different bargain that the seller later amended unilaterally.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this agreement arose through the transaction the parties actually made. The package announced restrictions; the manual and program supplied them; operation required an indication of assent; and return for a refund offered a practical means of rejection. Zeidenberg nevertheless used the consumer product to operate a commercial service and made two later purchases. Section 2-204 permits conduct to establish agreement. It does not require every term on the outside of the box, but neither does it permit a seller to insert new obligations after an already completed agreement.

The purchaser's contrary argument treats payment as the necessary final act of assent in every retail transaction. That does not account for this communicated offer and its review-and-return sequence. Lack of individual negotiation also does not itself resolve enforceability. Carnival Cruise Lines v. Shute, 499 U.S. 585 (1991), rejects that automatic objection in its setting but does not decide contested notice or Wisconsin formation here. American Airlines v. Wolens, January 18, 1995, permits enforcement of the actual voluntary bargain under its own preemption clause; it requires identifying the undertaking rather than manufacturing one.

Acceptance of goods under §2-606 is distinct from assent to contract terms. This single-form transaction does not decide §2-207's operation when parties exchange materially different forms. Nor does the Court decide terms first disclosed after irrevocable use, terms without a practical rejection route, or website terms lacking objective assent. Unconscionability, fraud, illegality and other generally applicable defenses remain available when genuinely preserved. No developed defense to this covenant requires additional proceedings merely to search for one.

**Precedent treatment:** Carnival Cruise is applied only against an automatic nonnegotiation objection, leaving its distinct maritime setting and the actual-notice inquiry intact. Wolens supplies the actual-undertaking analogy, not a Wisconsin rule of formation or immunity for every license.

##### Copyright subject matter and the distinct personal promise

**Holding and operative rule:** A fixed factual compilation can fall within §301's copyright subject-matter inquiry even when its facts or particular compilation lack copyright protection; absence of protection alone therefore does not end preemption analysis. Section 301 nevertheless does not preempt enforcement of this genuine, party-specific noncommercial-use promise, because the accepted undertaking changes the right asserted and binds only the parties, rather than imposing an equivalent exclusive right under §106 against persons who made no promise.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that subject matter and equivalence are separate questions. The database's assumed lack of copyright does not remove the compilation from the first inquiry. On the second, this action requires an undertaking governing purchased access, not copying alone. Zeidenberg accepted the consumer transaction on the condition that he would not use it commercially. Enforcing that exchange does not give ProCD ownership of the facts or a claim against an independent acquirer who never assented.

The objection that mass contracts could recreate copyright in unprotected information requires attention to the right actually enforced. A contract label or an additional pleading allegation does not invariably avoid §301. Here the real promise changes the parties' legal relationship; it is not merely a relabeled claim to control copying by the public. Section 301(b)(3) preserves nonequivalent rights, not every right described as contractual. Wolens, January 18, 1995, and Cipollone v. Liggett Group, 505 U.S. 504 (1992), supply clause-specific analogies distinguishing voluntarily assumed obligations from externally prescribed duties. Neither substitutes for §301's enacted equivalence inquiry.

Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone Service, 499 U.S. 340 (1991), denies copyright in facts based merely on collection effort; that limit remains intact. The assumed database premise does not adjudicate the copyrightability of every compilation element or enlarge the distinct program copyright. Nor does a holding under §301 answer whether an independent federal rule forbids a particular covenant. Nominal promises that do not qualitatively change the asserted right, first sale, reverse engineering, fair use, misuse, public policy, competition law and compulsory-license questions are not decided for materially different transactions. This judgment gives no right against nonassenting persons.

**Precedent treatment:** Feist's originality and factual-public-domain limits remain unchanged. Wolens and Cipollone's warranty component are extended by analogy only after independently construing §301; their differently worded preemption clauses create no categorical contract exemption.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Carnival Cruise Lines v. Shute, 499 U.S. 585 (1991):** Its treatment of standard terms supports rejecting invalidity solely from lack of negotiation. It does not replace the notice, rejection and assent inquiry under Wisconsin law.
- **American Airlines v. Wolens, January 18, 1995:** The actual-undertaking distinction is applied by analogy; the airline clause and its limits retain their separate force. Ordinary law must first establish the promise, and courts may not enlarge it through external policy.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, 505 U.S. 504 (1992):** The voluntary-warranty component supplies an analogy about the source of an obligation, not an exemption for all common-law claims or all contracts. Its claim-specific authority and cigarette-statute boundaries remain unchanged.
- **Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone Service, 499 U.S. 340 (1991):** The rule that effort alone supplies no copyright in facts is preserved; enforcing this personal undertaking does not change the originality requirement or create rights against the public.

#### Law After Decision

Wisconsin's ordinary assent rules apply to this form of software transaction, with actual notice, review, meaningful rejection and objective assent doing the work. The Court rejects both a rule that payment necessarily ends formation in every such transaction and a rule validating every enclosed term. Under §301, inclusion within copyright subject matter and equivalence remain distinct inquiries. This accepted promise is enforceable between the contracting parties; neither copyright in facts nor universal immunity for license claims follows. The decision adds these applications as of January 27, 1997, without displacing Feist's originality rule or enlarging Wolens and Cipollone beyond their statutory settings.

### 1997-04-21 — New Mexico Department of Human Services v. Joseph A. and Josephine A.; Tenth Circuit No. 94-2160, 69 F.3d 1081; separate Supreme Court docket unassigned

Authority: [Record](../records/New_Mexico_Department_of_Human_Services_v_Joseph_A_merits_1997-04-21.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification below.

**1. The agreed termination condition governs.** The Department may obtain termination by establishing twelve consecutive months of substantial and continuous compliance with the decree's essential purposes and specified obligations. Immaterial departures from particular criteria need not defeat release, but general improvement cannot excuse a material failure to establish fair, reasonable and timely adoption decisionmaking and fair, reasonable and adequate procedures and practices securing access to permanent homes.

**Controlling explanation:** ITT Continental Baking directs attention to the consent decree's agreed obligations, interpreted in light of their purposes rather than replaced by a new decree. The 1983 judgment protects both the children's right to those obligations and the Department's right to termination when its own condition is met. Substantial compliance is not perfect compliance, nor is it whatever an unexplained overall impression approves. The court must relate material deviations to the essential objectives and the obligations chosen to accomplish them. No universal percentage creates a safe harbor. The required institutional procedures must genuinely be in place; that is an existing obligation, not a new indefinite guarantee against every future lapse or an extension of the bargained twelve-month period.

**2. Rule 52 requires findings that disclose why the condition is or is not satisfied.** The District Court must supply enough subsidiary findings and explanation of material data, case evidence and methodology to permit review of its ultimate compliance conclusion. Rule 52(a) applies to a master's findings to the extent adopted; a lengthy evidence summary or repetition of the conclusion cannot replace the material findings, though every item and irrelevant detail need not be recited.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley v. Everglades Drainage District requires findings sufficient to reveal the factual basis of the ultimate conclusion. Supported findings retain clear-error protection. That protection does not allow an appellate court to supply an unexpressed methodology, resolve credibility disputes or weigh conflicting evidence in place of the trial tribunal. The State's strongest contrary point is that an experienced judge adopted extensive reports and substantial compliance tolerates minor defects. Those considerations do not answer omissions about essential obligations and material conflicting evidence. Meaningful review requires an intelligible account, not a transcript of every evidentiary choice or a newly prescribed supervision system.

**3. The adopted reports omit material connections needed to review termination.** The reports do not adequately explain how their methodology and competing quantitative and qualitative evidence support the finding of substantial and continuous compliance. The Court therefore sustains vacatur of termination and remand for adequate findings, without deciding whether the Department has actually complied.

**Controlling explanation:** The report describes conference measures ranging from 6.2 to 63 percent and other content measures from 31 to 97 percent, but does not explain their relationship to its compliance conclusions. It likewise fails to account adequately for timely attorney notification of 26 percent and no notification for 8 percent; discounts adverse individual cases as selected instances without explaining why they are unrepresentative rather than evidence of broader defects; and does not adequately resolve the timely ADAPT-data challenge or explain or discredit testimony that custody duration increased from 1.5 to 2.1 years. These are evidence and explanation problems, not this Court's acceptance of every adverse measure or finding of systemic failure. A reasoned account may show why a shortfall is immaterial or an opposing measure more reliable, but it must come from the factfinding tribunal. The appeal is from the final termination order adopting both reports, and is not confined to the 1993 report; the 1989 report had already been found inadequate standing alone. The later State Auditor report and proposed supplemental study, not before the Special Master, supply no adverse finding. Timely quality-assurance evidence actually considered below is distinct.

**4. Proof of performance and modification of obligations are different inquiries.** The Department need not prove a significant change under Rufo to establish performance of this decree's existing termination condition. A request instead to reduce still-binding obligations because law or facts have changed must satisfy Rufo's changed-circumstances and tailoring requirements; ordinary compliance review cannot rewrite the decree to a constitutional minimum.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo governs relief from duties whose continued enforcement is said to have become inequitable. Its threshold serves a different question from whether agreed duties have been performed for the required period. Freeman and Missouri v. Jenkins reinforce the need to connect release findings to the actual remedy in their school-desegregation settings; neither substitutes the full school-release inquiry for this foster-care decree's language. No prison-conditions predicate for applying the PLRA is established. The Court imposes neither a new constitutional-violation prerequisite for enforcing the existing judgment nor an independent changed-circumstances hurdle for proving compliance.

**5. Remand preserves the decree and its lawful route to termination.** The existing decree remains effective unless lawfully terminated or modified. The District Court must address the material deviations, explain relevant methodology and resolve the material factual conflicts necessary for review, then enter the result warranted by supported findings under the existing twelve-month condition, including termination if that condition is proved.

**Controlling explanation:** Retaining the decree during that determination enforces the judgment already entered without predicting the result. The Court starts no new twelve-month clock, fixes no release date and requires no plaintiff consent, consensus, neutral-reviewer endorsement, new monitor or guarantee against every future lapse. It does not order automatic retrial of settled facts or automatic admission of new submissions; any additional proceedings remain governed by ordinary authority. No §1988 fee issue is before the Court: the separate monitoring-fee appeal at 28 F.3d 1056 is outside this grant, including prevailing-party eligibility, necessity of monitoring work and findings supporting a fee amount.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. ITT Continental Baking Co.: Applies interpretation of the agreed consent obligations, with purpose explaining their specified requirements rather than authorizing replacement terms.
- Kelley v. Everglades Drainage District: Applies the requirement for findings revealing the factual basis of the ultimate conclusion under Rule 52.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail: Preserves its significant-change and suitable-tailoring rule for modification; it creates no additional hurdle to proving performance under an existing release clause and permits no automatic reduction to the constitutional minimum.
- Freeman v. Pitts and Missouri v. Jenkins: Preserve their actual school-desegregation release rules. Their demand for findings connected to the operative remedy is instructive, but their full school-specific inquiry does not replace this decree's own termination condition. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Termination of this decree depends on reviewable proof of its own sustained-compliance condition. Required procedures must be established, material departures explained, and relevant factual conflicts resolved; perfect performance and an indefinite guarantee are not required. Performance-based release remains distinct from changed-duty modification. The ruling establishes no general federal foster-care supervision mandate, numerical safe harbor or new fee rule.

### 1997-05-12 — Lambrix v. Singletary; No. 96-5658; OT1996, chunk 7, matter 83.

Authority: [Record](../records/Lambrix_v_Singletary_merits_1997-05-12.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Procedural default and Teague nonretroactivity are distinct, nonjurisdictional limits, with no inflexible rule requiring one always to precede the other. In this case the Court decides the accepted Teague issue while reserving the disputed state-default predicates; that choice neither excuses default nor decides the constitutional merits.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support from Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stone-Zsela and O'Connor disagree with reaching Teague before the default inquiry in this case.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court ordinarily respects the independent state ground before granting federal relief, but these two defenses do not establish jurisdictional priority. Stringer rejected a Teague ground and remanded an unresolved default question without creating a universal order. Schiro confirms that nonretroactivity is nonjurisdictional; Caspari distinguishes proper presentation of a preserved subsidiary defense from other omitted questions. Here Teague is expressly accepted for review.

The State repeatedly asserted default, so the Court does not proceed on a supposed wholesale waiver. The present dispute concerns the adequacy and operation of the actual state rule and any legally sufficient excuse, questions not resolved by the lower appellate disposition. Addressing the accepted finality issue now does not decide those unsettled predicates or establish an advisory constitutional rule: it resolves a distinct restriction on this pending claim. The strongest alternative is to return immediately for default because a sufficient, preserved bar could end the case. That route is lawful, but is not obligatory before this accepted nonjurisdictional question. No established jurisdictional defect is bypassed, and no mandatory Teague-first rule is announced.

##### II. This claim invokes an old guidance rule at November 24, 1986 finality

**Holding and operative rule:** For Lambrix's judgment final on November 24, 1986, the claim that inadequate aggravator guidance in Florida's legally consequential capital jury recommendation can infect the final death sentence invokes an already dictated rule. Teague therefore does not bar this claim as new merely because the judge separately must exercise independent judgment; the ruling establishes no actual instructional defect, harmful influence or failed cure on this record.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support from Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices. O'Connor affirmatively joins this holding once Teague is reached, while maintaining her objection to that sequence. Stone-Zsela takes no position on the new-rule question. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject the holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Godfrey already required meaningful guidance of capital aggravation. Teague and Caspari require comparison of the precise application with law at finality, not merely citation of a broad principle. Stringer applies Godfrey's existing protection to consequential weighing; Gray likewise distinguishes preserving an established protection's real function from creating a new procedural entitlement. Those current decisions govern the inquiry without being recast as cases available in 1986.

Prefinality Florida law establishes the function at issue. LeDuc gave a death recommendation operative weight if all relevant data were considered, without making it binding. Messer treated an informed jury contribution as a distinct statutory safeguard: later judicial consideration did not replace wrongly excluded accomplice-sentence and proposed psychiatric mitigation before the jury. Godfrey supplies the federal duty; the state safeguard alone does not.

Ross supplies the strongest contrary argument. It requires the judge's independent reasoned judgment and recognizes judicial experience as protection against lay notions of heinousness. Spaziano and Baldwin likewise preserve judicial sentencing authority. But Ross simultaneously retains the recommendation's weight and LeDuc's qualification. Its remand corrected excessive judicial deference, not defective jury guidance. Eutzy preserves weight and independence together, and rejected the instruction challenge only because the jury recommended life; it did not decide the instructions' merits.

The independent judge therefore adds a protection without making the consequential jury contribution disappear. Applying meaningful guidance to that contribution preserves Godfrey's existing function rather than creating a right to jury-imposed sentencing. Neither identical power nor equal weight for life and death recommendations is required. The later date of Espinosa, a reserved question or reasonable disagreement asserted without a substantive distinction cannot alone establish novelty. Actual cure remains a separate inquiry. LeDuc's affirmance, Messer's particular new-jury remedy, Ross's judicial remand and Eutzy's favorable-life reasoning do not mechanically select a remedy here.

##### III-A. Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support at this exact scope from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. The further binding old-rule instruction rests separately on Part III-B's five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals disposed of the claim without resolving the State's asserted procedural bar. The actual operation of the rule and the State's litigation conduct bear on independence, adequacy, preservation and an applicable excuse. Those genuine unfinished inquiries require a bounded remand; neither an unestablished procedural premise nor a generalized waiver assertion supplies their answer.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial. Florida's identified ground concerns his failure to pursue that issue on direct appeal, not failure to object at trial. Its separate successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and alternative merits rejection do not establish a federal cause determination. The lower court must assess the actual state materials and any properly available cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice contention. It may not treat a proposed exception as already established or a present reservation as a finding that the bar is inadequate.

The common direction sets aside the reviewed appellate disposition and requires default to be addressed before any available constitutional merits and relief. It does not establish a universal order for nonjurisdictional defenses. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, declares no waiver or exception and creates no present right to a new penalty proceeding. The Justices' different positions on Teague do not erase their agreement on this operative remand task; those differences are preserved in Parts I–II, Part III-B and the separate writings.

##### III-B. The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by the same five Justices as Part II. They directly adopt this additional binding direction. Stone-Zsela joins Part III-A's common vacatur and default remand, but not the instruction deciding Teague now or foreclosing its consideration if default fails; his distinct mandate is stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on a Teague conclusion the Court rejects. Vacatur therefore removes that judgment and binds further proceedings to the old-rule determination; it is not an invitation to repeat the rejected ground. The accepted claim includes the heinous, atrocious or cruel and cold, calculated and premeditated instruction issues. The court of appeals assumed inadequate guidance for nonretroactivity analysis, which establishes neither a violation nor its effect.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial; Florida relied on failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. That is not an unpreserved trial objection. Coleman and Ford require attention to the actual independent state ground, its adequacy when compliance was required, preservation and an applicable excuse. The separate state treatment of successive appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and its alternative merits rejection are not federal findings of cause. Ordinary cause and prejudice remain distinct from Sawyer's clear-and-convincing requirement that no reasonable juror would find death eligibility absent constitutional error; a selection defect alone does not meet that gateway.

If an available violation is established, Espinosa and Stringer require genuine authorized reweighing or Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt addressing the recommendation's transmitted influence. Remaining valid aggravators, bare proportionality review or an independent-judge label alone supplies neither cure. If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required. None is presently ordered.

Gray preserves any genuinely applicable, unresolved statutory condition before an operative writ. This decision settles neither chapter 153's temporal reach, amended §2254(d)'s application, chapter 154 qualification nor an exhaustion waiver or statutory exception. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, conditional writ or revived deadline, and no guilt retrial, life sentence, release, stay or execution authorization. Unrelated assistance, double-jeopardy, testimony and guilt rulings remain untouched.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Teague v. Lane:** Applies the dictated-at-finality inquiry; this claim is old, so the Court does not adopt an alternative exception holding. Both contemporary exceptions remain in force.
- **Godfrey v. Georgia:** Supplies the prefinality requirement of meaningful capital guidance that follows the legally consequential weighing contribution here.
- **Stringer v. Black:** Applies its old-rule and actual-cure principles without making its Mississippi setting identical to Florida or converting its default remand into a universal sequence.
- **Espinosa v. Florida:** Its influential-recommendation merits rule remains controlling; this decision answers the distinct November 1986 finality question without finding an actual violation or cure failure.
- **Gray v. Netherland:** Applies functional comparison with an established capital protection while preserving separate claim, effect and statutory gates; its particular preparation injury and final remedy are not transferred here.
- **Caspari v. Bohlen:** Preserves precise finality and application analysis and the distinction between presentation and preservation; reasonable substantive institutional differences must be tested, not assumed from labels.
- **Gilmore v. Taylor:** Its specific new instructional application and two-exception analysis remain intact; the Court does not treat every later capital application as old.
- **Schiro v. Farley:** Preserves discretionary treatment of a late nonjurisdictional Teague defense; this case involves an expressly accepted question and supplies no generic waiver finding.
- **Spaziano v. Florida:** Preserves States' latitude to employ judicial sentencing; that authority does not make an influential jury contribution nonexistent.
- **Baldwin v. Alabama:** Its independently reasoned sentencing rationale and reservation are distinguished from an exemption for legally consequential unguided weighing; neither dictates automatic cure here.
- **Zant v. Stephens:** Its nonweighing holding does not authorize retention of unconstitutional influence in a weighing contribution.
- **Barclay v. Florida:** Its state-law invalidity and actual review do not establish a universal cure for constitutional aggravation error.
- **LeDuc v. State:** Supplies qualified prefinality weight for a death recommendation when all relevant data were considered, not binding force or a universal new-jury remedy.
- **Messer v. State:** Supplies the distinct informed-jury safeguard under Florida law; its excluded-mitigation holding and particular remedy do not themselves create the federal rule or dictate this habeas remedy.
- **Ross v. State:** Preserves recommendation weight and independent judicial judgment together; its correction of excessive deference does not establish that separate findings cured this different alleged defect.
- **Eutzy v. State:** Its favorable-life instruction rationale and its treatment of an evidentially unsupported factor do not resolve a vague-factor death-recommendation claim.
- **Coleman v. Thompson:** Preserves independent, adequate and unexcused state procedural bars and ordinary cause-and-prejudice analysis; no bar or excuse is established here.
- **Ford v. Georgia:** Preserves the requirement to assess the state rule's adequacy when compliance was required; the 1994 assertion alone does not decide that historical operation.
- **Sawyer v. Whitley:** Preserves the distinct death-eligibility gateway and clear-and-convincing burden; ordinary selection error is not itself gateway innocence.
- **Chapman v. California:** Supplies the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden on the constitutional harmlessness route, distinct from authorized reweighing.

Maynard, Clemons, Walton, Florida Smith and Grossman postdate Lambrix's finality and are not treated as decisions already available in November 1986. Existing correction law remains binding on any later merits inquiry; no prior decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

This particular influential-recommendation claim is an old-rule application at November 24, 1986 finality and cannot again be denied on the Teague new-rule ground. The decision leaves Florida's lawful allocation of independent judicial sentencing intact and establishes no equal weight for life and death recommendations. Existing Espinosa/Stringer cure requirements remain; actual default, constitutional defect, transmitted effect, lawful cure and genuinely applicable statutory conditions are distinct. The sequence chosen here creates neither a mandatory default-first nor mandatory Teague-first rule for all habeas cases. No general extension of every capital protection, new Teague exception or immediate entitlement to sentence relief follows.

### 1997-05-12 — Reyes-Hernandez v. INS; Seventh Circuit No. 95-3677, 89 F.3d 490; Supreme Court docket unassigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Reyes_Hernandez_v_INS_merits_1997-05-12.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Section 242(g) does not withdraw review of these completed-adjudication claims

**Holding and operative rule:** After its April 1, 1997 commencement, §242(g) reaches past, pending and future proceedings but withdraws review of the identified executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders, rather than every legal challenge arising from completed adjudication. These challenges to temporal eligibility, the legality of the completed waiver denial and treatment of a supplemental brief remain outside that withdrawal and may use the otherwise saved former §106 review route.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, has eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the threshold result.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Yang*'s April 1 commencement holding and construes the scope it reserved. Corrected §306(c)(1) applies the general amendments through §309 while extending §242(g) across old and new proceedings. Corrected §309(c)(1) preserves earlier proceedings and review subject to the stated transition rules: paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only before the evidentiary hearing has commenced, with notice at least thirty days before that hearing; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. Neither predicate exists after this completed 1995 adjudication. The October 1995 final administrative order does not meet §309(c)(4)'s requirement of an order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; no agency election or later final order is established. Section 306(c)(2) protects existing §106 judgments from invalidation or required reconsideration by paragraph (1), but creates no jurisdiction.

The contrary construction stresses the broad reference to any claim notwithstanding other law and the exception for review provided in §242. But making the three enumerated executive choices encompass every adjudicated legal issue would erase the detailed saving scheme beyond Congress's stated exceptions. Review of a completed legal result differs from challenging the executive action to adjudicate. Former §106 review is not declared to be review textually provided in §242; it survives through §309 only for claims outside the specified withdrawal. This construction does not preserve every discretionary or constitutional claim. *Felker* requires identification of the particular channel withdrawn and retained; neither a hypothetical §2241 remedy nor §1651 supplies the jurisdiction here.

##### II. The two AEDPA restrictions do not attach these new consequences to the completed concession

**Holding and operative rule:** On the uncontested premise that Reyes surrendered a colorable defense to deportability before enactment in order to pursue the then-available waiver-and-review path, §440(d) does not remove eligibility for that original waiver claim, and §440(a) does not eliminate ordinary review of its lawful denial, because neither clearly prescribes those new consequences for that completed choice. A genuine defense is necessary to this lost-option rationale, not a universal element of retroactivity analysis or an exemption for every preenactment conviction, pending case or later reopening request.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Landgraf* separately to each provision: first examine enacted temporal direction, then the new legal consequence attached to the completed act. Section 440(f) directs the reach of subsection (e), not either subsection at issue. Section 440(d) would remove eligibility that formed part of Reyes's completed surrender. Section 440(a) would separately remove the then-available opportunity to correct an unlawful denial through ordinary review after that surrender. Discretionary relief was never guaranteed, but the absence of an entitlement to mercy does not answer whether a real legal option has acquired a new disability.

The strongest objection treats §440(a) solely as regulation of present judicial power. *Landgraf* does not let the jurisdictional label replace examination of the actual consequence. This record supplies a completed concession, more than mere pendency or a general hope that a forum will persist. *Felker* applied its review restriction to authorization proceedings begun after enactment, a different temporal event. The Government did not dispute the colorable-defense predicate; the Court invents no particular defense and orders no factfinding to reopen that accepted premise. An utterly insubstantial defense would not establish this surrendered-option theory. Other temporal predicates remain open, and no universal subjective-reliance requirement, constitutional invalidation, habeas holding or promise of favorable discretion is adopted. The IIRIRA dual-offense technical correction supplies no additional conviction finding.

##### III. Eligibility does not compel a favorable waiver balance

**Holding and operative rule:** An eligible §212(c) applicant remains subject to a reasoned individualized exercise of lawful discretion, and the BIA may adopt an immigration judge's adequate reasons without independently repeating the entire balance. Here consideration of rehabilitation and family hardship against serious, recent and repeated drug offenses lawfully supports denial; the reviewing court may not replace that balance merely because the favorable equities are substantial.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes. The jurisdictional dissenters do not reach this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies *Yang*'s distinction between eligibility, permissible factors and reasoned administration. Permission to exercise discretion does not permit pretext, disregard of binding rules or an unexplained departure from genuinely settled policy. Those defects are not established here. The immigration judge addressed family hardship and rehabilitation, yet gave greater weight to repeated serious drug violations; the BIA adopted those actual reasons. Adoption of an adequate explanation is not absence of analysis. Reyes's stronger-equities argument asks for a fresh judicial weighing, not correction of a demonstrated legal error. The Court sustains this denial without creating a presumption against every eligible drug offender or foreclosing review of an actual policy departure.

##### IV. A supplemental appellate brief did not require original factfinding or automatic reopening

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the BIA could require the established motion to supplement, remand or reopen before considering new post-hearing evidence offered in an appellate brief, rather than treating that brief as a different motion or conducting original appellate factfinding. The decision does not permit disregard of material evidence already admitted or decide the eligibility, timeliness, merits or disposition of any later motion.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, has six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes refusing to receive new evidence through an improper vehicle from ignoring evidence already in the record. The supplemental brief described later employment, sobriety and volunteer work, but the agency had not admitted that material. It required the established procedural route and expressly identified reopening as remaining possible. *Abudu* supplies the governing reopening framework; *Doherty*'s fractured result supplies no controlling general rationale. #### Holdings

##### I. An evidence warrant does not authorize an unrelated audience in the home

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers violate the Fourth Amendment when they procure the entry and recording of private media observers inside a home during execution of a search warrant and that presence serves no legitimate execution or investigative purpose. A valid warrant authorizes its evidentiary intrusion, not the additional exposure of home life for an unrelated purpose; genuine assistance, valid occupant consent and necessary investigative documentation present different circumstances.

**Authority:** Parts I–II of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court have nine direct joins. Parts III–IV, including the conduct-date application and affirmance, are joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer and control by five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Bivens identifies the warrant's bounds; Andresen requires restraint of needless documentary intrusions even during a lawful search. Wilson v. Arkansas and Richards establish that the manner of executing a warrant remains subject to reasonableness. The alleged crew located no evidence, performed no protective function and documented nothing for the investigation. Its observation and preservation of private persons and materials therefore added an intrusion the warrant's purpose did not justify. The family's compelled submission to the officers was not consent to a separate audience. Public interest in police activity and press status furnish no additional entry authority. Section 3105 permits a person to aid an authorized officer at that officer's request while the officer is present and executing the warrant; it does not license unrelated attendance. **Holding and operative rule:** On this legal collateral-order appeal, the Court assesses whether the assumed conduct was apparently unlawful under law existing on March 5, 1992; a present violation alone does not defeat immunity, and a factually identical earlier case is not indispensable. The appeal does not permit findings about Mottola's actual role or resolution of disputed liability facts.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson protects reasonable legal mistakes while requiring a particularized account of the claimed right. Elder requires consideration of all relevant existing authority, and Pinder and Lanier preserve the distinction between an abstract right and an obvious application of established law. Johnson and Behrens confine the immediate appeal to its properly assumed legal question. Bivens supplies the existing individual-officer Fourth Amendment damages channel; FDIC v. ##### III. The alleged purposeless invitation was sufficiently clearly forbidden

**Holding and operative rule:** On the pleaded facts, pre-March 5, 1992 law made the officer's gratuitous admission of nonassisting outsiders to expose private home life for an unrelated purpose apparently unlawful. Mottola is therefore not entitled to dismissal on qualified immunity for this claim, but responsibility, causation and compensable injury remain to be proved.

**Controlling explanation:** Bivens's warrant limits and Payton's protection of the home supply a concrete purpose-bound restraint. Andresen limits unnecessary exposure during documentary searches; Matias applies that discipline in the Second Circuit, and Rivera rejects the notion that entry authority eliminates additional protections of household members outside the investigation. The earlier assistance cases, including Clouston and Wright, distinguish persons who help execute the warrant from unrelated observers. Together these authorities address the particular excess alleged here, rather than a general right to privacy. Section 3105 confirms the execution-assistance distinction but does not substitute a statutory violation for constitutional notice. Soldal, Wilson, Bills and other later decisions supply no retrospective notice. The Court preserves Harlow–Anderson and does not adopt a new immunity regime.

The Court does not reach force, detention, property-destruction, pre-warrant-entry or separately treated photograph issues; substantive due process; CBS's separate position or settlement; unserved agents; or the criminal proceeding's demand for footage. #### Holdings

##### Federal immunity and the State's timing of appellate review

**Holding and operative rule:** Neither §1983, the Supremacy Clause, nor federal qualified-immunity doctrine requires a State to provide an interlocutory appeal from denial of immunity when its neutral final-judgment rule preserves meaningful adjudication and review of the substantive defense. Section 1291's federal collateral-order appeal is not itself a substantive component that state appellate courts must adopt; discriminatory or substantively destructive state procedures present a different question.

**Authority:** The entire Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices identified in Opinion Topology, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Mitchell's immediate federal appeal rests on the construction of §1291, Congress's federal final-decision statute. Idaho administers its own appellate jurisdiction. Similar wording does not authorize this Court to rewrite the state high court's interpretation of its own finality rule, and §1983 does not enact §1291 for state courts.

Qualified immunity protects against trial burdens as well as an adverse damages judgment. Delayed appellate correction therefore can leave an official bearing burdens that later reversal cannot remove. That consequence does not itself supply appellate jurisdiction. Johnson v. Jones, Swint, and Behrens preserve limits on federal immediate review even for a substantial immunity claim. Their distinctions separate the substantive protection from the statutory route for correcting error.

Howlett and Testa require competent state courts to respect federal law; Felder rejects an incompatible state condition that defeats a federal claim. Those principles remain fully operative. Idaho's rule does not discriminate against the federal defense or nullify its content on this record. Exceptions allowing other interlocutory appeals do not alone establish discrimination. Dice involved a procedure furnished by the substantive federal statute, a source absent here.

Idaho Rule 12(a) permits, but does not compel, interlocutory review of an otherwise nonappealable controlling legal question with substantial grounds for disagreement when immediate review may materially advance orderly resolution. This decision does not depend on a request or permission under that rule and finds neither.

The trial court must apply the federal substantive defense, and ordinary final review remains available. The Court reserves a procedure that discriminates, deliberately evades federal law, denies meaningful review, or defeats the substantive protection. It decides no immunity entitlement, disputed fact, §1983 violation, or damages.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Mitchell v. Forsyth:** Its qualifying immediate federal immunity appeal remains a construction of §1291; it does not independently impose state appellate jurisdiction.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its separation of legal immunity questions from factual-sufficiency disputes illustrates that substantive immunity does not create unrestricted immediate review.
- **Swint v. Chambers County Commission:** Its limits on collateral appellate jurisdiction remain effective; an available appeal does not automatically carry unrelated nonappealable issues.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its authorization of qualifying successive-stage federal appeals remains subject to the federal statute and cumulative jurisdictional limits; it does not bind a State to that appellate structure.
- **Howlett v. Rose:** Applied to require state-court fidelity to federal substantive law and to forbid discrimination or nullification, without imposing an identical federal appellate system.
- **Testa v. Katt:** Its obligation to respect federal law is preserved; neutral judicial administration is not rejected merely because it differs from federal procedure.
- **Felder v. Casey:** Distinguished because the incompatible notice condition there defeated the federal claim, unlike the neutral timing rule presented here.
- **Dice v. Akron, Canton & Youngstown Railroad Co.:** Distinguished because the relevant procedure came from the substantive federal statute; no equivalent source mandates this state appeal.

Federal qualified immunity retains its substantive protection in state court, including protection from trial when the defense applies. Idaho may use its neutral final-judgment rule without incorporating §1291. A procedural label supplies no immunity from preemption if a different rule discriminates against, evades, or defeats the federal protection. The merits of immunity and the §1983 action remain undecided.

#### Holdings

##### Proof of unconstitutional motive

**Holding and operative rule:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by all nine participating Justices, supplies the controlling rationale for every holding below. Stone-Zsela separately proposes a change to immunity but independently joins this disposition and analysis under the existing defense; that proposal is not part of the Court's rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Kimberlin separates motive in the constitutional wrong from Harlow's objective immunity defense and rejects direct-only proof. It did not decide the alternative heightened quantum. The Court decides that question now: §1983, the First Amendment rule at issue and the Federal Rules provide no source for a special clear-and-convincing burden. Protecting officials against costly unfounded suits is a substantial concern, but it cannot supply an independent evidentiary code. New York Times' speech-protective rule for defamation does not establish the same quantum for official retaliation. Anderson v. Liberty Lobby tells courts to apply an independently governing substantive burden at summary judgment; it does not authorize invention of one. ##### Discovery and stage-specific screening

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow protects against unwarranted litigation burdens, while Anderson v. Creighton permits appropriately tailored factual inquiry. They do not permit excluding circumstantial evidence or evidence held by the official simply because the plaintiff cannot possess it before discovery. Rules 7(a), 12(e), 26 and 56 provide tools for clarification and controlled development. A properly supported summary-judgment motion requires affirmative responsive evidence; a general attack on credibility is insufficient. Courts nevertheless cannot decide a genuine credibility dispute by demanding conclusive pre-discovery proof obtainable only through necessary inquiry. The D.C. Circuit's writings must be distinguished: Williams' absolute proof-first proposal lacked a majority; D. Ginsburg permitted tailored discovery on a sufficiently specific forecast tied to the elevated burden; Edwards and four colleagues applied ordinary rules. **Holding and operative rule:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle applies Mt. Healthy's distinction between an actual same-decision showing and a hypothetical permissible reason. That causal distinction applies here without importing school-specific curricular rules into prisons. Britton's asserted safekeeping explanation may prevail if the necessary facts are established; its possible validity alone does not erase the motive-dependent claim. Conversely, some improper purpose or mixed motives do not alone establish recovery. Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and the independent immunity defense remain unresolved. Kimberlin and Settle govern current procedure and doctrine, not the notice Britton possessed in 1989. Pell and Turner continue to limit prison-access rights; no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized. The Court cannot select contested inferences about threats, elapsed time, other prisoners' treatment or property handling to enter a new merits dismissal on this record.

**Remedy and limits:** Vacate the en banc judgment only insofar as it imposes the heightened burden and directions dependent on it, and remand the live retaliation claim under ordinary civil standards, preserving the existing remand otherwise. Previously resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The District's municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No final immunity, liability, injury, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

#### Holdings

##### I. Protected advocacy and a distinct evidentiary act

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor has absolute immunity under §1983 for preparing and filing an information or warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case when performing an advocate's function. An allegation of bad purpose or false evidence does not remove that protection from the advocacy function, but the protection does not extend to a distinct personal evidentiary certification merely because the same prosecutor performs both acts.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the protection of legitimate charging and advocacy and the need to distinguish the personal evidentiary act, but not Part I's refusal to recognize an additional exception for knowing fabrication or deliberately false material assertions causing a constitutional deprivation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the act performed determines the immunity. Imbler protects initiating a prosecution and presenting the State's case; Burns preserves the advocate's presentation to a judge while requiring a separate inquiry into other functions. Buckley preserves genuine evaluation and preparation for advocacy while withholding absolute protection from ordinary investigative evidence gathering. Antoine requires historical and functional justification rather than reliance on an act's importance to judicial proceedings. Those decisions require protection for Kalina's legal preparation and presentation without treating every act in the charging transaction as advocacy. A prosecutor may present another person's evidence without personally guaranteeing its truth. Separating those functions therefore does not expose the protected legal work merely by relabeling a claim. The line also does not turn on whether advocacy occurred before or after probable cause, or on whether an allegation of dishonesty ultimately succeeds. The Court does not adopt a general dishonesty exception to absolute immunity for otherwise protected advocacy.

##### II. Personal factual certification supporting an arrest warrant

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions offered to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness and has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for that certification. This classification depends on the personal evidentiary undertaking, not on proof of falsity, and does not withdraw immunity from ordinary courtroom witness testimony.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Kalina's personal oath supplies evidence rather than the advocate's legal presentation of another person's evidence. Malley denies absolute immunity to the officer who supplies a warrant application and provides the relevant comparison; prosecutorial title does not change the function of furnishing the factual assurance needed to procure process. Imbler and Burns protect advocacy, but their protection does not absorb a separately assumed witness function. Buckley and Antoine require that distinction even when the acts contribute to one prosecution. Kalina's claimed protection therefore fails as to her personally executed certification. The question is not whether someone else actually would have supplied a truthful certification, nor whether Kalina knowingly lied. Those are not premises necessary to classify the act. Nor does this holding make every sworn statement actionable: it concerns the factual certification used to obtain this arrest warrant, preserves ordinary testimony immunity, and leaves constitutional injury and the remaining defenses for further proceedings.

##### III. The immunity ruling permits further proceedings, not a liability judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath leaves Fletcher to establish an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; Kalina retains qualified immunity and other available personal defenses. The existing qualified-immunity framework governs the remand, using the law and information relevant to the conduct at issue, without a new defense-burden rule or a finding that any element or defense has been established.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins the unresolved-liability and bounded-remand propositions and accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework as governing because his proposed reforms are not adopted; he adds no separate ground on the source-specific causation and notice explanations below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a function-specific denial of immunity is not a decision on damages liability. Hafer permits a personal-capacity §1983 claim while preserving personal immunities and the plaintiff's obligation to establish the officer's own constitutional violation. A judge's issuance of the warrant does not categorically break causation when material deception procured it; a truthful, corrected account may nevertheless establish probable cause. Neither culpability nor the content and effect of that corrected account is decided here. Harlow and Anderson preserve the existing objective inquiry into clearly established law in the particular context. Elder requires independent consideration of relevant precedent on the preserved immunity issue, and Lanier rejects a categorical demand for a factually identical prior case. Neither later decision supplies retrospective notice of unlawfulness. Disputed historical facts remain subject to the ordinary procedural rules, and the legal immunity determination belongs to the judge. The broader fourth question presented cannot turn this interlocutory immunity appeal into a determination of constitutional liability. The lower court must separate any claims resting only on protected advocacy from those resting on the personal oath; no damages award, mandatory trial or automatic finding against either party follows.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409:** Preserved for prosecutorial advocacy; distinguished from personally supplying the warrant-supporting factual oath. The Court adopts no general dishonesty exception to otherwise protected advocacy.
- **Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478:** Applied to protect legal presentation to a judge while requiring historical and functional justification for protection of a different act.
- **Malley v. Briggs, 475 U.S. 335:** Applied to the personal warrant-affiant function; a prosecutor does not gain absolute protection for the same evidentiary undertaking through official title. The warrant's issuance does not by itself resolve responsibility for material deception.
- **Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259:** Applied and extended to distinguish personal factual certification from genuine advocacy preparation; its investigation, press-activity and qualified-immunity boundaries remain intact. No categorical pre-charge/post-charge line is adopted.
- **Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., 508 U.S. 429:** Applied its historically justified functional inquiry; importance to adjudication does not itself establish absolute immunity.
- **Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21:** Applied the separation between personal-capacity liability, an actionable constitutional violation and personal defenses; official authority alone does not bar the suit.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800:** Preserved the existing objective clearly-established-law protection; no replacement qualified-immunity test is adopted.
- **Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635:** Preserved the particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness without requiring a prior case with identical facts.
- **Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510:** Applied independent consideration of all relevant precedent on the preserved immunity issue, with notice fixed to the conduct date and claim/evidence preservation limits intact.
- **United States v. Lanier, decided March 31, 1997:** Applied its rejection of categorical source and factual-twin restrictions on fair warning; it supplies no retrospective notice and does not decide Kalina's immunity.

#### Law After Decision

From June 16, 1997, absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect a prosecutor's personal factual oath used to procure an arrest warrant. Protected preparation and legal presentation remain distinct and retain absolute immunity; the charging transaction cannot be classified as a single undifferentiated act. The holding adds that function-specific certification rule without changing ordinary testimony immunity, the elements of §1983 liability or the existing qualified-immunity framework. Stone-Zsela's proposed dishonesty exception, replacement test and defense-burden allocation are noncontrolling. No actual constitutional violation, falsity, materiality, causation, damages or ultimate personal defense is determined.

### 1997-06-16 — Salazar-Haro v. INS; 95 F.3d 309 (3d Cir. 1996); Third Circuit No. 96-3007

#### Holdings

##### I. IIRIRA's transition provisions do not decide AEDPA's separate restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** The saved former procedure governs this old deportation proceeding, subject to applicable transition exceptions, while §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not itself withdraw review of the completed-adjudication claims presented here. That conclusion preserves no exemption from AEDPA §440(a), whose separate temporal operation must be decided. Section 306(c)(2) preserves an actual judicial judgment or order entered under former INA §106 (8 U.S.C. §1105a), applying §106 as amended by AEDPA §440, against an asserted operation of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate that judgment or order or require its reconsideration. The Third Circuit's September 13, 1996 dismissal falls within that protection, which neither independently confers jurisdiction nor reopens merits review nor bars ordinary appellate review of the judgment's correctness under applicable law.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, has eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this transition disposition and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Yang supplies the April 1, 1997 commencement rule; Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto apply the corrected §§306 and 309 and distinguish the three identified executive decisions or actions—commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing orders—from review of a completed legal adjudication. Corrected §309(c)(1) preserves proceedings begun before the new scheme's commencement subject to the particular exceptions. Paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only when the evidentiary hearing has not commenced, with notice at least thirty days before it; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; this earlier order does not meet that trigger. IIRIRA §306(c)(2), Pub. L. 104-208, applies only when a judicial judgment or order was actually entered under former INA §106 (8 U.S.C. §1105a), that section was applied in its form amended by AEDPA §440, Pub. L. 104-132, and an asserted effect of §306(c)(1) would invalidate the judgment or order or require reconsideration. When those conditions are met, paragraph (2) protects the judgment or order against that effect. Public Law 104-302, effective September 30, 1996, made §306(c)(1)'s general applicability subject to §309 while retaining §242(g)'s special retroactive application; it left paragraph (2)'s protection unchanged. The Third Circuit's September 13, 1996 dismissal applied former §106 as amended by AEDPA §440(a), so it is a judgment within the protected category. Paragraph (2) preserves that judgment against disruption resulting from §306(c)(1) without supplying independent jurisdiction, reopening merits review or preventing ordinary appellate review of its correctness under applicable law. The date of this Court's consideration does not apply the permanent scheme wholesale, and these transition savings do not nullify AEDPA's independent bar.

##### II. Section 440(a) withdraws this pending ordinary-review channel

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA §440(a) applies to this qualifying final deportation order despite the January 17, 1996 petition because withdrawal of future ordinary appellate review attaches no new legal disability to a protected completed event established by this record. Filing, prior conviction, family hardship and an expectation of favorable review alone establish no such disability; the agency had already considered discretionary waiver under the former law.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf first asks whether Congress prescribed temporal reach and otherwise examines the actual new legal consequence attached to a completed event; a jurisdictional label does not substitute for that inquiry. Rivers preserves the provision-specific method. Section 440(f)'s express timing rule concerns subsection (e), not a saving clause for subsection (a). Hincapie-Nieto applies §440(a) to pending ordinary review where filing and expectation alone identify no protected completed choice. Reyes-Hernandez differs because an uncontested surrender of a colorable defense acquired a new consequence; that is one possible protected predicate, not a universal prerequisite or a rule excluding every other completed event. No such distinguishing event is established here. Past convictions already made Salazar-Haro deportable, and the agency actually considered his waiver request under the earlier law. This is not a new §440(d) eligibility denial, and that subsection's separate operation is not decided. The Court adopts neither a blanket exemption for all pending petitions nor a categorical rule that every jurisdictional amendment is free from retroactivity limits.

##### III. The present record does not require deciding an unfiled collateral case

**Holding and operative rule:** This record does not require preservation of ordinary appellate review for another weighing of discretionary equities, and a constitutional label or §1651 alone does not restore that jurisdiction. Affirmance neither approves eliminating every meaningful judicial forum for an actually presented legal or constitutional defect nor decides the availability, scope or custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates of immigration review under §2241.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's Court opinion has eight Associate joins. Stone-Zsela also joins the present-record conclusion against mere discretionary reweighing, while proposing an affirmative §2241 construction rather than joining its reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hincapie-Nieto distinguishes this ordinary channel from a concrete challenge that executive restraint exceeds legal authority. Here family hardship and rehabilitation were considered against repeated serious drug dealing; dissatisfaction with that balance identifies no distinct constitutional or statutory defect. The Court does not decide whether that balance was the best exercise of discretion. Felker sustained a restricted review channel alongside an actual retained original proceeding that received independent consideration. It does not establish the existence or adequacy of an imaginary immigration substitute. Flores preserves actual individual safeguards at its custody and facial-review scope without settling these different collateral questions. Because no developed legal defect demands resolution here, the Court need neither pronounce §2241 available nor declare every other forum foreclosed. Section 1651 assists existing jurisdiction rather than creating a free-standing source. No custody status, collateral filing or adequate substitute is presumed.

##### IV. The mandate is jurisdictional, not a waiver judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's ordinary-review dismissal is affirmed without adopting its hypothetical merits footnote or adjudicating an unfiled collateral claim. No waiver, reopening, new hearing, transfer, stay or execution direction follows from this channel-specific decision.

**Authority:** Part IV of Breyer's Court opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The withdrawn jurisdiction cannot support an agency-merits adjudication. A prediction of what the lower court would have done had jurisdiction existed does not become a judgment for this Court to affirm. Nor does an abstract possibility of another remedy establish that a new petition has been filed, that its predicates are met or that relief should issue. Hincapie-Nieto supplies that confined disposition while leaving genuinely presented future claims to their proper statutory and constitutional analysis.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Landgraf supplies the controlling sequence and completed-event inquiry, with no automatic answer from pendency or a jurisdictional label.
- Rivers preserves the provision-specific temporal analysis and does not turn corrective purpose into prescribed retrospective reach.
- Hincapie-Nieto controls the distinction between an ordinary-review bar, a protected completed event and an unpresented collateral challenge; its reservation of actual §2241 questions remains intact.
- Reyes-Hernandez controls the limited §242(g) construction and is distinguished on the surrendered-colorable-defense predicate. That predicate remains one lawful temporal basis rather than a universal test.
- INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang supplies the transition commencement and separation of eligibility from reasoned discretion; no agency-merits review occurs here.
- Felker preserves the difference between a barred appellate mechanism and an actual retained original channel; it does not establish immigration habeas jurisdiction or the adequacy of a hypothetical route.
- Reno v. Flores retains its limited custody holding and actual safeguards without deciding review of this deportation order's legal basis.
- The Japanese Immigrant Case supplies antecedent due-process protection in deportation proceedings, not a right to judicial rebalancing of every discretionary waiver denial.
- Ex parte McCardle, Ex parte Yerger and Heikkila v. Barber retain their channel-specific historical significance; none is extended into a present holding on every immigration habeas predicate.

#### Law After Decision

Section 440(a) bars this pending ordinary petition under the six-Justice completed-event analysis. The old proceeding remains subject to the corrected transition map, and §440(d)'s separate eligibility operation is not decided. Section 306(c)(2)'s protection requires an actual judicial judgment or order under former INA §106 as amended by AEDPA §440 and an asserted effect of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate it or require reconsideration. It therefore protects the Third Circuit's September 13, 1996 dismissal under §106 as amended by §440(a) from that transition-rule disruption. The jurisdictional dismissal establishes neither approval of the waiver balance nor a rule closing every meaningful forum for a concrete legal defect. No majority adopts affirmative immigration §2241 jurisdiction, and no majority replaces Landgraf with the separate relevant-activity approach. The Court overrules no precedent.

These Justices retain the approach expressed in their Landgraf and Felker writings and their current Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto positions; they do not join the proposition that surrender of an earlier defense can preserve this future channel. They join the transition construction, the bounded constitutional and collateral reservation and the actual mandate. Their position leaves §440(d)'s distinct eligibility question unresolved and adopts neither a waiver-merits judgment nor elimination of every meaningful forum.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The completed-event inquiry permits withdrawal of direct review here: convictions already supplied deportability, the agency considered the waiver and no surrendered defense or comparable completed choice acquired a new substantive disability. But he would construe §440(a), in its INA setting and with §2241, affirmatively to preserve qualifying collateral review of substantial legal or constitutional objections to custody or deportation beyond lawful authority. Section 2241 supplies that source when custody and its other statutory requirements are satisfied; neither free-floating constitutional jurisdiction nor §1651 alone is sufficient. A retained forum must be able to test the actual legal basis of restraint and grant effective relief, rather than review detention conditions alone.

Felker supports attention to an actual retained route but does not itself settle this immigration construction. Flores's safeguards and the antecedent due-process protection reinforce the need for effective review in the case presented. Stone-Zsela would not decide every future habeas boundary or assume its predicates here, and would leave open a future showing that the retained route cannot hear a substantial unlawful-restraint claim. The adverse family-hardship, rehabilitation and trafficking balance alone supplies no such claim; he does not reweigh it or adopt the lower court's hypothetical footnote. He agrees with the present dismissal and orders no collateral relief or new procedure. His affirmative construction has no additional join.
