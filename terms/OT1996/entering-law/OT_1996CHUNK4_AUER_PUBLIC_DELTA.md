# Auer v. Robbins; No. 95-897.

Authority: [Public decision](../records/Auer_v_Robbins_merits_1997-02-19.md).

## Event

Auer v. Robbins, No. 95-897. Argued December 10, 1996; decided February 19, 1997. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit. St. Louis police sergeants and a lieutenant challenge their treatment as exempt executive or administrative employees under the Fair Labor Standards Act. The questions concern the salary-basis rule's validity for public employees, practical exposure to impermissible disciplinary deductions and correction of an improper deduction. The Board also asserts that it is an arm of Missouri. Salary amount and the appellate duties determination are uncontested predicates; the latter is not independently reconsidered.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms, subject to the correction conditions stated in its mandate.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed subject to actual correction, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Before disposing of the affected salary-basis claim on a corrected-exemption ground, the lower courts must give effect to both reimbursement of Guzy's improper deduction and a promise of future compliance. Neither is adjudged complete. Without satisfaction, the employer cannot rely on restored exemption, and the applicable compensation consequences remain for lawful determination.

The Board's state-arm defense is rejected. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with that threshold resolution without adopting its ground. The Court awards no fixed overtime sum, restores no part of the $9,284 pre-appeal district award and orders no general reopening of application evidence or the uncontested duties ruling.

## Holdings

### The Board's local relationship does not make it an arm of Missouri

**Holding and operative rule:** The Board is not an arm of Missouri entitled to the State's immunity in these actions because its combined financial, budgetary and operational relationship is local. State creation, statutory regulation and gubernatorial appointments do not alone make a separate public body the State; treasury exposure carries substantial weight alongside institutional control rather than serving as an indispensable sole condition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice agrees with the threshold resolution only.

**Controlling explanation:** Hess requires attention to the actual entity rather than its governmental label, and Mt. Healthy distinguishes local public bodies from the State itself. Missouri created and regulates the Board and the governor appoints four members, with the mayor serving as the fifth. Those controls matter, but the record's municipal financing, city budget and appropriation role, limits requiring city approval and local operational relationship establish the relevant character of this Board. No single appointment or financial fact substitutes for that combined inquiry. Because the Board is not a state arm, the Court need not decide whether litigation conduct effected an authorized waiver, whether Congress validly abrogated state immunity for this claim, or whether an officer route would permit the requested relief. Seminole Tribe supplies no categorical Article I answer to those unnecessary questions.

### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Fixed salary reasonably distinguishes exempt compensation from pay varying with work quality or quantity. Congress's delegation includes public employment. Disciplinary needs do not make the criterion unreasonable or establish an exception. Major-safety rules prevent serious danger to the plant or other employees; residency does not qualify. Specified absences and furloughs differ from general discipline. No rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith major-safety penalties retain the narrow scope stated above.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

### The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds

**Holding and operative rule:** The responsible Secretary's fair and considered interpretation of the salary regulation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Presentation in an amicus brief does not alone disqualify that interpretation, but supplies no automatic authority to an agency lawyer, power to amend operative text or deference to new factual assertions about this employer.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice proposes a different interpretive rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Thomas Jefferson University applies Seminole Rock's bounded own-regulation rule; Stinson preserves the difference between interpreting a rule and contradicting or amending it. Martin requires attention to the statutory allocation of administrative responsibility, while Bowen excludes unsupported litigation rationalizations. The Secretary's position here interprets the actual salary and correction text and is consistent with the supported administrative treatment, including the correction letter. It is a fair and considered legal construction, not an employer-specific factual determination resulting from a Department investigation. Its amicus form does not make it an impermissible invention or itself establish its correctness. The canon favoring narrow construction of exemptions does not prevent the Secretary from resolving actual regulatory ambiguity within the congressional delegation. Courts still determine whether the reading fits the text and lawful authority; they do not accept the agency's characterization of unproved facts merely because its legal construction controls.

### Practical exposure requires an actual practice or an effective particular policy

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The salary text concerns real susceptibility to reductions, not only deductions already made and not every abstract possibility listed in a broad disciplinary code. An effective policy can alter compensation arrangements before the first deduction; an undifferentiated range of sanctions governing exempt and nonexempt workers does not itself communicate that these salaried officers will receive impermissible deductions. Guzy's negotiated two-day residency suspension instead of termination was treated below as an exceptional event. The later rule made termination the exclusive residency sanction; it did not repeal every disciplinary authorization. That episode alone does not establish recurring deductions or a general significant likelihood.

The officers' argument that the manual itself defeats any salary agreement rests on the broader legal construction the Court rejects. Their additional examples, witness-understanding contentions and related evidentiary-burden submission remain disputed submissions, not grounds for a new blanket factual or forfeiture finding. The Court adopts no numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or universal employer-compliance presumption, and directs no general factual reopening. Ordinary preservation and mandate rules retain their force.

### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The text separates two eligibility alternatives from two cumulative acts of correction. Intentionality does not defeat the other-than-lack-of-work alternative. Nor does the text require payment immediately upon discovery. It nevertheless requires actual reimbursement and a promise; eligibility alone accomplishes neither. The correction mechanism does not convert a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. Deductions generally made when work is unavailable indicate no intention to pay on a salary basis and defeat exemption during that period.

The lower opinion quoted both conditions and required reimbursement, but its formal disposition did not expressly establish a remand implementation proceeding. This Court therefore states the operative condition in its own mandate: on return, the lower courts must secure both required acts before disposing of the affected claim on the ground of corrected exemption. Neither completed payment nor a completed promise is found. If the employer does not perform, it cannot rely on restored exemption, and lawful compensation consequences remain to be determined. No new correction condition, fixed compliance timetable, automatic overtime amount or damages award is added.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. and Mt. Healthy are applied to the Board's actual local relationship; treasury exposure remains important without becoming the exclusive condition of immunity.
- Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida remains limited to its actual holdings and unresolved State-amenability rationale. No waiver, abrogation or retrospective officer-remedy rule is added.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala and Bowles v. Seminole Rock retain controlling weight for a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation, with text, consistency, lawful authority and actual-ground limits intact.
- Stinson v. United States preserves authoritative interpretation within, rather than amendment of, the operative rule; its Sentencing Commission setting is not mechanically transferred to factual assertions here.
- Martin v. OSHRC supplies attention to actual interpretive responsibility and reasonableness; Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital preserves the exclusion of unsupported litigation rationalizations.

## Law After Decision

The Board may be subjected to these FLSA proceedings without deciding the State's own immunity. Salary basis remains a valid component of the public executive/administrative exemption. Practical exposure, actual deductions and correction are distinct inquiries, and every stated regulatory exception retains its conditions. The own-regulation controlling-weight rule remains law; the Chief Justice's proposed replacement receives no additional join. A correction-dependent exemption requires both reimbursement and the future promise, secured through the present mandate. Duties and the former district award are not reopened.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins validity, practical exposure and correction, but would replace the controlling-weight component of Thomas Jefferson University and the corresponding general Seminole Rock rule. He acknowledges that he joined TJU and that it already protects plain text, consistency, explanation and the actual agency ground. His disagreement concerns the remaining proposition that the administering agency's selection among permissible meanings controls because it wrote and administers the regulation.

He would have courts determine meaning independently using ordinary interpretive tools. If genuine uncertainty remains, an authoritative and considered agency interpretation should receive special persuasive weight when it is consistent or adequately explained, fairly noticed, within delegated authority, grounded in relevant expertise and more than a restatement of the statute. Weight would follow those reasons, rather than agency authorship alone. An interpretation may neither enlarge statute or regulation nor impose liability without the notice required by law. Agency amicus participation neither makes its reading controlling nor automatically disqualifies it as litigation invention.

In his view, delegation to prescribe an exemption differs from the judicial responsibility to determine the adopted rule's meaning. Expertise may persuade, and an actual delegation may assign policy discretion, without every ambiguity transferring interpretive authority. He accepts the Secretary's practical-exposure reading independently because it fits the text, salary concept and the distinction between an abstract sanction, an effective policy and an actual practice. He likewise accepts the correction provision's alternatives and mandatory reimbursement and future promise. He does not reach the separately asserted affirmative-burden contention because he regards it as inadequately preserved.

His proposed change would preserve lawful delegated policy choices, operative regulatory text, ordinary reasoned-decisionmaking requirements, review of the agency's actual ground and the earlier decisions' independent substantive holdings. He would not reopen TJU's completed reimbursement judgment or declare its actual construction substantively wrong. Statute-specific allocations of authority require separate examination. Concrete reliance involving settled transactions, compensation arrangements and fairly communicated obligations deserves weight; an administrative preference for controlling deference is not itself that reliance. This record identifies no concrete reliance requiring a different result where independent interpretation reaches the same salary construction and correction mechanism. He makes no finding about reliance across other programs, authorizes no general reopening, and leaves distinct retrospective-liability claims to their applicable notice, finality and remedial rules. This proposed doctrinal change is noncontrolling.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment is affirmed with this Court's enforceable correction condition. On return, the lower courts must give effect to actual reimbursement and a future-compliance promise before disposing of the affected salary-basis claim on a corrected-exemption ground. Without performance that ground remains unavailable, and compensation consequences require lawful determination. No completed cure, express earlier remand or implementation schedule is presumed. The uncontested duties ruling remains intact; the Court neither restores the former $9,284 district award nor orders a general new evidentiary inquiry.
