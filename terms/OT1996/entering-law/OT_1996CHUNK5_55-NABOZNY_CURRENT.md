### 1997-01-13 — Walmer v. Department of Defense; Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377, 52 F.3d 851; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

Authority: [Record](../records/Walmer_v_Department_of_Defense_merits_1997-01-13.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Ordinary rationality sustains this preliminary denial on the stipulated conduct record

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged conduct-based military personnel rule receives ordinary rational-basis review, which permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable predictions and imperfect fit while requiring a relationship to the actual disadvantage and forbidding bare harm or irrational prejudice. On this preliminary record of admitted homosexual acts and stipulated ineligibility for the retention exceptions, discipline, morale and workable military service relationships supply a permissible predictive relationship, so denial of equal-protection preliminary relief is affirmed without a final ruling on another or materially developed record.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins to this ground and the preliminary disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits conceivable legitimate reasons without formal findings or empirical certainty. Heller requires a relation to the specific disadvantage and also preserves relevant classwide generalizations. Romer applies those limits to a blanket civil disability; its ordinary-review rule neither assigns an orientation scrutiny tier nor makes every conduct-based personnel classification invalid. The Court examines this military rule's connection to discipline, morale and workable service relationships instead of accepting the military label as an answer.

The stipulated acts and unavailable retention exceptions define this application. Reasonable military predictions may address service relationships classwide without proof that this officer's performance has already impaired a unit. Goldman and Rostker preserve substantial respect for professional military judgments while leaving constitutional review intact. A more individualized policy is not constitutionally required merely because it is possible. Bare private hostility would not be a legitimate objective, but the identified personnel relationship supplies a distinct predictive ground here.

Rich's fraudulent-entry facts are not this record, and its suggestion about satisfying a hypothetical stricter standard is unnecessary. The Court sustains the preliminary denial through direct ordinary-review analysis, not factual identity with Rich or automatic foreclosure by it. Bowers's specified-conduct holding does not itself decide the equal-protection tier or application. Kelley and Virginia concern sex classifications and supply no transferred burden here.

Walmer's preliminary showing does not establish the requisite merits basis for relief under the inquiry applied below. The Court finds none of the other three equitable factors satisfied merely because the court of appeals assumed them. It orders no discharge and decides neither an orientation-only inference, later statements legislation nor another claimant's exception eligibility.

**Precedent treatment:** Beach, both relevant Heller propositions and Romer are applied at their actual ordinary-review scope; Goldman and Rostker supply deference without immunity. Bowers, Kelley and Virginia are distinguished at the questions they decided.

#### II. The attainder prohibition does not extend to this executive military regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** The Bill of Attainder Clause restrains legislative punishment, and the challenged executive Army regulation does not satisfy that legislative-action predicate. The attainder ground therefore fails without deciding whether the separation would constitute punishment if the required legislative predicate existed; other constitutional, statutory and administrative restraints on executive action remain distinct.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books identify legislative action as an antecedent condition distinct from identification and punishment. Walmer does not contend that this measure is legislation; she asks to extend the prohibition to executive military regulation. The constitutional allocation does not support that extension. Other sources of law may constrain executive abuse without changing the Clause's institutional target.

The district court also discussed punishment, but the court of appeals did not reach it and the Court has no need to do so. An honorable-separation label is therefore not held conclusively nonpunitive, and no hypothetical legislative separation measure is adjudicated. The abandoned APA theory is not revived; neither an independent due-process claim nor an unpresented statutory challenge is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books supply and preserve the legislative-action prerequisite. Their distinct punishment inquiries are neither displaced nor answered for this separation.

#### Precedent Treatment

- FCC v. Beach Communications: applies conceivable legitimate grounds and tolerance for imperfect fit without formal findings, subject to the prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice.
- Heller v. Doe: preserves the need for a relationship to the actual disadvantage and the separate allowance for relevant family-information generalizations; no heightened scrutiny follows from either holding.
- Romer v. Evans: applies ordinary rational-basis limits and distinguishes its blanket withdrawal and foreclosure of civil protection from this service-specific conduct rule. It is neither overruled nor converted into a Government actual-objective burden.
- Goldman v. Weinberger and Rostker v. Goldberg: preserve substantial military deference without constitutional immunity; an established right's separate scrutiny rule is not imported into this ordinary equality inquiry.
- Bowers v. Hardwick: remains effective at its specified-conduct question and does not automatically settle this equal-protection tier or application.
- Kelley and United States v. Virginia: retain their sex-classification requirements; their burdens are not transferred to a different classification by analogy alone.
- Nixon v. Administrator of General Services, Selective Service System v. Minnesota Public Interest Research Group, and Ambassador Books: preserve legislative action as necessary to an attainder claim, separately from punishment. No new ruling on the punishment character of separation is entered.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 13, 1997, ordinary rational-basis review governs this preliminary, stipulated-conduct challenge to the earlier Army regulation, and the identified military personnel relationship sustains denial of interim relief. The Court creates no heightened sexual-orientation tier, Government actual-purpose proof burden, individualized-impairment prerequisite, least-restrictive-means rule or constitutional right to military service. Reasonable military judgments remain reviewable, and bare hostility remains insufficient. The executive regulation cannot be challenged as a bill of attainder on the presented extension theory, but other legal restraints are not displaced. The retention exceptions remain part of the regulation; their complete criteria, validity and application to another claimant are not adjudicated.

### 1997-01-21 - Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth; historical petition identifier No. 95-617; no separate merits docket assigned; OT1996.

Authority: [Record](../records/Curtis_v_School_Committee_of_Falmouth_merits_1997-01-21.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Every holding below has nine direct rationale votes.

##### I. The optional program does not establish the claimed parental-liberty infringement

**Holding and operative rule:** The described school condom-availability program does not violate parental liberty or familial privacy merely because parents cannot require the school to exclude their children or notify them of participation. Program entry is optional, abstention carries no educational or disciplinary consequence, and the program does not prohibit parental teaching, alter custody, compel treatment or sexual conduct, or require assent to a message; the junior-high counseling condition applies only after a student elects to request condoms.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Meyer and Pierce, which protect parental direction of upbringing and education, together with Yoder's protection against the particular compulsory educational interference with religious family life and Prince's recognition that parental authority does not exclude every public role concerning children. Those decisions do not establish the particular school-enforced veto sought here.

The school provides the item directly, not merely information about it. That difference matters, but it does not itself displace parental authority. Parents remain free to teach against participation and instruct their children to abstain. Students may comply without losing instruction, grades, benefits, or standing at school. Compulsory school attendance does not turn every separable optional resource into compelled participation.

A junior-high student requesting condoms must receive counseling before receipt. The Court does not erase that condition or approve unknown counseling content. Nothing in the stated condition requires entry into the program, a prescribed affirmation, sexual activity, or a medical procedure. No independent coercive-counseling claim is established here.

A. St. P. C. protects against a complete, potentially indefinite parental-contact cutoff; Baby Richard protects against serious avoidable harm in the manner of compulsory custody transfer; M.L.B. protects necessary-record access in a provided first appeal from permanent parental termination. None of those deprivations occurs here. Practical difficulty in securing a child's adherence to parental teaching does not, on these facts, establish a constitutional right to compel the school to enforce the requested refusal.

**Limits:** This holding does not decide confidential medical treatment, prescription drugs, compelled sex education, distribution to a child unable to consent, or a program using penalty or deception to undermine parental authority. It neither denies parental liberty nor recognizes an independent constitutional entitlement to receive condoms.

##### II. The stated program imposes no established constitutional burden on religious exercise

**Holding and operative rule:** A program that students may decline without sacrificing ordinary education or another benefit, and that requires neither religiously prohibited participation nor assent, does not burden these families' free exercise merely by making the described condoms available contrary to their religious teaching. The stated program establishes no religious targeting, unequal treatment, penalty for religious refusal, or comparable official pressure to participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Lyng and Bowen v. Roy distinguish sincere conflict with government activity from interference with protected religious exercise; they do not place all government services beyond constitutional review. Here the school demands no endorsement of contraception or premarital sex, no use of condoms, and no surrender of worship or religious instruction. The junior-high receipt condition does not compel anyone to enter the program and supplies no established contrary religious affirmation.

The parents' concerns about peer influence and compulsory attendance deserve consideration. Lee's treatment of substantial official pressure at school-directed graduation prayer remains intact. Students here may avoid the separable resource without giving up the educational occasion or apparently joining a state-directed religious exercise. The asserted generalized peer influence does not establish comparable official pressure under these program terms. The holding therefore does not depend on a universal rule that only formal punishment can coerce.

The claim fails at its constitutional burden threshold. The Court need not decide whether Smith would independently defeat a claim involving an actual burden under a neutral law. Invoking parental liberty and free exercise together supplies neither the missing infringement nor automatic strict scrutiny. Lukumi's religious-targeting protections and Settle's prohibition on supported religious exclusion remain fully applicable to materially different facts.

**Limits:** The Court does not approve religious targeting, compelled participation in sexual instruction, denial of an exemption from a mandatory practice, or benefits conditioned on conduct forbidden by faith. The separate statutory inquiry under RFRA is not decided.

##### III. Neither parental exclusion nor notification is constitutionally required on these grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** Because neither claimed constitutional deprivation is established by this program's stated operation, the Constitution does not require the requested parental exclusion or notification mechanism, and summary judgment for the school defendants stands on the granted constitutional claims. No injunction, new evidentiary proceeding, or prescribed parental procedure follows.

**Controlling explanation:** An exemption from compelled conduct differs from a parental right to bar a child's voluntary access. The child can already decline this program without consequence; the claimed school-enforced veto requires a substantive constitutional source that the presented parental and religious claims do not supply. Casey permitted a qualified parental-consent arrangement for abortion while preserving an independent judicial route; permission for such a statute does not command parental control over every preventive item.

The parents' expert affidavits concerning possible adverse consequences are not findings of harm or harmlessness. Under the adopted no-infringement grounds, disputed policy wisdom and comparative health effects do not create a material constitutional fact requiring trial. The judgment does not establish that the program is medically necessary, effective, harmless, or the least restrictive policy.

**Limits:** The Court does not prohibit voluntary parental accommodation, resolve different consent or disclosure laws, or decide an independent RFRA claim. Unargued state statutory claims concerning statutory-rape accomplice liability, abuse reporting, and moral education, and unpresented state constitutional claims, remain outside review. No finding that a medical-consent claim was waived is made.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meyer v. Nebraska, 262 U.S. 390, and Pierce v. Society of Sisters, 268 U.S. 510:** Apply the protected parental role without extending it to compulsory school enforcement of the refusal sought here.
- **Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205, and Prince v. Massachusetts, 321 U.S. 158:** Preserve their distinct treatment of family religious direction and public authority concerning children; neither compels the requested veto over this optional resource.
- **Lyng v. Northwest Indian Cemetery Protective Association, 485 U.S. 439, and Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693:** Apply their distinction between conflict with belief and interference with protected exercise, without creating immunity for all school services.
- **Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872:** Does not supply an additional neutral-law holding; pleading combined interests does not itself establish an infringement. Its application to a genuinely burdening program is unnecessary to decide.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992:** Preserves the rule against substantial official participation pressure in the school-directed religious exercise there. The separable optional resource here does not present the same state-created pressure; formal voluntariness is not universally conclusive.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah, June 11, 1993, and Settle v. Dickson County School Board, June 25, 1996:** Preserve protection against religious targeting and supported religious exclusion; no such act is established by this program's stated terms.
- **Planned Parenthood v. Casey, June 29, 1992:** Distinguishes permission for the abortion-consent statute from a constitutional command for parental control over this item. Outside its medical-emergency exception, the sustained statute ordinarily requires the minor's and one parent's informed consent, allows family substitutions, and permits independent court authorization without first approaching a parent, on maturity and actual informed consent or alternatively best interests. Nothing here modifies that statute's safeguards or makes parental involvement an absolute veto.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C., November 20, 1995; Baby Richard v. Kirchner, June 21, 1996; and M.L.B. v. S.L.J., December 16, 1996:** Distinguish their respective complete-contact cutoff, compulsory custody-transition injury, and first-appellate-review termination barriers. Their rules remain unchanged.
- **Church of Scientology v. Clearwater, December 5, 1994; Swanner, March 27, 1995; and American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995:** Preserve RFRA's separate person-and-application burden and justification inquiry. None supplies a constitutional rule that neutral administration can never burden religion; no statutory RFRA claim is resolved here.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 21, 1997, the described optional school program requires neither the requested parental exclusion nor notification mechanism under the presented parental-liberty and free-exercise theories. The decision establishes that confined constitutional boundary; it creates neither general school-health immunity nor a child's constitutional entitlement to the service. Existing family-status protections, actual-pressure and religious-targeting rules, qualified abortion-consent law, and RFRA's separate statutory inquiry retain their prior force.

### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's creation of roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise concern affirmative conduct. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver. Neither lower-court disagreement nor absence of identical facts independently establishes immunity. Collins, decided after Uhlrig's death, and later Elder and Pinder govern present analysis but cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court leaves constitutional culpability, the affirmative-danger merits and the Tenth Circuit's five-part test undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald supplies objective qualified immunity and remains intact.
- Anderson v. Creighton supplies particularized apparent unlawfulness without requiring an identical prior case.
- Elder v. Holloway is applied to require examination of all relevant conduct-time authority while preserving the favorable factual account and ordinary claim/evidence limits; it supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Pinder v. Johnson is applied to distinguish an affirmative deprivation from omitted protection and to keep notice separate from merits. Its March 1989 result does not resolve this later conduct automatically, and its substantive reservation remains open.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights retains its no-general-workplace-duty rule and its separate, bounded arbitrariness holding. It does not reject every affirmative-danger claim or supply notice before its February 26, 1992 decision.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County and Daniels v. Williams preserve the distinctions between a general protective duty, an affirmative deprivation and negligence; the Court does not convert them into a complete constitutional answer here.
- Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are considered as conduct-time circuit authority at their actual scopes, without converting their formulations into Supreme Court holdings.

#### Law After Decision

The decision establishes only the particularized immunity result for the five administrators on this reviewed claim. It supplies no general affirmative-danger test, categorical public-employment bar, mandatory deceit or assurance requirement, no-awareness finding or constitutional approval of budget choices. The distinction between a general workplace-safety duty and an attributable affirmative deprivation remains. A different official act, record or conduct-time legal landscape requires its own analysis.

### 1997-02-24 - Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.; First Circuit No. 95-1275, 68 F.3d 525; no separate Supreme Court merits docket assigned; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Record](../records/Brown_v_Hot_Sexy_Safer_Productions_merits_1997-02-24.md).

#### Holdings

#### The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Parental liberty protects choice among lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not give each parent authority to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. Accepting the alleged compulsory exposure and aftermath, this complaint establishes no displacement of family custody, educational choice or parental teaching that supports the requested substantive federal veto; the ruling does not approve pretextual curricular action or decide independent state-law permission rights.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined on each of the three granted holdings by every other Justice, giving each proposition nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Meyer and Pierce protect parental direction of education and upbringing. They do not make the public school a forum in which every parent may prescribe the particular lessons a child receives. The parents remain free to choose lawful educational alternatives and to instruct their children concerning the ideas encountered at school. Public-school enrollment does not waive that continuing role.

The Court acknowledges the difference from Curtis: attendance here was required, while Curtis concerned a separable optional resource and expressly reserved compulsory instruction. Its result cannot automatically decide this case. The particular compulsion alleged here is exposure to a presentation, not displacement of parental custody or instruction, compelled profession or personal performance by these plaintiffs. Those limits support the present conclusion without treating attendance as voluntary.

Settle requires a genuine pedagogical basis; calling an event curriculum is insufficient. The Court neither endorses the alleged crude conduct as sound teaching nor holds that any presentation bearing an educational label is constitutional. It decides that these allegations do not establish the particular parental deprivation asserted. The written-permission policy may impose an independent obligation, but its alleged breach does not itself create the substantive federal right the parents seek. State-law remedies and materially different claims remain outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Meyer and Pierce are applied at the scope of the parental interest they protect. Curtis's reservation of compulsory instruction is preserved and the present allegations are examined independently. Settle's genuine-pedagogy requirement remains intact.

#### These allegations establish neither the claimed student privacy deprivation nor extreme official abuse

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory exposure to the alleged presentation and subsequent coarse imitation does not, on this complaint, establish the students' asserted decisional-privacy or bodily-integrity deprivation or extreme abuse of official power. The conclusion rests on the actual allegations, including the absence of compelled personal performance, affirmation, intimate disclosure, physical contact or treatment; it is no categorical rule that words, a single event or compulsory educational programming can never violate the Constitution.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this holding in the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** A constitutional privacy or bodily-integrity claim requires an invasion of the protected interest, not simply exposure to ideas or language the listener finds offensive. These students were required to attend. They were not alleged to have been selected for a skit, required to affirm the message, disclose intimate information, engage in sexual or religious conduct, or undergo bodily intrusion. The Court considers that account together with the alleged weeks of coarse imitation; it does not discard the aftermath or convert unfavorable facts into trial findings.

The parents' strongest objection is that the school's compulsory authority gave the presentation an intrusive character beyond ordinary offensive speech. That circumstance matters, but it does not establish the particular personal deprivation pleaded. The alleged program, taken as a whole and with favorable inferences, also does not establish the extreme official abuse asserted here. The Court announces no universal rule that verbal conduct cannot do so and does not decide the rights of a student personally subjected to a materially different act.

Curtis supplies no categorical answer because it reserved compulsory instruction. Settle likewise does not license religious exclusion, pretextual pedagogy or arbitrary conduct merely because it occurs in school. The present holding leaves protections against compelled profession or participation, bodily intrusion, religious establishment or targeting, and independently established discrimination intact. It does not transform authority to restrict certain school speech into an individual federal entitlement to censorship of every offensive presentation.

**Precedent treatment:** Curtis's limits and Settle's genuine-operation inquiry are preserved. Neither decision supplies general school immunity or resolves materially different personal coercion or invasion claims.

#### The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Title IX requires discrimination, exclusion or a hostile educational condition imposed because of sex; sexual subject matter and offense alone do not establish that relationship. The complaint connects the alleged later hostility to disagreement with the presentation's message rather than the plaintiffs' sex, so this element's absence requires dismissal without deciding severity or pervasiveness, a comprehensive recipient-liability rule, or the treatment of materially different sex-based targeting.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this sufficient statutory ground. No severity, attribution or constitutional speech-defense holding supplies an alternative basis.

**Controlling explanation:** The presentation's sexual content and alleged offensiveness are accepted. So are the subsequent weeks of coarse imitation. Neither fact, alone or in their stated context, supplies the missing connection between the treatment of these plaintiffs and their sex. They allege disagreement with the program's message and ensuing hostility on that basis. The program's delivery to an audience containing both sexes is contextual evidence, not a categorical defense to sex discrimination.

Harris requires a contextual inquiry and rejects psychological injury as a prerequisite; DeAngelis likewise rejects mandatory deterioration in performance, physical advances or similar added thresholds. Those employment decisions inform the analysis by analogy, but the Court does not transplant a complete Title VII attribution formula into Title IX. Personal targeting, touching, measurable academic loss and psychological injury are not indispensable elements announced here. Nor does the Court hold that one episode can never suffice. It does not decide whether this entire program and aftermath meet a fully elaborated Title IX severity-or-pervasiveness standard.

Franklin recognizes compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination. Availability of that remedy cannot supply the statutory violation absent from this complaint. The decision leaves questions involving sex-specific humiliation, school-authorized sexual targeting and deliberate indifference to known sex-based harassment for cases that present them, without prescribing their complete liability rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris and DeAngelis retain their contextual protections and operate here only by analogy. Franklin's compensation rule remains available for a proved intentional violation; it does not dispense with the because-of-sex requirement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Meyer v. Nebraska and Pierce v. Society of Sisters protect parental educational choice and instruction; they do not supply the lesson-by-lesson veto sought on these allegations.
- Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth retains its rule for the optional program there and its express reservation of compulsory instruction and genuine religious compulsion. Today's required attendance receives its own analysis.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board continues to require genuine pedagogical reasons and to forbid religious exclusion disguised as curricular administration; an educational label is insufficient.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems and DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association preserve contextual harassment analysis without mandatory psychological injury, physical advances or measurable performance loss. Their employment rules do not automatically determine Title IX attribution or its complete harassment standard.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools preserves compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination without supplying a missing statutory element or deciding the reserved recipient-liability questions here.

#### Law After Decision

The three pleaded federal theories fail at their stated grounds. Compulsory attendance remains a material fact, but these allegations establish neither the asserted parental or personal constitutional deprivation nor discrimination because of sex. The Court adopts no general curricular immunity, categorical exemption for a single offensive episode, added injury prerequisite or comprehensive Title IX recipient rule. The separate ungranted constitutional and statutory dismissals and the state-law dispute receive no new controlling merits determination.

### 1997-01-13 — Walmer v. Department of Defense; Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377, 52 F.3d 851; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by Justice Thomas.** They join both Court parts and regard the specific military relationship as sufficient. They also draw on Bowers's recognition of authority to regulate the specified conduct: a reasonable conduct-based personnel policy may rest on a permissible moral judgment as well as military discipline. Their Romer dissent distinguished such judgment from a bare wish to harm persons. They do not claim that Bowers already decided equal protection, that every private aversion is legitimate or that the Court has overruled Romer. Beach's tolerance for reasonable predictions and imperfect fit supports this narrower service policy. No individual-performance finding or least restrictive alternative is required. This additional conduct-morality ground is theirs alone and is not attributed to the other three equality affirmers.

**Justice Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg.** They join the attainder holding but would vacate the equality-based denial and remand. Heller and Romer require examination of the connection between this actual disadvantage and a legitimate objective. Rich concerned fraudulent denial on entry under another regulation, so treating it as conclusive leaves this admitted-acts classification insufficiently examined. Stevens's Beach position likewise requires a relationship to the actual distinction, and Ginsburg's Virginia position supports applying the proper inquiry without importing that case's sex-classification tier.

They accept substantial deference to reasonable military predictions, the availability of conceivable legitimate reasons and Walmer's burden to establish entitlement to preliminary relief. They find legal inadequacy in foreclosure by the older case, not proof that the Army lacks a rational basis. They demand neither actual-purpose proof from the Government, heightened scrutiny, an individualized impairment finding nor a new evidentiary hearing. On remand the courts should reassess the merits question under ordinary rationality and address irreparable injury, comparative harm and public interest if the renewed merits assessment makes them material. Those factors were never found satisfied. They would neither enjoin separation automatically nor finally invalidate the policy.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** He joins the legislative-predicate attainder holding and would vacate the equality-based denial under a different inquiry. His Part A treats mandatory separation for homosexual acts as an intentional sexual-orientation classification, whether described as conduct or a deliberate status proxy. Bowers does not exempt it from equality review. Romer supplies an ordinary-review floor and a warning against unexplained class disadvantage, but does not itself allocate a justification burden to the Government. He would make that doctrinal change here: the Government must identify an actual military objective and establish a real relationship between it and the intentional classification, without relying on moral disapproval, private bias or stereotype.

His reason is that merely conceivable explanations would permit this intentional disadvantage without establishing its actual connection to the military function invoked. Professional judgments and reasonable contemporaneous predictions about discipline, cohesion, readiness and command receive substantial but nonconclusive weight; an assertion of deference does not carry the Government's burden. Experience and institutional knowledge may suffice without scientific certainty or a universal formal-findings requirement. Part A independently supports his remand because the lower courts treated the claim as foreclosed, placed the practical burden on Walmer to disprove the rule, and did not resolve the military justification his proposed allocation requires.

His separable Part B additionally adopts intermediate scrutiny: the Government must identify an actual important military objective and establish a substantial relationship to it, with substantial weight to supported professional judgment. Neither Romer nor the public-university sex cases already supplies that tier. Separating Part B leaves Part A's independent Government burden intact.

He retains Bowers's specified-conduct holding, ordinary regulation of materially relevant conduct and the absence of a constitutional entitlement to service. He requires neither least restrictive means nor an individual exception in every case, and he does not infer that military concerns lack support from the absence of an individual performance finding. The district court should assess the military showing on a developed record and apply all preliminary-relief requirements. He would order no injunction, immediate status change or final invalidation. Any concrete personnel commitments or settled arrangements must be shown in the actual record and given their proper remedial weight; abstract command responsibility, convenience, institutional preference or expected continued exclusion does not establish protected reliance. He makes no finding here that such commitments exist and adds no automatic transition requirement. His Part A burden and Part B tier each remain noncontrolling.

### 1997-02-24 - Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.; First Circuit No. 95-1275, 68 F.3d 525; no separate Supreme Court merits docket assigned; OT1996, chunk 4.
