### Qualified-immunity review

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

### Personal supervisory wrongdoing and conduct-time immunity

#### Doe v. Taylor Independent School District

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** October 16, 1995.

##### Knowing, causally effective supervisory acquiescence in sexual abuse through school authority

**Holding:** A school supervisor violates a pupil's bodily-integrity right by actually knowing of sexual abuse through school authority or a substantial risk of it, deliberately and unreasonably disregarding the known danger despite authority and a reasonable opportunity to protect, and thereby causing injury or its continuation. A reasonable response is not actionable merely because injury occurs; awareness may be proved circumstantially, but warning signs do not compel it and what the official merely should have known is insufficient. The properly assumed Lankford facts describe this personal knowing acquiescence, for which preconduct law gave fair notice, so immunity does not bar this claim at this stage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct votes.

**Material application:** The teacher's exploitation of official authority and access supplies the governmental connection although some acts occurred away from school. The alleged warnings and culpable nonresponse are assumed for legal review; earlier contact, later intercourse and continuing abuse remain distinct for causation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Negligent failure to discover misconduct, a reporting-law breach alone, supervisory office or vicarious liability does not suffice. No communicated approval, actual liability, damages, district liability, separate Title IX claim or additional equal-protection merits ruling is established. May 1987 authorities cannot give retroactive notice for earlier omissions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Lankford's immunity denial, 5–4, and return the surviving claim for proof of actual awareness, culpable inaction and attributable injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ingraham supplies bodily security; Monell and Rizzo preserve personal attribution and causal linkage. Hafer confirms individual responsibility; Elder requires all relevant conduct-date law. Collins's voluntary-employment omissions do not decide knowing participation in deliberate bodily abuse. Farmer is an actual-awareness analogy, not retroactive notice. Albright supplies no controlling general rule displacing this claim. The earlier supervisory cases inform the specific notice inquiry without becoming interchangeable general duties; Jefferson and Lopez's later dates and distinct direct-action/supervisory settings limit their use.

##### Caplinger's immunity on the distinct particularized notice question

**Holding:** Caplinger receives qualified immunity because conduct-time law did not clearly establish that his particular supervisory conduct, on his own assumed information and responses, violated Doe's federal right. The Court neither decides that conduct's constitutional merits nor imputes Lankford's fuller knowledge to him.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela concurs only in the judgment on a separate merits ground; that ground is not controlling.

**Material application:** Caplinger had limited later information and undertook inquiries, an interview at which Doe denied sex, and warnings; a promised wider meeting did not occur. These circumstances are distinct from Lankford's alleged knowing acquiescence.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No constitutional approval of Caplinger's response, broad school-official immunity, mandatory merits-first sequence, or resolution of the conflicting January/February notice descriptions follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm Caplinger's immunity, unanimous in judgment. The legal/factual appellate boundary is separately recorded under Federal Courts, Doe, October 16, 1995, Fifth Circuit No. 90-8431.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Elder and Hafer require individualized conduct-date examination. The May 1987 Jefferson and Lopez decisions neither supply earlier notice nor equate the superintendent's circumstances with the principal's.

#### Hodge v. Jones

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-1182; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** October 23, 1995.

##### Damages immunity for the preserved confidential-record retention claim

**Holding:** The individual officials have qualified immunity for the challenged 1989–1990 retention of the cleared, confidential child-protection investigation report because the asserted federal disclosure, expunction and procedural-treatment duties were not then clearly established. Whether the preserved conduct violated a constitutional or state-created substantive right remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgment on a noncontrolling merits ground.

**Material application:** The retained report recorded clearance, not parental dangerousness. The preserved claim establishes no removal, interference with a particular parental decision, public publication, benefit or employment loss, or revoked clearance. Statutory access by specified recipients was limited, not absolute secrecy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal tangible-loss requirement, general authorization of confidential records, inaccurate entries or disclosure is adopted. The distinct erroneous AMF sexual-abuse code was not pleaded. The lower court's characterization of AMF as the statutory central registry is neither an enacted command nor resolved here. Constitutional informational privacy, any disputed substantive statutory entitlement, broader disclosure and fees remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm judgment for the individual defendants, unanimous in judgment. Prospective mootness is a distinct holding under Federal Courts, Hodge, October 23, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 93-1182.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Elder governs particularized historical notice; Whalen concerns personal information and confidentiality; Paul separates reputation from protected deprivation; Roth and Olim separate substantive interests from procedure alone. Collins preserves the distinction between state duty and federal injury. Albright's fractured views impose no controlling confinement-or-monetary-loss rule. Doe's physical-abuse holding neither decides informational retention nor supplies retrospective notice.

### Affirmative danger and conduct-time qualified immunity

#### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### Do protective assurances or custody of a private assailant establish a custodial duty to protect the threatened family?

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. An alleged affirmative deprivation of otherwise available private protection remains analytically distinct from an omission of public protection; absence of custody of the threatened persons does not itself resolve that different claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale. Part I's separate appellate holding appears under Federal Courts, Qualified-immunity appeals and presentation.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a protection duty founded merely on official knowledge and assurances, but its discussion of increased vulnerability does not fully resolve this alleged affirmative course of conduct. The substantive-due-process inquiry concerns the official's own deprivation and the culpability attached to that deprivation, not simply the making of a statement. Daniels excludes mere negligence; the Court does not decide its reserved questions about recklessness or gross negligence. Pinder alleged that Johnson's assurance induced her to return to work rather than provide or arrange private protection. That favorable account must remain distinct from a general right to police protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court establishes no general constitutional duty to detain an assailant, warn a victim, or fulfill every protective promise. It does not hold that custody is indispensable to every affirmative-danger claim, or decide the constitutional merits of the assumed affirmative-deprivation theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- DeShaney rejects knowledge-and-promise protection duties without deciding this entire affirmative-increased-vulnerability theory.
- Daniels excludes merely negligent deprivation while leaving its reserved culpability questions unresolved here.

##### Was Johnson's alleged conduct sufficiently established as unconstitutional by March 10, 1989 to defeat qualified immunity?

**Holding:** Qualified immunity turns on whether all relevant law existing when the officer acted made the particular conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, without demanding a factual twin. On the favorable allegations here, law existing March 10, 1989 did not sufficiently establish that this noncoercive undertaking, release of the assailant, and uncorrected exposure constituted Johnson's own substantive-due-process deprivation; he therefore receives individual immunity on the reviewed claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II's notice standard joined by all nine Justices; Part III's application and affirmance joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice majority. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from the immunity disposition; their substantive theory is not controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not identical prior facts, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant law. DeShaney had rejected an omission-based duty while leaving the distinct affirmative theory incomplete. Bowers discussed active official wrongdoing but rejected release-based liability; Archie discussed obstruction of private aid in a materially different culpability setting and reached an adverse outcome; Jensen's discussion of a distinct danger did not preserve a knowledge-and-promise special relationship after DeShaney. Those sources did not sufficiently identify the assumed noncoercive interference here as the officer's own deprivation. A judge's disagreement, the number of judges on each side, or an officer's subjective belief in legality does not independently establish immunity. Later cases cannot retroactively supply the missing conduct-time notice.

**Material application:** The Court assumes that the assurance caused the mother to return to work and forgo alternative protection and that the remaining favorable allegations are true. It does not award immunity because actual reliance was absent, choose disputed facts against Pinder, or reject constitutional liability on the merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is notice-specific and claim-specific. Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets do not supply retroactive notice; Love establishes no mandatory merits-first sequence. Neither broader affirmative-danger liability nor the ultimate culpability and causation merits is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's reviewed individual-immunity judgment for Johnson is affirmed, 5-4. No new constitutional merits judgment or damages award is entered. Official-capacity and municipal claims, equal protection, section 1985, state wrongful-death and survival claims, and other excluded matters remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Anderson supplies the objective, particularized notice inquiry without an exact-facts requirement.
- Elder requires review of all relevant contemporaneous authority rather than an isolated citation or subjective belief.
- DeShaney supplies the rejection of a general protective duty but does not itself resolve the assumed affirmative-deprivation merits.
- Bowers, Archie and Jensen are considered as conduct-time circuit-law notice sources; their distinct facts, outcomes and culpability limits are preserved, and they are not converted into Supreme Court holdings.
- Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets cannot establish notice for conduct preceding those decisions; Love does not impose mandatory sequencing.

### Municipal liability and public employment

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing supervisory acquiescence in school-authority sexual abuse

### Knowing supervisory acquiescence in school-authority sexual abuse

**Governing question:** When a school supervisor's own inaction violates a pupil's bodily integrity.

**Current rule:** A supervisor is personally responsible when the supervisor actually knows of sexual abuse through school authority or a substantial risk of it, deliberately and unreasonably disregards that known danger despite authority and a reasonable opportunity to protect the pupil, and that culpable inaction causes the injury or its continuation. A reasonable response is not actionable merely because injury continues. Circumstantial evidence may establish awareness, but warning signs do not compel that finding and what the official merely should have known is insufficient. Supervisory status, negligence, failure to discover misconduct, or breach of a state reporting duty alone does not establish the federal wrong.

**Authority by component:**

- Personal culpability and causation: Knowing, causally effective acquiescence in a teacher’s sexual abuse is personal wrongdoing, 1995-10-16; Ginsburg Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Doe v. Taylor ISD, 1995-10-16.

**Exceptions and limits:** Individual qualified immunity requires its separate conduct-time notice inquiry; recognition of the current rule supplies no retroactive notice. No general monitoring duty, vicarious liability, or finding of ultimate liability follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Doe v. Taylor Independent School District.

### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's creation of roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise concern affirmative conduct. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver. Neither lower-court disagreement nor absence of identical facts independently establishes immunity. Collins, decided after Uhlrig's death, and later Elder and Pinder govern present analysis but cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court leaves constitutional culpability, the affirmative-danger merits and the Tenth Circuit's five-part test undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald supplies objective qualified immunity and remains intact.
- Anderson v. Creighton supplies particularized apparent unlawfulness without requiring an identical prior case.
- Elder v. Holloway is applied to require examination of all relevant conduct-time authority while preserving the favorable factual account and ordinary claim/evidence limits; it supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Pinder v. Johnson is applied to distinguish an affirmative deprivation from omitted protection and to keep notice separate from merits. Its March 1989 result does not resolve this later conduct automatically, and its substantive reservation remains open.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights retains its no-general-workplace-duty rule and its separate, bounded arbitrariness holding. It does not reject every affirmative-danger claim or supply notice before its February 26, 1992 decision.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County and Daniels v. Williams preserve the distinctions between a general protective duty, an affirmative deprivation and negligence; the Court does not convert them into a complete constitutional answer here.
- Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are considered as conduct-time circuit authority at their actual scopes, without converting their formulations into Supreme Court holdings.

#### Law After Decision

The decision establishes only the particularized immunity result for the five administrators on this reviewed claim. It supplies no general affirmative-danger test, categorical public-employment bar, mandatory deceit or assurance requirement, no-awareness finding or constitutional approval of budget choices. The distinction between a general workplace-safety duty and an attributable affirmative deprivation remains. A different official act, record or conduct-time legal landscape requires its own analysis.

### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment. He would reach the constitutional merits under Collins's workplace-duty boundary and a proposed rule for the reserved affirmative-danger claim. He would require an official's affirmative creation or material increase of a specific foreseeable danger, or knowing obstruction of available protection, with conscious disregard of a known serious risk when time and practical opportunity permit a response, and a causal connection to the injury. Assurances, reliance and imminence may bear on that inquiry but are not universally necessary elements; he would add no undefined independent conscience-shocking requirement after those conditions are proved.

On this record, closing the unit and delegating placement were affirmative policy acts, but the warnings, file access and clinical process do not demonstrate the personal culpable contribution his rule requires. No named defendant is shown personally to have selected Waddell's destination, given Uhlrig a concrete assurance inducing abandonment of available protection, concealed a known immediate threat or personally overridden clinical safeguards to direct her into the encounter. He does not find that all officials lacked awareness or that the decision was sound policy. The gravity of the harm and possible negligence cannot alone make senior administrators constitutional insurers.

He would preserve liability questions involving concealment of an imminent threat, an affirmative direction into a uniquely dangerous encounter after a safety assurance, or a personal override of safeguards with deliberate indifference. Independently pleaded federal rights and genuinely different compulsory or intentionally harmful conduct remain outside his no-general-duty reasoning. Because his merits inquiry finds no constitutional violation, he does not decide qualified immunity. His proposed framework and application are noncontrolling.

### 1997-03-10 — Nabozny v. Podlesny; Seventh Circuit No. 95-3634, 92 F.3d 446; no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Nabozny_v_Podlesny_merits_1997-03-10.md).

#### Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. That direct common coalition controls each holding below.

###### Intentionally unequal school protection because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials who intentionally withhold ordinarily available protection because a student is male must establish that the sex distinction substantially serves an important governmental objective. The supported evidence permits a finding of purposeful unequal administration without a sufficient objective, so the sex claim independently survives summary judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Yick Wo forbids deliberately unequal administration of an existing protection. Feeney requires purposeful selection because of the adverse effect, and Arlington Heights permits that purpose to be shown through statements, departures and context. Hogan and Virginia supply the demanding important-objective and substantial-relationship inquiry for sex discrimination. Officials need not give identical discipline in every case, and negligence, unequal outcomes or violation of a state policy alone does not establish this claim. Here the alleged responses to male-on-male abuse, admitted ordinary enforcement and differing treatment of male-on-female abuse permit the requisite finding. Missing names and dates in some comparator examples affect proof but do not erase the direct evidence. Defendants may establish their contrary account at trial; credibility cannot be resolved against Nabozny now.

###### Intentionally unequal protection because of sexual orientation

**Holding and operative rule:** Intentional denial of ordinarily available school protection because of sexual orientation violates equal protection when the distinction has no rational relation to a legitimate objective. The orientation claim separately survives on the supported account without assignment of a heightened scrutiny tier.

**Controlling explanation:** Romer preserves ordinary rational-basis review, including conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit, while enforcing Moreno’s and Cleburne’s prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice. Walmer applies that inquiry in its particular military setting; its service-related rationale supplies no reason to deny this student ordinary protection against battery. Wright likewise permits reasonable operational predictions but supplies no school justification and expressly reserves discriminatory administration; its marriage threshold does not govern this equality claim. Bowers’s specified-conduct holding does not authorize private violence or discriminatory nonprotection. The alleged statements and enforcement departures support purposeful selection for disadvantage because Nabozny was openly gay, and no legitimate disciplinary objective explains that treatment on the favorable account. Knowledge, deliberate indifference without discriminatory purpose and inconsistent enforcement alone do not suffice. Virginia establishes no sexual-orientation tier. Neither Curtis nor Brown school excuses intentional discrimination merely because it occurs in an educational setting.

###### Conduct-time equality law defeats individual immunity on these facts

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual officials receive no qualified immunity when the supported facts show purposeful, irrational withholding of an ordinary protective policy whose unlawfulness was apparent from the relevant law when they acted; a factually identical harassment case is unnecessary. The clearly established sex-equality ground independently defeats immunity, and the narrow irrational-discrimination ground also supports denial on the orientation claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson require objectively apparent unlawfulness at the particularized level of the conduct, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant conduct-time authority. Before the conduct began in 1988, Reed, Weinberger and Hogan prohibited arbitrary sex distinctions; Yick Wo, Feeney and Arlington Heights supplied the purposeful-administration rule; Moreno and Cleburne rejected bare harm and irrational prejudice. Bohen provided relevant circuit notice concerning intentional sex discrimination, although its setting was employment. This is alleged deliberate discrimination in an existing protection, not notice of a novel affirmative-danger duty. Pinder’s contrary immunity result concerned that distinct due-process uncertainty as of its conduct date. DeShaney’s later equality reservation can confirm the distinction only for conduct after its date and is unnecessary to pre-1989 notice. Romer, Virginia and later decisions furnish no retroactive notice. The school’s policy is evidence of practice and knowledge, not an independent federal cause or automatic immunity defeat. Each individual’s actual participation, purpose and causation remain disputed; denial of legal immunity on assumed facts does not decide liability.

###### The preserved due-process theories fail on their own actor-specific record

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that sufficiently culpable official creation or increase of danger, or an attributable policy affirmatively encouraging private injury, can establish a constitutional deprivation, the preserved failure-to-punish and harmful-policy theories do not establish that contribution here. Their dismissal therefore stands without a categorical rule against all affirmative-danger claims or a due-process immunity holding.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a general governmental duty to protect from private violence. Pinder separates omission of public protection from affirmative deprivation of available protection; Uhlrig leaves the merits of the latter theory open. Lack of custody alone cannot defeat that distinct claim, and no assurance/reliance chain is made indispensable.

The Court examines the favorable account beyond mere silence. Promises, stereotyped responses, counseling for leaving after the mock assault and return to the ordinary schedule support the equality claim but do not establish a separate act directing a new assault or depriving available protection. Davis and Blauert referred Nabozny for a protective schedule change; the later special-education placement alongside assailants does not identify a remaining defendant’s deliberate decision or establish its increased-danger contribution to a particular later injury. Social Services’ return order is not attributed to these defendants or a District policy. Crawford’s discouragement of charges followed the completed beating, cannot have caused it, and establishes no later injury or attributable District policy. He is not a remaining defendant. Repeated nonenforcement does not become affirmative injury merely by being called a policy. The Court neither adjudicates a new Crawford claim nor rules out liability for proved obstruction, culpable placement or other materially different affirmative conduct.

The Court decides no sexual-orientation scrutiny tier, general school-safety entitlement, complete affirmative-danger test, municipal policy, actual liability or measure of damages.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Yick Wo v. Hopkins: applied to purposeful unequal administration; absence of a general protective duty does not remove equality constraints.
- Personnel Administrator v. Feeney: applied for discriminatory purpose rather than adverse effect alone.
- Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp.: applied to evidence of purpose through statements, departures and context.
- Reed v. Reed: supplies conduct-time notice against arbitrary sex distinctions.
- Weinberger v. Wiesenfeld: supplies conduct-time notice against sex stereotypes in allocating governmental benefits.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan: supplies the sex-classification justification rule and conduct-time notice.
- United States v. Virginia: applies demanding intermediate review to sex discrimination without assigning an orientation tier or furnishing retroactive notice.
- Romer v. Evans: applies the ordinary-rationality floor against unexplained class disadvantage; heightened scrutiny remains unresolved.
- Department of Agriculture v. Moreno: applied against bare harm as a governmental objective, including in conduct-time notice.
- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center: applied against irrational prejudice, including in conduct-time notice.
- Walmer v. Department of Defense: its military rationale is distinguished; its ordinary-review limits remain intact.
- Bowers v. Hardwick: its specified-conduct holding is retained and supplies no authority for discriminatory denial of protection.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: the received qualified-immunity defense remains governing.
- Anderson v. Creighton: particularized apparent unlawfulness is applied without requiring a factual twin.
- Elder v. Holloway: all relevant contemporaneous authority must be considered; later cases do not supply notice retroactively.
- Pinder v. Johnson: its omission/affirmative-deprivation distinction is applied and its conduct-time immunity result distinguished; its reserved danger merits remain reserved.
- Uhlrig v. Harder: personal attribution and conduct-time limits are preserved; its immunity decision supplies no complete danger test.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services: its no-general-duty rule is applied without erasing equal protection or all affirmative-deprivation claims.
- Johnson v. Jones: favorable-facts discipline is preserved; the interlocutory factual-sufficiency restriction does not bar this final-judgment review.
- Behrens v. Pelletier: legal immunity and factual disputes remain distinct; no collateral-appeal restriction is imported here.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services: municipal policy, custom or final-policymaker attribution and causation remain necessary.
- Owen v. City of Independence: the District receives no qualified immunity.
- Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth: its optional-program holding does not decide intentional discriminatory protection.
- Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.: its actual presentation and Title IX holdings create no general school immunity.
- Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.: limited discretion concerning included abandoned arguments does not compel revival of the disavowed special-relationship argument or supply facts.
- Wright v. MetroHealth Medical Center: its actual operational rationality holding preserves the bare-harm limit; its marriage threshold and flight-team facts do not decide intentional school discrimination or furnish conduct-time notice.

#### Law After Decision

Schools remain free to make legitimate disciplinary judgments, but the protection they provide may not be intentionally withheld on the prohibited sex or irrational orientation grounds established here. The two equality claims and the officials’ conduct-time notice are distinct sufficient paths; orientation scrutiny is unchanged. The decision establishes no constitutional insurance against peer violence, no categorical school immunity and no general affirmative-danger framework. Individual liability and municipal attribution retain their separate proof requirements.

### 1997-03-10 — Nabozny v. Podlesny; Seventh Circuit No. 95-3634, 92 F.3d 446; no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Nabozny_v_Podlesny_merits_1997-03-10.md).

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs, joining the Court’s opinion and judgment. He would additionally assess affirmative danger by requiring an official’s personal material increase of a specific foreseeable danger, or knowing obstruction of available protection, with conscious disregard of a known serious risk and the required causal connection. Assurances and reliance may be evidence but are not indispensable elements. The actual placement, return and post-beating discouragement account establishes no surviving claim under that proposed analysis; he leaves genuinely proved concealment, direction into danger or obstruction of a concrete protective route open. Pinder and Uhlrig did not establish his proposed test.

He also regards the assumed discrimination as outside his narrower personal-defense position: defendants identify no binding precedent or enacted authority directly permitting deliberate discriminatory withholding of ordinary protection. Absence of a factual twin is not affirmative legal permission. Because the received Harlow defense also fails on the same supported facts, he does not propose replacing it in this case. Trial must determine actual intent, acts, causation and municipal responsibility, without automatic liability or an added factual-reasonableness defense. These additional propositions have no other Justice’s join.
