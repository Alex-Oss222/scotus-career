# Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.; First Circuit No. 95-1275, 68 F.3d 525; no separate Supreme Court merits docket assigned; OT1996, chunk 4.

Authority: [Public decision](../records/Brown_v_Hot_Sexy_Safer_Productions_merits_1997-02-24.md).

## Event

Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc., 68 F.3d 525. Argued November 6, 1996; decided February 24, 1997. On review of the First Circuit's October 23, 1995 judgment affirming dismissal of the federal claims. The granted questions concern parents' substantive educational liberty, students' privacy and bodily integrity, and Title IX. Two fifteen-year-old students allege that they were required to attend a ninety-minute AIDS-awareness presentation on April 8, 1992, featuring graphic language, simulated sexual conduct and suggestive demonstrations involving other selected students. They also allege weeks of coarse imitation afterward. The case is decided on the complaint and favorable inferences, without trial findings.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms dismissal on the three granted federal questions. Justice Ginsburg delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court on those questions. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also concurs separately.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join the three controlling grounds concerning parental liberty, the pleaded student privacy and bodily-integrity deprivation, and Title IX's because-of-sex requirement. No Justice dissents from the judgment.

The remaining dismissals are undisturbed. The Court makes no new determination of the distinct procedural-due-process, constitutional Free Exercise or RFRA merits, the independently dismissed request for prospective declaratory relief, or the state claims. The Chief Justice states additional constitutional grounds in his concurrence. He agrees that the separate RFRA and prospective-declaratory dismissals remain undisturbed and adds no ground concerning either. No discovery, trial, amendment, damages, program redesign or remand is ordered, and no blanket immunity for the defendants is declared.

## Holdings

### The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Parental liberty protects choice among lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not give each parent authority to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. Accepting the alleged compulsory exposure and aftermath, this complaint establishes no displacement of family custody, educational choice or parental teaching that supports the requested substantive federal veto; the ruling does not approve pretextual curricular action or decide independent state-law permission rights.

**Authority:** Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined on each of the three granted holdings by every other Justice, giving each proposition nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Meyer and Pierce protect parental direction of education and upbringing. They do not make the public school a forum in which every parent may prescribe the particular lessons a child receives. The parents remain free to choose lawful educational alternatives and to instruct their children concerning the ideas encountered at school. Public-school enrollment does not waive that continuing role.

The Court acknowledges the difference from Curtis: attendance here was required, while Curtis concerned a separable optional resource and expressly reserved compulsory instruction. Its result cannot automatically decide this case. The particular compulsion alleged here is exposure to a presentation, not displacement of parental custody or instruction, compelled profession or personal performance by these plaintiffs. Those limits support the present conclusion without treating attendance as voluntary.

Settle requires a genuine pedagogical basis; calling an event curriculum is insufficient. The Court neither endorses the alleged crude conduct as sound teaching nor holds that any presentation bearing an educational label is constitutional. It decides that these allegations do not establish the particular parental deprivation asserted. The written-permission policy may impose an independent obligation, but its alleged breach does not itself create the substantive federal right the parents seek. State-law remedies and materially different claims remain outside this holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Meyer and Pierce are applied at the scope of the parental interest they protect. Curtis's reservation of compulsory instruction is preserved and the present allegations are examined independently. Settle's genuine-pedagogy requirement remains intact.

### These allegations establish neither the claimed student privacy deprivation nor extreme official abuse

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory exposure to the alleged presentation and subsequent coarse imitation does not, on this complaint, establish the students' asserted decisional-privacy or bodily-integrity deprivation or extreme abuse of official power. The conclusion rests on the actual allegations, including the absence of compelled personal performance, affirmation, intimate disclosure, physical contact or treatment; it is no categorical rule that words, a single event or compulsory educational programming can never violate the Constitution.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this holding in the Court's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** A constitutional privacy or bodily-integrity claim requires an invasion of the protected interest, not simply exposure to ideas or language the listener finds offensive. These students were required to attend. They were not alleged to have been selected for a skit, required to affirm the message, disclose intimate information, engage in sexual or religious conduct, or undergo bodily intrusion. The Court considers that account together with the alleged weeks of coarse imitation; it does not discard the aftermath or convert unfavorable facts into trial findings.

The parents' strongest objection is that the school's compulsory authority gave the presentation an intrusive character beyond ordinary offensive speech. That circumstance matters, but it does not establish the particular personal deprivation pleaded. The alleged program, taken as a whole and with favorable inferences, also does not establish the extreme official abuse asserted here. The Court announces no universal rule that verbal conduct cannot do so and does not decide the rights of a student personally subjected to a materially different act.

Curtis supplies no categorical answer because it reserved compulsory instruction. Settle likewise does not license religious exclusion, pretextual pedagogy or arbitrary conduct merely because it occurs in school. The present holding leaves protections against compelled profession or participation, bodily intrusion, religious establishment or targeting, and independently established discrimination intact. It does not transform authority to restrict certain school speech into an individual federal entitlement to censorship of every offensive presentation.

**Precedent treatment:** Curtis's limits and Settle's genuine-operation inquiry are preserved. Neither decision supplies general school immunity or resolves materially different personal coercion or invasion claims.

### The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex

**Holding and operative rule:** Title IX requires discrimination, exclusion or a hostile educational condition imposed because of sex; sexual subject matter and offense alone do not establish that relationship. The complaint connects the alleged later hostility to disagreement with the presentation's message rather than the plaintiffs' sex, so this element's absence requires dismissal without deciding severity or pervasiveness, a comprehensive recipient-liability rule, or the treatment of materially different sex-based targeting.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this sufficient statutory ground. No severity, attribution or constitutional speech-defense holding supplies an alternative basis.

**Controlling explanation:** The presentation's sexual content and alleged offensiveness are accepted. So are the subsequent weeks of coarse imitation. Neither fact, alone or in their stated context, supplies the missing connection between the treatment of these plaintiffs and their sex. They allege disagreement with the program's message and ensuing hostility on that basis. The program's delivery to an audience containing both sexes is contextual evidence, not a categorical defense to sex discrimination.

Harris requires a contextual inquiry and rejects psychological injury as a prerequisite; DeAngelis likewise rejects mandatory deterioration in performance, physical advances or similar added thresholds. Those employment decisions inform the analysis by analogy, but the Court does not transplant a complete Title VII attribution formula into Title IX. Personal targeting, touching, measurable academic loss and psychological injury are not indispensable elements announced here. Nor does the Court hold that one episode can never suffice. It does not decide whether this entire program and aftermath meet a fully elaborated Title IX severity-or-pervasiveness standard.

Franklin recognizes compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination. Availability of that remedy cannot supply the statutory violation absent from this complaint. The decision leaves questions involving sex-specific humiliation, school-authorized sexual targeting and deliberate indifference to known sex-based harassment for cases that present them, without prescribing their complete liability rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris and DeAngelis retain their contextual protections and operate here only by analogy. Franklin's compensation rule remains available for a proved intentional violation; it does not dispense with the because-of-sex requirement.

## Precedent Treatment

- Meyer v. Nebraska and Pierce v. Society of Sisters protect parental educational choice and instruction; they do not supply the lesson-by-lesson veto sought on these allegations.
- Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth retains its rule for the optional program there and its express reservation of compulsory instruction and genuine religious compulsion. Today's required attendance receives its own analysis.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board continues to require genuine pedagogical reasons and to forbid religious exclusion disguised as curricular administration; an educational label is insufficient.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems and DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association preserve contextual harassment analysis without mandatory psychological injury, physical advances or measurable performance loss. Their employment rules do not automatically determine Title IX attribution or its complete harassment standard.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools preserves compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination without supplying a missing statutory element or deciding the reserved recipient-liability questions here.

## Law After Decision

The three pleaded federal theories fail at their stated grounds. Compulsory attendance remains a material fact, but these allegations establish neither the asserted parental or personal constitutional deprivation nor discrimination because of sex. The Court adopts no general curricular immunity, categorical exemption for a single offensive episode, added injury prerequisite or comprehensive Title IX recipient rule. The separate ungranted constitutional and statutory dismissals and the state-law dispute receive no new controlling merits determination.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the three granted holdings and would also reject the procedural-due-process and constitutional Free Exercise theories. Assuming the written positive-subscription policy created a state-law interest, the plaintiffs failed to preserve a developed argument that Massachusetts postdeprivation remedies were inadequate. A general assertion that the exposure cannot be undone does not preserve that distinct challenge. He therefore does not decide whether the planned departure from the policy was random and unauthorized under Parratt and Hudson, or instead implicated Zinermon's concern with foreseeable deprivation through delegated authority. Nor does he find that a remedy compensates every possible harm. The local policy's written parental-permission requirement and the statute's separate religious opt-out from instruction on disease retain their own terms; possible state-law relief is not adjudicated.

On constitutional religion, he distinguishes compulsory listening from compelled profession or religiously forbidden conduct. These plaintiffs were not required to perform, affirm, disclose private information or submit to bodily intrusion, and no religious exercise was prohibited, penalized or conditioned on abandoning belief. Applying Smith's neutral, generally applicable rule in that setting, he finds no constitutional Free Exercise violation. Yoder's protected religious family direction and Lukumi's prohibition on religious targeting remain distinct; neither offense at an idea nor curricular labeling alone decides whether religion has been burdened. His position does not authorize establishment, targeting, pretextual curriculum or coercive participation, and does not treat public-school attendance as a waiver of parental instruction.

He reaches no RFRA temporal or substantive rule and supplies no new ground concerning the independently dismissed request for prospective declaratory relief. Because he identifies no constitutional violation on his analysis, he does not decide qualified immunity. His additional procedure and constitutional religion grounds have no other Justice's join and do not enlarge the Court's three holdings.

## Procedure After Action

The federal dismissal stands. There is no remand or order for discovery, trial, amendment, damages, a revised school program or a further filing. The remaining lower dispositions are undisturbed. Any independently available remedy under state law or the School Committee's permission policy lies outside the granted federal questions and receives no new adjudication.
