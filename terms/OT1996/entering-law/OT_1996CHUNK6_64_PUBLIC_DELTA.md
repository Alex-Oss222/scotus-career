## Public Projection

## Event

**Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission**, No. 95-992. Merits decision, March 31, 1997. Argued October 7, 1996. On direct appeal from the three-judge District Court for the District of Columbia, 910 F. Supp. 734, after the 1994 remand. The granted question asks whether the developed record establishes advancement and fit under the intermediate scrutiny prescribed for the general full-power must-carry core.

**Statutory setting relevant to fit:** Under 47 U.S.C.§534, systems with 12 or fewer usable activated channels must carry at least three local commercial signals; systems also having 300 or fewer subscribers are exempt from that section only while they delete no broadcast signal. Systems with more than 12 channels carry eligible local commercial signals up to one-third of usable activated capacity. This is not a combined commercial-and-educational ceiling. Substantially duplicative commercial signals already represented in carriage and additional same-network affiliates need not be carried; voluntary duplicates and multiple affiliates count toward the obligation. When eligible stations exceed the required number, the operator selects among them, but cannot substitute low power for full power and must choose the elected network's affiliate with the closest city-of-license reference point to the principal headend.

Section535 adds separate educational duties. Systems with 12 or fewer channels carry one qualified local educational signal and may carry more. Systems with 13–36 carry at least one and need not carry more than three, subject to continued carriage; they may carry more. If no qualified local station is present, the former category must import one qualified educational signal and the latter at least one. For that importation, the smallest systems need not remove programming actually provided on March 29, 1990 but must use the first available channel. All qualified local educational stations carried on that date retain carriage unless operator and station give written waiver consent. Systems above36 channels, including those expanding beyond36, carry requesting qualified local educational stations; a system required to carry three need not add stations substantially duplicating another qualified local educational station requesting carriage. This is not a general three-station cap. A13–36-channel system already carrying a State-network educational affiliate need not add another same-network affiliate whose programming substantially duplicates the carried affiliate.

Commercial distant signals require indemnification of increased copyright liability. A commercial station below the specified headend levels (−45dBm UHF; −49dBm VHF) is excluded if it does not agree to bear the costs of delivering a good-quality or baseband signal. Educational carriage is not required without delivery of a good-quality or baseband signal; its distant-signal indemnity exception concerns adding a signal and expressly does not erase the continued-carriage rule. These are actual programming obligations, including primary video, audio and captions. Commercial carriage includes technically feasible program-related blanking-interval or subcarrier material; educational carriage includes technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education or language purposes. Other ancillary material remains discretionary. Positioning and availability duties also constrain operation; the law does not merely reserve empty channels. No new dispute over ancillary material, positioning, payment, notice or enforcement is decided.

The reserved low-power duty depends on an insufficient number of full-power local commercial stations: systems with35 or fewer channels carry one qualified low-power station, and larger systems two. Its disputed programming criterion concerns local needs inadequately served because of full-power stations' geographic distance. The separate market-modification factor asks whether another eligible station supplies the community news, sports or events; it is not that requirement imposed on the requesting station. These provisions' independent classifications remain outside the granted question.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment on the reviewed general full-power core and does not reach the separate low-power or market-modification classification questions.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirm within the reviewed full-power scope, 5–4. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg.

The reviewed carriage duties remain in force. The dissenters would reverse their constitutional approval and remand for declaratory and prospective relief limited to the invalid duty. All nine decline the separate low-power and market-modification classification questions; neither is adjudicated valid, invalid or severable here. Properly supported system-specific challenges remain available. No new quota, Act-wide injunction, damages, takings disposition or religious-claim disposition issues.

## Holdings

### I. The granted question does not decide the reserved classifications

**Rule and authority:** Review of whether the general full-power scheme satisfies its prescribed intermediate standard does not adjudicate the separate content classification of low-power eligibility under §534(h)(2)(B) or the market-modification factor under §534(h)(1)(C)(ii)(III). All nine join Part I and decline to expand this expressly limited review.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner I classified the full-power core but reserved those different provisions. A former remand does not permanently enlarge a subsequent limited appellate grant. Time Warner actually presses both challenges; the Government's identified preservation objection concerns low power. Merits briefing alone does not change the granted question, and neither classification is a necessary subsidiary step in reviewing the core's record support and fit. The 1995 Rules 18.3, 14.1(a) and 24.1(a) preserve that question boundary and permit, rather than require, consideration of otherwise cognizable plain error. The Court declines that discretionary expansion given the distinct operations, injury and relief questions. It adopts no categorical standing bar, abandonment finding or judgment that either criterion is neutral. The district court's grouping of a market factor with low power does not merge them, and viewpoint neutrality or benevolent motive alone does not answer content classification. Their constitutional validity remains outside this judgment.

### II. The developed record supports preservation of independent broadcast sources

**Rule and authority:** The general full-power carriage duties materially advance the substantial interests in preserving free local broadcasting and a multiplicity of independently available information sources on this developed record. Part II controls through Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer under the existing intermediate standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner I recognizes ordinary cable editorial and programmer speech protection and permits respect for supported legislative predictions while requiring independent judicial scrutiny. The developed evidence links cable bottleneck control, incentives to favor affiliated programming and advertising, drops or refusals and adverse repositioning to diminished broadcast audience, revenue and continued service. Those circumstances support preventive protection of significant local sources without proof that the entire industry will collapse or that every carried station individually faces failure. Contrary growth and profit evidence matters but does not negate every supported local threat. The sufficient justification is preservation and diversity, not an independently sufficient ruling that the Act is tailored against economic predation. Breyer shares that common rationale directly: bottleneck choices can restrict audience options without predatory intent. Congressional prediction is not conclusive, and a desirable broadcasting policy alone would not suffice.

### III. The present burden and fit satisfy intermediate review

**Rule and authority:** On this developed facial record, the reviewed duties do not burden substantially more cable editorial discretion than necessary to advance those interests; intermediate review requires adequate fit, not the least restrictive conceivable arrangement. Part III has the same five direct joins and permits final affirmance without a further trial here.

**Controlling explanation:** O'Brien and Ward supply the intermediate-fit inquiry. The capacity categories, commercial nonduplication provisions and separate noncommercial rules described above confine the legal obligation; existing voluntary carriage counts without extinguishing editorial freedom to choose differently. The evidence that many systems added no station and that additions occupied 1.2 percent of all channels bears on incremental burden. That statistic does not measure every legally reserved channel, displaced program or foregone choice. Expanding capacity and the remaining editorial space also matter, alongside the burdens on operators, cable programmers and viewers. The district court considered regulated leased access, A/B reception switches, narrower former FCC-type rules and subsidies, and the developed record supports its conclusion that these alternatives would not protect the accepted interests as effectively. Alternatives are relevant without becoming a least-restrictive-means command. The predictive sufficiency question can be decided on this record despite conflicting forecasts; no general exemption of legislative facts from summary-judgment rules follows. The ruling neither preapproves a more burdensome future law nor forecloses a supported as-applied displacement challenge.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC (1994):** Applied the majority full-power classification, intermediate standard and limited predictive-deference principle; did not convert its four-Justice former insufficiency explanation into a holding or decide the reserved classifications.
- **United States v. O'Brien:** Applied the requirement of a substantial interest unrelated to speech suppression and a sufficiently fitted burden.
- **Ward v. Rock Against Racism:** Applied adequate tailoring without demanding the least restrictive imaginable alternative; concrete alternatives remain relevant evidence of fit.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium v. FCC:** Preserved ordinary cable speech protection, separate statutory components and independent justification/means review. Its contextual rationales and separate strict-content position supply no universal replacement standard here.
- **Broadcast spectrum-scarcity doctrine:** Not extended to cable or used to reduce review to rational-basis scrutiny.

## Law After Decision

The developed record sustains the general full-power core under existing content-neutral intermediate scrutiny through a directly shared preservation/diversity rationale. Congress retains room for supported preventive judgments; courts retain independent review of actual advancement, editorial burdens and fit. No independent economic-predation application, new general cable test, approval of the two reserved classifications or immunity from supported as-applied review is established.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, concurring.** Must carry burdens operators, cable programmers and cable viewers, but also preserves independently available speech for noncable viewers with fewer alternatives. His actual Denver approach weighs the relevant speech interests and fit in their concrete setting. Bottleneck control can reduce available sources without predatory conduct. He joins preservation/diversity, the proper added-channel measure, consideration of alternatives and the result; he does not adopt a freestanding economic-predation justification or treat deference as surrender of judicial review.

**Stevens, concurring.** Congress's substantial predictive evidence warrants respect without proof that every forecast will come true. His Turner I position accepted the limited deference principle without joining the complete insufficiency explanation. He regards the congressional evidence as sufficient without treating additional remand evidence as constitutionally indispensable, while joining the Court's fuller developed-record reasoning and fit conclusion. He adopts neither conclusiveness of legislative findings nor a universal broadcast-industry-collapse prerequisite.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, dissenting in part.** They would reverse even under the governing intermediate rule. Healthy industry trends do not disprove every local threat, but incentives and selected harms insufficiently justify this nationwide editorial preference, including stations not shown threatened and burdens on competing programmers. The added-channel average understates the whole legal restriction; narrower alternatives and unequal local burdens expose inadequate fit. They require neither certain harm, collapse of the entire industry nor the least restrictive imaginable rule. They also retain their Turner I objection to the general content classification without purporting to overrule it here. They join the express-grant scope ruling, preserving their separate content objections to low-power eligibility and market modification for proper review; benign purpose and viewpoint neutrality do not resolve those objections.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed full-power duties remain enforceable under the affirmed facial judgment. No additional trial or remedial order is required here. Properly supported as-applied challenges and the separately reserved classification questions require their own lawful presentation; this decision dictates no outcome for them.
