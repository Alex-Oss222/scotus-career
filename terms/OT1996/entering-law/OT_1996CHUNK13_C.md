#### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.

#### Hawaiian Airlines, Inc. v. Norris, 512 U.S. 246

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-2058, including the related Finazzo judgment under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The related Finazzo judgment is included under Rule 12.2; the airline and officers actions retain their distinct surviving counts.

##### Independent state rights remain enforceable without interpreting the labor agreement

**Holding:** The Railway Labor Act's exclusive machinery for minor disputes covers claims founded on a collective-bargaining agreement or requiring interpretation of its disputed terms; it does not displace independent state-law claims resolvable through factual questions about conduct or motive without that interpretation. Norris's public-policy and whistleblower theories meet that independence test on the presented preemption question, so both reviewed Hawaii judgments are affirmed, leaving the surviving claims, their ordinary elements and defenses, and any relief for state proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion for a unanimous Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the complete rule, application, precedent treatment, and bounded affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A statutory or nonnegotiable label does not by itself establish independence. Courts examine the claim's actual elements; a state claim using disputed contractual just cause as its substantive standard differs from the factual retaliatory-motive inquiry here. Overlapping facts, parallel remedies, unfinished grievance steps, or an asserted contract defense do not alone defeat an independent right. The separate federal dismissal of the CBA claim remains intact. State elements, defenses, ordinary limits against duplicate recovery, and genuine arbitration duties remain applicable. Affirmance establishes no unsafe-aircraft finding, wrongful discharge, damages amount, reinstatement, or new FAA cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lingle v. Norge Division of Magic Chef, Inc., 486 U.S. 399 (1988): extends its independent-right and necessary-interpretation distinction from the Labor Management Relations Act context to RLA preemption, without equating the two statutes in every respect.
- Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. Buell, 480 U.S. 557 (1987): applies its refusal to substitute RLA grievance machinery for an independent substantive protection; no new federal injury or whistleblower action follows.
- Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Railway Labor Executives' Assn., 491 U.S. 299 (1989): preserves its arguable-contractual-justification test within the major/minor distinction for contractual disputes; it does not answer the antecedent source-of-right question.
- Andrews v. Louisville & Nashville Railroad Co., 406 U.S. 320 (1972): distinguishes its agreement-founded entitlement while preserving compulsory adjustment for that category.
- Elgin, Joliet & Eastern Railway Co. v. Burley, 325 U.S. 711 (1945): disavows its broader suggestion that an omitted contractual case necessarily includes an independent state-law claim within minor disputes; that dictum does not control. Its contractual and representation holdings are not overruled.
- Livadas v. Bradshaw, June 13, 1994: applies the distinction between factual consultation and necessary interpretation, preserving genuinely contract-dependent claims; its separate bargaining-policy and §1983 holdings are not extended here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both reviewed Hawaii judgments are affirmed: the airline action's public-policy preemption disposition and the officers action's public-policy and whistleblower dispositions. Their existing remands remain effective for adjudication of surviving state claims, defenses and relief, subject to ordinary limits on duplicate recovery. The separate federal dismissal of the airline contract claim remains intact. No extra airline-count judgment, liability, damages, reinstatement or renewed grievance is ordered.

#### Livadas v. Bradshaw, 512 U.S. 107

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1920.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

For the other questions in Livadas v. Bradshaw, 1994-06-13, No. 92-1920, see Civil Rights — Section 1983 supplies a channel for this identified federal labor right.

##### An independent wage-penalty claim is not preempted by §301

**Holding:** Section 301 does not displace a nonnegotiable state wage-payment claim whose resolution requires no interpretation of disputed collective-bargaining terms. Consulting an undisputed contractual wage rate to calculate the possible statutory penalty supplies arithmetic, not the contract interpretation needed for §301 preemption; genuinely contract-dependent claims remain different.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this antecedent holding.

**Material application:** Section 201 requires earned unpaid wages immediately on discharge; §203 addresses willful delay, continuing wages at the same rate until payment or commencement of an action for no more than thirty days. Section 219 bars private agreements setting aside those protections but permits more frequent, greater or earlier payment. Section 229 permits wage-collection suits despite arbitration, with a separate exception for disputes over interpretation or application of a bargaining agreement containing arbitration. The broader administrative exclusion is the Commissioner's policy, not §229's command. Contract §18.8 permitted this direct claim through arbitration or an authorized tribunal or agency without contract interpretation. No penalty is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lingle v. Norge Division of Magic Chef, Inc., 486 U.S. 399: applies independence from contract interpretation; use of undisputed wage information does not change the claim's source.
- Allis-Chalmers Corp. v. Lueck, 471 U.S. 202: preserves preemption of genuinely contract-dependent claims; it does not preempt this arithmetic use.
- Wooddell v. International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, December 4, 1991: preserves the distinct §301 route for a beneficiary enforcing a qualifying interunion contract, not every union-related claim.

##### California may not penalize completion of protected collective bargaining

**Holding:** The NLRA forbids California's policy withholding otherwise available enforcement of an independent wage right solely because an employee is covered by a collective-bargaining agreement containing arbitration. The employee's protected freedom to bargain includes carrying bargaining through to such an agreement; this rule neither compels employer assent nor generally requires the State to create an enforcement service.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt this statutory labor-right holding.

**Material application:** The holding does not guarantee identical access through every enforcement route. Proceedings under §§98 and 98.1 differ from representation under §98.3(a), which depends on the Commissioner's assessment of financial inability to employ counsel and a valid, enforceable claim. The employee's own §218 suit and §218.5 fee provision remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nash v. Florida Industrial Commission, 389 U.S. 235: applies the prohibition on state benefit conditions burdening federally protected labor activity.
- Golden State Transit Corp. v. Los Angeles, 493 U.S. 103: applies protection of the federally secured bargaining process against governmental interference.
- Fort Halifax Packing Co. v. Coyne, 482 U.S. 1: preserves independent minimum labor standards and does not authorize this penalty for protected bargaining.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for source-authorized relief against the official-capacity policy. Monetary-relief and Eleventh Amendment constraints remain distinct. No employer wage penalty, personal damages or automatic reinstatement of a money award is ordered; willfulness, the effect of backpay and other penalty defenses remain unresolved.

#### First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-560.
**Decided:** May 22, 1995.

##### Antecedent assent and delegation

**Holding:** Courts independently determine agreement to arbitrate arbitrability unless ordinary applicable contract law establishes clear and unmistakable delegation. Presenting an objection while denying panel jurisdiction does not itself establish delegation; company submission does not itself establish an owner's personal assent. Where parties actually delegate arbitrability, limited award review governs that decision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer on the antecedent-assent rule and application; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer alone on the additional conditional actual-delegation review rule (Stone-Zsela reserves that proposition) directly join this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Broad clauses, incorporated rules and independent nonsignatory-binding theories are not resolved. The antecedent-assent rule has nine joins; the conditional limited-review rule for actual delegation has eight, excluding Stone-Zsela. No judgment-only or fractured-decision aggregation supplies either coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, Inc. v. Communications Workers, 475 U.S. 643: applies clear-and-unmistakable delegation.
- Volt Information Sciences, Inc. v. Board of Trustees, 489 U.S. 468: applies arbitration's contractual foundation.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: federal coverage supplies no missing assent.
- Mastrobuono v. Shearson Lehman Hutton, Inc., March 6, 1995: actual integrated contract terms preserve the antecedent-consent inquiry.

##### Ordinary appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently review district-court legal conclusions and review factual findings for clear error. Arbitration supplies no additional appellate deference to the district judge's independent arbitrability determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Technologies, 475 U.S. 643: preserves decisionmaker/merits distinctions.
- Volt, 489 U.S. 468: actual agreement explains arbitral deference; it insulates no district-court legal conclusion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Third Circuit on arbitrability and appellate review. Vacatur of the award against the Kaplans personally remains effective; MKI's separate corporate submission remains distinct. No new FAA merits-vacatur ground or rehearing of agreed arbitration merits is authorized.

#### Farrar v. Hobby, 506 U.S. 103 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-990.

**Decided:** December 14, 1992.

##### Enforceable nominal relief establishes eligibility

**Holding:** A civil-rights plaintiff who obtains an enforceable nominal-damages judgment against the defendant from whom fees are sought is a prevailing party under §1988. The judgment must directly benefit the plaintiff by changing the defendant’s legal obligation; its nominal amount does not defeat eligibility.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of White’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine Justices support this controlling proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 — applied; nominal relief vindicates the established procedural violation without presumed compensatory injury.
- Hewitt v. Helms, 482 U.S. 755 — distinguished; favorable language without enforceable relief differs from a payment judgment.
- Rhodes v. Stewart, 488 U.S. 1 — distinguished; ineffective declaratory relief differs from a judgment directly benefiting the estate.
- Texas State Teachers Assn. v. Garland Independent School District, 489 U.S. 782 — applied and clarified; magnitude affects amount, not eligibility where nominal relief is enforceable.

##### Reasonable amount requires reconsideration on this review

**Holding:** When the court of appeals denies fees solely because a nominal-damages plaintiff is ineligible, correction of that error does not itself determine a reasonable amount. On this record, the fee judgment is vacated and remanded for preserved reasonableness objections and an explained Hensley assessment of actual success, allowing reduction to zero without automatically reinstating the prior award or requiring proportionality to damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of White’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices control the remedy and stated fee principles.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424 — applied; actual success, unrelated unsuccessful work and a reasoned discretionary assessment govern amount.
- City of Riverside v. Rivera, 477 U.S. 561 — applied within its authority; no mechanical proportionality requirement, without guaranteed fees for nominal relief.
- Blanchard v. Bergeron, 489 U.S. 87 — applied; the court must determine a reasonable statutory fee in the circumstances.
- City of Burlington v. Dague, June 24, 1992 — distinguished; contingency enhancement under the two environmental statutes does not decide this eligibility or remand question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Eligibility does not guarantee a positive fee. Actual success, exclusion of unrelated unsuccessful work and an explained Hensley assessment govern; no damages-to-fees ratio or invented public benefit is permitted. The decision does not categorically forbid affirmance on a sufficient alternative fee ground in another case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the fee judgment and remand for preserved reasonableness objections and lower-court review as necessary. The prior $280,000 award is not automatically restored; the result may be a reduced award or zero.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

### Independent state rights and actual labor-contract interpretation

**Governing question:** When a collective-bargaining agreement displaces an otherwise independent state-law claim under §301 or the Railway Labor Act.

**Current rule:** A state claim enforcing an independent, nonnegotiable right is not preempted merely because the workplace has a collective-bargaining agreement, the agreement supplies an undisputed wage figure, the same facts support a contract grievance, or a defense refers to the agreement. ## Current-term public law, published positions and procedure

### 1997-01-06 — DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association; Fifth Circuit No. 93-8700, 51 F.3d 591; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

Authority: [Record](../records/DeAngelis_v_El_Paso_Association_merits_1997-01-06.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. The credited course of publication does not meet Title VII's objective threshold

**Holding and operative rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when the complete circumstances establish conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions, objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as abusive; no physical advance, superior-subordinate relationship, psychological injury or measurable work loss is indispensable. Crediting this verdict record and favorable reasonable inferences, the supported course of newsletter commentary falls below that objective threshold, so the Association's hostile-environment judgment is affirmed.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; six direct votes support this rule and application. Stone-Zsela's judgment vote supplies no additional statutory join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Harris's all-circumstances inquiry, which preserves an objective boundary while rejecting injury and productivity prerequisites. Meritor supplies the requirement that discriminatory harassment alter working conditions. The ten reproduced columns must be considered together, including general comments about women, the references associated with DeAngelis, extensive workplace circulation, humiliation and alleged interference with command. General comments are not excluded merely because they do not name her.

The jury's favorable inferences receive their proper force. Even on that account, the irregular sequence, degree, limited direct targeting, editorial setting and actual workplace responses establish offensive intermittent commentary rather than the required abusive alteration. Broad circulation and repeated ridicule matter, but do not eliminate that statutory distinction. Good ratings, upheld discipline and supportive responses are contextual evidence, not indispensable defenses. Neither an invitation to respond nor the membership's protection of the author's anonymity creates consent or a numerical safe harbor.

The lower opinion's suggestions that equal opportunity must be destroyed or work performance palpably deteriorate are disapproved. Citation to Harris alone would not cure those suggestions. The Court instead independently applies the correct standard and sustains the judgment on that ground. It neither disbelieves DeAngelis's testimony nor requires physical threats, advances or supervisory harassment. Because the statutory ground is sufficient, the Court does not decide the preserved First Amendment defense. It adopts no newsletter immunity and no general rule that offensive expression cannot constitute harassment.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris is applied without adding a prerequisite; Meritor's alteration-of-conditions boundary remains controlling. No constitutional speech precedent is extended by this holding.

#### II. The complaint-related publications establish no actionable retaliatory consequence here

**Holding and operative rule:** Protected activity and hostile commentary do not alone establish Title VII retaliation without a cognizable adverse consequence. The mocking EEOC reference, routine minutes reporting the complaint and announced intention to sue if the action proved groundless establish no denial of an employment or Association benefit, concrete imposed penalty or comparably supported material disadvantage on this record; judgment for the Association is therefore affirmed.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court distinguishes opposition to a discrimination complaint from a proved retaliatory consequence. The publications reveal disparagement and contemplated conditional litigation, but do not establish lost pay, promotion, assignment, authority, membership, representation or a comparable imposed disadvantage caused by the complaint. No actual retaliatory lawsuit or unreported sanction is found. The same record can raise distinct hostile-environment and retaliation questions; disagreement on the former does not supply the missing consequence for the latter.

This record-specific conclusion does not confine all actionable retaliation to ultimate employment decisions. It does not hold that threats, harassment or public shaming can never establish a materially adverse consequence on stronger facts, and it decides no comprehensive standard for every retaliation claim. The Court does not infer failure from missing research materials or convert the opportunity to answer the newsletter into a condition of protection.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's distinction between offensive treatment and legally sufficient discrimination is preserved at its hostile-environment scope; its injury rule is not converted into a comprehensive retaliation test.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harris v. Forklift Systems: applies the objective and subjective totality inquiry and rejects mandatory work-loss, injury, advance and hierarchy requirements; the present application adds no such requirement.
- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson: applies the requirement of discriminatory alteration of employment conditions; extreme examples remain illustrations.
- Wisconsin v. Mitchell, Hurley and R. A. V.: their existing holdings remain intact. The Court does not reach the expressive defense, adopt R. A. V.'s separate content-selection rationale, or decide a general immunity for association publications.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 6, 1997, the particular newsletter record is insufficient under Harris even with testimony and reasonable verdict-favorable inferences credited. Title VII retains its contextual severe-or-pervasive test, with no numerical publication threshold or mandatory tangible injury. Retaliation fails here for lack of a supported material consequence; the decision leaves broader retaliation categories open. No controlling First Amendment rule is added. Stone-Zsela's expressive ground and Stevens and Ginsburg's proposed hostile-environment remand remain noncontrolling.

### 1997-01-06 — DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association; Fifth Circuit No. 93-8700, 51 F.3d 591; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment; no joins.** He joins the retaliation holding but rejects statutory insufficiency on hostile environment. The repeated sex-derogatory message, distribution to more than seven hundred colleagues, connections to DeAngelis, recurring comments and workplace incidents, humiliation, claimed loss of authority, promotion deterrence and refusal to identify the columnist permit a rational jury's severe-or-pervasive finding under Harris. Physical threats, supervisory power, reduced performance and the written medium are not statutory barriers. He therefore reaches the preserved expressive defense.

In his view, this award punishes protected editorial opinion or satire solely for its sex-derogatory viewpoint. Such civil liability is content based and is not narrowly tailored here to the compelling interest in eradicating sex discrimination. The publication itself is the independently asserted wrong; the record supplies no separate discriminatory employment act, true threat, authority-backed directive or denial of employment or associational rights to which the expression is integral or evidentiary. Hurley preserves the expressive inquiry, and Mitchell distinguishes punishment of protected belief from use of expression to establish an independently unlawful act. He would prohibit Title VII damages resting solely on such protected opinion or satire, while preserving liability for discriminatory decisions, quid pro quo demands, true threats, authority-backed directives and expression integral to or used as evidence of independently unlawful employment conduct. His rule is confined to this expressive application and does not declare that speech cannot itself be workplace harassment. That constitutional ground alone supports his hostile-environment affirmance. On retaliation, he finds no material employment or membership disadvantage caused by the complaint and leaves stronger threats, harassment and public-shaming cases open.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** They join Part II but would reverse the hostile-environment portion and remand. Harris protects disadvantageous conditions without proof of lost output. The lower opinion's reliance on destroyed opportunity, palpable performance deterioration, missing advances and absence of a superior harasser materially narrowed its review of the jury verdict. Its correct abstract recitation of Harris and the substantial contrary contextual evidence do not cure that error. Repeated sex-based attacks, broad circulation, mocking, humiliation and alleged impairment of command warrant review without those added thresholds. Ginsburg's Harris explanation makes professional disadvantage relevant without measurable productivity loss. They find a review error, not liability as a matter of law. Mitchell and Hurley keep the preserved expressive defense distinct; it and any remaining properly preserved grounds must be resolved before liability could stand. They would restore neither damages nor a particular allocation and reject both automatic newsletter immunity and automatic loss of protection for an offensive editorial.

### 1997-02-26 — Andrade v. Mayfair Management, Inc.; Fourth Circuit No. 95-2476; 88 F.3d 258.

Authority: [Record](../records/Andrade_v_Mayfair_Management_Inc_merits_1997-02-26.md).

#### Holdings

All holdings below are in Justice Breyer's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices. Each is the holding of the Court, not an aggregation of separate reasons.

##### Employer responsibility through delegated authority

**Operative rule.** An employer may be responsible under Title VII for a supervisor-created hostile environment when actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the creation or maintenance of that environment, even without actual or constructive notice to higher management. A supervisory title or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; the employee must establish the connection between delegated authority and the misconduct, while negligent failure to correct known or reasonably knowable harassment remains a distinct route to liability.

**Controlling explanation.** Meritor directs courts to agency principles and rejects both automatic imputation of every supervisor's misconduct and a universal defense based on lack of notice. The statutory employer-agent relationship gives legal significance to authority the employer actually entrusted to the wrongdoer. Hiring, firing, discipline and threatened employment action may establish the necessary connection. An express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; daily control of working conditions can also aid unlawful conduct. The employer's argument that personal misconduct necessarily falls outside its responsibility would eliminate the agency inquiry Meritor preserves. Conversely, the employee cannot establish attribution simply by pointing to the administrator's rank. Harris and DeAngelis define an actionable hostile environment without supplying this separate attribution predicate. The administrator's control and hiring and firing authority therefore require examination, even though he reported to a corporate officer. The Court does not adopt a general liability rule for every supervisor's private act or a new affirmative defense.

##### Corrected review of the employer's judgment

**Operative rule.** A Rule 50 judgment resting on the legal premise that higher management's lack of notice necessarily defeats supervisor-agency attribution must be reconsidered under the proper agency standard, with the evidence and permissible inferences viewed in the verdict winner's favor. Vacatur does not itself establish attribution, restore the verdict or require a new trial; preserved alternative grounds, evidentiary sufficiency and genuine instruction or preservation questions remain for the lower court.

**Controlling explanation.** The jury's $25,000 award does not answer whether the correct attribution question was submitted or whether the record legally supports the verdict. The lower courts foreclosed the agency route because the administrator was not a proprietor, partner or corporate officer and higher management lacked notice. That categorical barrier cannot stand under Meritor. The proper response is to return the preserved attribution issue for review under the corrected rule, not to treat every allegation as a finding or assume that no other preserved ground can matter. Constructive discharge was separately rejected and lies outside the granted questions; this remand does not reopen it.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied to require an actual agency inquiry; neither supervisor status nor absence of higher-management notice supplies a categorical answer.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Preserved as the objective-and-subjective hostile-environment standard without mandatory psychological injury; it does not establish employer attribution.
- **DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers Association:** Preserved for the severity-or-pervasiveness inquiry and its context; proof of an unlawful environment does not dispense with the separate attribution requirement.

No precedent is overruled. The Court resolves neither constructive discharge nor a general affirmative-defense regime.

#### Law After Decision

Title VII retains distinct routes through agency attribution and employer negligence. Courts must examine whether delegated authority actually enabled the supervisor's harassment; higher management's lack of notice alone cannot foreclose that route. The hostile-environment threshold remains separate. The legal rule changes the analysis of the employer's Rule 50 judgment, while leaving factual sufficiency, preserved alternative grounds and the appropriate next proceeding to the lower court.

### 1997-03-19 — Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.; court-of-appeals No. 95-30510.

Authority: [Record](../records/Oncale_v_Sundowner_Offshore_Services_Inc_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Same-sex harassment remains subject to Title VII

**Rule:** Title VII does not exclude discriminatory harassment merely because the offender and employee share a sex. The claimant must plead and ultimately prove that the challenged employment conditions were imposed because of sex and must satisfy the remaining requirements of the asserted claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor establishes that abusive working conditions can constitute statutory discrimination. The enacted protection concerns the individual employee's treatment, not an unstated requirement that employee and offender belong to different sex categories. A categorical exclusion therefore cannot support this dismissal. Rejecting that exclusion does not prove any allegation, create liability for all objectionable conduct, or adopt a free-standing dignity cause of action. The independent statutory-employer ruling outside the grant remains untouched.

##### Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary element

**Rule:** Discrimination because of sex may be established through competent direct or circumstantial evidence without proving sexual desire, a particular sexual orientation, or the presence of an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish discriminatory causation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hicks preserves the claimant's ultimate persuasion burden without making one evidentiary route compulsory. Sex-directed hostility, selective conduct, discriminatory statements, threats and the circumstances can support a causal inference; this illustrative list is neither exclusive nor a checklist. Desire can be relevant, but making it indispensable would exclude sex-based mistreatment for reasons the statute does not recognize. The trier must evaluate the actual inference and competing explanations. Ordinary interpersonal hostility unconnected to sex remains outside this cause, and no orientation identity is found here.

##### Apply Harris in the actual workplace context

**Rule:** A hostile-environment claim requires sex-based conduct sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively hostile or abusive environment in context, which the claimant actually perceived as abusive. An all-male workforce supplies context, not categorical immunity; neither psychiatric injury nor measurable productivity loss is indispensable.

**Controlling explanation:** Harris requires an objective and subjective inquiry into the circumstances and supplies the severe-or-pervasive threshold. Context helps distinguish actionable abuse from ordinary offensive banter; it cannot excuse forced physical humiliation or rape threats simply by labeling them rough camaraderie. The alleged conduct may satisfy the threshold, but allegations are not findings and gravity does not substitute for unwelcome treatment, discriminatory causation or employer attribution. No single factor, diagnosis, treatment, universal reasonable-woman standard or medical proof is made compulsory.

##### Attribution, other claims and relief remain distinct on remand

**Rule:** If employer attribution is reached, Andrade requires examination whether actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided the hostile environment; job title alone is insufficient, and absence of higher-management notice alone does not defeat that agency route. Negligence and any preserved quid-pro-quo claim remain distinct inquiries, and this reversal decides neither their factual predicates nor relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Andrade supplies the governing agency rule, including that employment action or economic loss is not invariably necessary for its authority-aided-environment route. A quid-pro-quo theory separately requires the alleged demand, use of employment power and employment-condition consequence to be identified; coverage does not collapse the two theories. The lower courts must apply the correct rules to the affected claims and any properly presented independent defenses in ordinary course. The Court does not reinstate an unchallenged coworker-employer theory, decide Lyons's status, find constructive discharge, order discovery or trial, or award reinstatement, backpay or punitive damages. Remedy questions for the 1991 conduct remain open.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson:** Applied the recognition of discriminatory abusive working conditions, extending its statutory application to employees and offenders who share a sex without eliminating any element.
- **Harris v. Forklift Systems:** Applied its contextual severe-or-pervasive, objective and subjective inquiry; no psychiatric injury or measurable economic harm is added.
- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks:** Preserved ultimate proof of discrimination and the availability of circumstantial inference; no compulsory comparator or desire showing is created.
- **Andrade v. Mayfair Management:** Applied the actual-authority/material-aid employer-attribution rule, keeping negligence and employment consequences distinct.
- **Brown v. Polk County and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Their separate statutory and constitutional grounds do not replace Title VII's requirements or decide a sexual-orientation coverage question here.
- **Garcia's categorical same-sex exclusion:** The lower-court rule cannot govern Title VII after this decision.

#### Law After Decision

Title VII reaches discriminatory harassment without an opposite-sex requirement. Desire and workforce composition create no categorical gates or immunity. The employee still must establish sex causation and every element of the applicable harassment theory; Harris and Andrade retain their independent force. No sexual-orientation coverage holding or automatic liability rule is adopted.
