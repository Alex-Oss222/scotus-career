### Religious viewpoints in a school forum

#### Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, 508 U.S. 384 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2024.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### A religious perspective cannot be excluded from an admitted subject

**Holding:** When a public school makes an after-hours forum available for private discussion of family life and child-rearing, it may not reject that otherwise eligible discussion solely because the viewpoint is religious. This prohibition applies even assuming a nonpublic forum; it does not require an unlimited public forum or invalidate neutral restrictions on subjects and activities outside the actual forum.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s speech portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association, 460 U.S. 37: viewpoint neutrality within a nonpublic forum applied.
- Cornelius v. NAACP Legal Defense & Educational Fund, 473 U.S. 788: permissible forum limits distinguished from forbidden viewpoint exclusion.
- Widmar v. Vincent, 454 U.S. 263: religious perspective does not by itself remove otherwise eligible private speech from equal-access protection.

##### Equal private access does not establish religion here

**Holding:** Allowing this voluntary, publicly accessible, after-hours film presentation without school sponsorship does not violate the Establishment Clause. Equal access serves a secular forum purpose and neither primarily advances religion as governmental activity nor creates excessive entanglement; this holding authorizes access to private speech, not an official devotional program.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s establishment portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The separate Sunday-worship application and the facial validity of every religious-use restriction are outside review. No unlimited public forum, school sponsorship, compulsory worship or public devotional financing is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for nondiscriminatory treatment and declaratory or injunctive relief as necessary; genuinely neutral scheduling, safety and subject limits remain available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Widmar, 454 U.S. 263: neutral access and private attribution applied.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602: purpose, primary effect and entanglement applied, not replaced.
- Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992: the official-worship and pressure holdings distinguished and preserved.

#### Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 93-2148; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 28 F.3d 1501.
**Decided:** December 11, 1995.

##### Religious instruction on an admitted moral-development subject

**Holding:** A school opening an after-school period to comparable private moral- and character-development instruction and activities may not exclude an otherwise eligible club because its approach is religious. The rule applies to this actual forum even if nonpublic; genuine subject, activity, scheduling, supervision, safety and access limits remain permissible when applied without viewpoint discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes.

**Material application:** Both the Scouts and Club concern moral development. Prayer and Bible instruction do not transform the religious viewpoint into a disqualifying subject; express exclusion even of Scout religious speech confirms the viewpoint line. Access after 6 p.m. does not cure discrimination in the comparable 3–6 p.m. period.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Schools need not open every facility to every religious activity or retain this forum permanently. Neutral prospective forum changes remain available; standalone worship in a different forum and the facial validity of every religious-use rule are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lamb's Chapel supplies the subject/viewpoint distinction; Perry and Cornelius permit genuine, reasonable and viewpoint-neutral management without reclassifying an admitted religious perspective out of the forum.

##### Establishment consequences of these actual private-use arrangements

**Holding:** Equal access on these forum terms does not establish religion: the forum serves a secular purpose, the expression remains private in context, and access creates neither governmental advancement of religion nor excessive entanglement. Private sponsorship must be real; selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured attribution could alter the conclusion, and neutral availability or a disclaimer alone cannot cure official religious activity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for the contextual explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join Part I's concrete rejection of the establishment defense but not Part II's complete rationale.

**Material application:** Parent leadership, voluntary attendance with parental permission and notices of private sponsorship establish actual separation. The 11–15-year-old audience, ongoing extracurricular day and available late bus do not on this record convert the private program into school speech; earlier involvement of a former board member establishes no current control.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new constitutional checklist, fixed disclaimer, universal monitoring duty, official prayer, compelled participation or public-funding entitlement is created. Independent religion-clause claims are unnecessary. No Equal Access Act holding is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's reversal and remedial remand, 8–1; lower courts determine nondiscriminatory declaratory or injunctive relief while lawful neutral forum management remains. No damages, deadline or new hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon supplies purpose, effect and entanglement; Lamb's Chapel applies equal access to private school-forum expression. Chabad demands actual attribution inquiry without a universal sign rule. Pinette supplies no comprehensive forum exemption; Rosenberger's printing benefit establishes no devotional-funding entitlement. Mergens distinguishes private expression from school sponsorship within its own statutory setting.

### Private religious displays in public forums

#### Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-780.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

For the other questions in Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette, decided June 29, 1995, No. 94-780. see Federal Courts — Mootness and seasonal permits (The expired permit dispute remains reviewable).

##### Ohio’s categorical Establishment Clause defense cannot sustain this exclusion

**Holding:** Ohio may not exclude this genuinely private religious display from Capitol Square’s actual, generally available forum on the categorical ground that its religious message and proximity to the Statehouse necessarily establish religion. The judgment concerns the proposal presented, including its offered private-attribution safeguard; it neither finds the later sign adequate nor creates an unconditional right to every unattended display.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Parts II–III, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer: seven votes for this bounded speech and access holding. Those seven do not adopt one comprehensive Establishment Clause test.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The offered readable clarification of private sponsorship had negotiable wording; it is distinct from the later actual sign and the injunction's terms. No finding establishes approved final wording or the sign's adequacy. Government-sponsored displays, actual religious favoritism and future rules remain open. Parts II–III have seven direct joins; Part IV's four-vote contextual explanation and the three-vote private-access rationale supply no controlling comprehensive test or Marks subset. No categorical forum immunity or universal disclaimer requirement is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Widmar v. Vincent: applies protection of religious expression in a genuinely open forum, while preserving examination of an actual establishment defense.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District: applies its controlling protection against exclusion of a religious perspective from comparable private access; its contextual reasoning is not converted into categorical forum immunity.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman and County of Allegheny v. ACLU: not overruled; the Court does not replace existing establishment law with a comprehensive test in this case.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit at the original scope of the Count One merits judgment: the December 21–24, 1993 permit period remains expired. #### Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1047.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

For the other questions in Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller, decided June 29, 1995, No. 93-1047. see Federal Courts — Mootness and seasonal permits (The recurring access dispute remains live, and only the Rotunda claim is reviewed).

##### Georgia’s categorical defense cannot sustain summary judgment excluding the private religious display

**Holding:** A State that actually opens a designated forum to comparable private unattended expression may not exclude an otherwise eligible religious viewpoint merely by asserting that its Capitol location necessarily establishes religion. On this record that categorical defense does not justify judgment for Georgia; lawful access must be provided on the neutral terms governing comparable private expression, with the actual form of relief left for proceedings below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part II, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight votes for this bounded judgment and speech holding; Stevens dissents from its application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The indoor designated forum actually admits comparable sizeable unattended exhibits for several days. The Court does not endorse all the en banc reasoning or guarantee an unconditional display, and comparable secular access cannot be retained while excluding the otherwise eligible religious viewpoint.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: applies the actual designated-forum status and preserves lawful neutral management and prospective forum changes.
- Widmar v. Vincent and Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District: apply protection of comparable private religious expression and the prohibition on religious viewpoint exclusion.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: applies the bounded rejection of a categorical establishment defense, not a universal forum exemption.

##### Interior location does not itself make clearly private expression governmental religion

**Holding:** Neutral access does not establish religion merely because a genuine private-expression forum lies inside a state capitol, when the actual context makes clear that the religious expression remains private rather than governmental. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preferential treatment or obscured private sponsorship can change that conclusion; neither a forum label nor any unspecified sign automatically resolves attribution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter, Part III, joined by Stone, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five votes for this contextual rule. The additional decree-specific inquiry proposed in Part IV has only O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; it is not a five-Justice mandate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The identifying greeting names Chabad but does not expressly disclaim state sponsorship; an offered disclaimer is not a finding of an effective completed safeguard. The four-Justice additional decree-specific attribution inquiry is not part of the mandate. Neither a universal sign duty, preferential religious access nor a duty to keep the forum open is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- County of Allegheny v. ACLU: preserves contextual examination of apparent governmental affiliation, rather than title alone.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: preserves its shared access holding and distinct attribution rationales; no categorical immunity is drawn from its separate opinions.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman: remains in force; no comprehensive replacement establishment test is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit reversal of State summary judgment on Count II for further proceedings and lawful relief on neutral terms. No Supreme Court permit, retrospective 1991 display, fixed sign, permanent seasonal entitlement, damages amount or compulsory forum closure is ordered. Count I remains undisturbed; lawful physical, safety, scheduling and identification rules and genuinely prospective viewpoint-neutral forum closure or redefinition remain possible.

### Private student publications and religious viewpoints

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.
- Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen: apply neutral-benefit and private-direction distinctions within the actual program, not neutrality alone.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet: distinguishes denominational governmental authority and retains religious-neutrality limits.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.
- Lee v. Weisman: preserves limits on governmental religious participation and coercion; this private printing benefit compels no student worship.
- Church of Scientology v. Clearwater: distinguishes RFRA burdens and exemptions from constitutional financing authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the religious-viewpoint exclusion under otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms. Examine any properly available independent Virginia funding defense consistently with the federal viewpoint holding, without declaring applicability, validity or revival of forfeited defenses. No full payment, damages, immunity, fee or compelled-fee refund is determined.

### Facial challenges to child-pornography definitions

#### United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-723.
**Decided:** November 29, 1994.

For the other questions in this decision, see Criminal Procedure — Knowledge of regulated sexual depictions (The enacted knowledge term reaches minority and sexually explicit character); Federal Courts — Defending an existing favorable judgment (The two renewed alternatives may defend the existing favorable judgment).

##### The asserted age-eighteen line does not establish substantial facial overbreadth

**Holding:** The renewed objection that §2256 defines a minor as a person under eighteen, rather than under sixteen, does not establish substantial First Amendment overbreadth in §§2252(a)(1)–(2) as construed here. Difficulty estimating age near eighteen, together with decisions permitting adult treatment of some older adolescents in other settings, does not require facial invalidation of this protective category.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747 (1982), applies the requirement of substantial overbreadth for facial invalidation and its protection of children from sexual exploitation. Its approval of a statute protecting children under sixteen does not establish sixteen as the highest permissible protective age.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general constitutional age-of-majority rule is announced, and no as-applied challenge is foreclosed merely by this facial disposition.

##### The complete lascivious-exhibition category defeats the word-substitution facial attack

**Holding:** Replacing lewd with lascivious in §2256’s category of lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area does not itself make §§2252(a)(1)–(2) facially vague or substantially overbroad. The complete category retains a sexual-conduct limit and does not cover nudity merely because a person’s body is visible; whether these particular tapes satisfy the definition remains open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *New York v. Ferber*, 458 U.S. 747 (1982), applies the meaningful limit supplied by lewd exhibition of the genitals and the substantial-overbreadth standard; it does not equate ordinary nudity with the prohibited sexual conduct.
- *Miller v. California*, 413 U.S. 15 (1973), supplies the ordinary lewd-exhibition terminology considered in this comparison; the Court does not add Miller’s complete obscenity test as a requirement for the child-pornography category.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The other objection concerning actual or simulated bestiality and sadistic or masochistic abuse is not reached. No unofficial-code punctuation is adopted and no definition of every possible application is supplied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded, 7–2. The facial-scienter invalidation and the two renewed facial-definition defenses do not sustain the appellate judgment. The existing minority-knowledge finding is recognized; the indictment-scienter, actual-tape-content and implementation issues remain. No count is unconditionally reinstated or invalidated, and no acquittal, automatic retrial or harmlessness finding is ordered.

### Official school prayer

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

#### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1121.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal

**Holding:** Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. The court independently determines those cumulative conditions and, when they are satisfied, must reinstate without discretionary balancing, retaining independent responsibility for all claims, defenses, and remedies; this expressly retrospective entitlement does not authorize legislative findings, dictated liability, or unrestricted reopening of completed merits judgments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and remedy stated here. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas dissent; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability. The statutory conditions are satisfied, including the timely February 11, 1992 motion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: applies its five-Justice Article III holding sustaining §27A(b)'s bounded mandatory reinstatement procedure to this qualifying completed private limitations dismissal; preserves independent eligibility and merits adjudication and all constitutional limits.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: applies its distinction between genuine legislative change and prescribed judicial adjudication; Morgan Stanley supplies the completed-dismissal rule that Robertson did not decide.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: applies its protection of the independent judicial function; a class-defined entitlement to renewed adjudication does not dictate findings or securities liability.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: preserves protection of completed judicial determinations while distinguishing the statutory entitlement to litigate a limitations-dismissed claim from a legislative declaration of facts or liability contrary to an adjudication.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529: preserves finality against reopening by later judicial doctrine alone; the entitlement here comes from an express statute whose Article III validity Morgan Stanley establishes.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes the continuing operation of prospective equitable relief; that setting does not itself decide this completed limitations dismissal.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own defense; no governmental waiver of a private defendant's defense supplies the holding here.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304: leaves its due-process revival rule intact without treating it as a controlling answer to Article III.
- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S. 350: identifies the limitations rule that produced the dismissal and the statutory response; §27A(b) supplies reinstatement without establishing the securities merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may not command findings contrary to the record, impose liability on identified defendants by legislative declaration, or disguise an individualized award as a procedural reopening law. The Court decides no general power to set aside completed judgments adjudicating the underlying merits, no constitutionality of every retrospective statute, and no independent due-process question; applicable due-process protections and other constitutional restrictions remain. Section 27A(a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, federal waiver, continuing prospective relief, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. The decision neither adjudicates other parties' completed cases nor creates criminal or innocence remedies. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses remain for the courts; reinstatement establishes none of them.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment of the Sixth Circuit is reversed. The case is remanded for reinstatement under the satisfied conditions of §27A(b) and further judicial proceedings on the underlying securities claims. Reinstatement is required, not a discretionary remedy. The courts retain responsibility for the merits, defenses, and any relief; no merits outcome is prescribed.

**Material judgment:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support reversal; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent.

#### Weiss v. United States, 510 U.S. 163

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1482, including Hernandez under Supreme Court Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

For the other questions in Weiss v. United States, 1994-01-19, No. 92-1482, including Hernandez under Supreme Court Rule 12.2, see Criminal Procedure — Fixed tenure and impartial military adjudication.

##### Commissioned officers assigned germane military judicial duties

**Holding:** The Appointments Clause applies to military judges, but the already commissioned officers serving as trial and appellate judges in these cases require no second appointment: their judicial duties are germane to the military offices conferred by presidential appointment and Senate confirmation. Assignment by a Judge Advocate General does not itself confer a new, unrelated federal office; the holding does not authorize that result or decide civilian appellate appointments or assignment to principal offices.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices support the confined existing-commission and germane-duty proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Buckley v. Valeo: applied to the officer character of the military judges' duties; military office is not exempt from the Appointments Clause.
- Shoemaker v. United States: applied to germane additional duties within an existing commissioned office; no general authority to fill unrelated offices is recognized.
- Freytag v. Commissioner: its insistence on constitutional appointment of officers is preserved; the present judges already hold constitutionally sufficient commissions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Civilian appellate appointments, unrelated new offices and assignment to principal offices are not decided. The tenure holding depends on this system's protections, including limits on command evaluation, improper censure and influence, judicial review and disqualification; supported retaliation, reassignment and structural-bias challenges remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Court of Military Appeals judgments, including Hernandez under Rule 12.2, are affirmed on the appointment and fixed-tenure objections. No second appointment, fixed term or retrial is ordered.

#### Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-453.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For reviewability of the original categorical-prohibition period, see Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, June 29, 1992, No. 91-453, under Federal Courts — Finality and earlier-period takings claims.

##### Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use

**Holding:** A land-use regulation that removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property effects a taking requiring just compensation unless the prohibited use was already outside the owner's title under background state property or nuisance law. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable background limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use in the property's circumstances; a new legislative harm declaration alone does not suffice, and ordinary adjudication supplies the proof requirements without a special heightened quantum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and its explanation. Kennedy supports reversal through an expectations-sensitive analysis, without joining this categorical formulation. No Marks combination is required or used.

**Material application:** Total loss is the trial finding assumed for the legal question, not a Council concession or appellate affirmance of value. A new legislative declaration of harm cannot replace substantiated background law; changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance principles.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the trial court's total-loss finding to decide the presented legal issue; it makes no new appraisal and does not resolve the Council's preserved challenge to that finding. No unconditional entitlement to the trial award, final application of state nuisance law, right to anticipated profits, new parcel rule, or disposition of post-amendment permission is adopted. Legitimate environmental purposes remain sufficient to justify regulation; compensation is a distinct consequence. The Court overrules no earlier decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-compensation judgment is reversed and remanded for background property and nuisance law and the remaining defenses, including the sustained extent and duration of loss. Any compensation covers the actual taking period; the $1,232,387.50 trial award is not reinstated automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pennsylvania Coal Co. v. Mahon, 260 U.S. 393 (1922): applies its recognition that regulation can go too far despite valid public purposes; does not attribute today's exact categorical rule to that decision.
- Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 (1980): specifies and extends its complete-economic-use formulation into the rule and necessary exception stated here; does not adjudicate its distinct legitimate-interest formulation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978): preserves contextual analysis for partial deprivation, including economic impact, expectations and the character of governmental action; no percentage automatically defeats or establishes compensation.
- Mugler v. Kansas, 123 U.S. 623 (1887), Hadacheck v. Sebastian, 239 U.S. 394 (1915), Miller v. Schoene, 276 U.S. 272 (1928), and Goldblatt v. Hempstead, 369 U.S. 590 (1962): limits a broad noxious-use reading that would make any legislative harm characterization conclusive despite complete beneficial-use deprivation; preserves their actual outcomes and lawful protective regulation.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 (1987): distinguishes its productive-enterprise and partial-interest setting; no reversal of its actual holding or adoption of a new denominator follows.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): applies existing rules and understandings to identify the protected property interest; does not decide a procedural due-process claim.
- Scranton v. Wheeler, 179 U.S. 141 (1900): applies its antecedent-title-limit principle; a previously existing servitude is distinct from a newly imposed public burden.

#### Dolan v. City of Tigard, 512 U.S. 374

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-518.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419: applied for the distinction between compelled physical occupation and ordinary use regulation; no completed occupation is found here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; all five support both the rule and this application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

**Holding:** Increased traffic and the usefulness of alternative transportation establish the purpose nexus for a pedestrian and bicycle route, but the finding that this route could offset some traffic does not adequately connect the extent of the demanded strip to the development's impacts. #### Libretti v. United States, 516 U.S. 29

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-7427.
**Decided:** November 7, 1995.

##### Consent, factual admission and the statutory bounds of criminal forfeiture

**Holding:** A defendant may knowingly and voluntarily waive the forfeiture jury protection and admit facts supporting forfeiture, but consent cannot enlarge §853's punishment or transfer an interest beyond the defendant's lawful power of disposition. An admission may establish a statutory predicate; an all-assets agreement cannot replace a predicate Congress requires.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I-B, unanimous on these common propositions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful acquisition alone does not immunize property: §853(a)(1) reaches directly or indirectly obtained proceeds, (a)(2) the defendant's property used or intended in any manner or part to commit or facilitate the violation, and (a)(3) for a continuing criminal enterprise his interests, claims and property or contractual rights supplying control over it. Under §853(p), the defendant's act or omission must make qualifying property unavailable by inability to locate it despite due diligence, third-party transfer/sale/deposit, location beyond jurisdiction, substantial diminution, or commingling not divisible without difficulty; then other defendant property must be forfeited up to its value. Section 853(d)'s rebuttable presumption at trial requires the Government to prove by a preponderance both acquisition during the violation or within a reasonable time afterward and the absence of a likely source other than the covered violation. An all-assets promise proves neither predicate. No asset-by-asset eligibility or invalidity is found here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godinez distinguishes competence from knowing voluntary choice; Olano intentional relinquishment from inaction; Mezzanatto's limited consent holding does not authorize an unlawful sentence or waiver of every protection.

##### Former Rule 11(f) and a separate forfeiture factual-basis inquiry

**Holding:** Former Rule 11(f) requires a factual basis for the substantive guilty plea, not a separate guilt-style factual-basis inquiry into the extent of forfeiture imposed as sentence under §§848 and 853. That procedural construction neither authorizes punishment exceeding the statutes nor requires acceptance of a defective agreement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I-A; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela affirms on separate statutory-record and informed-waiver grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rules 7(c)(2) and 31(e)'s distinct notice and special-verdict safeguards do not turn each property interest into a CCE guilt element. The precise extent of any independent inquiry duty outside Rule 11(f) remains open. No later Rule 32.2 procedure applies.

##### The particular forfeiture determination and the Sixth Amendment

**Holding:** Under the §§848 and 853 scheme reviewed, the extent of forfeiture following conviction is a sentencing determination, not an element of CCE guilt requiring a Sixth Amendment jury determination. This does not place every sentence-affecting fact outside the jury guarantee.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reserve the constitutional source of the forfeiture jury protection; their reservation does not displace the majority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gaudin remains fully effective for enacted offense elements. McMillan, Cabana and Spaziano supply the bounded guilt/punishment distinction. Austin and Alexander establish forfeiture's punitive character in their settings, which does not itself import every guilt-trial safeguard. No Excessive Fines claim is decided.

##### Waiver of the Rule 31(e) special verdict by affirmative agreement and courtroom assent

**Holding:** Libretti's represented agreement to forfeit identified interests, signed jury waiver and express courtroom assent to ending trial and proceeding to sentence waive the Rule 31(e) special verdict on this record. Former Rule 11(c) requires no additional forfeiture-specific advisement, and Rule 23(a) requires no new forfeiture-specific writing for this statutory sentencing verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg separately uphold informed relinquishment but do not join this broader sufficient-waiver formulation; Stevens does not independently decide the application.

**Material application:** Affirmative represented assent, the agreement's actual admissions and scope, express explanation that trial would end, and earlier jury-forfeiture references support waiver. Neither competence, a signature nor silence alone establishes knowing surrender of every sentencing issue.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A judge may give an explicit special-verdict explanation and must not affirmatively mislead or leave evident confusion uncorrected. Coercion, voluntariness, understanding and agreement-scope challenges retain their distinct predicates. Private legal advice is not assumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godinez, Olano and Mezzanatto preserve informed intentional choice within the protection actually surrendered, without automatic waiver from a plea or statutory classification alone.

##### Rejection of a wholesale new factual-basis proceeding on the existing record

**Holding:** The trial evidence, sentencing materials, §853-referenced agreement and the judge's response to Libretti's preserved objection support rejecting the demanded wholesale new forfeiture factual-basis proceeding. The Court leaves the precise independent duty outside Rule 11(f) unresolved and makes no new asset-by-asset §853(a) or (p) adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. Scalia and Thomas withhold this additional appraisal, Stone-Zsela supplies a separate record-limited ground, and Stevens would remand for statutory assurance.

**Material application:** The later April order's adverse Wyochem/Porter substitute-property finding, reserved tracing questions and third-party return orders survive at their actual scope. The pending appeal defeated defendant-specific reopening, not separate §853(n) jurisdiction. The $8,416.23 General Chemical check designated as substitute property is distinct from the Columbia IRA and Porter's $1,333.45 claim; designation alone proves no §853(p) predicate. On that determination the court must amend the order. It is not a defendant's postjudgment-reconsideration route, and general innocent-owner terminology does not replace those criteria.

### 1997-01-14 - Chamber of Commerce v. Reich; D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; 74 F.3d 1322; no Supreme Court docket number assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Chamber_of_Commerce_v_Reich_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. Each proposition below therefore has nine votes; judgment agreement and reasoning agreement coincide.

##### I. Review of the implementing officer

**Holding and operative rule.** A person injured by an officer's implementation of a restriction outside delegated statutory authority may seek prospective equitable restraint under federal-question jurisdiction unless Congress has displaced that remedy. Section 702 waives sovereign immunity for this nonmonetary official-capacity suit against the implementing Secretary even without a pleaded APA review cause, while preserving other legal and equitable review limits and relief prohibitions in another consent-to-suit statute; any injunction must identify the officer or successor responsible for compliance.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 1331 supplies jurisdiction; traditional officer-directed equity supplies the action; §702 addresses sovereign immunity. None independently creates a damages claim or a cause for every statutory error. McAnnulty, Stark and Harmon establish that the absence of an express review provision does not itself eliminate prospective officer relief. Larson and Dugan support the confined distinction between authorized action and action beyond delegated power, without making every allegation of error sufficient. The employers challenge an operative disqualification imposed by the Secretary, not merely the wisdom of an authorized purchase. Their claim presents a judicially manageable boundary between two federal statutes. Dalton bars reopening its particular completed base-closure package; the Procurement Act contains no comparable structure foreclosing this action. Franklin preserves review of a completed subordinate act despite later presidential involvement. The origin of this restriction in a presidential order does not answer whether its implementing officer has statutory authority. The Court neither decides the President's APA status nor authorizes coercive relief against him.

##### II. The procurement delegation does not authorize this labor-policy sanction

**Holding and operative rule.** The Procurement Act's general economy-and-efficiency delegation does not authorize Executive Order 12954's government-wide disqualification based on permanent replacement of economic strikers, because that sanction alters the bargaining choices Congress preserved under the NLRA. This statutory holding leaves Congress free to change those choices and preserves ordinary review of materially different proprietary conditions and lawful centralized purchasing standards.

**Controlling explanation.** Mackay Radio recognizes the use of permanent replacements for economic strikers within the NLRA's remaining protections; it does not equate those strikers with unfair-labor-practice strikers. Insurance Agents and Machinists identify the economic weapons Congress left to the bargaining parties. A general purchasing delegation must operate with that allocation, not silently displace it. Boston Harbor protects a lawful bargain justified by the public purchaser's project interests; a procurement label alone does not turn general labor policy into proprietary conduct. This order makes replacement itself the disqualifying event and reaches affiliates capable of supplying the contract, even when the striking workers do not perform that contract. The Government's interest in dependable supply and retained experience is legitimate, but the policy's actual operation goes beyond a supported performance bargain. Its threshold and discretionary exceptions do not cure that statutory mismatch. The Court neither imposes a universal one-project rule nor invalidates purchasing standards because of their size. It does not guarantee any employer government business. The ground is construction of the federal enactments together, not mechanical application of state-preemption labels or a constitutional limit on Congress.

**Operative qualifications.** The order concerns contracts above $100,000. After finding permanent replacement of lawfully striking workers, the Secretary may find termination for convenience appropriate unless the contracting agency head objects; termination is not mandatory. Debarment retains exceptions when the precipitating dispute has been resolved as determined by the Secretary or the agency head finds a compelling reason to lift it. Debarment normally reaches the organizational units found to have used permanent replacements; the regulations define a unit to include the responsible prime-contracting division or element and any affiliate capable of supplying the contracted goods or services. In deciding whether a dispute ended, the regulations consider a formal settlement or agreed dispute-resolution procedure, informal resolution, the strikers' return, and other factors tending to show the dispute has ended. The Court does not adopt the lower court's reading of those factors as a universal requirement of union approval or find that every application compels unlawful recognition.

##### III. The relief follows the statutory defect

**Holding and operative rule.** The judgment reversing dismissal and rejecting the challenged implementation is affirmed, and the case is remanded for appropriate prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against the implementing Secretary confined to the unlawful procurement restriction. No injunction against the President, damages, contract award, striker reinstatement or employee-specific bargaining remedy is ordered.

**Controlling explanation.** The reviewable defect concerns the Secretary's implementation of an unauthorized restriction. Prospective officer relief addresses that defect without transferring purchasing decisions to the courts or deciding individual labor rights. The lower court must formulate the appropriate decree consistently with §702's responsible-officer requirement and retained legal and equitable limits. No independent nondelegation ruling, mandatory procurement-findings procedure or decision on a materially different proprietary measure is necessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

- McAnnulty, 187 U.S. 94: applied for prospective review of officer action beyond delegated statutory authority despite the absence of an express review provision.
- Stark v. Wickard, 321 U.S. 288: applied for the availability of judicial protection against officer action exceeding statutory limits.
- Harmon v. Brucker, 355 U.S. 579: applied for judicial determination of an officer's statutory authority before accepting a claim of unreviewable discretion.
- Larson v. Domestic & Foreign Commerce Corp., 337 U.S. 682: applied at its confined officer-action scope; statutory error is not converted into a universal immunity exception.
- Dugan v. Rank, 372 U.S. 609: applied for the corresponding distinction between sovereign relief and action outside lawful officer authority.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992: its review of completed subordinate action is preserved; presidential APA status and coercive presidential relief remain reserved.
- Dalton v. Specter, May 23, 1994: distinguished because its completed base-closure scheme supplies preclusion absent here; its rule is not general executive immunity.
- NLRB v. Mackay Radio & Telegraph Co., 304 U.S. 333: applied for permanent replacement of economic strikers within the NLRA's retained protections, without changing the protection of unfair-labor-practice strikers.
- NLRB v. Insurance Agents' International Union, 361 U.S. 477: applied for Congress's preservation of economic pressure in bargaining.
- Machinists v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Commission, 427 U.S. 132: its account of the economic choices Congress left unregulated informs the statutory construction; it is not mechanically transferred from state regulation to every federal enactment.
- Boston Harbor, March 8, 1993: distinguished on the actual proprietary bargain; its protection of lawful project purchasing and its rejection of procurement labels as sufficient remain intact. No universal one-project restriction is added.

Executive Order 12954's challenged replacement-based restriction cannot operate under the general procurement delegation. Lawful proprietary measures and centralized purchasing standards remain subject to their own statutory justification; no entitlement to a government contract follows. The Court adds this statute-specific limit without changing Dalton's actual preclusion rule or deciding presidential APA status.

##### The amended escheat provision still takes the presented succession interests without compensation

**Holding and operative rule:** Amended §207 of the Indian Land Consolidation Act effects an uncompensated taking as applied to these fractional trust-land interests: it eliminates intestate succession and confines devise to existing co-owners of the same parcel, leaving these owners unable to transmit valuable interests to their chosen descendants even though each devise preserves an existing fraction. **Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Hodel v. Irving, which protects the valuable power to transmit property at death while permitting Congress to address the serious problem of fractionated Indian lands. Penn Central supplies the examination of economic impact, investment-backed expectations, and the character of the governmental action. Low annual income does not establish that these interests lack value: their reported aggregate value is $1,239. Expectations of investment return are not strong, but that consideration does not answer the extraordinary destruction of succession.

Congress made real changes. Subsection (a) reaches an interest of 2 percent or less incapable of earning $100 in any one of the five years from death. Earnings below $100 in any one of the preceding five years create a rebuttable presumption of that future incapacity. Subsection (b) permits devise to another owner of an undivided interest in the same parcel. Subsection (c) permits a Secretary-approved tribal disposition code to take precedence over subsection (a), but prohibits approval of a code that fails to prevent further descent or fractionation of escheatable interests.

Those changes remain insufficient here. Intestacy is wholly barred, the devise class excludes these descendants, and no operative alternative code is identified for this estate. Each devise passes one existing fraction to one descendant; none divides that fraction further. Retained lifetime enjoyment and transfer do not adequately replace succession. Congress's important consolidation objective cannot by itself answer the uncompensated loss. Nearly six years to arrange a lifetime transfer or qualifying devise do not cure the inadequate transmission choices this mechanism preserves. The Court therefore sustains the existing declaratory and injunctive protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not adopt respondents' proposed categorical extension of Lucas, hold every inheritance restriction a taking, value compensation, prescribe a consolidation plan, or decide a different tribal code. Purchase, compensated acquisition, voluntary consolidation, and other constitutional measures remain available; no particular alternative receives advance approval. The Court decides no independent individualized-notice or due-process claim.

- **Hodel v. Irving, 481 U.S. 704 (1987):** Applies its protection against the extraordinary uncompensated destruction of succession and resolves the amended statute that Irving reserved. - **Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978):** Applies its impact, expectations, and governmental-character analysis in the succession setting; no factor becomes conclusive by itself.

Missouri created and regulates the Board and the governor appoints four members, with the mayor serving as the fifth. Those controls matter, but the record's municipal financing, city budget and appropriation role, limits requiring city approval and local operational relationship establish the relevant character of this Board. No single appointment or financial fact substitutes for that combined inquiry. Because the Board is not a state arm, the Court need not decide whether litigation conduct effected an authorized waiver, whether Congress validly abrogated state immunity for this claim, or whether an officer route would permit the requested relief. Seminole Tribe supplies no categorical Article I answer to those unnecessary questions.

#### The salary-basis rule validly implements the public-employee exemption

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 213(a)(1)'s express power to define and delimit the exemption permits the Secretary to require salary-basis compensation for these public executive and administrative employees. Section 207(k)'s separate law-enforcement hours treatment, public-accountability absence provisions and the need for police discipline do not require a general exemption for short disciplinary deductions.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Fixed salary reasonably distinguishes exempt compensation from pay varying with work quality or quantity. Congress's delegation includes public employment. Disciplinary needs do not make the criterion unreasonable or establish an exception. Major-safety rules prevent serious danger to the plant or other employees; residency does not qualify. Specified absences and furloughs differ from general discipline. No rulemaking petition or refusal to amend is found.

**Operative regulatory qualifications:** Under then-effective §541.118(a), the employee generally receives the full predetermined salary for any week with work; a week without work need not be paid. Employer- or business-caused lack of work permits no deduction when the employee is ready, willing and able to work. A day or more of personal absence other than sickness or accident may be deducted. Day-or-more sickness or disability deductions must follow a bona fide plan, policy or practice compensating salary loss from both sickness and disability; salary need not be paid before qualification, after leave exhaustion or for compensated days. Alternatively, deductions for a working day or more are permitted under a state sickness-and-disability insurance law or private sickness-and-disability insurance plan when benefits follow that law or plan. Industrial-accident compensation must comply with the applicable compensation law or plan, and the employer must also provide a plan for other sickness and disability. Jury service, witness attendance and temporary military leave permit no salary deductions, but the specified fees or military pay for that week may be offset. Good-faith major-safety penalties retain the narrow scope stated above.

Section 541.5d(a) preserves otherwise qualifying public employees under a pay system established by statute, ordinance, regulation or a policy or practice based on public accountability, which accrues both personal and sick leave and requires reduced pay or unpaid leave for absences shorter than a workday for personal reasons, illness or injury when accrued leave is unused because permission was not sought or was denied, leave was exhausted, or the employee chose unpaid leave. Under §541.5d(b), a budget-required furlough disqualifies salary status only in the workweek in which it occurs and pay is reduced. These exceptions remain distinct from correction of an improper deduction.

#### The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds

**Holding and operative rule:** The responsible Secretary's fair and considered interpretation of the salary regulation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Presentation in an amicus brief does not alone disqualify that interpretation, but supplies no automatic authority to an agency lawyer, power to amend operative text or deference to new factual assertions about this employer.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by the seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice proposes a different interpretive rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Thomas Jefferson University applies Seminole Rock's bounded own-regulation rule; Stinson preserves the difference between interpreting a rule and contradicting or amending it. Martin requires attention to the statutory allocation of administrative responsibility, while Bowen excludes unsupported litigation rationalizations. The Secretary's position here interprets the actual salary and correction text and is consistent with the supported administrative treatment, including the correction letter. It is a fair and considered legal construction, not an employer-specific factual determination resulting from a Department investigation. Its amicus form does not make it an impermissible invention or itself establish its correctness. The canon favoring narrow construction of exemptions does not prevent the Secretary from resolving actual regulatory ambiguity within the congressional delegation. Courts still determine whether the reading fits the text and lawful authority; they do not accept the agency's characterization of unproved facts merely because its legal construction controls.

#### Practical exposure requires an actual practice or an effective particular policy

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee is subject to improper salary reduction when an actual practice of such deductions exists or an effectively communicated, sufficiently particular policy creates a significant practical likelihood of them. A merely theoretical disciplinary possibility is insufficient, but an actual deduction from each employee is unnecessary; the mixed manual and Guzy's exceptional negotiated sanction do not themselves establish the required policy or recurring practice here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The salary text concerns real susceptibility to reductions, not only deductions already made and not every abstract possibility listed in a broad disciplinary code. An effective policy can alter compensation arrangements before the first deduction; an undifferentiated range of sanctions governing exempt and nonexempt workers does not itself communicate that these salaried officers will receive impermissible deductions. Guzy's negotiated two-day residency suspension instead of termination was treated below as an exceptional event. The later rule made termination the exclusive residency sanction; it did not repeal every disciplinary authorization. That episode alone does not establish recurring deductions or a general significant likelihood.

The officers' argument that the manual itself defeats any salary agreement rests on the broader legal construction the Court rejects. Their additional examples, witness-understanding contentions and related evidentiary-burden submission remain disputed submissions, not grounds for a new blanket factual or forfeiture finding. The Court adopts no numerical deduction threshold, compulsory notice format or universal employer-compliance presumption, and directs no general factual reopening. Ordinary preservation and mandate rules retain their force.

#### Correction eligibility requires actual reimbursement and future compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §541.118(a)(6), an improper deduction that is inadvertent **or** made for reasons other than lack of work may qualify for correction; reimbursement **and** a promise of future compliance are both necessary to preserve exemption by that route. Guzy's isolated intentional disciplinary deduction qualifies under the second alternative, without an immediate-upon-discovery deadline, but corrected exempt status cannot support disposal of the affected salary claim until both acts are satisfied.

**Authority:** Part V of Justice Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous authority.

**Controlling explanation:** The text separates two eligibility alternatives from two cumulative acts of correction. Intentionality does not defeat the other-than-lack-of-work alternative. Nor does the text require payment immediately upon discovery. It nevertheless requires actual reimbursement and a promise; eligibility alone accomplishes neither. The correction mechanism does not convert a settled recurring practice into an isolated error. Deductions generally made when work is unavailable indicate no intention to pay on a salary basis and defeat exemption during that period.

The lower opinion quoted both conditions and required reimbursement, but its formal disposition did not expressly establish a remand implementation proceeding. This Court therefore states the operative condition in its own mandate: on return, the lower courts must secure both required acts before disposing of the affected claim on the ground of corrected exemption. Neither completed payment nor a completed promise is found. If the employer does not perform, it cannot rely on restored exemption, and lawful compensation consequences remain to be determined. No new correction condition, fixed compliance timetable, automatic overtime amount or damages award is added.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. and Mt. Healthy are applied to the Board's actual local relationship; treasury exposure remains important without becoming the exclusive condition of immunity.
- Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida remains limited to its actual holdings and unresolved State-amenability rationale. No waiver, abrogation or retrospective officer-remedy rule is added.
- Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala and Bowles v. Seminole Rock retain controlling weight for a permissible agency interpretation of its own regulation, with text, consistency, lawful authority and actual-ground limits intact.
- Stinson v. United States preserves authoritative interpretation within, rather than amendment of, the operative rule; its Sentencing Commission setting is not mechanically transferred to factual assertions here.
- Martin v. OSHRC supplies attention to actual interpretive responsibility and reasonableness; Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital preserves the exclusion of unsupported litigation rationalizations.

#### Law After Decision

The Board may be subjected to these FLSA proceedings without deciding the State's own immunity. Salary basis remains a valid component of the public executive/administrative exemption. Practical exposure, actual deductions and correction are distinct inquiries, and every stated regulatory exception retains its conditions. The own-regulation controlling-weight rule remains law; the Chief Justice's proposed replacement receives no additional join. A correction-dependent exemption requires both reimbursement and the future promise, secured through the present mandate. Duties and the former district award are not reopened.

### 1997-03-03 — United States v. Gonzales; No. 95-1605.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Gonzales_merits_1997-03-03.md).

#### Holdings

Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those seven Justices adopt both holdings and their explanations. Justices Stevens and Breyer dissent; no proposition depends on combining separate writings.

##### Existing state imprisonment is within the concurrency prohibition

**Holding and operative rule.** A federal district court imposing a term under 18 U.S.C. §924(c) may not direct it to run concurrently with an already imposed state or federal term of imprisonment. The statute's unqualified reference to any other term includes state imprisonment; the general sentencing discretion in §3584 supplies no exception to that specific command.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress expressly confined the predicate crime to one prosecutable in federal court but repeated no federal limitation in the distinct concurrency prohibition. The reference that includes the predicate sentence gives an example within the comprehensive class rather than limiting that class. The specific notwithstanding command therefore controls over general discretion. Respondents' strongest argument is that mandatory addition can depend on whether state or federal proceedings occurred first. That concern does not insert a federal-only qualification Congress omitted, and reserving the later-state question preserves the limits of this case rather than answering that different question. Witte's sentence coordination remains conditional on governing statutes and Guidelines; it does not authorize overriding this statutory restriction. Deal construed the separate second-or-subsequent-conviction language and does not independently decide this phrase. Text and context leave no residual ambiguity requiring lenity. Aggregate punishment concerns cannot create an unprovided exception, though lawful choices concerning other terms remain available.

##### Other sentencing choices and service sequence remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 924(c) does not otherwise withdraw the federal court's lawful authority to arrange non-§924(c) sentences under §3584 and applicable law, and it does not require the firearm term to be served first. The erroneous federal-only concurrency premise must be removed on remand without indiscriminately reinstating every original sentence or disturbing unrelated appellate rulings.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 3584 generally makes simultaneously imposed terms concurrent unless the court orders otherwise or a statute requires consecutiveness, and makes terms imposed at different times consecutive unless the court orders concurrency. Its separate prohibition of consecutive terms for an attempt and the offense that was the attempt's sole objective remains intact; no such pair is found here, and that provision supplies no firearm exception. Section 3584(b) requires attention to §3553(a)'s factors as to each offense when exercising the discretion the law permits. Administrative aggregation under §3584(c) does not establish which term must be served first. The District Court's concurrent treatment of the drug terms therefore presents a separate inquiry from the mandatory firearm addition. The Court does not decide a later state sentencing court's authority, credit calculations, the administrative order of service, the validity of underlying convictions or any independent constitutional or duplicative-enhancement challenge.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Witte v. United States**: Its conditional coordination rule is preserved; applicable statutes and Guidelines govern the available adjustment, and coordination cannot override §924(c)'s particular command or cure an independent successive-prosecution bar.
- **Deal v. United States**: Its construction of second-or-subsequent firearm convictions remains controlling at its own scope; the different phrase does not independently resolve state-term concurrency.

No earlier decision is overruled. The Court supplies no general sentencing-severity cap or new constitutional rule.

#### Law After Decision

For the later-sentencing federal court, the firearm term must be consecutive to existing state and federal imprisonment. Lawful arrangement of other terms remains distinct. The holding creates no rule commanding a later state court, no administrative sequence and no determination of an independent constitutional challenge.

### 1997-03-19 — Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District; court-of-appeals No. 94-60631.

Authority: [Record](../records/Ingebretsen_v_Jackson_Public_School_District_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Imminent personal exposure permits this challenge

**Rule and authority:** A student facing a concrete, imminent application of an official school-event mechanism may seek prospective relief before the threatened exposure occurs. Part I has all nine joins and controls; standing does not establish the threatened conduct's illegality.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires personal injury, a causal connection and redressability, rather than abstract disagreement with a statute. These enrolled students face impending events under a mandatory permission mechanism administered by school officials, and the hearing record supports that concrete prospect. Their parents may represent their children's interests through the proper representative route. The absence of a completed local policy does not make the impending exposure remote, while the availability of lawful applications remains relevant to the merits and remedy. This holding establishes neither general taxpayer standing nor a right for an abstract objector elsewhere.

##### II. Official school worship cannot be insulated by student delivery or a disclaimer

**Rule and authority:** A school violates the Establishment Clause when it organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event; student initiation or delivery and a statutory disclaimer do not alone make that exercise private. Part II controls through Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, independently of a separate quantified-pressure showing.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee supplies the prohibition on official sponsorship of school worship. The mandatory event permission and religious-content eligibility terms give officials an operational role that must be examined, rather than disregarded because the speaker is a student. Section 1(2) permits student-initiated, nonsectarian, nonproselytizing prayer at compulsory and noncompulsory events; the State may not administer those conditions through official devotional organization or religious supervision. Section 1(4)'s disclaimer does not erase actual official conduct. Section 2, by contrast, denies schools authority to prescribe prayer's form or content. That is a statutory command, not a factual finding that it is always obeyed or always violated. Pinette and Chabad require attention to attribution, while Rosenberger protects neutral support for independently edited private speech; none makes every school religious statement official. The supported prospective mechanism warrants bounded interim protection, not a final holding against every permissible application.

##### III. School-directed worship in a pressured setting is independently forbidden

**Rule and authority:** School direction of religious exercise in compulsory or comparably pressured school settings violates the Establishment Clause when the official setting places actual pressure on students to participate. Part III independently controls through Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; mere offense at another student's private viewpoint does not suffice.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee's distinct school-pressure holding supplies this ground. Official control, students' attendance and participation expectations, and the setting's practical demands matter even without a legal punishment for declining prayer. The State cannot dispose of that inquiry merely by describing participation as voluntary or substituting a student speaker. These circumstances support interim protection against the official mechanism. The Court does not presume coercion from hearing private religious speech, require a quantified social-pressure measurement, or hold every voluntary gathering coercive. This is an alternative ground with its own six-Justice coalition, not an aggregation with Part II.

##### IV. Correct the graduation exception and preserve lawful expression

**Rule and authority:** Preliminary relief must restrain supported unlawful official worship while preserving independently lawful statutory provisions and genuinely private expression. Part IV's legal limit has all nine joins; its continued restraint, removal of graduation's categorical exemption and remand have Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Lee does not exempt graduation merely because students initiate and deliver prayer; the same official attribution and pressure inquiries apply. A genuinely independent student's religious viewpoint remains protected. The lower court must therefore remove its automatic graduation safe harbor and frame the interim order around the actual official mechanism. Section 1(3)'s protection of other voluntary expression, Section 2's separate voluntary-prayer provision and no-prescription command, and Section 1(5)'s severability direction must each receive effect. The supported threat of official worship establishes the relevant interim constitutional injury; narrowing protects the countervailing private-speech interests. The Court neither compels a new forum nor deletes the religious filters to create a broader statutory access entitlement. State-law construction and the precise severed decree remain below. Scalia and Thomas agree with the private-expression limit but would remove the retained restraint too.

##### V-A. Actual adequate representation defeats intervention as of right here

**Rule and authority:** Under Rule 24(a)(2), a timely applicant with a protectable interest potentially impaired by litigation is entitled to intervene if existing representation may be inadequate; a shared desired outcome or the State's official identity does not conclusively establish adequacy. All eight Associates join Part V-A and hold the actual representation adequate here, affirming denial without deciding unnecessary alternative elements.

**Controlling explanation:** Trbovich supplies the minimal burden for showing possible inadequacy and recognizes that governmental and private interests may differ. The Attorney General here actually advanced the proposed intervenors' private-expression interests, and they identify no relevant interest omitted from this statutory-validity controversy. The required narrowed decree protects those independent interests. Their private status alone therefore does not establish a representational gap on this record. Amicus participation could not replace an otherwise established right to intervene. Materially changed interests may be considered on a proper later application; this judgment orders neither a renewed hearing nor automatic future intervention.

##### V-B. A common question permits but does not compel permissive intervention

**Rule and authority:** Rule 24(b) permits intervention where its common-question condition is met, subject to the court's discretion and consideration of undue delay or prejudice to the original parties; it does not require admission merely because participation would be relevant. All eight Associates join Part V-B and affirm the denial as within discretion on this record.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court's duplication and delay assessment is permissible in this narrow statutory proceeding. The opportunity to make amicus submissions helps explain why excluding duplicative party participation caused no demonstrated prejudice, but does not defeat intervention as of right. The Court adopts neither an absolute rule against applicants without novel legal arguments nor an unreviewable discretion or universal plain-error requirement. Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without adding a ground.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Applied its controlling concrete-injury, causation and redressability requirements to imminent personal exposure; no nonmajority redressability rationale becomes law.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Applied both independently sufficient majorities, with their distinct joins, to official school worship and pressure. Student delivery creates no categorical graduation exception.
- **Capitol Square Review Board v. Pinette:** Preserved protection for independently attributable private religious access; neither a forum label nor a disclaimer settles every setting.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio:** Applied contextual attribution; the surrounding governmental role remains material.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors:** Distinguished neutral assistance to independently edited private expression from official devotional organization.
- **Lamb's Chapel and Good News Club:** Preserved genuine private religious expression without converting every school event into an opened private forum.
- **Brown v. Polk County:** Applied the distinction between lawful government control and overbroad suppression of independent religious expression, without importing employment burdens into school-prayer adjudication.
- **Trbovich v. United Mine Workers:** Applied its minimal possible-inadequacy burden and individualized representation inquiry; no conclusive presumption of governmental adequacy is adopted.
- **Jones II:** Rejected the lower-court graduation criteria as a categorical safe harbor inconsistent with governing Lee; lawful private graduation speech remains distinct.

#### Law After Decision

The school-religion holdings apply existing sponsorship and pressure prohibitions while clarifying that student delivery and graduation do not create categorical exceptions. Both grounds control independently. Genuine private expression and independently lawful provisions remain protected. The intervention holdings apply ordinary Rule 24 to actual representation and discretionary case management, establishing no universal entitlement or governmental-adequacy rule.

### 1997-03-31 — Robinson v. City of Edmond; lower-court Nos. 94-6237 and 95-6008.

Authority: [Record](../records/Robinson_v_City_of_Edmond_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. A recurring personal municipal communication supplies standing here

**Rule and authority:** A person who actually and repeatedly receives the City's own allegedly sectarian civic identification may seek prospective relief against that continuing use when the asserted personal injury is attributable to the City and redressable by the requested relief. All eight Associate Justices join Part I; Stone-Zsela supports reaching the merits without adding a ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires concrete personal injury, causation, redressability and proof appropriate to the stage of litigation; Valley Forge excludes an abstract demand for constitutional compliance. Feldman's supported recurring receipt of the cross-bearing City water bill, together with his direct encounter and personal objection, provides the required individual relationship to the continuing use. His 1988 encounter alone would not establish present prospective injury. Residence, work, taxation, general awareness and the City's decision not to contest standing are not substitutes for that relationship. The filed account of monthly receipt is accepted as an accurate account of this litigated record, considered with the actual posttrial findings and established continuing municipal use; it is not a new sworn exhibit. The Court assigns no unnamed lower finding of changed behavior to Feldman and imposes no universal avoidance-expense requirement. Because his claim supports the shared prospective relief, the Court need not separately decide the other three merits plaintiffs' standing for that same relief or any distinct personal remedy. Orza's dismissed merits claim is not before the Court.

##### II. The adopted municipal seal is the City's own expression

**Rule and authority:** A symbol selected and adopted as the municipality's official seal and used to authenticate its own communications, personnel and property is attributable to the municipality, even if a private person originally designed it. Part II has all nine joins; attribution alone does not establish that every governmental religious acknowledgment is unconstitutional.

**Controlling explanation:** Pinette and Chabad distinguish official adoption from private religious expression admitted through neutral access; Rosenberger protects neutral support for independently edited private speech. Those protections do not convert the City's own identifier into a private message. Edmond selected the seal through its authorized design process, registered it for municipal services and uses it in ordinary official business. No neutral forum, independent speaker or disclaimer separates that continuing civic identification from the City. The actual adopted function, rather than the designer's private status or beliefs, resolves attribution. The constitutional character of that expression requires the further inquiries below.

##### III. Context establishes an impermissible official religious affiliation

**Rule and authority:** Government violates the Establishment Clause when the whole context of its own continuing civic expression identifies it with a particular faith; a secular historical purpose and surrounding secular images do not by themselves cure that effect. Part III controls through Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon supplies the independent inquiry into religious effect, and Allegheny requires consideration of the expression's actual context. The unmistakable Christian cross occupies its own identifying quadrant in the seal carried throughout the City's official operations. The Court considers the composite design, its history, municipal uses and absence of church control. Assuming the district court's historical-purpose finding, the other three quadrants do not make this religious identifier neutral, and age is neither a constitutional safe harbor nor proof of affiliation by itself. Favorable witnesses' reactions do not displace that contextual inquiry. The rule requires neither a majority-offense poll nor coercion; it also creates no automatic rule against an isolated graphic regardless of setting. Purpose and entanglement need not be independently decided. No new credibility finding or universal rule for reviewing constitutional facts supplies the result.

##### IV. Making a faith's badge the City's permanent civil identifier is independently forbidden

**Rule and authority:** A city may not deliberately and pervasively use a recognizable symbol of a particular faith as a permanent identifying part of its own badge of present civil authority. Part IV has all nine direct joins and independently supports the judgment on the combined adoption, institutional-identification and continuing-use facts established here.

**Controlling explanation:** Kennedy's separate writing in Allegheny identifies a boundary between permissible acknowledgment and permanent official religious identification. The Court now directly adopts that bounded distinction on the institutional-use facts here. Edmond crosses that boundary by placing this freestanding Christian cross in its selected civic seal and carrying the seal through its ordinary official business. This is the government's own institutional identity, rather than another speaker's religious message or an exhibit about a particular historical artifact. The conclusion does not require a proved evangelical motive, church control, an added proselytizing inscription, a compulsory profession, denial of a benefit or visual dominance over every secular quadrant. It does require the actual combination present here; government ownership or religious content alone is insufficient. Religious place names, specific historical buildings, inherited coats of arms, holiday acknowledgments, historical exhibits and private symbols retain their own contexts. The Court announces no categorical approval or invalidation of them. This ground has nine direct votes; its authority does not depend on combining incompatible rationales.

##### V. Relief must end the present official use and preserve the distinction from history

**Rule and authority:** Prospective relief must end the unconstitutional use of the cross-bearing seal as the City's current official identifier, with a reasonable implementation period fixed below; accurate contextual preservation of historical records and displays remains permissible. All nine join PartV.

**Controlling explanation:** The violation concerns the City's continuing institutional identification. An order directed to that use remedies it without rewriting history or suppressing private expression. The district court may set the practical implementation period, but longevity does not authorize continued official adoption without end. The Court supplies no replacement design and commands no destruction of artifacts. Personal liability, immunity, nominal damages, unappealed Free Exercise and state-law claims, the production dispute and independent fees or costs remain outside this merits judgment. Existing proceedings for appropriate relief and fees may continue without a new fee award from this Court.

### 1997-03-31 — United States v. Lanier; No. 95-1717.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Lanier_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins. Each following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Fair warning turns on the law when the official acted

**Rule:** Section 242, alone or as authoritatively construed, must make the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acts; a novel or unforeseeable criminal application is forbidden. Willful bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws supplies the statute's willfulness and definite-right limitations. Vagueness, lenity and the prohibition on unexpected retrospective judicial enlargement protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. The courts interpret Congress's criminal enactment rather than create common-law crimes. The reprehensibility or state criminality of the alleged conduct does not substitute for identification of a federal right and adequate notice of its application. Current decisions govern the reviewing court's legal method at their actual scopes; a decision issued after the conduct cannot itself supply earlier notice.

##### Relevant authority is not confined to Supreme Court decisions

**Rule:** Fair warning under §242 may be supplied by the relevant pre-existing legal landscape, including lower-court authority; it does not categorically require a Supreme Court decision on the particular application. Neither one lower-court decision nor a fixed numerical consensus is automatically sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws did not prescribe the Sixth Circuit's Supreme-Court-only restriction. Elder supplies the examination of all relevant authority in assessing clear constitutional law; actual conflicts, source authority and their relation to the charged acts matter. Excluding a whole relevant class of decisions can wrongly convert established law into unknowable law. Conversely, source breadth does not eliminate the demand for sufficient clarity in the particular context.

##### Obvious applications need no factual twin

**Rule:** Existing constitutional law can give fair warning of an obviously covered application without an earlier case involving fundamentally similar facts. A broad invocation of liberty or bodily security is insufficient where the particular application remains unsettled, including where an earlier decision expressly leaves it open.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson supplies a particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness, not a demand for repetition of every factual detail. Officials cannot insist upon a decision matching their office, victim and precise method when established law applies with obvious clarity. At the same time, Elder and the actual Pinder and Uhlrig decisions preserve attention to unsettled applications and personal responsibility. Those distinctions prevent an abstract right from automatically resolving each charged assault, official nexus or notice question. The Court rejects the categorical similarity prerequisite while leaving those count-specific determinations below.

##### The same clarity protection leaves criminal elements intact

**Rule:** Section 242 fair warning and civil clearly-established-law analysis use the same legal-clarity threshold: constitutional unlawfulness must be apparent in light of pre-existing law. That common protection supplies neither automatic criminal liability nor a substitute for constitutional deprivation, action under color of law, criminal willfulness, the Government's criminal burden of proof and every other statutory element.

**Controlling explanation:** Due process does not demand a categorically clearer legal rule in the criminal case than the clarity protection furnished in the civil inquiry. But a conclusion about civil damages immunity cannot itself convict anyone. Screws's willfulness requirement performs separate work, and judicial title alone does not establish that each act occurred under color of state law. The Sixth Circuit must keep legal notice and proof of each criminal predicate distinct, rather than use either to erase the other.

##### Review the counts without the rejected legal shortcuts

**Rule:** The appellate court must reconsider the case without a Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite and without categorical exclusion of due-process rights or direct official assaults outside custody from §242. The judgment resolves no count's ultimate constitutional basis, fair warning, official nexus, proof or sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Screws and United States v. Price foreclose excluding rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process wholesale. DeShaney's limits on affirmative protection against private violence do not immunize an official's own assault merely because the victim was outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendment. Actual Collins, Pinder, Uhlrig and Nabozny preserve the distinction among personal official wrongdoing, omitted protection and supervisory attribution. These are legal directions for review, not new findings of assault, automatic restoration of the verdicts, a universal constitutional-assault formula or an order for a new evidentiary hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Screws v. United States:** Applied definite-right and willfulness protections; rejected a supposed Supreme-Court-only source restriction and categorical exclusion of due-process rights.
- **United States v. Price:** Preserved §242's reach to protected due-process rights without deciding the elements of each charged count.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied particularized apparent unlawfulness; no factual-twin requirement follows.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Applied consideration of relevant existing authority while preserving temporal limits.
- **Pinder v. Johnson and Uhlrig v. Harder:** Preserved their particularized notice, unsettled-application and personal-attribution boundaries; later holdings cannot create earlier notice.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District and Nabozny v. Podlesny:** Kept constitutional wrongdoing, personal participation and supervisory theories distinct; their later dates cannot establish notice for earlier conduct.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserved the difference between a constitutional deprivation and every injurious official act; no broad federal tort rule is created.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Distinguished limits on protection from private violence from the official's own asserted assault.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied the specific-provision rule when a particular Amendment governs, without treating the Fourth and Eighth Amendments as exhaustive of every abusive official act.

Section 242 has no Supreme-Court-only or factual-twin prerequisite. Pre-existing law must make the particular constitutional unlawfulness apparent, using the same legal-clarity protection as clearly-established-law analysis while preserving every distinct criminal element and burden. The Court creates no new substantive right or count-specific liability holding.

### 1997-04-21 — Kadic v. Karadzic; Doe v. Karadzic; Second Circuit Nos. 94-9035 and 94-9069.

Authority: [Record](../records/Kadic_and_Doe_v_Karadzic_merits_1997-04-21.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Jurisdiction and the domestic action are separate questions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350 gives the district court jurisdiction over these substantial allegations by aliens of torts violating the law of nations; it imposes no universal official-action requirement on norms that bind private individuals. A federal forum, an applicable international norm, a domestic civil action, this defendant's responsibility, and authority for the requested remedy remain separate requirements.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls. Unless a different coalition is specified below, Parts IV–VI have that same nine-Justice support; Parts II–III have the seven-Justice coalition identified in the opinion table.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Abebe-Jira's separation of jurisdiction from liability. The complaints allege international wrongs, rather than merely an ordinary domestic assault involving foreign persons. Their asserted source of civil liability presents a substantive question within that jurisdiction. A norm's personal scope must be determined from that norm: an official-action requirement attached to one prohibition cannot be transferred to another that expressly binds individuals. Suter supplies the distinction between an enforceable obligation and an action; neither that distinction nor an eventual failure on the merits defeats jurisdiction over this pleaded category. The allegations do not establish that a plaintiff has proved every required element.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's forum/action distinction is applied; Suter's source-specific distinction between obligation and action remains effective.

##### II. A confined civil action reaches personally committed or directed genocide

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350's reception of law-of-nations tort adjudication supports a domestic civil action against an individual for personally perpetrating, planning, or directing genocide, whether or not that individual acted under state authority. The plaintiff must establish intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group as such, through killing members, causing serious bodily or mental harm, deliberately imposing conditions calculated to bring about the group's physical destruction in whole or in part, imposing measures intended to prevent births, or forcibly transferring children to another group, together with the defendant's qualifying responsibility, causation, and a lawful basis for relief.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas accept the norm's private coverage but reject this additional domestic action.

**Controlling explanation:** Abebe-Jira recognizes the particular statutory reception of a determinate personal tort while reserving private violence. The Court now resolves that reservation for genocide. Article IV of the Genocide Convention expressly subjects private individuals to its prohibition, and Article II supplies the defined destructive acts and group-destruction intent. These provisions identify the obligatory norm; the distinct domestic source is the received-action construction of §1350. The Convention and the criminal implementation chapter are not themselves treated as civil causes. Section 1092's disclaimer of an enforceable substantive or procedural right created by that criminal chapter remains fully effective. It does not repeal this independently received action, and the criminal statute's territorial and nationality limits govern prosecution rather than silently amending §1350. The contrary argument correctly demands a domestic source but gives insufficient effect to the particular reception function already recognized in Abebe-Jira. The alleged personal planning and direction of a campaign against protected groups suffice to defeat categorical dismissal. Ethnic cleansing, rape, forced pregnancy, and murder must still satisfy the defined act and specific intent; their labels prove neither. No universal action for every international norm or liability from command title follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira is extended to this separately defined private wrong, while its official-torture holding remains intact. Atherton's rejection of common law based merely on a general federal interest is preserved. Central Bank's confined received-action method supplies an analogy, not an international aiding-and-abetting standard.

##### III. The pleaded core war crimes do not require a recognized state actor

**Holding and operative rule:** The same confined received domestic action reaches personally perpetrated, planned, or directed deliberate killing, torture, and grave sexual violence against protected persons as core war crimes, without requiring that the defendant act for a diplomatically recognized state. Each claim requires the relevant armed conflict, protected victim, prohibited act, connection between the act and the conflict, personal responsibility, causation, and independently supported relief; the Court recognizes no civil action for every breach of a treaty provision.

**Authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by the same seven Justices as Part II. Scalia and Thomas accept the individual international obligations but reject the additional received civil action.

**Operative norm and limits:** Common Article 3 binds each party to a noninternational armed conflict occurring in the territory of a High Contracting Party. It protects persons taking no active part in hostilities, including members of armed forces who have laid down their arms and persons out of combat through sickness, wounds, detention, or any other cause. It requires humane treatment without adverse distinction based on race, colour, religion or faith, sex, birth, wealth, or similar criteria. At any time and place it prohibits, against those persons, violence to life and person, especially murder, mutilation, cruel treatment and torture; hostage-taking; outrages upon personal dignity, especially humiliating and degrading treatment; and sentences or executions without a previous judgment by a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees. Its further commands and permissions remain distinct: the wounded and sick must be collected and cared for; an impartial humanitarian body may offer services; the parties should endeavour to put other Convention provisions into force through special agreements; application does not affect the parties' legal status. The civil holding concerns the pleaded core wrongs, not automatic enforcement of every one of these clauses.

**Controlling explanation:** Common Article 3 applies to each party to the defined conflict. A categorical recognized-state requirement would defeat that individual protection. Abebe-Jira's particular reception rationale supplies the domestic source; neither the existence of a treaty obligation nor the seriousness of an alleged injury alone creates a civil action. Yamashita confirms individual duties in war, but its military adjudication does not supply this domestic civil action or decide an absent commander's liability. The complaints' allegations of personal planning and direction permit proceedings on the defined claims. They do not establish protected status, the conflict connection, any defendant's responsibility, or damages. The Court does not determine Protocol II's customary status, decide alternative international-conflict theories, adopt a comprehensive command-responsibility rule, or transplant Central Bank's securities-assistance standard. These reservations preserve questions unnecessary to the pleaded deliberate-conduct ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's received-action rationale is extended at this confined scope. Yamashita is applied only for individual international obligations, preserving the difference between military prosecution and civil liability. Central Bank remains confined to its securities setting.

##### IV. Official torture and the enacted TVPA route retain their separate conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** Abebe-Jira's action remains available for deliberate personal participation in or supervision of severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment, or intimidation; ordinary private torture or killing outside a proved genocide or war-crime setting requires an independently authorized route. The Torture Victim Protection Act separately authorizes damages against an individual who, under actual or apparent authority or color of law of a foreign nation, subjects an individual to the statute's defined torture or extrajudicial killing, subject to its beneficiaries, exhaustion requirement, ten-year limit, definitions, and exclusions stated here.

**Authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion has nine direct joins. Scalia and Thomas apply the controlling official-torture holding without withdrawing their disagreement with its source; they independently accept the TVPA's enacted action.

**Statutory conditions:** Torture damages run to the injured individual; killing damages run to the individual's legal representative or a person entitled to claim in a wrongful-death action. The court shall decline to hear a claim when adequate and available remedies in the place where the conduct occurred have not been exhausted. Suit must commence within ten years after the cause arose. Extrajudicial killing means a deliberated killing not authorized by a previous judgment of a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees; it excludes a killing lawfully carried out under foreign national authority under international law. Torture requires intentionally inflicted severe physical or mental pain or suffering against a person in the offender's custody or physical control, excluding pain arising only from, inherent in, or incidental to lawful sanctions. Its purposes include obtaining information or a confession from the victim or a third person; punishing the victim for an act the victim or a third person committed or is suspected of committing; intimidating or coercing the victim or a third person; and discrimination of any kind. Mental pain means prolonged mental harm caused by intentional or threatened severe physical pain; administration, application, or threatened use of mind-altering substances or procedures calculated to disrupt profoundly the senses or personality; threatened imminent death; or a threat that another person imminently will suffer death, severe physical pain or suffering, or those substances or procedures.

**Controlling explanation:** An enacted cause remains distinct from a received action. The TVPA permits these claims to be considered under its actual words; neither a generalized allegation of cruelty nor inability to identify a better forum establishes its statutory conditions. Its definitions do not automatically rewrite every element of Abebe-Jira's separate action. Where torture or killing also satisfies the distinct genocide or war-crime norm, Parts II–III govern that additional route; ordinary private violence acquires no federal cause merely from its gravity. Section 1331 supplies federal-question jurisdiction for the enacted TVPA claim, but the Court does not decide whether it supplies a universal customary-law action. Nor is a further independent ATS action for ordinary official execution necessary to the disposition. The absence of a finding about exhaustion, timeliness, or a defined element requires claim-specific adjudication, not assumed compliance or forfeiture.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's official-torture action and limits are applied without alteration. Suter's separation of statutory obligation, action, and relief is preserved.

##### V. Nonrecognition alone does not defeat an adequately alleged official connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Diplomatic nonrecognition does not categorically foreclose proof of governmental attributes or concerted action with an existing foreign state where the applicable norm requires official action. The TVPA still requires actual or apparent authority or color of law attributable to a foreign nation; a claimed title or mere semblance of authority is insufficient by itself.

**Authority:** Part V of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court's blanket approach failed to address the alternative allegations of concerted action with Yugoslavia and Serbia. The plaintiffs may also attempt to establish the alleged government's population, territory, governmental organization, and capacity for relations. Allowing proof does not diplomatically recognize a government or establish its attributes. Lugar supplies a useful analogy concerning joint action with government, but does not transfer every §1983 rule into international law or the TVPA. The operative source must establish the necessary official connection in each claim. Neither an alleged presidency nor the Court's rejection of categorical dismissal supplies that proof.

**Precedent treatment:** Lugar's concerted-action analysis supplies a bounded analogy; its domestic §1983 framework is not adopted wholesale. Abebe-Jira's official-authority requirement remains effective for its own norm.

##### VI. The asserted foreign-affairs objection does not require dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** These defined claims against an individual are not categorically nonjusticiable merely because they arise during a foreign civil war or concern a political leader. Dismissal as a political question requires an identified constitutional commitment, absence of manageable judicial standards, or other concrete obstacle under Baker; none is established by this record.

**Authority:** Part VI of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by all other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires examination of the issue the court must actually decide. The Court can apply defined personal-tort elements without selecting Bosnia's government, fixing borders, or directing negotiations. Abebe-Jira applies that distinction to foreign individual misconduct, while Nixon concerns a particular Senate impeachment procedure and supplies no categorical foreign-affairs bar. The published United States position expressly disclaims a political-question obstacle; it reinforces, rather than replaces, the independent judicial inquiry. No present executive recognition or immunity determination in this defendant's favor is established. Act of state was not asserted below and is not decided. The absence of an established suitable alternative forum on this record neither creates a worldwide forum duty nor resolves any properly supported later forum contention.

**Precedent treatment:** Baker and Abebe-Jira are applied. Nixon's issue-specific nonjusticiability holding remains confined to its actual constitutional and procedural setting.

##### VII. Invitee status supplies no general immunity; particular service questions remain

**Holding and operative rule:** The United Nations Headquarters Agreement gives these invitees no general immunity from suit or from the reported service outside the headquarters district: §9's consent requirement for service inside that district, §15's privileges for designated member-state representatives, and §11's transit protections do not establish that asserted immunity. Kadic's court-authorized delivery through a security officer and admitted receipt permit continuation; Doe's intercepted hotel-lobby delivery, disputed distance, and remaining service issues require their own adjudication.

**Authority:** Part VII's rejection of general invitee immunity is joined by all nine. Its plaintiff-specific service application is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition without joining this additional service rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Agreement differentiates the headquarters district, designated representatives, and travel to the United Nations. It does not convert every invitee into a diplomat immune from civil proceedings. The State Department letter reports no invitee immunity. A narrowly presented question about direct airport-to-headquarters transit remains open because the reported service circumstances do not present it. The two plaintiffs' delivery records cannot be collapsed: acknowledged receipt under an alternate-service order in Kadic differs from the contested delivery in Doe. Rejection of a general immunity defense does not adjudicate every ordinary service or personal-jurisdiction objection. No general exception to sovereign or diplomatic immunity for international torts is announced.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's preservation of independently supported immunity questions remains intact; neither its justiciability holding nor today's decision displaces immunity rules not presented here.

##### VIII. Affirmance restores proceedings, not liability or an award

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's reversal of the blanket dismissal is affirmed, and both actions return for claim-specific service, factual, liability, and remedial adjudication under the surviving federal routes. Supplemental claims may receive renewed consideration because federal claims survive, but neither an exercise of supplemental jurisdiction nor any state-law judgment follows automatically.

**Authority:** The affirmance and full scope of surviving actions have seven judgment votes, with Scalia and Thomas dissenting only as to the new actions in Parts II–III. All nine agree on the ordinary proof and remedial limits; all eight Associates join the supplemental-claim rationale, while Stone-Zsela joins that disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The pleaded conduct suffices to defeat the categorical grounds on which dismissal rested. Compensation still requires an authorized action, personal responsibility, causation, and proved loss. Abebe-Jira's punitive rule retains its established scope for proved official torture and is not automatically extended to every additional category. Franklin's appropriate-relief principle presupposes an action and left punitive authority open. Any additional relief needs its own legal source and predicate facts. The Court awards no damages, punishment, fees, class certification, or injunction, and authorizes no prospective supervision of a foreign government. Supplemental jurisdiction is reconsidered under its ordinary limits; survival of a federal claim is not an instruction to enter judgment on a state claim. The remand preserves proof and defenses without reviving categorical grounds rejected here.

**Precedent treatment:** Abebe-Jira's proved-torture remedy rules remain within their original scope. Franklin preserves the action/remedy distinction and supplies no independent punitive authorization.

- Abebe-Jira v. Negewo (January 14, 1997): its forum/action distinction, official-torture action, source-specific reception rationale, and concrete political-question inquiry remain controlling. The received action is extended only to the defined personal genocide and core-war-crime wrongs; its existing remedy rules are not extended automatically.
- Suter v. Artist M.: obligation, action, and relief remain separate; its administrative-plan holding supplies no categorical prohibition of this received tort action.

### 1997-04-28 — Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno; Third Circuit No. 95-7137; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Pic_A_State_Pennsylvania_v_Reno_merits_1997-04-28.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The stopped business presents a concrete and ripe challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A business presents a concrete, ripe pre-enforcement challenge when a criminal statute directly reaches its existing activity, it has stopped that activity but intends to resume, serious penalties create a sufficiently real enforcement risk, and the requested relief would remove that present federal constraint. Those conditions are met here; neither an actual prosecution nor resolution of every independent state-law restriction is required.

**Authority:** Kennedy's unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies all three holdings at their stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories supplies the inquiry into fitness for judicial decision and hardship from withholding review. Pic-A-State stopped the very interstate procurement operation the amendment targeted; its reported intent to resume and exposure to substantial criminal sanctions establish a present injury. Bennett supplies personal injury, traceability and likely redress at the litigation stage, not automatic standing for every economically affected business. The Government's statement that prosecution is unlikely bears on risk but does not repudiate enforcement. Relief would remove an actual federal barrier even if Pennsylvania law separately remained relevant. The predominantly legal commerce question needs no prosecution to sharpen it, while withholding review would require continued cessation or exposure to punishment. The absence of a disavowal is not sufficient by itself in every case. Younger supplies no abstention ground where no state prosecution is pending. The prospective constitutional challenge against the enforcing Attorney General is a proper channel; standing does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

##### II. Congress may prohibit the actual interstate procurement transmission

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit a procurement business's knowing interstate or foreign transmission of information to obtain, for a person in one State, a ticket, chance, share or interest in a lottery, gift enterprise or similar scheme conducted by another State. The challenged commercial transmission is interstate commerce itself even when the physical ticket remains in the conducting State; the prohibition remains subject to the agreement exception and separate §1307 exemptions stated below.

**Controlling explanation:** Champion v. Ames establishes congressional power to prohibit interstate commerce in lottery interests; Gibbons recognizes interstate commercial intercourse beyond the movement of tangible paper. Pic-A-State's computer communications accomplish the economic procurement transaction across state lines. The amendment closes that commercial route around the existing physical-ticket prohibition. Robertson confirms that an actual interstate business operation needs no independently substantial effect from each transaction. Lopez requires the regulated class's connection to commerce among the States to be identified before aggregation or substantiality performs constitutional work, and it rejects a remote chain from local possession through social costs; it does not displace direct regulation of interstate commerce itself, where no aggregation or substantial-effects analysis is required. Harris, Hale and American Life League likewise preserve genuine economic-class and direct-market grounds. Here the enacted knowing-transmission and procurement-business elements themselves supply the connection, without a judicially added element or an aggregation holding. Hodel permits rational means within that established power: closing the procurement gap and accommodating different state lottery choices meet that requirement. Revenue preservation alone would not establish federal power over local conduct. Congress need not prohibit every lottery, prove a particular revenue loss, or make transaction-specific findings to regulate this interstate business.

##### III. The agreement exception supplies bounded congressional authorization

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1301 permits the otherwise covered procurement business when an agreement between the States in question or appropriate authorities of those States permits it. That express federal choice removes dormant Commerce Clause restraints on the particular state choices it authorizes, while leaving independent constitutional restraints intact.

**Controlling explanation:** Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin recognizes congressional power to authorize state burdens that the dormant Commerce Clause would otherwise forbid, and Northeast Bancorp requires the authorization to be clear. The agreement condition states the permitted category expressly. It accommodates state choices within Congress's independently valid regulation of interstate procurement; state permission for the underlying lottery does not displace the federal condition. Tracy separated ordinary economic rationality from discrimination analysis and reserved congressional authorization, so it does not supply a broader permission here. No qualifying agreement is established for Pic-A-State. The Court neither validates every Pennsylvania lottery restriction nor transfers unlimited federal legislative power to the States. The holding removes only the dormant implication and decides no independent First Amendment, equal-protection or nondelegation claim.

##### Coverage, distinct exceptions, and questions not reached

The procurement clause requires a person engaged in that business, a customer in one State, a covered scheme conducted by another State, and knowing interstate or foreign transmission for procurement. It does not prohibit every lottery communication or wholly local purchase. The separate physical-carriage and receipt clauses remain distinct. The penalty is a fine under title 18, imprisonment for no more than two years, or both.

Section 1307(a)(1) separately exempts advertisements, prize lists or other information about a state-authorized, state-conducted lottery when contained in a publication published in that State or another lottery State, or broadcast by a station licensed in such a State. Section 1307(a)(2) exempts such information about other schemes authorized or not prohibited in the conducting State when conducted by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or as a clearly occasional and ancillary commercial promotional activity. Section 1307(b) separately exempts transportation or mailing of equipment, tickets or materials concerning a state-authorized lottery to addresses within its conducting State, and materials designed for an authorized foreign lottery to addressees within that foreign country. State includes the States, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and United States territories or possessions; foreign country includes an empire, country, dominion, colony, protectorate or subdivision other than the United States and its territories or possessions. The subsection-(b) lottery definition concerns pooled ticket-or-chance proceeds allocated by chance and excludes sporting-event bets or wagers. A nonprofit is an organization that would qualify as tax exempt under Internal Revenue Code §501. No fact places this business within an exemption; these provisions supply no blanket exemption for every lawful state lottery. No rule governs hypothetical wholly local transmissions or validates an interstate element unrelated to the commercial transaction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Abbott Laboratories is applied to this predominantly legal challenge and the hardship of cessation or criminal exposure.
- Bennett v. Spear is applied for personal injury, traceability and likely redress; its distinct ESA and APA routes are not imported.
- Younger v. Harris does not require abstention on this record without a pending state prosecution.
- Champion v. Ames supports Congress's authority to prohibit interstate lottery commerce.
- Gibbons v. Ogden supports regulation of interstate commercial intercourse beyond tangible paper, applied here to procurement information.
- Lopez is applied at its first step: the knowing interstate procurement transmission is interstate commerce itself, so the connection is identified without a remote social-cost chain and no aggregation or substantial-effects analysis is required.
- Harris retains its economic vehicle-taking-class ground, distinct from a remote social-cost chain.
- Bishop/Stokes retains its application of that economic-class rule to the two charged vehicle takings.
- Hale retains its ground for possession integral to the direct transfer-and-stock regime.
- Robertson supports evaluating the enterprise's actual interstate operation without a separately substantial effect from each transaction.
- American Life League retains its direct-interference-with-the-medical-market ground; this case supplies no exhaustive commerce formula.
- Hodel supports rational statutory means after the commercial connection is established.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin permits congressional authorization of state burdens otherwise forbidden by the dormant Commerce Clause, without power to remove independent constitutional restraints.
- Northeast Bancorp requires clear congressional authorization, supplied here by the express agreement condition.
- General Motors Corp. v. Tracy remains a distinct discrimination and rationality decision; its reserved authorization question is answered here only for this enacted agreement structure.

#### Law After Decision

The Interstate Wagering Amendment remains enforceable against covered interstate procurement businesses, subject to its agreement exception and §1307's different exemptions. Actual interstate commercial communications can support Congress's power without an aggregation theory or physical movement of the purchased paper. Congress's express authorization removes the dormant Commerce Clause objection only within the authorized category. The Court does not decide ordinary local lottery administration or independent constitutional claims.
