**An actual prior simulation decision addresses the closest issue.** *Kimberlin v. Quinlan* unanimously rejected a categorical direct-evidence requirement in motive-dependent constitutional claims. Legally competent circumstantial proof remains available, alongside specific nonconclusory support, ordinary procedural burdens, tailored discovery, and the clearly-established-law defense. It did not abolish every heightened specificity rule or adopt a new clear-and-convincing burden. Thus the district court's direct-only rationale cannot govern this record, but the new burden question still requires decision.

*Leatherman v. Tarrant County*—Stone's unanimous municipal-liability opinion—holds that courts cannot impose an extra factual-particularity category beyond the Federal Rules for municipal §1983 complaints. Its express reservation of individual-officer immunity pleading must be preserved. It supplies a reasoned analogy concerning judicial authority to rewrite Rules 8 and 9; it does not already decide the reserved issue. Rule 56 remains a genuine-evidence screen and does not authorize credibility weighing.

**Motive, merits, and immunity are separate.** Governing Harlow/Anderson immunity is objective; an underlying retaliation cause can nevertheless require proof of prohibited purpose. Eliminating subjective malice from the defense did not erase motive from the constitutional wrong. Simulated *Settle v. Dickson County* applies the ordinary Mt. Healthy sequence: protected conduct with a substantial or motivating causal role, followed by the defendant's preponderance showing of the same decision without it. Its school context is distinct, but it rejects a merely possible lawful reason as automatically conclusive. *Kimberlin* preserves Pell/Turner prison limitations; identifying protected communication remains necessary.

**Procedural means.** Courts may resolve a dispositive legal defense early, insist on identification of the challenged acts, and tailor requests to relevant disputed elements. Requiring conclusive proof of an official's state of mind before allowing necessary, properly targeted discovery risks converting case management into a substantive evidentiary barrier. Conversely, unsupported conclusory allegations do not create a right to unlimited discovery. Existing rules and their stage-specific burdens must do the work.

## C. LITIGATION POSITIONS, LAWFUL PATHS, AND UNCERTAINTY

Crawford-El argues that §1983, the Constitution, and the Rules provide no special clear-and-convincing burden and that motive commonly must be inferred from information held by officials. Britton invokes protection from disruptive litigation, facially lawful property handling, and gaps in proof of personal retaliatory intent.

The Court may reject the heightened burden while preserving early legal rulings and targeted discovery; remand to apply ordinary standards to a supported claim; or uphold an independent properly preserved deficiency. A remand does not require trial, infer retaliatory motive, or reinstate unrelated claims. The Court must distinguish a lawful same-decision defense from a rule that any conceivable innocent explanation defeats motive evidence. Municipal attribution, conversion, and separate previously resolved constitutional theories retain their actual procedural status.
