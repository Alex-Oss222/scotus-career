## Standards and Tests selection: Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error

### Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error

**Governing question:** Constitutionally invalid aggravation in a weighing State, whether used directly by the judge or transmitted through a jury recommendation carrying legally required great weight.

**Current rule:** Remaining valid aggravators do not alone cure a constitutional weighing error, although an invalid weighing factor does not automatically negate death eligibility if valid eligibility findings remain. The state judicial system must provide genuine reweighing without the invalid factor where state law authorizes it, or constitutional harmless-error review proving beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not affect the sentence. Bare factor counting, proportionality review or a harmless-error label supplies neither cure; no particular verbal formula is indispensable if the substantive finding is actually made. A jury's inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction can infect the final death sentence when the judge must give the resulting recommendation great weight, even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. Correction must address that transmitted influence. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required. Neither automatic life imprisonment nor disturbance of guilt follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Distinct weighing error and eligibility, with lawful correction routes: Stringer v. Black, “A weighing error needs a constitutionally adequate cure”, March 9, 1992; Kennedy for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor.
- Actual judicial reliance, substantive cure and no magic-words requirement: Sochor v. Florida, “The judge's documented coldness reliance requires correction” and “The existing appellate explanation does not cure the error”, June 8, 1992; respectively the eight-Justice Part III-B-1 and six-Justice Parts III-B-2/IV, applying Stringer, Clemons, Parker and Chapman without changing their correction standard.
- A legally influential recommendation and correction of its transmitted effect: Espinosa v. Florida, “A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator” and “Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence”, June 29, 1992; per curiam joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Espinosa*, June 29, 1992, extending the rule to the recommendation's legally required influence. The earlier direct-weighing rule remains supplied by *Stringer*, March 9, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** On the harmless-error route the State bears Chapman's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. That requirement is distinct from authority to perform a genuine reweighing and from collateral-review gateways.

**Exceptions and limits:** States retain latitude in assigning sentencing roles, but must give constitutional guidance to an influential recommendation. No factor-specific juror finding is presumed, and constitutional permission for a kind of appellate cure does not establish that state law authorizes it. Independent preservation, default and habeas barriers remain intact.

**Related current rules:** “Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations” preserves the distinct inference boundary for a legally available but evidentially unsupported jury factor. “Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims” governs access to review of otherwise barred claims, not correction of an available claim's weighing error.

**Holdings navigation:** Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992) — Capital sentencing and habeas retroactivity; Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992) — Capital sentencing; Espinosa v. Florida, 505 U.S. 1079 (1992) — Capital sentencing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

### Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

**Governing question:** Whether an omitted Teague defense must be entertained when first raised in Supreme Court merits briefing.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. When the State omitted it below and in its certiorari opposition and first asserted it in merits briefing, the Court may decline consideration. The permission is discretionary, not a mandatory waiver rule. A consistently preserved subsidiary defense limiting the very constitutional rule and habeas relief under review presents the distinct fair-inclusion setting addressed in Caspari; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Discretion to decline the late nonjurisdictional defense: Schiro v. Farley — Late presentation of nonretroactivity, 1994-01-19. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens also agree with merits reach, but their agreement is unnecessary to the opinion's controlling authority.
- Preserved subsidiary-defense distinction: Caspari v. Bohlen — The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here, 1994-02-23. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Present operation effective:** Schiro v. Farley, 1994-01-19, for discretionary treatment of the late defense. Caspari, 1994-02-23, supplies the distinct preserved-defense comparison, not a later change to that discretion.

**Related current rules:** “Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review” supplies the question-presentation boundary. Neither decision changes Teague’s new-rule inquiry or either contemporary exception.

**Holdings navigation:** Late presentation of nonretroactivity; The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here.

## Standards and Tests selection: Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims

### Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims

**Governing question:** Review of successive, abusive or procedurally defaulted capital habeas claims without otherwise required cause and prejudice.

**Current rule:** The prisoner must show by clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the prisoner eligible for death under applicable state law. Examine the offense, required aggravating facts and every other necessary eligibility condition. Additional mitigation affecting only the discretionary selection of death does not satisfy this gateway. Opening the gateway permits consideration of an otherwise barred claim; it does not itself establish the claim or grant relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Eligibility-only scope, no-reasonable-juror inquiry and clear-and-convincing burden: Sawyer v. Whitley, “Capital innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred claims”, June 22, 1992; Kennedy's opinion joined by White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. Broader judgment concurrences supply no component.

**Present operation effective:** *Sawyer*, June 22, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary cause and prejudice, exhaustion, retroactivity, merits standards and proper factual-development rules remain distinct. Neither ordinary sentencing prejudice nor discretionary mitigation replaces this gateway. Freestanding innocence claims remain unresolved. No categorical exclusion of impeachment evidence follows from its insufficiency on Sawyer’s record.

**Holdings navigation:** Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333 (1992) — Habeas procedural gateways.

**Related current rules:** Crime innocence as a gateway to otherwise barred habeas claims states Schlup’s distinct January 23, 1995 offense-innocence inquiry; it does not change this death-eligibility quantum.

#### Espinosa v. Florida, 505 U.S. 1079 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7390.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

##### A legally influential recommendation cannot rest on an inadequately narrowed aggravator

**Holding:** When a weighing State requires its sentencing judge to give great weight to a jury's recommendation, an inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction to that jury can infect the final sentence even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. The especially wicked, evil, atrocious or cruel instruction given here lacks the guidance the Eighth Amendment requires; the judge's separate findings do not by themselves cure the recommendation's legal influence.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Material application:** The jury returned an 11–1 general death recommendation under the unelaborated instruction. The judge gave operative weight to four aggravators and found two mitigators. No factor-specific jury finding is inferred; the required legal weight of the recommendation supplies the indirect channel of error.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 (1980), Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 (1988), and Shell v. Mississippi, 498 U.S. 1 (1990): apply their requirement of meaningful guidance for a capital aggravating category; the instruction here is insufficiently narrowed.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992: applies the distinction between valid eligibility and constitutionally defective weighing; surviving valid aggravators alone do not remove the error.
- Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: applies its weighing-error rule while distinguishing its independent state preservation bar to a different jury claim and its separate factual-invalidity issue.
- Mills v. Maryland, 486 U.S. 367 (1988): applies the refusal to presume that jurors disregard an instruction's constitutionally defective route; no factor-specific verdict is invented.
- Walton v. Arizona, 497 U.S. 639 (1990): preserves genuine judicial sentencing and the presumption that judges apply legal narrowing; distinguishes a system in which a separate unguided recommendation carries required weight.
- Baldwin v. Alabama, 472 U.S. 372 (1985), and Spaziano v. Florida, 468 U.S. 447 (1984): preserve States' latitude in allocating sentencing responsibility; that latitude does not validate influential unguided weighing.
- Tedder v. State, 322 So.2d 908 (Fla. 1975), Smith v. State, 515 So.2d 182 (Fla. 1987), and Grossman v. State, 525 So.2d 833 (Fla. 1988): supply the state-law requirement to give great weight to jury recommendations of life or death.
- Smalley v. State, 546 So.2d 720 (Fla. 1989): rejects its premise that the jury's advisory designation alone makes this vague instruction constitutionally immaterial.

##### Correction must address the recommendation's influence on the death sentence

**Holding:** Reversal of the rejection of this sentencing claim requires further proceedings that remove the influence of the invalid jury recommendation through lawful reweighing or harmless-error review satisfying Chapman beyond a reasonable doubt. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required; valid remaining aggravators alone do not establish a cure, and neither an automatic life sentence nor disturbance of the guilt judgment follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven Justices adopt the defect and corrective requirements. White favors argument and casts no present merits vote; Scalia dissents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic reduction to life, retrial of guilt, adjudication of unrelated aggravators, factor-by-factor jury finding, or collateral-review gateway rule follows. The petition's severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions are not granted or adjudicated in this limited summary action; no separate denial of them is invented. No prior Supreme Court decision is overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court grants limited review and reverses the rejection of the sentencing claim. Remand requires lawful reweighing or Chapman review addressing the recommendation's influence, and new sentencing if no lawful cure is available. Guilt and the ungranted severance and mitigation/time/funding/proportionality questions remain outside the disposition; no separate denial or automatic life sentence follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 (1990): applies lawful appellate reweighing or harmless-error correction; merely counting valid aggravators does not cure the error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 (1967): applies harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; no lesser standard or burden on the defendant is created.
- Stringer v. Black, simulated March 9, 1992, and Sochor v. Florida, simulated June 8, 1992: apply actual cure requirements to the recommendation's transmitted influence; no unreasoned harmlessness label suffices.
- Parker v. Dugger, 498 U.S. 308 (1991): applies review grounded in the real sentencing record and mitigation, not an assumed alternative record.

#### Stringer v. Black, 503 U.S. 222 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-6616.
**Decided:** March 9, 1992.

##### Whether the Mississippi vague-aggravator claim seeks a new rule after February 19, 1985 finality

**Holding:** For a sentence final on February 19, 1985, Godfrey already prohibited the unguided heinousness aggravation later addressed in Maynard, and applying that protection to Mississippi’s weighing system does not create a new rule. Teague therefore does not bar consideration of this claim on the ground that Maynard or the corrective principles discussed in Clemons postdated finality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is confined to the old-rule application established by Godfrey for this finality date. No Teague exception or unresolved procedural-default defense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 — applied, not replaced: the claim seeks an old rule at the relevant finality date.
- Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied: both novelty of the later rule and novelty of its application must be considered.
- Godfrey v. Georgia, 446 U.S. 420 — applied: meaningful guidance was already required for the heinousness aggravator.
- Maynard v. Cartwright, 486 U.S. 356 — treated as applying Godfrey’s old rule to equivalent language.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied as identifying permissible correction, not a newly created protection in this setting.

##### What correction a constitutionally invalid aggravator requires in a capital weighing system

**Holding:** When a capital sentencer weighs a constitutionally invalid aggravating factor against mitigation, remaining valid aggravators do not by themselves cure the error. The state judicial system must supply genuine reweighing without the invalid factor, where authorized, or constitutional harmless-error review establishing that the invalid factor did not affect the sentence; the adequacy of any cure here remains for further proceedings.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; six Justices adopt this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Genuine authorized reweighing and constitutional harmlessness are distinct corrective routes. The latter places on the State the burden of proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. Remaining valid aggravators or bare proportionality review alone do not cure the error; death eligibility is not automatically negated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit’s Teague disposition is reversed and remanded for consideration of the preserved procedural-default defense and adequate state correction. If relief otherwise becomes warranted, a conditional writ may permit lawful resentencing or authorized appellate correction. No unconditional release, automatic life sentence, or finding that a particular state cure failed is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zant v. Stephens, 462 U.S. 862 — distinguished: its nonweighing holding does not automatically cure a weighing-system error.
- Barclay v. Florida, 463 U.S. 939 — distinguished: its actual review and state-law invalidity do not justify uncorrected constitutional aggravation.
- Clemons v. Mississippi, 494 U.S. 738 — applied: genuine appellate reweighing or constitutional harmlessness can correct a weighing error.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18 — applied to the harmless-error route: the State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 — distinguished: duplication in narrowing does not answer vague-factor weighting and cannot supply a prefinality reliance premise.

#### Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1500.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here

**Holding:** In this habeas case, Missouri's consistently asserted Teague defense is fairly included as a subsidiary limit on applying the very constitutional rule and granting the writ challenged by the petition, although the stated question names the double-jeopardy issue rather than Teague. The Court therefore considers that nonjurisdictional defense; preservation, disclosure in the petition's body or logical priority alone does not make every independent omitted question reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido: applied to distinguish subsidiary legal arguments from distinct questions; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate requirements.
- Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.: preserved; an omitted independent question does not become reviewable merely through notice, logical priority or ordinary economy. This confined same-rule habeas limitation is distinguished from its separate intervention determination.
- Schiro v. Farley: distinguished on presentation; the defense there first appeared at merits briefing after earlier omissions. Its discretionary treatment of a nonjurisdictional defense remains intact.

##### The requested extension was a new rule at finality

**Holding:** Bohlen's conviction and sentence became final on January 2, 1986, and the rule forbidding renewed proof of persistent-offender status in this noncapital proceeding was not dictated by the precedent then in force. Federal habeas relief therefore depends on a recognized Teague exception; the Court does not decide whether the proposed double-jeopardy protection should govern a case eligible for a decision on the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt the finality-specific new-rule determination, not a prospective rule permitting every noncapital sentencing rehearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applied to the date at which the state judgment became final and the distinction between dictated applications and new rules.
- Graham v. Collins: applied in its controlling form; a dictated application does not become new merely because the facts differ or courts disagree.
- Gilmore v. Taylor: applied to preserve the possibility of a genuinely new noncapital extension after substantive comparison.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; their extension to this noncapital status proceeding was not dictated at finality, and their ultimate reach is not decided.
- United States v. DiFrancesco: applied as prefinality support for distinguishing sentencing determinations from offense acquittals.
- Griffith v. Kentucky: used for the finality convention; direct review was exhausted and the certiorari period expired on January 2, with January 1 a legal holiday.
- United States v. Dixon: its retention of Grady remains in force but does not answer whether this sentencing-status proceeding has the necessary jeopardy character.

##### Neither Teague exception permits this writ

**Holding:** The proposed rule falls within neither the exception for rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, nor the exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Its protection against renewed litigation of offender status does not remove the robberies or these authorized sentences from lawful punishment and does not supply a watershed accuracy procedure; the habeas grant on this new rule must therefore be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices apply both exceptions as they exist at this decision's date. Neither exception is eliminated or prospectively narrowed out of existence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applies both contemporary exceptions after finding a new rule; neither is abolished.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applies the substantive exception's protection against punishment beyond the State's constitutional power; this claim does not satisfy it.
- Sawyer v. Smith: applies the distinction between an important procedural protection and the exceptional accuracy-and-fairness requirement.
- Bullington v. Missouri: preserved; refusing retroactive extension does not overrule its established capital-acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both contemporary Teague exceptions remain. The State's consistently presented defense is subsidiary here; preservation, mention in the petition body and logical priority do not automatically make an omitted independent question reviewable. The ultimate constitutional reach of double jeopardy to the noncapital status hearing is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas grant on this claim is reversed, 8–1, under Teague. No new State proof opportunity, replacement sentence or release deadline is prescribed, and unrelated properly preserved claims are not disturbed.

#### Gilmore v. Taylor, 508 U.S. 333 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1738.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### Falconer announces a new rule for this collateral proceeding

**Holding:** Falconer’s specific requirement governing the interaction of these murder and voluntary-manslaughter instructions was not dictated by precedent at Taylor’s finality and is therefore a new rule under Teague. The State’s concession of error under Falconer does not waive its preserved retroactivity objection, and this conclusion does not approve the charge on direct review or exclude all defense-instruction errors from due process.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288: finality-era dictated-by-precedent inquiry applied.
- Cupp v. Naughten, 414 U.S. 141: whole-charge fairness preserved but not treated as dictating this specific rule.
- Connecticut v. Johnson, 460 U.S. 73: plurality discussion of elemental presumptions distinguished; it is not treated as a controlling holding compelling Falconer.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370: later capital-instruction reasonable-likelihood analysis distinguished; its date does not itself supply a 1987 finality-era source.
- Patterson v. New York, 432 U.S. 197, and Martin v. Ohio, 480 U.S. 228: offense-element burdens distinguished from affirmative-defense allocations.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991: the whole-charge reasonable-likelihood holding preserved; it neither decides Falconer nor supplies a source existing at 1987 finality.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993: the capital-mitigation holding distinguished on its earlier capital authorities.

##### Neither then-existing Teague exception permits this new instructional rule

**Holding:** The Falconer rule regulates trial procedure rather than removing conduct or a class of persons from the State’s substantive power to punish, and it is not a watershed procedural rule essential to the fundamental accuracy and fairness of the criminal process. Neither exception recognized by Teague at this event’s date permits its retroactive use to obtain the reviewed habeas relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State preserved Teague despite its Falconer-error concession. Direct-review validity of the charge and harmlessness are not decided; no categorical exclusion of defense-instruction errors from due process is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the conditional habeas judgment; the writ cannot issue on this new procedural rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague, 489 U.S. 288: both the substantive and watershed exceptions applied in their event-date form.
- Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S. 227: the demanding watershed category applied; importance of a procedural protection alone is insufficient.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: the preserved-Doyle Chapman holding remains distinct; harmlessness is not reached here.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993: the absence of a valid reasonable-doubt verdict is a different instructional defect and does not bypass Teague in this case.

#### Gray v. Netherland, 518 U.S. 152

**Docket or dockets:** 95-6510
**Decided:** June 20, 1996

##### Did reopening the due-process claim also reopen the independently dismissed Brady ground?

**Holding:** The partial amendment reopening the identified Sorrell due-process claim while expressly preserving other dismissals did not reopen the independent Brady ground. That dismissal stays outside general availability or merits reconsideration, while related evidence remains usable to prove the distinct live preparation injury and its effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment. Dismissal left undisturbed, 9–0.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shared evidence does not merge claims or satisfy separate procedural predicates. Reported uncontested state-habeas factual availability does not resolve trial counsel's disputed knowledge. No new suppression, materiality, appellate-abandonment or comprehensive state-default finding is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kyles's collective lawful-use rule applies to available Brady claims and does not reopen dismissals. Picard, Anderson and Vasquez require claim-specific comparison; Coleman preserves established independent and adequate state claim defaults.

##### Is meaningful opportunity to answer this capital evidence a rule dictated at finality?

**Holding:** The established opportunity to deny or explain material capital-sentencing information is denied when supported State assurances and a materially different last-minute presentation deprive the defendant of a real opportunity to investigate and answer. This supported loss of protected function, rather than a fixed disclosure interval, invokes law dictated before October 5, 1987 finality and is not a new rule barred by Teague.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; six. O'Connor's join does not extend to the final violation, effect or remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal notice period, automatic exclusion or relief for mere surprise is created. No Teague exception is invoked or abolished. Separate affirmative-deception formulations still require their own proper presentation and legal predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner supplies the prefinality opportunity to respond; eventual public disclosure is not necessarily effective response. Weatherford's no-general-discovery rule remains. Graham supplies functional comparison, not new law inserted at finality; Teague and Caspari retain the dictated-at-finality inquiry and both contemporary exceptions for substantive restrictions and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness.

##### Are presentation, denial of opportunity and prejudicial effect established here?

**Holding:** The reopened claim fairly encompasses the preparation injury, and accepted existing findings establish denial of the protected opportunity and prejudice in selecting death. Neither absence of a formal continuance request nor surviving vileness eligibility defeats relief on this record; no newly recast deception or dismissed Brady claim must first succeed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five.

**Material application:** State Claim IX, the state appeal and federal Ground A presented delayed disclosure, impaired preparation and unfairness. Accepted evidence supplements that same injury. The prosecutor's statements-only assurance, reasonable reliance and last-minute photographs, police and medical-examiner evidence impaired investigation, impeachment, objections and response. Actual preparation objections and exclusion requests were made; the absent formal continuance motion establishes no feasible cure or adequate bar. The findings establish substantial probability of a different sentence, substantial injurious influence, and inability to find beyond reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal collateral error-effect standard is selected because relief follows under both competing approaches. No structural-error category, automatic exclusion of all uncharged-crime evidence, other-perpetrator finding or universal exhaustion of related theories is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Picard and Anderson require claim substance, not identical language; Vasquez permits supplementation without fundamental alteration while retaining its deliberate-withholding reservation. Keeney retains Townsend's fifth hearing circumstance for inadequately developed material facts of a properly presented claim, subject to Fay's knowing, considered choice by the prisoner to bypass state process. Ordinary counsel negligence or counsel's choice without prisoner participation is not automatically bypass. Established deliberate bypass permits refusal of the otherwise required hearing rather than requiring denial; Coleman remains distinct. Tuggle separates lawful eligibility from actual sentence selection. Brecht's preserved-Doyle and O'Neal's personal-intent holdings retain their limits; Chapman and Kotteakos supply the two satisfied competing effect inquiries without a general extension.

##### How must conditional sentencing relief be implemented?

**Holding:** The established violation and prejudicial effect require reversal of the adverse due-process disposition and restoration and implementation of conditional death-sentence relief. Before an operative writ, any genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory condition material to relief must be resolved; once satisfied or inapplicable, Virginia must provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable prospective District Court period or a lawful noncapital disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five. Reversed in part and remanded, 5–4, on this claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The established constitutional violation and effect are not returned for unrestricted reconsideration. Actually applicable amended §2254(d), state-fact-presumption or chapter 154 conditions require temporal and predicate analysis; §107(c)'s pending-case clause neither establishes Virginia qualification nor resolves chapter 153 transition. No express exhaustion waiver, statutory exception or satisfaction is presumed. The completed 1994 hearing differs from a new hearing; none is authorized or exempted categorically from §2254(e)(2). The reservation concerns actual legal conditions, not hypothetical impediments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The old 180-day clock is neither reinstated nor declared expired. No guilt retrial, acquittal, fixed replacement sentence, release from independent lawful custody, new Supreme Court stay or execution authorization follows. Independent Brady, Smallwood, investigator-assistance, guilt-sufficiency and ungranted vileness-instruction rulings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham and Simmons preserve sentence-only relief and unaffected guilt. Landgraf requires provision-specific temporal analysis; Keeney does not erase applicable later statutory restrictions. No general AEDPA temporal holding is made.
