## Standards and Tests selection: Reviewability of the Secretary's completed census determination

### Reviewability of the Secretary's completed census determination

**Governing question:** APA review of a completed population count followed by presidential transmission of apportionment figures.

**Current rule:** The Secretary's completed census determination is final agency action under §704 despite the President's later statutory transmission of the resulting apportionment. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit counting entirely to unreviewable discretion: the obligation to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice. General supervision and possible correction do not make this completed decision tentative; the prescribed transmission differs from a statutorily independent presidential policy judgment.

**Authority by component:**

- Finality and reviewability: Franklin v. Massachusetts, “The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable”, June 26, 1992; Stone's Part IV joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter.

**Present operation effective:** *Franklin*, June 26, 1992.

**Open question:** The President's APA status and susceptibility to coercive judicial relief are left undecided. This rule does not turn remaining agency discretion into entitlement to a selected outcome.

**Holdings navigation:** Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992) — Census determinations and judicial review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Review preclusion for the approved 1990 base-closure package

### Review preclusion for the approved 1990 base-closure package

**Governing question:** Whether implementation litigation may reopen the approved package under the 1990 base-closure statute.

**Current rule:** The 1990 base-closure statute’s integrated selection, recommendation, presidential approval and congressional-review process, with its constrained choices and strict deadlines, precludes an APA challenge to the approved closure selection through litigation nominally directed at implementation and antecedent selection defects. The holding is tied to that completed statutory package. It does not establish a categorical rule that every presidential recommendation is nonfinal, or resolve presidential APA status as a controlling rationale.

**Authority by component:**

- Scheme-specific preclusion of the completed selection challenge: Dalton v. Specter — The approved closure selection cannot be reopened through this implementation challenge, 1994-05-23. Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices. The statutory-preclusion ground alone commands the Court; a four-Justice concurrence on recommendation finality is not controlling.

**Present operation effective:** Dalton v. Specter, 1994-05-23.

**Open question:** Distinct timely claims to compel an unperformed statutory duty, constitutional claims and genuinely ultra vires conduct remain outside the holding. The broader four-Justice rationale is not governing law.

**Holdings navigation:** The approved closure selection cannot be reopened through this implementation challenge.

## Standards and Tests selection: Proprietary public purchases and labor preemption

### Proprietary public purchases and labor preemption

**Governing question:** What instructions govern proprietary public purchases and labor preemption?

**Current rule:** Garmon and Machinists preemption do not bar a project-specific contracting condition when the State acts for its proprietary interests as owner and purchaser, analogous private conduct is lawful, and Congress has not expressly or impliedly displaced the proprietary conduct. Public ownership or a procurement label alone is insufficient; purchasing cannot regulate unrelated conduct or impose general labor policy. The holding creates no general NLRA-employer status for a State. Distinct §§8(e) and 8(f) limits remain, including §8(f)'s preservation of employee elections under §§9(c) and 9(e).

**Authority by component:**

- Genuine proprietary project purchases are not state labor regulation: Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor) — Genuine proprietary project purchases are not state labor regulation, 1993-03-08. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: unanimous controlling authority in both dockets.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-03-08; Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor), for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor), 507 U.S. 218 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

### Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

**Governing question:** A state class-settlement judgment releasing claims within exclusive federal adjudicatory jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** Section 1738 first requires the effect the rendering State gives its judgment; class form, consent disposition and exclusive federal jurisdiction alone create no categorical exception. Approval of an authorized representative release differs from adjudicating the released federal liability. Exchange Act §27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 for this judgment category. An incorporated, affirmed Delaware release has its ordinary state-law effect subject to Delaware's identical-factual-predicate and representative limits; alleged ordinary state-law error does not itself reopen that judgment. Recognition remains subject to constitutional authority to bind absent persons and does not confer state jurisdiction over federal securities liability or authorize coercive interference with federal litigation.

**Authority by component:**

- State-law-first framework: Matsushita — Section 1738 governs this kind of judgment, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part I.
- Ordinary Delaware recognition: the same Record, “This judgment receives ordinary Delaware preclusive effect,” Part II, seven Justices (all except Stevens and Ginsburg).
- Section 27 nonrepeal: the same Record, “Section 27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 here,” Part III, eight Justices (all except Ginsburg).

**Present operation effective:** Matsushita, February 27, 1996.

**Open question:** Actual constitutional adequacy, lawful binding and the complete permissible scope of collateral review, including effect of a full and fair prior adequacy determination, remain unresolved. No universal same-transaction release rule or fiction of individually negotiated consent arises from failure to opt out; valid opt-outs remain outside the release.

**Related current rules:** Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies the independent constitutional minimum.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367.

## Standards and Tests selection: Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

### Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

**Governing question:** The due-process prerequisite to claim or issue preclusion against a nonparty.

**Current rule:** Identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources, public importance and fiscal reliance alone do not authorize binding absent persons' own legal rights. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process. Constitutionally inadequate representation is not independently cured by notice, opportunities to object or opt out; notice alone imposes no general duty on strangers to intervene. Issue preclusion, like claim preclusion, requires a lawful basis to bind the person. Genuine representative public-right adjudication and use of precedent in a later case remain distinct from extinguishing a stranger's personal claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Representation independent of notice and exclusion rights: Matsushita — Constitutional representation and unresolved review questions limit the remand, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part IV-A; eight Associates reserve complete collateral-review scope in IV-B.
- Nonparty binding and direct personal tax claims, including the issue-preclusion alternative: Richards — Holdings II–IV, June 10, 1996; Stone-Zsela for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Richards, June 10, 1996; Matsushita supplies the retained representation component.

**Open question:** The Court assumes without deciding whether adequate representation may cure absent notice in some representative proceedings. It neither requires individual notice in every such action nor constitutionalizes every federal class-action requirement. The broader adequacy of officials' representation of constituents, ordinary state-law-error review and Matsushita's collateral-review boundary are not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367; Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793.

## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

### Judicial claim construction and the retained infringement jury function

**Governing question:** Allocation of claim meaning and factual infringement work in a patent damages action.

**Current rule:** A patent damages action carries the Seventh Amendment jury right, while the court determines the public claim’s operative legal meaning using the claim, specification and prosecution history. Judicial construction includes terms of art and genuinely disputed technical usage or expert credibility embedded in that interpretive task, evaluated for consistency with the whole instrument. The jury retains disputed infringement, product operation, external identity and other factual questions outside construction. Technical complexity alone neither removes protected facts from the jury nor makes all patent issues legal.

**Authority by component:**

- Markman — Part I, legal meaning and retained factual work, 1996-04-23; Souter for all nine.
- Markman — Part II, technical and credibility disputes within construction, 1996-04-23; Souter and all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela’s contrary embedded-fact limitation is noncontrolling.

**Present operation effective:** Markman, April 23, 1996.

**Open question:** Trial allocation does not settle appellate review of every subsidiary finding. No universal pretrial construction hearing, exclusion of all extrinsic evidence, allocation for other instruments or comprehensive means-plus-function rule is prescribed.

**Holdings navigation:** Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., 517 U.S. 370.

## Standards and Tests selection: Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

### Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

**Governing question:** Compensation for a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use of the relevant property.

**Current rule:** Complete deprivation effects a taking requiring just compensation unless background state property or nuisance law already excluded the prohibited use from the owner's title. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate a background limitation that independently prohibits the use in the property's circumstances. A new legislative declaration that the use is harmful is insufficient. Apply ordinary adjudicative proof requirements, without a special heightened quantum. Changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance law; legislative labeling cannot substitute for an antecedent title limit. Compensation concerns any actual taking period, not an assumed permanent loss.

**Authority by component:**

- Categorical compensation, background-law exception and allocation: Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, “Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use”, June 29, 1992; Scalia, Stone, White, O'Connor and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lucas*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Penn Central's contextual analysis remains for partial deprivation. No percentage shortcut, parcel-definition rule, automatic valuation, right to anticipated profits or finding that every environmental restriction is invalid follows. Valid authority to regulate remains distinct from responsibility to compensate.

**Holdings navigation:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992) — Complete economic deprivation of land.

## Standards and Tests selection: Proof before a potentially indefinite complete parental-contact cutoff

### Proof before a potentially indefinite complete parental-contact cutoff

**Governing question:** The sexual-abuse finding triggering Louisiana Revised Statutes §9:364(D)'s severe parental-contact restriction.

**Current rule:** Sexual abuse of the parent's child or children must be proved by clear and convincing evidence before §9:364(D) imposes mandatory loss of all visitation and contact until an adversarial hearing establishes both successful completion of a program designed for sexual abusers and that supervised visitation is in the children's best interests. The statutory treatment requires evaluation and psychotherapy specifically designed for perpetrators of family violence and conducted by licensed mental-health professionals. Bare preponderance cannot supply the predicate for that complete, potentially indefinite exclusion.

**Authority by component:**

- Heightened proof for the specified deprivation: Clear and convincing proof must precede this statutory all-contact restriction, 1995-11-20; Stone-Zsela, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** A. St. P. C. v. B. C., 1995-11-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary investigation, emergency intervention, temporary separation and lesser custody or supervised-contact measures retain their existing requirements. The rule supplies no universal custody burden, periodic-review schedule, restoration procedure, treatment-admission condition or entitlement to contact.

**Holdings navigation:** A. St. P. C. v. B. C..

## Standards and Tests selection: Genuine pedagogy and religious perspectives in graded assignments

### Genuine pedagogy and religious perspectives in graded assignments

**Governing question:** A student’s challenge to rejection of a religious research topic within a teacher-assessed curricular assignment.

**Current rule:** Teachers may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns; religious conviction creates no exemption. But categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal ban on academic religious subjects, is not legitimate pedagogy merely because so labeled. Schools retain authority to select knowledge, method or perspective taught and to distinguish objective inquiry from testimony or devotional advocacy; no universal curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule follows. Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible academic justification where supported evidence permits a material inference of impermissible religious causation. The student must establish protected conduct and a substantial or motivating role; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent it.

**Authority by component:**

- Legitimate-pedagogy boundary and actual causal proof: I. Curricular authority must rest on genuine, legitimate pedagogy and II. The affirmative record leaves a material causal dispute, 1996-06-25; Souter’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Settle, 1996-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary genuine factual or evaluative errors may remain within pedagogical discretion; the rule creates no classroom forum, automatic topic approval, compelled grade, independent investigative duty or conclusive liability from an impermissible factor where the same-decision defense is proved.

**Holdings navigation:** Settle v. Dickson County School Board.

## Standards and Tests selection: Privacy Act incorporation of FOIA in federal bargaining requests

### Privacy Act incorporation of FOIA in federal bargaining requests

**Governing question:** When a federal labor-information duty can compel personal-record disclosure through the Privacy Act’s FOIA exception.

**Current rule:** Section 7114(b)(4) operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law. For a record in a system of records, §552a(b) prohibits disclosure absent the subject’s written request or prior written consent or an applicable enumerated exception. Under §552a(b)(2), FOIA itself must require disclosure. Collective-bargaining usefulness does not enlarge Reporters Committee’s ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry. Exemption 6 balances concrete privacy against demonstrated incremental public understanding; it permits withholding rather than commanding secrecy, while the Privacy Act supplies the prohibition through this invoked route when FOIA does not require release.

**Authority by component:**

- Labor duty, Privacy Act trigger and incorporated FOIA inquiry: Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority — The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration, 1994-02-23. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.
- Distinct Exemption 6 permission and resulting Privacy Act prohibition: Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority — The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record, 1994-02-23. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Present operation effective:** Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 1994-02-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** The labor duty concerns requested, normally maintained, reasonably available data necessary for full and proper bargaining discussion, understanding and negotiation, excluding management/supervisory bargaining guidance, advice, counsel and training. Exemption 6 covers personnel, medical and similar files and a clearly unwarranted invasion; Exemption 7(C)’s different law-enforcement threshold is not substituted. Scattered public availability does not eliminate dissemination privacy. Routine use, subject consent, other exceptions and a different demonstrated transparency use remain separate. Requester neutrality does not erase a protected person’s position when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground.

**Holdings navigation:** The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration; The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA plan commands and indirect economic effects

### ERISA plan commands and indirect economic effects

**Governing question:** When state benefit mandates or indirect price effects relate to covered plans under §514(a).

**Current rule:** ERISA §514(a) preempts a replacement-coverage obligation insofar as it uses a covered plan’s benefit content as the mandatory legal measure, even if replacement is separately administered through exempt workers’ compensation arrangements. Requirements measured solely by exempt governmental or church plans remain outside that holding. By contrast, the reviewed commercial-insurer hospital differentials and conditional HMO assessment are not preempted merely because price or participation effects influence plan choices: the measures neither use ERISA status or plan benefits as their legal benchmark nor command benefits, eligibility, uniform administration or an alternative enforcement remedy. Ordinary indirect economic influence is not itself such a command. The replacement-coverage obligation’s 52-week cap supplies no federal safe harbor.

**Authority by component:**

- Covered-plan content as a mandatory legal measure: District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade — Covered-plan content as a mandatory legal measure, 1992-12-14. Thomas’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt the same operative rule.

- The reviewed 13% hospital differential is not preempted: The reviewed 13% hospital differential is not preempted, 1995-04-26. Justice Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined on this proposition by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine direct votes. The direct self-funded application is outside this merits holding.
- The additional commercial-insurer 11% charge is not preempted: The additional commercial-insurer 11% charge is not preempted, 1995-04-26. Souter’s Court opinion and the same nine named Justices directly adopt this distinct application. It is not a holding that the additional 11% applies to every payor or that the reproduced period fixes liability under a later extension.
- The conditional HMO assessment is not preempted: The conditional HMO assessment is not preempted, 1995-04-26. Souter’s Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine direct votes for this separately analyzed assessment. The decision supplies no insurance-saving holding for the distinct 9% measure.

**Present operation effective:** Travelers, April 26, 1995, clarifies the previously reserved indirect-economic-effects boundary; Board of Trade, December 14, 1992, continues to govern covered-plan benchmarks.

**Holdings navigation:** District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade, 506 U.S. 125 (1992); The reviewed 13% hospital differential is not preempted; The additional commercial-insurer 11% charge is not preempted; The conditional HMO assessment is not preempted.

**Exceptions and limits:** No rate or combined-charge safe harbor is created. The possibility of effects sufficiently acute to operate as a substantive coverage command or effectively restrict insurer choice remains open. This is a rule about the ERISA connection, not a new hospital-payment code or permission to disregard a measure’s enacted conditions.

**Open question:** Direct self-funded application, insurance saving and deemer questions remain unresolved. The insurer/HMO rulings do not decide them. Laws specifically referring to covered plans, mandating benefits, interfering with uniform administration or supplying alternative enforcement remain outside the nonpreemption holding.

## Standards and Tests selection: Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

### Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce

**Governing question:** When a state or local economic measure must satisfy the discrimination rule rather than ordinary incidental-burden balancing.

**Current rule:** A measure discriminates when it favors local economic interests by burdening interstate competitors, including a waste charge expressly keyed to out-of-state origin, compulsory routing that reserves a processing service to a designated local private operator, or an integrated assessment-and-distribution scheme that collects from the whole market but returns the proceeds only to local producers. The entire linked operation matters; formally equal collection does not cleanse its discriminatory distribution. The government must show a legitimate local purpose that cannot adequately be served by reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Ordinary incidental-burden balancing does not validate discrimination. Conservation, sanitation, cost recovery and financing a favored private facility do not themselves meet that burden; contracting with a private operator does not make compulsory market regulation governmental market participation. A municipality that actually purchases collection and disposal may specify the destination as part of that purchased performance, including through a paid hauler and privately owned facility. Separate suppliers or prices do not themselves turn purchased disposal into unrelated downstream business. This bounded purchasing authority does not reach independent transactions merely because government has a contract or financial stake.

**Authority by component:**

- Origin-based surcharge and justification burden: Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality — An express waste-origin surcharge triggers the discrimination rule; Conservation and cost-recovery labels do not justify this private-facility differential, 1994-04-04. Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.
- Private processing monopoly and neutral alternatives: C & A Carbone, Inc. v. Town of Clarkstown — Compulsory use of this local private processor discriminates in the service market; Sanitary service and financing do not justify this discriminatory route, 1994-05-16. Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the discrimination classification and its stated private-service boundary. O'Connor agrees only with the judgment on a distinct Pike ground. Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this application of the discrimination rule. No independent Pike holding is adopted by that coalition.
- Integrated levy, local distribution and justification: West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy — The assessment and dedicated local distribution must be examined together; the local preference lacks a sufficient justification, 1994-06-17. Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg: six Justices adopt this rule and its application. Justices Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on the narrower dedicated-industry-tax ground. Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg: six Justices adopt the justification analysis and its application. Eight Justices support reversal and remand; Justice Blackmun dissents.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden

### Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden

**Governing question:** When an otherwise discriminatory tax can compensate for a tax already borne by the corresponding intrastate activity.

**Current rule:** The State must identify the actual intrastate burden for which it compensates, show that the interstate charge roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and establish that the taxed events are substantially equivalent, reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies for one another. General income taxation and waste disposal are not such equivalent events. For a use tax said to equalize local sales taxes, the comparison must use the sales tax actually imposed in the affected locality. Statewide averages and burdens elsewhere cannot cure an excess there. Equal or lower use-tax burdens in other localities are not invalidated merely because an excess exists elsewhere; relief addresses the established inequality through lawful remedial procedures. A resident shareholder’s location and asserted substantial equality with a separate corporate-level tax supply no exception: an issuer-based tax preference favoring local corporate activity must satisfy the same actual-burden and equivalent-event requirements. Typical price/earnings ratios or aggregate corporate contributions do not themselves establish the matching burden.

**Authority by component:**

- Three compensatory-tax predicates: Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality — General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax, 1994-04-04. Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.
- Local comparison, bounded invalidity and relief: Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman — The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality; Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures, 1994-05-23. Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Blackmun agrees with the judgment without joining this full framework.
- Fulton — holdings 1–4, investment discrimination and rejection of Darnell’s shortcut, 1996-02-21; Thomas for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Fulton, February 21, 1996, removing the contrary Darnell permission; the earlier compensatory predicates survive.

**Holdings navigation:** General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax; The compensating burden must exist in the affected locality; Relief must address the established inequality through lawful state procedures; Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner, 516 U.S. 325.

**Material development:** Darnell is overruled only insofar as it permits discriminatory taxation of residents’ out-of-state shares merely by reference to a different corporate-level local tax and asserted substantial equality. Its independent equal-protection and standing determinations remain.

## Standards and Tests selection: Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications

### Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications

**Governing question:** What instructions govern rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications?

**Current rule:** For an economic classification involving neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right under the question reviewed, a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis suffices and the challenger must negate it. Formal legislative findings are unnecessary. Conceivable differences in bargaining arrangements, regulatory costs or competition can justify a line without proof that every regulated entity shares a monopoly. Moreno and Cleburne retain their prohibition of irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications; this rule supplies no resolution of a distinct heightened-scrutiny or First Amendment claim.

## Standards and Tests selection: Vested LHWCA entitlement and third-party settlement approval

### Vested LHWCA entitlement and third-party settlement approval

**Governing question:** When LHWCA §33(g)'s approval and notification conditions attach to a worker's third-party recovery.

**Current rule:** A worker whose substantive compensation right has vested is a person entitled to compensation despite disputed employer liability, absence of payments or absence of an award. For a covered third-party settlement below the statutory compensation entitlement, §33(g)(1) requires prescribed written employer-and-carrier approval before execution and filing within thirty days. Failure to obtain and file required approval terminates compensation and medical benefits under §33(g)(2). Judgments and settlements at least equal to the compensation entitlement require notification rather than that prior approval.

**Authority by component:**

- Vested entitlement, approval/filing and notification relationship: Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co., “Vested entitlement exists before payment or an award”, June 22, 1992; Kennedy, Stone, White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Cowart*, June 22, 1992.

**Open question:** Whether employer participation independently satisfies or excuses approval, any preserved alternative-approval or waiver issue, the gross-versus-net comparison and effects on other final awards remain unresolved. Injury alone does not establish vested entitlement, and not every settlement requires approval. No new deference rule follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co., 505 U.S. 469 (1992) — Longshore compensation and third-party settlements.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

### ERISA employee status under general common-law agency

**Governing question:** Classification as an employee under ERISA's circular employee definition.

**Current rule:** ERISA incorporates general common-law agency, rather than any particular State's law or a substitute based on benefit expectations, reliance and bargaining power. Courts must weigh all incidents of the relationship, including control over the manner and means of work, skill, tools, work location, duration, assignment rights, scheduling discretion, payment, assistants, integration into the regular business, the hiring party's business, benefits and tax treatment. No factor, contractual label or expectation of benefits is decisive. Employee classification precedes the resulting vesting or forfeiture inquiry; this construction does not extend to statutes with broader express definitions, including the FLSA's suffer-or-permit definition.

**Authority by component:**
- Statutory construction and all-incidents inquiry: Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, March 24, 1992, “ERISA employee status follows general common-law agency”; Souter's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Darden, March 24, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden, 503 U.S. 318 (1992) — ERISA employee classification.

#### Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1502.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For the separate standing and constitutional-counting holdings in Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992, No. 91-1502, see Federal Courts — Standing and representation injury and Election Law — Interstate congressional apportionment.

##### The Secretary's completed census determination is reviewable

**Holding:** The Secretary's completed census determination supplies final agency action under APA §704 even though the President later transmits the resulting apportionment statement. The census statutes neither preclude review nor commit the counting decision entirely to unreviewable discretion: the duty to count the proper population supplies law to apply, while leaving substantial methodological choice to the Secretary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, controls with five votes. White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas disagree with this rule while agreeing with reversal on their own reviewability ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Presidential transmission under §2a does not confer an independent presidential choice of the population counted under §141. The President's own APA status and susceptibility to coercive relief remain undecided; Waterman's independent foreign-affairs decision structure is different.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

#### Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 510 U.S. 487

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1223.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration

**Holding:** Section 7114(b)(4)'s duty to supply qualifying bargaining data operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law; when the asserted exception to the Privacy Act is §552a(b)(2), the question is whether FOIA itself requires disclosure. **Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labor duty concerns requested data normally maintained by the agency in its regular course of business, reasonably available and necessary for full and proper discussion, understanding and negotiation of subjects within collective bargaining, and not guidance, advice, counsel or training provided for management officials or supervisors relating to collective bargaining. These conditions are not independently disputed or newly adjudicated here. For a record contained in a system of records, §552a(b) bars agency disclosure absent a written request by, or prior written consent of, the individual to whom it pertains, or an applicable enumerated exception. A union request is not the subject individual's request or consent. Routine use under §552a(b)(3) and other exceptions are distinct and undecided; their applicability is not guaranteed.

- Department of the Treasury, IRS v. FLRA: applied for respecting enacted limits on labor-agency authority; the uncontested bargaining-necessity determination is not reconsidered.
- Department of State v. Ray: preserved; the incorporated Exemption 6 inquiry remains a concrete balance, not a rule that another statute's useful purpose automatically establishes FOIA-required disclosure.

**Holding:** On these requests, official disclosure of employee home addresses would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy under Exemption 6 because the real interest in controlling dissemination of home locations outweighs the absent or negligible demonstrated addition to public understanding after names and work stations were supplied. Exemption 6 permits withholding and FOIA therefore does not require disclosure; the Privacy Act supplies the resulting prohibition against compelling release through the invoked §552a(b)(2) route, without deciding a different demonstrated transparency use, routine use, subject consent or another exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exemption 6 applies to personnel, medical and similar files whose disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy; the requested employee-address records fall within that category. Exemption 7(C) instead concerns information compiled for law-enforcement purposes and disclosures that could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion; that different standard is not used here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of State v. Ray: applied for concrete privacy injury and demonstrated incremental public understanding; its confidentiality assurance is not a prerequisite or automatic exemption, and no general bar to derivative benefits or prior-wrongdoing requirement is added.
- Department of the Air Force v. Rose: applied; disclosure remains the general rule, with the enacted Exemption 6 privacy balance preserved.
- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: applied to the relevant public interest and continued control over officially disseminated personal information despite scattered public availability; its rap-sheet sensitivity and Exemption 7(C) threshold do not replace the different record or Exemption 6 standard here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Enforcement of both the Gulfport and Lowry disclosure orders is reversed on the asserted §552a(b)(2) ground. No new necessity determination, constitutional privacy remedy or approval of a routine use is ordered; other disclosure routes must meet their own requirements.

**Holding:** A patent infringement action seeking legal damages carries the Seventh Amendment jury right. #### Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., 510 U.S. 569

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1292.

**Decided:** 1994-03-07.

##### Commercial parody and the integrated statutory inquiry

**Holding:** Section 107 requires the four fair-use factors to be considered together; commercial parody may qualify, and neither profit motive nor a parody label settles the inquiry or creates a dispositive presumption of unfairness. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. **Holding:** The published original song's creative nature weighs within §107's second factor and is not erased by the claim of parody, but it contributes little to distinguishing fair from unfair parody because parody ordinarily must use recognizable expressive works. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. **Holding:** For this transformative parody, commerciality alone does not raise a presumption of market harm; §107 requires examination of substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including the effect of widespread comparable conduct. - Sony, 464 U.S. 417: commercial market-harm language is confined to its context; no universal presumption follows for this transformative use.

**Holding:** Fair use remains an affirmative defense, and defendants seeking summary judgment must establish it on a record permitting judgment as a matter of law; rejection of the appellate commerciality and harm presumptions does not shift that burden. #### West Lynn Creamery, Inc. v. Healy, 512 U.S. 186

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-141.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

##### The assessment and dedicated local distribution must be examined together

**Holding:** A State discriminates against interstate commerce when it assesses sales in a commodity market without regard to origin but dedicates the proceeds to competing in-state producers so that the linked arrangement offsets the local side's burden while leaving out-of-state production exposed. Courts must examine the supported competitive connection between assessment and distribution; different legal payors and recipients do not prevent discrimination, but merely sharing a general treasury does not establish it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg: six Justices adopt this rule and its application. Justices Scalia and Thomas concur in the judgment on the narrower dedicated-industry-tax ground.

Dedicated payments go only to Massachusetts producers in proportion to the preceding month's production. Credited production is capped at 200,000 pounds per producer per month; the federal minimum plus fund payment cannot exceed $15 per hundredweight, and surplus returns to dealers. These limits do not remove the exclusive competitive benefit. The State neither buys nor sells milk.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Baldwin v. G. A. F. Seelig, Inc., 294 U.S. 511 (1935): applies its prohibition on protecting local milk production from interstate competition to the operation of the linked fund.
- Bacchus Imports, Ltd. v. Dias, 468 U.S. 263 (1984): applies its rule against local-product tax preferences despite the State's ordinary taxing authority.
- New Energy Co. of Indiana v. Limbach, 486 U.S. 269 (1988): applies its discriminatory-tax rule while preserving the distinction for ordinary direct assistance; no general subsidy immunity is adopted.
- Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman, May 23, 1994: follows its requirement to examine the relevant competitive comparison rather than treating a broader fiscal description as a cure.

**Holding:** Once the linked assessment and distribution discriminate, the State must establish a legitimate local purpose that adequate reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot serve; protecting local producers against interstate competition is not itself a sufficient purpose. **Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg: six Justices adopt the justification analysis and its application. Eight Justices support reversal and remand; Justice Blackmun dissents.

- Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality, April 4, 1994: follows origin-discrimination scrutiny; no new compensatory-tax entitlement is created.
- Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc., 397 U.S. 137 (1970): distinguishes its incidental-burden balancing because this arrangement discriminates.

#### Oregon Waste Systems, Inc. v. Department of Environmental Quality and Columbia Resource Co. v. Environmental Quality Commission, 511 U.S. 93

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-70 and 93-108.

##### An express waste-origin surcharge triggers the discrimination rule

**Holding:** A State discriminates against interstate commerce when it imposes a higher governmental disposal charge on otherwise comparable waste because it was generated outside the State; a small amount or environmental objective does not remove that express geographic preference. Such a differential can survive only if the State establishes a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve; ordinary Pike balancing of evenhanded incidental burdens does not govern this classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.

- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill, Inc. v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, 504 U.S. 353 (1992): applied to regulation of private landfills.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, 502 U.S. 437 (1992): its express-geographic-discrimination rule is preserved and applied.
- Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc., 397 U.S. 137 (1970): distinguished as governing evenhanded regulation with incidental burdens.

##### General income taxes do not establish a compensatory disposal tax

**Holding:** A compensatory-tax defense requires an identified intrastate burden, an interstate charge that roughly approximates and does not exceed that burden, and substantially equivalent taxable events capable of operating as mutually exclusive proxies. Oregon's reliance on residents' general income-tax contributions fails because income generation and disposal of waste are not substantially equivalent events; even a quantified revenue comparison would not cure that independently sufficient defect, and the statutory cost command is not proof of a matching burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maryland v. Louisiana, 451 U.S. 725 (1981): applied to require identification of the actual domestic burden being compensated.
- Henneford v. Silas Mason Co., 300 U.S. 577 (1937): preserved for genuinely complementary taxes on substantially equivalent events; general taxation is not such a proxy.
- Armco Inc. v. Hardesty, 467 U.S. 638 (1984): applied to the independently fatal absence of substantially equivalent taxable events.

##### Conservation and cost-recovery labels do not justify this private-facility differential

**Holding:** Oregon's conservation purpose, comprehensive regulatory program and statutory direction to recover otherwise-unpaid costs do not establish that this origin differential satisfies the discrimination rule: the record supplies no demonstrated origin-specific added cost that defeats reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives. Because the facilities are private, regulation does not make this a proprietary charge by a government owner, and a user-fee analogy cannot eliminate nondiscrimination as a separate requirement; the Court leaves government-owned landfill charges and materially different proven cost justifications undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; eight Justices directly adopt this proposition and its application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt: applied to preserve legitimate environmental aims while requiring an adequate nondiscriminatory-alternatives showing.
- Sporhase v. Nebraska ex rel. Douglas, 458 U.S. 941 (1982): distinguished; conservation concerns do not validate this origin preference at private landfills.
- Evansville-Vanderburgh Airport Authority District v. Delta Airlines, Inc., 405 U.S. 707 (1972): reasonable approximation does not displace its independent nondiscrimination condition.
- Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. County of Kent, January 24, 1994: its distinct nondiscrimination requirement is applied; unlike that record, this fee expressly classifies by interstate origin.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources: applied to distinguish private-facility regulation from state market participation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statutory direction to recover costs is not a lower-court finding of numerical equivalence. The Court supplies no contrary numerical finding, no universal bar to cost recovery and no rule for government-owned landfills. Materially different proven origin-specific costs remain subject to the governing justification requirement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both matters are reversed and remanded for relief from the origin differential through the existing state review proceeding. No replacement rate, refund entitlement or amount, alteration of the separately unchallenged charge, required waste shipment or reinstatement of severed state-law provisions is ordered.

#### Fulton Corp. v. Faulkner, 516 U.S. 325

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1239.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### The issuer-based deduction discriminates against interstate investment

**Holding:** A State discriminates against interstate commerce when the tax on a resident's otherwise comparable corporate shares increases as the issuer's local income-tax exposure decreases. Neither the shareholder's local residence nor drafting the geographic advantage as a deduction removes that discrimination, and proof that a particular issuer already lost an investment is unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** The tax is twenty-five cents per $100 of share value, reduced according to the issuer's North Carolina-apportioned income. For corporate shareholders the linked dividend-deduction proportion applies without the ordinary $15,000 dividend-deduction cap.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Oregon Waste's express-geographic-discrimination rule and West Lynn's examination of linked operation apply; a deduction label or shareholder residence does not remove discrimination.

##### Corporate income and share ownership are not shown to be equivalent taxable events

**Holding:** A compensatory-tax defense requires the State to identify the actual intrastate burden being offset, show a roughly equivalent and nonexcessive interstate charge, and establish substantially equivalent taxable events reasonably treated as mutually exclusive proxies. North Carolina's corporate-income and shareholder-property taxes do not satisfy those requirements merely because corporate profits may influence share value; the failure to establish equivalent events independently defeats the defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Different nominal taxpayers do not invariably prevent compensation, and income and property taxes may coexist. This State failed to establish equivalent events.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Oregon Waste supplies the cumulative compensatory conditions, Maryland v. Louisiana the actual-local-burden requirement and Armco the independently fatal equivalent-events defect. Henneford preserves genuinely complementary taxation.

**Independent alternative holding:** A State does not establish the compensatory defense's actual-burden and approximate-equivalence requirements merely by invoking typical price/earnings ratios or aggregate corporate tax contributions. North Carolina's comparison fails to demonstrate the required matching burden on this record, an additional ground for rejecting its defense.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The 1990 claim is not governed by a new universal calculation using the later 7.75% rate; the state discussion also identifies the earlier 7% rate. Its general price/earnings reasoning is neither a statutory command nor an adopted equality finding for every taxpayer. Associated Industries requires the relevant actual matching burden, rather than aggregate or other-transaction offsets.

##### Darnell's contrary Commerce Clause permission is overruled

**Holding:** Darnell v. Indiana is overruled to the extent it permits discriminatory taxation of residents' shares in out-of-state corporations under the Commerce Clause merely by reference to a different corporate-level local tax and an asserted substantial equality. Its distinct equal-protection and standing determinations are not erased.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Darnell v. Indiana, 226 U.S. 390, is expressly overruled only on its incompatible Commerce Clause permission. Oregon Waste and Associated Industries require the cumulative inquiry; Darnell's separate standing and equal-protection determinations remain.

##### Invalidity requires lawful remedial proceedings, not a predetermined refund

**Holding:** Reversal of the tax's constitutional justification requires remand for state-law severability and constitutionally adequate relief. When a State compelled payment before a meaningful opportunity to contest the tax, McKesson requires adequate backward-looking correction; independently, Reich forbids withdrawing a clearly held-out postpayment remedy after payment and substituting an unpursued prepayment route.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** McKesson permits lawful refunds, constitutionally permissible equalizing retrospective treatment or a lawful combination where backward-looking relief is required. Reich's protection applies to a clearly held-out postpayment remedy even after voluntary payment; reasonable previously announced conditions and prospective changes with fair notice remain permissible.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for severability and constitutionally adequate relief according to actual payments, preservation, procedures and lawful state choices. No fixed refund, fee award, interest, assessment against others, blanket stock exemption, deduction change or automatic reinstatement of the intermediate appellate remedy follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McKesson's conditional backward-looking duty and Reich's independent no-withdrawal protection govern relief without selecting a universal refund formula.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/civil-rights.md

#### FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., 508 U.S. 307 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-603.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., June 1, 1993, No. 92-603, see Federal Courts — Legislative reenactment and mootness.

##### Common-owner bargaining and regulatory costs sustain the economic line

**Holding:** For an economic classification involving neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right under the question reviewed, a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis suffices; the challenger must negate such a basis, and formal legislative findings are unnecessary. Congress could rationally distinguish these commonly owned, controlled or managed dwellings from separately owned properties because bargaining arrangements and the relationship between regulatory cost and consumer protection differ; Moreno and Cleburne continue to forbid irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Parts I and II-A; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States Railroad Retirement Board v. Fritz, 449 U.S. 166: conceivable legitimate grounds and imperfect economic fit applied.
- Sullivan v. Stroop, 496 U.S. 478: ordinary review of social and economic classifications applied.
- Nordlinger v. Hahn, June 18, 1992: the rational-basis holding applied without converting its particular tax grounds into universal immunity from review.
- Department of Agriculture v. Moreno, 413 U.S. 528: illegitimate-purpose limit preserved.
- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: irrational-prejudice limit preserved.

##### Competitive entry independently supports the ownership distinction

**Independent alternative holding:** Congress could independently distinguish separately owned buildings because an incumbent provider can add nearby buildings at a lower marginal cost than a new entrant can incur to install its own reception facilities, creating a plausible first-mover advantage. This conceivable competition concern is sufficient under ordinary rational-basis review without proof that every regulated system possesses monopoly power.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Thomas’s Part II-B; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The regulatory-cost and competition grounds are independently sufficient. No First Amendment or heightened-scrutiny question is decided, and no actual monopoly is found.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the equal-protection invalidation; the separate questions not decided may receive further proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fritz, 449 U.S. 166: a second conceivable legitimate economic basis independently sustains the line.
- Nordlinger, June 18, 1992: multiple sufficient rational grounds and tolerance for imperfect fit applied within ordinary review.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/civil-rights.md

#### Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-351.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Holding:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Holding:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. Continuing family knowledge rationally supports that participation; the availability of testimony or a less extensive form of participation does not by itself defeat ordinary rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S. 221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Holding:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new scrutiny tier or national reasonable-doubt requirement is adopted; heightened scrutiny and Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule remain outside review. Party status is sustained categorically, not every hearing’s fairness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of unequal proof burdens and remand for severability and lawful equalization; reverse categorical invalidation of family-party participation subject to counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. No blanket release.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: private interest, error risk and safeguards, and government interests applied separately from equality review.
- Parham, 442 U.S. 584: informational participation distinguished from final decisional control.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/maritime-law.md

#### Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co., 505 U.S. 469 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-17.
**Decided:** June 22, 1992.

##### Whether vested compensation entitlement triggers §33(g) before payment or an award

**Holding:** A worker whose substantive compensation right has vested is a person entitled to compensation under LHWCA §33(g), although the employer disputes liability, makes no current payments and is not subject to an award. For a covered third-party settlement below the statutory compensation entitlement, §33(g)(1) requires prescribed written approval from employer and carrier before execution and filing within thirty days; failure to obtain and file the required approval terminates compensation and medical benefits under §33(g)(2). Judgments and settlements at least equal to the compensation entitlement require notification rather than that prior approval.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt vested entitlement, the necessary relationship of approval and notification, and the application. No alternative ground or Marks inquiry supports the ruling.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for vested entitlement and the distinct statutory approval, filing and notification conditions.

**Material application:** Cowart's legal right vested before acknowledgment or adjudication; the informal Labor Department notice was not an award. The estate therefore cannot avoid §33(g) on the theory that the employer was not paying and no award existed. Notification retains an independent role for judgments and sufficiently large settlements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Not every injured person has a vested compensation right and not every settlement requires prior approval. Employer participation, alternative approval, waiver, gross-versus-net comparison, reopening other final awards, retroactive effect, constitutional challenges and deference to a newly formulated litigation position are undecided. Funding and knowing the settlement are not held incapable of mattering in a properly presented approval or waiver dispute. No new compensation category, equitable exception or general invariant-word canon is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit on the entitlement question. The administrative award remains overturned; no benefits are restored on the rejected payment-or-award theory, and no new compensation calculation, administrative award or hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Demarest v. Manspeaker, 498 U.S. 184, requires clear enacted meaning to prevail over contrary administrative practice or retention of isolated words. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837, and K mart Corp. v. Cartier, Inc., 486 U.S. 281, do not permit deference contrary to unambiguous text; no new litigation-position deference rule is adopted. Sullivan v. Stroop, 496 U.S. 478, and Sorenson v. Secretary of Treasury, 475 U.S. 851, support consistent meaning where linked provisions reinforce it. Banks v. Chicago Grain Trimmers Association, Inc., 390 U.S. 459, supplies approval's protection against inadequate third-party settlement before liability acknowledgment. Northeast Marine Terminal Co. v. Caputo, 432 U.S. 249, and Voris v. Eikel, 346 U.S. 328, retain remedial-construction principles without authorizing a textual exception. Potomac Electric Power Co. v. Director, OWCP, 449 U.S. 268, preserves the Board's adjudicative rather than policymaking role. National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992, preserves Chevron's text-and-structure limits without requiring deference under this different statute. The Board's O'Leary and Dorsey payment-based interpretations are rejected; they are administrative rulings, not Supreme Court precedents.

## Selected authority from state/holdings/first-amendment.md

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.
