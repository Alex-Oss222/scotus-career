#### O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC, 512 U.S. 79

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-489.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### These acquired California claims carry California imputation law

**Holding:** On the pre-FIRREA framework, California law governs imputation and related defenses to the institution's California negligence and fiduciary-duty claims acquired by the federal receiver: fund protection, greater recovery and general administrative uniformity do not establish the particular significant conflict with federal policy required for the proposed federal exception. The Ninth Circuit's federal-common-law ruling is reversed and the case remanded to determine the preserved state-law questions, without directing judgment for either party.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same pre-FIRREA ground, independently sufficient to reject the proposed rule without deciding FIRREA's temporal reach.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Erie Railroad v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64: applies the absence of general federal common law to privately generated state causes; exceptional federal fields are not abolished.
- Wallis v. Pan American Petroleum Corp., 384 U.S. 63: applies its requirement of an identified significant conflict with federal policy; ordinary federal financial interests are insufficient here.
- Boyle v. United Technologies Corp., 487 U.S. 500: preserves federal-interest and conflict prerequisites for exceptional displacement; the necessary conflict is absent in these acquired claims.
- United States v. Kimbell Foods, Inc., 440 U.S. 715: distinguishes federally generated program rights and preserves its rejection of automatic nationwide uniformity even there.
- United States v. Texas, April 5, 1993: distinguishes the established federal delay-compensation obligation on an assumed federal debt; it supplies no new receiver exemption from state tort defenses.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: preserves the separation of sovereign consent, federal-party status and substantive cause of action; this action remains state created.

##### FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule

**Holding:** If FIRREA governs these claims, §1821(d)(2)(A)(i) transfers the institution's rights, titles, powers and privileges to the FDIC subject to the legal incidents of those rights and the federal exceptions Congress actually enacted; it does not authorize adding the proposed federal exemption from California imputation law. The Court leaves FIRREA's temporal reach unresolved because the independently sufficient pre-FIRREA analysis produces the same state-law remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this conditional statutory construction and its temporal reservation. It is not a finding that FIRREA applies retrospectively.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide California imputation, adverse-interest exceptions, estoppel, engagement scope, duty, breach, causation, recoverable client losses, assigned investor claims, a statute-of-frauds defense, or FIRREA's temporal reach. Reversal does not reinstate the unexplained district-court summary judgment. The complaint does not seek reimbursement of the investors' rescission payments.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Erie Railroad v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64: preserves state substantive law where no enacted or otherwise valid federal displacement is established.
- Boyle v. United Technologies Corp., 487 U.S. 500: remains available within its actual exceptional-federal-law requirements; this construction does not occupy every federal common-law field.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, April 26, 1994: preserves its temporal-direction and new-consequences framework; FIRREA's temporal application is unnecessary to decide and is not presumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for preserved California-law questions. The unexplained original summary judgment is not reinstated automatically. No liability, damages or refund is ordered; FIRREA's temporal reach remains unresolved.

#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1488.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether the separate b.h. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy (Federal Courts — Mootness and decree scope).

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. The absence of an individual right, the existence of an action, and the scope of relief remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980) — preserves statutory-right enforcement under §1983; a violation of federal law still must invade an enforceable right.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987) — distinguishes its defined rent and utility entitlement; its enforceability holding and separate foreclosure inquiry remain operative.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990) — distinguishes its substantive reimbursement obligation and supplied benchmarks; neither its Medicaid holding nor its rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal is overruled.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981) — applies its clear-notice principle to the asserted funding obligation; no categorical immunity for state-plan provisions is created.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action

**Holding:** The Act does not create an implied private cause of action to enforce §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause because the claimants have not established congressional intent to create that remedy. This conclusion concerns the distinct action asserted directly under the Act and does not make failure to imply an action a bar to an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this independently necessary ground for rejecting the second enforcement route sustained below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to imply a private action does not foreclose an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy; other statutory provisions require their own examination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort v. Ash, 422 U.S. 66 (1975) — applies its inquiry into an implied private remedy, with congressional intent decisive.
- Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis, 444 U.S. 11 (1979) — applies its requirement of congressional intent to create the particular remedy asserted; statutory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992, decision — preserves the distinction between the existence of a cognizable action and appropriate relief within it; no Title IX holding is narrowed.

##### Whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. The reporting ground therefore cannot independently sustain the challenged caseworker injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-A, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this separate statutory holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting obligation and administrative enforcement remain. Section 671(a)(16)’s case-plan and review claim is not decided through this reporting holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wright, 479 U.S. 418 — applies the need to identify a privately enforceable statutory right; its distinct rent holding remains in force.
- Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 — applies provision-specific examination of a binding individual entitlement; no general rule against enforcement of plan provisions replaces it.

##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9) does not create an implied private action for these respondents to obtain the challenged caseworker relief: its reporting requirement and the Act's enforcement structure do not establish congressional intent to authorize that action. Rejection of this distinct statutory vehicle does not decide private enforcement of §671(a)(16).

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-B, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the ground rejecting direct implied enforcement of the reporting claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains open as to enforceable right, implied action, violation and additional lawful relief. No constitutional claim is revived or nationwide three-day deadline imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.


#### Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-854.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance

**Holding:** The established private action under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 permits recovery from a person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct, subject to proof of that fraud, the defendant’s qualifying participation, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable loss. This is accessory responsibility within the received investor action, not a finding that the assistant made a primary misrepresentation or a rule imposing liability through professional status or control alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the existence of the confined action and its received-remedy rationale. No fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life & Casualty Co., 404 U.S. 6 — applied as recognition of the received private action; it did not itself decide accessory liability.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — applied for bounded development of the received action and distinguished as to its actual contribution holding; shared liability must still be established before allocation.
- Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Curran, 456 U.S. 353 — used by analogy for the significance of an established judicial remedy; its different statutory setting does not establish congressional enactment of this action.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — applied: the underlying wrong must involve the required manipulation or deception, not unfairness or fiduciary misconduct alone.
- Pinter v. Dahl, 486 U.S. 622 — distinguished: its construction of express seller liability under §12 does not settle this received §10(b) action; its own statutory holding remains intact.
- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Hochfelder itself did not decide that question.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Huddleston itself did not decide that question.

##### Knowledge or severe recklessness in affirmative substantial assistance

**Holding:** For affirmative substantial assistance, the defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the fraudulent character of the activity and the assisting role of its conduct; severe recklessness means an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it. No independent disclosure duty is required for that affirmative participation, but negligence, valuation concerns alone, professional status, ordinary services without qualifying culpability and assistance, or mere silence without a relevant duty do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; all five adopt this confined mental-state and affirmative-assistance rule, not a universal recklessness rule for every securities claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its exclusion of negligence remains controlling; its recklessness reservation is resolved only for this private affirmative-assistance setting.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — its deception-or-manipulation requirement prevents ordinary investment risk and professional error from becoming securities fraud.
- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — its confined affirmative-assistance and extreme-recklessness analysis is adopted for the bank claim; inconsistent knowledge-only or categorical duty requirements for such affirmative assistance cannot govern after this decision. Its separate rulings concerning Pring are outside this review.

##### Triable issues require further proceedings, not a liability award

**Holding:** Viewed favorably to the purchasers at summary judgment, the appraisal warnings, internal concerns and agreement postponing an already demanded independent review until after the offering permit a genuine dispute over severe recklessness and substantial affirmative assistance; the Tenth Circuit’s reinstatement of the pleaded bank claim is affirmed and the case remanded. The purchasers must still prove the underlying violation, the bank’s qualifying assistance and culpability, legally sufficient reliance on the underlying fraud, causation and recoverable loss under the ordinary civil burden; neither the default nor the bonds’ face value establishes fraud damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the record application, preserved proof requirements and limited affirmance and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — affirmed insofar as it reverses summary judgment on the purchasers’ affirmative-assistance claim against Central Bank; triable issues are not findings of fraud or damages.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — the ordinary preponderance standard is preserved; neither institutional status nor information held by the defendant shifts that burden.
- Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485 U.S. 224 — its reliance requirement is preserved. Today’s decision establishes no presumption for these municipal bonds and, as part of the retained accessory action, requires reliance on the underlying fraud rather than on the assistant’s concealed participation.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — its shared-liability prerequisite and contribution limits remain effective; this remand establishes no entitlement or allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the full rules for duty-based omissions, duty-free inaction, agency attribution, a universal securities recklessness rule, other enforcement or statutory settings, or Stone's additional concurrence formulations. The §20(a) defense for a controller acting in good faith without directly or indirectly inducing the violation remains distinct. Reliance on the underlying fraud is required; investors need not personally rely on concealed assistance, and no reliance presumption is established for these bonds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit insofar as it reinstated the pleaded bank claim and remand, 5–4. The bank's summary judgment cannot rest on the rejected categorical grounds or the disputed assistance and culpability record. No liability, damages, automatic indemnity, unpleaded primary claim or adjudication of another defendant's claim is supplied.


#### Nixon v. United States, 506 U.S. 224 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-740.

**Decided:** January 13, 1993.

##### The presented committee-evidence challenge is not judicially revisable

**Holding:** The Senate’s use of a committee to receive testimony, while the full Senate receives the record, hears argument and retains deliberation and judgment, falls within the impeachment-trial procedure committed to the Senate and is not judicially revisable on this claim. This holding does not decide whether courts may enforce the oath, presidential-trial presiding-officer or two-thirds requirements, or review a purported proceeding that abandons adjudication altogether.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Six of nine Justices adopt this exact ground and its limits; White and Blackmun’s merits ground is noncontrolling, and Souter does not formally join the Court’s opinion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 — applied through its issue-specific textual-commitment and manageable-standards inquiry; political subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486 — distinguished because independently fixed legislative qualifications differ from the evidentiary method left to the Senate.
- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, decision — preserved and distinguished; its apportionment review holding does not create impeachment-procedure review.
- United States v. Muñoz-Flores, 495 U.S. 385 — preserved; interbranch disagreement alone does not bar constitutional review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling merits approval of the Senate’s procedure or general immunity for politically significant action follows. The oath, presidential-trial presiding-officer and two-thirds commands, and abandonment of adjudication altogether, remain distinct reserved questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal, 9–0 in judgment; no reinstatement, salary award or supervisory remand. The six-Justice nonjusticiability rationale alone controls the presented objection.

##### Whether Congress’s equal-proportions apportionment choice is judicially reviewable

**Holding:** Courts may decide whether the equal-proportions method violates the constitutional requirement to apportion Representatives among States according to their respective numbers. **Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine participating Justices adopt this proposition at its stated scope; it controls by an express majority, with no judgment-only or narrower-ground vote.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The possibility that several constitutional methods exist does not foreclose this constitutional challenge. Baker’s political-question limits in other settings remain; Count II is separate.

- Wesberry v. Sanders, 376 U.S. 1 (1964) — applies its recognition of concrete congressional voting injuries while preserving the distinction between reviewability and appropriate relief; no general rule governing presidential defendants is adopted.

#### Franklin v. Massachusetts, 505 U.S. 788 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1502.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For the constitutional counting question, see Franklin v. Massachusetts, June 26, 1992, No. 91-1502, under Election Law — Interstate congressional apportionment; for finality, statutory authority and administrative review, see Administrative Law — Census determinations and judicial review.

##### Standing for the continuing apportionment injury

**Holding:** A State and its voters have standing to contest inclusion of an identified population in the operative apportionment when that inclusion costs the State a seat and authoritative relief concerning the responsible census determination would likely correct the allocation through the prescribed statutory sequence. Likely redress here does not require an injunction against the President or a general assumption that persons outside the litigation follow judicial advice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the inclusion-injury standing holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is the proved injury from inclusion of the overseas population. The Court does not find that a particular alternative residence dataset would restore a seat, establish a general premise that nonparties follow judicial advice, or decide coercive relief against the President.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The District Court's judgment is reversed and its corrective relief dissolved. No replacement allocation, further factual proceedings or injunction against the President is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, this Court: applied for continuing representational injury and likely declaratory redress through the allocation process; the express unanimous holding remains controlling and its reservation of coercive presidential relief remains intact.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, this Court: applied for injury, causation, likely redress and evidentiary demands; the noncontrolling application concerning absent funding agencies is not promoted into law. Its rule for procedure connected to concrete injury does not require certainty that a new method changes the result.

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-631; 94-797; 94-929.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

## Standards and Tests selection: State-law incidents of claims acquired by the FDIC

### State-law incidents of claims acquired by the FDIC

**Governing question:** Whether FDIC receivership creates a federal rule displacing state imputation defenses to acquired state tort claims.

**Current rule:** When the FDIC acquires an institution’s state-law tort claims, state law ordinarily supplies the associated imputation rules and defenses. Federal financial interests or a preference for uniformity alone do not justify new federal common law; a significant conflict with an identified federal policy is required. For pre-FIRREA receivership claims the ordinary rule applies independently of that Act. If FIRREA applies, §1821’s succession to the institution’s rights carries their legal incidents subject to Congress’s enacted exceptions; it supplies no additional categorical anti-imputation rule.

**Authority by component:**

- State-law rule and federal-common-law limit: O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC — These acquired California claims carry California imputation law, 1994-06-13. Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same pre-FIRREA ground, independently sufficient to reject the proposed rule without deciding FIRREA's temporal reach.
- Independent conditional FIRREA ground: O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC — FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule, 1994-06-13. Part II of Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this conditional statutory construction and its temporal reservation. It is not a finding that FIRREA applies retrospectively.

**Present operation effective:** O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC, 1994-06-13.

**Open question:** The decision leaves FIRREA’s temporal applicability and the eventual operation of the particular state-law defense for the lower courts.

**Holdings navigation:** These acquired California claims carry California imputation law; FIRREA, if applicable, supplies no additional anti-imputation rule.


## Standards and Tests selection: Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses

### Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses

**Governing question:** Private enforcement of 42 U.S.C. §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause and §671(a)(9)'s reporting clause through §1983 or an action implied directly under the Act.

**Current rule:** Read within the Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, §671(a)(15) creates no individual right enforceable under §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement and caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing right. Nor does §671(a)(15) imply a separate private action: beneficial purpose does not establish congressional intent to create the requested remedy. Section 671(a)(9) requires a plan to provide reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse or exploitation and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B. That administrative direction does not create the asserted private §1983 right or an implied action to obtain caseworker relief. The §1983-right and implied-action inquiries remain distinct; failure to imply an action does not bar an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Authority by component:**
- No individual §671(a)(15) right: Suter v. Artist M., March 25, 1992, “Section 671(a)(15) supplies no individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable under §1983,” Part II.
- No separate implied (15) action: the same record, “Section 671(a)(15) supplies no separate implied action,” Part III.
- Reporting trigger and absence of asserted §1983 right: the same record, “The asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is not enforceable through §1983,” Part IV-A.
- No implied reporting action: the same record, “The asserted §671(a)(9) claim supplies no separate implied action,” Part IV-B. White for O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supplies six controlling joins for each component.

**Present operation effective:** Suter, March 25, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general rule makes state-plan provisions unenforceable. Administrative funding review does not necessarily foreclose an otherwise existing §1983 right, and the State's administrative obligations remain. Appropriate relief presupposes a cognizable action and cannot create the missing right or action.

**Open question:** Private enforceability of §671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains undecided, as do the preserved claim's merits and proper relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992) — Child-welfare statutory enforcement.


## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

### Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

**Governing question:** Whether remedial silence or Spending Clause notice bars compensatory damages in the established Title IX private action.

**Current rule:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing relief does not itself create a new cause of action. Title IX's established private action permits compensatory damages for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation, and compensable loss. The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar that compensation for intentional breach of Title IX's express nondiscrimination condition.

**Authority by component:**

- Appropriate relief in an existing action and availability of Title IX compensation: Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools — Compensatory relief in the established Title IX action, February 26, 1992.
- Rejection of the categorical funding-notice bar: Franklin — Notice does not bar damages for the pleaded intentional violation, February 26, 1992. Both holdings rest on White's opinion joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; Scalia and Thomas supply judgment votes only on a narrower statutory ground.

**Present operation effective:** *Franklin*, February 26, 1992.

**Open question:** The decision supplies no final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, liability for unintended discriminatory effects, or resolution of genuinely ambiguous funding conditions.

**Holdings navigation:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992) — Statutory discrimination remedies.
