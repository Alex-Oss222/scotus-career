## Standards and Tests selection: Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses

### Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses

**Governing question:** Private enforcement of 42 U.S.C. §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause and §671(a)(9)'s reporting clause through §1983 or an action implied directly under the Act.

**Current rule:** Read within the Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, §671(a)(15) creates no individual right enforceable under §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement and caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing right. Nor does §671(a)(15) imply a separate private action: beneficial purpose does not establish congressional intent to create the requested remedy. Section 671(a)(9) requires a plan to provide reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse or exploitation and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B. That administrative direction does not create the asserted private §1983 right or an implied action to obtain caseworker relief. The §1983-right and implied-action inquiries remain distinct; failure to imply an action does not bar an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Authority by component:**
- No individual §671(a)(15) right: Suter v. Artist M., March 25, 1992, “Section 671(a)(15) supplies no individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable under §1983,” Part II.
- No separate implied (15) action: the same record, “Section 671(a)(15) supplies no separate implied action,” Part III.
- Reporting trigger and absence of asserted §1983 right: the same record, “The asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is not enforceable through §1983,” Part IV-A.
- No implied reporting action: the same record, “The asserted §671(a)(9) claim supplies no separate implied action,” Part IV-B. White for O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supplies six controlling joins for each component.

**Present operation effective:** Suter, March 25, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general rule makes state-plan provisions unenforceable. Administrative funding review does not necessarily foreclose an otherwise existing §1983 right, and the State's administrative obligations remain. Appropriate relief presupposes a cognizable action and cannot create the missing right or action.

**Open question:** Private enforceability of §671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains undecided, as do the preserved claim's merits and proper relief.

**Holdings navigation:** Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992) — Child-welfare statutory enforcement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal state enforcement for employees who collectively bargain

### Equal state enforcement for employees who collectively bargain

**Governing question:** Whether a State may withhold ordinary labor-law enforcement solely because employees completed protected collective bargaining.

**Current rule:** A State may not deny enforcement of an otherwise independent wage right solely because the employee’s collective-bargaining agreement contains an arbitration clause. Conditioning state assistance on avoiding completed collective bargaining impermissibly penalizes the federally protected activity. Section 1983 supplies a remedy for the identified individual federal labor right infringed by that policy; a generalized assertion of federal preemption alone is not thereby made an enforceable individual right.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected bargaining and the specific §1983 channel: Livadas v. Bradshaw — California may not penalize completion of protected collective bargaining; Section 1983 supplies a channel for this identified federal labor right, 1994-06-13. Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt this statutory labor-right holding. Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt the right, nonforeclosure and bounded-remedy analysis.

**Present operation effective:** Livadas v. Bradshaw, 1994-06-13.

**Holdings navigation:** Section 1983 supplies a channel for this identified federal labor right; California may not penalize completion of protected collective bargaining.

#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1488.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether the separate b.h. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy (Federal Courts — Mootness and decree scope).

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. The absence of an individual right, the existence of an action, and the scope of relief remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980) — preserves statutory-right enforcement under §1983; a violation of federal law still must invade an enforceable right.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987) — distinguishes its defined rent and utility entitlement; its enforceability holding and separate foreclosure inquiry remain operative.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990) — distinguishes its substantive reimbursement obligation and supplied benchmarks; neither its Medicaid holding nor its rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal is overruled.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981) — applies its clear-notice principle to the asserted funding obligation; no categorical immunity for state-plan provisions is created.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action

**Holding:** The Act does not create an implied private cause of action to enforce §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause because the claimants have not established congressional intent to create that remedy. This conclusion concerns the distinct action asserted directly under the Act and does not make failure to imply an action a bar to an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this independently necessary ground for rejecting the second enforcement route sustained below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to imply a private action does not foreclose an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy; other statutory provisions require their own examination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort v. Ash, 422 U.S. 66 (1975) — applies its inquiry into an implied private remedy, with congressional intent decisive.
- Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis, 444 U.S. 11 (1979) — applies its requirement of congressional intent to create the particular remedy asserted; statutory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992, decision — preserves the distinction between the existence of a cognizable action and appropriate relief within it; no Title IX holding is narrowed.

##### Whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. The reporting ground therefore cannot independently sustain the challenged caseworker injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-A, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this separate statutory holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting obligation and administrative enforcement remain. Section 671(a)(16)’s case-plan and review claim is not decided through this reporting holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wright, 479 U.S. 418 — applies the need to identify a privately enforceable statutory right; its distinct rent holding remains in force.
- Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 — applies provision-specific examination of a binding individual entitlement; no general rule against enforcement of plan provisions replaces it.

##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9) does not create an implied private action for these respondents to obtain the challenged caseworker relief: its reporting requirement and the Act's enforcement structure do not establish congressional intent to authorize that action. Rejection of this distinct statutory vehicle does not decide private enforcement of §671(a)(16).

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-B, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the ground rejecting direct implied enforcement of the reporting claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains open as to enforceable right, implied action, violation and additional lawful relief. No constitutional claim is revived or nationwide three-day deadline imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort, 422 U.S. 66, and Transamerica, 444 U.S. 11 — apply the congressional-intent requirement to the distinct implied reporting action.
- Franklin, February 26, 1992, decision — applies its action/remedy distinction without changing the established Title IX action or relief available within it.

#### Livadas v. Bradshaw, 512 U.S. 107

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1920.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

For the other questions in Livadas v. Bradshaw, 1994-06-13, No. 92-1920, see Labor and Employment — An independent wage-penalty claim is not preempted by §301; Labor and Employment — California may not penalize completion of protected collective bargaining.

##### Section 1983 supplies a channel for this identified federal labor right

**Holding:** Section 1983 permits Livadas to enforce her definite individual federal right to carry protected collective bargaining through to an agreement without this state-imposed loss of enforcement access. The NLRA does not foreclose that action against governmental interference; federal priority alone does not make every preemption rule a privately enforceable right or establish entitlement to a particular remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's unanimous Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg adopt the right, nonforeclosure and bounded-remedy analysis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No personal qualified-immunity question is decided. The Court does not determine the employer's liability, every possible contract waiver, or the effect of reinstatement and backpay on the penalty claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Golden State Transit Corp. v. Los Angeles, 493 U.S. 103: applies its separate federal-right and congressional-foreclosure inquiries to this governmental interference.
- Suter v. Artist M., March 25, 1992: distinguishes its specific absence of the asserted §671(a)(15) and §671(a)(9) rights and implied actions; it creates no universal state-plan or administrative-review bar.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: preserves the distinction between a right, an available cause, immunity and recoverable relief; no agency-damages extension follows here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for source-authorized relief against the official-capacity policy. Monetary-relief and Eleventh Amendment constraints remain distinct. No employer wage penalty, personal damages or automatic reinstatement of a money award is ordered; willfulness, the effect of backpay and other penalty defenses remain unresolved.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Blackmun, joined by Stevens and, on §671(a)(15) only, Stone-Zsela, would apply *Wilder*'s protection of a binding individual entitlement to the mandatory, individualized reasonable-efforts obligation. The absence of the essential caseworker permits a judicial inquiry without prescribing every welfare decision; administrative funding withdrawal does not establish incompatibility with §1983. The supported three-day remedy is confined to this record. Blackmun and Stevens also would sustain the reporting claim under §1983, while Stone-Zsela leaves its enforceability and merits for provision-specific consideration. All three leave a redundant implied action under §671(a)(15) undecided. Stone-Zsela's separate writing preserves that narrower position on §671(a)(9) and distinguishes the existing §671(a)(15) injunction from possible additional relief on remand. These positions do not control the rejected reasonable-efforts and reporting claims.
