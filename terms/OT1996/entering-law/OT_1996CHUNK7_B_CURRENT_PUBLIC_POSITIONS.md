### 1996-11-04 — California v. Roy, No. 95-2025; October Term 1996, chunk 1.

Authority: [Record](../records/California_v_Roy_summary_review_1996-11-04.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The existing official appellate party may seek review

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1254(1) requires a petitioner who was a party in the court of appeals; on this record, that court's repeated identification of the Attorney General as an official-capacity respondent and the timely petition expressly naming the incumbent in that capacity satisfy the requirement. The Court accepts the existing official party's identity despite the contrary earlier custodial pleadings; it neither adds a party nor holds that a caption, counsel's participation or a State's interest invariably establishes party status.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls by eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejecting the objection and reaching the case, without joining this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the official litigant identified by the court of appeals may seek review through the incumbent Attorney General. Scofield requires a party below; Perini keeps that statutory requirement distinct from injury. California's interest in its conviction alone would not suffice.

The amended habeas petition names Gomez and Borg, the answer identifies them as proper respondents, and the subsequent order substitutes Merkle for Borg. Those documents establish custodial participation and replacement. They do not supply an appellate ruling removing the Attorney General's office. The June 1995 panel opinion, September rehearing order and April 1996 en banc opinion repeatedly name Van de Kamp as a respondent. The Court accepts that appellate identification on these competing documents. Lungren's timely, expressly official petition identifies the incumbent of the same office.

FRAP 43(c)(2) and FRCP 25(d)(2) permit designation of an existing official-capacity party by title, subject to the court's authority to require a name. Their corresponding automatic-substitution provisions operate when an existing official-capacity party leaves office during the respective appellate proceeding or action. They disregard an immaterial misnomer, and omission of a formal substitution order does not defeat substitution. These rules continue an existing party; they do not create one. The Court does not assume that the change of Attorney General occurred during this appeal or this Court's proceeding. The appellate identification is the decisive predicate, not an imagined intervention or succession order.

Smith v. Barry supports reading a timely document for the litigant it actually identifies, while retaining mandatory party information. No custodian is added to the petition under Rule 12.4, and Rule 35.3 does not establish original party status below. Whether the People separately qualify is unnecessary to decide. The Attorney General's appearance as counsel does not independently establish party status.

**Precedent treatment:** Scofield and Perini retain their actual-party requirement; Smith is applied to identification in a timely filing without dispensing with that requirement.

##### II. This personal-intent omission permits harmless-error review under Chapman

**Holding and operative rule:** The preserved omission of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery is a constitutional trial error subject to harmlessness review, not an error requiring automatic reversal merely because an element was omitted. For this personal-intent violation properly available on federal habeas, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict; neither collateral posture nor absence of accompanying prosecutorial argument substitutes a weaker burden.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously. Agreement on this category and burden does not establish agreement on the method or result of harmlessness review.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the jury's responsibility to determine the defendant's required intent remains protected, while the effect of this instructional omission may be examined. Gaudin requires jury determination of an offense element but does not classify every failure to submit an element as structural. Sullivan addresses a different defect: an unconstitutional reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings leaves no valid reasonable-doubt verdict to examine. The omission here does not destroy that standard throughout the charge.

O'Neal requires the State's Chapman showing for its preserved personal-intent category. That case involved combined instructions and prosecutorial argument permitting conviction without the defendant's own required intent. This charge permits the same constitutional failure by omitting the purpose to facilitate robbery. An additional prosecutorial statement is not the basis of the protection and supplies no reason to withhold it here. Knowledge of the perpetrator's unlawful purpose and affirmative assistance were required, so mere presence was insufficient; neither knowledge nor assistance is legally identical to the omitted purpose.

The State retains the burden. Roy need not prove innocence, and the reviewing court may not supply the missing intent itself. This decision does not adopt Chapman for every constitutional habeas claim, reopen a resolved exhaustion issue, or decide the temporal operation of AEDPA.

**Precedent treatment:** Gaudin's jury-allocation rule is applied and its structural-error reservation respected; Sullivan's rule remains intact at its stated scope; O'Neal's protected personal-intent category applies to the omission without accompanying argument; Chapman's actual-contribution burden remains the governing burden.

##### III. Necessary equivalence is sufficient, but not the exclusive permissible inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Chapman, the State may establish that this omission did not contribute to the actual verdict through supported whole-record inferences as well as findings necessarily embracing the omitted purpose; it may not substitute evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction, equate knowledge plus assistance with purpose, or infer purpose from general mental capacity. Under Yates, the court must examine whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting the proposed inference; if the omission kept the relevant personal-purpose question from the jury so that the inference cannot reliably address its actual verdict, strong evidence alone does not carry the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, controls by five direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports the remand on his separately stated whole-record Chapman ground but does not join Part III's further analysis. Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg dissent from the remand. No combination of separate opinions or narrower-ground synthesis supplies this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the inquiry concerns what the error did to the verdict actually returned. Yates requires examination of the evidence the instructions put before the jury and distinguishes actual contribution from proof sufficient to convict. Necessary equivalence between a finding and the omitted purpose is one sufficient route. It is not invariably the only way to determine the effect of this knowledge-and-assistance charge.

The Ninth Circuit expressly found the omission not harmless under Chapman before applying a separate collateral-review analysis. Remand is warranted because its necessary-findings restriction foreclosed the qualified actual-effect inquiry, not because Chapman was never considered. The charge submitted knowledge, affirmative aid and related mental-state questions. That permits examination of their connection to the actual verdict, without assuming the jury considered the omitted purpose. The receiving court must address the possibility that the omission itself prevented the relevant consideration; inability to establish a reliable connection defeats the State's showing.

The record requires more than an overwhelming-evidence label. Possession of property, wet clothing, assistance testimony and reported statements must be assessed with the verdicts and the conflicts in those accounts. The clothing allocated to McHargue in the lower opinions cannot be treated as undisputed property in Roy's backpack. Hudspeth's impeachment and rebuttal and Hall's reported statements and retraction matter together; no verdict establishes wholesale acceptance or rejection of every witness. The possible inference that Roy helped a companion without intending to facilitate robbery is an inference to assess, not a newly found event. The State's and district court's functional-equivalence conclusions are legal assessments, not express jury findings. These matters remain for the proper inquiry, and the Ninth Circuit may again conclude that the State fails its burden.

**Precedent treatment:** Chapman and Yates are applied together to permit a supported actual-effect inquiry while preserving the jury's role; neither sufficiency alone nor a hypothetical verdict satisfies them. O'Neal's whole-record command remains subject to that actual-verdict requirement.

##### IV. The limited remand preserves the record, argument and remedial boundaries

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is vacated for a qualified Chapman review of the preserved personal-intent claim affecting the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions, with District Court proceedings if otherwise proper; no present harmlessness finding follows. If an available violation is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, conditional relief must allow the State a reasonable period to retry those convictions; proper harmlessness permits denial, and neither this remand nor the special-circumstance argument restores reversed punishment, affects the Clark conviction or orders immediate release.

**Authority:** Part III's remand instructions have the five direct joins stated above; Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote and agrees separately with the Chapman and reasonable-retrial remedy. The five-Justice rationale itself controls without enlarging Stone-Zsela's join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that renewed review must apply the proper method to the supported record and respect genuine preservation limits. O'Neal distinguishes a corrected harmlessness inquiry from an automatic writ and requires a reasonable retrial opportunity if an available violation warrants relief. No fixed retrial deadline is set.

Yee distinguishes an additional argument about a preserved question from a new constitutional claim. The special-circumstance argument concerns this preserved harmlessness question, but Roy's objection to its first-instance use still requires consideration. The asserted oral concession is not established, and the objection alone creates no forfeiture.

The instructions must be read together. CALJIC 8.80's intentional-aid requirement applies conditionally to a defendant who was not the actual killer; it is not an unconditional finding of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery. Absence of personal knife use does not itself eliminate every actual-killer theory. CALJIC 8.81.17's robbery-related purpose of the killing differs from Roy's own aiding purpose. The state reversal prevents restoration of the special-circumstance punishment; it does not automatically determine every evidentiary implication of the findings. General specific-intent or diminished-capacity instructions do not silently insert the omitted purpose clause.

The Court therefore adopts neither an automatic special-circumstance cure nor a categorical exclusion of the findings from consideration. It supplies no new trial finding, admissibility ruling on a juror declaration, exhaustion bar, or factual resolution from an unavailable transcript passage. Preservation, independent habeas requirements and any event-date statutory transition remain distinct. This remand addresses the identified method defect, without reviving a resolved gate or ordering new factfinding merely because a source is unavailable.

**Precedent treatment:** Yee preserves the distinction between a new argument and a new claim without compelling disputed first-instance application; O'Neal supplies the conditional-relief and reasonable-retrial rule, without automatic release.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **UAW v. Scofield, 382 U.S. 205:** Applies the requirement that a petitioner under §1254(1) be an actual party below; official identification satisfies it here without permitting review by any interested nonparty.
- **Director, OWCP v. Perini North River Associates, 459 U.S. 297:** Preserves the distinction between statutory party status and injury; a State's enforcement interest alone does not answer the party question.
- **Smith v. Barry:** Applies practical identification in a timely filing while retaining mandatory information about the actual litigant; no late party is added.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury allocation of an offense element and respects its reservation of universal structural-error treatment.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes a particular purpose omission from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard for all findings; its structural rule remains intact.
- **O'Neal v. McAninch:** Applies its State-borne Chapman rule to this preserved personal-intent omission without requiring accompanying argument, and retains conditional relief with a reasonable retrial opportunity. Its unadopted general extension to all ordinary constitutional trial errors does not become law.
- **Chapman v. California:** Retains the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to show that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies the actual-contribution and instructional-focus requirements; the Court rejects both sufficiency alone and an exclusive necessary-findings method for this charge, subject to the stated reliability qualification.
- **Yee v. City of Escondido:** Applies the difference between an additional argument about a preserved question and a new claim without eliminating legitimate preservation and first-instance limits.

#### Law After Decision

Effective November 4, 1996, the defined personal-intent habeas category includes this preserved omission of the defendant's purpose to facilitate robbery. It does not depend on accompanying prosecutorial argument. The State must satisfy Chapman through a permissible inquiry into the actual verdict. Necessary findings may suffice, but the controlling method also permits supported inferences meeting the express Yates condition; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to the actual verdict. Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive actual-findings approach does not control, and Stevens's vote to affirm does not join that approach.

The existing official-party requirement remains unchanged. This record satisfies it through the appellate identification of the Attorney General's office and the timely official petition, without a rule making every caption conclusive. No general Chapman rule for all habeas errors, new innocence gate, automatic special-circumstance cure, exhaustion ruling, statutory-transition ruling or universal structural-error rule is established. No holding on harmlessness of this actual record is made. The conditional-relief rule remains operative if the State fails its showing on an available violation.



### 1997-02-18 - Ingalls Shipbuilding, Inc. v. Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs; No. 95-1081.

Authority: [Record](../records/Ingalls_Shipbuilding_v_Director_OWCP_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. A spouse's contingent death-benefit claim does not trigger predeath forfeiture

**Holding and operative rule:** An injured worker's spouse is not a person entitled to LHWCA death-benefit compensation before the covered death and qualifying survivorship that create that statutory right. A spouse's predeath third-party release therefore does not trigger §33(g)'s employer-approval forfeiture of the later death-benefit claim merely because the release covers a possible future wrongful-death action.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Cowart makes an existing substantive right, rather than payment or a formal award, the entitlement threshold. Mrs. Yates had no vested §9 death-benefit right when the predeath releases were signed. Her husband was alive, and the required covered death and her qualifying survivorship might never occur. Her consortium claim and possible wrongful-death claim are not the distinct federal death-benefit entitlement; her husband's disability entitlement does not become hers. The later death and causation stipulation cannot retroactively supply those missing prerequisites.

Ingalls argues that approval protects its exposure and that the words concerning compensation to which a person would be entitled include potential beneficiaries. That comparison measures the compensation of a person already entitled when settling; it does not erase the threshold identifying that person. The employer's protective policy cannot add a forfeiture the enacted condition does not reach. Cowart's current approval, filing and notification requirements remain distinct: covered settlements below entitlement require prescribed prior written employer-and-carrier approval and thirty-day filing, while judgments and settlements at least equal to the compensation entitlement require notification rather than that prior approval. The Court does not hold that every third-party recovery requires approval or apply later procedural amendments automatically to releases ending in January 1984. It decides no vested disability claim, postdeath settlement, benefit amount, §33(f) offset allocation, contract credit or deference question.

**Precedent treatment:** Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co. is applied at its existing-right threshold; its payment-independent entitlement rule and separate approval/notification branches remain in force.

##### II. The Director may respond in this private-party review

**Holding and operative rule:** The Director of OWCP, as the Secretary of Labor's designated representative, is a proper respondent under the then-effective Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 15(a) in an employer-initiated appeal from a Benefits Review Board order while the private parties maintain a live controversy. This respondent role does not authorize an independent Director appeal based only on a general administrative interest or decide whether the Director could alone continue the controversy after all private adversity ends.

**Authority:** Part II of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this component.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 15(a) requires the agency to be named respondent and includes an agency, board, commission or officer within that term. The Department administers the Act while the Board performs its independent adjudicative work. That internal division does not require the adjudicator itself to advocate or exclude the Department's designated litigating representative from the existing review. The 1996 designation regulation identifies the Director as the Secretary's proper party in §21(c) proceedings. Section 921a provides Secretary-appointed representation in the stated court proceedings but expressly excepts this Court. Those representation provisions identify who acts for the Department; Rule 15 supplies the respondent-status ground.

The Board-only objection confuses the allocation of decisional responsibility within the Department with its representation in appellate review. The Director need not have authored or defend the Board's order to represent the Department's view. Newport News denied independent §921(c) review based on general administrative interest and reserved respondent status. Ingalls initiated this review; it and Mrs. Yates supply the live controversy. The holding neither manufactures aggrievement by regulation nor extends jurisdiction through party nomenclature. It grants no relief outside the live appeal, resolves no special-fund case and adopts no merits-deference rule.

**Precedent treatment:** Director, OWCP v. Newport News Shipbuilding & Dry Dock Co. retains its independent-review limit; the respondent question it reserved is now resolved only for this live private-party review.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Estate of Cowart v. Nicklos Drilling Co.: applied to distinguish vested compensation rights from potential future benefits; no payment-or-award prerequisite is restored and no general approval exception is created.
- Director, OWCP v. Newport News Shipbuilding & Dry Dock Co.: its bar on independent review based solely on general administrative interest remains; its respondent-status reservation is resolved at the limited scope of the present private-party appeal. Its distinct Special Fund reservation is preserved.

#### Law After Decision

Section 33(g)'s entitlement threshold does not attach to a spouse's merely potential death-benefit right before the covered death and qualifying survivorship. The Director may represent the Department as respondent under Rule 15(a) in the live review brought by Ingalls, while Newport News's independent-appeal limit survives. Neither holding decides compensation amounts, §33(f) credits, private contract interpretation, universal postdeath-settlement treatment or litigation after private adversity ends.



### 1997-02-26 - United States v. Wells; No. 95-1228; respondents Jerry E. Wells and Kenneth R. Steele.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Wells_merits_1997-02-26.md).

#### Holdings

#### The Court may review the statutory question actually decided below

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government may argue that §1014 lacks a materiality element where that legal question was presented in appellate briefing, decided by the court of appeals and included in the question granted, despite its earlier request for instructions under then-prevailing circuit law. This bounded choice to review statutory meaning does not excuse every waiver or decide the independent effect of the indictment, instructions or case-specific prejudice.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Lebron permits consideration of an included legal argument on an adequate established record while preserving limits on distinct claims, questions and missing facts. Here the Government presented its construction in supplemental briefing after Gaudin, the Eighth Circuit passed on it, and respondents have had notice and a full opportunity to contest it. The Court is reviewing that appellate statutory ruling, not introducing a new factual theory or deciding a question never presented below.

The Government's trial position does not become irrelevant. Rule 30's restriction on assigning error in an accepted charge and the doctrines governing invited error may matter to particular trial objections. They do not require the Court to leave the expressly decided statutory construction unreviewed. An allegation of materiality in the indictment or an instruction affecting the jury's understanding of falsity may have consequences apart from whether Congress made materiality an element. Those matters remain for lawful consideration rather than being resolved against respondents by the decision to reach the statute.

**Precedent treatment:** Lebron's discretionary included-argument principle is applied to an actually decided and granted legal question, without turning it into a universal preservation exception.

#### Section 1014 does not add an objective materiality element

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1014 does not require an additional finding that a statement had a natural tendency or objective capacity to influence the institution's decision. The Government must still prove beyond a reasonable doubt the applicable conduct—knowingly making a false statement or report, or willfully overvaluing land, property or security—and the purpose of influencing an institution enumerated by the statute in a covered transaction.

The covered transactions are an application, advance, discount, purchase, purchase agreement, repurchase agreement, commitment or loan; a change or extension of one through renewal, deferment or otherwise; or acceptance, release or substitution of its security. The institutional requirement includes the FDIC-insured institutions relevant here; it is not displaced by calling a transaction financial.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. These eight Justices also support vacating the appellate new-trial judgment's contrary statutory premise. Stevens dissents from this holding and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted provision addresses any knowing false statement and expressly requires a purpose to influence the specified institution. It does not add the different objective-capacity requirement respondents seek. Purpose and materiality are not identical, but recognizing their difference does not permit inserting the second condition. The statute's fault, institutional and transaction requirements must each be enforced; a practical expectation that consequential lies will predominate, prosecutorial restraint or sentencing discretion cannot replace them.

Respondents rely on received false-statement law and the consolidation note's assurance of no unlisted substantive change. Those sources warrant consideration, but they do not establish a settled meaning of the enacted phrase that overcomes this provision's complete wording and express purpose condition. The Court does not rest on an exact count of predecessor statutes or infer that every omission in every criminal statute is decisive. Nor does punishment severity create an element after the provision's text and context resolve the issue. Lenity applies when genuine interpretive ambiguity remains, not as an automatic addition of the disputed requirement.

Kay rejected a defense based on lack of actual influence under its predecessor text; it does not exhaustively construe every later provision. Williams held that the particular checks were not factual assertions; it did not establish universal materiality. Gaudin assigns an offense element to the jury when the statute contains it and does not create that element for §1014. Knowledge, the applicable false-statement or willful-overvaluation conduct, influencing purpose and statutory coverage remain necessary jury findings under the governing criminal standard. The Court decides neither every §1001 clause nor every fraud statute.

**Precedent treatment:** Kay and Williams retain their actual predecessor-statute and false-assertion holdings without supplying a universal materiality element. Gaudin's jury-allocation rule remains fully controlling for genuine offense elements.

#### Acquittals and independently available trial issues remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** The decision on materiality does not reopen the two acquittals or itself resolve independent indictment, instruction, evidentiary, conspiracy or sentencing grounds. The court of appeals must first determine which remaining grounds are properly available and then adjudicate them under their own law; reserving a ground neither certifies its preservation nor requires a new trial, conviction reinstatement or any particular sentence.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices as to these limits. The eight-Justice vacatur rests on Part II; Stevens would instead affirm the new-trial judgment under his retained materiality requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on a statutory element the Court rejects. Removing that premise does not answer whether the indictment's materiality allegation has an independent consequence or whether the actual falsity instruction affected a finding the statute genuinely requires. The lower court addressed some conspiracy and expert-evidence questions but left other materiality-expert, multiple-conspiracy and Government sentencing questions unresolved when it ordered retrial. None is automatically decided by the present holding.

Roy distinguishes a legally available harmless-error inquiry from an actual finding that a particular error did not affect a verdict. Its preserved personal-intent habeas holding supplies no automatic answer for this charge or direct appeal. The Court neither decides that an independent violation occurred nor substitutes appellate findings for the jury. Further proceedings concern the two appealed convictions only; the two acquittals remain outside every permitted remedy.

**Precedent treatment:** Gaudin and Roy retain their distinct element-allocation and error-review functions; neither supplies an automatic result on the independent grounds left open here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp. permits the bounded review of an included legal argument; review here does not abolish waiver, invited error or limits on new claims and facts.
- United States v. Gaudin continues to require the jury to determine every actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt. This decision construes §1014 and does not narrow that constitutional rule.
- Kay v. United States supports rejecting an actual-influence defense within its predecessor setting without deciding every later false-statement statute.
- Williams v. United States retains its holding that the particular checks were not factual assertions; it supplies no universal materiality rule.
- California v. Roy preserves the distinction between permissible error review and an established harmlessness result. Its particular personal-intent habeas rule is not automatically transferred to this direct appeal.

#### Law After Decision

Materiality is not an additional §1014 element. Actual statutory knowledge, false-statement or willful-overvaluation conduct, influencing purpose, institutional coverage and transaction coverage remain essential. The Government's earlier trial position does not bar this decided-and-granted statutory question, but independently available trial objections retain their force. No conviction, sentence or acquitted count is automatically restored.



### 1996-11-04 — California v. Roy, No. 95-2025; October Term 1996, chunk 1.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment; no joins.** He joins Part II and supports vacatur and remand because the Ninth Circuit's necessity formulation does not replace the whole-record Chapman inquiry required for the preserved personal-intent claim. Gaudin protects jury determination of elements without making every omission structural; Sullivan's destruction of the reasonable-doubt verdict differs from this assessable omission. O'Neal puts on the State the burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the violation did not contribute to the verdict. He would neither supply intent himself, shift the burden to Roy nor demand innocence. If a properly available violation fails the State's showing, relief for the affected convictions must permit a reasonable retrial opportunity. Preservation, exhaustion, independent habeas requirements and statutory transition remain separate. He makes no final harmlessness finding and sets no retrial deadline. He does not join Parts I or III's additional reasoning.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** He joins Parts I and II but would affirm the habeas-relief judgment on this record. His Rose concurrence and his agreement with Yates require rigorous inquiry into the error's contribution to the actual verdict. The distinct omitted purpose, the supported inference of companion assistance without a robbery purpose, the disputed advance-plan account and the absence of an established independent purpose finding prevent the State from proving harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt. This does not establish that every defense assertion is true or that the jury rejected all of Hudspeth's testimony. Stevens accepts that the Ninth Circuit's general necessary-findings formula is too broad; he does not adopt it as the exclusive rule for every omission. A proper actual-contribution inquiry nevertheless supports the judgment already reached. No identified unfinished material task justifies another remand merely to repeat an adverse Chapman determination. He would leave conditional relief for the two Mannix convictions in place, with a reasonable retrial opportunity and no immediate unconditional release.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** They join Parts I and II but would affirm. For this omitted-purpose error, harmlessness requires that the jury's other determinations effectively embrace Roy's required purpose, or that the evidence and findings establish that the jury could not have reached what it did find without also finding that purpose. The State must establish that connection beyond a reasonable doubt. Carella's separate opinion and Scalia's Yates opinion explain why independent appellate certainty that a properly instructed jury would convict cannot replace the jury's actual findings; Sullivan and Gaudin preserve the jury's constitutional role. Whole-record review identifies what the actual verdict embraces; it does not authorize an appellate substitute verdict.

Knowledge and assistance do not necessarily establish purpose. Neither the Clark findings nor general capacity answers the missing Mannix purpose. The conditional nonkiller clause, the distinction between intentional assistance to first-degree murder and purpose to facilitate robbery, the robbery-related purpose of the killing, and the cross-reference to deficient aiding instructions prevent treating the special circumstances as an established equivalent finding. The asserted preservation objection does not itself supply forfeiture, and the record does not identify an unresolved genuine finding question requiring another remand. A valid equivalent finding could make an omission harmless, so their rule is not automatic structural reversal. They would affirm conditional relief only for the two Mannix convictions, allowing a reasonable retrial period. They adopt no innocence prerequisite or discretionary-habeas rule, and Ginsburg joins no such proposition.



### 1997-02-18 - Ingalls Shipbuilding, Inc. v. Director, Office of Workers' Compensation Programs; No. 95-1081.

Authority: [Record](../records/Ingalls_Shipbuilding_v_Director_OWCP_merits_1997-02-18.md).

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Thomas.** Both join Part I's refusal to forfeit the death-benefit claim based on predeath releases. They would reverse only the ruling that the Director is a party respondent. In their reading, Rule 15 names the agency whose order is reviewed; here that entity is the independent Benefits Review Board. The Department's common administrative umbrella does not turn the Director into the Board or make its order his. A command to name the agency whose order is reviewed does not independently confer substantive party capacity on every officer designated to represent the Department. Section 921a and the regulation address representation where capacity exists, and cannot create the missing capacity; section 921a also expressly excepts proceedings in this Court. Rule 1(b)'s prohibition on enlarging or diminishing jurisdiction corroborates the limit on using procedural rules to create an entitlement to participate. Newport News's restriction on independent review and reservation of respondent status do not supply an affirmative respondent right. Continuing adversity between the employer and widow establishes the controversy but does not answer who may enter it as a party. The Director may present the Department's position as an amicus, a nonparty friend of the court; genuine special-fund or financial-interest cases present different questions. Their objection does not undermine the private appeal, require forfeiture of the widow's claim or decide those other settings.



### 1997-02-26 - United States v. Wells; No. 95-1228; respondents Jerry E. Wells and Kenneth R. Steele.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Wells_merits_1997-02-26.md).

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He agrees that the Court may decide the statutory issue and that acquittals and independent trial matters retain their proper limits, but would construe §1014 to require materiality and affirm the new-trial judgment. The received meaning of false-statement offenses, the statute's objective and the consolidation assurance support retaining that limitation; Congress's omission of the word does not alone show an intention to abolish it.

In his view, a speaker's purpose to curry favor differs from a statement's objective capacity to matter to the institution. The majority's culpability requirements therefore do not answer the risk of imposing serious criminal liability for trivial flattery or an inconsequential lie. His Aguilar and X-Citement positions support examining the offense's received legal meaning and object, rather than grammar alone. Williams and restraint in construing serious criminal prohibitions reinforce the narrower reading. Kay rejected an actual-reliance defense under its predecessor; objective materiality does not require proof that the decisionmaker actually relied on the statement.

Once materiality is retained, Gaudin requires the jury to decide it. Respondents contested that issue and the judge expressly removed it from their jury's deliberations. Stevens would preserve the new-trial judgment on that record, not order acquittal or declare every omitted-element error automatically reversible. Roy permits harmlessness review within its category but does not establish harmlessness of this different withdrawal. His position leaves the two acquittals intact and decides no independent instruction, indictment, evidentiary, conspiracy or sentencing issue unnecessarily.

### 1997-02-26 - United States v. Wells; No. 95-1228; respondents Jerry E. Wells and Kenneth R. Steele.

Authority: [Record](../records/United_States_v_Wells_merits_1997-02-26.md).
