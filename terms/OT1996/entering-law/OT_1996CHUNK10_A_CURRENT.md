### 1997-01-13 — Walmer v. Department of Defense; Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377, 52 F.3d 851; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

Authority: [Record](../records/Walmer_v_Department_of_Defense_merits_1997-01-13.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Ordinary rationality sustains this preliminary denial on the stipulated conduct record

**Holding and operative rule:** The challenged conduct-based military personnel rule receives ordinary rational-basis review, which permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable predictions and imperfect fit while requiring a relationship to the actual disadvantage and forbidding bare harm or irrational prejudice. On this preliminary record of admitted homosexual acts and stipulated ineligibility for the retention exceptions, discipline, morale and workable military service relationships supply a permissible predictive relationship, so denial of equal-protection preliminary relief is affirmed without a final ruling on another or materially developed record.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins to this ground and the preliminary disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Beach permits conceivable legitimate reasons without formal findings or empirical certainty. Heller requires a relation to the specific disadvantage and also preserves relevant classwide generalizations. Romer applies those limits to a blanket civil disability; its ordinary-review rule neither assigns an orientation scrutiny tier nor makes every conduct-based personnel classification invalid. The Court examines this military rule's connection to discipline, morale and workable service relationships instead of accepting the military label as an answer.

The stipulated acts and unavailable retention exceptions define this application. Reasonable military predictions may address service relationships classwide without proof that this officer's performance has already impaired a unit. Goldman and Rostker preserve substantial respect for professional military judgments while leaving constitutional review intact. A more individualized policy is not constitutionally required merely because it is possible. Bare private hostility would not be a legitimate objective, but the identified personnel relationship supplies a distinct predictive ground here.

Rich's fraudulent-entry facts are not this record, and its suggestion about satisfying a hypothetical stricter standard is unnecessary. The Court sustains the preliminary denial through direct ordinary-review analysis, not factual identity with Rich or automatic foreclosure by it. Bowers's specified-conduct holding does not itself decide the equal-protection tier or application. Kelley and Virginia concern sex classifications and supply no transferred burden here.

Walmer's preliminary showing does not establish the requisite merits basis for relief under the inquiry applied below. The Court finds none of the other three equitable factors satisfied merely because the court of appeals assumed them. It orders no discharge and decides neither an orientation-only inference, later statements legislation nor another claimant's exception eligibility.

**Precedent treatment:** Beach, both relevant Heller propositions and Romer are applied at their actual ordinary-review scope; Goldman and Rostker supply deference without immunity. Bowers, Kelley and Virginia are distinguished at the questions they decided.

#### II. The attainder prohibition does not extend to this executive military regulation

**Holding and operative rule:** The Bill of Attainder Clause restrains legislative punishment, and the challenged executive Army regulation does not satisfy that legislative-action predicate. The attainder ground therefore fails without deciding whether the separation would constitute punishment if the required legislative predicate existed; other constitutional, statutory and administrative restraints on executive action remain distinct.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous direct authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books identify legislative action as an antecedent condition distinct from identification and punishment. Walmer does not contend that this measure is legislation; she asks to extend the prohibition to executive military regulation. The constitutional allocation does not support that extension. Other sources of law may constrain executive abuse without changing the Clause's institutional target.

The district court also discussed punishment, but the court of appeals did not reach it and the Court has no need to do so. An honorable-separation label is therefore not held conclusively nonpunitive, and no hypothetical legislative separation measure is adjudicated. The abandoned APA theory is not revived; neither an independent due-process claim nor an unpresented statutory challenge is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books supply and preserve the legislative-action prerequisite. Their distinct punishment inquiries are neither displaced nor answered for this separation.

#### Precedent Treatment

- FCC v. Beach Communications: applies conceivable legitimate grounds and tolerance for imperfect fit without formal findings, subject to the prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice.
- Heller v. Doe: preserves the need for a relationship to the actual disadvantage and the separate allowance for relevant family-information generalizations; no heightened scrutiny follows from either holding.
- Romer v. Evans: applies ordinary rational-basis limits and distinguishes its blanket withdrawal and foreclosure of civil protection from this service-specific conduct rule. It is neither overruled nor converted into a Government actual-objective burden.
- Goldman v. Weinberger and Rostker v. Goldberg: preserve substantial military deference without constitutional immunity; an established right's separate scrutiny rule is not imported into this ordinary equality inquiry.
- Bowers v. Hardwick: remains effective at its specified-conduct question and does not automatically settle this equal-protection tier or application.
- Kelley and United States v. Virginia: retain their sex-classification requirements; their burdens are not transferred to a different classification by analogy alone.
- Nixon v. Administrator of General Services, Selective Service System v. Minnesota Public Interest Research Group, and Ambassador Books: preserve legislative action as necessary to an attainder claim, separately from punishment. No new ruling on the punishment character of separation is entered.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 13, 1997, ordinary rational-basis review governs this preliminary, stipulated-conduct challenge to the earlier Army regulation, and the identified military personnel relationship sustains denial of interim relief. The Court creates no heightened sexual-orientation tier, Government actual-purpose proof burden, individualized-impairment prerequisite, least-restrictive-means rule or constitutional right to military service. Reasonable military judgments remain reviewable, and bare hostility remains insufficient. The executive regulation cannot be challenged as a bill of attainder on the presented extension theory, but other legal restraints are not displaced. The retention exceptions remain part of the regulation; their complete criteria, validity and application to another claimant are not adjudicated.


### 1997-02-18 - Uhlrig v. Harder; Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190, 64 F.3d 567; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

Authority: [Record](../records/Uhlrig_v_Harder_merits_1997-02-18.md).

#### Holdings

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's creation of roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise concern affirmative conduct. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver. Neither lower-court disagreement nor absence of identical facts independently establishes immunity. Collins, decided after Uhlrig's death, and later Elder and Pinder govern present analysis but cannot provide retrospective notice. The Court leaves constitutional culpability, the affirmative-danger merits and the Tenth Circuit's five-part test undecided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald supplies objective qualified immunity and remains intact.
- Anderson v. Creighton supplies particularized apparent unlawfulness without requiring an identical prior case.
- Elder v. Holloway is applied to require examination of all relevant conduct-time authority while preserving the favorable factual account and ordinary claim/evidence limits; it supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Pinder v. Johnson is applied to distinguish an affirmative deprivation from omitted protection and to keep notice separate from merits. Its March 1989 result does not resolve this later conduct automatically, and its substantive reservation remains open.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights retains its no-general-workplace-duty rule and its separate, bounded arbitrariness holding. It does not reject every affirmative-danger claim or supply notice before its February 26, 1992 decision.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County and Daniels v. Williams preserve the distinctions between a general protective duty, an affirmative deprivation and negligence; the Court does not convert them into a complete constitutional answer here.
- Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are considered as conduct-time circuit authority at their actual scopes, without converting their formulations into Supreme Court holdings.

#### Law After Decision

The decision establishes only the particularized immunity result for the five administrators on this reviewed claim. It supplies no general affirmative-danger test, categorical public-employment bar, mandatory deceit or assurance requirement, no-awareness finding or constitutional approval of budget choices. The distinction between a general workplace-safety duty and an attributable affirmative deprivation remains. A different official act, record or conduct-time legal landscape requires its own analysis.


### 1997-03-03 — Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona; No. 95-974.

Authority: [Record](../records/Arizonans_for_Official_English_v_Arizona_merits_1997-03-03.md).

#### Holdings

Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined throughout by the other eight participating Justices. Each proposition below commands all nine votes; no alternative rationale or combination of separate writings is needed.

##### A continuing personal controversy is absent

**Holding and operative rule.** An individual employee's prospective challenge to an employment rule becomes moot when she voluntarily leaves the covered employment, alleges no intention to return and establishes no realistic recurrence or surviving claim. A general prayer for relief cannot preserve that controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person.

**Controlling explanation.** Preiser requires a live controversy throughout review. Yniguez brought an individual action and left state employment for another job; no class, replacement claimant or supported repetition exception remains. Will's statutory limit and Hafer's distinction between official and individual capacities defeat the inferred nominal-damages route. The initially named officers' personal-capacity claims had been dismissed, and the appeal did not keep those claims or a former co-plaintiff alive. This is not a general requirement that every complaint expressly say nominal damages: the obstacle here is the absence of a legally available damages claim against a remaining defendant. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity. A proposed consensual payment cannot create the cause of action, and the Attorney General's permitted constitutional argument under §2403(b) did not reverse the appellate court's unrevised denial of State party status. Agreement among litigants cannot supply Article III power. A pleaded claim against an amenable individual defendant would present a different question, which the Court does not decide.

##### Corrective authority and equitable judgment clearing

**Holding and operative rule.** The Court may correct a lower federal court's jurisdictional error without exercising authority over the underlying constitutional merits. Exercising that limited authority here, it vacates the appellate judgment and directs vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal as moot, leaving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing unresolved.

**Controlling explanation.** Bender, applying Corrick, supplies the limited power to correct the lower court's exercise of jurisdiction; §2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Neither intervention, a notice of appeal nor political commitment establishes a sponsor's automatic standing. Lujan and Brown Group preserve the need for constitutional injury or legally sufficient representation. Once the corrective basis is established, Bancorp requires an equitable judgment-disposition inquiry, not automatic vacatur. The prevailing plaintiff left the covered employment during postjudgment review efforts; this was not a reviewing loser's settlement or abandonment. The notices and attempted State intervention preceded her departure, and the appellate court later adjudicated constitutionality after her individual controversy had ended. With no lawful damages residue, leaving either constitutional judgment operative would preserve unreviewable merits adjudication. Munsingwear's judgment-clearing practice and Anderson support that remedy at Bancorp's equitable scope. The Court does not declare the original declaration void when entered, resolve every intervention ruling or the complete Attorney General notice, fix the precise day within April 1990 when mootness attached, or infer improper conduct from the resignation or its reporting.

##### Certification cannot restore the controversy

**Holding and operative rule.** Certification of an unsettled state-law question may assist a live federal controversy, but it cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction. No certification order issues in this moot action.

**Controlling explanation.** Arizona's highest court is the authoritative expositor of Article XXVIII. Authoritative construction of an unsettled provision may clarify a constitutional dispute in later live litigation. That discretionary means of clarification supplies neither a new plaintiff nor a surviving injury here. The Court neither construes Article XXVIII nor decides its First Amendment validity, and it imposes no mandatory certification rule for every future case.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Preiser v. Newkirk**: Applied to require a continuing controversy, not merely an originally live dispute.
- **Will v. Michigan Department of State Police**: Applied to preserve State and official-capacity nonpersonhood for §1983 damages while distinguishing genuine individual-capacity liability; immunity waiver cannot erase the statutory limit.
- **Hafer v. Melo**: Applied to preserve State and official-capacity nonpersonhood for §1983 damages while distinguishing genuine individual-capacity liability; immunity waiver cannot erase the statutory limit.
- **Bender v. Williamsport Area School District**: Applied for limited correction of lower-court jurisdictional error without reaching the underlying merits.
- **United States v. Corrick**: Applied for limited correction of lower-court jurisdictional error without reaching the underlying merits.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife**: Their constitutional injury and representation limits remain intact; Brown Group's particular statutory relaxation of a prudential condition creates no initiative-sponsor exception to Article III.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group**: Their constitutional injury and representation limits remain intact; Brown Group's particular statutory relaxation of a prudential condition creates no initiative-sponsor exception to Article III.
- **U.S. Bancorp Mortgage v. Bonner Mall Partnership**: Applied as an equitable vacatur rule, distinguishing unilateral action by the prevailing party from a reviewing loser's voluntary settlement.
- **United States v. Munsingwear**: Applied to judgment clearing at that equitable scope, without an automatic-vacatur command.
- **Anderson v. Green**: Applied to judgment clearing at that equitable scope, without an automatic-vacatur command.

No precedent is overruled. Sponsor standing in a different state-law setting and Article XXVIII's meaning and constitutionality remain open.

#### Law After Decision

A past individual employment dispute does not authorize continued constitutional review after its prospective injury ends, and a legally unavailable damages theory cannot preserve it. Limited corrective review and equitable vacatur remain distinct from merits jurisdiction. The law creates no automatic federal standing for initiative sponsors and leaves Article XXVIII for authoritative construction and any proper constitutional challenge in live litigation.


### 1997-03-18 — Young v. Harper; No. 95-1598.

Authority: [Record](../records/Young_v_Harper_merits_1997-03-18.md).

#### Holdings

Part I is directly joined by all eight Associate Justices. Parts II–IV are directly joined by all nine Justices. These are majority holdings at their stated scopes; no separate-rationale synthesis is involved.

###### Part I — Accepted rehearing makes this petition timely

**Holding and operative rule:** When the appellate court expressly permits a late rehearing filing, treats it as timely and keeps the proceeding open pending its resolution, the accepted rehearing’s denial is the relevant finality point for the ensuing certiorari period. This petition, filed within 90 days of denial on that sequence, is timely.

**Controlling explanation:** The governing Rule 13.3 measures the period from denial of timely rehearing, and FRAP 40 permits enlargement by court order. Bowman distinguishes an unauthorized late submission from one the court permits and entertains. Jenkins similarly respects the lower court’s actual treatment as substantive rehearing without allowing manipulation of this Court’s statutory period. Here leave was granted, the filing was treated as timely and the mandate was withheld. A merely unauthorized late paper, an en banc suggestion not treated as rehearing, a mandate date alone or a later order entered solely to extend this Court’s filing time would not establish the same result. The Court waives no statutory period and announces no rule that every late submission restarts it.

###### Part II — Existing parole-like community liberty requires protection

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State releases a prisoner to live at home, work and conduct ordinary community life under parole-like supervision and conditions, termination of that existing conditional liberty requires Morrissey’s protections. Retained legal custody, an overcrowding objective, the preparole label and a separate decision about formal parole do not eliminate that interest.

**Controlling explanation:** Morrissey protects substantial conditional freedom already possessed while accounting for a continuing sentence and lawful grounds for return. Greenholtz distinguishes a request for initial release, and Meachum’s ordinary internal-transfer setting does not describe this community life. Sandin expressly preserves directly constitutional liberty alongside its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement inquiry; no exhaustive program-entitlement finding is needed here. Residence, employment and supervision establish the relevant difference, without making five months a minimum constitutional duration.

The Governor’s denial is not automatically an adequate revocation proceeding. The August 1991 procedure cannot establish a return condition communicated at Harper’s October 1990 release or validate his March return by itself. Yet the earlier procedure’s silence on parole denial does not establish the whole program’s terms, prove compliance with every condition or make release irrevocable. The Court does not invalidate the later procedure prospectively or decide every lawful ground for return. Formal parole, short furloughs, work details and ordinary internal transfers remain distinct.

###### Part III — Morrissey’s ordinary stages and qualifications govern

**Holding and operative rule:** Termination of this community liberty requires the ordinary preliminary and final revocation protections described below. Final revocation must rest on a constitutionally adequate determination, but a completed final hearing is not invariably required before initial arrest or detention.

**Required process and operative qualifications:** When the participant is arrested and detained pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the place of the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground for detention for a release-condition violation. A person not directly involved in the case decides; that person need not be a judge and may be a different parole officer. The participant receives notice of the inquiry, its purpose and the alleged violations, may appear and speak, and may present relevant documents and witnesses. On request he may question an adverse informant in his presence. If the officer determines that identifying the informant would expose that person to harm, confrontation and cross-examination of that informant may be withheld. The officer summarizes the proceedings and states the reasons and evidence supporting the determination; formal judicial findings are unnecessary. Supported probable cause permits continued detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final revocation hearing, if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation. It must resolve contested relevant facts and whether the circumstances warrant revocation, allowing denial and mitigation. Minimum protections are written notice of the claimed violations or asserted grounds; disclosure of adverse evidence; an opportunity to be heard personally and present witnesses and documents; confrontation and cross-examination unless the officer specifically finds good cause to withhold them; a neutral and detached decisionmaker, whose members need not be judges or lawyers; and a written statement of the evidence relied on and reasons.

Informal evidence, including letters and affidavits, may be considered. Issues already determined adversely in another forum, including a relevant criminal conviction, need not be relitigated. Morrissey’s discussion of two months establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor. Preliminary informant risk and final-stage specific good cause are distinct exceptions, each permitting rather than requiring withholding upon the necessary finding. No such finding, valid waiver, dispositive admission, prior determination or supported probable-cause decision is established here. This case decides no separate counsel question.

**Controlling explanation:** Morrissey’s flexible administrative process protects reliable identification of a lawful ground and the judgment whether revocation is warranted. Calling the program preparole cannot replace those safeguards with a telephone return instruction. Conversely, procedural protection does not convert revocation into a criminal trial or require a judicial decisionmaker. Temporary detention remains distinct from final deprivation and must receive the appropriate prompt preliminary and final protections. The Court adds no evidentiary burden, fixed deadline, automatic-release consequence or new adjudicatory code.

###### Part IV — Affirmance preserves lawful grounds and bounded relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tenth Circuit correctly reversed denial of habeas relief because the termination of Harper’s community liberty required Morrissey process. Proceedings must apply that determination, while Oklahoma retains authority to establish a legally available revocation ground through adequate procedures or another independent lawful basis for custody.

**Controlling explanation:** The procedural violation does not establish formal-parole entitlement, expiration of sentence, absence of every possible release-condition violation or a particular release date. The original telephone instruction does not itself supply an adequate revocation determination. The Court neither directs unconditional release nor prescribes a new deadline for state proceedings; any subsequent revocation or continuing custody must be assessed under the governing law and actual record. Lynce supplies no hypothetical exhaustion defect and no implied waiver here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Morrissey v. Brewer: applied to existing parole-like community liberty with both stages, distinct confrontation exceptions, informal evidence and bounded relief intact.
- Greenholtz v. Inmates of Nebraska Penal and Correctional Complex: its initial-release distinction is preserved; this case concerns freedom already conferred.
- Meachum v. Fano: its ordinary internal-transfer setting is distinguished from actual community release.
- Sandin v. Conner: its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule and preservation of direct liberty are applied without an exhaustive independent state-entitlement finding.
- Bowman v. Loperena: applied to rehearing the lower court permits and entertains, rather than an unauthorized late submission.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, 495 U.S. 33 (1990): actual rehearing treatment determines finality within the statutory limit; orders merely extending this Court’s filing time remain insufficient.
- Lynce v. Mathis: genuine habeas threshold predicates remain necessary; no exhaustion defect or waiver is inferred from missing papers.
- United States v. Gonzales: its sentence-concurrency construction is distinguished from conditional-release process and supplies no authority for summary revocation.

#### Law After Decision

Parole-like community freedom receives Morrissey protection by its actual character, irrespective of the preparole name. Existing safeguards retain their ordinary flexibility and limitations, and lawful revocation remains possible. Initial parole eligibility and complete state-program entitlement remain separate questions. Accepted rehearing on the stated sequence makes this review timely without a general enlargement of the certiorari period.


### 1997-03-19 — Bennett v. Spear; No. 95-813.

Authority: [Record](../records/Bennett_v_Spear_merits_1997-03-19.md).

#### Holdings

The Opinion of the Court is directly joined by all nine Justices. That common coalition controls every proposition below.

###### Petitioners adequately plead their own constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Irrigators alleging concrete loss of their project water from a Biological Opinion that the operating agency has announced it will follow adequately plead injury, fair traceability and likely redress from removal of the challenged constraint. They must support standing with the evidence required at each later litigation stage; statutory permission alone never replaces Article III injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires a concrete personal interest and proof appropriate to the stage, but its three-Justice absent-agency discussion supplies no categorical rule defeating this claim. The alleged loss is the petitioners’ own, and the Bureau’s announced compliance links the Service’s completed action to that threatened injury. The Bureau’s operational authority does not make its response unpredictable as a matter of law or require certainty that every eventual choice will change. Setting aside the challenged constraint can afford concrete relief. Humane Society’s missing connection between a later permit amendment and an already completed transfer is absent here. Tracy recognizes cognizable economic injury without making every economic claimant eligible to sue. Arizonans likewise requires an actual continuing controversy; no later fact eliminating this alleged injury is established. Merits uncertainty about the biological conclusion is not a pleading-stage standing defect.

###### The citizen-suit language is broad within its actual channels

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1540(g)’s authorization to any person removes an additional species-preservation zone-of-interests restriction for claims that its particular channels authorize. Section (g)(1)(C) permits the alleged failure to perform a nondiscretionary §1533 duty, including the pleaded failure to consider economic effects of an asserted de facto habitat designation; §(g)(1)(A) does not authorize general supervision of the Secretary’s alleged §1536 maladministration.

**Controlling explanation:** The breadth of the plaintiff language cannot be restricted by an unwritten environmental preference, but it does not erase the enacted division among person-in-violation actions, emergency enforcement and Secretary-duty suits. Treating every alleged administrative error as a violation under (A) would disregard (C)’s specific limit to nondiscretionary §1533 acts. Petitioners may pursue the asserted §1533(b)(2) consideration duty through (C) at this threshold stage; the Court does not find that a designation occurred or triggered a duty. The §1536 consultation claims require a different review route.

When designation or revision occurs, §1533(b)(2) requires consideration of economic and other relevant impacts. Exclusion of an area is discretionary when exclusion benefits outweigh inclusion benefits, and remains forbidden if the best scientific and commercial data show that failure to designate will result in extinction. Reviewability commands neither a particular exclusion nor wholesale economic balancing during §1536 consultation. The (B) emergency channel is not invoked. Article III remains independently necessary.

**Notice and enforcement limits:** The (A) route generally requires 60 days’ written notice to the Secretary and alleged violator; a commenced civil-penalty action or a commenced, diligently prosecuted United States criminal action addressing the violation bars it. The (B) route separately requires 60 days’ notice explaining the claimed emergency and is barred by the Secretary’s commenced diligent emergency-determination action. The (C) route requires 60 days’ notice to the Secretary, except that suit concerning an emergency posing significant risk to the well-being of a fish, wildlife or plant species may follow notification immediately. No actual compliance, applicable emergency or enforcement bar is found or waived here.

###### The consultation claims have an APA route and a provision-specific interest

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed §1536 consultation claims may proceed under APA §§702 and 704 because the ESA does not preclude that review and its citizen-suit channels provide no other adequate judicial remedy for these administrative claims. Petitioners’ alleged injury from restrictions unsupported by the best scientific and commercial data is at least arguably within the interests protected by §1536(a)(2)’s data requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** Review must concern the provision sued upon, not an assumption that every ESA provision protects only species or that every economically affected person may enforce every conservation duty. Requiring the best available scientific and commercial data serves sound decisionmaking and protects affected interests against restrictions unsupported by that information. The Court does not replace agency scientific judgment with independent judicial biology or find the data claim correct. Section 1540(g)(5) preserves other available rights but does not itself establish every APA prerequisite. Dalton’s indivisible base-closure package, deadlines and confined review scheme justified preclusion there; no comparable structure is shown here. Its noncontrolling recommendation-finality theory is not a general bar to agency review.

**Retained APA limits:** Section 702 addresses relief other than money damages and preserves other legal and equitable limits and relief forbidden by another consent-to-suit statute. Any mandatory or injunctive decree must identify by name or title the responsible federal officers and their successors. Section 704 requires final action and no other adequate court remedy. Preliminary or intermediate action ordinarily receives review with final action. Unless a statute expressly requires otherwise, otherwise final action remains final despite an application for a declaratory order or reconsideration; an administrative appeal does not postpone finality unless an agency rule requires it and makes the action inoperative meanwhile. This case grants no decree and establishes no unproved exhaustion or review prerequisite.

###### The Biological Opinion is completed action with legal consequences

**Holding and operative rule:** This Biological Opinion is final agency action because it completes the Service’s consultation determination and its incidental-take statement carries legal consequences: compliance with its terms changes prohibited-taking liability under §1536(o)(2). The Bureau’s retained operational discretion does not convert that completed and consequential action into tentative advice.

**Controlling explanation:** Franklin requires examination of a completed subordinate agency determination even though a later governmental step remains. The Service has completed its assigned consultation here, and the statement conditions protection from taking liability on compliance with its requirements. Practical influence and announced Bureau compliance support causation, but legal consequences supply the distinct finality ground. The Court does not hold every advisory report, prediction or preliminary analysis final, nor does it decide Franklin’s reserved presidential-status question.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Section 1536(a)(2) requires avoidance of likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat using the best scientific and commercial data available, unless the Committee has granted an exemption under (h); none is found here. Under (b)(4), the Secretary must conclude that the action will not violate (a)(2), or offer reasonable and prudent alternatives believed not to violate it, and conclude that incidental taking will not violate that subsection. For a marine mammal, taking also requires §1371(a)(5) authorization. The resulting statement specifies the taking’s impact, reasonable and prudent minimization measures, any required marine-mammal measures, and implementing terms including reporting. Section (o)(2) protects only taking that complies with those terms; it is not unconditional immunity from every ESA duty. The separate exemption-based protection in (o)(1) is not the source of this statement’s effect. No marine-mammal finding is needed for these fish.

The Court reserves the statutory and scientific merits, whether a designation or nondiscretionary duty was actually triggered, the applicable standard of review’s merits application, any properly remaining threshold issue and final relief. The NEPA dismissal remains outside the reviewed questions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife: applies personal injury, causation, redressability and litigation-stage proof; its citizen-suit limit remains, and the noncontrolling absent-agency application is not expanded.
- Franklin v. Massachusetts: applies its controlling reviewability of completed subordinate agency action despite a later governmental step; no presidential-status question is decided.
- Dalton v. Specter: its statutory-scheme preclusion holding is distinguished; the four-Justice recommendation-finality theory supplies no binding rule.
- Humane Society of the United States v. Babbitt: its missing causal and remedial link is distinguished by the pleaded Bureau response and continuing water constraint here.
- General Motors Corp. v. Tracy: preserves cognizable personal economic injury without dispensing with the remaining standing requirements.
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona: requires an actual continuing controversy and a legally available claim and remedy; no analogous loss of this alleged injury is established.

#### Law After Decision

Economic interests do not disqualify an otherwise injured plaintiff from authorized ESA citizen suits. Each claim must still fit its actual statutory channel; the §1536 consultation claims use the APA and its provision-specific interests and finality requirements. The completed Biological Opinion’s conditional taking consequences establish finality here. These reviewability holdings establish no biological error or entitlement to particular water operations, and leave Article III proof, statutory notice and all retained review limits intact.


### 1997-04-21 — Blessing v. Freestone, No. 95-1441

Authority: [Record](../records/Blessing_v_Freestone_merits_1997-04-21.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each following proposition has nine direct votes. Scalia and Kennedy also state a separate, noncontrolling reservation about the original meaning of §1983; it changes none of these joins or the mandate.

**1. A §1983 statutory claim must identify an individual right.** A claimant must identify the particular statutory provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding rather than merely precatory obligation, and defining the asserted individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than leaving it vague or amorphous. The text, statutory setting and actual entitlement asserted govern; violation of a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish the right.

**Controlling explanation:** Wright and Wilder recognize defined individual entitlements within funding statutes; they do not make an entire program an indivisible promise. Livadas separately identifies the federal right, the §1983 channel, congressional foreclosure, defendant and remedy. Suter likewise preserves provision-specific adjudication rather than one answer for all state-plan provisions. The families must therefore identify what each particular command entitles them to demand. Program importance, mandatory wording and remedial usefulness cannot take the place of that inquiry; qualitative language is not universally unenforceable merely because applying it requires judgment.

**2. Title IV-D supplies no individual right to general administrative adequacy.** The federal substantial-compliance measure does not give each beneficiary a personal right to compel systemwide compliance. The same general theory fails when restated as an entitlement to sufficient agency staffing or satisfactory computerized administration as such; rejection of those theories does not decide whether a distinct, actually asserted provision creates a particular individual right.

**Controlling explanation:** The substantial-compliance measure evaluates aggregate performance for federal oversight and funding consequences. A State may meet that benchmark while failing one family; aggregate success cannot extinguish an independently existing personal right. Section 654(3) directs a single, separate organizational unit meeting prescribed staffing requirements, but identifies no beneficiary's right to a particular staff complement, caseload or assigned worker. Former and amended §654(16), their associated requirements and §654a's management and performance functions similarly organize program administration. Their technical detail does not make the system itself an individual entitlement. Mandatory duties important to effective collections therefore do not sustain the general claim recognized below. Alleged shortages, backlogs and accounting defects may still bear on a proved violation or fitted remedy, but create no right by themselves. No separately pleaded claim to each standardized computer requirement, confidentiality protection or individual transmission deadline is established here. The Court adjudicates no individual clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 and announces no categorical rule against regulatory elaboration of an established statutory right. A genuinely distinct claim must receive its own provision-specific inquiry, rather than automatic approval or rejection because it appears in an administrative section.

**3. General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983.** Once a provision creates an individual federal right, that right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes the channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Title IV-D's federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions do not alone foreclose every otherwise existing individual claim; any specific incompatible remedy must be assessed for the actual provision and claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Livadas separates enforceability from whether a right exists. Funding sanctions regulate the federal-state relationship and offer no general administrative forum for a family to obtain case-specific relief. Their detail and practical importance do not create the incompatibility found in the different remedial arrangements of Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson. Wright and Wilder likewise reject automatic foreclosure from federal administrative enforcement. The District Court's chapterwide preclusion ground cannot support dismissal of every possible concrete right. This holding does not establish a particular right, excuse ordinary defendant or relief requirements, or resolve a hypothetical provision-specific remedy not identified here.

**4. State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 prohibit rejecting enforceability merely because a Social Security Act provision appears among requirements for a state plan or its contents. They do not dispense with the individual-right inquiry or alter the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not enforceable in a private action.

**Controlling explanation:** These provisions apply to actions pending on their respective October 20 and October 31, 1994 enactment dates and actions brought thereafter, including this action. Their text leaves other established private-action grounds intact except for overturning any state-plan ground applied in Suter but not in earlier enforcement decisions, and expressly saves the §671(a)(15) result. The Court applies Suter's actual provision-specific rights and separate implied-action holdings, including its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) case-plan claim. No broader state-plan immunity is imported through the statutory reference, and no every-provision entitlement follows from removing that categorical obstacle.

**5. Madrid's concrete distribution allegation and the operative law require separate examination.** A broad prayer for programwide relief does not erase Madrid's allegation that collected support was not distributed under former §657(b)(1). The District Court must identify each asserted provision, conduct, remedy, governing version and effective date before deciding right, violation and relief; neither a superseded duty nor a duty not yet effective may silently support an injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** Former §657(b)(1) is materially more specific than an aggregate administrative benchmark, but specificity does not decide Madrid's unadjudicated eligibility, payment facts, legal right, defendant or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not promise a fresh $50 for each installment regardless of timing. Subsection (b) covers collections under an approved plan in fiscal years beginning after September 30, 1976, subject to subsection (d)'s distinct foster-care rules. Those rules first reimburse current foster-care maintenance, direct qualifying excess up to ordered support to the placement agency for the child's interests, then reimburse unrepaid past foster-care or family-assistance payments from the remainder, with the federal financing share reimbursed appropriately and any balance going to that agency for the child's interests. The agency may reserve its payments for future needs or make them available to the person meeting the child's daily needs. No relevant exception or payment fact is established for Madrid. The 1996 changes likewise have separate operative dates and transitions; compilation together does not make all duties presently applicable. The court below must apply them to identified claims and dates, without inferring Arizona implementation or default.

**Statutory timing limits on the remand:**

L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.

Each year of a two-year session counts separately. **6. Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation.** On remand, the District Court must identify and assess the actual individual claims, then any §1983 foreclosure, proper defendant, breach, causation and lawful relief. Neither general administrative failure nor rejection of the programwide theory authorizes an undifferentiated substantial-compliance injunction, blanket dismissal, specified payment, damages award or prescribed staffing or computer redesign.

**Controlling explanation:** General administrative shortcomings can be evidence relevant to an established right and a remedy fitted to its violation. They are not a general commission to supervise Arizona's program. The Court decides no directly implied Title IV-D action through the §1983 inquiry. The Eleventh Amendment argument and request to overrule Maine v. The Ninth Circuit's undifferentiated right cannot stand, but the State's requested dismissal of every possible particular claim does not follow.

### 1997-05-27 — Clinton v. Jones; No. 95-1853; 520 U.S. 681 (1997).

Authority: [Record](../records/Clinton_v_Jones_merits_1997-05-27.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to these holdings:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg join all five parts, including the linked Ferguson remedy. Every holding below therefore has eight direct Court-opinion votes. Breyer's concurrence in the judgment supplies no formal join to the Court opinion. No fractured-opinion synthesis is needed.

##### I. Immediate review reaches this integrated immunity and trial-protection order

**Holding and operative rule.** An appeal from the conclusive rejection of the President's asserted immunity from present suit permits review of this same order's categorical trial protection, including its alternative docket-control basis, because those rulings determine the scope and remedy of the same asserted temporal immunity. It does not permit review of independent liability claims or defenses, and creates no automatic appeal from a later scheduling order.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the asserted protection cannot be restored by review after the litigation from which immunity is claimed. Fitzgerald supplies the immediate-review rule for that immunity question. The District Court denied dismissal, allowed discovery and withheld trial for the Presidency. Those rulings define one asserted temporal protection; deciding its categorical trial limit is necessary to meaningful review of the order. Swint forbids adding an independent nonappealable liability question merely because another party properly appeals immunity. It reserved genuinely necessary or inextricable connected review, and this integrated order presents that narrower case. An alternative reference to docket control does not place the same relief beyond review. Common facts, efficiency and presidential importance would not suffice. Removing Ferguson's exclusively derivative trial protection does not adjudicate his independent defenses. The undeveloped official-defamation defense, a general pendent-party doctrine and future process orders remain outside this ruling. The challenge to discovery protection pending the completed lower appeal remains moot.

**Precedent treatment.** Fitzgerald is applied for immediate review of an asserted immunity from present suit. Swint's limit on unrelated liability review remains intact; its connected-review reservation is resolved only for this legally integrated protection order.

##### II. No temporary immunity for the presented pre-presidential conduct

**Holding and operative rule.** A sitting President has no constitutional temporary immunity from a properly brought federal civil action seeking damages for alleged unofficial conduct before taking office. The Presidency alone neither requires dismissal without prejudice nor suspends that action until the term ends; concrete demands upon official duties require their own accommodation analysis.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the constitutional protection of the Presidency concerns the exercise of its assigned powers. Fitzgerald protects official presidential decisionmaking from distortion by personal damages liability. The 1991 conduct alleged here preceded Clinton's Presidency and was no exercise of presidential responsibility. Adjudicating that conduct does not give the Judiciary authority to perform an executive function. Forrester requires attention to the function involved rather than the actor's title and recognizes the President's distinct official-function protection; Antoine likewise insists on a justified connection between immunity and the protected function, without deciding presidential immunity itself.

The President's present responsibilities remain substantial. Time and energy consumed by litigation may affect those responsibilities, and the Court does not predict that every demand will be harmless. But that concern does not justify immunity from this whole action merely because the defendant now holds office. The response must address the actual demand and the constitutional duty it threatens. An assessment of political motives behind the complaint or the abstract importance of the plaintiff's claim supplies no constitutional immunity rule.

United States v. Nixon rejects an absolute premise that Article III process cannot reach a President. Its criminal-evidence setting does not decide the balance for every civil demand, which that decision expressly left open. The actual Nixon v. United States decision concerns Senate impeachment procedure assigned by the Constitution; it does not exempt private presidential conduct from adjudication. Plaut applies Morgan Stanley's rule permitting §27A(b)'s objectively defined reinstatement of completed private securities limitations dismissals while courts independently determine eligibility and the merits. That bounded statutory rule and Reich's confined officer review establish no general exemption from interbranch effects. The holding is confined to this federal civil action concerning alleged unofficial pre-office conduct. Liability, state-court jurisdiction, criminal proceedings and compulsory attendance at a particular time or place remain undecided.

**Precedent treatment.** Fitzgerald's official-responsibility immunity is preserved and distinguished from temporary status-based immunity. Forrester supplies the function/identity distinction; Antoine supplies a limited functional analogy. United States v. Nixon supplies the possibility of presidential exposure to judicial process while preserving its civil-evidence reservation. Nixon v. United States, Morgan Stanley, Plaut and Reich retain their confined structural and remedial boundaries; none creates a general civil exemption.

##### III. Official-act immunity turns on function, not presidential status

**Holding and operative rule.** Fitzgerald's absolute personal civil-damages immunity continues to protect conduct within the outer perimeter of presidential official responsibility; title alone cannot convert pre-presidential conduct into an official presidential act, and alleged illegality or improper motive alone does not strip an otherwise official act of that protection. The possible official-function defense concerning post-inauguration press-secretary statements remains for initial District Court consideration on a developed record.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that identifying the function and deciding its legality are different inquiries. Forrester supplies the functional distinction: immunity follows the responsibility performed, rather than the identity of the official. Fitzgerald deliberately protects the outer perimeter of presidential responsibility, so an allegation that an official act was wrongful cannot itself defeat the protection. Conversely, present office cannot supply a presidential function for conduct occurring before the office was assumed. The allegations' references to Clinton's governorship and state personnel do not transform Arkansas conduct into presidential conduct; whether state action or another defense is established remains separate.

The complaint also concerns statements attributed to the presidential press secretary after inauguration. The circumstances of those statements, Clinton's legal responsibility and the elements of defamation have not been sufficiently developed or decided below. The Court neither classifies those statements nor declares every allegation in the complaint unofficial. Rejection of the broad temporary-immunity claim leaves the actual official-act defense available for its own adjudication. The Court announces no universal classification sequence, reasonable-officeholder test, motive exception or exception for allegations of grave wrongdoing. Fitzgerald is not overruled or replaced by qualified immunity. The merits of the federal and state claims, state-office defenses and any particular relief for a proven unlawful official act remain open.

**Precedent treatment.** Fitzgerald retains its outer-perimeter rule and its refusal to make alleged improper purpose alone decisive. Forrester is applied to distinguish function from identity without diluting the President's recognized official-function immunity.

##### IV. This categorical trial stay is an abuse of discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court abused its discretion by ordering in advance that trial await the end of the Presidency without a developed showing connecting that duration to particular presidential duties and without sufficiently accounting for the plaintiff's timely-adjudication and evidence interests. Ordinary stay authority survives the rejection of constitutional immunity, but generalized burdens and the duration of office do not justify this order.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that immunity and docket management are distinct. Discovery continued, so the trial stay was not literally the same protection as exemption from the action. The Court therefore affirms the Eighth Circuit's judgment without adopting the proposition that every discretionary trial stay is equivalent to immunity. Landis recognizes ordinary authority to control a docket, but requires a clear showing of hardship or inequity when even a fair possibility exists that a stay will harm the other party. Extraordinary public interests can justify a moderate, nonoppressive delay; relief must be bounded when entered. The presidential term's endpoint supplies a calendar limit, not a justification for that whole period. No particular demand or official conflict establishes the present duration, while delay may impair access to witnesses and evidence. Allowing discovery does not establish that all trial-delay prejudice disappears. No actual loss of evidence or disputed survival-law conclusion is necessary. A later, supported request remains possible.

**Linked Ferguson remedy.** Ferguson's trial protection was expressly derivative of the presidential protection just rejected. That stay loses its stated basis and is removed; no independent Ferguson immunity or personal defense is adjudicated. Discovery against both defendants remains subject to lawful management and privilege.

**Precedent treatment.** Landis is applied for the triggered applicant's burden, consideration of competing interests and bounded, nonoppressive duration. It creates no special presumption of presidential postponement and is not displaced by the denial of immunity.

##### V. Concrete presidential duties receive case-specific accommodation

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court must give substantial respect to the President's constitutional duties throughout this action; a party seeking protection must identify the particular litigation demand and the conflict or hardship, and any relief must respond to its supported need, duration and alternatives while accounting for the plaintiff's legitimate interests. The court may adjust timing, sequence or manner and may grant a justified, bounded stay, but its discretion cannot authorize significant constitutionally forbidden impairment of another branch's assigned functions.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the proper inquiry concerns concrete duties and litigation demands, not a general privilege of office. Articles II and III preserve each branch's assigned work. The Judiciary may adjudicate a private controversy without acquiring authority to obstruct the Executive's constitutional performance. At the same time, the President's title does not establish a particular conflict or shift to the plaintiff the burden to disprove it. The applicant must identify the need for protection. Landis places the clear-showing burden on the applicant when even a fair possibility of harm from a requested stay exists; that conditional burden is retained in its own terms.

Ordinary procedural tools permit practical accommodation. Timing, sequence, the manner of testimony, protective measures and limited delay can respond to an identified conflict where authorized. The court must examine how long protection is needed, whether alternatives address the problem, and what delay would do to timely adjudication and preservation of evidence. Relief cannot extend beyond its justified scope and remains subject to reconsideration as circumstances change. No party has an absolute entitlement to a particular date, place or method of testimony.

The Court rejects the proposed status-based presumption of postponement, including a shift requiring Jones first to prove exceptional or irreparable prejudice. It also rejects treating presidential constitutional work as merely an ordinary convenience. These conclusions do not resolve a concrete compulsion, privilege, sanction or irreconcilable scheduling dispute: none is before the Court. No universal hearing, special proof quantum, fixed discovery limit or new interlocutory appeal is established. The Court's judgment neither finds that this litigation can never seriously interfere with official duties nor decides the precise constitutional remedy if an actual future demand does so. Breyer's further restriction on second-guessing a reasonable presidential scheduling explanation is stated in his separate opinion, not adopted as part of this holding.

**Precedent treatment.** Landis continues to govern a supported stay request at its stated harm trigger. Fitzgerald's institutional protection explains the significance of presidential duties without supplying immunity for private conduct. United States v. Nixon does not settle every civil-process conflict; Reich's presidential-coercion reservation remains intact.

##### VI. The standard governing proceedings after this decision

**Holding and operative rule.** The District Court must proceed with the federal action under ordinary law, separately determine any properly presented official-act or other defense, and consider a specifically supported request for accommodation under Part V; neither the Presidency nor rejection of temporary immunity predetermines a later scheduling dispute. Parts I–V supply controlling rules with the coalitions stated above, while the two concurrences add no controlling classification test, constitutional exception, presumption or standard for compelled attendance.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the mandate requires continued adjudication, not liability or immediate trial on a specified date. The existing categorical stays fall for the reasons given in Part IV, including the derivative Ferguson consequence. The court retains its ordinary procedural authority within the constitutional and Landis limits already explained. The official-defamation issue must receive initial district-court consideration on a developed record. No present judicial finding establishes the truth of the allegations, satisfaction of a federal claim, privilege, an actual conflict with presidential work or the adequacy of any future accommodation. The detailed guidance proposed by Stone-Zsela and Breyer's further scheduling restriction are separate positions; they cannot enlarge the mandate or become a universal test through their proximity to majority reasoning.

**Precedent treatment.** The mandate applies the preserved Fitzgerald and Landis rules and the confined Swint connected-review application without altering independent defenses or existing requirements for later appellate review.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Nixon v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 731, retains absolute personal damages immunity for presidential conduct within the outer perimeter of official responsibility and supports immediate review of an asserted immunity from suit. It supplies no temporary immunity based solely on current presidential status; improper motive alone does not defeat its existing official-act rule.
- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219, supplies the distinction between function and identity while recognizing the President's unique official-function immunity. The Court does not replace that immunity with a new classification test.
- Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., 508 U.S. 429, supplies a limited analogy requiring functional justification for immunity; its court-reporting holding is not a presidential-immunity decision.
- Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35, continues to bar attachment of independent nonappealable liability questions to an immunity appeal. Its reservation concerning necessary connected review is resolved only for this integrated temporal-protection order.
- Landis v. North American Co., 299 U.S. 248, remains controlling for ordinary stay authority, its clear-showing requirement when even a fair possibility of harm exists, and moderate, nonoppressive duration bounded at entry. It establishes no presumption of postponement for the Presidency.
- United States v. Nixon, 418 U.S. 683, rejects absolute immunity from judicial process in its criminal-evidence setting; its civil-evidence reservation survives.
- Nixon v. United States, 506 U.S. 224, remains confined to the presented Senate impeachment-procedure challenge; it creates no presidential civil exemption.
- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., 511 U.S. 658, and Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., 514 U.S. 211, permit §27A(b)'s objectively defined, mandatory reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals while courts independently determine eligibility and the merits. They preserve limits on legislative findings and dictated liability, establish no unrestricted power to reopen completed merits judgments and create no presidential civil exemption.
- Chamber of Commerce v. Reich, decided January 14, 1997, retains its confined officer-review holding and its reservation concerning coercive relief against the President. This decision supplies no answer to every future coercive demand.

No precedent is overruled. Authorities discussed only in the concurrences establish no additional treatment by the Court.

#### Law After Decision

Effective May 27, 1997, the federal civil action may proceed despite the defendant's Presidency, and its current categorical trial stays cannot continue. This adds a controlling rejection of temporary status-based immunity for the presented unofficial pre-office conduct and a confined connected-review application under Swint. Fitzgerald's official-act immunity and Landis's ordinary stay authority remain in force. A specifically supported demand for protection of presidential duties receives substantial constitutional respect under the Court's accommodation rule; office alone supplies no term-long presumption or shifted burden.

The post-inauguration defamation immunity issue, attribution and merits await initial district-court consideration on a developed record. Other defenses, the underlying truth of the allegations, state-court proceedings, criminal liability, concrete compulsion and the consequences of an actual irreconcilable conflict remain undecided. The concurrences' additional formulations and reserved views are noncontrolling.



### 1997-01-13 — Walmer v. Department of Defense; Tenth Circuit No. 93-3377, 52 F.3d 851; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by Justice Thomas.** They join both Court parts and regard the specific military relationship as sufficient. They also draw on Bowers's recognition of authority to regulate the specified conduct: a reasonable conduct-based personnel policy may rest on a permissible moral judgment as well as military discipline. Their Romer dissent distinguished such judgment from a bare wish to harm persons. They do not claim that Bowers already decided equal protection, that every private aversion is legitimate or that the Court has overruled Romer. Beach's tolerance for reasonable predictions and imperfect fit supports this narrower service policy. No individual-performance finding or least restrictive alternative is required. This additional conduct-morality ground is theirs alone and is not attributed to the other three equality affirmers.

**Justice Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg.** They join the attainder holding but would vacate the equality-based denial and remand. Heller and Romer require examination of the connection between this actual disadvantage and a legitimate objective. Rich concerned fraudulent denial on entry under another regulation, so treating it as conclusive leaves this admitted-acts classification insufficiently examined. Stevens's Beach position likewise requires a relationship to the actual distinction, and Ginsburg's Virginia position supports applying the proper inquiry without importing that case's sex-classification tier.

They accept substantial deference to reasonable military predictions, the availability of conceivable legitimate reasons and Walmer's burden to establish entitlement to preliminary relief. They find legal inadequacy in foreclosure by the older case, not proof that the Army lacks a rational basis. They demand neither actual-purpose proof from the Government, heightened scrutiny, an individualized impairment finding nor a new evidentiary hearing. On remand the courts should reassess the merits question under ordinary rationality and address irreparable injury, comparative harm and public interest if the renewed merits assessment makes them material. Those factors were never found satisfied. They would neither enjoin separation automatically nor finally invalidate the policy.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** He joins the legislative-predicate attainder holding and would vacate the equality-based denial under a different inquiry. His Part A treats mandatory separation for homosexual acts as an intentional sexual-orientation classification, whether described as conduct or a deliberate status proxy. Bowers does not exempt it from equality review. Romer supplies an ordinary-review floor and a warning against unexplained class disadvantage, but does not itself allocate a justification burden to the Government. He would make that doctrinal change here: the Government must identify an actual military objective and establish a real relationship between it and the intentional classification, without relying on moral disapproval, private bias or stereotype.

His reason is that merely conceivable explanations would permit this intentional disadvantage without establishing its actual connection to the military function invoked. Professional judgments and reasonable contemporaneous predictions about discipline, cohesion, readiness and command receive substantial but nonconclusive weight; an assertion of deference does not carry the Government's burden. Experience and institutional knowledge may suffice without scientific certainty or a universal formal-findings requirement. Part A independently supports his remand because the lower courts treated the claim as foreclosed, placed the practical burden on Walmer to disprove the rule, and did not resolve the military justification his proposed allocation requires.

His separable Part B additionally adopts intermediate scrutiny: the Government must identify an actual important military objective and establish a substantial relationship to it, with substantial weight to supported professional judgment. Neither Romer nor the public-university sex cases already supplies that tier. Separating Part B leaves Part A's independent Government burden intact.

He retains Bowers's specified-conduct holding, ordinary regulation of materially relevant conduct and the absence of a constitutional entitlement to service. He requires neither least restrictive means nor an individual exception in every case, and he does not infer that military concerns lack support from the absence of an individual performance finding. The district court should assess the military showing on a developed record and apply all preliminary-relief requirements. He would order no injunction, immediate status change or final invalidation. Any concrete personnel commitments or settled arrangements must be shown in the actual record and given their proper remedial weight; abstract command responsibility, convenience, institutional preference or expected continued exclusion does not establish protected reliance. He makes no finding here that such commitments exist and adds no automatic transition requirement. His Part A burden and Part B tier each remain noncontrolling.

### 1997-05-27 — Clinton v. Jones; No. 95-1853; 520 U.S. 681 (1997).

Authority: [Record](../records/Clinton_v_Jones_merits_1997-05-27.md).

##### Stone-Zsela, concurring; no joins

Stone-Zsela joins the Court and its entire judgment. His concurrence proposes a more explicit objective method for distinguishing presidential functions from private conduct. A court should first identify the conduct specifically and the presidential authority claimed; then determine whether objectively established circumstances connect the conduct to performing, directing or supervising a responsibility entrusted by the Constitution or federal law, including Fitzgerald's outer perimeter; then check whether a reasonably informed officeholder would understand it as presidential responsibility rather than a private act. Legal classification remains the judge's task. An ordinary observer's assessment can expose a self-serving label but cannot create or eliminate constitutional power, and subjective good faith is not dispositive. Forrester supports the functional starting point, not this particular proposed check.

Title, location, staff, security, public resources or an official label alone does not establish presidential function. Nor does alleged illegality, retaliation or personal motivation automatically remove an otherwise official act from Fitzgerald's protection. Directing military operations and supervising a department ordinarily perform presidential functions even when their legality is disputed. A private encounter does not become presidential government merely because an official event or staff is involved. The present pre-presidential allegations require no decision of a contested presidential-act classification; the proposed method is guidance in his separate opinion.

He agrees that the actual categorical stay fails and that Landis preserves tailored relief on demonstrated need. He separates official-action review from personal damages immunity: courts may assess executive legality even when Fitzgerald forecloses a particular damages remedy. He would be prepared in a properly presented case to reconsider or limit Fitzgerald concerning proved treason or grave, deliberate constitutional wrongdoing. He adopts no such exception here, and an allegation of unlawfulness alone supplies none.

His broader view is that purely private conduct does not become a presidential function because it occurs during the term, including conduct that may violate criminal law. Criminal liability nonetheless requires an applicable offense, jurisdiction, proof and resolution of any immunity or supremacy issue. He reserves prosecution timing, federal or state criminal jurisdiction, criminally charged official acts, comparable state-court civil proceedings, the post-inauguration defamation defense and all merits questions. His concurrence changes no controlling law on those subjects.

##### Breyer, concurring in the judgment; no joins

Breyer agrees that no automatic immunity protects the presented private conduct and that the existing stay is premature because the President has not supplied a specific reasoned explanation of need. He agrees with preservation of Fitzgerald's official-function immunity and the undeveloped press-secretary defense. His disagreement concerns the constitutional protection of presidential time, not the judgment or a finding that this trial already interferes with official duties.

Article II places the entire executive responsibility in one nationally elected individual. The President can delegate tasks but cannot delegate ultimate responsibility; Congress and courts can act through other members or substitutes. Significant interference with presidential performance therefore affects the Executive as an institution. Fitzgerald protects against diversion of time and energy as well as distortion of official decisions by feared liability. Harlow, Imbler, Tenney and Mitchell likewise recognize litigation burdens as relevant to effective public service. These authorities do not establish present immunity for private acts, but they support constitutional protection when a particular judicial demand threatens official performance.

The historical writings of Story, Adams, Ellsworth and Jefferson support functional protection of ongoing duties without establishing their broadest immunity assertions. Voluntary presidential testimony proves no unlimited power to compel civil-litigation demands. United States v. Nixon involved criminal evidence and individual liberty and expressly left civil-evidence balancing open. Youngstown's review helps determine executive authority itself; its burdens do not automatically settle the distraction caused by a private damages action. Breyer will not rest constitutional protection on predictions that such suits will be few, sanctions will eliminate every burden or ordinary discretion necessarily will suffice.

Once the President gives a reasoned explanation identifying a conflict between a particular judicial demand and public duties, Article II constrains scheduling. In the ordinary civil case where postponement would cause no overwhelming or enormous harm to the plaintiff, the court must avoid significant interference with those duties. Judicial power to second-guess a reasonable presidential explanation of scheduling needs is very limited. This is a constitutional requirement, not merely optional courtesy. Bare reliance on office remains insufficient, and the President retains the initial obligation to explain the need. Breyer does not decide the result if delay would cause the extraordinary plaintiff harm he identifies, or prescribe a fixed hearing, new proof standard, automatic stay or new appeal. The precise contours, possible future presumptions and consequences of an actual irreconcilable conflict remain open. His further formulation is noncontrolling.
