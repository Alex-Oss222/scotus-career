#### New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 91-543, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-558, County of Allegany, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-563, County of Cortland, New York v. United States et al. The same component-specific relief applies in all three dockets.

For the ripe-review holding in *New York v. United States*, June 19, 1992, Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563, see Federal Courts — Ripeness of future statutory obligations.

##### Whether the Waste Act's general responsibility clause independently commands state regulation

**Holding:** Read as a whole, the 1985 Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act's general state-responsibility language describes the objective implemented through its monetary, access and take-title mechanisms and adds no independently enforceable command to regulate. That plausible construction avoids serious structural problems without contradicting Congress's arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction does not erase the express compulsory title and liability terms or authorize judicial rewriting; those terms receive their own constitutional review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to identify the structural problem with a freestanding state-legislative command.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452 (1991): applied; displacement of the usual federal balance requires statutory clarity.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568 (1988): applied; a plausible construction avoiding serious constitutional problems is preferred, without judicial rewriting.

##### Whether the Act's surcharge collection and milestone-conditioned distributions are lawful federal monetary inducements

**Holding:** Congress may authorize the Act's interstate waste surcharges, collect a share for a segregated federal spending program, and condition distributions on explicit, disposal-related milestones. On this record these conditions are authorized inducements; segregation of the funds and state choice whether to qualify do not remove the program from the spending power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** The program serves safe disposal, supplies clear notice of the disposal conduct and deadlines earning distributions, and relates the conditions to that expenditure. No independent constitutional prohibition breached by satisfying those conditions is identified. The federal tax on interstate commerce is not independently challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dole's general-welfare, notice, relatedness, independent-constitutional-limit and noncompulsion requirements remain. No numerical coercion threshold or authority to condition unrelated programs is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the monetary incentives in all three dockets. Lawful surcharge collection and distributions remain operative; the monetary 1993 take-title-or-forfeit alternative is not the compulsory 1996 mechanism severed below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied; general welfare, clear notice, relatedness, independent constitutional limits, and the boundary against compulsion remain in force.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished on the decisive express-congressional-authorization ground; unilateral state discrimination remains invalid.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 (1946): applied; Congress may authorize otherwise impermissible interstate-commerce burdens.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981): applied as Dole's notice requirement; the milestones unambiguously state what earns a distribution.

##### Whether Congress may authorize higher access charges and exclusion of waste from jurisdictions missing statutory disposal deadlines

**Holding:** Congress may conditionally authorize sited States and regional compacts to increase access charges and exclude waste from jurisdictions missing the Act's deadlines. These provisions do not compel an unsited State to legislate, spend, or administer a federal program because the consequences of its refusal fall on waste generators seeking disposal, and the State remains free to choose its own regulatory response.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** Declining the milestones exposes private waste producers to increased costs and eventual loss of outside disposal access; it does not itself transfer obligations to the State or require an appropriation. Political pressure from affected residents is distinct from a legal command, and New York remains free to regulate in its own manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision supplies no federal disposal fallback, no duty on sited States to accept New York waste and no permission for discrimination beyond Congress's express authorization.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the access incentives in all three dockets. The authorized higher charges and exclusion remain operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied by analogy to a lawful alternative borne outside the unwilling State government; the actual federal fallback in Hodel is not fabricated here.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): applied in its cooperative-regulation scope; incentives do not establish unlimited power to direct legislation.
- Northeast Bancorp, Inc. v. Board of Governors, 472 U.S. 159 (1985): applied; express congressional authorization removes the otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraint.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; unauthorized origin-based private-waste exclusion remains impermissible.
- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; its invalid unauthorized discriminatory surcharge is not approval or disapproval of the express federal choices here.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): clarified and extended; its legislative-compulsion boundary applies to mandatory state ownership/liability offered as the alternative to state regulation.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983): applied by structural analogy; assent to legislation does not cure its departure from the constitutional allocation of authority.
- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims, 341 U.S. 22 (1951): distinguished in the asserted compact/estoppel setting; this Court decides no duty under a compact New York actually joined.
- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether the remaining monetary and access incentives deny New York a republican form of government

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that this Guarantee Clause claim may be heard, the surviving monetary and access incentives do not deny New York a republican form of government because they preserve its legislative choices and officials' accountability to its voters. The Court decides neither general Guarantee Clause justiciability nor a separate Guarantee objection to the already invalidated take-title command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the assumption-and-merits rationale. Stone-Zsela separately rejects the claim after finding this limited institutional claim justiciable. White, Blackmun, and Stevens vote to reject the challenge as part of their affirm-all disposition but do not join Part V.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes this claim is justiciable without deciding that issue. Stone's affirmative decision to hear the limited institutional claim has no other join and creates no controlling justiciability rule. No separate Guarantee ground is decided for the already invalidated take-title command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remaining Guarantee Clause challenge is rejected in all three dockets. The surviving incentives leave New York's legislative choice and elected officials' accountability intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Luther v. Borden, 48 U.S. 1 (1849): left intact in its rival-government recognition setting; no blanket justiciability or nonjusticiability rule is announced here.
- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962): retained; the Court does not resolve the general political-question boundary.
- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied to the continuing choice under conditional spending; no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule is inferred.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to the distinction between an authorized conditional arrangement and compulsory state regulation; it supplies no blanket Guarantee Clause justiciability rule.

##### Whether the invalid compulsory 1996 take-title mechanism is severable from the remainder of the Act

**Holding:** An invalid provision is severed when the remainder remains fully operative and it is not evident that Congress would have rejected that remainder independently. The compulsory 1996 title, possession, and associated liability mechanism is severable here; the remaining financing and access incentives stay in force, without a court-created replacement program or a duty on other States to accept New York's waste.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The remaining financing and access provisions function independently and advance disposal capacity. Absence of a severability clause creates no presumption against severance; the Act's objective is capacity, not the invalid sanction for its own sake.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained 1993 monetary take-title-or-forfeit alternative remains distinct from the invalid compulsory 1996 mechanism. The Court creates no federal substitute or duty on other States to accept waste.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the compulsory 1996 title, possession and associated liability mechanism and remand all three dockets for corresponding declaratory relief. The remaining financing and access arrangements stay in force; no damages, disposal site or federal administrator is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Severance and remand are supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, 6–3; White, Blackmun and Stevens would uphold the entire Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock, 480 U.S. 678 (1987): applied without changing the severability standard or treating silence as nonseverability.
- Reagan v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 154 U.S. 362 (1894): applied; the failure of an enforcement device need not defeat the independently operative statutory purpose.
- United States v. Jackson, 390 U.S. 570 (1968): applied; an invalid enforcement measure may be severed while preserving an independently functioning scheme.

#### United States v. Harris

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-297.
**Decided:** April 27, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Criminal Procedure — Armed vehicle taking and cumulative firearm punishment (Knowing participation and intentional assistance give the original offense definite content; Section 924(c) expressly adds a consecutive term for this armed carjacking; Hunter permits the clearly authorized punishment in this single prosecution).

##### The vehicle-taking economic class supplies the commerce connection

**Holding:** Congress may regulate the armed forcible acquisition of the covered vehicle proved here as an economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme, while original §2119's enacted requirement that the particular vehicle have been transported, shipped, or received in interstate or foreign commerce must be proved. Neither remote movement alone nor generalized crime, insurance, or productivity costs independently supplies this holding's constitutional ground. The decision creates no rival commerce test and no automatic instrumentality rule beyond these facts and the enacted nexus.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's commerce portion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a narrower ground: he would sustain the application because the offense directly attacks possession and control of a covered motor vehicle functioning as an interstate transportation instrumentality or thing in commerce, and because the statute requires proof of the particular vehicle's interstate connection. He does not rely on remote historical movement alone.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995: applies its controlling connection-before-magnitude architecture for the first time, in a direct vehicle/instrumentality and economic vehicle-taking setting; the economic vehicle-taking class within the interstate motor-vehicle theft-prevention scheme supplies the connection, and Lopez's inadequate stand-alone possession/downstream-effects chain is distinguished; no missing element is supplied.
- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111 (1942): applies aggregation within a regulated economic class rather than demanding a substantial effect from each individual taking.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146 (1971): applies Congress's authority over the economic class despite an individual instance's local character.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: preserves its distinction between actual commercial operation and remote object history; prior manufacture alone is not the constitutional ground here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both convictions and each defendant's forty-one-month Count One term plus consecutive sixty-month Count Two term are affirmed on all three presented challenges. No merger, vacatur, concurrent conversion, sentence increase, resentencing, new trial or exploratory remand is ordered.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. The interstate-externality reservation is unchanged.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** February 12, 1996.

##### Congress may include possession in this regulation of the machinegun market

**Holding:** Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market, subject to the subsection's government-related and prior-lawful-possession exceptions, when possession and transfer are sufficiently integrated that excluding possession would permit acquisition, retention, concealment, or continued circulation of the prohibited stock and thereby materially impair enforcement of the valid transfer/stock regime. The relationship is one of anti-evasion and material impairment under the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, and it defeats the presented commerce challenge without a transaction-specific interstate element. General gun violence, fear, insurance cost, marketability alone, or remote interstate history does not supply the ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 922(o)(2) excepts transfers to or by, and possession by or under the authority of, the United States or its departments or agencies, a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions; it separately excepts lawful transfer or possession of a machinegun lawfully possessed before May 19, 1986. Earlier manufacture alone does not satisfy that second exception. No count is newly found within either exception.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lopez is clarified and developed: Lopez reserved intrastate inclusion genuinely necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime, and this decision supplies the first operational rule for that route. Wickard and Perez are preserved for economic-class aggregation. Harris, Bishop/Stokes and American Life League rest on distinct mechanisms; none is converted into the source of Hale's anti-evasion rule, and no other statute's interstate element is transferred into §922(o).

#### Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-711 and 90-712.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Presley concerns the Common Fund Resolution in No. 90-711; Mack concerns the Unit System in No. 90-712. One opinion supplies the same controlling grounds for both appealed noncoverage judgments.

##### Whether the Common Fund Resolution and Unit System require §5 preclearance

**Holding:** Section 5 does not require preclearance of these reallocations of governing authority because they do not alter voting procedures, candidacy, the electorate, or the method of selecting the relevant offices and have no direct relation to voting. This ruling does not resolve whether an extreme transfer could effectively replace an elective office with an appointive one, nor does it exempt a change merely because it occurs after an election.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The Common Fund Resolution replaces individual district road-fund authority with collective commission control. The Unit System places road operations under an appointed engineer while retaining substantial authority, including appointment and budget powers, in the elected commission. Neither changes the relevant selection mechanism or electorate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The listed electoral categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. Effective replacement of an elective office with an appointive office remains unresolved. The Court decides no racial-purpose or discriminatory-effect question, §2 claim, Title VI claim, or constitutional claim; the unappealed Road Supervision ruling is undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appealed noncoverage judgments are affirmed. Neither challenged resolution requires §5 preclearance or is enjoined under §5 on these grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Allen v. State Board of Elections*, 393 U.S. 544 — broad coverage of changes affecting electoral choice remains; elective-to-appointive selection is distinguished from these retained elected offices.
- *Perkins v. Matthews*, 400 U.S. 379 — changes to the electorate and voting arrangements remain covered; coverage remains distinct from discrimination merits.
- *Dougherty County Board of Education v. White*, 439 U.S. 32 — candidacy restrictions directly related to electoral choice remain covered.
- *McCain v. Lybrand*, 465 U.S. 236, and *City of Lockhart v. United States*, 460 U.S. 125 — changes in electoral arrangements remain within §5; neither decides ordinary internal governing duties.
- *NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission*, 470 U.S. 166 — practical changes to election timing or procedure require proper review; postelection timing alone supplies no exemption here.

##### Whether the Attorney General's interpretation permits coverage without the statutory connection to voting

**Holding:** The Attorney General's interpretation of §5 is entitled to substantial consideration but cannot extend preclearance to governing changes that lack the statutory connection to voting. The proposed significant-transfer/different-constituencies test cannot substitute for that connection in these appeals.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The rejected test concerns significant transfers of authority between officials responsible to substantially different constituencies. That formulation can reach governing responsibilities without identifying any change in voting and therefore cannot substitute for the required connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The distinction between §5 coverage and review of discriminatory effect remains intact. Administrative expertise retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Board of Commissioners of Sheffield*, 435 U.S. 110 — administrative interpretation retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837 — preserved; deference does not authorize exceeding the statute's scope.
- *Beer v. United States*, 425 U.S. 130 — coverage and discriminatory-effect review remain separate inquiries.
- *South Carolina v. Katzenbach*, 383 U.S. 301 — §5's remedial function is preserved; it does not by itself establish coverage of every governing change.

#### Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia, 517 U.S. 186

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-203.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### The identified convention payment preserves a live statutory controversy

**Holding:** The convention's completion does not moot this appeal because Morse seeks return of his identified $45 payment on a substantial statutory restoration theory not conclusively eliminated by the judgment. That personal controversy permits decision of coverage and private-action questions without establishing repayment, the proper refund defendant or every plaintiff's prospective standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all seven other Associates: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join this threshold explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Morse paid the fee and repaid a campaign reimbursement. Ultimate statutory authorization for money remains a merits question. Future-participation requests need their own live basis; no common recurrence holding or general §5 damages rule is established. The statutory three-judge injunctive judgment and direct-review provisions provide the review channel.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County distinguishes an existing action from available relief; Meghrig requires actual statutory authority for restoration without excluding every restitution theory.

##### Section 5 covers the pleaded primary-like convention's changed financial prerequisite

**Holding:** On the accepted dismissal-stage allegations, a changed financial prerequisite to this qualifying party's primary-like convention selecting its preferential general-ballot nominee falls within §5. The combined electoral function, pleaded participation practice and Virginia statutory nomination relationship establish coverage without a categorical convention exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. Broader delegation and party-office reasoning is not the Court's holding.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. As of this date the Court has not adopted a general rule governing remote historical movement in every statute.

**Authority by component:**

- Architecture; connection before magnitude; recognition of necessary inclusion; no-insertion rule: United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.
- Economic vehicle-taking class and enacted vehicle nexus: United States v. Harris, April 27, 1995; O’Connor’s eight-Justice commerce rationale. Stone concurs in the judgment on the narrower direct-instrumentality/vehicle-specific nexus ground.
- Direct interference with an interstate service market: American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995; Kennedy Part I, unanimous.
- Application of Harris without new doctrine: United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, February 5, 1996; O’Connor Part II, unanimous.
- Anti-evasion/material-impairment rule for the post-1986 machinegun regime: United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996; O’Connor Part I, unanimous.

**Present operation effective:** Hale, February 12, 1996, for the anti-evasion/material-impairment branch; the controlling architecture dates to Lopez, April 26, 1995.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

**Exceptions and limits:** Noneconomic intrastate conduct is not categorically immune. Traditional State subject matter is not categorically immune. Formal legislative findings are neither universally required nor sufficient by themselves. Wickard and Perez retain their economic-class scope. Garcia’s rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains intact. Remote movement and generalized social costs independently establish none of the current Commerce grounds. The Court has not yet adopted a general material-interstate-externality doctrine. Not every protester is an interstate enterprise; the service-market ground reaches conduct directed at provision or receipt of the covered services. Hale preserves § 922(o)'s government-related channels and prior-lawful pre-ban stock. No Second Amendment, amended-statute or independent indictment rule is established by these commerce holdings.

**Material development:**

- April 26, 1995 — Lopez: ADDED / CHANGED. Added the controlling decision sequence of regulated class → interstate mechanism → magnitude/aggregation → fit/boundary. Changed the prior substantial-effects law only to the extent necessary to reject aggregation of generalized downstream consequences as a substitute for the missing Commerce connection. Preserved Wickard and Perez within their genuine economic-class settings. Reserved fuller Necessary-and-Proper inclusion questions.
- April 27, 1995 — Harris: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that Lopez does not disable direct regulation of an economic vehicle-taking class connected to an interstate motor-vehicle regime when the statute separately requires a particular-vehicle interstate nexus. Stone’s narrower concurrence emphasizes direct protection of the transportation instrumentality. No general remote-movement rule is added.
- November 13, 1995 — American Life League: CLARIFIED / APPLIED. Clarified that intentional obstruction directed at provision or receipt of services can be regulated when it directly interferes with an actual interstate service market. This is not downstream crime-cost aggregation.
- February 5, 1996 — Bishop/Stokes: APPLIED ONLY. Applied Harris. No new Commerce rule.
- February 12, 1996 — Hale: ADDED. Added the first operational anti-evasion/material-impairment doctrine. Intrastate possession may be included where excluding it would materially undermine or permit evasion of a valid federal interstate market regime. This development uses the Necessary and Proper Clause together with Commerce authority. Hale does not create a general firearm possession power.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Lopez; United States v. Harris; American Life League v. Reno; United States v. Bishop; United States v. Stokes, judgment below 66 F.3d 569; United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016.



## Standards and Tests selection: Actual and justifiable reliance for the actual-fraud discharge exception

### Actual and justifiable reliance for the actual-fraud discharge exception

**Governing question:** The reliance requirement for an actual-fraud claim under §523(a)(2)(A).

**Current rule:** The creditor must prove actual and justifiable reliance by the ordinary preponderance standard. Justifiability depends on that recipient’s knowledge, capacities and circumstances. No universal investigation duty applies, but known or personally obvious falsity defeats justifiability and an actually discovered warning may require inquiry under that individualized standard. Objective imprudence can bear on whether claimed actual reliance occurred, but is not a separate universal prudence condition. Fraud, reliance, justifiability, the obtained-by nexus and the extent of nondischargeable debt remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- Field — Part I, actual and justifiable reliance, 1995-11-28; Souter for all nine, including the two Justices dissenting only from the application and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Field, November 28, 1995.

**Exceptions and limits:** Subsection (A) excludes statements respecting the debtor’s or an insider’s financial condition. Subsection (B) separately requires a materially false written statement of that kind, reasonable creditor reliance and the debtor’s causing publication with intent to deceive. This holding does not separately classify a claim of false pretenses or representation without actual fraud.

**Holdings navigation:** Field v. Mans, 516 U.S. 59.



## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

### Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

**Governing question:** Whether §510(c) permits a court to reduce rank because an allowed penalty competes with compensatory creditors.

**Current rule:** Statutory priority does not immunize a claim from §510(c), and lack of priority does not itself justify subordination. After notice and a hearing, §510(c) permits distributional subordination of all or part of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, under principles of equitable subordination, and transfer of a lien securing a subordinated claim. A legally sufficient, supported claim-specific ground is required; noncompensatory character, estate insufficiency and reduced recovery for innocent or value-giving creditors cannot alone replace Congress’s distribution choices, even when described as individual balancing. This limit applies to both postpetition administrative penalties and prepetition nonpriority penalties.

**Authority by component:**

- Noland — Parts I–II, real statutory equity and forbidden class replacement, 1996-05-13; Thomas for all nine.
- CFI — Part II, the same boundary within the general-unsecured class, 1996-06-20; Souter for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** CFI, June 20, 1996, for application to nonpriority penalties; Noland’s administrative-priority rule effective May 13, 1996.

**Controlling construction:** Allowed §503(b)(1)(C) postpetition tax penalties retain case-era §507(a)(1) priority after conversion through §348(d)’s §503(b) exception. Section 726(a)(4)’s fourth-rank category instead requires a fine, penalty, forfeiture or multiple/exemplary/punitive damages arising before the earlier of the relief order or trustee appointment, and only to the extent noncompensatory. Section 726 distribution remains expressly subject to §510.

**Open question:** Misconduct’s universal necessity remains unresolved; no entitlement without misconduct or frozen catalogue of equitable grounds follows. Rejection of a class-based reason neither orders a new misconduct hearing nor establishes a different ground, final payment or tax priority.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Noland, 517 U.S. 535; United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., 518 U.S. 213 (1996).



## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Morgan Stanley's and Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b).

### 1996-11-06 — Lopez v. Monterey County; No. 95-1201

Authority: [Record](../records/Lopez_v_Monterey_County_merits_1996-11-06.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Kennedy's Parts I–IV, joined by all eight other Justices, control all four holdings. The judgment coalition and full-opinion joins are unanimous; the additional grounds in the separate concurrence are not necessary to these holdings.

##### I. State compulsion does not exempt a covered County's administration

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered political subdivision administers a voting change when its officials give the change operative effect within the subdivision. Section 5 therefore requires Monterey County to obtain preclearance before administering a postbaseline judicial-election change even if noncovered California compels the change and leaves the County no discretion; the duty is the County's and does not require California to preclear its laws in the abstract.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that execution of an electoral change is distinct from enactment of it. Section 5 separately reaches a covered jurisdiction that enacts or seeks to administer a change. A condition of local policy discretion would erase the second route whenever a noncovered State required its covered subdivision to operate the new system. The statute contains no such condition. Monterey County conducts these public judicial elections and gives the countywide electorate operative effect; the voters experience the same change regardless of its legislative origin.

Presley supplies the distinction between a change in voting and a transfer of internal governing duties. Morse confirms the importance of the practice's actual electoral operation while preserving the designated substantive-preclearance process. Neither already decided this local-discretion question, and the Court adopts no general state-delegation test. The holding answers the legal question even assuming California law entirely displaced the County ordinances; it does not decide that disputed state-law characterization. Nor does it impose a statewide submission obligation, determine discriminatory purpose or retrogression, or confer general federal power to redesign California's judiciary.

**Precedent treatment:** Presley is applied to the direct electoral operation; its internal-governance exclusion remains. Morse's bounded functional and statutory-sequencing principles are applied without enlarging its party-convention holding.

##### II. Electorate changes and the limits of prior submission

**Holding and operative rule:** Replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate for municipal judges is a change affecting voting under §§5 and 14(c)(1). A later related submission does not preclear earlier changes that it failed to identify; the district court must determine the precise reach of the 1983 submission as to the final ordinance while treating the earlier unidentified consolidations as unprecleared.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this consolidation changes who elects each judge, rather than merely the work performed after election. At the November 1, 1968 baseline, voters elected the judges of their separate districts. The six ordinances adopted between 1972 and 1983 ultimately placed the judges before one countywide electorate. Presley preserves coverage for that direct alteration of electoral choice. Allen and Perkins supply the same distinction through their treatment of electoral mechanisms and boundaries.

McCain and Clark require an actual, identifiable submission of the change for which preclearance is claimed; ambiguity is not blanket approval of earlier practices. The record establishes that California's 1983 submission did not identify or describe the earlier ordinances. Whether it reached the final 1983 ordinance must be determined from the submission's actual terms. The County cannot obtain approval of the whole consolidation sequence merely by pointing to that later submission. Coverage and lack of preclearance establish the nonuse obligation, not a finding that the system has discriminatory purpose or retrogressive effect.

**Precedent treatment:** Presley, Allen and Perkins retain coverage for direct changes in electoral choice. McCain v. Lybrand and Clark v. Roemer retain their requirements of specific submission and narrow treatment of claimed administrative approval.

##### III. Effective designation and prospective enforcement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 4(b)'s administrative coverage determination may not be collaterally retried in this §5 enforcement action; statutory bailout under §4(a) is a distinct route. Delay cannot authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change, although reliance, completed acts and practical timing may bear on lawfully available retrospective relief or the form of a lawful transition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the County's published designation remains operative in this case. Congress separately restricted review of the coverage determination and provided a judicial bailout process. The County has not obtained release from coverage through that process. Its objection cannot convert this enforcement suit into a collateral retrial of the administrative findings.

Nor does the age of the ordinances excuse their future use without submission. Section 5 conditions administration on preclearance; the passage of time supplies no substitute. Clark enforces that statutory sequence, and Hampton County confirms that elapsed time and electoral implementation do not themselves remove the review obligation. The practical cost of delay warrants a fitted transition, not continued use of the same uncleared governmental plan as an ordinary electoral system. The Court makes no factual bailout determination, awards no damages, and decides no backward-looking relief concerning a completed election, judicial tenure or completed judicial acts.

**Precedent treatment:** Clark and NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission are applied to prospective enforcement; their remedial attention to actual circumstances is preserved. No new general retrospective equitable rule is announced.

##### IV. Enforcement and a genuinely judicial temporary remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** A §5 enforcement court may determine coverage and submission status and enforce nonuse, but may neither grant substantive preclearance nor authorize an unprecleared County or State electoral plan by calling it court ordered. A necessary, temporary plan wholly formulated by the court may operate without separate §5 preclearance only when independent lawful remedial authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary, and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial approval does not change the governmental origin of an electoral plan. Connor distinguishes a wholly judicial remedy; McDaniel prevents a court order from insulating governmental policy choices from submission. Here the district court reinstated substantially the County's at-large system and authorized ordinary six-year terms. It did not formulate a wholly judicial temporary remedy. Clark's reservation for an extreme circumstance supplies no basis on this record: prolonged notice of the problem, abandonment of the County's D.C. preclearance action and ordinary terms do not establish a last-minute necessity. This decision recognizes no broader emergency exception.

Morse and Shaw preserve Congress's separate preclearance channels. The enforcement court cannot decide purpose or effect itself, even in light of the County's stipulation concerning its inability to prove nonretrogression. Administrative difficulty does not enlarge its authority.

The earlier four-division arrangement is a material option for consideration. It retained a single municipal court and received preclearance on March 6, 1995; its terms reportedly end in January 1997. Johnson v. Miller prevents racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate, standing alone, from supplying a constitutional defect. But neither that rule nor preclearance answers the separate objections concerning electoral and jurisdictional linkage, minimum population, divided municipalities and candidate residence, or establishes independent remedial authority. The Court selects no interim plan. Those matters, the exact 1983 submission and any actual officeholder circumstances require lawful determination below.

**Precedent treatment:** Connor and McDaniel preserve the genuinely judicial/governmental-plan distinction; Clark supplies the nonuse rule and bounded emergency reservation; Morse and Shaw preserve the designated channels; Johnson governs the design-only constitutional objection without approving this particular plan.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission:** Distinguishes internal allocations of governing authority from the direct electorate change here; the former exclusion remains intact.
- **Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia:** Applies its bounded concern with actual electoral operation and its separate preclearance channels; no general delegation test or new private-action rule is adopted.
- **Allen v. State Board of Elections:** Applies protection of electoral choice to the changed means of electing judges.
- **Perkins v. Matthews:** Applies coverage of changes in electoral boundaries and the electorate, without equating coverage with discrimination.
- **McCain v. Lybrand:** Requires identification of the submitted change and rejects blanket approval of unidentified earlier practices.
- **Clark v. Roemer:** Enforces nonuse pending preclearance and preserves, without applying, its narrow extreme-circumstance reservation; the present ordinary six-year elections do not qualify.
- **NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission:** Passage of time and implementation do not eliminate the submission requirement; actual circumstances remain relevant to lawful relief.
- **Connor v. Johnson:** Retains the distinction for a wholly judicial remedial plan.
- **McDaniel v. Sanchez:** A court's adoption of governmental policy does not transform it into an exempt judicial plan.
- **Shaw v. Reno, designated-channel holding:** Substantive preclearance remains with the Attorney General or D.D.C.; no enforcement-court substitute is authorized.
- **Johnson v. Miller:** Racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate alone does not establish constitutional injury or trigger strict scrutiny; separate statutory and constitutional objections remain open.

#### Law After Decision

Section 5 now expressly reaches a covered County's nondiscretionary administration of state-mandated voting changes. The election must not proceed under the unprecleared governmental system merely because the State requires it, delay has occurred or a federal court has ordered it used. Existing direct-voting coverage, specific-submission, designated-channel and genuinely judicial-remedy rules remain. The new construction neither places all California law under §5 nor changes the substantive purpose/effect burdens. The temporary divisions remain an option for lawful consideration, with their independent defects and authority unresolved.

### 1997-03-31 — Young v. Fordice; No. 95-2031.

Authority: [Record](../records/Young_v_Fordice_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins; every following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Federal implementation does not exempt discretionary voting changes

**Rule:** A covered jurisdiction must preclear new voter-registration practices embodying state policy choices even when adopted to comply with federal legislation. The NVRA preserves Voting Rights Act obligations; this decision does not decide an act compelled in every detail by federal law and leaving no state discretion.

**Controlling explanation:** Voting Rights Coalition preserves the valid federal-registration duties and distinct state-election questions; it supplies no automatic preclearance. Presley locates this case within practices directly governing registration and voting. Lopez prevents state-law compulsion from excusing a covered jurisdiction's administration of voting changes, but the Court need not mechanically extend that ruling to the different wholly federal hypothetical. Mississippi retained choices about instructions, forms, agencies, assistance, reporting and how applicants understand federal-only registration. Federal compliance does not exempt those choices from the statutory sequence.

##### This Provisional Plan never became the §5 comparison baseline

**Rule:** The Provisional Plan did not become a practice in force or effect for §5 comparison on the combined circumstances of forty-one days of registration use, adoption by only about one-third of registration officials, no election held or imminent, and prompt abandonment when anticipated state legislation would not be enacted. The Old System supplies the benchmark for this New System challenge; no single listed circumstance creates a general threshold.

**Controlling explanation:** Perkins and Lockhart preserve the principle that a practice may be operative despite a state-law defect. State illegality alone therefore cannot exclude a baseline. Here the limited use and uptake, nonelectoral setting and prompt abandonment together establish the tentative character of the arrangement, rather than a settled registration practice displaced later. Actual registrations are not erased from the facts: the Court finds neither that nobody registered nor that nobody relied on the plan. It determines the system-level §5 comparison, not whether every individual registration was legally valid, and adopts no forty-one-day, one-third-adoption, intent or election-use bright line.

##### The New System still changes the Old System

**Rule:** Retention of old state-election qualifications does not remove §5 coverage where the New System changes the Old System's registration instructions, agency designations, manuals, reporting, assistance or communication of federal-only and combined registration status. Both minor and major covered changes require preclearance without prior proof that they harm minority voters.

**Controlling explanation:** Allen and Hampton County require submission of changes in voting practices, not just changes deemed major or discriminatory in the enforcement suit. Mississippi's New System introduces discretionary procedures absent from the Old System; calling it a return to old state qualifications does not restore the former machinery. The changes can affect who registers, the information received and the administration of forms and voter categories. This supplies an independent coverage ground even though the Provisional Plan itself never became the benchmark. Substantive purpose and effect belong to the designated preclearance process, not this coverage decision.

##### Approval covers the identified submission, not every later contingency

**Rule:** Preclearance of a submitted registration arrangement does not approve materially different later choices that the submission did not identify. The submitting jurisdiction must describe its changes sufficiently; ambiguity does not require the Attorney General to infer and approve every possible later arrangement.

**Controlling explanation:** McCain and Lopez require accurate identification of what was submitted and approved. The earlier submission proceeded on an arrangement allowing NVRA registration to serve federal and state elections; the later dual arrangement's new communications and administration were not submitted as such. Neither federal enactment nor the Attorney General's knowledge of legislative uncertainty supplies blanket approval of unpresented choices. Particular genuine submission questions remain for the district court on the actual terms. The Court does not rescind the earlier approval or make it subject to judicial review merely because it fails to cover a later change.

##### Enforce nonuse through lawful, fitted relief

**Rule:** The enforcement court determines coverage and submission status and enforces nonuse of covered unprecleared practices; only the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia supplies substantive preclearance. Relief must preserve lawful NVRA duties and remain within independent remedial authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Clark, Morse, Shaw and Lopez preserve that division of responsibility. Calling a State-selected plan court ordered does not exempt it. Under Connor and McDaniel, a necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by a court may operate without separate §5 approval only when independent lawful authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement. No such plan or emergency exception is selected here. The district court must identify the covered changes, enforce nonuse and permit expeditious use of the designated channels without blocking compliance with a federal command. The Court orders no identical rolls, racial-purpose or retrogression finding, damages, cancellation of registrations or completed-election consequence.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson:** Preserved federal registration duties and distinct state-election administration; no automatic submission exemption or compulsory identical-roll remedy follows.
- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission:** Applied the direct-voting boundary to registration machinery, without extending §5 to every allocation of governmental power.
- **Perkins v. Matthews and City of Lockhart v. United States:** Preserved actual operative practice as potentially controlling despite state-law defects; mere illegality does not eliminate a baseline.
- **Allen v. State Board of Elections and NAACP v. Hampton County:** Applied coverage to minor as well as major voting changes and kept substantive discrimination separate.
- **McCain v. Lybrand:** Applied specific-submission requirements; an earlier approval does not encompass unpresented later choices.
- **Lopez v. Monterey County:** Applied administration, specific submission and nonuse principles with its lawful judicial-remedy qualifications; the wholly federal ministerial hypothetical remains undecided.
- **Clark v. Roemer:** Applied nonuse pending preclearance; no new emergency or retrospective equitable rule is announced.
- **Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia and Shaw v. Reno:** Preserved designated preclearance channels and the distinction between coverage and substantive review.
- **Connor v. Johnson and McDaniel v. Sanchez:** Preserved the distinction between a wholly judicial temporary remedy and judicial adoption of governmental policy.

#### Law After Decision

Discretionary NVRA implementation remains subject to §5. The Old System, not this short and incompletely adopted Provisional Plan, is the benchmark; the New System includes unapproved changes from it. Earlier approval remains submission-specific. Neither federal duties nor the possibility of separate federal and state rolls authorizes unprecleared discretionary details, and no rule declares every temporary or state-law-defective practice ineligible as a baseline.

### 1997-04-28 — Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno; Third Circuit No. 95-7137; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Pic_A_State_Pennsylvania_v_Reno_merits_1997-04-28.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The stopped business presents a concrete and ripe challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A business presents a concrete, ripe pre-enforcement challenge when a criminal statute directly reaches its existing activity, it has stopped that activity but intends to resume, serious penalties create a sufficiently real enforcement risk, and the requested relief would remove that present federal constraint. Those conditions are met here; neither an actual prosecution nor resolution of every independent state-law restriction is required.

**Authority:** Kennedy's unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies all three holdings at their stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories supplies the inquiry into fitness for judicial decision and hardship from withholding review. Pic-A-State stopped the very interstate procurement operation the amendment targeted; its reported intent to resume and exposure to substantial criminal sanctions establish a present injury. Bennett supplies personal injury, traceability and likely redress at the litigation stage, not automatic standing for every economically affected business. The Government's statement that prosecution is unlikely bears on risk but does not repudiate enforcement. Relief would remove an actual federal barrier even if Pennsylvania law separately remained relevant. The predominantly legal commerce question needs no prosecution to sharpen it, while withholding review would require continued cessation or exposure to punishment. The absence of a disavowal is not sufficient by itself in every case. Younger supplies no abstention ground where no state prosecution is pending. The prospective constitutional challenge against the enforcing Attorney General is a proper channel; standing does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

##### II. Congress may prohibit the actual interstate procurement transmission

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit a procurement business's knowing interstate or foreign transmission of information to obtain, for a person in one State, a ticket, chance, share or interest in a lottery, gift enterprise or similar scheme conducted by another State. The challenged commercial transmission is interstate commerce itself even when the physical ticket remains in the conducting State; the prohibition remains subject to the agreement exception and separate §1307 exemptions stated below.

**Controlling explanation:** Champion v. Ames establishes congressional power to prohibit interstate commerce in lottery interests; Gibbons recognizes interstate commercial intercourse beyond the movement of tangible paper. Pic-A-State's computer communications accomplish the economic procurement transaction across state lines. The amendment closes that commercial route around the existing physical-ticket prohibition. Robertson confirms that an actual interstate business operation needs no independently substantial effect from each transaction. Lopez requires the regulated class's connection to commerce among the States to be identified before aggregation or substantiality performs constitutional work, and it rejects a remote chain from local possession through social costs; it does not displace direct regulation of interstate commerce itself, where no aggregation or substantial-effects analysis is required. Harris, Hale and American Life League likewise preserve genuine economic-class and direct-market grounds. Here the enacted knowing-transmission and procurement-business elements themselves supply the connection, without a judicially added element or an aggregation holding. Hodel permits rational means within that established power: closing the procurement gap and accommodating different state lottery choices meet that requirement. Revenue preservation alone would not establish federal power over local conduct. Congress need not prohibit every lottery, prove a particular revenue loss, or make transaction-specific findings to regulate this interstate business.

##### III. The agreement exception supplies bounded congressional authorization

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1301 permits the otherwise covered procurement business when an agreement between the States in question or appropriate authorities of those States permits it. That express federal choice removes dormant Commerce Clause restraints on the particular state choices it authorizes, while leaving independent constitutional restraints intact.

**Controlling explanation:** Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin recognizes congressional power to authorize state burdens that the dormant Commerce Clause would otherwise forbid, and Northeast Bancorp requires the authorization to be clear. The agreement condition states the permitted category expressly. It accommodates state choices within Congress's independently valid regulation of interstate procurement; state permission for the underlying lottery does not displace the federal condition. Tracy separated ordinary economic rationality from discrimination analysis and reserved congressional authorization, so it does not supply a broader permission here. No qualifying agreement is established for Pic-A-State. The Court neither validates every Pennsylvania lottery restriction nor transfers unlimited federal legislative power to the States. The holding removes only the dormant implication and decides no independent First Amendment, equal-protection or nondelegation claim.

##### Coverage, distinct exceptions, and questions not reached

The procurement clause requires a person engaged in that business, a customer in one State, a covered scheme conducted by another State, and knowing interstate or foreign transmission for procurement. It does not prohibit every lottery communication or wholly local purchase. The separate physical-carriage and receipt clauses remain distinct. The penalty is a fine under title 18, imprisonment for no more than two years, or both.

Section 1307(a)(1) separately exempts advertisements, prize lists or other information about a state-authorized, state-conducted lottery when contained in a publication published in that State or another lottery State, or broadcast by a station licensed in such a State. Section 1307(a)(2) exempts such information about other schemes authorized or not prohibited in the conducting State when conducted by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or as a clearly occasional and ancillary commercial promotional activity. Section 1307(b) separately exempts transportation or mailing of equipment, tickets or materials concerning a state-authorized lottery to addresses within its conducting State, and materials designed for an authorized foreign lottery to addressees within that foreign country. State includes the States, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and United States territories or possessions; foreign country includes an empire, country, dominion, colony, protectorate or subdivision other than the United States and its territories or possessions. The subsection-(b) lottery definition concerns pooled ticket-or-chance proceeds allocated by chance and excludes sporting-event bets or wagers. A nonprofit is an organization that would qualify as tax exempt under Internal Revenue Code §501. No fact places this business within an exemption; these provisions supply no blanket exemption for every lawful state lottery. No rule governs hypothetical wholly local transmissions or validates an interstate element unrelated to the commercial transaction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Abbott Laboratories is applied to this predominantly legal challenge and the hardship of cessation or criminal exposure.
- Bennett v. Spear is applied for personal injury, traceability and likely redress; its distinct ESA and APA routes are not imported.
- Younger v. Harris does not require abstention on this record without a pending state prosecution.

### 1997-05-12 — Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board; Price v. Bossier Parish School Board; Nos. 95-1455 and 95-1508.

Authority: [Record](../records/Reno_v_Bossier_Parish_School_Board_merits_1997-05-12.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. Distinct statutory inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5's effect inquiry compares the proposed voting change with the operative benchmark for retrogression; a violation of §2 does not itself establish prohibited §5 effect. A proved §2 violation is also not an independent ground for withholding §5 preclearance, but clearance leaves a separate §2 challenge available.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I. All nine join the effect-benchmark and independent-action propositions. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply six direct votes for the complete nonincorporation rule; Stone-Zsela does not join that additional proposition, and Stevens and Souter oppose it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court keeps the two statutory tasks distinct. *Beer* supplies the retrogression inquiry, and *Lockhart* preserves comparison with the operative voting practice. Section 2 separately asks whether minority voters have equal electoral opportunity. *De Grandy* requires the statutory predicates and totality inquiry and rejects a command to maximize majority-minority districts. The objection that Congress cannot permit clearance of an unlawful plan mistakes clearance for a determination that the plan is lawful for every purpose. Section 2 enforcement remains available, with its own standards and burdens. Neither the stipulated lack of retrogression nor the possibility of two additional majority-Black districts decides §2 liability. The Court makes no original §2 finding and requires no proportional representation.

##### II. Relevant evidence remains relevant to purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** Evidence bearing on racial dilution may also bear on the purpose of a voting change and may not be categorically excluded from §5 purpose review merely because it supports a §2 theory. When such an exclusion may have affected the purpose determination, the ultimate preclearance judgment must be vacated for consideration of the relevant evidence without treating it as conclusive proof or altering the jurisdiction's existing burden to establish absence of prohibited purpose.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by all nine Justices, controls directly.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that evidentiary relevance and substantive liability are different questions. *Arlington Heights* makes impact, historical background, the sequence of events, departures from ordinary procedure or substantive criteria, contemporaneous explanations and possible pretext relevant to intent. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* preserve purpose as an inquiry distinct from nonretrogressive effects. The Board's selection of the Police Jury plan, rejection of the alternative, parish history and disputed explanations therefore require consideration for what they show about its actual purpose. They are not presumptive proof. The contrary argument—that evidence of a permissible result cannot show an impermissible purpose—does not justify excluding evidence capable of supporting the required inference. The district court's apparent categorical separation leaves uncertainty whether it applied the correct inquiry. The Court remands rather than finds purpose itself and creates neither a same-decision defense nor a new causation rule or burden shift.

##### III. Unconstitutional purposeful racial dilution is not confined to intended retrogression

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5 prohibits preclearance of a covered voting change adopted with an unconstitutional purpose to dilute voting opportunity on account of race, even when that purpose does not include worsening the existing benchmark. Nonretrogressive effect therefore does not resolve that purpose inquiry; the court must determine actual purpose, preserving the existing preclearance burden and without treating every consideration of race or rejection of a feasible alternative as unlawful.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, has five direct votes and controls the remand. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not decide this larger scope question.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the independent purpose prohibition its own work. The statutory bar addresses a purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race, not merely a purpose to worsen the existing arrangement. *Beer* defines effect through retrogression; it does not replace the separate purpose language. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* confirm that benign comparative effects do not dispose of unlawful intent. Intentional unconstitutional dilution consequently cannot obtain clearance simply because the resulting plan does not make an already deficient arrangement worse. This holding does not incorporate every §2 results violation. *Shaw* preserves the distinction between purposeful racial dilution and racial consideration in territorial design alone; *De Grandy* rejects automatic liability from the feasibility of additional districts. The Board's actual purpose remains for the factfinder. No same-decision defense, altered allocation of proof, maximization duty or proportional-representation guarantee follows.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Beer v. United States*: Retains the retrogression benchmark for §5 effect; the separate purpose rule does not overrule it.
- *City of Lockhart v. United States*: Applies its comparison with the operative voting practice, without merging §2 liability into §5.
- *Johnson v. De Grandy*: Preserves separate statutory predicates, totality proof and rejection of maximization; feasible alternative districts are evidence, not liability by themselves.
- *Village of Arlington Heights v. Metropolitan Housing Development Corp.*: Applies its contextual intent evidence; no new same-decision or burden rule is adopted here.
- *City of Pleasant Grove v. United States* and *City of Richmond v. United States*: Apply their separation of purpose and effect to require an independent purpose inquiry.
- *Shaw v. Reno*: Preserves the distinction between purposeful deprivation and racial territorial design alone.
- *Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia* and *Young v. Fordice*: Preserve the designated preclearance process, independent enforcement and distinct coverage, benchmark, purpose and effect tasks; neither previously resolved this purpose-scope question.

#### Law After Decision

Effective May 12, 1997, §5 clearance does not depend on separate §2 compliance, but cannot be granted for a change adopted with unconstitutional purposeful racial vote dilution merely because retrogression was neither intended nor achieved. Dilution evidence must receive its proper evidentiary consideration. The effect benchmark, independent §2 standards, existing allocation of the preclearance burden and designated review channels remain unchanged. Actual liability and purpose in this case remain unresolved.

### 1997-06-16 — Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash; No. 96-454; October Term 1996; inventory matter 115, chunk 10.

Authority: [Record](../records/Associates_Commercial_Corp_v_Rash_merits_1997-06-16.md).

#### Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** When a Chapter 13 debtor retains and uses collateral under §1325(a)(5)(B), §506(a) values the secured claim by the price a willing buyer in the debtor's trade, business or situation would pay a willing seller for comparable property of like age and condition serving that proposed use. The court must use replacement value with appropriate evidentiary adjustments, rather than net foreclosure value for a sale the plan prevents.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court by Ginsburg, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, with the author included in that eight-Justice coalition. All eight adopt the rule, explanation and limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 506(a)'s first sentence identifies what is valued: the creditor's interest in the estate's actual collateral. It does not choose the valuation market. The second sentence directs attention to the valuation's purpose and the proposed disposition or use. Here the plan prevents foreclosure and retains the truck for the debtors' freight-hauling business. Valuing a sale that will not occur would disregard that direction and erase the relevant distinction between surrender and retention. The creditor-interest language does not compel that result. Timbers bars compensation merely for delayed foreclosure; replacement valuation follows a separate confirmation command rather than adding such compensation. Dewsnup's Chapter 7 lien rule and Nobelman's home-mortgage protection concern different provisions. BFP's independent valuation under realistic forced-sale conditions likewise follows a different statutory purpose and transaction. Replacement value does not mean new-property cost, debtor-specific earnings, a mandatory retail quotation or a midpoint between incompatible standards. The bankruptcy court determines the appropriate market evidence. A retail measure must exclude separately included warranties, storage, reconditioning or other benefits the debtors do not receive through retention. Accessories or modifications beyond the creditor's lien cannot enlarge secured value. These adjustments do not require mechanically subtracting all dealer profit or overhead from a demonstrated comparable-market price. The Court determines no dollar value, interest rate or disposition expense.

**Limits:** The holding does not resolve surrender, abandonment or materially different proposed uses. The unappealed determination concerning lease earnings and the unargued income-capitalization theory remain outside review. The $31,875 adopted below was a wholesale estimate derived from the debtors' $42,500 retail estimate, not a finding of separately quantified foreclosure expenses.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United Savings Association of Texas v. Timbers of Inwood Forest Associates: preserves its no-delay-compensation rule; distinguishes that question from the §506(a) market used at confirmation.
- Dewsnup v. Timm: preserves its bounded Chapter 7 §506(d) lien holding; it supplies no universal valuation rule.
- Nobelman v. American Savings Bank: preserves protection of the specified principal-residence mortgage rights without extending it to this truck.
- BFP v. Resolution Trust Corp.: preserves independent §548 valuation under realistically comparable forced-sale conditions; the different proposed transaction here requires the §506(a) retention measure. No foreclosure-price safe harbor is adopted.

#### Law After Decision

Retained-use Chapter 13 collateral is valued by adjusted replacement value. Evidentiary flexibility identifies that value without permitting substitution of a different governing market standard. Other bankruptcy provisions, state-law lien boundaries and independently protected rights retain their existing scope.

### 1997-06-25 — City of Boerne v. Flores; No. 95-2074.

Authority: [Record](../records/City_of_Boerne_v_Flores_merits_1997-06-25.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy. The opinion does not contain a separate historical reconstruction of the Fourteenth Amendment's adoption.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5 gives Congress substantial choice in making constitutional protection effective. South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan recognize preventive measures that extend beyond adjudicated violations; City of Rome likewise preserves effective enforcement in its setting. Their breadth does not erase the distinction between enforcing a right and changing it. The Court examines the constitutional guarantee, the threatened violation and the practical statutory design. It asks whether the remedy bears a supportable substantive relationship to that injury, not whether Congress compiled a prescribed quantity or form of evidence. Congress need not prove a violation in every affected jurisdiction, make formal findings, or require individualized proof for each prohibited act. Geographic limits and expiration provisions can illuminate fit but are not indispensable conditions. The contrary position correctly emphasizes that prevention would be ineffective if confined to conduct already adjudged unconstitutional. The Court accepts that premise; what it rejects is an enforcement power without a limiting connection to the right being enforced. The independent Fifteenth Amendment power remains governed by its existing precedents and is not further resolved here.

##### II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power

**Holding and operative rule:** RFRA's general application to State and local laws cannot be sustained under §5 because it substitutes its substantial-burden, compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means standard across the field for the constitutional boundary retained in Smith, without the required enforcement relationship. The Fifth Circuit's contrary power ruling is reversed, and the RFRA claim against the City must be dismissed.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith remains the constitutional rule for genuinely neutral and generally applicable laws. Lukumi independently protects against objective religious targeting and substantial secular underinclusion; RFRA does not merely enforce those limits. RFRA demands a compelling governmental interest pursued by the least restrictive means whenever covered government action substantially burdens religious exercise, even under neutral and generally applicable laws. Its State and local coverage extends across subjects, governmental functions and rules without a limiting constitutional class, administrative setting or supported remedial problem that fits that universal substitution. The objection concerns the relation between its broad command and the constitutional wrong, not a numerical deficiency in legislative examples. Congress's disagreement with Smith cannot itself authorize rewriting the guarantee through §5. Broad preventive legislation remains possible when appropriately fitted to constitutional injury. The Court does not reconsider Smith, decide the permit denial's neutrality, motive, substantial burden or justification, or deny every other available source of congressional authority to protect religious exercise. Federal RFRA remains outside this case, including its rule concerning later federal enactments. No general hearing requirement or substitute exemption code is created.

##### III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** Church of Scientology and Swanner did not decide the constitutional power question and therefore do not establish §5 authority for RFRA's universal State and local application. Their prospective statutory applications cannot govern future State and local RFRA claims resting only on that power after this decision, but their completed judgments retain finality and their independent grounds and statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation.

**Controlling explanation:** Both decisions expressly reserved RFRA's constitutionality while applying its claimant-specific statutory standard. Resolving that reservation does not overrule a constitutional holding that neither decision made. The superior constitutional allocation now prevents continued reliance on §5 alone for the universal State and local command. Concrete reliance on final judgments is protected by leaving them intact; general reliance on a future unauthorized federal claim cannot supply missing legislative power. The Court orders no blanket reopening, retrospective recovery or reconsideration of independent constitutional or state-law grounds. Distinct transaction or remedial reliance claims require their own lawful source and are not presumed. Agostini retains Lemon as the governing Establishment Clause test and updates it into one inquiry with three routes: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives the attribution-and-function inquiry. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test. Its overruling of Aguilar's categorical onsite rule and displacement of Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale concern a different Establishment Clause setting and supply no RFRA power ruling. Plyler's distinction between continuing decree terms and underlying federal rights likewise does not authorize Congress to redefine those rights. The remand leaves every separately pleaded and preserved claim for its own analysis and decides no permit entitlement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Smith remains controlling for genuinely neutral and generally applicable laws; the Court neither overrules it nor decides its correctness anew.
- Lukumi preserves the separate constitutional rules for objective religious targeting, substantial secular underinclusion and demanding justification. Boerne's actual conduct is not classified under those rules here.
- South Carolina v. Katzenbach retains its recognition of effective preventive enforcement in its constitutional setting; its remedies do not establish an unlimited power to redefine rights.
- Katzenbach v. Morgan retains broad preventive authority tied to enforcement, without authorizing replacement of the substantive guarantee.
- City of Rome remains intact within its Fifteenth Amendment setting; no further independent Fifteenth Amendment rule is announced.
- Church of Scientology's constitutional reservation is resolved for §5-based universal State and local application; completed judgments and independent grounds remain intact, and its statutory reasoning survives where validly operative.
- Swanner receives the same prospective limitation; its independent and reserved claims are not extinguished.
- Agostini updates the governing Lemon test into the three-route inquiry described above, overrules Aguilar's categorical treatment of the safeguarded onsite public remedial service and displaces Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale. Ball's completed judgment and unaddressed holdings remain intact; Agostini supplies no RFRA source-of-power ruling.
- Plyler retains its rule for conditional termination under the original PLRA; its prospective-remedy reasoning does not settle congressional authority to alter the constitutional right.

#### Law After Decision

RFRA's universal State and local application cannot rest on §5. Congress retains preventive and remedial power with a congruent and proportional relation to constitutional injury; no universal requirement of formal findings, geographic limits, sunsets or individualized violations is adopted. Smith and Lukumi continue to define the relevant constitutional baseline. Federal RFRA, final judgments and independently available claims remain undisturbed. The earlier RFRA applications are limited prospectively in the invalid field, rather than erased or converted into prior constitutional holdings.

### 1997-06-25 — Lawyer v. Department of Justice; No. 95-2024.

#### Holdings

No combination of separate writings or narrowest-ground inference supplies the Court's authority.

##### I. The personal challenge to this decree remains reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** A voter with an established personal relationship to the challenged electoral assignment and decree may obtain review of the decree and the disposition of his own claims even when his design-only constitutional theory fails. ##### II. General consent authority does not settle every districting-authority objection

**Holding and operative rule:** A lawful consent decree may impose obligations broader than the relief obtainable after a trial without invariably requiring a prior adjudication of liability, but it must resolve a dispute within subject-matter jurisdiction, remain within the pleadings' general scope, advance the underlying law's objectives and comply with law. **Controlling explanation:** Firefighters supplies both settlement latitude and these limits. The Court need not decide whether a federal districting decree invariably requires an established violation or whether authorized litigation settlement necessarily fulfills every requirement of the State's redistricting opportunity. Defendants denied liability and conceded a reasonable basis only for settlement; the separate lower concurrence supplied no majority adjudication or unconditional admission. RFRA gives religiously motivated claimants a statutory exemption argument unavailable to atheists or agnostics asserting otherwise comparable secular interests. Applying Wallace's neutrality principle, he regards that preference for religion over irreligion as an additional constitutional defect. He does not assume that this church automatically qualifies for an exemption, condemn every religious accommodation, or decide federal RFRA's disposition in this State and local case. No other Justice joins this ground.

Scalia concurs in the Court's full operative reasoning and separately defends Smith. He rejects the claim that historical practice establishes the general judicial exemption regime proposed by the dissent. Protecting religious exercise from discriminatory treatment does not make courts general administrators of exemptions from neutral, generally applicable law. His additional historical defense is his own; the Court's enforcement holding does not depend on it.

O'Connor dissents from immediate decision and would order focused briefing and reargument on Smith, the neutral-law burden question, Sherbert and Yoder. She considers Smith wrong and would restore compelling-interest review of substantial burdens from neutral laws. She agrees Congress cannot redefine constitutional rights and conditionally accepts RFRA's §5 invalidity if Smith is correct. That conditional view does not supply a present merits vote: she would first reconsider the baseline. She grants no permit relief and does not now uphold RFRA.

Souter dissents. His doubts about Smith warrant properly focused briefing before either endorsing or rejecting it in a case whose §5 question depends on the constitutional baseline. He would order reargument; because the Court declines, he would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. He does not accept RFRA's invalidity even on an assumed Smith baseline, affirm the Fifth Circuit or hold RFRA valid.

Breyer dissents and would obtain reargument on the constitutional baseline before deciding Congress's power. He shares the need to reconsider Smith but does not adopt every historical assertion in O'Connor's critique or her conditional conclusion that RFRA exceeds §5 if Smith is correct. He reserves RFRA's validity under either baseline and does not claim Congress may simply redefine rights.

### 1997-06-25 — Lawyer v. Department of Justice; No. 95-2024.

Authority: [Record](../records/Lawyer_v_Department_of_Justice_merits_1997-06-25.md).

**Souter, concurring, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.** These Justices join the Court's confined remand and personally reaffirm Shaw, Miller and Vera's distinction between shared territorial design and independently cognizable unequal treatment. They also reject an invariable adjudicated-violation prerequisite for a properly supported districting settlement. Firefighters permits lawful consent obligations beyond trial remedies, subject to jurisdiction, pleading scope, the underlying law and protection of a nonconsenter's valid claims. Authorized state settlement can exercise the State's opportunity without a formal legislative session; it does not become a legislative enactment or dispense with judicial authority. Here the lower court's assessment depended on the rejected design-only predicate. Lawful settlement capacity and the possibility that Plan 386 is itself constitutional do not make that error immaterial. The return must determine the actual preserved basis without treating paragraph 9 as either an established independent claim or an allegation that never existed. These views supply no new claim, automatic hearing or different remedy. Their broader districting-consent position is not a holding of the Court.

**Scalia, concurring, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.** These Justices join the Court's common ground and judgment while preserving the additional requirement that federal judicial replacement of state boundaries rest on a legally established federal violation and a proper opportunity for normal state redistricting in response. Growe protects the State's actual mapmaking process. Authority to litigate and propose a compromise is distinct from state enactment and from authority for federal judicial compulsion; the political branches' refusal to convene before liability is adjudicated does not conclusively answer their state-opportunity objection. They accept Firefighters's general settlement principles without accepting that those principles dispense with the districting-specific conditions. They acknowledge Shaw, Miller and Vera as governing law but maintain their published objection to the design-only boundary. Their proposed predominance trigger is not adopted here and would require justification, not automatic invalidation. They reserve Plan 386's merits and join only the confined return, without ordering a finding that Plan 330 is unconstitutional, a new claim, a map or an election timetable. Their additional authority requirement remains noncontrolling.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The Chief Justice would affirm approval of Plan 386 and leave it in force. A federal court may approve a State-supported redistricting settlement without first adjudicating the superseded plan unconstitutional when authorized state representatives choose the replacement, the State has had a genuine opportunity to act, no unlawful duty is imposed on a nonsettling plaintiff, and the court independently considers the new plan's legality. Florida exercised its choice through the Attorney General and counsel for both legislative chambers after its political branches declined to convene. This treats state primacy as an allocation of choice, without calling the proposal a legislative enactment. Lawyer obtained elimination of Plan 330 and retained a hearing and appeal on Plan 386; consent did not purchase immunity from his constitutional challenge. An additional declaration concerning the discarded plan was unnecessary.

He would accept the supported purpose finding under ordinary clear-error review. Crossing water or county lines, irregular shape and minority percentages are relevant but do not by themselves show that race displaced ordinary districting criteria. The record supplied geographic and traditional explanations. That finding is not indispensable to rejecting the presented design-only theory: even proved predominance alone would be insufficient under Shaw, Miller and Vera. Miller also permits otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions to improve electoral opportunity without an exact federal command or a prior adjudication requiring the chosen map; it creates no racial-maximization entitlement. The Chief Justice does not approve unauthorized executive redistricting, new duties imposed on an objector, or a settlement evading proof of a still-operative violation. He does not immunize any independently cognizable deprivation. Any preserved exclusion, dilution, unequal-participation or other independent claim requires its own adjudication under its elements, burdens, justification and remedy; future challenges based on materially different evidence or administration remain available. His affirmance rests on the presented challenge and supplies no finding that paragraph 9 was absent or waived, no adjudication of a distinct claim from that allegation, and no unpresented statutory decision.
