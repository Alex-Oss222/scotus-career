## Watts and Putra

Watts was convicted under 21 U.S.C. §841(a)(1) of possession of cocaine base with intent to distribute and acquitted under 18 U.S.C. §924(c) of using a firearm in relation to a drug offense. Police found cocaine base in a kitchen cabinet and two loaded guns with ammunition in a bedroom closet. The district court found by a preponderance that he possessed the guns in connection with the drug offense and applied the two-level §2D1.1(b)(1) adjustment. The Ninth Circuit vacated on its categorical rule against reliance on facts underlying an acquitted charge, and rejected the Government's attempted distinction between the firearm connection needed for the offense and the adjustment. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 149–150, citing 67 F.3d 790, 796–797.

Putra was convicted of aiding and abetting possession with intent to distribute one ounce of cocaine on May 8, 1992, and acquitted of the corresponding charge involving five ounces on May 9. Both transactions were videotaped. The district court found involvement in the second sale by a preponderance and aggregated the quantities under §1B1.3. The Ninth Circuit vacated and remanded for resentencing; it treated use of the second transaction as punishment for the acquitted offense. Judge Wallace dissented from that lower-court rule. The single Rule 12.4 petition covers both judgments. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 150–151, citing 78 F.3d 1386, 1389–1390. No exact sentence or Guidelines range is supplied by this extraction.

## Old Chief

The federal charges following the 1993 confrontation included assault with a dangerous weapon, use of a firearm in a crime of violence, and §922(g)(1) possession by a prohibited person. The prior judgment admitted at trial described a December 18, 1988 assault causing serious bodily injury and a five-year sentence. Old Chief offered to admit qualifying prior-conviction status, moved to exclude the name/nature, and renewed his objection when the Government introduced the judgment. The prosecutor refused a stipulation and the judge allowed that refusal based on the prosecution's general freedom to choose its evidence. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 174–177.

The proposed instruction needed clarification that the admitted conviction satisfied the legal definition, including the §921(a)(20) exclusions. Its wording was not the trial court's stated ground for refusing the offer. The instruction actually given referred to credibility despite Old Chief not testifying; he had requested an instruction of that kind and withdrew it only after the charge. The neutral packet must keep that procedural fact separate from the preserved objection to the conviction record. Source record recitation: 519 U.S. 175–177 n.2.

The Ninth Circuit affirmed under its rule allowing probative proof of the prior offense notwithstanding the offer. It did not pass on harmless error. Source record recitation: 519 U.S. 177, 192 n.11. That absence is not a finding that the error was harmless or harmful. A final new-trial disposition would require a supported harmlessness assessment distinct from deciding Rule 403 error.

## Walters

The argument date is November 6, 1996. The EEOC sued on April 7, 1993 over Walters's discharge after her sex-discrimination complaint; she intervened and additionally asserted discriminatory failure to promote. The district court dismissed under 864 F.Supp. 71 (N.D. Ill. 1994), and the Seventh Circuit affirmed at 60 F.3d 1225, applying *Zimmerman*, 704 F.2d 347, 354. The employer sought dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction; this procedural label does not independently decide whether numerosity is jurisdictional. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 202, 204–205.

The parties stipulated that the employer did not reach the threshold in 1989. Its working days were Monday–Friday. For most of 1990 its payroll had 15–17 employees each working day, but only nine weeks had actual compensation to 15 employees on every working day; two hourly part-time employees ordinarily skipped one day weekly. Paid leave counted as compensation under the alternative count. The transcript of those stipulations is not independently recovered. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 205 and its continuation. Application may proceed on those stipulated facts without inventing additional payroll disputes; employee status remains governed by the appropriate common-law relationship inquiry.
