## O'Gilvie

Argument occurred October 9, 1996. The initial tort verdict awarded $1,525,000 in actual damages and $10 million in punitive damages; the federal tax litigation addresses the latter proceeds. The husband sought a refund; the Government brought separate erroneous-refund recovery suits against the children. The Tenth Circuit reversed the taxpayers' favorable district-court judgments. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 79–82.

## Greene

The judgment below is *Greene v. State*, 266 Ga. 439, 440–442, 469 S.E.2d 129, 134–135 (1996). The trial court excluded five prospective jurors for cause over Greene's objection after they expressed reservations about capital punishment. The lower opinion described *Witt* as “controlling authority” both for death qualification and for appellate deference to trial-court demeanor/credibility findings. The supplied packet's single-juror shorthand should not be taken to restrict the reviewed issue to one identified juror. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 145–146. No individual juror qualification is independently established by this extraction.

## M.L.B.

The children's father and stepmother filed the termination/adoption suit in November 1993. The mother counterclaimed for custody and disputed the visitation account. Following three evidentiary dates, the chancery decree filed December 14, 1994 terminated her parental rights and approved adoption. The decree recited the statutory grounds and the clear-and-convincing burden but gave no account of the supporting evidence. These are characteristics of the order, not an independent finding that the termination evidence was insufficient.

The mother filed a timely appeal in January 1995 and paid the $100 filing fee. The clerk then estimated $2,352.36 for preparing/transmitting the record: $1,900 transcript, $438 other record documents, $4.36 binders and $10 mailing. She could not pay and sought indigent appellate status; the Mississippi Supreme Court denied that request in August 1995 under its rule limiting indigent status in civil cases to trial proceedings. The appeal was dismissed for nonpayment. Her federal challenge to the appellate cost barrier was presented in the chancery court and the state supreme court. Source recitation: 519 U.S. 106–109 and notes 1–3.

Mississippi Appellate Rule 10(b)(2), as recited at 109, required relevant transcript portions for an evidentiary-sufficiency challenge.
