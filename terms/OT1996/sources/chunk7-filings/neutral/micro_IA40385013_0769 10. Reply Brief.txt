Since most courts review tribal court judgments under
the principles of comity applicable to the enforcement of
foreign judgments, successful enforcement of tribal judg-
ments generally requires a showing that the underlying
tribal proceedings were full, fair, and non-discriminatory.
See Wilson v. Marchington, 934 F.Supp. 1187, 1193 (D.Mont.
1996), appeal docketed, No. 96-35145 (9th Cir. Jan. 30, 1996);
see generally Br. of United States at 6. That requirement,
which is inapplicable to the enforcement of state court
judgments, forecloses any concern that tribal courts are
not up to the task that Congress and this Court have
secured for them.
