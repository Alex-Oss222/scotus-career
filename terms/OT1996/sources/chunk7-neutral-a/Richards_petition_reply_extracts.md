This case revolves around police entry into the petitioner’s hotel room in Madison, Wisconsin, at 3:40 a.m. on December 31, 1991. The police did not comply with the announcement rule in effecting the entry. The magistrate who issued the warrant specifically determined that the particular circumstances did not justify a no- knock warrant, and he struck those portions of the warrant that would have authorized such execution. The Wisconsin supreme court opinion relates, The dispositive facts for purposes of this appeal can be stated succinctly: on December 31, 1991, police executed a search warrant for the motel room of the defendant seeking evidence of the felonious crime of Possession with Intent to Deliver a Controlled Substance in violation of Wis. Stat. § 161.41 (In) (1991-92). They did not knock and announce prior to their entry. Drugs were seized. The circuit court denied Richards’[s]} motion to suppress. Richards subsequently entered pleas of no contest to the felony of possession of cocaine base with the intent to deliver, Wis. Stat. $161.41(1m), and a tax stamp violation, Wis. Stat. $139.95(2). The court found him guilty and sentenced him to 13 years imprisonment on the possession with intent count and three years concurrent imprisonment on the tax stamp count. Richards appealed. The court of appeals upheld the circuit court’s ruling, relying on (State v. ]} „ 181 Wis. 2d 410, 511 N.W.2d 591 (1994)). Richards filed a petition for review which we granted. 201 Wis. 2d at 843. For the purposes of this petition, Richards does not contest this recitation of facts. The Wisconsin Supreme Court held that the particular circumstances of this case did not matter in light of the blanket exception to the announcement rule. If the United States Supreme Court finds the Wisconsin blanket rule repugnant to the Fourth Amendment, then and only then will further factual analysis be required on remand to determine whether specific exigent circumstances were present.
