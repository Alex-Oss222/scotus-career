If the plaintiff has established that the defendant engaged any 
deceptive trade practices as I have defined them, by a 
preponderance of the evidence, then you should find for the 
plaintiff. If the plaintiff fails to establish that by a 
preponderance of the evidence, then you should find for the 
defendant. 


The fifth issue for you to determine is another Consumer 
Protection Act requirement concerning the issue of “whether 
or not the developer made any misrepresentations, omission 
statements, that would lead the plaintiffs to believe that the 
common elements of the condominium had a quality useful 
life or characteristics which they do not, in fact, have.” 


In order for the plaintiff to meet that requirement of the 
Statute, it has to show, by a preponderance of the evidence, 
that the defendant made a representation that “consumer 
goods, consumer realty or consumer services have a 
sponsorship approval, accessory characteristic, 
-(unintelligible) use, benefit or quantity, which they do not 
have. A merchant has a sponsorship, approval status, 
affiliation, or connection which he does not have deteriorated, 
altered, reconditioned, reclaimed or second-hand consumer 
goods are original or new, or consumer goods consumer 
realty, consumer services are of a particular standard quality, 
grade, style or model which they are not, for consumer 
protection purposes, the meaning of any statement or 
representation is determined not only by what is explicitly 
stated, but also by what is reasonably applied.

“This section of the act does not require the seller to have 
knowledge of the falsity or intent to deceive. This section 
only requires a false deceptive statement that has the capacity 
to mislead the consumer.” 


So the plaintiff has to show by a preponderance of the 
evidence that Wellington Homes made representations, 
misrepresentations, omissions, or statements that would lead 
the plaintiffs to believe that the common elements at the 
condominium had a quality useful life or characteristics 
which they do not have. 


‘The sixth issue which you have to determine is whether or 


not the plaintiff's have shown that the defendant's concealed 
any of the conditions at Lords Landing from the plaintiffs. 


“The Maryland Consumer Protection Act prohibits a failure 
to state a material fact if the failure deceives or tends to 
deceive. An omission is considered material if a significant 
number of unsophisticated consumers would attach 
importance to the information in determining a choice of 
action. This section of the Act does not require the seller to 
have knowledge of the falsity or intent to deceive. This 
section requires only a false or deceptive statement that has 
the capacity to mislead the consumer.” 


The seventh issue that you must determine concerns an issue 
of a fraudulent misrepresentation by the defendant with 
regard to the conditions of the common elements. 


“The Consumer Protection Act prohibits any deception, fraud, 
pretense, false premise, misrepresentation, or knowing 
concealment, suppression, or omission of any material fact 
with the intent that a consumer rely on the same in 
connection with the promotion or sale of consumer goods, 


4a 
consumer realty, or consumer service, or a contract or other 
agreement for the evaluation, perfection, marketing, 
brokering, or promotion of an invention, or the subsequent 
performance of a merchant with respect to an agreement of 
sale, lease or rental. An omission is considered material if 
a significant number of unsophisticated consumers of goods 
or services would attach importance to the information in 
deciding on a course of action.” 


If the plaintiff proves, by a preponderance of the evidence, 
that there was a fraudulent misrepresentation, as I've just 
defined it, by the defendant, you should find for the plaintiff. 
If the plaintiff does not prove that by a preponderance of the 
evidence, you should find for the defendant. 


If you find for the defendant on all of these issues, that ends 
your verdict. You will then have concluded that the plaintiff 
has not established, by a preponderance of the evidence, that 
under any of the theories of the cause of action, the plaintiff 
has established by a preponderance of the evidence, that the 
defendant is responsible for any of these conditions that may 
have existed at Lord’s Landing. 


On the other hand, if you find for the plaintiff for any one of 
those issues, then you should go on and determine a fair 
amount of money that will compensate the plaintiff for any 
loss that they have sustained as damages. And so there will 
be on your verdict sheet a question, “In what amount do you 
assess damages?”
