The Fifth Circuit U.S. Court of Appeals, in an opinion 
drafted by Judge John Minor Wisdom, agreed with and affirmed 
the district court’s decision, after analyzing both the purpose of 
ERISA and Louisiana community property law and tradition. 
The Court held that under the facts of this case, Louisiana 


community property law is not sufficiently “related to” an 
employee benefit plan to necessitate ERISA preemption, 
because nothing was sought from the plan or its fiduciary, and 
no duty would be imposed on the administrator. 


Judge Wisdom noted that the area of state domestic 
relations law has long been “sacrosanct” and protected against 
federal intrusion. The Court noted that this decision relates not 
to the plan but to the disposition of the proceeds only after 


payment to the designated beneficiary. The Court also 
determined that neither ERISA’s spendthrift provision‘ nor the 


IRA provision enacted as part of ERISA’ had displaced or 
preempted Louisiana’s community property law. 


The Fifth Circuit decision reiterated its prior position* 
that “ERISA was not meant to consume everything in its path.”

The Purpose of the Employee 
Retirement Income Security Act 


In 1974 the Congress adopted ERISA. The purposes of 
the Act are set out at 29 U.S.C. §1001. Generally, they set out 


that retirement plans and their management have grown into a 
national concern that affects interstate commerce; that there 
existed a lack of information given to the employee as to the 
terms of their retirement plans. In addition to addressing these 
concerns, the purpose of ERISA is to provide safeguards as to 
the administration of the plans, provide minimum standards of 
financial soundness of the plan to pay promised benefits, and 
ensure reasonable vesting provisions. 


The purpose also includes establishing standards for the 
conduct, responsibility and obligations of fiduciaries of employee 
access to the federal courts. The Act sets out the purpose of 
requiring plan termination insurance; and protects employees by 
improving the equitable character of benefit plans by requiring 
them to vest the accrued benefits of employees with significant 
periods of service." 


Curiously absent from the stated purpose of ERISA is 
the establishment of nationally uniform property rights as 
between spouses.
