RICHARD PELLEGRINI, being duly sworn, deposes and 
says: 


1. I am Director of the Bureau of Financial Management 
and Information Support, New York State Department of 
Health (“Department”) and, as such, I am familiar with the 
facts hereinafter set forth. 


2. New York Public Health Law (“PHL”) § 2807-d is a rev- 
enue raising measure that imposes a percentage assessment 
on hospital gruss receipts. This assessment is imposed on all 
hospitals in New York with certain narrowly designed excep- 
tions relating to financially distressed institutions. See PHL 
§ 2807-d(1). “Hospitals” as defined in PHL 2807-d and in 
PHL Article 28 generally (see PHL § 2801(1)) encompass a 
variety of medical institutions, including diagnostic and treat- 
ment centers issued operating certificates pursuant to PHL § 
2805. The two clinics operated by plaintiff in New York and 
referenced in the Complaint herein are PHL Article 28 
licensed diagnostic and treatment centers and, as such, they 
come within the provisions of the gross receipts assessment 
provisions of PHL § 2807-d. 


3. The Department has designated Blue Cross of Central 
New York, Inc. (BCCNY) to act as the Department’s admin- 
istrative agent for collecting these assessments. Hospitals 
have been instructed to forward such assessments on a 
monthly basis to BCCNY and BCCNY in turn forwards said 
monies to the State’s general fund. These instructions are con- 
tained in a letter dated 1/24/91 which was sent by the Depart- 
ment to all affected health care facilities in New York. A copy 
of this letter of 1/24/91 is annexed hereto as Exhibit A. 


4. The provisions of PHL 2807-d apply to plaintiffs’ clin- 
ics solely because they are “hospitals” as defined in Arti- 
cle 28 of the Public Health Law and the provisions of PHL 
2807-d apply to almost all hospitals. The status of these facil- 
ities as “hospitals” is in turn based on the nature of the med- 
ical services they provided. The fact that these clinics are

operated by an ERISA qualified union benefits fund is 
entirely irrelevant to their status as “hospitals” and to the 


fact that, as hospitals, they come within the ambit of PHL 
§ 2807-d.
