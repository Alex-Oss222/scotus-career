STATEMENT OF THE CASE 


We have no problem with the statement of the case in 
petitioner's brief and as stated in the Reasons for Judgment of 
the District Court and the Opinion of the Fifth Circuit Court 


of Appeals. 


We do however deny that there is a conflict between the 
holding of the Ninth Circuit in Ablamis v. Roper, 937 Federal 
Reporter 2nd 1450 and the holding of the Fifth Circuit in this 
matter because the causes of action are different. In Ablames, 
the Trustee of profit sharing trusts brought a declaratory 
judgment against the Executor of Glee Ann Ablamis. In the 
Boggs case all distribution of retirement funds have been paid 
to the employee and his designated beneficiary. 


We have strong differences with the conclusion of petitioner 
in his Petition For Writ of Certiorari in that: 


|. The entire ERISA law is directed toward employees and 
administrators of the retirement trusts in order to protect the 
interest of the employee. Nowhere is the entire act does it 
attempt to regulate ownership of retirement funds as between 
members of a family nor does the ERISA provisions attempt 
to control the ownership of the funds after receipt by the 
employee or his beneficiary as set out in the laws of the State 
of residence and/or domicile of the employee or his 
beneficiary. 


2. In the appendix to the petitioner's petition is cited 29 USC 
132 - Civil Enforcement. This section sets out Persons 
empowered to bring a civil action. Nowhere in this section 
does the law mention an action by a beneficiary against a 
forced heir of the employee. 


ARGUMENT 


We believe that the District Court correctly stated the law 
in its foot note #1 on page 5 of the Reasons For Judgment 


quoted in petitioner's appendix to the Petition marked "A-1". 


The District Court's Reasons For Judgment on page 6 
states: 


"In enacting ERISA, Congress declared that its policy 
was the protection of employees vis a vis their employers --- 
The Act does not display any particular interest in preserving 
maximum benefits to any particular beneficiary”. 


And on page 10 of the District Court's Reasons for 
Judgment is: 


"Secondly, because Louisiana law provided Dorothy 
Boggs with an undivided interest in the retirement plans at the 
time the fund was established, which was well before ERISA 
was passed in 1974, allowing ERISA to preempt her estate 
created property right would violate the Fifth Amendment's 
prohibition against the government's “»*ing private property 
without just compensation" --. 

Further more we do not feel that we can speak or argue 
more forcefully than the holding of the Court of Appeals for 
the Fifth Circuit. On page A-21 of petitioner's petition the 
Court states: 


"This Court concludes that, under the facts of the case, 
the Louisiana community property law is not sufficiently 
‘related to' an employee benefit plan to necessitate ERISA 
preemption. Nothing is sought from the plan or its fiduciary. 
No duty will be imposed on the plan or the administrator. 
Benefits will continue to be paid to the beneficiary in the 
manner provided in the plan. A spouse's accounting 
obligation under community property law affects ‘employee 
benefit plans’ in too tenuous, remote, or peripheral a manner 
to warrant a finding that the law ‘relates to’ the plan. 21 Our 
decision relates not to the plan but to the disposition of the 


proceeds only after payment to the designated beneficiary. 
This is no greater effect than the state's garnishment laws. 
Mackey, 486 US 825 (1988).” 


The holding of the Fifth Circuit failed to follow the 
reasoning of the Ablamis case for reasons stated on Page A- 
24, A-22 and A-23. We add that the Ablames case was 
brought about by a trustee of two profit sharing trusts seeking 
a declaratory judgment of the Court as to whom to pay the 
proceeds of the plans - the employee's stated beneficiary or 
the heirs of the first wife. 


In our case the funds are being paid to the employee's 
designated beneficiary, so the administrator of our fund is 
following the holding of the Ablamis case. But here, we are 
seeking the following the law of Louisiana, not as it applies 
to the retirement or investment plan, but as the Louisiana laws 


of ownership of community property apply to the ‘unds ai.er 
they are received by the beneficiary. 


We had previously objected to the removal of our case in 
the State Court to the Federal Court by objecting to the 
jurisdiction. Maybe that was the wrong term. Our position 
was brought about mainly by the holding of the Court in 
Metropolitan Life Insurance Co, v. Taylor, 481 US 58. This 
was an action between an employer and an employee. The 
original suit was filed in the State Court by the employee and 
General Motors the employer and Metropolitan the 
administrator of the trust fund removed the matter to the 
Federal Court. In this case the court held that because this 
was a suit by a beneficiary to recover benefits from a covered 
plan, it falls directly under Section 502 (a) (i) (b) of ERISA 
which provides an exclusive federal cause of action for 
resolution of such disputes. As such the action was governed 


~~ rt ee 


by Section 502 (a) i (b) because of its civil enforcement 
provision. The civil enforcement provision of ERISA are 
covered in 29 USC 1132. (a) allows a civil action be brought 
by: 


(1) A participant or beneficiary - (A) for the relief provided 
for in subsection (c) or (B) to recover benefits due him under 
the terms of his plan, to enforce his rights under the terms of 
the plan, or to clarify his rights to future benefits under the 
terms of the plan. Subsection "C" deals with an 
administrator's refusal to supply sufficient information and the 
failure to provide annual reports in complete form. 


In Metropolitan the Court held its exclusive jurisdiction 
only because the civil enforcement provision of ERISA. We 
cannot find any need for civil enforcement of ERISA in our 
case. All payments under the plans have been paid to 
petitioner and are still being paid to her. She nor the sons of 
Isaac Boggs, Jr. are opposed to the method of payment by the 
administrator of the retirement pian. 


Judge King who was on the panel in the Fifth Circuit 
dissented in the original opinion. She later moved for an En 
Banc Hearing which was refused by the Court. From the 
refusal she wrote a 37 page dissent which has been filed by 
petitioner as a supplemental appendix. 


After reading Judge King's dissent over four times and 
reading most of the cases cited we do not feel that the dissent 
brings anything new to this matter. Her conclusion is that the 
ERISA preemption is all inclusive. It differs from the holding 
of the majority which stated: "In other words, ERISA was 
not meant to consume everything in its path". 


In two places she cites Meeks v. Tullis, 791 F. Supp. 154, 
157 (WD La. 1992). The facts of the Meeks case are quite 


similar to the facts of our case. It was heard by a Magistrate 
who stated that the attorney for Meeks made no effort to 
justify their position except to quote from the dissent in the 
Ablamis case. The author of this brief called the attorney to 
ask why he did not make greater effort to establish his 
position and why he did not appeal. He stated that this client 
did not believe that it was worth the effort and did not desire 
to assume the cost of an appeal. We do not believe that the 
Meeks case should be considered by the Court especially in 
view of the Fifth Circuit's decision here.
