CONCLUSION 


The City of Edmond has adopted, trademarked, sponsored, 
and laid exclusive claim to an emblem which inculcates a 
symbol that is unmistakable in its identification with the 
Christian religion. The City then uses that emblem in a manner 
that promotes a unique identification between the City and that 
emblem. It requires little analysis and only minimal 
understanding of the nature of advertising to detect thinly 
veiled advancement of Christianity by the distinctive emblem. 

That the Edmond Seal incorporates other images does not 
save it under Lynch. The primary effect (and not a 51% 
“effect” of balancing religious symbols with secular symbols) is 
that of promotion of a religious symbol. In short, although 
Defendants propose that they can mix “water” and “wine” and 
get “water,” they cannot. The result in the instant case is not 
“water” but “diluted wine” that is still unmistakably wine. In 
either case, such symbolism, as a form of “Christian-lite,” 
should be equally unpalatable to Christians and nonchristians 
alike. 

Accordingly, Respondents respectfully request that this 
Court deny Edmond’s Petition to review the decision of the 
Tenth Circuit, a decision in accord with the Circuit’s own 
precedent, not in conflict with precedent from other circuits and 


consistent with the decisions of this Court. In the event that 
this Court should accept certiorari, Respondents would urge the 
affirmance of the Tenth Circuit's decision upon the same 
reasons and circumstances.

Edmond seal contest slated 


A contest, backed by the local governing board, will open 
here Thursday to design and select an official seal for the City 
of Edmond. 

And there are substantial awards in the offing for the three 
best seal designers. Designer of the winning seal contest entry 
will receive a $50 U.S. savings bond, and $25 bonds will go to

council meeting. “We need an official seal to decorate our city 
hall stationery, and to be flown as a flag in front of our city 
hall,” he explained. Council promptly endorsed the suggestion 
and appropriated the money to furnish the savings bond awards. 

The contest period is July 15 through August 15. Entries 
should be mailed or brought to the Edmond Publishing Co. 
office at 13 S. Broadway. The entry designs should be done 
with black India ink on the whitest paper available. The entries 
should not exceed 8 1/2 x 11 inches, maximum size. 

The designer may, if he wishes, attach a separate sheet 
explaining the symbolism or meaning of the various drawings 
contained in his design.

Contest closed, judges picked 


Hopeful seal designs for an official city insignia were 
accepted by the council Monday night with the closing of the 
recent 30-day contest.

named five judges to pick the three winners.

instructor Mrs. Carl (Kathryn) Alcorn; long-time city residents 
Mrs. Dixie Clegern and Mrs. E. E. Griffin; and artist Mrs. 
Wayne (Gaye) Nichols. 

The contest was sponsored by the Edmond newspapers, and 
prizes are being furnished by the city. First prize is a $50 U.S. 
savings bond, and second and third prizes are $25 bonds. 

The contest was promoted to select an official seal for the 
City of Edmond to use on city stationery and as the design for 
a city flag. 

Most of the entries stressed Edmond’s cultural and 
commercial factions, its “churchiness,” and the friendliness of 
its residents. 

Almost all the entries included in their designs a sketch of 
Old North tower. 

Entries were deposited with City Clerk Woody Bryan to be 
turned over to the judging committee. 

Awards will be announced and presented at the earliest 
possible date, said Mayor Charlie Johnson.
