## Controlling holdings

### I. Jurisdiction does not itself establish a damages action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350 supplies district-court jurisdiction over an alien's civil tort action alleging a violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty; these substantial allegations of official torture fall within that jurisdiction. Jurisdiction alone neither supplies a domestic damages action nor establishes liability, sovereign-immunity waiver or authority for every asserted international claim.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute identifies the forum and category of controversy. Whether domestic law provides the asserted action is a separate question. Suter requires attention to the source of an enforceable right and action, and Meyer distinguishes federal adjudicative authority and consent from substantive liability. Those distinctions do not make a substantial source-of-action dispute a reason to deny jurisdiction over this pleaded category. The plaintiffs are aliens alleging torts violating the established prohibition of official torture. Their allegations establish the statutory kind of controversy; findings and governing law must separately support relief. No sovereign is made a defendant and no immunity issue is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Suter's source/action distinction and Meyer's separation of jurisdiction, consent and substantive liability are preserved; neither previously decided §1350's present application.

### II. A confined received action for personally committed official torture

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350's reception of judicial adjudication of law-of-nations torts, read in its common-law setting, supports a confined domestic civil action against an individual for deliberate participation in or personal supervision of official torture. The actionable norm forbids severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation; international law supplies that specific obligatory prohibition, while the received domestic action supplies authority to enforce it.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Universal condemnation alone is not domestic remedial authority, and the jurisdictional label alone cannot resolve the action question. Congress expressly provided adjudication of law-of-nations torts against the common-law background in which such wrongs received judicial remedies. That particular statutory function supports this determinate, established personal-tort action. Filartiga supplies persuasive evidence of judicial recognition of the official-torture prohibition and its civil enforcement. Central Bank's confined received-action reasoning and Musick, Peeler's treatment of incidents of an established action offer analogies; neither itself creates an action under §1350. Suter's administrative-plan provisions supply no contrary rule for this received tort, and O'Melveny rejected a new receiver preference rather than this source-specific construction. The judgment decides categorical authority on the proved torture ground; it adds no TVPA claim, damages allocation or amount assessment.

## Holdings

### I. Jurisdiction does not itself establish a damages action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350 supplies district-court jurisdiction over an alien's civil tort action alleging a violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty; these substantial allegations of official torture fall within that jurisdiction. Jurisdiction alone neither supplies a domestic damages action nor establishes liability, sovereign-immunity waiver or authority for every asserted international claim.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute identifies the forum and category of controversy. Whether domestic law provides the asserted action is a separate question. Suter requires attention to the source of an enforceable right and action, and Meyer distinguishes federal adjudicative authority and consent from substantive liability. Those distinctions do not make a substantial source-of-action dispute a reason to deny jurisdiction over this pleaded category. The plaintiffs are aliens alleging torts violating the established prohibition of official torture. Their allegations establish the statutory kind of controversy; findings and governing law must separately support relief. No sovereign is made a defendant and no immunity issue is decided.

**Precedent treatment:** Suter's source/action distinction and Meyer's separation of jurisdiction, consent and substantive liability are preserved; neither previously decided §1350's present application.

### II. A confined received action for personally committed official torture

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1350's reception of judicial adjudication of law-of-nations torts, read in its common-law setting, supports a confined domestic civil action against an individual for deliberate participation in or personal supervision of official torture. The actionable norm forbids severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation; international law supplies that specific obligatory prohibition, while the received domestic action supplies authority to enforce it.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Universal condemnation alone is not domestic remedial authority, and the jurisdictional label alone cannot resolve the action question. Congress expressly provided adjudication of law-of-nations torts against the common-law background in which such wrongs received judicial remedies. That particular statutory function supports this determinate, established personal-tort action. Filartiga supplies persuasive evidence of judicial recognition of the official-torture prohibition and its civil enforcement. Central Bank's confined received-action reasoning and Musick, Peeler's treatment of incidents of an established action offer analogies; neither itself creates an action under §1350. Suter's administrative-plan provisions supply no contrary rule for this received tort, and O'Melveny rejected a new receiver preference rather than this source-specific construction. The contrary argument correctly separates jurisdiction from liability but gives insufficient effect to the statute's particular reception of tort adjudication. The supported trial findings establish Negewo's direct participation and personal supervision concerning Abebe-Jira and Taye, and personal supervision of some torture concerning Demissie. Official title alone is insufficient. The Court sustains the judgment on torture, without adopting a general Lincoln Mills or legislative-history ground, and announces no action for every customary norm, private nonstate violence, corporate liability, absent-superior liability or every cruel, inhuman or degrading act.

**Precedent treatment:** Suter and O'Melveny are distinguished at their source-specific limits; Central Bank and Musick, Peeler supply confined received-action analogies, not existing ATS holdings. Filartiga is persuasive lower-court authority for this established personal tort.

### III. Compensation for proved injury within that action

**Holding and operative rule:** The recognized action permits compensatory damages for proved injury caused by the individual's qualifying official torture. The established trial findings support categorical compensatory authority for each plaintiff, without substituting allegations or official position for personal responsibility and causation.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Traditional tort reparation compensates an injured person for loss caused by the defendant's wrongful conduct. Franklin preserves appropriate relief within a cognizable action, while requiring the action to be established first. Part II supplies that separate source here. The bench-trial findings establish the personal torture ground; this is not a damages award based solely on the complaint. The source objection therefore does not defeat compensation once the confined action is recognized. Each plaintiff's $200,000 compensatory award remains in place against the granted categorical objection. The Court neither enlarges the judgment nor adjudicates an independent challenge to amount or trial allocation.

**Precedent treatment:** Franklin's action/remedy distinction is applied without turning its Title IX holding into an independent ATS cause or an amount-of-damages ruling.

### IV. Punitive authority requires an independently justified aggravated-tort remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Within this established action for intentionally inflicted official torture, traditional common-law remedies permit punitive damages for malicious, willful or equivalently reckless aggravated wrongdoing; punitive relief is permitted, not required in every case. The punitive culpability standard does not dilute the underlying action's intentional-official-torture predicate, and the proved deliberate personal torture supports punitive availability here while amount and lawful assessment remain distinct questions.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stevens's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices directly adopt its complete punitive-culpability formulation. Stone-Zsela joins the authority for punitive relief and its application to the proved deliberate personal torture, but does not join the formulation concerning equivalently reckless aggravation; seven directly support the deliberate-torture ground sustaining this judgment. Scalia and Thomas reject relief on this asserted action because they reject its source.

**Controlling explanation:** Punishment cannot be inferred from compensation alone. Arms distinguishes compensation from the traditional punitive response to aggravated personal wrongdoing. Filartiga's remedial decision supplies a persuasive application to official torture. The domestic action's common-law remedial character and that independently supported aggravated-tort remedy authorize punishment here; Franklin expressly left punitive authority open and does not answer this question. The concern that punitive relief has penal and international consequences warrants attention to the source, culpability and remedy, but does not eliminate an ordinary remedy for this established intentional personal tort. No sovereign or population is punished and no criminal conviction is entered. The Court does not import Filartiga's default, its assessment of a foreign prosecution, its choice-of-law findings or its dollar amounts. Each plaintiff's $300,000 punitive award survives the categorical authority objection. TXO and BMW retain their actual constraints and disagreements: this grant supplies no independent amount challenge, wealth finding, assessment formula, required ratio or certification of every possible calculation. The Court authorizes no universal punitive remedy for international wrongs.

**Precedent treatment:** Arms supplies the traditional distinction between reparation and punishment for aggravated wrongdoing; Franklin's punitive reservation remains intact and is not treated as authorization. TXO and BMW remain controlling at their existing procedural and substantive scopes; no new excessiveness test is announced.

### V. The foreign setting does not make this individual tort a political question

**Holding and operative rule:** Adjudication of these individual official-torture claims is not categorically barred as a political question because it applies a determinate prohibition to personal conduct without deciding the legitimacy of Ethiopia's government, directing United States diplomacy or resolving a constitutionally committed war-powers dispute. A concrete immunity defense or contrary political-branch determination would require its own consideration; none is supplied or decided here.

**Authority:** Part V of Stevens's opinion, joined by all other participating Justices: nine direct joins. Scalia and Thomas's agreement does not supply an action or their support for the damages judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Baker requires a concrete constitutional commitment or obstacle to judicial decision, not merely a foreign setting. Montana and Nixon preserve that issue-specific inquiry. Courts can decide personal conduct under an established torture prohibition and apply ordinary remedial law without choosing a foreign government or supervising diplomacy. Sanchez-Espinoza concerned United States foreign-policy operations and domestic sovereign immunity; its express distinction of suits against foreign officials defeats using it as a categorical prohibition here. The Court does not decide a new immunity question or an unpresented defense. The absence of a political-question bar does not answer the independent source-of-action and remedy inquiries.

**Precedent treatment:** Baker's issue-specific inquiry, as retained in Montana and Nixon, is applied. Sanchez-Espinoza is distinguished at its domestic-immunity and United States foreign-policy setting; its result is not extended to this defendant.

## Precedent Treatment

- Suter v. Artist M.: its separation of enforceable right, action and relief remains; its administrative-plan ruling does not foreclose this source-specific received action.
- FDIC v. Meyer: jurisdiction and consent remain distinct from a substantive action; no sovereign-immunity rule is enlarged.
- O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: the rejection of a new receiver preference is distinguished from §1350's particular common-law function.
- Central Bank of Denver v. First Interstate Bank and Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance: their confined received-action reasoning supports analogy, without importing securities liability or independently establishing an ATS action.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools: appropriate remedies presuppose an action; compensation is supported at that scope, and punitive authority is independently decided here.
- Milwaukee & St. Paul Railway Co. v. Arms: its traditional distinction between compensation and punitive relief for aggravated wrongdoing informs the punitive rule.
- TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources and BMW of North America v. Gore: their actual procedural and substantive punitive limits remain unchanged; no new amount-review rule is adopted.
- Baker v. Carr, United States Department of Commerce v. Montana and Nixon v. United States: their concrete political-question inquiry is applied without a foreign-affairs category bar.
- Filartiga's Second Circuit and damages decisions supply persuasive support for this established official-torture action and its remedies. Sanchez-Espinoza's domestic-immunity and United States foreign-policy setting is distinguished, consistently with its express distinction of foreign-official suits. These lower-court decisions are not treated as prior Supreme Court holdings.

## Law After Decision

Section 1350's forum grant remains distinct from an actionable norm, a domestic action and the permitted relief. The Court recognizes a confined received action for proved personal official torture, with compensatory relief and independently justified punitive authority. It does not make every customary-international-law norm privately enforceable, impose liability from official title, adjudicate a sovereign's immunity or authorize general supervision of foreign policy. The underlying tort still requires intentional infliction under official authority; the punitive aggravation formulation does not create a cause for merely reckless conduct.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurs in Parts I and V and dissents from the damages judgment. They accept the statutory forum and reject a categorical political-question bar, but find no sufficient congressional authority for the asserted domestic damages action. The prohibition of torture does not itself authorize a federal cause of action. A forum for independently actionable law-of-nations torts does not necessarily authorize judicial recognition of every later norm; the common-law setting and lower-court practice are the strongest affirmative arguments, but do not establish the needed source for these Justices.

Suter requires an actual source of private liability; O'Melveny rejects the manufacture of a federal rule from general federal interests; Meyer keeps jurisdiction and substantive action distinct. Franklin presupposed a cognizable action, and their concurrence there relied on a narrower enacted-remedies ground. Central Bank's binding received-securities holding remains confined to its established setting, and Musick, Peeler concerns incidents of already recognized liability. Neither supplies the missing ATS premise. Sanchez-Espinoza's domestic-immunity result is not a categorical bar to foreign-official suits. Scalia and Thomas would reverse the damages judgment and remand on the absence-of-action ground, rejecting both compensation and punishment through that ground. They announce no categorical objection to punitive damages in an otherwise authorized intentional-tort action, do not approve torture, and do not reach an unpleaded TVPA claim, limitations or appointed-counsel question.

## Procedure After Action

The Eleventh Circuit judgment remains in force. No remand, new trial, reallocation or recalculation is directed. The ungranted limitations and appointed-counsel questions are not reopened; ordinary jurisdictional, personal-responsibility and amount-of-damages limits remain.
