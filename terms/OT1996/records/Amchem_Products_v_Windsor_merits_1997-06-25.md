## Law of the decision

### I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions

**Holding and operative rule:** A nonmerits Rule 23 defect that prevents this proposed settlement class from binding absent persons is logically antecedent to the Article III questions arising from those persons' inclusion in that class. The Court may deny certification on that ground without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate any person's substantive claim; an individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's Court opinion is joined by all seven other participating Justices: eight direct votes. O'Connor does not participate.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. forbids assumed constitutional power to decide disputed statutory merits. The present order of decision does not exercise that power. Denying the representative device establishes that this proposed class cannot bind its absent members, without determining anyone's damages entitlement, future injury or individual right to sue. The standing objections specific to the proposed class therefore need not be answered to deny certification. This limited sequencing rule does not authorize a court to assume jurisdiction over an individual action or decide its merits first. The Court likewise leaves the broader constitutional notice objection unanswered because Rule 23 independently defeats this class.

### II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** A settlement-only class must satisfy the applicable requirements of Rule 23(a) and (b), including typicality, adequate representation and predominance under Rule 23(b)(3). Because no trial is proposed, a court need not examine difficulties managing a trial; settlement's actual operation and bargain may inform the remaining inquiries, but their requirements cannot be relaxed or replaced by overall fairness or the desirability of settlement.

**Authority:** Part II has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. The disagreement in the separate writing concerns the appellate application and remedy, not permission to dispense with certification requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Falcon requires rigorous attention to the actual prerequisites for representative adjudication. A settlement eliminates the need to manage a trial but still binds persons absent from its negotiation. The court must therefore examine who is represented and which claims and interests the bargain resolves. Rule 23(e)'s fairness review does not substitute for Rule 23(a) and (b). The settling parties' argument that the bargain creates a common interest in compensation does not make distinct substantive rights identical. Matsushita and Richards distinguish genuine representative authority from notice, opportunity to object or a shared objective. Castano requires the actual law-and-proof showing, without a categorical ban based on class size, novelty or settlement pressure. Settlement may bear on the real class and its interests; the Court does not treat it as relevant only to a mechanical manageability inquiry. Nor does the rule disfavor settlement classes that independently meet the prerequisites.

### III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** This proposed undivided class fails Rule 23(a)(4) because the same representatives and counsel do not adequately protect the materially conflicting bargaining interests of currently injured and exposure-only claimants. Notice, the opportunity to opt out and the settlement's overall economic attractions cannot cure that defect.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: six direct votes with Thomas. Stevens and Breyer do not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Hansberry requires genuinely adequate representation before absent persons may be bound. Matsushita preserves that requirement independently of notice and opt-out protections, and Richards rejects shared interests as a substitute for lawful representation. Here currently injured claimants seek ample immediate payments. Those who may develop disease later need protection against inflation, diagnostic uncertainty, annual caps and the loss of future claims. The agreement's fixed categories and payments, caps and absent inflation adjustment make these divergent bargaining incentives concrete. One undivided representative group cannot exchange the future protections for present benefits without independent protection of the interests surrendered. Shutts's notice and exclusion safeguards serve a different function and do not align those incentives. The availability of compensation therefore cannot answer the defect. The Court does not prescribe a universal subclass architecture, prohibit settlement of future claims, decide constitutional notice sufficiency or resolve Matsushita's reserved collateral-review question. A materially different proposal may use separately represented subclasses, narrower groups or another adequate structure, but no redesign is approved here.

### IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance

**Holding and operative rule:** The proposed class also fails Rule 23(b)(3) because its common asbestos-exposure and settlement objectives do not predominate over the materially different state laws, exposure histories, causation, injuries, defenses and individual claims. Removing trial-management concerns does not remove those substantive differences.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same six-Justice coalition as Part III and independently supports affirmance. Stevens and Breyer withhold this application as well.

**Controlling explanation:** Castano requires a demonstrated grouping of the governing law and proof rather than an abstract common description. Common exposure does not resolve product identification, latency, injury, limitations, defenses or damages across the many covered claims and States. The agreement's common payment mechanism is a compromise of those rights, not proof that common questions predominate over them. Falcon's insistence on the actual certification prerequisites remains relevant even when no trial is contemplated. The contrary contention that settlement removes the need to try individual questions confuses trial management with the substantive predominance requirement. Both remain distinct under Rule 23(b)(3). This application establishes no categorical prohibition of nationwide, mass-tort, issue or settlement classes, and it decides neither the validity of any particular state-law claim nor the fairness of a future bargain. Metro-North's FELA limits do not resolve the state-law claims collected here or make all exposure-only claimants legally uninjured.

## Public Projection

## Event

Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor, No. 96-270. Argued February 18, 1997; decided June 25, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit, whose May 10, 1996 judgment vacated certification, settlement approval and the related injunction. The question is whether settlement-only certification relaxes Rule 23's predominance and adequate-representation requirements and whether this asbestos class satisfies them.

The complaint and proposed settlement were filed together on January 15, 1993, without an intention to litigate. Nine named plaintiffs sought to represent currently injured persons, exposure-only claimants and family members with differing claims under many States' laws, without subclasses. The agreement prescribed disease categories and payment ranges, annual caps and no inflation adjustment, while restricting some state-law claims. The settling parties seek reinstatement; the objectors defend decertification. Class-related standing and notice objections are presented but do not require decision once certification fails.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. Justice O'Connor takes no part in the consideration or decision. No reason for nonparticipation is stated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Third Circuit's judgment. The proposed class cannot be certified and cannot bind absent claimants through this settlement.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer.
**Not participating:** O'Connor.

Certification, settlement approval and the classwide injunction remain vacated. Stevens and Breyer would vacate the Third Circuit's judgment and remand for settlement-sensitive review of the district court's findings; they would not reinstate or approve the class or settlement. No individual damages claim or future agreement is adjudicated.
