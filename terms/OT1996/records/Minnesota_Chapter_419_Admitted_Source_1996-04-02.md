## Scope and transition

Sections 4–6 conditionally permit simultaneous candidacy for one major party and one or more minor parties for the same partisan office, with written consent of the state chairs of the parties sought. Nomination by petition also requires the candidate's written consent. A candidate who loses the major-party primary forfeits the minor-party nominations; a party candidate may not also file as an independent in the same election. The affidavit rules change accordingly. Sections 7–8 require a single candidate listing with the applicable party names; sections 2–3 prescribe party-status rules, including limitations on counting cross-nominated candidates' votes toward party status. Section 9 repeals the prior section 204D.10, subdivision 2. These distinct provisions are preserved in the source and are not collapsed into a single exception.

Section 10 makes the act effective for the 1996 state primary and thereafter. It suspends these amendments while the Eighth Circuit's McKenna decision is stayed or its mandate is recalled. If that decision is reversed, the amendments expire and prior law revives. This admission does not establish whether a stay or recall occurred in this timeline. It also does not decide mootness, the old prohibition's constitutionality, a particular interim filing's validity, or any independent challenge to the new consent, ballot or party-status provisions.
