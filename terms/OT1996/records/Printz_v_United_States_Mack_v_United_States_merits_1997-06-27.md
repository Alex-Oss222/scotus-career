## Event, route and chronology

Printz v. United States, No. 95-1478; Mack v. United States, No. 95-1503. Consolidated cases. Argued December 3, 1996; decided June 27, 1997. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. Its September 8, 1995 judgment, 66 F.3d 1025, sustained the Brady Act's interim duties imposed on chief law-enforcement officers and dismissed the sheriffs' severability appeals as moot. The questions are whether Congress may impose the mandatory interim background inquiry and related administrative duties, and what statutory consequences follow if a command is invalid.

The District Courts had invalidated the mandatory inquiry while leaving the conditional destruction and written-reasons provisions standing and severing rather than invalidating the entire Act. Both sides appealed. The sheriffs seek relief from compulsory officer duties; the United States seeks affirmance. Neither action is a class action. The officers assert an absence of budget provision for the federal work, exposure to liability for unauthorized expenditures, and state-law prohibitions on undertaking the responsibilities. Those assertions are not findings of cost, liability or state law. Montana and Arizona did not require implementation, and the officers chose not to implement the program; that general posture establishes neither receipt nor nonreceipt of a particular statement, records, determination or request.

## Controlling holdings

##### The officers may challenge the compulsory duties imposed on their offices

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal command requiring these plaintiff officers to perform identified work through their offices presents a concrete injury redressable by relief against that command. Their official status does not bar review, but it supplies no standing to obtain an advisory decision about independent obligations imposed only on absent dealers or purchasers.

**Authority:** The jurisdictional section of Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Raines distinguishes concrete injury from unauthorized legislators' abstract institutional dilution after fully effective votes. These officers instead challenge duties assigned directly to their offices. The Government's disavowal of criminal prosecution does not remove the asserted compulsory civil work. The Court reaches that controversy without inventing an actual prosecution or deciding the independent obligations of persons who are not parties. The grant does not reopen the separate lower-court vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment dispositions.

##### Part I — Appropriate temporary executive records assistance is not categorically forbidden

**Holding and operative rule:** In implementing an otherwise valid direct regulation of private transactions, Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary records-based assistance within a state executive officer's proper function under an affirmative statutory direction, while federal law supplies the substantive transaction rule and federal authorities retain responsibility for coercive enforcement. That source-and-function rule defeats the asserted categorical incapacity to require the confined assistance presented here, but does not decide whether the actual compulsory arrangement is permissible in light of the officers' unresolved fiscal and unauthorized-expenditure objections.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt this limited rule; their agreement does not extend to unconditional validity of the officer obligations, a right to fair compensation, or the ultimate disposition. The four Justices who reject Part I contribute no vote to it through their agreement with the remand.

**Controlling explanation:** New York forbids compelling a State to legislate for private actors or assume their property and liabilities. It did not decide every directive to an executive officer. Here Congress directly regulates commercial handgun transfers, supplies the transaction rule and expressly directs interim assistance. The executive task concerns receiving prescribed information, consulting available records and making the eligibility assessment those records support; it leaves state policymaking and general coercive enforcement with their respective governments. Garcia and Baker preserve federal regulation affecting state activity, and FERC and Testa show that federal and state responsibilities need not always be performed through wholly separate institutions. Their distinct settings do not themselves decide this case.

The structural objection correctly identifies real work and use of state resources. Part I neither treats that work as free nor makes convenience, low cost or temporary duration a constitutional power. It instead distinguishes the source, function and responsibility assigned. Voting Rights Coalition rests on separate election powers and supplies no general commerce exception. Lopez limits commerce power and forbids inserting a missing statutory element; this transaction regime has its own commercial basis, and the Necessary and Proper Clause implements that established power.

The reasonable-effort term, available-record language, interim context and absence of a command to arrest or prevent a transfer support the confined question decided. The word including does not mean only; the Court makes no categorical textual excision of every conceivable nonrecord act and supplies no independent investigative power. It decides no compulsory field-investigation, permanent state enforcement-organization or general policy-administration command. Nor does the officer's oath independently create a task absent enacted direction. The remaining fiscal objection concerns whether this actual arrangement may finally be sustained, and is genuinely left open.

**Statutory scope and conditions:** Section 922(s)(2) directs reasonable effort within five business days after the specified dealer notice, including research in available state and local systems and the designated national system. It does not require an arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Section (6)(B) concerns a transmitted purchaser statement: unless the officer determines the transaction unlawful, the statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the purchaser made the statement; disclosure is limited to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection, and use is limited to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Section (6)(C) separately requires written reasons only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. No transaction-specific trigger is found satisfied or unsatisfied here.

**Limits:** The holding preserves New York's prohibition, the distinct sources for state-court and election duties, valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation and lawful spending arrangements. It establishes no general cheap-command exception, permanent compulsory records power, state-law veto of valid federal law or constitutional payment entitlement. No categorical holding validates or invalidates every ancillary information duty or independent dealer obligation.

##### Part II — The limited mandate binds without a majority fiscal rationale

**Operative disposition:** In both dockets, vacate the appellate judgments only insofar as they unconditionally sustain the challenged officer obligations, and remand for development and consideration of the existing budget and unauthorized-expenditure objections under Part I. No substantive entitlement to compensation, compensation cause of action, measure of fair value, state-law liability or taking is decided.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supply five votes for that judgment and its confined mandate. Souter's affirmative fair-value rationale has no other join. The other four support the remand only under the independently controlling Part I rule, while maintaining that compulsory administration should instead be invalidated. There is no majority fiscal rule and no Marks synthesis.

**Scope of the return:** The courts must genuinely consider the asserted objections and their legal consequences; ultimate constitutional validity has not already been decided against the officers. Development remains confined to the existing controversy and whatever ordinary proceedings are necessary. The mandate neither requires a new money claim or automatic hearing nor finds waiver, expenditure, liability, receipt of a form, retained information, an ineligibility determination or a request for reasons. It does not automatically revive every severability issue, decide absent parties' rights or declare that particular lower interim relief has revived or expired. The criminal-vagueness, unraised civil-vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment matters outside the grant remain undisturbed.

## Precedent treatment and resulting law

- New York v. United States: retains the prohibition on compulsory state legislation or ownership/liability and its lawful-inducement and generally applicable regulation boundaries. Part I answers the distinct limited executive-assistance question without overruling that holding. Its independently operative remainder rule remains available when a proper severability question must be decided; it does not resolve absent parties' duties here.
- United States v. Lopez: retains its commercial-connection requirement and prohibition on adding a missing element. The direct handgun-transaction setting is distinguished from the school-possession/productivity chain.
- United States v. Harris and United States v. Hale: retain their economic-class and integral transfer/stock grounds. Valid commercial regulation does not itself decide the propriety of every means used to implement it.
- Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson: retains separate congressional-election and presidential-registration sources. Its mandatory duties are not converted into a general commerce-power exception based on administrative cost or convenience.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority and South Carolina v. Baker: preserve generally applicable federal regulation affecting state activity; no immunity for every traditional governmental function is revived.
- FERC v. Mississippi: retains its adjudicative and cooperative setting in a federally preemptible field; it supplies context rather than automatic approval of every executive command.
- Testa v. Katt: preserves the distinct obligation of state adjudication under the Supremacy Clause; function matters when an official performs work traditionally assigned to judges.
- Raines v. Byrd: distinguishes unauthorized institutional dilution from duties imposed directly on the officers; no universal exclusion of official injury or standing for absent parties follows.
- Alaska Airlines v. Brock: retains the independently operative remainder and congressional-design inquiry. The Court does not decide whether independent dealer transmission or waiting duties continue in a hypothetical scheme with an invalid officer command.

Effective June 27, 1997, the categorical structural objection does not alone defeat the confined temporary executive records assistance identified in Part I. The actual compulsory arrangement remains subject to genuine consideration of the existing fiscal and unauthorized-expenditure objections. No five-Justice holding finally sustains or invalidates those duties or establishes a payment right. The statutory triggers remain intact, and general nonparticipation supplies no transaction-specific finding. Independent dealer, purchaser and permanent federal-system questions are not adjudicated. The judgment creates no universal compensation test, blanket information-duty immunity or new substantive severability rule.

## Published noncontrolling continuity

All following positions are published in the consolidated Printz/Mack decision of June 27, 1997. Their additional grounds are noncontrolling except for the exact jurisdiction and Part I joins separately identified above.

**Souter, Part II of the opinion announcing the judgment.** His reason for remand is that appropriate executive assistance cannot, in his view, mean compulsory administrative support without an obligation to pay fair value. Federalist Nos. 27 and 44 describe an auxiliary executive role and the force of the constitutional oath; Nos. 36 and 45 reinforce the execution examples. They support properly directed assistance under an enacted command within the officer's branch function, not an automatic duty created by an oath alone. New York continues to protect legislative choice. Early administrative examples alone would not establish his conclusion.

The officers' assertions that their budgets make no provision for this work and that they face unauthorized-expenditure liability therefore require development and consideration. He regards that issue as a condition on sustaining the existing compulsory arrangement, not as a reason to invent a damages action. He finds no compensation amount, taking, liability, pleaded money cause or payment defendant established and orders no appropriation. The Court leaves the fiscal question open, permitting his stronger view to be considered without adopting it. No other Justice joins that affirmative fair-value rationale.

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment and dissenting from Part I; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the structural and conditional-judgment grounds.** New York's accountability principle cannot be avoided by addressing state executive officers instead of legislatures. The Brady inquiry and its implicit required acceptance of forms allocate federal implementation to unwilling state offices. Reasonable effort concerns the amount of compelled work, not the State's choice whether to undertake it. A valid commercial object and an assumed promise of reimbursement do not cure that structural defect. Garcia and Baker regulate state activity; Testa and FERC retain distinct adjudicative or cooperative settings; Voting Rights Coalition rests on constitutional election sources absent here.

They would reverse as to mandatory checking and implicit acceptance. They would not declare the destruction and requested-reasons clauses unconstitutional, adjudicate genuinely voluntary or separately triggered applications, or give officers a general license to retain or misuse information. General nonparticipation is not proof that a particular form or request never existed. They would reserve absent dealers' transmission and waiting questions, rather than declare the entire remainder severed and valid.

Part I nevertheless supplies the law governing these cases. Under that rule the actual fiscal objections remain unresolved, and leaving unconditional appellate approval intact would foreclose a real part of the controversy. They therefore accept this confined vacatur and remand while maintaining their preferred structural reversal. They join no compensation entitlement, fiscal cure, source-and-function rationale, state-law veto or search for hypothetical new claims. Their agreement with the mandate does not erase their substantive disagreement.

O'Connor adds, without the others' joins to this additional qualification, that voluntary participation and federally funded contractual cooperation remain available, and that purely ministerial reporting requirements are not resolved by the compelled investigation at issue. That reservation creates no exemption for every task described as ministerial.

**Stevens, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer join.** They would affirm the confined interim duties and their conditional safeguards. Congress directly governs commercial handgun transfers, retains the substantive policy and coercive consequences, and requires temporary, resource-sensitive information assistance. The scheme requires no state legislation, assumption of private property and liability, independent enforcement regime, arrest or prosecution. New York's contrary setting remains binding; supremacy and oath obligations, Garcia, Baker and the cooperation recognized in FERC and Testa support appropriate assistance within an established federal power. Election duties retain their own sources.

The budget and state-law assertions deserve an answer, but do not establish a constitutional payment condition or justify returning this constitutional judgment. The duty's actual functions and the responsibilities left to the State supply their answer; they do not presume free implementation or nonexistent exposure. Reasonable effort can account for resources without becoming optional whenever a budget lacks a line item. Their affirmance decides no state-law liability, taking or every possible fiscal claim. It requires no severance and provides no general permission for permanent state conscription.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment.** He would affirm both judgments and deny the requested injunction against the confined duties. Congress may require temporary, narrowly bounded assistance using lawfully available government records as an incident of otherwise valid private-transaction regulation, with federal substantive rules and federal responsibility for coercive enforcement. The mandatory eligibility assessment is real; calling supporting tasks ministerial does not eliminate it or its cost.

He reads reasonable effort in the whole interim scheme, its available-record tasks and the absence of a command to stop transfers or arrest purchasers. Including does not itself mean only. Ordinary checking and interpretation remain required; the construction adds no commerce element or administrative power and does not excuse all inquiry. He would authorize no compulsory independent field investigation, permanent state enforcement organization or transfer of general federal regulatory responsibility. The conditional explanations, destruction and information protections retain their separate statutory predicates; adverse dealer notification remains discretionary.

His position narrows his earlier categorical objection to compelled executive administration. The reason for that refinement is the difference between incidental records work and independent regulatory or coercive responsibility, not convenience or a claim that state work is costless. New York's compulsory-legislation prohibition, Lopez's source and no-rewriting limits, and the distinct election, state-court, preemption and voluntary-cooperation rules remain. His position creates no permanent duty, retroactive officer liability or reopening of a completed judgment. The asserted expenditures do not change his conclusion on these limited duties, and no severance or further fiscal proceeding is required. He leaves materially different commands and unpresented applications for another case.

**Breyer, additional opinion concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment.** Dividing execution between federal and state officials can preserve state autonomy and avoid expansion of a centralized federal bureaucracy. Federalism alone does not establish that separate federal implementation always protects liberty better. Comparative federal arrangements can illustrate that possibility, but are neither American constitutional authority nor proof that a foreign system can be transplanted here. His sufficient domestic grounds remain valid commerce power, necessary implementation, reasonable effort and the State's retained responsibilities. This supplements his full join in Stevens's opinion; it creates no general cost-benefit override, cheap-command exception or compensation cure.

## Event

Printz v. United States, No. 95-1478; Mack v. United States, No. 95-1503. Consolidated cases. Argued December 3, 1996; decided June 27, 1997. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. ## Holdings

##### The officers may challenge the compulsory duties imposed on their offices

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal command requiring these plaintiff officers to perform identified work through their offices presents a concrete injury redressable by relief against that command. Their official status does not bar review, but it supplies no standing to obtain an advisory decision about independent obligations imposed only on absent dealers or purchasers.

**Authority:** The jurisdictional section of Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Raines distinguishes concrete injury from unauthorized legislators' abstract institutional dilution after fully effective votes. These officers instead challenge duties assigned directly to their offices. The Government's disavowal of criminal prosecution does not remove the asserted compulsory civil work. The Court reaches that controversy without inventing an actual prosecution or deciding the independent obligations of persons who are not parties. The grant does not reopen the separate lower-court vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment dispositions.

##### Part I — Appropriate temporary executive records assistance is not categorically forbidden

**Holding and operative rule:** In implementing an otherwise valid direct regulation of private transactions, Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary records-based assistance within a state executive officer's proper function under an affirmative statutory direction, while federal law supplies the substantive transaction rule and federal authorities retain responsibility for coercive enforcement. That source-and-function rule defeats the asserted categorical incapacity to require the confined assistance presented here, but does not decide whether the actual compulsory arrangement is permissible in light of the officers' unresolved fiscal and unauthorized-expenditure objections.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt this limited rule; their agreement does not extend to unconditional validity of the officer obligations, a right to fair compensation, or the ultimate disposition. The four Justices who reject Part I contribute no vote to it through their agreement with the remand.

**Controlling explanation:** New York forbids compelling a State to legislate for private actors or assume their property and liabilities. It did not decide every directive to an executive officer. Here Congress directly regulates commercial handgun transfers, supplies the transaction rule and expressly directs interim assistance. The executive task concerns receiving prescribed information, consulting available records and making the eligibility assessment those records support; it leaves state policymaking and general coercive enforcement with their respective governments. Garcia and Baker preserve federal regulation affecting state activity, and FERC and Testa show that federal and state responsibilities need not always be performed through wholly separate institutions. Their distinct settings do not themselves decide this case.

The structural objection correctly identifies real work and use of state resources. Part I neither treats that work as free nor makes convenience, low cost or temporary duration a constitutional power. It instead distinguishes the source, function and responsibility assigned. Voting Rights Coalition rests on separate election powers and supplies no general commerce exception. Lopez limits commerce power and forbids inserting a missing statutory element; this transaction regime has its own commercial basis, and the Necessary and Proper Clause implements that established power.

The reasonable-effort term, available-record language, interim context and absence of a command to arrest or prevent a transfer support the confined question decided. The word including does not mean only; the Court makes no categorical textual excision of every conceivable nonrecord act and supplies no independent investigative power. It decides no compulsory field-investigation, permanent state enforcement-organization or general policy-administration command. Nor does the officer's oath independently create a task absent enacted direction. The remaining fiscal objection concerns whether this actual arrangement may finally be sustained, and is genuinely left open.

**Statutory scope and conditions:** Section 922(s)(2) directs reasonable effort within five business days after the specified dealer notice, including research in available state and local systems and the designated national system. It does not require an arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Section (6)(B) concerns a transmitted purchaser statement: unless the officer determines the transaction unlawful, the statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the purchaser made the statement; disclosure is limited to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection, and use is limited to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Section (6)(C) separately requires written reasons only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. No transaction-specific trigger is found satisfied or unsatisfied here.

**Limits:** The holding preserves New York's prohibition, the distinct sources for state-court and election duties, valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation and lawful spending arrangements. It establishes no general cheap-command exception, permanent compulsory records power, state-law veto of valid federal law or constitutional payment entitlement. No categorical holding validates or invalidates every ancillary information duty or independent dealer obligation.

##### Part II — The limited mandate binds without a majority fiscal rationale

**Operative disposition:** In both dockets, vacate the appellate judgments only insofar as they unconditionally sustain the challenged officer obligations, and remand for development and consideration of the existing budget and unauthorized-expenditure objections under Part I. No substantive entitlement to compensation, compensation cause of action, measure of fair value, state-law liability or taking is decided.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supply five votes for that judgment and its confined mandate. Souter's affirmative fair-value rationale has no other join. The other four support the remand only under the independently controlling Part I rule, while maintaining that compulsory administration should instead be invalidated. There is no majority fiscal rule and no Marks synthesis.

**Scope of the return:** The courts must genuinely consider the asserted objections and their legal consequences; ultimate constitutional validity has not already been decided against the officers. Development remains confined to the existing controversy and whatever ordinary proceedings are necessary. The mandate neither requires a new money claim or automatic hearing nor finds waiver, expenditure, liability, receipt of a form, retained information, an ineligibility determination or a request for reasons. It does not automatically revive every severability issue, decide absent parties' rights or declare that particular lower interim relief has revived or expired. The criminal-vagueness, unraised civil-vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment matters outside the grant remain undisturbed.

## Precedent Treatment

- New York v. United States: retains the prohibition on compulsory state legislation or ownership/liability and its lawful-inducement and generally applicable regulation boundaries. Part I answers the distinct limited executive-assistance question without overruling that holding. Its independently operative remainder rule remains available when a proper severability question must be decided; it does not resolve absent parties' duties here.
- United States v. Lopez: retains its commercial-connection requirement and prohibition on adding a missing element. The direct handgun-transaction setting is distinguished from the school-possession/productivity chain.
- United States v. Harris and United States v. Hale: retain their economic-class and integral transfer/stock grounds. Valid commercial regulation does not itself decide the propriety of every means used to implement it.
- Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson: retains separate congressional-election and presidential-registration sources. Its mandatory duties are not converted into a general commerce-power exception based on administrative cost or convenience.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority and South Carolina v. Baker: preserve generally applicable federal regulation affecting state activity; no immunity for every traditional governmental function is revived.
- FERC v. Mississippi: retains its adjudicative and cooperative setting in a federally preemptible field; it supplies context rather than automatic approval of every executive command.
- Testa v. Katt: preserves the distinct obligation of state adjudication under the Supremacy Clause; function matters when an official performs work traditionally assigned to judges.
- Raines v. Byrd: distinguishes unauthorized institutional dilution from duties imposed directly on the officers; no universal exclusion of official injury or standing for absent parties follows.
- Alaska Airlines v. Brock: retains the independently operative remainder and congressional-design inquiry. The Court does not decide whether independent dealer transmission or waiting duties continue in a hypothetical scheme with an invalid officer command.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, Part II of the opinion announcing the judgment.** His reason for remand is that appropriate executive assistance cannot, in his view, mean compulsory administrative support without an obligation to pay fair value. Federalist Nos. 27 and 44 describe an auxiliary executive role and the force of the constitutional oath; Nos. 36 and 45 reinforce the execution examples. They support properly directed assistance under an enacted command within the officer's branch function, not an automatic duty created by an oath alone. New York continues to protect legislative choice. Early administrative examples alone would not establish his conclusion.

The officers' assertions that their budgets make no provision for this work and that they face unauthorized-expenditure liability therefore require development and consideration. He regards that issue as a condition on sustaining the existing compulsory arrangement, not as a reason to invent a damages action. He finds no compensation amount, taking, liability, pleaded money cause or payment defendant established and orders no appropriation. The Court leaves the fiscal question open, permitting his stronger view to be considered without adopting it. No other Justice joins that affirmative fair-value rationale.

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment and dissenting from Part I; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join the structural and conditional-judgment grounds.** New York's accountability principle cannot be avoided by addressing state executive officers instead of legislatures. The Brady inquiry and its implicit required acceptance of forms allocate federal implementation to unwilling state offices. Reasonable effort concerns the amount of compelled work, not the State's choice whether to undertake it. A valid commercial object and an assumed promise of reimbursement do not cure that structural defect. Garcia and Baker regulate state activity; Testa and FERC retain distinct adjudicative or cooperative settings; Voting Rights Coalition rests on constitutional election sources absent here.

They would reverse as to mandatory checking and implicit acceptance. They would not declare the destruction and requested-reasons clauses unconstitutional, adjudicate genuinely voluntary or separately triggered applications, or give officers a general license to retain or misuse information. General nonparticipation is not proof that a particular form or request never existed. They would reserve absent dealers' transmission and waiting questions, rather than declare the entire remainder severed and valid.

Part I nevertheless supplies the law governing these cases. Under that rule the actual fiscal objections remain unresolved, and leaving unconditional appellate approval intact would foreclose a real part of the controversy. They therefore accept this confined vacatur and remand while maintaining their preferred structural reversal. They join no compensation entitlement, fiscal cure, source-and-function rationale, state-law veto or search for hypothetical new claims. Their agreement with the mandate does not erase their substantive disagreement.

O'Connor adds, without the others' joins to this additional qualification, that voluntary participation and federally funded contractual cooperation remain available, and that purely ministerial reporting requirements are not resolved by the compelled investigation at issue. That reservation creates no exemption for every task described as ministerial.

**Stevens, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer join.** They would affirm the confined interim duties and their conditional safeguards. Congress directly governs commercial handgun transfers, retains the substantive policy and coercive consequences, and requires temporary, resource-sensitive information assistance. The scheme requires no state legislation, assumption of private property and liability, independent enforcement regime, arrest or prosecution. New York's contrary setting remains binding; supremacy and oath obligations, Garcia, Baker and the cooperation recognized in FERC and Testa support appropriate assistance within an established federal power. Election duties retain their own sources.

The budget and state-law assertions deserve an answer, but do not establish a constitutional payment condition or justify returning this constitutional judgment. The duty's actual functions and the responsibilities left to the State supply their answer; they do not presume free implementation or nonexistent exposure. Reasonable effort can account for resources without becoming optional whenever a budget lacks a line item. Their affirmance decides no state-law liability, taking or every possible fiscal claim. It requires no severance and provides no general permission for permanent state conscription.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment.** He would affirm both judgments and deny the requested injunction against the confined duties. Congress may require temporary, narrowly bounded assistance using lawfully available government records as an incident of otherwise valid private-transaction regulation, with federal substantive rules and federal responsibility for coercive enforcement. The mandatory eligibility assessment is real; calling supporting tasks ministerial does not eliminate it or its cost.

He reads reasonable effort in the whole interim scheme, its available-record tasks and the absence of a command to stop transfers or arrest purchasers. Including does not itself mean only. Ordinary checking and interpretation remain required; the construction adds no commerce element or administrative power and does not excuse all inquiry. He would authorize no compulsory independent field investigation, permanent state enforcement organization or transfer of general federal regulatory responsibility. The conditional explanations, destruction and information protections retain their separate statutory predicates; adverse dealer notification remains discretionary.

His position narrows his earlier categorical objection to compelled executive administration. The reason for that refinement is the difference between incidental records work and independent regulatory or coercive responsibility, not convenience or a claim that state work is costless. New York's compulsory-legislation prohibition, Lopez's source and no-rewriting limits, and the distinct election, state-court, preemption and voluntary-cooperation rules remain. His position creates no permanent duty, retroactive officer liability or reopening of a completed judgment. The asserted expenditures do not change his conclusion on these limited duties, and no severance or further fiscal proceeding is required. He leaves materially different commands and unpresented applications for another case.

**Breyer, additional opinion concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment.** Dividing execution between federal and state officials can preserve state autonomy and avoid expansion of a centralized federal bureaucracy. Federalism alone does not establish that separate federal implementation always protects liberty better. Comparative federal arrangements can illustrate that possibility, but are neither American constitutional authority nor proof that a foreign system can be transplanted here. His sufficient domestic grounds remain valid commerce power, necessary implementation, reasonable effort and the State's retained responsibilities. This supplements his full join in Stevens's opinion; it creates no general cost-benefit override, cheap-command exception or compensation cure.
