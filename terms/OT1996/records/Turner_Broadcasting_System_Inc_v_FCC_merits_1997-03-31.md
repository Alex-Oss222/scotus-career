##### General full-power core: supported paths

**A — affirm under the actual intermediate standard.** The record supplies substantial support for Congress's prediction that the cable distribution bottleneck, advertising incentives and vertically integrated programming incentives threaten carriage and the continuing availability of significant local stations. Drops, refusals and adverse repositioning, their persistence, and the link between cable audience and station revenue are materially more than an assertion that free broadcasting is desirable. Industrywide growth does not negate a supported threat to particular local service or require Congress to wait for collapse of the entire industry. Congress may adopt a preventive response, while the courts independently evaluate support and fit.

Carriage obligations have real editorial costs, including effects on choices already made. The 1.2 percent statistic concerns **added must-carry stations' channels**, not the entire reserved capacity or every displaced expression. The evidence that many systems needed no additions and that capacity was expanding bears on fit without eliminating the protected interest. The core's capacity, duplication and signal-quality limits remain relevant at their enacted scope. The district court examined leased access at regulated prices, A/B switches, the more limited prior FCC-style rules, and subsidies. On the record recited, it reasonably found that these alternatives would not protect the accepted interests as effectively. Intermediate review requires an adequate fit, not the theoretically least burdensome arrangement or proof of certain future harm.

The strongest objection to A is that relatively healthy broadcast economics, local variation, weak signals or unwanted stations, and the burdens borne by competing cable programmers undermine a uniform response. The affirming position accepts those contrary facts and expressive interests; it finds them insufficient to make the supported prediction unreasonable or the limited structural response substantially excessive. It does not treat congressional judgment as conclusive, automatically approve every local carriage application, or announce that a small national average answers every individual burden.

**B — reverse for inadequate justification and fit.** The same expanded evidence still does not adequately connect a systemwide compulsory editorial preference with serious threatened loss of free local service across the scheme's actual reach. Aggregate growth alone does not disprove every local concern, but admitted incentives alone do not establish the needed scope either. The Act protects stations beyond those shown threatened by anticompetitive exclusion and burdens independent cable programmers as well as cable owners. The added-channel average understates the full legal restriction. Concrete narrower alternatives and the disparity between the asserted harm and nationwide carriage make the burden excessive even applying Turner I's intermediate rule.

The strongest objection to B is that it effectively demands certainty, a collapsing industry or the least restrictive means. The reversing position disclaims all three. Its objection is the adequacy of this scheme's factual and remedial fit, not the impossibility of lawful preventive carriage regulation. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg additionally preserve their Turner I objection that the preference's actual operation selects favored broadcasting content; that reservation does not turn the now-controlling full-power classification into an unentered overruling. The independent intermediate-scrutiny insufficiency is sufficient for their present reversal position.

**C — vacate for trial.** Jackson's concern supplies a genuine alternative: disputed factual predicates may require trial rather than appellate resolution of competing affidavits. The eight do not select that path here. The affirming four regard the issue as sufficiency of supported legislative prediction after extensive development, not a need to settle whose forecast will certainly prove correct. The reversing four regard the government's showing, even without converting contrary evidence into new findings, as insufficient under the governing substantive test. None adopts a general rule that legislative facts are exempt from Rule 56, that every conflicting expert opinion requires trial, or that all relevant facts here are undisputed.

###### Core duties, their qualifications and their actual role

**Commercial capacity.** Section 534(b)(1)(A) requires a system with twelve or fewer usable activated channels to carry **at least three** local commercial signals. Its exemption for a system with 300 or fewer subscribers lasts only while the system does not delete any broadcast television signal, and exempts only §534 duties. A system with more than twelve channels is subject to the local-commercial obligation up to one-third of its usable activated channels, §534(b)(1)(B). Neither provision requires unavailable/ineligible stations, limits voluntary carriage to that number, or supplies a ceiling on added §535 obligations. Selection when stations exceed the required number remains the operator's, subject to the closest-city-of-license affiliate rule and the prohibition on substituting low-power carriage for a local commercial station. These terms constrain compulsion but do not make it voluntary or eliminate the burden of maintaining stations already carried.

**Educational capacity is additional.** Section 535(a) operates in addition to §534. Twelve-or-fewer-channel systems ordinarily carry one qualified local educational signal, with voluntary additional carriage and the continued-carriage rule preserved. If beyond any qualified local educational station's presence, they import one chosen station; they need not remove programming actually provided on March 29, 1990 for that importation but must use the first channel available. Systems with thirteen through thirty-six channels carry at least one and need not carry more than three qualified local educational signals, subject to continued carriage, and may carry more; absent a local qualified station they must import at least one. Growth beyond thirty-six channels on or after March 29, 1990 activates the each-requesting-qualified-local-station rule with its other conditions. Above thirty-six channels, the general rule requires each requesting qualified local educational station, with the **additional** substantially duplicative station exception when three must be carried; there is no general three-station cap.

**Existing educational carriage and signal quality.** Section 535(c), notwithstanding the other provisions, retains every qualified local educational station carried March 29, 1990; waiver for a specific operator/station requires both parties' written consent. Section 535(g)(4), also notwithstanding the other provisions, separately removes compulsory carriage when a good-quality or baseband signal is not delivered to the principal headend. Both commands remain stated; no unsupported actual conflict is decided. Continued carriage is a real constraint on future editorial changes. The affirming ground therefore does not treat the ordinary capacity categories as universal release from earlier commitments.

**Commercial and educational duplication differ.** Section 534(b)(5) permits omission of a substantially duplicative commercial signal or another affiliate of a network; it does not forbid such carriage. Every voluntarily carried duplicate or multiple affiliate counts toward the subsection (b)(1) number. Section 535(b)(3)(C), for thirteen-to-thirty-six-channel systems, permits omission of an additional affiliate of the **same State public television network only when** its programming substantially duplicates the carried affiliate. Section 535(e), for systems above thirty-six channels that must carry three, permits omission of **additional** substantially duplicative educational stations by comparison with another qualified local educational station **requesting carriage**, with FCC's definition promoting distinctive educational services. No commercial duplication rule is transplanted into §535. Section 535(f) also prevents a carried educational station from using network nonduplication rights to delete another carried local educational station's programs.

**Signal and geographic eligibility.** The commercial definition covers full-power, regularly licensed, noneducational stations in the relevant market and excludes low-power stations, translators and passive repeaters. Distant-signal treatment requires the station's increased-copyright indemnity. Below −45 dBm UHF or −49 dBm VHF at the principal headend, the station is excluded unless it agrees to bear delivery costs for a good-quality or baseband signal. Educational qualification has its separate public/nonprofit license and CPB-grant-eligibility route, municipal-ownership/predominantly-educational-programming route, and stated translator/reserved-channel inclusions. Educational locality is satisfied by a principal-community reference point within fifty miles **or** a Grade B contour encompassing the headend. Section 535(i)(2)'s indemnity condition for adding a distant educational signal expressly excepts stations already carried under subsection (c). These limits mitigate specific mismatches; they do not establish universal station vulnerability or permit deleting every costly signal.

**The speech burden includes full service and placement.** Section 534(b)(3) requires entire primary video/audio and line-21 captions, technically feasible program-related ancillary material, and the entire schedule subject to the identified exclusivity/substitution regulations. Other ancillary material is discretionary, and appropriate feasible signal enhancements may be removed for headend use. Section 535(g)(1) separately covers entire primary video/audio/captions and technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education or language, leaving other material discretionary. Required carriage occupies usable expressive capacity; it is not an empty-channel reservation. Commercial material-nondegradation and technically feasible quality-comparison rules and educational commercial-equivalent bandwidth/technical-capacity and nondegradation rules apply separately. Commercial stations may elect over-the-air, July 19, 1985 or January 1, 1992 positioning; educational stations have only the first two statutory choices, apart from agreed alternatives. FCC resolves positioning disputes.

**Availability, deletion and price are separate constraints.** Commercial required signals reach every subscriber under §534(b)(7)'s receiver-connection conditions, including converter information and an offer of sale/lease in the specified subscriber-installed-connection circumstance. Educational signals must be on the lowest-priced tier that includes local commercial broadcasts. Commercial deletion or repositioning requires thirty days' written station notice and cannot occur during major audience-measurement periods. Educational repositioning, including deletion, requires thirty days' notice to the station **and subscribers**. Neither notice mechanism authorizes evasion of substantive carriage/placement duties. Additional educational signals and additional required low-power signals beyond the first may use unused PEG capacity only on their respective terms and with franchising approval.

Section 534(b)(10) bars requesting or accepting mandatory-carriage/placement compensation, subject to good-signal delivery costs, distant-signal copyright indemnity and continuation of a pre-June 26, 1990 agreement only until its expiration. Section 535(i) separately bars accepting mandatory-carriage compensation except delivery costs and preserves its distinct distant-signal qualification. The free-carriage commands add economic and editorial consequences; the district court's rejection of regulated paid leased access as equally effective is therefore material, not answered by pretending that must-carry permits an ordinary commercial rental price.

**Enforcement differs by section.** A commercial station first gives written notice; the operator has thirty days to commence carriage or explain its position. FCC review gives an opportunity to respond, a 120-day determination, and, on a carriage violation, carriage for at least twelve months. Section 535(j) instead provides a station complaint, operator response opportunity, 120-day decision, particular findings and necessary remedial action; it does not incorporate the commercial preliminary-notice or minimum-twelve-month terms. Compliance requires dismissal under each. This facial case establishes no particular FCC violation or remedial order, and these distinctions do not create new questions outside the reviewed burden-and-fit issue.

###### What the exact core terms do to the competing fit positions

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer continue to find the evidence sufficient for the **commercial full-power requirements**, for the **educational requirements at their separate statutory scope**, and for their **combined burden**. Their reasoning accounts for additional educational capacity, continued carriage, the true duplication permissions, compulsory full schedules/placement and the inability to charge for required carriage. They do not base affirmance on a combined one-third cap or on the proposition that all affected stations are commercial competitors for advertising. Broadcast availability and independent informational sources support the educational obligations; the economic account of advertising and affiliated programming more directly addresses the commercial relationship and overall bottleneck. Local/qualified/signal limitations, differentiated capacity treatment and true duplication relief remain relevant, but the empirical record and statutory fit must do the constitutional work together. Preventing a supported loss of local broadcast availability can justify continuing existing carriage as well as restoring deleted stations; existing voluntary carriage does not prove that legal compulsion has no burden.

Their strongest revised objection is that the additional educational obligations and historical continued-carriage command could create materially greater displacement on some systems than a commercial quota or added-channel average suggests. They accept that possibility rather than convert the 1.2 percent statistic into an aggregate-burden finding. The general facial record supports the structural protection with these statutory qualifications; a particular application presenting a materially different excessive burden remains subject to its own supported challenge. That reservation does not require a challenger to prove every application invalid, or substitute possible later relief for the present Government showing. The current showing is judged sufficient at the scheme's actual facial scope.

O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg continue to find insufficient fit for the **commercial full-power requirements**, for the **educational requirements at their separate scope**, and for their **combined burden**. They credit the mitigating features just recited, including the conditional small-system exemption, differing duplication permissions, signal/copyright safeguards and the first-available-channel rule. Their objection does not pretend there is an unqualified nationwide obligation to carry every station. It remains that actual compelled carriage and frozen editorial choices extend beyond a sufficiently established need, while the Government has not adequately answered materially narrower responses. Additional §535 duties and continued carriage underscore why a low added-channel percentage cannot alone prove proportionate interference. Free local educational expression and source diversity are important interests, but importance does not prove this scope necessary. ## Event

**Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission**, No. 95-992. Merits decision, March 31, 1997. Argued October 7, 1996. On direct appeal from the three-judge District Court for the District of Columbia, 910 F. Supp. 734, after the 1994 remand. The granted question asks whether the developed record establishes advancement and fit under the intermediate scrutiny prescribed for the general full-power must-carry core.

**Statutory setting relevant to fit:** Under 47 U.S.C.§534, systems with 12 or fewer usable activated channels must carry at least three local commercial signals; systems also having 300 or fewer subscribers are exempt from that section only while they delete no broadcast signal. Systems with more than 12 channels carry eligible local commercial signals up to one-third of usable activated capacity. This is not a combined commercial-and-educational ceiling. Substantially duplicative commercial signals already represented in carriage and additional same-network affiliates need not be carried; voluntary duplicates and multiple affiliates count toward the obligation. When eligible stations exceed the required number, the operator selects among them, but cannot substitute low power for full power and must choose the elected network's affiliate with the closest city-of-license reference point to the principal headend.

Section535 adds separate educational duties. Systems with 12 or fewer channels carry one qualified local educational signal and may carry more. Systems with 13–36 carry at least one and need not carry more than three, subject to continued carriage; they may carry more. If no qualified local station is present, the former category must import one qualified educational signal and the latter at least one. For that importation, the smallest systems need not remove programming actually provided on March 29, 1990 but must use the first available channel. All qualified local educational stations carried on that date retain carriage unless operator and station give written waiver consent. Systems above36 channels, including those expanding beyond36, carry requesting qualified local educational stations; a system required to carry three need not add stations substantially duplicating another qualified local educational station requesting carriage. This is not a general three-station cap. A13–36-channel system already carrying a State-network educational affiliate need not add another same-network affiliate whose programming substantially duplicates the carried affiliate.

Commercial distant signals require indemnification of increased copyright liability. A commercial station below the specified headend levels (−45dBm UHF; −49dBm VHF) is excluded if it does not agree to bear the costs of delivering a good-quality or baseband signal. Educational carriage is not required without delivery of a good-quality or baseband signal; its distant-signal indemnity exception concerns adding a signal and expressly does not erase the continued-carriage rule. These are actual programming obligations, including primary video, audio and captions. Commercial carriage includes technically feasible program-related blanking-interval or subcarrier material; educational carriage includes technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education or language purposes. Other ancillary material remains discretionary. Positioning and availability duties also constrain operation; the law does not merely reserve empty channels. No new dispute over ancillary material, positioning, payment, notice or enforcement is decided.

The reserved low-power duty depends on an insufficient number of full-power local commercial stations: systems with35 or fewer channels carry one qualified low-power station, and larger systems two. Its disputed programming criterion concerns local needs inadequately served because of full-power stations' geographic distance. The separate market-modification factor asks whether another eligible station supplies the community news, sports or events; it is not that requirement imposed on the requesting station. These provisions' independent classifications remain outside the granted question.

## Holdings

### I. The granted question does not decide the reserved classifications

**Rule and authority:** Review of whether the general full-power scheme satisfies its prescribed intermediate standard does not adjudicate the separate content classification of low-power eligibility under §534(h)(2)(B) or the market-modification factor under §534(h)(1)(C)(ii)(III). All nine join Part I and decline to expand this expressly limited review.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner I classified the full-power core but reserved those different provisions. A former remand does not permanently enlarge a subsequent limited appellate grant. Time Warner actually presses both challenges; the Government's identified preservation objection concerns low power. Merits briefing alone does not change the granted question, and neither classification is a necessary subsidiary step in reviewing the core's record support and fit. The 1995 Rules 18.3, 14.1(a) and 24.1(a) preserve that question boundary and permit, rather than require, consideration of otherwise cognizable plain error. The Court declines that discretionary expansion given the distinct operations, injury and relief questions. It adopts no categorical standing bar, abandonment finding or judgment that either criterion is neutral. The district court's grouping of a market factor with low power does not merge them, and viewpoint neutrality or benevolent motive alone does not answer content classification. Their constitutional validity remains outside this judgment.

### II. The developed record supports preservation of independent broadcast sources

**Rule and authority:** The general full-power carriage duties materially advance the substantial interests in preserving free local broadcasting and a multiplicity of independently available information sources on this developed record. Part II controls through Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer under the existing intermediate standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner I recognizes ordinary cable editorial and programmer speech protection and permits respect for supported legislative predictions while requiring independent judicial scrutiny. The developed evidence links cable bottleneck control, incentives to favor affiliated programming and advertising, drops or refusals and adverse repositioning to diminished broadcast audience, revenue and continued service. Those circumstances support preventive protection of significant local sources without proof that the entire industry will collapse or that every carried station individually faces failure. Contrary growth and profit evidence matters but does not negate every supported local threat. The sufficient justification is preservation and diversity, not an independently sufficient ruling that the Act is tailored against economic predation. Breyer shares that common rationale directly: bottleneck choices can restrict audience options without predatory intent. Congressional prediction is not conclusive, and a desirable broadcasting policy alone would not suffice.

### III. The present burden and fit satisfy intermediate review

**Rule and authority:** On this developed facial record, the reviewed duties do not burden substantially more cable editorial discretion than necessary to advance those interests; intermediate review requires adequate fit, not the least restrictive conceivable arrangement. Part III has the same five direct joins and permits final affirmance without a further trial here.

**Controlling explanation:** O'Brien and Ward supply the intermediate-fit inquiry. The capacity categories, commercial nonduplication provisions and separate noncommercial rules described above confine the legal obligation; existing voluntary carriage counts without extinguishing editorial freedom to choose differently. The evidence that many systems added no station and that additions occupied 1.2 percent of all channels bears on incremental burden. That statistic does not measure every legally reserved channel, displaced program or foregone choice. Expanding capacity and the remaining editorial space also matter, alongside the burdens on operators, cable programmers and viewers. The district court considered regulated leased access, A/B reception switches, narrower former FCC-type rules and subsidies, and the developed record supports its conclusion that these alternatives would not protect the accepted interests as effectively. Alternatives are relevant without becoming a least-restrictive-means command. The predictive sufficiency question can be decided on this record despite conflicting forecasts; no general exemption of legislative facts from summary-judgment rules follows. The ruling neither preapproves a more burdensome future law nor forecloses a supported as-applied displacement challenge.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC (1994):** Applied the majority full-power classification, intermediate standard and limited predictive-deference principle; did not convert its four-Justice former insufficiency explanation into a holding or decide the reserved classifications.
- **United States v. O'Brien:** Applied the requirement of a substantial interest unrelated to speech suppression and a sufficiently fitted burden.
- **Ward v. Rock Against Racism:** Applied adequate tailoring without demanding the least restrictive imaginable alternative; concrete alternatives remain relevant evidence of fit.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium v. FCC:** Preserved ordinary cable speech protection, separate statutory components and independent justification/means review. Its contextual rationales and separate strict-content position supply no universal replacement standard here.
- **Broadcast spectrum-scarcity doctrine:** Not extended to cable or used to reduce review to rational-basis scrutiny.

## Law After Decision

The developed record sustains the general full-power core under existing content-neutral intermediate scrutiny through a directly shared preservation/diversity rationale. Congress retains room for supported preventive judgments; courts retain independent review of actual advancement, editorial burdens and fit. No independent economic-predation application, new general cable test, approval of the two reserved classifications or immunity from supported as-applied review is established.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, concurring.** Must carry burdens operators, cable programmers and cable viewers, but also preserves independently available speech for noncable viewers with fewer alternatives. His actual Denver approach weighs the relevant speech interests and fit in their concrete setting. Bottleneck control can reduce available sources without predatory conduct. He joins preservation/diversity, the proper added-channel measure, consideration of alternatives and the result; he does not adopt a freestanding economic-predation justification or treat deference as surrender of judicial review.

**Stevens, concurring.** Congress's substantial predictive evidence warrants respect without proof that every forecast will come true. His Turner I position accepted the limited deference principle without joining the complete insufficiency explanation. He regards the congressional evidence as sufficient without treating additional remand evidence as constitutionally indispensable, while joining the Court's fuller developed-record reasoning and fit conclusion. He adopts neither conclusiveness of legislative findings nor a universal broadcast-industry-collapse prerequisite.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg, dissenting in part.** They would reverse even under the governing intermediate rule. Healthy industry trends do not disprove every local threat, but incentives and selected harms insufficiently justify this nationwide editorial preference, including stations not shown threatened and burdens on competing programmers. The added-channel average understates the whole legal restriction; narrower alternatives and unequal local burdens expose inadequate fit. They require neither certain harm, collapse of the entire industry nor the least restrictive imaginable rule. They also retain their Turner I objection to the general content classification without purporting to overrule it here. They join the express-grant scope ruling, preserving their separate content objections to low-power eligibility and market modification for proper review; benign purpose and viewpoint neutrality do not resolve those objections.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed full-power duties remain enforceable under the affirmed facial judgment. No additional trial or remedial order is required here. Properly supported as-applied challenges and the separately reserved classification questions require their own lawful presentation; this decision dictates no outcome for them.
