## Controlling holdings

### I. Congress may protect the legally fixed interstate payment obligation

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may punish willful post-enactment failure to pay a legally fixed past-due child-support obligation when the obligor and child reside in different States, because §228 directly protects that cross-border economic payment obligation. The different-state relation is essential to this holding; the Court sustains neither regulation of every family debt nor federal creation or revision of the underlying support duty.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This common nine-Justice coalition controls every holding and the affirmance in both prosecutions.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 228(a) requires willful failure to pay a past-due support obligation for a child residing in another State. Section 228(d)(1) requires an amount fixed by a court order or State-law administrative process for support of a child, or a child and custodial parent, unpaid longer than one year or exceeding $5,000. Those thresholds are alternatives; the indictments happen to allege both. Neither the amount nor duration substitutes for the interstate relation.

The object regulated is the required cross-border payment itself. Different residence does constitutional work because the existing obligation calls for satisfaction between the obligor and children in another State. That is a legal characterization of the charged class, not a finding of actual transfer. A completed payment need not precede an offense addressing its willful withholding. The State-law origin and absence of a negotiated bargain do not erase the obligation's economic payment character.

The strongest objection treats the default as a local family failure with a geographical label. Lopez rejects deriving federal power from remote chains through poverty, welfare expense, crime or productivity. The Court relies on none. Harris and Bishop/Stokes protect the covered vehicle as an interstate instrumentality against direct forcible taking and require proof of the enacted vehicle element; Hale addresses possession integral to direct market regulation. They require attention to the actual object and relation, not an automatic commerce label for every debt or object with interstate history. The present due cross-border payment supplies that relation independently.

Lopez also forbids rewriting a statute to supply a missing element. No flight, travel, voluntary State crossing, bank wire, particular instrument or motive to evade is added here. The Court decides no statute lacking the different-state condition and no unrelated obligation bearing only an incidental geographical feature. It makes no finding of deliberate evasion or ability to pay.

### II. Direct regulation of these private obligors neither commandeers a State nor issues a domestic-relations decree

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 228's direct regulation of the private obligor does not compel State administration, and the limited domestic-relations exception does not bar these federal prosecutions. The existing operative support order supplies a predicate; federal courts may apply the offense's elements and lawful defenses but may not create or modify that obligation, custody, parentage or domestic status.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part II in full.

**Controlling explanation:** New York v. United States distinguishes valid direct private regulation from compelled State responsibility. Section 228 commands no State legislature, court or executive officer to legislate, enforce the debt, investigate for federal officials, alter an order or assume the obligor's liability. Printz's qualified executive-assistance rule concerns a different means of implementation. This prosecution requires no approval of compulsory State assistance and presents no analogous fiscal question. The absence of commandeering alone supplies no commerce power; Part I independently supplies it.

Ankenbrandt v. Richards confines the statutory diversity exception to issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees, rather than imposing a general Article III exclusion for all matters involving families. This federal criminal proceeding issues no such decree. The State-law origin of the debt therefore neither defeats the established federal power nor transfers family-law authority to the federal tribunal. The Court does not recalculate support, determine a modification or prescribe State enforcement machinery. A cognizable challenge to whether the predicate order exists and remains operative is not foreclosed. Hypothetical interference with a different State proceeding is not decided.

### III. Both prosecutions remain confined to post-enactment failures and proof of every element

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal conviction and punishment must rest on willful failures after §228's October 25, 1992 enactment, with every applicable element proved beyond a reasonable doubt. Earlier arrears may establish lawful background or past-due status, but a completed pre-enactment failure cannot itself constitute the federal offense.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part III in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Mussari's alleged period starts November 1, 1988, and Schroeder's February 1, 1992. Neither indictment's earlier starting date enlarges the enacted offense. The Ex Post Facto Clause independently prohibits punishment for conduct completed before enactment. Landgraf and Rivers reinforce attention to the legal consequences attached to completed conduct; their distinct civil approaches do not replace that criminal prohibition or require agreement on every earlier temporal rationale.

The constitutional ruling establishes neither defendant's willfulness, capacity to pay, qualifying fixed amount, required unpaid duration, different-state residence for the necessary period, or any sentencing fact. Ability-related defenses recognized by law and other lawful defenses remain available. Each existing prosecution returns for ordinary proceedings within its charges and applicable statutory, temporal and procedural limits. No new allegation, amended indictment, offense period, conviction or special trial instruction beyond this legal boundary is directed. The Court adopts no comprehensive continuing-offense theory to eliminate a separate proof or notice question.

### IV. Each appellate trial remand stands; any restitution remains a separate conditional issue

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment reversing each district-court indictment dismissal and remanding each prosecution is affirmed, subject to the post-enactment limit and ordinary proof requirements. Section 228(c) requires restitution under §3663 upon conviction equal to the past-due obligation existing at sentencing; this pretrial decision fixes no amount and does not decide the precise lawful treatment of older arrears in such an order.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part IV and both disposition components in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Sustaining the presented constitutional basis for prosecution does not decide guilt or sentencing. The statutory first-offense exposure is a fine, imprisonment up to six months, or both; a subsequent offense permits a fine, imprisonment up to two years, or both. No prior offense or sentence is found here. Restitution's separate command is conditional on conviction. The rule against punishing a completed pre-enactment omission does not automatically exclude every older arrear from a later restitution calculation; nor does the sentencing-balance language automatically approve inclusion without the separate statutory and constitutional inquiry. Both questions remain for any proceeding in which they actually arise. Neither dismissal is restored. The Court orders no detention, conviction, restitution award, compensation proceeding, State-order revision or compulsory State administrative act.

### Contrary paths and narrower or broader alternatives

**Rejecting the interstate-payment classification.** The strongest rival treats the underlying act as failure to perform a noncommercial family duty. Different residence, on that view, is a jurisdictional label that cannot change the activity's character, and the district court's rejection of AFDC or general poverty effects follows Lopez. This path is legally supported by Lopez's demand for a real commercial connection and by the fact that Harris concerned direct protection of an interstate instrumentality and Hale concerned an integral market relation. It would reverse the Ninth Circuit on the commerce question and restore each constitutional dismissal without finding either defendant innocent on the facts.

The eight Associates reject that path here because the law reaches an existing monetary obligation with a current cross-border payment relation, rather than deriving commerce from family hardship. Their ground does not depend on AFDC effects, moral disapproval, or the fact that all conduct might influence the economy. The relevant distinction is the obligation's legally required interstate satisfaction, as presented in these charged different-state relationships. Their ruling is confined to that class and retains every statutory element. No separate broad effects rationale is added as an independently sufficient holding.

**Saving construction through travel, evasion or payment method.** A narrower offense requiring flight, deliberate State crossing, or an actual interstate instrument might present an additional connection. But those conditions are not enacted and the record establishes none. Lopez's no-rewriting rule forecloses supplying them as the basis for this judgment. The enacted willfulness condition remains real; it is not replaced with a finding of interstate evasion.

**A categorical family-law exclusion or an automatic Printz analogy.** Ankenbrandt and New York support distinguishing decrees and State administration, not forbidding all direct federal regulation affecting families. Printz does not hold that every valid federal crime may recruit State officers, and these prosecutions require no such conclusion. Neither a categorical exception nor a fiscal remand follows.

**An immediate guilt or restitution disposition.** The statutory threshold and constitutional power do not establish the charged facts. A decision sustaining the statute therefore returns each existing prosecution rather than entering convictions or calculating arrears. The offense-date limit and older-arrears restitution question remain distinct. These narrower limits are essential to the common join, not optional observations.

### Reconciled components and sufficient grounds

**M1 — Actual offense and common constitutional question.** Section 228(a), as enacted October 25, 1992, punishes willful failure to pay a past-due support obligation with respect to a child residing in another State. Section 228(d)(1) requires an amount fixed by a court order or State-law administrative process for support of a child, or of a child and the custodial parent, which has remained unpaid longer than one year **or** exceeds $5,000. Those are alternatives, although these indictments allege both. Willfulness and the different-state condition remain elements; the word debt, the amount, or the duration alone is not a constitutional substitute for the required interstate relation.

Both January 11, 1995 indictments are included. Mussari's concerns two Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning November 1, 1988; Schroeder's concerns four Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning February 1, 1992. The Court need not resolve the one-day discrepancy in the sources describing the initial District Court dismissal to review the identified appellate judgment. It finds no ability to pay, deliberate evasion, actual interstate travel, flight, guilt, specific arrears balance, or defect in a State support order. Constitutional review proceeds on the charged class and preserved question, leaving statutory proof and lawful defenses to each prosecution.

**M2 — Direct protection of these interstate payment obligations.** All eight Associates sustain the statute against the presented Commerce Clause objection and would affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of each separate district-court indictment dismissal, preserving the trial remand in each prosecution. Their common sufficient ground is confined to the legally fixed private payment obligation for support of children residing across State lines. Section 228 addresses willful nonpayment of that cross-border economic obligation itself. It does not seek federal power through a chain from local family conflict to poverty, welfare expenditures, crime, or national productivity. Its different-state condition does actual work in delimiting the cross-border payment relation. A transfer required by an existing support obligation does not cease to be an economic payment because State family law created it rather than a commercial bargain.

Lopez, April 26, 1995, rejects a remote effects chain for stand-alone school-zone possession and forbids inserting a missing commerce element. It does not exclude every regulation touching family life or every payment lacking a negotiated exchange. Harris, April 27, 1995, and Bishop/Stokes, February 5, 1996, sustain direct forcible taking prohibitions protecting covered interstate instrumentalities while requiring the enacted vehicle element; Hale, February 12, 1996, sustains possession integral to direct market regulation. They establish a need to identify the actual regulated object and relation, not a rule that every thing with interstate history or every debt is automatically commerce. Here the object is the due cross-border payment obligation, not a locally possessed object with remote travel or hypothetical downstream economic effects.

The cross-border payment ground is a legal characterization of the charged class, not a new finding that either defendant actually transferred money, traveled, or used a particular payment route. The common ground permits protection of the specified interstate payment obligation without finding an actual completed transfer. Withholding the required payment is the conduct Congress addresses; requiring that the money already have crossed State lines would not follow from this offense. The Court adds no flight, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire, particular payment-instrument, or motive-to-evade element. It neither constitutionalizes all unpaid family obligations nor establishes federal power to create or revise the underlying support duty. The different-state condition is necessary to this rationale; no sufficiency judgment is made about a statute lacking it or about an unrelated debt with an incidental geographical feature. The opinion should not adopt an unqualified formula that every obligation is a thing in interstate commerce merely because it is called a debt.

**M3 — Private regulation, State law, and domestic relations.** All eight reject a separate Tenth Amendment or categorical domestic-relations ground for these dismissals. The federal statute directs the private obligor to satisfy an existing obligation; it does not command a State to legislate, enforce the debt, investigate for federal officers, alter an order, or assume the obligor's liability. New York preserves direct federal regulation within enumerated power while forbidding the compulsory State responsibility it addressed. Printz's qualified executive-assistance rule is neither needed to authorize this private duty nor a reason to invent a State fiscal issue.

Ankenbrandt, June 15, 1992, confines the statutory diversity exception to issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees and rejects a general Article III domestic-relations exclusion. This federal criminal prosecution issues no such decree and treats the existing support obligation as a predicate. Its federal character must still rest on the sufficient commerce ground in M2; merely showing an absence of commandeering does not establish enumerated power. Conversely, the State-law origin of the obligation does not defeat direct federal enforcement within that power. The Court does not reopen custody, recalculate support, adjudicate a modification, prescribe future State enforcement machinery, or resolve a hypothetical interference with a different State proceeding. A lawful defense bearing on an actual statutory predicate remains available.

## Holdings

### I. Congress may protect the legally fixed interstate payment obligation

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may punish willful post-enactment failure to pay a legally fixed past-due child-support obligation when the obligor and child reside in different States, because §228 directly protects that cross-border economic payment obligation. The different-state relation is essential to this holding; the Court sustains neither regulation of every family debt nor federal creation or revision of the underlying support duty.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. This common nine-Justice coalition controls every holding and the affirmance in both prosecutions.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 228(a) requires willful failure to pay a past-due support obligation for a child residing in another State. Section 228(d)(1) requires an amount fixed by a court order or State-law administrative process for support of a child, or a child and custodial parent, unpaid longer than one year or exceeding $5,000. Those thresholds are alternatives; the indictments happen to allege both. Neither the amount nor duration substitutes for the interstate relation.

The object regulated is the required cross-border payment itself. Different residence does constitutional work because the existing obligation calls for satisfaction between the obligor and children in another State. That is a legal characterization of the charged class, not a finding of actual transfer. A completed payment need not precede an offense addressing its willful withholding. The State-law origin and absence of a negotiated bargain do not erase the obligation's economic payment character.

The strongest objection treats the default as a local family failure with a geographical label. Lopez rejects deriving federal power from remote chains through poverty, welfare expense, crime or productivity. The Court relies on none. Harris and Bishop/Stokes protect the covered vehicle as an interstate instrumentality against direct forcible taking and require proof of the enacted vehicle element; Hale addresses possession integral to direct market regulation. They require attention to the actual object and relation, not an automatic commerce label for every debt or object with interstate history. The present due cross-border payment supplies that relation independently.

Lopez also forbids rewriting a statute to supply a missing element. No flight, travel, voluntary State crossing, bank wire, particular instrument or motive to evade is added here. The Court decides no statute lacking the different-state condition and no unrelated obligation bearing only an incidental geographical feature. It makes no finding of deliberate evasion or ability to pay.

### II. Direct regulation of these private obligors neither commandeers a State nor issues a domestic-relations decree

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 228's direct regulation of the private obligor does not compel State administration, and the limited domestic-relations exception does not bar these federal prosecutions. The existing operative support order supplies a predicate; federal courts may apply the offense's elements and lawful defenses but may not create or modify that obligation, custody, parentage or domestic status.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part II in full.

**Controlling explanation:** New York v. United States distinguishes valid direct private regulation from compelled State responsibility. Section 228 commands no State legislature, court or executive officer to legislate, enforce the debt, investigate for federal officials, alter an order or assume the obligor's liability. Printz's qualified executive-assistance rule concerns a different means of implementation. This prosecution requires no approval of compulsory State assistance and presents no analogous fiscal question. The absence of commandeering alone supplies no commerce power; Part I independently supplies it.

Ankenbrandt v. Richards confines the statutory diversity exception to issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees, rather than imposing a general Article III exclusion for all matters involving families. This federal criminal proceeding issues no such decree. The State-law origin of the debt therefore neither defeats the established federal power nor transfers family-law authority to the federal tribunal. The Court does not recalculate support, determine a modification or prescribe State enforcement machinery. A cognizable challenge to whether the predicate order exists and remains operative is not foreclosed. Hypothetical interference with a different State proceeding is not decided.

### III. Both prosecutions remain confined to post-enactment failures and proof of every element

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal conviction and punishment must rest on willful failures after §228's October 25, 1992 enactment, with every applicable element proved beyond a reasonable doubt. Earlier arrears may establish lawful background or past-due status, but a completed pre-enactment failure cannot itself constitute the federal offense.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part III in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Mussari's alleged period starts November 1, 1988, and Schroeder's February 1, 1992. Neither indictment's earlier starting date enlarges the enacted offense. The Ex Post Facto Clause independently prohibits punishment for conduct completed before enactment. Landgraf and Rivers reinforce attention to the legal consequences attached to completed conduct; their distinct civil approaches do not replace that criminal prohibition or require agreement on every earlier temporal rationale.

The constitutional ruling establishes neither defendant's willfulness, capacity to pay, qualifying fixed amount, required unpaid duration, different-state residence for the necessary period, or any sentencing fact. Ability-related defenses recognized by law and other lawful defenses remain available. Each existing prosecution returns for ordinary proceedings within its charges and applicable statutory, temporal and procedural limits. No new allegation, amended indictment, offense period, conviction or special trial instruction beyond this legal boundary is directed. The Court adopts no comprehensive continuing-offense theory to eliminate a separate proof or notice question.

### IV. Each appellate trial remand stands; any restitution remains a separate conditional issue

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment reversing each district-court indictment dismissal and remanding each prosecution is affirmed, subject to the post-enactment limit and ordinary proof requirements. Section 228(c) requires restitution under §3663 upon conviction equal to the past-due obligation existing at sentencing; this pretrial decision fixes no amount and does not decide the precise lawful treatment of older arrears in such an order.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part IV and both disposition components in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Sustaining the presented constitutional basis for prosecution does not decide guilt or sentencing. The statutory first-offense exposure is a fine, imprisonment up to six months, or both; a subsequent offense permits a fine, imprisonment up to two years, or both. No prior offense or sentence is found here. Restitution's separate command is conditional on conviction. The rule against punishing a completed pre-enactment omission does not automatically exclude every older arrear from a later restitution calculation; nor does the sentencing-balance language automatically approve inclusion without the separate statutory and constitutional inquiry. Both questions remain for any proceeding in which they actually arise. Neither dismissal is restored. The Court orders no detention, conviction, restitution award, compensation proceeding, State-order revision or compulsory State administrative act.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Lopez is applied through its connection-before-magnitude sequence: §228's concrete statutory cross-border payment mechanism supplies the connection Lopez requires, without aggregation or a downstream-effects chain. Lopez's rejection of that chain for stand-alone school-zone possession and its prohibition on supplying a missing statutory commerce element are retained.
- United States v. Harris and Bishop/Stokes preserve their actual direct-instrumentality ground and proof of the enacted vehicle nexus. Their result is not converted into an automatic instrumentality rule or a holding about every debt.
- United States v. Hale preserves its integral-market ground and rejection of a universal transaction-specific nexus requirement; the present payment relation must independently support federal power.
- New York v. United States preserves the distinction between direct private regulation within enumerated power and compulsory State responsibility.
- Printz/Mack's qualified source-and-function rule and confined fiscal mandate concern compelled public administration. They supply neither the commerce premise nor a fiscal remand for these private obligations; no compensation rule is adopted.
- Ankenbrandt v. Richards preserves the statutory diversity-decree exception and rejects a general Article III family-law exclusion. Federal enforcement here issues no domestic-relations decree.
- Landgraf and Rivers retain their civil temporal rules at their own scope; the independent Ex Post Facto prohibition controls completed pre-enactment criminal conduct. No earlier decision is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Section 228 may directly protect the legally fixed payment obligation owed across State lines on the narrow ground stated. Its actual elements remain necessary; no generalized economic-effects rationale, universal debt rule or judicially added travel requirement governs. State authority to establish and modify the underlying support duty remains intact. The criminal offense-date boundary and any eventual treatment of older arrears in restitution remain distinct.
