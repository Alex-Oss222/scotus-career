## Controlling holdings

### I. An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison

**Holding and operative rule:** When an actually available, unconditional and legally adequate admission establishes the complete prior-conviction status element, that element is the Government's sole legitimate purpose for the evidence, and the name or nature of the conviction presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, Rule 403 requires exclusion of that name or nature. On this record, admitting the similar-assault judgment after rejecting the offered substitute on general prosecutorial-choice grounds was an abuse of discretion; the Ninth Circuit's no-error judgment is reversed.

The admission must establish a conviction qualifying under §922(g)(1) and §921(a)(20). The latter excludes Federal or State antitrust, unfair-trade, restraint-of-trade and similar business-regulation offenses, and State offenses classified as misdemeanors and punishable by two years or less. Conviction status follows the law of the jurisdiction of the proceedings. Expunged or set-aside convictions, pardoned convictions and convictions for which civil rights have been restored do not count, subject to the statute's qualification when the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No such exclusion is found applicable here; the offered instruction requires appropriate consensual clarification rather than an assumption that its original wording was flawless.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins to the complete evidentiary rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The prior judgment remains relevant under Rule 401. The question is whether its legitimate evidentiary value, assessed with actually available alternatives, justifies its unfair-prejudice danger. Huddleston recognizes Rule 403's contextual safeguards and attention to other proof. Review concerns the circumstances when the trial judge ruled, not hindsight.

The Government ordinarily may present convincing, coherent evidence instead of a defendant's preferred abstraction. Narrative integrity helps the jury understand the charged event and need not disappear whenever a fact is conceded. Here, however, the prior conviction supplies a detached legal status. An adequate admission supplies that complete proposition without reconstructing the earlier assault. Its name and nature add negligible legitimate force for the sole status purpose while inviting the jury to infer a disposition toward violence in a prosecution involving assault and firearms. General prosecutorial choice does not resolve that balance.

The rule does not let defendants stipulate away the narrative of a charged event, require acceptance of partial or qualified admissions, or exclude every prior-conviction record. A distinct permissible purpose requires its own lawful showing and Rule 403 balance. A sanitized judgment may also be considered if it supplies legally complete status without the impermissible prejudice. Status remains an element to be established through adequate evidence and instructions; it is not simply removed from the jury. The curable wording issue supplies no new waiver or independent affirmance ground, and the separate credibility-instruction history does not forfeit the preserved objection to the judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Huddleston's safeguards are applied to comparative status proof. Gaudin's requirement that the element be established remains intact; no general compulsory-stipulation doctrine is adopted.

### II. Harmlessness remains a separate whole-record inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The established preserved Rule 403 error is subject to Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos: the Government must establish, from the whole record, fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt defeats harmlessness. The case is remanded for that inquiry and lawful consequences for affected convictions; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, without a present finding that every count was affected.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins to the mandate and its governing standard. Stone-Zsela does not join the decision to leave the prejudice result open.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson separates a sufficient record for identifying evidentiary error from the full record needed to assess its effect. The Ninth Circuit decided no-error admissibility and did not reach harmlessness. That omission establishes neither harmlessness nor prejudice. Kotteakos requires assessment of the error's influence, not simply whether other evidence could support guilt, and puts grave doubt against a harmlessness conclusion. Rule 52(a) preserves that inquiry; Olano's ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error does not govern this preserved objection.

The error found is nonconstitutional. Chapman is not substituted merely because the criminal trial involved a jury, and Roy's omitted personal-intent-element rule addresses a different defect. The lower court must assess properly available arguments on the actual whole record and determine the lawful effect on any affected convictions. **Precedent treatment:** Williamson's preserved nonconstitutional-error rule is applied unchanged; Kotteakos supplies the substantial-influence inquiry, Olano is distinguished at preservation, and Chapman and Roy remain confined to the constitutional errors they govern.

## Holdings

### I. An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison

**Holding and operative rule:** When an actually available, unconditional and legally adequate admission establishes the complete prior-conviction status element, that element is the Government's sole legitimate purpose for the evidence, and the name or nature of the conviction presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, Rule 403 requires exclusion of that name or nature. On this record, admitting the similar-assault judgment after rejecting the offered substitute on general prosecutorial-choice grounds was an abuse of discretion; the Ninth Circuit's no-error judgment is reversed.

The admission must establish a conviction qualifying under §922(g)(1) and §921(a)(20). The latter excludes Federal or State antitrust, unfair-trade, restraint-of-trade and similar business-regulation offenses, and State offenses classified as misdemeanors and punishable by two years or less. Conviction status follows the law of the jurisdiction of the proceedings. Expunged or set-aside convictions, pardoned convictions and convictions for which civil rights have been restored do not count, subject to the statute's qualification when the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No such exclusion is found applicable here; the offered instruction requires appropriate consensual clarification rather than an assumption that its original wording was flawless.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six direct joins to the complete evidentiary rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The prior judgment remains relevant under Rule 401. The question is whether its legitimate evidentiary value, assessed with actually available alternatives, justifies its unfair-prejudice danger. Huddleston recognizes Rule 403's contextual safeguards and attention to other proof. Review concerns the circumstances when the trial judge ruled, not hindsight.

The Government ordinarily may present convincing, coherent evidence instead of a defendant's preferred abstraction. Narrative integrity helps the jury understand the charged event and need not disappear whenever a fact is conceded. Here, however, the prior conviction supplies a detached legal status. An adequate admission supplies that complete proposition without reconstructing the earlier assault. Its name and nature add negligible legitimate force for the sole status purpose while inviting the jury to infer a disposition toward violence in a prosecution involving assault and firearms. General prosecutorial choice does not resolve that balance.

The rule does not let defendants stipulate away the narrative of a charged event, require acceptance of partial or qualified admissions, or exclude every prior-conviction record. A distinct permissible purpose requires its own lawful showing and Rule 403 balance. A sanitized judgment may also be considered if it supplies legally complete status without the impermissible prejudice. Status remains an element to be established through adequate evidence and instructions; it is not simply removed from the jury. The curable wording issue supplies no new waiver or independent affirmance ground, and the separate credibility-instruction history does not forfeit the preserved objection to the judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Huddleston's safeguards are applied to comparative status proof. Gaudin's requirement that the element be established remains intact; no general compulsory-stipulation doctrine is adopted.

### II. Harmlessness remains a separate whole-record inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The established preserved Rule 403 error is subject to Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos: the Government must establish, from the whole record, fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt defeats harmlessness. The case is remanded for that inquiry and lawful consequences for affected convictions; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, without a present finding that every count was affected.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins to the mandate and its governing standard. Stone-Zsela does not join the decision to leave the prejudice result open.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson separates a sufficient record for identifying evidentiary error from the full record needed to assess its effect. The Ninth Circuit decided no-error admissibility and did not reach harmlessness. That omission establishes neither harmlessness nor prejudice. Kotteakos requires assessment of the error's influence, not simply whether other evidence could support guilt, and puts grave doubt against a harmlessness conclusion. Rule 52(a) preserves that inquiry; Olano's ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error does not govern this preserved objection.

The error found is nonconstitutional. Chapman is not substituted merely because the criminal trial involved a jury, and Roy's omitted personal-intent-element rule addresses a different defect. Reversal of the erroneous appellate legal conclusion does not itself require unconditional conviction vacatur and retrial. The lower court must assess properly available arguments on the actual whole record and determine the lawful effect on any affected convictions. No new hearing, acquittal, automatic retrial or unsupported count-specific disposition is ordered.

**Precedent treatment:** Williamson's preserved nonconstitutional-error rule is applied unchanged; Kotteakos supplies the substantial-influence inquiry, Olano is distinguished at preservation, and Chapman and Roy remain confined to the constitutional errors they govern.

## Precedent Treatment

- Huddleston v. United States: applies contextual Rule 403 safeguards, including actually available evidentiary alternatives, while preserving legitimate purposes and ordinary narrative evidence.
- United States v. Gaudin: preserves the need to establish the status element through adequate evidence and jury instructions; an admission does not authorize deletion of an element.
- Williamson v. United States: applies its controlling separation of evidentiary error from whole-record prejudice and its Government-burden remand rule; the formal reversal here does not eliminate harmlessness.
- Kotteakos v. United States: applies whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, with grave doubt defeating harmlessness.
- United States v. Olano: distinguishes the ordinary defendant burden for forfeited error; no forfeiture of the preserved judgment objection is inferred.
- Chapman v. California and California v. Roy: retain their constitutional-error scope; neither supplies the standard for this ordinary Rule 403 violation.

## Law After Decision

Effective January 7, 1997, a legally complete available status admission must be considered in Rule 403's balance when a prior conviction's name or nature is offered solely for felon-status proof. Exclusion follows under the stated substantial-outweighing conditions; the conviction record is not rendered irrelevant and other permissible purposes remain distinct. The Court adds that controlling evidentiary application while applying Williamson's existing preserved-error remedy without change. It makes no present harmlessness finding. Stone-Zsela's mandatory new-trial position and the three-Justice no-abuse position are noncontrolling.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** He joins the Rule 403 holding and reversal because the comparison is between two means of proving the same complete legal status, not between proof and no proof. Ordinary prosecutorial narrative control has limited force for status detached from the charged episode. An unconditional, legally complete admission supplies that element, while identifying the similar assault adds no narrative link to present possession and creates acute propensity danger. He preserves the Government's ability to prove a distinct nonpropensity purpose under Rule 403 and rejects any general right to stipulate away event narrative or require acceptance of partial or qualified admissions.

He differs on remedy. In his view, the close resemblance to violent conduct and the unnecessary character information prevent the Government from showing harmlessness on this record. He would vacate the conviction and remand for a new trial rather than leave harmlessness open. At retrial the Government could use the adequate admission or another sanitized means for status; prior-offense details could be introduced only for a distinct lawful purpose satisfying Rule 403. His prejudice conclusion and mandatory new-trial remedy command no additional join and do not change the Court's five-Justice remand.

**Justice O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.** They would affirm because the authentic judgment directly proves a statutory element. A defendant's offer neither removes that element nor makes ordinary specific proof irrelevant; the Government retains its burden and its legitimate interest in persuasive evidence. Huddleston's safeguards and the Rule's substantial-outweighing requirement preserve meaningful trial discretion. They recognize the similar-assault propensity danger but would not find the name and basic nature of this qualifying conviction inadmissible as a matter of law merely because an admission proves the same legal status. They question the compulsory-substitution consequence and the sharp distinction between status proof and narrative proof, without treating relevance as conclusive admissibility or prosecutorial choice as immune from Rule 403.

They do not rely on the curable instruction wording as a new ground, and they do not treat the credibility instruction as waiver of the separate objection. Once the Court's error rule governs, they accept the ordinary Williamson/Kotteakos harmlessness process and oppose automatic retrial; that conditional remedy position is not a vote to find error or reverse. No omitted-element constitutional error or Roy substitute standard is claimed.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded for the Government-burden, whole-record harmlessness inquiry and lawful consequences for affected convictions. No immediate acquittal, unconditional retrial, replacement sentence, new hearing or finding that all counts were affected is ordered. If the established error requires relief, a new trial must follow for the affected conviction or convictions under ordinary procedure. Supreme Court merits review is complete; no retained-jurisdiction return is set.
