## Controlling law of the decision

### I. The showing does not justify modifying this continuing injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A party seeking relief from the prospective operation of an injunction under Rule 60(b)(5) must show a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable; any modification must respond suitably to that change. New expense, administrative difficulty, the availability of a cheaper constitutional arrangement or anticipated changes in the Court's membership or views do not by themselves satisfy that burden; the showing here establishes no qualifying change, so denial of relief was not an abuse of discretion.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by the other members of the five-Justice Court coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All five adopt this rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo permits adjustment to significant changed circumstances without demanding an unforeseeable catastrophe. Mere foreseeability does not bar relief. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a particular risk impose a heavy burden, and a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation is not erased merely because the Constitution would allow less; this injunction, however, implements an adjudicated constitutional rule rather than a negotiated promise. The City’s costs and reduced practical access deserve consideration. They show serious burdens of compliance, but do not establish a material change in the program, its relationship with the schools or the facts underlying Aguilar sufficient to make enforcement inequitable. The Court does not discount them simply because they were foreseeable.

The asserted legal change is also absent. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding. Rosenberger permitted controlled vendor financing of a private publication; Chabad and Pinette addressed private expression in public forums. Those distinctions do not remove the direct rule governing public instruction here. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to follow that rule until this Court changed it. A prediction that this Court might reconsider a precedent is not an intervening holding. The Court separately considers and rejects the present request to overrule Aguilar; it does not make the decree immune to a later qualifying motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo is applied to this continuing constitutional injunction with its actual conditions preserved. Rodriguez de Quijas is applied to lower-court obedience. Kiryas Joel, Zobrest, Rosenberger, Chabad and Pinette are distinguished at their actual scopes; none previously displaced Aguilar.

### II. Aguilar remains controlling for this public instructional program

**Holding and operative rule:** Aguilar and the pertinent public-instruction principles of Ball remain in force. The described Title I program is not removed from Aguilar's rule merely because eligible pupils are selected neutrally and public employees, supervision and secular materials are used inside religious schools; no sufficient legal basis for overruling that rule has been shown here.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by the same five-Justice Court coalition. This is a direct majority decision retaining precedent, not a synthesis of the separate concurrences.

**Controlling explanation:** The safeguards now emphasized were central to the original dispute. The record identifies no actual indoctrination, transfer of public money to the schools or religious control of public teachers. Aguilar nevertheless addressed the institutional relationship created by public instruction inside religious schools and the continuing monitoring required to keep it secular. The absence of reported proselytizing does not disprove that structural judgment. Public control is material, but it does not itself establish a new fact or legal reason requiring repudiation of the directly applicable decision.

The contrary argument presses the real costs and diminished access caused by off-site delivery and the difficulty of treating safeguards as a source of entanglement. Those objections do not establish that the rule cannot operate or that its factual premise has collapsed. Wolman preserves properly administered neutral-site services, and the injunction has been administered through off-premises arrangements. The private-expression cases neither decide the public-instruction question nor silently replace Lemon. Ball's Shared Time program used full-time public teachers; its Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers teaching their pupils under their principals. Neither judgment is overruled.

This decision adopts no comprehensive replacement for establishment law and no categorical prohibition on every secular service delivered at a religious institution. Transportation, health, meals, disability services, private-directed benefits and a materially different separated-instruction program retain their own governing questions. Witters and Mueller remain within their existing limits. No particular alternative program is approved.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar and Ball are retained; Wolman supplies the surviving neutral-site alternative. Lemon is neither revised nor replaced. Witters and Mueller retain their bounded benefit holdings; the forum and printing-benefit precedents remain intact.

### Issue 1: Relief from the continuing Aguilar injunction

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Rule 60(b)(5) question first because it decides the judgment. The denial of relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, and the question is whether the City carried its burden of showing a significant change in fact or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable. Stone does not decide whether a differently designed program would be valid, because this motion fails at the threshold of change.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Rule 60(b)(5) permits relief from the prospective effect of a decree on a significant change in circumstances, as the Court held in Rufo. Under Rufo, actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden; mere foreseeability does not bar relief, and an unforeseeable catastrophe is not required. Rufo's protection of voluntarily accepted consent obligations does not convert this adjudicated injunction into a bargain. Increased expense and practical access difficulties must be assessed for significant change and continued inequity; the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice. A change in law must be an actual change in the rule that governs, not tension between later decisions and an earlier one. Aguilar was binding on the lower courts and remains the controlling rule: Kiryas Joel expressly left Aguilar and Ball in place, Zobrest produced no Establishment Clause merits holding, and Rosenberger and Chabad decide program-specific questions of access and attribution while retaining Lemon. Tension is not change. Only this Court may overrule its decisions, and it should do so only for a legal reason under the law of precedent, not because the motion invites reconsideration.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The City did not establish a significant change in fact or law. The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying relief, and the Second Circuit correctly affirmed.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The decree governs the City's future administration, so Rule 60(b)(5) applies, and the burden was the City's. The cost of mobile units and off-site service, and the reduced practical access that followed, are relevant to the motion. Stone does not reject them merely because they were foreseeable. He concludes that the showing identifies the burden of operating under the injunction but does not establish a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable. Nor does the record identify a material change in the program's design, the schools' relationship to it, or the facts on which Aguilar rested. The claim that later opinions undermine Aguilar requires this Court to consider its continued authority under the law of precedent; it does not establish that the lower courts were already free to disregard it. A change in the Court's expected vote is not a change in law, and the opinion says so plainly. The lower courts did what Rodriguez de Quijas required.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide that a differently designed program is valid or invalid and does not make the decree immune from modification on a qualifying showing. This Court may reconsider Aguilar in a proper vehicle without requiring another decision already to have displaced it; Stone addresses and rejects the present request for reconsideration in Issue 2.

### Issue 2: Whether Aguilar and Ball should be retained

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the question because it was granted and because the City's claim of changed law depends on it. He decides it only as far as that claim requires: whether a legal reason justifies overruling.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Aguilar and Ball are retained. Stone changes precedent only for a stated legal reason: conflict with superior text or structure, internal contradiction, unworkability, a collapsed factual premise, or systematic defeat of the doctrine's own protection. None is shown. First, the monitoring that Aguilar found entangling follows from placing public employees inside religious classrooms, and delivery at neutral sites avoids both that monitoring and the symbolic union, so the rule can be satisfied and has been for years; Wolman preserves properly administered neutral-site services. Second, Aguilar did not rest on proof of actual indoctrination, so the absence of such a finding does not disprove the structural risk it identified. Third, the Funding Wall permits secular instruction at a religious institution when the funded work is separated from religious activity in governance, personnel, content and accounts, meets the same regulation as other recipients, and allows no diversion. Onsite delivery or incidental cost savings do not by themselves establish a breach. Neutral eligibility and public personnel are relevant but do not alone resolve every separation question. Stone retains Aguilar's directly applicable rule here because he finds no sufficient legal reason to displace its treatment of this program; he does not add a categorical Funding Wall rule against all onsite secular instruction. Fourth, the actual costs, inefficiency and reduced access bear on the asserted unworkability and continued equity. Stone concludes that the showing establishes serious administrative burdens, but not failure of the rule's operation or a collapsed factual premise sufficient to justify the requested overruling.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Aguilar and Ball remain good law. A publicly staffed and supervised Title I program delivered inside religious schools is not taken outside their rule because its eligibility criteria are neutral and its teachers are public employees.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The children are selected by educational need, and the teachers remain public employees using public materials. The record identifies no finding of actual indoctrination, a transfer of public funds to the schools, or religious control of those teachers. Stone does not turn the absence of such findings into proof that the schools' own instruction was displaced or that fiscal diversion occurred. He nonetheless regards the monitoring and institutional relationship addressed by Aguilar as sufficient reasons to retain its rule for this program: public staffing and secular materials do not, in his view, supply the legal reason required to overrule that structural judgment. This conclusion applies Aguilar; it is not an independent factual finding that every onsite secular service is inseparable from religion. Transportation, health services, meals, publicly controlled disability services and genuinely separated secular instruction remain subject to their own governing rules.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not forbid all public benefit that reaches religious-school settings. Witters and Mueller retain their actual pre-divergence holdings. Stone joined Rosenberger's actual vendor-funded private-publication holding; he neither recasts it as access without financing nor derives a ban on all incidental institutional cost savings from it. ### Competing paths and controlling scope available for assembly

The reversal path permits supplementary remedial services for individually eligible children selected under neutral public criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision using public secular materials. The service may not become religious instruction, team teaching or part of the religious school's worship; actual public control, nondiversion and the supplementary character remain material. Location alone does not prove indoctrination, endorsement or excessive entanglement, and ordinary supervision enforcing those restrictions is not automatically excessive. Neither on-site delivery nor a funding entitlement is commanded. This is sufficient common ground for O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas without adopting the historical opinion's entire reorganization of Lemon.

The strongest objection is that these safeguards already existed: no new factual transformation and no local Zobrest merits decision eroded the older rule. The reversal Justices therefore must defend the correctness and continuing practical consequences of the present constitutional change openly. O'Connor's original dissents identify the public-control/monitoring problem directly; Kennedy's Kiryas Joel reservation and subsequent actual program analysis support its reconsideration; Scalia and Thomas's own positions independently support the same narrower result. ## Holdings

### I. The showing does not justify modifying this continuing injunction

**Holding and operative rule:** A party seeking relief from the prospective operation of an injunction under Rule 60(b)(5) must show a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable; any modification must respond suitably to that change. New expense, administrative difficulty, the availability of a cheaper constitutional arrangement or anticipated changes in the Court's membership or views do not by themselves satisfy that burden; the showing here establishes no qualifying change, so denial of relief was not an abuse of discretion.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by the other members of the five-Justice Court coalition identified in Opinion Topology. All five adopt this rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo permits adjustment to significant changed circumstances without demanding an unforeseeable catastrophe. Mere foreseeability does not bar relief. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a particular risk impose a heavy burden, and a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation is not erased merely because the Constitution would allow less; this injunction, however, implements an adjudicated constitutional rule rather than a negotiated promise. The City’s costs and reduced practical access deserve consideration. They show serious burdens of compliance, but do not establish a material change in the program, its relationship with the schools or the facts underlying Aguilar sufficient to make enforcement inequitable. The Court does not discount them simply because they were foreseeable.

The asserted legal change is also absent. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding. Rosenberger permitted controlled vendor financing of a private publication; Chabad and Pinette addressed private expression in public forums. Those distinctions do not remove the direct rule governing public instruction here. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to follow that rule until this Court changed it. A prediction that this Court might reconsider a precedent is not an intervening holding. The Court separately considers and rejects the present request to overrule Aguilar; it does not make the decree immune to a later qualifying motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo is applied to this continuing constitutional injunction with its actual conditions preserved. Rodriguez de Quijas is applied to lower-court obedience. Kiryas Joel, Zobrest, Rosenberger, Chabad and Pinette are distinguished at their actual scopes; none previously displaced Aguilar.

### II. Aguilar remains controlling for this public instructional program

**Holding and operative rule:** Aguilar and the pertinent public-instruction principles of Ball remain in force. The described Title I program is not removed from Aguilar's rule merely because eligible pupils are selected neutrally and public employees, supervision and secular materials are used inside religious schools; no sufficient legal basis for overruling that rule has been shown here.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by the same five-Justice Court coalition. This is a direct majority decision retaining precedent, not a synthesis of the separate concurrences.

**Controlling explanation:** The safeguards now emphasized were central to the original dispute. The record identifies no actual indoctrination, transfer of public money to the schools or religious control of public teachers. Aguilar nevertheless addressed the institutional relationship created by public instruction inside religious schools and the continuing monitoring required to keep it secular. The absence of reported proselytizing does not disprove that structural judgment. Public control is material, but it does not itself establish a new fact or legal reason requiring repudiation of the directly applicable decision.

The contrary argument presses the real costs and diminished access caused by off-site delivery and the difficulty of treating safeguards as a source of entanglement. Those objections do not establish that the rule cannot operate or that its factual premise has collapsed. Wolman preserves properly administered neutral-site services, and the injunction has been administered through off-premises arrangements. The private-expression cases neither decide the public-instruction question nor silently replace Lemon. Ball's Shared Time program used full-time public teachers; its Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers teaching their pupils under their principals. Neither judgment is overruled.

This decision adopts no comprehensive replacement for establishment law and no categorical prohibition on every secular service delivered at a religious institution. Transportation, health, meals, disability services, private-directed benefits and a materially different separated-instruction program retain their own governing questions. Witters and Mueller remain within their existing limits. No particular alternative program is approved.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar and Ball are retained; Wolman supplies the surviving neutral-site alternative. Lemon is neither revised nor replaced. Witters and Mueller retain their bounded benefit holdings; the forum and printing-benefit precedents remain intact.

## Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, concurring.** Browder distinguishes an appeal from denial of Rule 60 relief from an appeal reopening the underlying judgment. These Justices would independently refuse to use this modification appeal to generate the very change in law that would supposedly justify reopening the Court's original constitutional adjudication. The lower courts followed still-binding law. This Court's ability to reconsider precedent in a proper case does not make Rule 60 an indefinite rehearing mechanism. Stone-Zsela does not join that additional ground.

**Souter, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, concurring.** Direct public teachers operating inside religious schools provide substantial educational support to those institutions, a relationship different from private speakers using a public forum or a privately edited publication receiving a controlled printing benefit. Neutral pupil selection and public supervision do not resolve every question of institutional subsidy, apparent affiliation and sustained contact. Aguilar and Ball addressed those structural concerns without requiring proof of actual religious instruction. These Justices would retain that affirmative substantive distinction in addition to the Court's grounds. Breyer does not join this additional account.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court and finds no conflict with superior text or structure, internal contradiction, unworkability, collapsed factual premise or systematic defeat of a doctrine's own protection sufficient to justify the requested overruling. The costs and reduced access are serious, but they have not established such a defect. His proposed Funding Wall would permit secular work at a religious institution if governance, personnel, content and accounts separate it from religious activity, ordinary regulation applies and diversion is prevented. On-site delivery or incidental institutional savings alone would not defeat that proposal. He does not treat neutral eligibility and public teachers as resolving every separation question, or adopt a categorical prohibition on all on-site secular services. That proposal is not the Court's test and approves no different program here.

**O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would expressly change the constitutional rule now and apply that ruling to prospective enforcement. They do not say that Zobrest already made the change or that the lower courts could anticipate it. Public employees may provide supplementary secular remedial services to neutrally selected eligible children on religious-school premises when public authorities control personnel, supervision, selection and materials, prevent diversion, and prohibit religious participation and cooperative religious teaching. Location does not alone establish religious inculcation, and ordinary supervision enforcing those restrictions is not necessarily excessive entanglement. Her Aguilar dissent and Ball distinction supply the specific objection to the original rule. The dissent would dissolve the on-premises prohibition for this controlled program, without ordering on-site services, permitting unrestricted aid or overruling Ball's separate Community Education result.
