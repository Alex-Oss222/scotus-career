## Controlling law of the decision

All three holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct supports at its stated level of generality. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

### I. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding and operative rule:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish forbidden religious advancement or excessive entanglement; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon supplies the governing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those protections remain essential to the permission recognized today. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

The strongest contrary argument is that these safeguards already existed when Aguilar was decided. The Court does not claim a newly discovered factual transformation. It corrects the legal inference openly because that inference confuses measures preserving secular delivery with religious sponsorship and institutional control. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. Lemon otherwise remains as it stood; the Court adopts no comprehensive reorganization of its method.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman remains the governing general test outside the decided correction.

### II. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding and operative rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

### III. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reconsider that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

## Published noncontrolling positions and continuity

### Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring; no other Justice joins

The Chief Justice joins the Court's narrow holding as independently sufficient and would also update Lemon's general method. In his proposed single inquiry, a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the establishment claim on that ground; and a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives an inquiry into the attribution and function of the governmental act under ordinary procedure. Clear cases remain applications of that inquiry. Purpose, effect and institutional relationships become evidence within it rather than freestanding prongs or a fallback test.

The residual inquiry identifies what government does: its message, event, supervisory act or expenditure. Financing turns on what government purchases or performs, without requiring that all the recipient's religious activity become government speech. Religious identity, private-access authorization and location matter in context but are not individually decisive. Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit adherence through public authority, govern theology, or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict scrutiny. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoring private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to a defined accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing ban.

Once prohibited governmental conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary; a secular aim cannot justify religious means. A provider's religious motivation does not make secular work devotional. Repeated monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, while civil governance of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden. Real operational separation and reliable accountability are required for funded work, but separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel are not invariably necessary. Shared structures neither excuse devotional financing nor automatically defeat a severable secular service.

For the Chief Justice, Aguilar's error is an internal legal defect: it condemns safeguards preserving secular services without identifying religious sponsorship, devotional financing or theological control. The cost of compliance matters to implementation, not constitutional validity. The record's public-control features support relief; absence of an adverse finding is not proof that every defect has been disproved, and no court should demand a guarantee against all future misconduct.

His broader view that parental selection alone cannot legitimate public purchase of religious formation decides no absent voucher program. Witters and Mueller retain their actual holdings pending a case presenting their scope or validity; he acknowledges that his broader view may require reconsideration then. Rosenberger's vendor-funded religious publication remains financing permissible on its actual terms, and religious editorial viewpoint is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Pinette and Chabad retain their actual rules without an automatic attribution rule for central governmental locations; Kiryas Joel's civil-authority restrictions remain. Private speech, free exercise, religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. The proposed general update has no controlling force.

### Justice Souter, dissenting

Stevens and Ginsburg join the dissent in full. Breyer joins Part II only.

In Part I, Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg would retain Aguilar's and Ball's applicable substantive rules. Public employees conducting education within a religious school's ordinary instructional setting supply governmental educational support, not independently attributable private speech. Public staffing, neutral eligibility and safeguards do not alone resolve the institutional subsidy and entanglement concerns. Rosenberger's private publication and the private-forum decisions therefore do not decide this case. These Justices do not find proved proselytizing and do not require it for their structural objection. Breyer does not join this affirmative direct-aid rationale.

In Part II, all four dissenters would adhere to the direct precedents. The safeguards and anticipated costs did not newly arise. Kiryas Joel preserved Aguilar and Ball, Zobrest supplied no establishment merits holding, and the bounded expression decisions did not erode the public-instruction rule sufficiently to justify its abandonment. They would affirm denial of relief and preserve the injunction.

### Justice Ginsburg, dissenting; joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer

The four dissenters would also affirm on an independent procedural ground. Browder limits an appeal from denial of Rule 60 relief; it does not reopen the original judgment for indefinite rehearing. The district court followed still-binding Aguilar, and Rufo requires a qualifying change rather than an appeal whose own desired outcome creates the prerequisite for reopening. This Court may reconsider its precedents in a proper case, but that power does not establish this vehicle's propriety. Their position does not turn an adjudicated injunction into a negotiated consent promise, deny Rule 60 relief after an actual qualifying change, or treat costs alone as sufficient. This writing addresses the route to relief independently of the substantive merits disagreement.

The only procedural continuation is implementation of the mandate in these two consolidated dockets. No roster, allotment or institutional-practice change occurs. The public merits judgment ends Supreme Court review while district-court modification remains to be performed. The Court preserves existing statutory service duties without choosing placements, delivery sites or a replacement program. No connected later event or unpresented merits consequence is admitted.

## Public Projection

## Event

Agostini v. Felton, No. 96-552, consolidated with Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, No. 96-553. October Term 1996, chunk 11, inventory No. 127. Merits decision, June 23, 1997. Argued April 15, 1997, on writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit.

The Board and a new group of parents filed motions for Rule 60 relief in October and December 1995. On August 7, 1996, the Second Circuit affirmed denial of relief from the September 26, 1985 permanent injunction implementing Aguilar. The question under Rule 60(b)(5) is whether that continuing onsite prohibition should remain in light of the governing Establishment Clause law and the preserved request to reconsider Aguilar. The two dockets concern the same prospective injunction and share one decision. Public teachers provide supplementary secular Title I instruction to independently eligible children under public control; the injunction requires offsite delivery.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgments in both dockets and remands for tailored prospective relief from the onsite prohibition, overruling Aguilar's categorical rule and displacing the corresponding Ball Shared Time rationale at the scope stated below.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded in both consolidated dockets, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Second Circuit judgments affirming denial of relief are vacated. Proceedings return for a district-court order removing the injunction's onsite prohibition to the extent it rests on the categorical ground rejected today. The City must obey the operative decree until judicial modification. ## Holdings

All three holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct supports at its stated level of generality. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

### I. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding and operative rule:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish forbidden religious advancement or excessive entanglement; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon supplies the governing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those protections remain essential to the permission recognized today. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

The strongest contrary argument is that these safeguards already existed when Aguilar was decided. The Court does not claim a newly discovered factual transformation. It corrects the legal inference openly because that inference confuses measures preserving secular delivery with religious sponsorship and institutional control. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. Lemon otherwise remains as it stood; the Court adopts no comprehensive reorganization of its method.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman remains the governing general test outside the decided correction.

### II. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding and operative rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

### III. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reconsider that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

## Law After Decision

Effective June 23, 1997, Aguilar no longer supplies a categorical constitutional bar to the separately administered, safeguarded public Title I service described in Holding I, and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale has no contrary force at that scope. Lemon otherwise continues to govern through its existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiries. There is no newly adopted general test or new universal order of analysis.

For this continuing injunction, today's substantive change satisfies Rule 60(b)(5)'s legal-change requirement, and removal of the now-unsupported onsite prohibition must follow through a tailored district-court order. Rufo's other requirements and Rodriguez de Quijas's rule for lower courts remain. Permission to provide the service is distinct from a statutory duty or funding entitlement. Independently supported prohibitions on religious instruction, diversion or improper institutional control remain available; neither religious location nor absence of reported misconduct resolves every possible claim.

The Court does not decide vouchers, provider redemption, monuments, legislative prayer, a general accommodation rule, all forms of direct aid, or the compatibility of every future application of the Chief Justice's separate proposal with existing precedents. The specific holdings in Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force. No controlling rationale is drawn from the separate opinions under Marks.

## Separate Writings

### Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring; no other Justice joins

The Chief Justice joins the Court's narrow holding as independently sufficient and would also update Lemon's general method. In his proposed single inquiry, a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the establishment claim on that ground; and a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives an inquiry into the attribution and function of the governmental act under ordinary procedure. Clear cases remain applications of that inquiry. Purpose, effect and institutional relationships become evidence within it rather than freestanding prongs or a fallback test.

The residual inquiry identifies what government does: its message, event, supervisory act or expenditure. Financing turns on what government purchases or performs, without requiring that all the recipient's religious activity become government speech. Religious identity, private-access authorization and location matter in context but are not individually decisive. Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit adherence through public authority, govern theology, or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict scrutiny. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoring private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to a defined accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing ban.

Once prohibited governmental conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary; a secular aim cannot justify religious means. A provider's religious motivation does not make secular work devotional. Repeated monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, while civil governance of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden. Real operational separation and reliable accountability are required for funded work, but separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel are not invariably necessary. Shared structures neither excuse devotional financing nor automatically defeat a severable secular service.

For the Chief Justice, Aguilar's error is an internal legal defect: it condemns safeguards preserving secular services without identifying religious sponsorship, devotional financing or theological control. The cost of compliance matters to implementation, not constitutional validity. The record's public-control features support relief; absence of an adverse finding is not proof that every defect has been disproved, and no court should demand a guarantee against all future misconduct.

His broader view that parental selection alone cannot legitimate public purchase of religious formation decides no absent voucher program. Witters and Mueller retain their actual holdings pending a case presenting their scope or validity; he acknowledges that his broader view may require reconsideration then. Rosenberger's vendor-funded religious publication remains financing permissible on its actual terms, and religious editorial viewpoint is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Pinette and Chabad retain their actual rules without an automatic attribution rule for central governmental locations; Kiryas Joel's civil-authority restrictions remain. Private speech, free exercise, religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. The proposed general update has no controlling force.

### Justice Souter, dissenting

Stevens and Ginsburg join the dissent in full. Breyer joins Part II only.

In Part I, Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg would retain Aguilar's and Ball's applicable substantive rules. Public employees conducting education within a religious school's ordinary instructional setting supply governmental educational support, not independently attributable private speech. Public staffing, neutral eligibility and safeguards do not alone resolve the institutional subsidy and entanglement concerns. Rosenberger's private publication and the private-forum decisions therefore do not decide this case. These Justices do not find proved proselytizing and do not require it for their structural objection. Breyer does not join this affirmative direct-aid rationale.

In Part II, all four dissenters would adhere to the direct precedents. The safeguards and anticipated costs did not newly arise. Kiryas Joel preserved Aguilar and Ball, Zobrest supplied no establishment merits holding, and the bounded expression decisions did not erode the public-instruction rule sufficiently to justify its abandonment. They would affirm denial of relief and preserve the injunction.

### Justice Ginsburg, dissenting; joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer

The four dissenters would also affirm on an independent procedural ground. Browder limits an appeal from denial of Rule 60 relief; it does not reopen the original judgment for indefinite rehearing. The district court followed still-binding Aguilar, and Rufo requires a qualifying change rather than an appeal whose own desired outcome creates the prerequisite for reopening. This Court may reconsider its precedents in a proper case, but that power does not establish this vehicle's propriety. Their position does not turn an adjudicated injunction into a negotiated consent promise, deny Rule 60 relief after an actual qualifying change, or treat costs alone as sufficient. This writing addresses the route to relief independently of the substantive merits disagreement.
