## Holdings

### I. The felony-drug category cannot replace the required execution inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not permit officers to dispense with announcement solely because a warrant seeks evidence of felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver. A valid search warrant authorizes the search but does not itself establish that an unannounced forcible entry is reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine in Parts I, II and IV; Part III has the distinct seven-Justice coalition specified below.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson v. Arkansas makes announcement of identity and authority part of reasonable dwelling entry while preserving supported exceptions. Wisconsin's category assumes danger and threatened destruction in every included case despite differences in occupants, evidence and execution. General associations among drugs, guns and easy disposal cannot do the work of a particular justification. Announcement protects voluntary admission, property, privacy and occupant safety even when officers hold a warrant. Buie rejects an automatic offense premise for a broader protective sweep; its permission to inspect immediately adjoining attack spaces rests on a different immediate risk. Maryland v. Wilson likewise permits a limited passenger-exit direction through its specific safety and incremental-intrusion comparison, not a general license for surprise dwelling entry. The Court therefore rejects this category without declaring every categorical Fourth Amendment rule invalid. Chandler's attention to the actual justification in its different testing context is consistent with, but does not enlarge, this holding.

### II. Reasonable suspicion governs a supported execution exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers must identify particular circumstances and rational inferences supporting reasonable suspicion that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including by allowing destruction of evidence. The threshold is below probable cause but requires more than the offense label or an unsupported hunch, and the relevant facts must be known when the challenged force begins.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson preserves announcement exceptions but leaves their precise quantum open. Buie's use of articulable suspicion for a limited protective step supports a practical threshold by analogy: execution of an independently authorized search may require a prompt response without awaiting probable cause of imminent destruction. This rule supplies no authority to search without a warrant or another lawful basis and does not transfer every protective-sweep rule to evidence searches. For the destruction branch, suspicion must connect delay for announcement and voluntary admission to a threat to preservation of the evidence. Officers need not hear destruction or wait until evidence is lost. A drug label or unsupported general weapons claim remains insufficient, and later discovery or flight cannot retroactively justify earlier force. The standard applies to the identified execution exceptions; the Court fixes no universal waiting time and does not equate seeing a uniform with knowing the officers' purpose. The independent useless-gesture contention need not be decided here.

### III. Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles

**Holding and operative rule:** In reviewing this execution under the reasonable-suspicion standard, the historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, with grounded experience given real weight; the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of those facts is independently assessed. That allocation permits review of the supported record without manufacturing new facts.

**Authority:** Breyer, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven direct joins. Scalia withholds this method; Stone-Zsela joins the substantive holdings and application without an additional position on it.

**Controlling explanation:** Ornelas separates historical factfinding from ultimate reasonable suspicion in its stop and warrantless-search setting. The same functional distinction applies here: the trial court decides what officers observed and whose testimony to credit, while the constitutional execution threshold remains a legal question. Ornelas did not previously decide every warrant-execution question; this is its reasoned application to the present one. Robinette demonstrates that correcting a general rule does not invariably decide its application or require remand. Here the original hearing already supplies the material observations and credibility determination. The Court respects those findings while testing their constitutional significance, and need not supplement them with assumed purpose knowledge or later events.

### IV. The credited contemporaneous facts justified this entry

**Holding and operative rule:** The credited look toward a nearby uniformed officer and surprised reaction, combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, supplied particularized reasonable suspicion of destruction risk before the first attempt to enter; the subsequent rapid closure and locking strengthened that justification for the later force. The entry was therefore reasonable despite the invalid categorical rule and the judge's advance refusal of no-knock authorization.

**Controlling explanation:** Pharo's maintenance ruse brought Richards to the door. The trial judge rejected the material portion of Richards's contrary account and inferred awareness of the uniformed officer from the credited observations. Recognition is an inference on disputed evidence, not a concession. The look toward the uniform and surprise preceded the first attempted entry and supported the inference that the ruse had exposed the police; rapid closure and locking strengthened it for later force. Combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, these facts supplied more than a general drug assumption. Closing against a supposed late-night maintenance intruder is a possible innocent account, but it does not eliminate reasonable suspicion from the credited police-recognition response. Neither intended destruction nor certainty of recognition had to be proved. Identity was shouted two or three seconds after closure, possibly while force was already underway; purpose was recalled as announced when the door opened. A belated announcement cannot cure prior unlawful force, but a destruction exception already justified this execution. Later window flight, drugs found above ceiling tiles, unproved destruction noises and an unproved weapon play no part in supplying pre-force justification. The original hearing suffices without deciding the objection to supplemental testimony. Advance refusal required attention to new facts, not disregard of the judge's ruling on the same general premise; the doorway developments supplied those new facts. The ruse was not consent to forced entry.

The Court does not decide exclusion after a violation, an independently sufficient futility ground, the cross-petition objection concerning the alternative remedy argument, or a general doctrine for every police-created exigency. A valid warrant alone is not declared an independent evidentiary source or proof of inevitable lawful discovery.

## Precedent Treatment

- Wilson v. Arkansas is applied to reject the offense category and given a reasonable-suspicion execution threshold; its constitutional exclusion question remains open.
- Maryland v. Buie supplies a practicable articulable-suspicion analogy and distinguishes a justified limited category from an automatic offense premise for a greater intrusion.
- Ornelas is applied by functional analogy to the review allocation in Part III, with the existing factual protections intact.
- Ohio v. Robinette supports review of interwoven federal/state reasoning and separates correction of a rule from application and remedy; it imposes no obligatory remand on an adequate record.
- Michigan v. Long supplies the independent-state-ground inquiry without displacing genuinely independent Wisconsin law.
- Maryland v. Wilson retains its limited passenger-exit holding, distinguished from forcible dwelling entry.
- Chandler retains its actual-justification analysis in the different bodily-testing setting and supplies no universal prohibition of categories.

## Law After Decision

Wisconsin may no longer rely on this felony-drug category alone. Unannounced execution requires reviewable reasonable suspicion grounded in the particular circumstances, and the time of the challenged force controls the justification. The Court applies a distinct factual/legal appellate allocation by seven direct joins. The existing conviction stands on the destruction-risk ground; no new constitutional exclusion rule, automatic valid-warrant immunity or universal waiting period follows.
