## Controlling holdings

### I. Section 510 includes prospective welfare-plan rights

**Holding and operative rule:** ERISA §510 protects against a covered discharge or other prohibited action undertaken for the purpose of interfering with attainment of a right a participant may become entitled to under an employee welfare benefit plan, even though the benefit is not subject to ERISA's statutory vesting requirements. Lawful authority to amend or terminate a welfare plan does not itself authorize interference through conduct forbidden by §510.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt this statutory coverage rule and its limits, notwithstanding their different formal dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that welfare benefits are within the attainment clause's protection. ERISA defines employee benefit plans to include welfare and pension plans. Section 510 addresses future attainment, while Congress elsewhere used narrower pension and nonforfeitable-benefit terms. Section 1051(1) exempts welfare plans from the participation and vesting part; it does not create an additional exemption from §510. The separate exercise-of-right and prospective-attainment clauses must retain their distinct work.

Respondents' strongest objection is that protection of amendable benefits would vest them indirectly and obstruct ordinary cost-saving restructuring. But a lawful revision of plan terms differs from a covered employment action undertaken for the prohibited purpose. Curtiss-Wright preserves amendment authority while requiring its lawful exercise. Lockheed separates sponsor design from implementation and independent statutory commands. Those decisions do not authorize evasion of plan procedures through a prohibited discharge. Enforcing §510's own conduct and purpose conditions answers the objection without erasing an entire defined category of plans.

The employees allege that the transfer of cargo-handling work and their discharge were designed to avoid contributions and minimize benefit payments. Those allegations must not be treated as findings. The absence of statutory vesting cannot alone sustain dismissal of their welfare theory. This holding does not promise continued employment, freeze plan terms, establish intent from cost reduction or settle every meaning of attainment. In particular, the separate argument that employees already eligible for benefits have already attained the relevant right remains open. Actual eligibility, entitlement, action, purpose, causation and authorized relief require their own consideration.

### II. The categorical dismissal is vacated for the remaining statutory inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's welfare-interference dismissal is vacated because its categorical nonvesting ground is erroneous, and the case is remanded for consideration of the properly remaining grounds. Plaintiffs must establish the applicable participant or beneficiary status, relevant plan right, prohibited action, interference purpose, causation and remedy authorized through §510's reference to §502; this decision establishes no violation or automatic recovery.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct joins support this vacatur and its scope. Stone-Zsela does not join Part II and would reverse the welfare dismissal with the reinstatement direction stated in his separate opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals dismissed the welfare theory because the benefits did not vest. Rejection of that ground does not decide every alternative argument the lower courts have not addressed. Whether a participant already eligible for the particular welfare benefit can invoke interference with attainment requires first consideration below. So do properly preserved questions concerning specific intent, eligibility, causation, mixed motives or business justification, proper defendants and relief. The Court adopts no new business-necessity test or presumption that a benefits-reducing transfer is unlawful.

Varity's function-specific fiduciary holdings and qualified individual-equity rule do not resolve this independent §510 theory. Mertens preserves the limits of the remedy actually authorized by §502; statutory coverage does not itself authorize compensatory damages. No employer or administrator status automatically establishes liability, and the circuit's nonemployer-conspiracy footnote is not adopted as a new holding of this Court.

The pension claim's reinstatement, Railroad Retirement Act exclusion, association-standing ruling, conditional request for individual class representatives, FELA and Rule 23 rulings, and fee-sanction remand are not independently reopened. The limitations contention declined below because it was first raised at appellate argument is not revived. These boundaries follow the granted welfare question; they do not affirm or reverse unrelated lower rulings by implication.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Curtiss-Wright Corp. v. Schoonejongen:** Preserves lawful amendment authority and its actual procedural exercise; that power supplies no immunity for independently prohibited discharge.
- **Lockheed Corp. v. Spink:** Applies the distinction between sponsor design and independent statutory obligations without classifying every business decision as fiduciary administration.
- **Varity Corp. v. Howe:** Its fiduciary and qualified individual-equity holdings remain separate; neither those holdings nor the disagreement about them eliminates §510's express route.
- **Mertens v. Hewitt Associates:** Preserves the equitable-category limit where §502(a)(3) relief is sought; coverage creates no compensatory-damages authorization.
- **Ingersoll-Rand Co. v. McClendon:** Its pension-interference setting remains intact; it did not already decide the disputed welfare-plan coverage question.
