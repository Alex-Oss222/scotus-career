## Holdings

### I. The stopped business presents a concrete and ripe challenge

**Holding and operative rule:** A business presents a concrete, ripe pre-enforcement challenge when a criminal statute directly reaches its existing activity, it has stopped that activity but intends to resume, serious penalties create a sufficiently real enforcement risk, and the requested relief would remove that present federal constraint. Those conditions are met here; neither an actual prosecution nor resolution of every independent state-law restriction is required.

**Authority:** Kennedy's unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies all three holdings at their stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Abbott Laboratories supplies the inquiry into fitness for judicial decision and hardship from withholding review. Pic-A-State stopped the very interstate procurement operation the amendment targeted; its reported intent to resume and exposure to substantial criminal sanctions establish a present injury. Bennett supplies personal injury, traceability and likely redress at the litigation stage, not automatic standing for every economically affected business. The Government's statement that prosecution is unlikely bears on risk but does not repudiate enforcement. Relief would remove an actual federal barrier even if Pennsylvania law separately remained relevant. The predominantly legal commerce question needs no prosecution to sharpen it, while withholding review would require continued cessation or exposure to punishment. The absence of a disavowal is not sufficient by itself in every case. Younger supplies no abstention ground where no state prosecution is pending. The prospective constitutional challenge against the enforcing Attorney General is a proper channel; standing does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

### II. Congress may prohibit the actual interstate procurement transmission

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit a procurement business's knowing interstate or foreign transmission of information to obtain, for a person in one State, a ticket, chance, share or interest in a lottery, gift enterprise or similar scheme conducted by another State. The challenged commercial transmission is interstate commerce itself even when the physical ticket remains in the conducting State; the prohibition remains subject to the agreement exception and separate §1307 exemptions stated below.

**Controlling explanation:** Champion v. Ames establishes congressional power to prohibit interstate commerce in lottery interests; Gibbons recognizes interstate commercial intercourse beyond the movement of tangible paper. Pic-A-State's computer communications accomplish the economic procurement transaction across state lines. The amendment closes that commercial route around the existing physical-ticket prohibition. Robertson confirms that an actual interstate business operation needs no independently substantial effect from each transaction. Lopez requires the regulated class's connection to commerce among the States to be identified before aggregation or substantiality performs constitutional work, and it rejects a remote chain from local possession through social costs; it does not displace direct regulation of interstate commerce itself, where no aggregation or substantial-effects analysis is required. Harris, Hale and American Life League likewise preserve genuine economic-class and direct-market grounds. Here the enacted knowing-transmission and procurement-business elements themselves supply the connection, without a judicially added element or an aggregation holding. Hodel permits rational means within that established power: closing the procurement gap and accommodating different state lottery choices meet that requirement. Revenue preservation alone would not establish federal power over local conduct. Congress need not prohibit every lottery, prove a particular revenue loss, or make transaction-specific findings to regulate this interstate business.

### III. The agreement exception supplies bounded congressional authorization

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1301 permits the otherwise covered procurement business when an agreement between the States in question or appropriate authorities of those States permits it. That express federal choice removes dormant Commerce Clause restraints on the particular state choices it authorizes, while leaving independent constitutional restraints intact.

**Controlling explanation:** Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin recognizes congressional power to authorize state burdens that the dormant Commerce Clause would otherwise forbid, and Northeast Bancorp requires the authorization to be clear. The agreement condition states the permitted category expressly. It accommodates state choices within Congress's independently valid regulation of interstate procurement; state permission for the underlying lottery does not displace the federal condition. Tracy separated ordinary economic rationality from discrimination analysis and reserved congressional authorization, so it does not supply a broader permission here. No qualifying agreement is established for Pic-A-State. The Court neither validates every Pennsylvania lottery restriction nor transfers unlimited federal legislative power to the States. The holding removes only the dormant implication and decides no independent First Amendment, equal-protection or nondelegation claim.

### Coverage, distinct exceptions, and questions not reached

The procurement clause requires a person engaged in that business, a customer in one State, a covered scheme conducted by another State, and knowing interstate or foreign transmission for procurement. It does not prohibit every lottery communication or wholly local purchase. The separate physical-carriage and receipt clauses remain distinct. The penalty is a fine under title 18, imprisonment for no more than two years, or both.

Section 1307(a)(1) separately exempts advertisements, prize lists or other information about a state-authorized, state-conducted lottery when contained in a publication published in that State or another lottery State, or broadcast by a station licensed in such a State. Section 1307(a)(2) exempts such information about other schemes authorized or not prohibited in the conducting State when conducted by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or as a clearly occasional and ancillary commercial promotional activity. Section 1307(b) separately exempts transportation or mailing of equipment, tickets or materials concerning a state-authorized lottery to addresses within its conducting State, and materials designed for an authorized foreign lottery to addressees within that foreign country. State includes the States, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and United States territories or possessions; foreign country includes an empire, country, dominion, colony, protectorate or subdivision other than the United States and its territories or possessions. The subsection-(b) lottery definition concerns pooled ticket-or-chance proceeds allocated by chance and excludes sporting-event bets or wagers. A nonprofit is an organization that would qualify as tax exempt under Internal Revenue Code §501. No fact places this business within an exemption; these provisions supply no blanket exemption for every lawful state lottery. No rule governs hypothetical wholly local transmissions or validates an interstate element unrelated to the commercial transaction.

## Law After Decision

The Interstate Wagering Amendment remains enforceable against covered interstate procurement businesses, subject to its agreement exception and §1307's different exemptions. Actual interstate commercial communications can support Congress's power without an aggregation theory or physical movement of the purchased paper. Congress's express authorization removes the dormant Commerce Clause objection only within the authorized category. The Court does not decide ordinary local lottery administration or independent constitutional claims.
