## Holdings

The Court’s opinion is directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. That seven-Justice coalition controls both holdings; no fractured-opinion synthesis is necessary.

#### Income-funded expenses do not invariably require reduction

**Holding and operative rule:** Payment of administration expenses from postmortem income attributable to a marital bequest does not alone require a dollar-for-dollar reduction of the estate-tax deduction. Under then-effective Treasury Regulation §20.2056(b)-4(a), valuation must account for a material limitation on the spouse’s income rights; the actual effect on the qualifying beneficial interest, rather than the allocation label alone, governs.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2056 concerns the qualifying property interest, and §2056(b)(4)(B) requires account of encumbrances without prescribing the Commissioner’s universal arithmetic here. The regulation’s net-value direction and material-limitation qualification operate together. Its administration-income example says the rule may apply; it makes neither every payment automatically dispositive nor all income-funded expenses exempt. Lawful allocation under an instrument and Georgia law cannot alone establish immateriality, and exclusion of actual postmortem income from the gross estate does not resolve the effect on the beneficial interest.

Thomas Jefferson University and Auer give controlling weight to the responsible agency’s fair and considered interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. Another reasonable reading would not by itself defeat the Commissioner. Here the asserted inevitable reduction eliminates the material-limitation inquiry required by the qualified text. Smiley preserves statutory boundaries and reasoned interpretive authority but supplies no tax measurement formula. Revenue Ruling 93-48’s treatment of deferred-tax interest informs the comparison without expressly deciding every administration expense or wholly withdrawing Revenue Ruling 73-98.

The Court does not decide whether every pertinent valuation fact must have been predictable at death, whether later events may enter valuation, or what complete calculation method applies. Expenses paid from qualifying principal retain their separate reduction treatment. The charitable deduction receives the same disposition because the parties treat this expense issue alike, not because §§2055 and 2056 or their regulations have identical terms.

#### The actual burden and preserved theory require affirmance

**Holding and operative rule:** For this administration-expense adjustment omitted from the original deficiency notice, Tax Court Rule 142(a) placed the burden on the Commissioner. Having pursued categorical reduction without offering and maintaining a quantitative-materiality ground for upsetting the Tax Court’s finding, the Commissioner has established no basis for reversal, and the Court does not reopen proof to supply that alternative theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The allocation of the burden is particular to this adjustment; it is not a general reversal of the taxpayer’s ordinary burden. The Tax Court found no material limitation on the beneficial interests, and the Commissioner’s legal theory sought to make the payment itself conclusive. Once that theory fails, a court cannot manufacture an alternative evidentiary demonstration. The estate’s total size is not made the decisive denominator, and the approximately $1.5 million paid from income is not declared immaterial in every estate. A properly litigated case must assess the material effect on the relevant interest under the governing law. This record supports neither a numerical threshold, percentage safe harbor, mandatory factor list nor automatic further hearing. The stipulated qualification of these trusts is not reopened, and no deficiency amount is recalculated.

The Court leaves §642(g), distinct qualification rules, principal-funded expenses and properly preserved valuation methods to their own provisions. A clearer prospective rule remains subject to lawful statutory and administrative authority; none is announced here.

## Law After Decision

The deductions remain governed by their distinct statutes and regulations. For the disputed income-funded expenses, the material limitation on the qualifying interest must be given legal effect, and allocation to income neither guarantees a full deduction nor automatically reduces it dollar for dollar. The Commissioner’s special burden and unmaintained alternative support this affirmance. No universal valuation-date theory, calculation formula or safe harbor becomes law.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Breyer. They would require a reduction because income diverted to estate administration no longer passes as beneficial value to the spouse or charity. The net-value direction and express administration-income example permit the Commissioner to treat a limitation consequential to value as material without an additional unspecified substantiality threshold. Thomas Jefferson University and Auer support that permissible interpretation. Qualification does not answer valuation, and bookkeeping allocation cannot eliminate the economic loss. Revenue Ruling 93-48’s distinct deferred-tax-interest treatment did not abandon the administration-expense position.

They do not finally adopt dollar-for-dollar subtraction. The proper measure, including any discounted-value calculation, was insufficiently resolved and should be determined on remand. The Commissioner retains the burden for this adjustment; their disagreement concerns the legal need for an additional quantitative threshold, not a shifted burden or invented evidence. Breyer particularly emphasizes that future payment and present value may differ. A justified administrative simplification could conceivably be reasonable, but no such explanation or final arithmetic is supplied here. Both dissenters would reverse and remand without a new deficiency amount.
