## Event and chronology

Department of State v. Legal Assistance for Vietnamese Asylum Seekers, Inc., No. 95-1521. Argued October 15, 1996; decided October 21, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit. The decisions under review, 45 F.3d 469 and 74 F.3d 1308, concern the Department's refusal to process immigrant-visa applications in Hong Kong for Vietnamese applicants screened out of refugee status. The question is whether the policy violates 8 U.S.C. §1152(a)(1), and how the intervening enactment of IIRIRA §633 affects the statutory premise, live claims and available relief. The Government seeks vacatur or reversal; respondents seek affirmance or dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted. This is an argued post-grant disposition, not an asylum-eligibility proceeding.

## Law of the decision

### Controlling holdings

#### Reconsideration after the intervening visa-processing enactment

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 633 directly addresses the processing-location premise of the judgment below and creates a reasonable probability that reconsideration will alter an outcome-material statutory premise or continuing relief. Independent consideration of the equities warrants vacatur and a confined remand, without deciding the old-law merits or the new provision's final construction and temporal reach.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls. The Court applies the reasonable-probability standard; a reasonable possibility alone is not its rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the new enactment warrants reconsideration because it addresses the very activity regulated by the judgment. The D.C. Circuit applied the nationality and residence nondiscrimination provision to the place of immigrant-visa processing. Congress has now added a clause concerning the Secretary's authority over processing procedures and locations. This is an enacted legal development, not simply a Government request for a different outcome.

Lawrence v. Chater supplies the separate requirements of outcome-material probability and independent equitable judgment. Both are met. Review remains timely; the amendment followed the appellate decisions; and the parties have submitted their opposing interpretations. The applicants have already endured substantial delay. Their asserted danger of forcible repatriation, allegedly following the end of a Hong Kong undertaking upon this Court's disposition, deserves concrete weight. But neither dismissal of this writ nor remand establishes continuing foreign protection, and leaving the judgment intact would not settle the amendment's effect on actual processing relief. A prompt, bounded remand permits the new clause, the asserted matured claims and the independent claims to be considered together.

Landgraf and Rivers preserve the distinction between new consequences for completed conduct and regulation of future activity. The Court does not decide that an injunction is necessarily prospective in every relevant sense, that corrective legislation necessarily reaches past conduct, or that the pending case automatically receives the new provision's asserted effect. The lower courts must resolve those questions under governing law. Congress's enactment alone neither moots the independent APA and constitutional claims nor establishes that any applicant must lose.

**Precedent treatment:** Lawrence is applied to an actual intervening statute without enlarging its agency-development holding. Landgraf and Rivers remain controlling on their distinct temporal-reach questions; the remand decides no conflict between their previously published rationales.
