Teague and Caspari require comparison of the precise application with law at finality, not merely citation of a broad principle. Stringer applies Godfrey's existing protection to consequential weighing; Gray likewise distinguishes preserving an established protection's real function from creating a new procedural entitlement. Those current decisions govern the inquiry without being recast as cases available in 1986.

Prefinality Florida law establishes the function at issue. LeDuc gave a death recommendation operative weight if all relevant data were considered, without making it binding. Messer treated an informed jury contribution as a distinct statutory safeguard: later judicial consideration did not replace wrongly excluded accomplice-sentence and proposed psychiatric mitigation before the jury. Godfrey supplies the federal duty; the state safeguard alone does not.

Ross supplies the strongest contrary argument. It requires the judge's independent reasoned judgment and recognizes judicial experience as protection against lay notions of heinousness. Spaziano and Baldwin likewise preserve judicial sentencing authority. But Ross simultaneously retains the recommendation's weight and LeDuc's qualification. Its remand corrected excessive judicial deference, not defective jury guidance. Eutzy preserves weight and independence together, and rejected the instruction challenge only because the jury recommended life; it did not decide the instructions' merits.

The independent judge therefore adds a protection without making the consequential jury contribution disappear. Applying meaningful guidance to that contribution preserves Godfrey's existing function rather than creating a right to jury-imposed sentencing. Neither identical power nor equal weight for life and death recommendations is required. The later date of Espinosa, a reserved question or reasonable disagreement asserted without a substantive distinction cannot alone establish novelty. Actual cure remains a separate inquiry. LeDuc's affirmance, Messer's particular new-jury remedy, Ross's judicial remand and Eutzy's favorable-life reasoning do not mechanically select a remedy here.

### III-A. Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Authority:** Part III-A of Justice Stevens's opinion has direct support at this exact scope from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. The further binding old-rule instruction rests separately on Part III-B's five-Justice coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals disposed of the claim without resolving the State's asserted procedural bar. The actual operation of the rule and the State's litigation conduct bear on independence, adequacy, preservation and an applicable excuse. Those genuine unfinished inquiries require a bounded remand; neither an unestablished procedural premise nor a generalized waiver assertion supplies their answer.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial. Florida's identified ground concerns his failure to pursue that issue on direct appeal, not failure to object at trial. Its separate successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and alternative merits rejection do not establish a federal cause determination. The lower court must assess the actual state materials and any properly available cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice contention. It may not treat a proposed exception as already established or a present reservation as a finding that the bar is inadequate.

The common direction sets aside the reviewed appellate disposition and requires default to be addressed before any available constitutional merits and relief. It does not establish a universal order for nonjurisdictional defenses. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, declares no waiver or exception and creates no present right to a new penalty proceeding. The Justices' different positions on Teague do not erase their agreement on this operative remand task; those differences are preserved in Parts I–II, Part III-B and the separate writings.

### III-B. The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Authority:** Part III-B of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by the same five Justices as Part II. They directly adopt this additional binding direction. Stone-Zsela joins Part III-A's common vacatur and default remand, but not the instruction deciding Teague now or foreclosing its consideration if default fails; his distinct mandate is stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The appellate judgment rested on a Teague conclusion the Court rejects. Vacatur therefore removes that judgment and binds further proceedings to the old-rule determination; it is not an invitation to repeat the rejected ground. The accepted claim includes the heinous, atrocious or cruel and cold, calculated and premeditated instruction issues. The court of appeals assumed inadequate guidance for nonretroactivity analysis, which establishes neither a violation nor its effect.

Lambrix requested a Dixon limiting instruction at trial; Florida relied on failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. That is not an unpreserved trial objection. Coleman and Ford require attention to the actual independent state ground, its adequacy when compliance was required, preservation and an applicable excuse. The separate state treatment of successive appellate-ineffectiveness allegations and its alternative merits rejection are not federal findings of cause. Ordinary cause and prejudice remain distinct from Sawyer's clear-and-convincing requirement that no reasonable juror would find death eligibility absent constitutional error; a selection defect alone does not meet that gateway.

If an available violation is established, Espinosa and Stringer require genuine authorized reweighing or Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt addressing the recommendation's transmitted influence. Remaining valid aggravators, bare proportionality review or an independent-judge label alone supplies neither cure. If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required. None is presently ordered.

Gray preserves any genuinely applicable, unresolved statutory condition before an operative writ. This decision settles neither chapter 153's temporal reach, amended §2254(d)'s application, chapter 154 qualification nor an exhaustion waiver or statutory exception. It orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, conditional writ or revived deadline, and no guilt retrial, life sentence, release, stay or execution authorization. Unrelated assistance, double-jeopardy, testimony and guilt rulings remain untouched.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Teague v. Lane:** Applies the dictated-at-finality inquiry; this claim is old, so the Court does not adopt an alternative exception holding. Both contemporary exceptions remain in force.
- **Godfrey v. Georgia:** Supplies the prefinality requirement of meaningful capital guidance that follows the legally consequential weighing contribution here.
- **Stringer v. Black:** Applies its old-rule and actual-cure principles without making its Mississippi setting identical to Florida or converting its default remand into a universal sequence.
- **Espinosa v. Florida:** Its influential-recommendation merits rule remains controlling; this decision answers the distinct November 1986 finality question without finding an actual violation or cure failure.
- **Gray v. Netherland:** Applies functional comparison with an established capital protection while preserving separate claim, effect and statutory gates; its particular preparation injury and final remedy are not transferred here.
- **Caspari v. Bohlen:** Preserves precise finality and application analysis and the distinction between presentation and preservation; reasonable substantive institutional differences must be tested, not assumed from labels.
- **Gilmore v. Taylor:** Its specific new instructional application and two-exception analysis remain intact; the Court does not treat every later capital application as old.
- **Schiro v. Farley:** Preserves discretionary treatment of a late nonjurisdictional Teague defense; this case involves an expressly accepted question and supplies no generic waiver finding.
- **Spaziano v. Florida:** Preserves States' latitude to employ judicial sentencing; that authority does not make an influential jury contribution nonexistent.
- **Baldwin v. Alabama:** Its independently reasoned sentencing rationale and reservation are distinguished from an exemption for legally consequential unguided weighing; neither dictates automatic cure here.
- **Zant v. Stephens:** Its nonweighing holding does not authorize retention of unconstitutional influence in a weighing contribution.
- **Barclay v. Florida:** Its state-law invalidity and actual review do not establish a universal cure for constitutional aggravation error.
- **LeDuc v. State:** Supplies qualified prefinality weight for a death recommendation when all relevant data were considered, not binding force or a universal new-jury remedy.
- **Messer v. State:** Supplies the distinct informed-jury safeguard under Florida law; its excluded-mitigation holding and particular remedy do not themselves create the federal rule or dictate this habeas remedy.
- **Ross v. State:** Preserves recommendation weight and independent judicial judgment together; its correction of excessive deference does not establish that separate findings cured this different alleged defect.
- **Eutzy v. State:** Its favorable-life instruction rationale and its treatment of an evidentially unsupported factor do not resolve a vague-factor death-recommendation claim.
- **Coleman v. Thompson:** Preserves independent, adequate and unexcused state procedural bars and ordinary cause-and-prejudice analysis; no bar or excuse is established here.
- **Ford v. Georgia:** Preserves the requirement to assess the state rule's adequacy when compliance was required; the 1994 assertion alone does not decide that historical operation.
- **Sawyer v. Whitley:** Preserves the distinct death-eligibility gateway and clear-and-convincing burden; ordinary selection error is not itself gateway innocence.
- **Chapman v. California:** Supplies the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden on the constitutional harmlessness route, distinct from authorized reweighing.

Maynard, Clemons, Walton, Florida Smith and Grossman postdate Lambrix's finality and are not treated as decisions already available in November 1986. Existing correction law remains binding on any later merits inquiry; no prior decision is overruled.

## Law After Decision

This particular influential-recommendation claim is an old-rule application at November 24, 1986 finality and cannot again be denied on the Teague new-rule ground. The decision leaves Florida's lawful allocation of independent judicial sentencing intact and establishes no equal weight for life and death recommendations. Existing Espinosa/Stringer cure requirements remain; actual default, constitutional defect, transmitted effect, lawful cure and genuinely applicable statutory conditions are distinct. The sequence chosen here creates neither a mandatory default-first nor mandatory Teague-first rule for all habeas cases. No general extension of every capital protection, new Teague exception or immediate entitlement to sentence relief follows.

## Separate Writings

**Justice O'Connor, concurring in Parts II–III and dissenting from Part I's sequencing.** She would first vacate and remand for the unresolved procedural bar. Coleman-based comity and avoidance favor resolving an adequate state ground before addressing another federal habeas limit. Exceptions are possible, including an unusually complicated default issue paired with an easily resolved Teague question, but she finds no supported reason to make an exception here. Repeated assertion of default and the lack of a lower adequacy decision warrant the bounded remand; an incomplete account of a state rule does not itself prove that the judicial question is exceptionally difficult.

Once the Court reaches Teague, she affirmatively agrees that the claim invokes old law and joins the binding remand subject to that holding. Godfrey's guidance duty follows the real contribution established by LeDuc's qualified death-recommendation weight and Messer's distinct informed-jury safeguard. Ross's independent judge and Eutzy's life-recommendation limit preserve separate roles and actual cure, rather than eliminating that contribution. Her Stringer and Espinosa joins, and her agreement with Gray's old-rule holding while reserving its final effect and remedy, keep classification distinct from present relief. She does not find default excused, an instruction actually unconstitutional, harmful transmission or a failed cure. Her alternative join is neither assent to reaching Teague now nor a formal reversal vote.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part III-A and dissenting from the present Teague determination and binding old-rule instruction.** He would vacate the Eleventh Circuit judgment and remand for the actual state bar and any applicable exception. Both defenses are nonjurisdictional, and no universal default-first ladder governs. A court may lawfully decide an easier dispositive threshold first; doing so is not inherently advisory. Here the unresolved state-rule and litigation questions make default-first review the useful bounded course because a sufficient, preserved and unexcused bar could dispose of the claim.

If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, he would deny relief on that ground. If it is unavailable or excused, he would then require the finality-specific Teague comparison under Espinosa, Stringer and Caspari, followed only through a legally available habeas channel by any merits and harmless-error inquiry. A label of novelty would not substitute for that comparison. He takes no present position on the Florida bar's ultimate validity, whether this claim is new, or entitlement to sentencing relief. His remand orders no automatic evidentiary hearing, establishes no waiver or exception and creates no right to a new penalty proceeding. His agreement to set aside and remand does not join the Court's instruction conclusively resolving Teague now.

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They would affirm on the ground that the requested application was new at November 24, 1986 finality and satisfies neither contemporary exception. The state decisions establish genuine recommendation weight and a distinct jury safeguard; the dissent does not deny those features or rely on the advisory label alone. But Godfrey's guidance requirement for the constitutional sentencer did not, in their view, compel the precise federal transmission rule despite the judge's independently reasoned sentence. Ross specifically interposes experienced judicial narrowing; Spaziano and Baldwin preserve a substantively distinct judicial function. Eutzy confirms that independence can coexist with great weight, without supplying a death-recommendation harmlessness holding.

Stringer's direct-weighing holding and current Espinosa remain binding. Their force does not establish that every later institutional application was dictated before finality. The contrary argument combines Messer's nonreplaceable jury contribution and Stringer's refusal to disregard consequential weighing. The dissent answers that a state statutory safeguard does not itself decide which defects in its input require a federal constitutional correction after an independent judicial decision. This is a finality distinction, not a finding of actual cure or an inference of novelty from disagreement alone. Kennedy and Thomas retain their Espinosa merits positions; Scalia's prior broader weighing objection is not adopted as the dissent's common ground.

The rule regulates selection of an otherwise permitted death sentence, rather than excluding conduct, persons or punishment from the State's substantive power. Its guidance protection, within a system also requiring independent judicial findings, does not satisfy the exceptional watershed category essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Both exceptions remain legally available; neither is abolished. The dissent decides no actual default, unconstitutional instruction, cure, statutory transition or execution event.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Eleventh Circuit for the unresolved procedural-default defense on the lawful record and ordinary proceedings, including adequacy at the relevant time, independence, preservation and any applicable excuse. An adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused bar requires denial on that ground. If the claim remains available, the lower courts address its actual merits, effect and authorized correction subject to genuinely applicable statutory gates. The binding old-rule determination is closed to reconsideration. The Court neither orders fresh evidence nor restores an old deadline, and unrelated habeas and guilt rulings remain undisturbed.
