### Financial leave and the complete current paper

**Holding and operative rule:** Gaydos's current sworn financial showing supports leave to proceed without prepayment, and the separate, documented review-access contention in her complete paper defeats the proposed Rule 39.8 denial. **Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this record-specific application.

**Controlling explanation:** The October 8 declaration reports no employment, $6,480 annual pension and alimony, approximately $300 in cash and accounts, a car worth about $200, and no dependents. Nothing in this record contradicts that present showing. Shieh requires assessment of actual means and permits particular clarification; it does not make affidavits irrebuttable. Martin, Jones and Shieh separately require assessment of the complete current paper. Although the demanded FOIA docketing has occurred and the original-jurisdiction request fails, the October supplement raises a different, arguable question: the applicant says ordinary review was obstructed by a demand for an additional opinion that does not exist. Rule 39.8 permits a supported denial; it does not compel denial merely because some requested relief is unavailable. No independent finding of malice supports denial here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 39.1 requires the motion to state whether leave was sought in another court and whether it was granted, together with the Form 4 notarized affidavit or section 1746 declaration. Neither misconduct nor timely correction of every deficiency is found.

**Controlling explanation:** The October 8 declaration reports no employment, $6,480 annual pension and alimony, approximately $300 in cash and accounts, a car worth about $200, and no dependents. Nothing in this record contradicts that present showing. Shieh requires assessment of actual means and permits particular clarification; it does not make affidavits irrebuttable. Martin, Jones and Shieh separately require assessment of the complete current paper. **Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 39.1 requires the motion to state whether leave was sought in another court and whether it was granted, together with the Form 4 notarized affidavit or section 1746 declaration. When a United States district court or court of appeals has appointed counsel under 18 U.S.C. §3006A or another applicable federal statute, no affidavit or declaration is required, but the motion must cite the appointment statute; no such appointment is established here. Neither misconduct nor timely correction of every deficiency is found.

### Extraordinary and original relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The requested writ and alternative original action are denied: completed docketing cannot be compelled again, and this record establishes neither a proper original-jurisdiction category nor a clear and indisputable entitlement to the remaining commands with no adequate ordinary avenue. The denial leaves ordinary clarification or correction of the distinct certiorari submission available under the existing Rules without determining its timeliness, adequacy or ultimate treatment.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt the reasons. The Chief Justice agrees with this disposition without joining Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The District Clerk docketed the FOIA complaint on September 9, and the complaint was dismissed the following week. Mandamus cannot compel the performed act or replace an appeal from the dismissal. A personal FOIA suit supplies no basis for this Court's original jurisdiction, and general accusations against clerks establish neither concrete disqualification nor a right to summonses here. The supplement's distinct access contention does not establish a right to require a new lower-court opinion. Rules 14.1(i) and 20.3 call for existing entered orders, opinions and essential material; they do not direct a lower court to write another opinion. The existing reasoned letter and an explanation that no additional opinion exists may be tendered through ordinary filing procedures. Blodgett applies Rule 20's requirement that adequate ordinary avenues be unavailable: this record does not establish that clarification of the stated deficiency or appropriate lower-court relief is unavailable or inadequate. Arguable access concerns therefore support financial leave without satisfying the more demanding prerequisites for extraordinary relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new filing is commanded, no acceptance order issues, no correction is declared untimely, and no new deadline or default is imposed. The Court decides no FOIA merits, clerk misconduct or separate certiorari outcome.

**Holding and operative rule:** No prospective condition issues for either noncriminal certiorari submissions or extraordinary-writ submissions.
