## Event, posture and participation

Foreman v. Dallas County, Nos. 96-987 and 96-1389. Summary disposition on June 27, 1997, without oral argument. Direct appeals under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from a three-judge District Court for the Northern District of Texas. No. 96-987 concerns refusal of injunctive relief; No. 96-1389 concerns the subsequent Rule 12(b)(6) final dismissal. The questions concern §5 coverage of county discretion in appointing precinct election judges, the reach of Texas's 1985 preclearance submission, and the consequence of the absent coverage-date baseline.

Dallas County changed its partisan appointment formulas in 1983 and later years, most recently in 1996. Precinct election judges supervise election-day voting. The state recodification and thirty-page change summary submitted in 1985 identified appointment beginning dates and duration but not the County's particular formulas. The parties agree the record does not establish the appointment method in force on November 1, 1972. The District Court treated statutory discretion and the recodification's approval as sufficient grounds to reject the challenge.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate in the submitted matter and decision. No Justice is excluded. Nine participants satisfy the six-Justice quorum; five votes suffice for the summary merits judgment. Section 1253 supplies the direct-appeal route, including qualifying interlocutory injunction orders; the two dockets retain their separate dispositions.

## Controlling holdings

### I. The final judgment supersedes the earlier injunction appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** A qualifying interlocutory refusal of an injunction is appealable under 28 U.S.C. §1253, but the subsequent final dismissal absorbs the same prospective-relief dispute presented here. No. 96-987 is dismissed on that ground; the final judgment remains reviewable in No. 96-1389.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela concurs only in the judgment on No. 96-987 and does not join this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1253 expressly reaches qualifying interlocutory injunction orders. Nonfinality alone therefore cannot defeat this route. Shaffer v. Carter supplies the merger principle when the later final decree embraces the same injunction controversy. The subsequent dismissal places the prospective-relief dispute before the Court through the final-judgment appeal, and no distinct surviving interlocutory issue is established. This ruling neither treats the earlier request as a temporary restraining order nor imposes a universal finality requirement under §1253.

### II. County discretion does not exempt a changed election-administration practice

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered county's change in the formula for selecting precinct officials who supervise election-day voting is subject to §5 when it differs from the operative coverage-date practice. Authority to exercise discretion under a precleared state statute does not exempt the new local method from preclearance.

**Authority:** Parts II–IV of the per curiam opinion are joined by all nine participating Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Presley requires a direct connection to voting, rather than the mere redistribution of governing authority. Election judges supervise polling; the formula selecting them concerns administration of voting itself. That connection distinguishes the County's methods from an internal allocation of road-government powers. Allen and Hampton County cover practical electoral changes, formal or informal. Monterey and Young establish that administration and discretionary implementation do not escape §5 through an enabling enactment. The contrary theory would make unexamined local choices precleared merely because state law permits them. The Court rejects that exemption without finding that the challenged method actually differs from the November 1, 1972 practice.

### III. The 1985 submission did not preclear undisclosed county formulas

**Holding and operative rule:** Preclearance of the state recodification and its identified changes to appointment timing and duration did not approve the County's partisan-selection formulas, which neither the code nor the explanatory submission identified. A distinct local method is approved only if the submission fairly identifies that method for review; a separate sufficiently specific submission or approval, if established, remains relevant.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition for Parts II–IV controls this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** McCain and Clark require identification of the actual submitted change. Monterey and Young apply that principle to administration under related legislation. The 1985 materials described when election-judge appointments would begin and how long they would last; they did not describe the County's evolving partisan formulas. Receipt of the complete code does not oblige the Attorney General to infer and approve every possible exercise of local discretion. This conclusion neither rescinds approval of the identified recodification nor prevents a jurisdiction from submitting a sufficiently described local implementation with state legislation.

### IV. The absent 1972 baseline requires remand and conditionally governs nonuse

**Holding and operative rule:** The final dismissal must be vacated because the erroneous discretion and submission grounds do not resolve whether the County changed its November 1, 1972 appointment practice. On remand the District Court must identify that practice, compare each challenged later method and determine actual submission status; it must enjoin prospective use of any legally relevant covered change that lacks preclearance.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition for Parts II–IV controls this holding and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Morse requires an actual baseline comparison, and Young prevents a legally unsupported benchmark from replacing it. The parties agree that this record does not establish the 1972 method. Later changes in formulas and insufficient submission do not themselves prove the missing comparison. The County may establish that no legally relevant change occurred; the voters retain the statutory right to nonuse if a changed method lacks preclearance. Clark supplies that prospective consequence, while Monterey and Young require fitted relief within lawful authority. The Court orders neither an immediate universal injunction before the necessary findings nor permission to use an established unprecleared change. The local court decides coverage and submission; substantive discriminatory purpose and effect remain for the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia. No completed-election consequence, cancellation of appointments, new partisan formula, damages or automatic evidentiary hearing is ordered.

Clark supplies that prospective consequence, while Monterey and Young require fitted relief within lawful authority. The Court orders neither an immediate universal injunction before the necessary findings nor permission to use an established unprecleared change. The local court decides coverage and submission; substantive discriminatory purpose and effect remain for the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia. - Presley supplies the direct-voting boundary; selection of officials supervising polls is distinguished from internal governing allocation.
- Allen and Hampton County preserve coverage of actual voting practices, including informal administrative changes.
- Monterey and Young reject the pertinent administration/discretion exemption and preserve submission-specific approval and lawful prospective enforcement.
- McCain and Clark require identification of the submitted change; mere receipt of related legislation is not blanket approval.
- Morse preserves a supported baseline and the designated substantive-preclearance channels. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

County discretion under a precleared statute does not exempt a changed polling-administration method. Approval remains confined to identified changes. Whether Dallas County changed its operative 1972 practice remains unresolved; prospective nonuse follows only upon establishing a covered change without preclearance. The decision does not adjudicate discriminatory purpose or effect.
