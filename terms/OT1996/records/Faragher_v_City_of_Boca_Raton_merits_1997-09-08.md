The questions concern responsibility for a hostile environment materially aided by supervisory authority outside ordinary employment scope, whether higher-management knowledge is necessary, whether preventive care and unreasonable employee nonuse can supply a complete defense without tangible employment action, and whether a formal policy suffices without effective dissemination or implementation.

## Controlling holdings

### I. Actual delegated authority can establish responsibility without higher-management notice

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Part I is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those nine directly support this rule. The eight Associates join Parts II–IV and the judgment in full; that common eight-Justice coalition controls the remaining holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII protects employment conditions and includes an employer's agent in its definition. Meritor directs an agency inquiry while rejecting both automatic responsibility for every supervisor's act and automatic immunity without management notice. Andrade supplies the required actual-power connection, and Oncale applies it while preserving discriminatory causation, an actionable environment and attribution as separate predicates.

Delegated control over daily working conditions may materially aid the discriminatory conditions even when the misconduct serves a personal purpose and violates employer policy. An employer's prohibition of harassment therefore does not alone answer whether its actual delegation aided the wrong. But employment merely providing an opportunity to encounter the plaintiff is not enough. The plaintiff must prove the connection.

Ordinary scope of employment, apparent authority and employer negligence remain distinct routes. Higher-management knowledge matters to negligence without becoming an additional element of proved actual-authority attribution. Harris and DeAngelis govern the separate contextual hostile-environment threshold; proof meeting that threshold does not itself establish agency or constructive notice. The Court applies the actual-authority route here and leaves the alternative routes undecided.

### II. The supported operational findings establish the required connection here

**Holding and operative rule:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part II. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry controlled assignments, reprimands and personnel-file discipline and selected recruits subject to higher approval. Silverman controlled daily duties, assignments and fitness routines. Their powers actually governed the working conditions of a geographically separated unit organized through their command. The district court's agency determination rested on those powers and the workplace structure, independently of its disputed constructive-notice ground.

This was more than supervisory capacity accompanied by an opportunity for misconduct. The operative control of Faragher's daily duties and on-site working conditions supplied the authority through which these supervisors maintained the repeated abusive environment. Remoteness and ineffective policy communication reinforce that practical structure; neither becomes a required element for every future plaintiff or a substitute for actual aid. The Court attributes this established course of hostile working conditions, not every personal act of every supervisor.

Historical facts receive clear-error review; their legal significance receives independent review. The Court adds no finding that Faragher feared discharge, received an order to submit, faced a particular threat, lost wages or suffered an employment penalty. Making a firing threat or completed adverse decision indispensable would restore the restriction Andrade rejects.

The strongest remand objection is that a list of powers shows only capacity and that the trial's agency label did not expressly distinguish facilitation from rank. The supported findings go further: they describe the actual organization and exercise of control over the conditions in which the established abuse was maintained. Their legal consequence can be decided without inventing another historical fact. Meritor's unresolved harassment findings and Andrade's unresolved verdict, instruction and sufficiency questions warranted their own remands; neither requires a new proceeding on this adequate bench record. Whether constructive notice or Gordon's knowledge independently establishes liability is unnecessary to decide.

### III. This ineffective-policy contention does not defeat liability; a general defense remains open

**Holding and operative rule:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part III. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees separately that an undistributed policy does not establish unreasonable nonuse and that later correction does not erase earlier attributable misconduct, but would reject the proposed complete defense rather than reserve it.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor does not make a paper grievance policy and noncomplaint conclusive. An uncommunicated procedure does not establish available protection that an employee unreasonably declined. Prompt action after eventual notice matters to the adequacy of that response; it does not establish effective protection during the earlier authority-aided environment or retroactively erase that conduct.

The grant permits consideration of a complete defense, and effective prevention and reasonable avoidance present serious interests. But Andrade already supplies a plaintiff-proved actual-aid limitation, and the City's present contention fails without deciding how genuinely effective measures would affect responsibility. The Court neither adopts a new burden-shifting formula nor rejects every possible complete defense. Nor does it decide a tangible-action exception or a general duty to complain.

No finding is made that no alternative official could ever have been approached, that the policy contained a particular promise, or that Faragher held an unproved belief. Questions about alternative reporting and whether dissemination would have exposed the misconduct belong to their proper agency, negligence or remedial inquiries; they need not be resolved against the City to sustain this independent ground. Failure to disseminate a policy alone does not prove every negligence element or create a freestanding statutory publication duty. Effective procedures, knowledge, correction and avoidable loss retain their possible legal significance where their actual predicates are presented.

No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Controlling explanation:** The sufficient actual-authority ground supports the existing judgment independently of constructive notice, imputation of Gordon's knowledge, ordinary employment scope or apparent authority. Those alternatives remain undecided; a defect in an unnecessary alternative does not require an exploratory proceeding on the sufficient ground.

Landgraf bars attaching the 1991 Act's new §102 compensatory and punitive liabilities to this 1985–1990 conduct; its dependent jury provision creates no new jury right here. Restoration of nominal relief does not enlarge that remedy. The separate $10,000 §1983 judgment against Terry and Silverman and $500 battery punitive award against Terry remain outside the grant, along with other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions.

## 2. Entering law and propositions that must remain separate

1. **Actionable environment.** Harris, November 9, 1993, establishes severe-or-pervasive, objectively and subjectively abusive sex-based conditions without a psychological-injury or tangible-loss prerequisite. Its exact Standards title is **Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite**, present operation effective Harris, November 9, 1993. DeAngelis, January 6, 1997, preserves that contextual inquiry without adding hierarchy, physical contact or lost-output requirements. The lower court accepted Faragher's hostile-environment finding. Attribution, rather than reconsideration of that finding, is the issue here.
2. **Actual authority and material aid.** Breyer's unanimous Andrade, February 26, 1997, requires a demonstrated connection between actual delegated supervisory authority and creation or maintenance of the environment. Title and proximity are insufficient; lack of higher-management notice is not a complete defense. Actual daily control of working conditions may supply the connection without an express threat, completed personnel decision or tangible economic loss. Thomas's unanimous Oncale, March 19, applies exactly that rule while retaining the distinct causation, environment and attribution predicates.
3. **Other agency and negligence routes.** Meritor, 477 U.S. 57, 69–73, directs an agency inquiry and rejects both automatic liability for every supervisor's misconduct and an invariably sufficient no-notice defense. Scope of employment, actual-authority aid, apparent authority and negligence are distinct. A conclusion that the acts pursued a personal object does not foreclose the authority-aid route. Negligence depends on its own knowledge, reasonable-care and causal predicates; severity and constructive notice are not identical legal conclusions.
4. **Policy and employee conduct.** Meritor rejects a defense made conclusive merely by a written grievance policy and nonuse. A meaningful, accessible complaint procedure and actual reasonable corrective measures can matter to the appropriate agency, negligence or remedy inquiry. Andrade expressly adopted no new general affirmative defense. No general two-part defense, tangible-action gateway, supervisor classification or mandatory complaint prerequisite enters this case as existing law.

## Public Projection

## Event

Faragher v. City of Boca Raton; Eleventh Circuit No. 94-4878. Argued and decided September 8, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to review the en banc judgment reported at 111 F.3d 1530, entered April 15, 1997 and amended April 28. The petition was filed June 2; the Court granted review July 14 on four employer-liability questions and received expedited merits briefing through August 29 for this sitting. No separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

The questions concern responsibility for a hostile environment materially aided by supervisory authority outside ordinary employment scope, whether higher-management knowledge is necessary, whether preventive care and unreasonable employee nonuse can supply a complete defense without tangible employment action, and whether a formal policy suffices without effective dissemination or implementation.

Faragher worked intermittently as a City ocean lifeguard during 1985–1990. A bench trial established repeated unwelcome sex-based touching and humiliating conduct by Terry and Silverman, who exercised command over assignments, discipline, training and daily work in a unit remote from higher officials. The City did not communicate its harassment policy to that unit. Faragher complained to Gordon, another supervisor, but not higher management; the City investigated promptly after another former employee's letter. The district court awarded her one dollar on the Title VII claim against the City. The appellate court accepted the hostile-environment finding but reversed that award on employer-attribution grounds. Faragher seeks restoration; the City seeks affirmance.

## Holdings

### I. Actual delegated authority can establish responsibility without higher-management notice

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's Part I is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those nine directly support this rule. The eight Associates join Parts II–IV and the judgment in full; that common eight-Justice coalition controls the remaining holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII protects employment conditions and includes an employer's agent in its definition. Meritor directs an agency inquiry while rejecting both automatic responsibility for every supervisor's act and automatic immunity without management notice. Andrade supplies the required actual-power connection, and Oncale applies it while preserving discriminatory causation, an actionable environment and attribution as separate predicates.

Delegated control over daily working conditions may materially aid the discriminatory conditions even when the misconduct serves a personal purpose and violates employer policy. An employer's prohibition of harassment therefore does not alone answer whether its actual delegation aided the wrong. But employment merely providing an opportunity to encounter the plaintiff is not enough. The plaintiff must prove the connection.

Ordinary scope of employment, apparent authority and employer negligence remain distinct routes. Higher-management knowledge matters to negligence without becoming an additional element of proved actual-authority attribution. Harris and DeAngelis govern the separate contextual hostile-environment threshold; proof meeting that threshold does not itself establish agency or constructive notice. The Court applies the actual-authority route here and leaves the alternative routes undecided.

### II. The supported operational findings establish the required connection here

**Holding and operative rule:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part II. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry controlled assignments, reprimands and personnel-file discipline and selected recruits subject to higher approval. Silverman controlled daily duties, assignments and fitness routines. Their powers actually governed the working conditions of a geographically separated unit organized through their command. The district court's agency determination rested on those powers and the workplace structure, independently of its disputed constructive-notice ground.

This was more than supervisory capacity accompanied by an opportunity for misconduct. The operative control of Faragher's daily duties and on-site working conditions supplied the authority through which these supervisors maintained the repeated abusive environment. Remoteness and ineffective policy communication reinforce that practical structure; neither becomes a required element for every future plaintiff or a substitute for actual aid. The Court attributes this established course of hostile working conditions, not every personal act of every supervisor.

Historical facts receive clear-error review; their legal significance receives independent review. The Court adds no finding that Faragher feared discharge, received an order to submit, faced a particular threat, lost wages or suffered an employment penalty. Making a firing threat or completed adverse decision indispensable would restore the restriction Andrade rejects.

The strongest remand objection is that a list of powers shows only capacity and that the trial's agency label did not expressly distinguish facilitation from rank. The supported findings go further: they describe the actual organization and exercise of control over the conditions in which the established abuse was maintained. Their legal consequence can be decided without inventing another historical fact. Meritor's unresolved harassment findings and Andrade's unresolved verdict, instruction and sufficiency questions warranted their own remands; neither requires a new proceeding on this adequate bench record. Whether constructive notice or Gordon's knowledge independently establishes liability is unnecessary to decide.

### III. This ineffective-policy contention does not defeat liability; a general defense remains open

**Holding and operative rule:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part III. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees separately that an undistributed policy does not establish unreasonable nonuse and that later correction does not erase earlier attributable misconduct, but would reject the proposed complete defense rather than reserve it.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor does not make a paper grievance policy and noncomplaint conclusive. An uncommunicated procedure does not establish available protection that an employee unreasonably declined. Prompt action after eventual notice matters to the adequacy of that response; it does not establish effective protection during the earlier authority-aided environment or retroactively erase that conduct.

The grant permits consideration of a complete defense, and effective prevention and reasonable avoidance present serious interests. But Andrade already supplies a plaintiff-proved actual-aid limitation, and the City's present contention fails without deciding how genuinely effective measures would affect responsibility. The Court neither adopts a new burden-shifting formula nor rejects every possible complete defense. Nor does it decide a tangible-action exception or a general duty to complain.

No finding is made that no alternative official could ever have been approached, that the policy contained a particular promise, or that Faragher held an unproved belief. Questions about alternative reporting and whether dissemination would have exposed the misconduct belong to their proper agency, negligence or remedial inquiries; they need not be resolved against the City to sustain this independent ground. Failure to disseminate a policy alone does not prove every negligence element or create a freestanding statutory publication duty. Effective procedures, knowledge, correction and avoidable loss retain their possible legal significance where their actual predicates are presented.

### IV. The existing nominal judgment is reinstated within the grant's limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is reversed only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and the case is remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further agency findings. No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part IV and this mandate. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela instead supports the distinct conditional remand described in his separate writing.

**Controlling explanation:** The sufficient actual-authority ground supports the existing judgment independently of constructive notice, imputation of Gordon's knowledge, ordinary employment scope or apparent authority. Those alternatives remain undecided; a defect in an unnecessary alternative does not require an exploratory proceeding on the sufficient ground.

Landgraf bars attaching the 1991 Act's new §102 compensatory and punitive liabilities to this 1985–1990 conduct; its dependent jury provision creates no new jury right here. Restoration of nominal relief does not enlarge that remedy. The separate $10,000 §1983 judgment against Terry and Silverman and $500 battery punitive award against Terry remain outside the grant, along with other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions.

Farrar separates enforceable nominal relief from the reasonable fee amount under §1988. That distinct statute supplies only a limited analogy; any Title VII fee question requires its own entitlement and reasonableness analysis. The Court fixes no fee, new damages, prospective injunction, constructive-discharge finding or employment reinstatement. The appellate remand implements the existing one-dollar judgment and sets no special deadline or retained proceeding.

## Law After Decision

The actual-authority route remains a fact-dependent ground separate from negligence, apparent authority and ordinary employment scope. Existing findings of the operational control that materially aided these hostile conditions suffice for the nominal City judgment. A paper policy, noncomplaint to higher management and eventual correction supply no defense on this record. The Court adopts neither a general preventive-care/nonuse defense nor a rule forbidding every such defense; its elements, burden, tangible-action relationship and any distinct remedial limitation remain open. No title-based liability, automatic knowledge imputation or general complaint duty follows.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Part I but would vacate for a limited agency determination. Meritor's agency instruction and Restatement §219(2)(d)'s aid principle give delegated power distinct work beyond ordinary scope or permission to harass. Assignments, discipline and access can facilitate coercive conditions beyond coworker proximity; actual connection remains necessary. Apparent authority separately requires employer-attributable manifestations and reasonable reliance, not belief that assault was lawfully authorized. Unsupported supervisory claims or proximity do not establish either route.

He rejects mandatory higher-management notice or a firing threat for actual-authority attribution. Harris severity does not establish constructive notice; that asks what reasonable employer care would have discovered. The command findings strongly support aid, but the trial's inference from agency status does not expressly resolve actual facilitation. He would require correct-rule application to the existing findings, with further findings only if a material issue remains. If they establish material aid, the one-dollar judgment should be restored without reopening the accepted environment; otherwise the preserved negligence and notice grounds must be resolved before judgment for either party. Clarification alone requires no new evidentiary trial.

Unlike the Court, he would reject a freestanding complete preventive-care/noncomplaint defense after materially aided misuse is proved. Title VII and Meritor supply no general immunity extinguishing that attributed discrimination. Reasonable care may defeat negligence; appropriate facts may defeat apparent authority or causation or limit recoverable avoidable loss under governing remedial law. Those inquiries do not erase proved actual-authority attribution. An undistributed policy cannot establish unreasonable nonuse, and later correction cannot erase earlier attributable misconduct. Failed dissemination alone is not negligence per se.

Landgraf bars new post-1991 damages. Personal constitutional and state claims remain outside his proposed disposition; collateral fees require their own statutory and reasonableness inquiry. His conditional restoration is not a vote for the Court's present restoration mandate.
