## Controlling law of the decision

### I. Effective accommodation concerns actual opportunity through three independent routes

**Holding and operative rule:** A recipient may establish effective accommodation of athletic interests and abilities through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing practice of responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's demonstrated, competitively viable interests and abilities; any one route suffices. Numerical imbalance alone neither establishes liability nor requires preferential treatment, but comparative aggregate interests do not excuse actual viable unmet demand where the recipient satisfies neither of the other routes.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part I. All nine Justices join at this level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley makes proportionality a safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Section 1681(b) bars compelled preference merely from numerical imbalance while allowing relevant statistics. Brown's contrary argument would be substantial if actual interest were presumed from enrollment or the alternatives were illusory. They are not. The record establishes material underrepresentation, no continuing responsive expansion and capable existing women's gymnastics, fencing and ski teams and a water-polo club whose competitive interests were unmet. Liability rests on those operational findings together, not percentages or the loss of an individual team alone.

Evidence of real interest, ability and competitive viability remains relevant. A greater aggregate willingness among men does not extinguish the women's demonstrated demand or make unequal opportunity self-justifying. The Court applies the interpretation accepted in Kelley without deciding a broader administrative-deference question, adopting a new survey or counting formula, or requiring unsupported or infeasible opportunities. Title IX does not guarantee a particular team or sport-for-sport counterparts. The independent duties concerning facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits remain binding even when a participation safe harbor is met.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley is applied to the established failure of all three routes; its independent benefits requirements and prohibition on a quota remain intact. Chevron and Martin retain the bounded interpretive roles Kelley gave them; neither authorizes an interpretation contrary to §1681(b).

### II. The challenged federal enforcement satisfies independent sex-classification scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal implementation and judicial enforcement of a sex-conscious athletic-accommodation rule must serve an actual important governmental objective and be substantially related to it; statutory compliance, ratios, budget pressure, stereotype or pretext alone cannot justify the classification. The application here meets that standard because it addresses demonstrated program-wide opportunity inequality by requiring effective accommodation of proved, competitively viable unmet interests, without making proportionality compulsory or extending the correction beyond the demonstrated need.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part II. All nine directly join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley required independent constitutional scrutiny of a public university's sex-conscious restructuring. The same requirement applies to the identified governmental classification in federal implementation and enforcement. Brown remains a private university; receipt of federal aid is not a finding of state action. Craig and Hogan require genuine purpose and substantial fit, not an exemption for a measure labeled remedial.

Correcting this demonstrated opportunity deficit is an important objective. The application responds to actual interested and able students rather than assumptions about what most women or men prefer. The three independent routes and the viable-demand limitation connect the obligation to the disadvantage shown. Its accommodation requirement is satisfied when effective opportunity is achieved; the rule operates for the actually underrepresented sex. The defense that the scheme inevitably compels numerical parity therefore fails on this application. This ruling approves no future sex-conscious order without the same independent inquiry into its actual objective and fit.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley's independent constitutional rule is extended from public-university restructuring to the challenged federal enforcement. Craig and Hogan supply the unchanged important-objective and substantial-relationship standard. Virginia's treatment of willing, capable students is consistent with the result, but no broader Virginia proposition is needed for this unanimous holding.

### III. Effective relief preserves a genuine institutional choice before a necessary specific command

**Holding and operative rule:** After a proven athletics violation, the court must require prompt effective correction while initially permitting the institution to choose among lawful means when another effective route remains available. It may reject an ineffective plan and impose necessary specific relief upon supported findings that institutional choice will not cure the violation; liability alone does not establish a present entitlement to the court's selected team list.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part III. All nine support both the continued preliminary protection and the vacatur of the separate final four-team order.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley establishes the equality duty without guaranteeing a particular sport. The established violation cannot continue indefinitely under the heading of institutional autonomy, but a court must distinguish enforcement of that duty from selection of every lawful means. The First Circuit returned the final plan choice because permissible reductions had been rejected out of hand and the circumstances of the expedited appeal had changed. Its judgment does not declare team-specific relief categorically unlawful.

Brown must now have a genuine opportunity to submit an effective lawful plan, including lawful reductions if they achieve compliance. The District Court retains supervision and may enter necessary more specific relief if supported findings show that the institutional choice fails to cure the violation. The existing preliminary protection for gymnastics and volleyball remains until replaced through that process. It is not the vacated final direction concerning gymnastics, fencing, skiing and water polo. No budget, future compliance finding or unconditional right to a particular team follows from this judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley is applied to preserve lawful compliance alternatives while enforcing actual opportunity. The violation-linked remedial principle also applied in Virginia remains consistent with this relief; no identical-program requirement or new remedial procedure is imported.

The Court does not reinstate the vacated final order elevating gymnastics, fencing, skiing and water polo. It sets no budget or roster, awards no damages and orders no funding sanction.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivers the unanimous Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join throughout. Part I governs statutory accommodation and liability; Part II governs the challenged federal sex classification; Part III governs the distinct preliminary and final remedies. There are no separate writings.

## Holdings

### I. Effective accommodation concerns actual opportunity through three independent routes

**Holding and operative rule:** A recipient may establish effective accommodation of athletic interests and abilities through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing practice of responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's demonstrated, competitively viable interests and abilities; any one route suffices. Numerical imbalance alone neither establishes liability nor requires preferential treatment, but comparative aggregate interests do not excuse actual viable unmet demand where the recipient satisfies neither of the other routes.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part I. All nine Justices join at this level of generality.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley makes proportionality a safe harbor, not a compulsory quota. Section 1681(b) bars compelled preference merely from numerical imbalance while allowing relevant statistics. Brown's contrary argument would be substantial if actual interest were presumed from enrollment or the alternatives were illusory. They are not. The record establishes material underrepresentation, no continuing responsive expansion and capable existing women's gymnastics, fencing and ski teams and a water-polo club whose competitive interests were unmet. Liability rests on those operational findings together, not percentages or the loss of an individual team alone.

Evidence of real interest, ability and competitive viability remains relevant. A greater aggregate willingness among men does not extinguish the women's demonstrated demand or make unequal opportunity self-justifying. The Court applies the interpretation accepted in Kelley without deciding a broader administrative-deference question, adopting a new survey or counting formula, or requiring unsupported or infeasible opportunities. Title IX does not guarantee a particular team or sport-for-sport counterparts. The independent duties concerning facilities, coaching, equipment, scheduling and benefits remain binding even when a participation safe harbor is met.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley is applied to the established failure of all three routes; its independent benefits requirements and prohibition on a quota remain intact. Chevron and Martin retain the bounded interpretive roles Kelley gave them; neither authorizes an interpretation contrary to §1681(b).

### II. The challenged federal enforcement satisfies independent sex-classification scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal implementation and judicial enforcement of a sex-conscious athletic-accommodation rule must serve an actual important governmental objective and be substantially related to it; statutory compliance, ratios, budget pressure, stereotype or pretext alone cannot justify the classification. The application here meets that standard because it addresses demonstrated program-wide opportunity inequality by requiring effective accommodation of proved, competitively viable unmet interests, without making proportionality compulsory or extending the correction beyond the demonstrated need.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part II. All nine directly join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley required independent constitutional scrutiny of a public university's sex-conscious restructuring. The same requirement applies to the identified governmental classification in federal implementation and enforcement. Brown remains a private university; receipt of federal aid is not a finding of state action. Craig and Hogan require genuine purpose and substantial fit, not an exemption for a measure labeled remedial.

Correcting this demonstrated opportunity deficit is an important objective. The application responds to actual interested and able students rather than assumptions about what most women or men prefer. The three independent routes and the viable-demand limitation connect the obligation to the disadvantage shown. Its accommodation requirement is satisfied when effective opportunity is achieved; the rule operates for the actually underrepresented sex. The defense that the scheme inevitably compels numerical parity therefore fails on this application. This ruling approves no future sex-conscious order without the same independent inquiry into its actual objective and fit.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley's independent constitutional rule is extended from public-university restructuring to the challenged federal enforcement. Craig and Hogan supply the unchanged important-objective and substantial-relationship standard. Virginia's treatment of willing, capable students is consistent with the result, but no broader Virginia proposition is needed for this unanimous holding.

### III. Effective relief preserves a genuine institutional choice before a necessary specific command

**Holding and operative rule:** After a proven athletics violation, the court must require prompt effective correction while initially permitting the institution to choose among lawful means when another effective route remains available. It may reject an ineffective plan and impose necessary specific relief upon supported findings that institutional choice will not cure the violation; liability alone does not establish a present entitlement to the court's selected team list.

**Authority:** Scalia's unanimous Opinion of the Court, Part III. All nine support both the continued preliminary protection and the vacatur of the separate final four-team order.

**Controlling explanation:** Kelley establishes the equality duty without guaranteeing a particular sport. The established violation cannot continue indefinitely under the heading of institutional autonomy, but a court must distinguish enforcement of that duty from selection of every lawful means. The First Circuit returned the final plan choice because permissible reductions had been rejected out of hand and the circumstances of the expedited appeal had changed. Its judgment does not declare team-specific relief categorically unlawful.

Brown must now have a genuine opportunity to submit an effective lawful plan, including lawful reductions if they achieve compliance. The District Court retains supervision and may enter necessary more specific relief if supported findings show that the institutional choice fails to cure the violation. The existing preliminary protection for gymnastics and volleyball remains until replaced through that process. It is not the vacated final direction concerning gymnastics, fencing, skiing and water polo. No budget, future compliance finding or unconditional right to a particular team follows from this judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Kelley is applied to preserve lawful compliance alternatives while enforcing actual opportunity. The violation-linked remedial principle also applied in Virginia remains consistent with this relief; no identical-program requirement or new remedial procedure is imported.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Kelley:** Applied to the independent participation routes and continued separate regulatory duties; its sex-classification scrutiny is extended to this federal enforcement. No sport guarantee or compulsory quota is created.
- **Craig v. Boren:** Applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the tier does not change.
- **Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan:** Applied for a genuine objective and fit to demonstrated disadvantage, without stereotype or pretext.
- **Chevron and Martin:** Their interpretive roles remain bounded by statutory text; no broader deference rule is decided.
- **United States v. Virginia:** Its concern for actual willing and capable students and effective, violation-linked repair remains intact.
- **Kinman:** Its harassment damages questions do not impose a notice, administrative sequence or damages condition on this prospective athletics case. No earlier decision is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The three accommodation routes remain independent. Prong three addresses actual, competitively viable unmet demand, not a mandatory comparison of aggregate male and female preferences. The federal sex-conscious enforcement rule must independently satisfy intermediate scrutiny and does so on this record. Liability is settled; the lawful means of prompt effective compliance remain initially Brown's to choose under judicial supervision, with necessary specific relief available on supported findings.

## Procedure After Action

The First Circuit's judgment is affirmed in full. Proceedings resume in the District Court for a new effective lawful compliance plan. The preliminary gymnastics/volleyball protection remains operative pending replacement through final remedial proceedings. The vacated four-team directive is not revived, and the Court anticipates no particular plan or finding.
