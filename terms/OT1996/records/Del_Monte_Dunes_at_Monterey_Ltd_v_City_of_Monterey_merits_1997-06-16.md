## Controlling holdings

### I. The final decision and the scope of review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definitive municipal decision fixing the permitted use of the parcel satisfies the final-decision requirement; later valuation does not make that decision provisional. This claim may proceed on the established absence of an adequate state compensation route for the challenged period, without eliminating any compensation prerequisite in other cases.

**Authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, I–III, is joined by all nine Justices; that common coalition controls Holdings I–III.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson County requires a final application of land-use rules and an adequate-compensation inquiry. Suitum separates a definitive use decision from valuation and ultimate liability. The City's fixed position and the earlier finality proceedings establish the former here; the case proceeded on the stated compensation-route predicate. Neither finality nor the futility of another proposal proves a taking. The grant presents the taking, jury and damages questions, while the City's preserved equal-protection objections and the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling retain their distinct status.

### II. A permit denial is not subject to Dolan's dedication burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The government must establish nexus and individualized rough proportionality when it demands conveyance of identified property interests as a permit condition. That special burden does not govern this ordinary permit-denial claim; the Ninth Circuit's contrary proportionality discussion supplies no independent ground to disturb the judgment because it was not part of the jury instructions and was unnecessary to verdict review.

**Controlling explanation:** Dolan protects the owner's interest against compelled dedication; it does not impose a government justification burden upon every unsuccessful development proposal. Prior dedication discussions do not change the action actually challenged. The jury instead received the Agins-based reasonable-relationship instruction and the complete-economic-deprivation alternative. Lucas requires proof of complete loss of economically beneficial use, after which the government must substantiate a background property or nuisance limitation that independently excluded the use; a newly declared environmental harm is insufficient by itself. Penn Central remains applicable to partial deprivation. Legitimate environmental aims, repeated rejection and a later sale price are relevant without conclusively proving or defeating either submitted taking theory. The Court does not replace Agins's received formulation with a new general rule.

### III. Compensation remains available through the established municipal action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 permits compensatory damages for a proved taking attributable to a municipality's own qualifying policy or final decision, subject to the action's constitutional and procedural predicates. Recovery is limited to the legally attributable extent and duration of the deprivation, accounting for sale proceeds or other recovery to prevent duplication; prospective relief alone does not extinguish a completed compensable injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell requires the municipality's own policy or final decision and causation, rather than liability solely for employing an official. McMillian preserves the function-specific inquiry into the institution represented by a final policymaker; it supplies no new finding about an unidentified Monterey official. First English permits compensation for the actual period of a temporary taking. Franklin distinguishes available relief in an established action from creation of a new action or proof of loss. Those principles defeat a categorical limit to prospective or administrative relief without guaranteeing anticipated profits, treating ordinary permit processing as compensable, or dispensing with proof. The questions reviewed establish no basis for a second damages amount or a construction order.

### IV. The statutory wording alone does not resolve jury entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983's provision for legal, equitable and other proper redress does not by itself confer a statutory jury right. Any jury entitlement in the present action must therefore be established under the Seventh Amendment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this statutory ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lorillard's inference from another statute depended on incorporated enforcement procedures as well as the availability of legal relief. Section 1983's remedial wording alone does not provide that answer. This conclusion leaves the damages action available and requires the separate constitutional inquiry; it neither decides that inquiry against the owner nor converts legal compensation into equitable relief.

### V. This legal §1983 action carries the constitutional jury right

**Holding and operative rule:** A §1983 action against a municipality seeking compensatory legal damages for violation of federally protected rights sounds in tort and falls within the legal actions protected by the Seventh Amendment. Its legal character does not disappear because the particular federal right concerns property or because an inverse-condemnation remedy could otherwise exist; equitable-only claims, sovereign immunity and allocation of subsidiary tasks remain separate questions.

**Authority:** Parts V–VII of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supply five direct joins for Holdings V–VII. No vote from a separate rationale is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Imbler, Carey and Memphis Community School District identify the tort character and compensatory purpose of §1983 relief. Curtis protects the jury in statutory tort actions seeking legal relief; Tull and Terry direct attention to the actual action and remedy. The present action seeks compensation for the municipality's alleged completed federal violation and proceeds on the absence of an adequate state compensation route. Calling the underlying injury a taking does not remove the statutory action's legal character. The contrary comparison to ordinary condemnation does not overcome that character here. The holding does not settle every inverse-condemnation proceeding, authorize damages against an immune sovereign, dispense with an underlying violation, or assign every subsidiary legal issue to a jury.

### VI. The disputed tasks submitted here belong to the jury

**Holding and operative rule:** Under legal standards stated by the judge, the jury may decide whether this parcel retained economically viable use and whether this particular final denial, in its development history, bore the required relationship to the City's specified legitimate purposes. The judge determines governing law, the relevant property interest, legal legitimacy of the purposes and evidentiary sufficiency; the jury may not choose a constitutional standard or revise general land-use policy.

**Controlling explanation:** Markman separately examines the action's jury status and allocation of its tasks. Its assignment of patent meaning, including embedded technical and credibility issues, to the judge remains intact. This permit denial's practical effects and highly particularized application of the stated purposes present different work. The actual instructions use the received Agins reasonable-relationship formulation, not Dolan's proportionality burden. The absence of objection to that wording does not waive the City's allocation objection, which the Court rejects on its merits. The decision does not comprehensively decide the full validity or elements of every substantial-advancement formulation, general zoning challenges, all as-applied regulations or all condemnation proceedings.

### VII. The supported taking verdict sustains the single award

**Holding and operative rule:** The City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law or a new trial on the reviewed grounds because sufficient evidence supports both undifferentiated taking alternatives under the instructions actually given. The taking verdict therefore sustains the existing single $1,450,000 compensatory award without decision of the separate equal-protection theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The owners offered evidence that the combined beach/access, viewshed and habitat requirements left no feasible development area and that further proposals could not satisfy the City's fixed position. Their evidence also addressed compliance with the fifteen conditions, prior grading/restoration approval, favorable habitat evidence and the City's role in the access difficulty. Crediting that evidence and reasonable favorable inferences supports the complete-deprivation and reasonable-relationship findings without reweighing credibility. No substantiated antecedent title limitation independently requires the entire deprivation found. The sale to California for $800,000 above the purchase price is relevant to value and compensation, but does not conclusively establish a productive use during the earlier period or payment for its loss. Neither denial of the preferred housing project alone nor disagreement among environmental experts would suffice. Support for both taking alternatives avoids any need to decide the preserved equal-protection objections; the award is not sustained by assuming those objections were abandoned. Affirmance gives effect to the owners' conditional additional-damages waiver and leaves the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling untouched.

## 122. Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey

### Reachability and common law

**D0 — Reach and finality:** Decide the three granted questions on the stipulated grant/abeyance posture. The City's definitive position, as established in the earlier finality litigation and the present record, permits consideration of the claimed period; Suitum confirms that definitive permissible use and later valuation are distinct. The absence of an adequate state compensation route for the challenged period is the packet's supplied predicate, not a general abolition of Williamson County. No separate equal-protection merits question or substantive-due-process appeal is invented. The City did preserve challenges below to jury allocation and legal sufficiency on both tried claims; the Ninth Circuit left its equal-protection objections undecided. Its observation that there was no objection to the wording of the taking instruction does not waive those allocation objections.

**D1 — Exactions and use restrictions:** Dolan's requirement that the government establish nexus and individualized rough proportionality applies to the identified property interests demanded as a permit condition. It is not a universal proportionality burden for an ordinary development refusal. The Ninth Circuit erred insofar as its rationale treated this final-denial claim as subject to that exactions rule. The trial instruction itself imposed the received Agins reasonable-relationship formulation and did not impose Dolan's special burden; an appellate rationale's error therefore does not automatically invalidate the instructions or require a new trial.

**D2 — Existing taking standards and evidence:** A complete deprivation of economically beneficial use invokes Lucas subject to its background-property/nuisance limitation; partial deprivation remains governed by Penn Central. The owner must establish the applicable predicate. Agins's distinct substantial-advancement formulation remains received law at this date, and this grant does not replace it with later doctrine. Neither repeated rejection nor environmental purpose alone proves or defeats a taking. Sale to California for more than the purchase price is relevant evidence of value, not conclusive proof of a productive use during the claimed deprivation. Finality likewise does not itself prove complete economic loss. These are existing-law and evidentiary-limit propositions, not a unanimous finding that either taking theory has been proved.

**D3 — Authorized damages:** Section 1983 supplies an existing action against a municipality for its own qualifying policy or final decision; Monell and the current function-specific McMillian rule still require attribution and causation. First English permits compensation for the actual period of a proved temporary taking when the constitutional and procedural predicates are met. Prospective or administrative relief alone does not extinguish that completed injury. This decision need not imply a new action, guarantee expected profits, order a permit, find an unidentified official a policymaker, or award a second damages sum. Franklin distinguishes remedial availability from creation of the action and proof of legally attributable loss. All eight accept availability at this scope, whatever their liability or jury position.

### Disputed jury propositions

**D4 — Jury right and factual application, supporting affirmance:** The presented §1983 action seeks compensatory legal damages for a completed unconstitutional interference with property. It resembles a property tort for Seventh Amendment purposes sufficiently to carry a jury right; the mere fact that its constitutional predicate concerns taking does not make it a sovereign's ordinary condemnation proceeding. The judge states the legal taking standards and identifies legally legitimate governmental interests. On this record the existence of economically viable uses and the relationship of this particular final denial to those stated purposes are factual applications the jury may decide under those standards. There is no general jury power to define property rights, select the constitutional test, set community land-use policy or determine every mixed constitutional question. Markman preserves the distinction between an action's jury status and subsidiary tasks without converting this record-specific application into patent-claim construction.

**D5 — Judicial taking determination, supporting vacatur:** The remedy's monetary form and §1983's general provision for legal and equitable proceedings do not themselves establish that the historically distinct determination whether government has effected a compensable taking belongs to a jury. The closer analogue is the sovereign-taking/compensation proceeding, not ordinary trespass liability, and the ultimate constitutional evaluation of a land-use restriction remains judicial. Markman requires attention to the actual task even within a damages action. On this view the judge must independently determine the constitutional taking under the correct legal standard, using properly determined facts; the jury verdict cannot by itself supply that legal determination. No separate constitutional jury entitlement to the factual predicates is recognized merely because this inverse-condemnation determination is brought through §1983; jury rights on a distinct independently legal claim remain a separate question. This conclusion neither makes disputed historical facts intrinsically questions of law nor forbids a jury's advisory use, and it does not require a fresh evidentiary trial if findings and legal conclusions on the existing record can properly resolve the issue. It rejects D4's allocation of the ultimate liability application without deciding every possible §1983 damages claim or taking procedure.

### Record application and remedy alternatives

**D6 — Affirmance path:** Applying the actual instructions rather than Dolan's special burden, the supported evidence permits the jury's verdict on both undifferentiated taking theories. The owners offered evidence that the combined beach/access, viewshed and habitat restrictions left no feasible development area, and that further proposals could not satisfy the City's fixed position. They also offered evidence of compliance with the fifteen conditions, earlier approval of grading/restoration, favorable habitat evidence, and the City's own role in the access difficulty. Crediting that evidence and the reasonable inferences available from it, the City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law. This is not a rule that loss of the preferred residential project alone equals loss of every viable use, that a government purchaser proves a taking, or that an environmental expert's disagreement automatically defeats a regulatory reason. The existing evidentiary support sustains both taking alternatives, making separate review of the equal-protection claim unnecessary. Affirm the judgment, including the single $1,450,000 award and the denial of the new-trial motion within the questions reviewed. The owners' conditional additional-damages waiver then operates according to its terms. No permit or extra award follows.

**D7 — Vacatur/remand path:** Vacate the appellate judgment and remand because D5 requires an independent judicial taking determination and the Ninth Circuit used an inapplicable Dolan burden in assessing the substantial-advancement theory. Do not enter judgment for the City or erase the separate equal-protection verdict. On remand the lower courts must address the preserved equal-protection challenges if needed to determine whether that independent claim sustains the common award; the Supreme Court does not decide that claim's merits outside the stated grant. The two taking theories were undifferentiated within that claim, so support for only one cannot be assumed to cure an allocation or legal defect in the other. If the taking ground remains necessary, the lower courts must obtain the required judicial findings/conclusions under the proper rules, with further trial proceedings only insofar as lawfully necessary. The owner's additional-damages cross-appeal is not deemed waived when the condition of affirmance has failed. The unappealed substantive-due-process ruling remains untouched, and the existing award is not declared either independently invulnerable or legally impossible.

### Enhanced alternative-path test

The strongest jury path is the legal-remedy/property-tort analogy of Tull and Terry and the concrete character of the instructions actually given: the judge specified legitimate purposes, leaving disputed use and relationship facts for application. Ordinary condemnation against a sovereign and adjudication of a broad zoning rule are different. Its strongest objection is that constitutional compensation reallocation remains a specialized judicial determination even if the same evidence is useful to a jury; a damages request does not decide every subsidiary allocation under Markman.

The strongest judicial path is that specific taking analogue and the institutional nature of deciding whether an otherwise legitimate restriction crosses the constitutional line. Its strongest objection is stripping the jury from an authorized legal damages action merely because a constitutional mixed question is involved. The four adopting D5 answer by confining their conclusion to this taking-liability task, preserving factfinding and acknowledging that an advisory verdict or existing trial record may eliminate the need for a new trial. They do not announce that all constitutional mixed questions are judicial.

The distinct remedy alternatives must also be tested. The fact that the trial jury never received Dolan's proportionality instruction supports affirmance if lawful independent review sustains its verdict; the appellate court's misuse alone need not require remand. Conversely, the existence of a separate equal-protection verdict cannot itself sustain affirmance after rejecting the taking ground because the City challenged that claim and the appellate court deliberately did not resolve its objections. D6 supplies the affirmers' lawful independent-review conclusion. D7 supplies the vacating Justices' bounded route without deciding an ungranted alternative claim or ordering an automatic new trial.

### Jury propositions and exact levels of agreement

**D3A — Statutory route before the constitutional question:** The words “action at law, suit in equity, or other proper proceeding for redress” in section 1983 do not by themselves establish a statutory jury entitlement. **D4A — Section 1983 legal-damages ground:** A section 1983 action for compensatory legal damages sounds in tort and belongs to the class of legal actions protected by the Seventh Amendment. The statutory action redresses a violation of federally protected rights; its legal character does not disappear merely because the particular right concerns property or an inverse-condemnation remedy could otherwise be available. This does not entitle a claimant seeking only equitable relief to a jury, dispense with the elements of the underlying violation, impose damages on a sovereign immune from such an action, or decide the allocation of every subsidiary issue. The present claim is against a municipality and rests on the supplied absence of an adequate state compensation route for the challenged period. Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this sufficient ground. Its date-eligible basis is the tort character recognized in Imbler, Carey and Memphis Community School District, the protection for statutory tort remedies in Curtis, and Tull/Terry's action-and-remedy inquiry; Markman independently governs the task allocation. No later historical holding is cited as authority.

**D4B — Additional property-tort explanation:** The same result follows by comparing this particular action for an uncompensated interference with property to common-law actions for injury to property. Ordinary direct condemnation supplies a forum acknowledging the obligation to compensate; the present action alleges that the municipality has denied both compensation and an adequate route to obtain it. It seeks compensation for the owner's legally attributable loss, not equitable restitution measured by the government's gain. Scalia does not join this additional explanation: in his view the section 1983 tort action supplies the relevant comparison without a separate classification keyed to the underlying property right. **D4C — Factual allocation in this case:** Under the judicially stated taking standards, whether the parcel retained an economically viable use is predominantly factual. The highly particularized question whether this final permit denial, in its actual development history, bore the required relationship to the City's specified purposes may likewise be decided by the jury. The judge determines the governing law and which asserted purposes are legally legitimate; the jury does not invent property rights, select a constitutional standard, determine the legal legitimacy of environmental protection, or revise the City's general zoning policies. Markman requires separate consideration of the action and the task; its instrument-construction allocation does not assign every constitutional mixed question to a judge. **D5 — Judicial taking determination:** The closer analogue is direct condemnation and its constitutionally specified compensation obligation, which does not carry a constitutional jury right merely because monetary relief is sought. Section 1983 supplies a remedy for the underlying right, but does not convert this particular compensation proceeding into a common-law tort for Seventh Amendment purposes. The judge must independently determine whether the actual regulation effected a compensable taking under the correct standard. Markman confirms that the jury status of an action and allocation of a particular task are distinct. Legal compensation history and judicial evaluation of the governmental-purpose relationship overcome the opposing general tort analogy for O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. They recognize no separate constitutional jury entitlement to the factual predicates merely from pleading this inverse-condemnation claim through section 1983; they do not make disputed historical facts intrinsically legal or eliminate a jury right on another independently legal claim. ## Holdings

### I. The final decision and the scope of review

**Holding and operative rule:** A definitive municipal decision fixing the permitted use of the parcel satisfies the final-decision requirement; later valuation does not make that decision provisional. This claim may proceed on the established absence of an adequate state compensation route for the challenged period, without eliminating any compensation prerequisite in other cases.

**Authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, I–III, is joined by all nine Justices; that common coalition controls Holdings I–III.

**Controlling explanation:** Williamson County requires a final application of land-use rules and an adequate-compensation inquiry. Suitum separates a definitive use decision from valuation and ultimate liability. The City's fixed position and the earlier finality proceedings establish the former here; the case proceeded on the stated compensation-route predicate. Neither finality nor the futility of another proposal proves a taking. The grant presents the taking, jury and damages questions, while the City's preserved equal-protection objections and the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling retain their distinct status.

### II. A permit denial is not subject to Dolan's dedication burden

**Holding and operative rule:** The government must establish nexus and individualized rough proportionality when it demands conveyance of identified property interests as a permit condition. That special burden does not govern this ordinary permit-denial claim; the Ninth Circuit's contrary proportionality discussion supplies no independent ground to disturb the judgment because it was not part of the jury instructions and was unnecessary to verdict review.

**Controlling explanation:** Dolan protects the owner's interest against compelled dedication; it does not impose a government justification burden upon every unsuccessful development proposal. Prior dedication discussions do not change the action actually challenged. The jury instead received the Agins-based reasonable-relationship instruction and the complete-economic-deprivation alternative. Lucas requires proof of complete loss of economically beneficial use, after which the government must substantiate a background property or nuisance limitation that independently excluded the use; a newly declared environmental harm is insufficient by itself. Penn Central remains applicable to partial deprivation. Legitimate environmental aims, repeated rejection and a later sale price are relevant without conclusively proving or defeating either submitted taking theory. The Court does not replace Agins's received formulation with a new general rule.

### III. Compensation remains available through the established municipal action

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983 permits compensatory damages for a proved taking attributable to a municipality's own qualifying policy or final decision, subject to the action's constitutional and procedural predicates. Recovery is limited to the legally attributable extent and duration of the deprivation, accounting for sale proceeds or other recovery to prevent duplication; prospective relief alone does not extinguish a completed compensable injury.

**Controlling explanation:** Monell requires the municipality's own policy or final decision and causation, rather than liability solely for employing an official. McMillian preserves the function-specific inquiry into the institution represented by a final policymaker; it supplies no new finding about an unidentified Monterey official. First English permits compensation for the actual period of a temporary taking. Franklin distinguishes available relief in an established action from creation of a new action or proof of loss. Those principles defeat a categorical limit to prospective or administrative relief without guaranteeing anticipated profits, treating ordinary permit processing as compensable, or dispensing with proof. The questions reviewed establish no basis for a second damages amount or a construction order.

### IV. The statutory wording alone does not resolve jury entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1983's provision for legal, equitable and other proper redress does not by itself confer a statutory jury right. Any jury entitlement in the present action must therefore be established under the Seventh Amendment.

**Authority:** Part IV of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela does not join this statutory ruling.

**Controlling explanation:** Lorillard's inference from another statute depended on incorporated enforcement procedures as well as the availability of legal relief. Section 1983's remedial wording alone does not provide that answer. This conclusion leaves the damages action available and requires the separate constitutional inquiry; it neither decides that inquiry against the owner nor converts legal compensation into equitable relief.

### V. This legal §1983 action carries the constitutional jury right

**Holding and operative rule:** A §1983 action against a municipality seeking compensatory legal damages for violation of federally protected rights sounds in tort and falls within the legal actions protected by the Seventh Amendment. Its legal character does not disappear because the particular federal right concerns property or because an inverse-condemnation remedy could otherwise exist; equitable-only claims, sovereign immunity and allocation of subsidiary tasks remain separate questions.

**Authority:** Parts V–VII of Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supply five direct joins for Holdings V–VII. No vote from a separate rationale is needed.

**Controlling explanation:** Imbler, Carey and Memphis Community School District identify the tort character and compensatory purpose of §1983 relief. Curtis protects the jury in statutory tort actions seeking legal relief; Tull and Terry direct attention to the actual action and remedy. The present action seeks compensation for the municipality's alleged completed federal violation and proceeds on the absence of an adequate state compensation route. Calling the underlying injury a taking does not remove the statutory action's legal character. The contrary comparison to ordinary condemnation does not overcome that character here. The holding does not settle every inverse-condemnation proceeding, authorize damages against an immune sovereign, dispense with an underlying violation, or assign every subsidiary legal issue to a jury.

### VI. The disputed tasks submitted here belong to the jury

**Holding and operative rule:** Under legal standards stated by the judge, the jury may decide whether this parcel retained economically viable use and whether this particular final denial, in its development history, bore the required relationship to the City's specified legitimate purposes. The judge determines governing law, the relevant property interest, legal legitimacy of the purposes and evidentiary sufficiency; the jury may not choose a constitutional standard or revise general land-use policy.

**Controlling explanation:** Markman separately examines the action's jury status and allocation of its tasks. Its assignment of patent meaning, including embedded technical and credibility issues, to the judge remains intact. This permit denial's practical effects and highly particularized application of the stated purposes present different work. The actual instructions use the received Agins reasonable-relationship formulation, not Dolan's proportionality burden. The absence of objection to that wording does not waive the City's allocation objection, which the Court rejects on its merits. The decision does not comprehensively decide the full validity or elements of every substantial-advancement formulation, general zoning challenges, all as-applied regulations or all condemnation proceedings.

### VII. The supported taking verdict sustains the single award

**Holding and operative rule:** The City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law or a new trial on the reviewed grounds because sufficient evidence supports both undifferentiated taking alternatives under the instructions actually given. The taking verdict therefore sustains the existing single $1,450,000 compensatory award without decision of the separate equal-protection theory.

**Controlling explanation:** The owners offered evidence that the combined beach/access, viewshed and habitat requirements left no feasible development area and that further proposals could not satisfy the City's fixed position. Their evidence also addressed compliance with the fifteen conditions, prior grading/restoration approval, favorable habitat evidence and the City's role in the access difficulty. Crediting that evidence and reasonable favorable inferences supports the complete-deprivation and reasonable-relationship findings without reweighing credibility. No substantiated antecedent title limitation independently requires the entire deprivation found. The sale to California for $800,000 above the purchase price is relevant to value and compensation, but does not conclusively establish a productive use during the earlier period or payment for its loss. Neither denial of the preferred housing project alone nor disagreement among environmental experts would suffice. Support for both taking alternatives avoids any need to decide the preserved equal-protection objections; the award is not sustained by assuming those objections were abandoned. Affirmance gives effect to the owners' conditional additional-damages waiver and leaves the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling untouched.

## Precedent Treatment

- Williamson County is applied to finality and the record's compensation-route predicate; no general prerequisite is abolished.
- Suitum is applied to distinguish definitive permitted use from valuation and taking liability.
- Dolan is confined to demanded conveyances of identified property interests; its government burden is not extended to this ordinary denial.
- Lucas is applied with its complete-loss prerequisite and substantiated background-property/nuisance exception; environmental purpose alone neither proves the exception nor defeats compensation.
- Penn Central retains its contextual role for partial deprivation.
- Agins supplies the received substantial-advancement formulation relevant to the actual instruction; this decision does not comprehensively approve every formulation or general zoning application.
- Monell and McMillian preserve institutional attribution and causation as requirements separate from a proved federal violation.
- First English permits compensation for an actual temporary taking period without guaranteeing profits or a permit.
- Franklin preserves the distinction between an existing action, available relief and proof of compensable injury.
- Lorillard is distinguished because its statutory-jury inference depended on incorporated procedures as well as legal remedies.
- Imbler, Carey and Memphis Community School District supply the legal-tort and compensatory character of §1983; no immunity question is decided here.
- Curtis applies Seventh Amendment protection to legal statutory tort relief.
- Tull and Terry supply the action-and-remedy inquiry without making a money request conclusive of every allocation.
- Markman preserves separate action and task inquiries and its actual judicial patent-construction rule; it does not place every constitutional mixed question with the judge.

## Law After Decision

Municipal §1983 compensation claims of the kind presented carry a constitutional jury right, with law and legally legitimate purposes supplied by the judge and these concrete factual applications entrusted to the jury. The existing verdict and award stand under the actual instructions. The statutory language alone establishes no jury right. Ordinary exactions, complete deprivation, partial deprivation, finality, municipal attribution and compensation remain distinct. No precedent is overruled.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer.** The closer analogue is the constitutional compensation proceeding in direct condemnation, which has no constitutional jury entitlement merely because money is sought. Section 1983 supplies a remedy for that right without changing the specific analogue. Markman requires a task-specific inquiry even within a legal action; these Justices would require the judge to determine independently whether a compensable taking occurred. They do not make disputed historical facts intrinsically legal, foreclose an advisory jury or require a new evidentiary trial where the existing record permits lawful findings and conclusions. They join the Court's ordinary-denial limit on Dolan, its other common-law propositions and the statutory-language ruling. Dolan's unnecessary appellate discussion is not an independent ground for their remand. They would preserve the separate equal-protection verdict but require resolution of the City's outstanding objections if necessary to sustain the common award. Their disposition would not activate the additional-damages waiver, order a permit or reinstate substantive due process; nor would it automatically extinguish the award.
