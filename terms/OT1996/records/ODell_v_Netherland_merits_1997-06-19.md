## Controlling holdings

### I. The opportunity to answer this future-danger case was dictated at finality

**Holding and operative rule:** When the State relies on future dangerousness to seek death and the only lawful noncapital sentence actually excludes ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to give the jury accurate information about that legal ineligibility. Gardner and Skipper dictated that limited protection before O'Dell's October 3, 1988 finality; Teague therefore does not bar the claim, although eligibility, violation and relief still require their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt the old-rule holding; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner protects the opportunity to deny or explain material information supporting a death sentence. Skipper expressly protects a response to the State's future-danger argument, independently of its additional mitigation rationale. Fixed legal ineligibility answers that argument by identifying the actual alternative to death. This concrete protected function, not a general appeal to fairness, supplies the dictated application.

The strongest objection distinguishes historical evidence about the defendant from legal information about postsentencing confinement. The distinction does not justify withholding the accurate response when the State makes future danger material. Ramos permits truthful information about commutation; it does not authorize exclusion of the ordinary-parole answer. Caldwell forbids misleading diminution of the sentencing jury's responsibility, not accurate explanation of its lawful choices.

Simmons states the present protection and its limits: accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction may suffice, no fixed script or immunity from clemency is required, and the State may prove danger within prison. Graham, Gray and Lambrix require functional comparison without inserting their later decisions into the law existing in 1988. Gilmore and Caspari still permit genuinely new procedural extensions after that comparison. No rule that every capital protection is old, or that every defendant is entitled to release predictions, follows.

### II. Independently, the confined protection satisfies the watershed exception if treated as new

**Independent alternative holding and operative rule:** Even if the parole-information protection were classified as new, Teague's watershed exception permits its application where future dangerousness is an operative basis for choosing death, the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and the defendant is denied an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule establishes retroactive availability in that confined category, not that O'Dell has proved those predicates or is presently entitled to sentencing relief.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Five adopt this independently sufficient alternative. The four Associates also join Part I; Stone-Zsela actually regards the rule as new. O'Connor takes no position on this alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Teague's exception, as applied in Sawyer, Gilmore and Caspari, requires a basic safeguard of accuracy and fundamental fairness, not merely an important procedural improvement. The confined conjunction here meets that demanding requirement. Death is sought on a prediction of future danger while the defendant lacks an effective means to explain the legal confinement alternative that directly answers the release premise. That loss corrupts the adversarial choice between the two lawful sentences. Gideon supplies the model of a bedrock safeguard for a fair adversarial proceeding; Crane and Gardner identify the basic importance of an effective chance to answer the case against the defendant. The analogy concerns that function, not equivalence between every evidentiary rule and the right to counsel.

The objection that this is only a helpful instruction understates the holding. A refused instruction alone is insufficient: an accurate alternative defense presentation may satisfy Simmons. Nor does the protection's limited incidence diminish its basic role where the conjunction exists. The exception covers no general class of capital-sentencing rules, no case without a future-danger ground, no legally available ordinary parole or practical release prediction, and no case with an effective corrective opportunity. It does not make the error structural, displace a separate effect inquiry, or abolish the substantive/new-procedure distinction.

### III. Retroactivity does not by itself establish a present sentencing writ

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's Teague disposition must be vacated with that bar unavailable under the preceding holdings, but an operative sentencing writ requires actual legal parole ineligibility, denial of Simmons's effective opportunity, the applicable error consequence, and resolution of genuinely applicable unresolved habeas conditions. The District Court's conditional order is not automatically reinstated, and the conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This five-Justice coalition supplies the formal vacatur and bounded-remand mandate; Stone-Zsela seeks the different conditional-restoration direction stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** Simmons permits accurate defense presentation as well as an adequate instruction. The refused instruction therefore does not establish every element of violation. O'Dell's remarks about sixteen years on a prior parole violation and never leaving prison differ from accurate legal information about this conviction's sole alternative, but remain part of the complete presentation. The verdict's vileness finding and evidence of an earlier prison murder do not automatically cure an influence on sentence selection. Prison dangerousness remains a lawful State argument, without creating a categorical exception to the parole-information protection.

Brecht applies Chapman to preserved Doyle error, and O'Neal extends that treatment to its identified personal-intent claim. Gray found the capital preparation injury harmful under both competing approaches and selected no universal collateral standard. The necessary standard here must be resolved under those holdings, or a supported outcome reached under both; no general prisoner-prejudice rule is assumed. The Court makes no present harm finding.

Gray also preserves genuinely applicable unadjudicated statutory conditions, each with its own temporal and substantive predicates; the Court invents no condition, waiver or satisfaction. If an available violation ultimately warrants relief, Virginia may provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital disposition. No new factual hearing, reinstated or expired deadline, release or execution order follows from this remand.

## Precedent treatment and resulting law

- Gardner v. Florida: applied to the effective opportunity to answer material capital-sentencing information; supplies prefinality law for Part I and the basic adversarial function relevant to Part II.
- Skipper v. South Carolina: applied to accurate rebuttal of the State's future-danger case; its independent mitigation holding remains intact.
- California v. Ramos: distinguished; truthful commutation information and state latitude do not authorize withholding the fixed ordinary-parole response.
- Caldwell v. Mississippi: distinguished; preserving the jury's responsibility does not forbid truthful information about its lawful sentencing choices.
- Simmons v. South Carolina: applied with its alternative means of informing the jury, parole and future-danger triggers, and record-specific error remedy preserved. Direct-review validity is distinct from retroactivity and present habeas entitlement.
- Teague v. Lane and Sawyer v. Smith: applied; the old-rule inquiry and demanding watershed exception remain distinct, and Part II recognizes this confined procedure as satisfying the exception if deemed new.
- Gideon v. Wainwright and Crane v. Kentucky: supply the fair-adversarial-proceeding analogy for Part II, not a rule that all useful evidence or instructions are watershed.
- Graham v. Collins, Gray v. Netherland and Lambrix v. Singletary: applied as disciplined functional-comparison decisions; their dates are not imported into the 1988 body of precedent.
- Gilmore v. Taylor and Caspari v. Bohlen: preserved; genuine new rules and both contemporary exceptions remain available for claim-specific analysis.
- Allridge v. Scott: distinguished; release predictions where ordinary parole remains possible differ from the fixed legal ineligibility at issue.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson and O'Neal v. McAninch: their category-specific Chapman holdings remain; neither establishes a universal collateral standard for this new category.
- Gray v. Netherland: its established merits and harm findings are not transferred to this record; its separation of genuine statutory conditions from constitutional entitlement is applied.
- Chapman v. California and Kotteakos v. United States: the competing error-effect approaches are not generally resolved for this category; no present harm finding is entered.

The confined Simmons opportunity-to-answer claim is available against the Teague defense through two independently majority-supported grounds: it was dictated at finality, and, even if new, it satisfies the watershed exception on Part II's exact conjunction. The alternative does not label Simmons new as Court law, extend watershed treatment to sentencing procedures generally or make relief automatic. Eligibility, effective opportunity, error effect and actually applicable habeas conditions remain separate; the universal collateral harmless-error question remains unresolved.

## Holdings

### I. The opportunity to answer this future-danger case was dictated at finality

**Holding and operative rule:** When the State relies on future dangerousness to seek death and the only lawful noncapital sentence actually excludes ordinary parole, the defendant must have an effective opportunity to give the jury accurate information about that legal ineligibility. Gardner and Skipper dictated that limited protection before O'Dell's October 3, 1988 finality; Teague therefore does not bar the claim, although eligibility, violation and relief still require their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt the old-rule holding; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner protects the opportunity to deny or explain material information supporting a death sentence. Skipper expressly protects a response to the State's future-danger argument, independently of its additional mitigation rationale. Fixed legal ineligibility answers that argument by identifying the actual alternative to death. This concrete protected function, not a general appeal to fairness, supplies the dictated application.

The strongest objection distinguishes historical evidence about the defendant from legal information about postsentencing confinement. The distinction does not justify withholding the accurate response when the State makes future danger material. Ramos permits truthful information about commutation; it does not authorize exclusion of the ordinary-parole answer. Caldwell forbids misleading diminution of the sentencing jury's responsibility, not accurate explanation of its lawful choices.

Simmons states the present protection and its limits: accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction may suffice, no fixed script or immunity from clemency is required, and the State may prove danger within prison. Graham, Gray and Lambrix require functional comparison without inserting their later decisions into the law existing in 1988. Gilmore and Caspari still permit genuinely new procedural extensions after that comparison. No rule that every capital protection is old, or that every defendant is entitled to release predictions, follows.

### II. Independently, the confined protection satisfies the watershed exception if treated as new

**Independent alternative holding and operative rule:** Even if the parole-information protection were classified as new, Teague's watershed exception permits its application where future dangerousness is an operative basis for choosing death, the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole, and the defendant is denied an effective opportunity to correct the materially misleading release premise through accurate defense presentation or an adequate instruction. The rule establishes retroactive availability in that confined category, not that O'Dell has proved those predicates or is presently entitled to sentencing relief.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Five adopt this independently sufficient alternative. The four Associates also join Part I; Stone-Zsela actually regards the rule as new. O'Connor takes no position on this alternative.

**Controlling explanation:** Teague's exception, as applied in Sawyer, Gilmore and Caspari, requires a basic safeguard of accuracy and fundamental fairness, not merely an important procedural improvement. The confined conjunction here meets that demanding requirement. Death is sought on a prediction of future danger while the defendant lacks an effective means to explain the legal confinement alternative that directly answers the release premise. That loss corrupts the adversarial choice between the two lawful sentences. Gideon supplies the model of a bedrock safeguard for a fair adversarial proceeding; Crane and Gardner identify the basic importance of an effective chance to answer the case against the defendant. The analogy concerns that function, not equivalence between every evidentiary rule and the right to counsel.

The objection that this is only a helpful instruction understates the holding. A refused instruction alone is insufficient: an accurate alternative defense presentation may satisfy Simmons. Nor does the protection's limited incidence diminish its basic role where the conjunction exists. The exception covers no general class of capital-sentencing rules, no case without a future-danger ground, no legally available ordinary parole or practical release prediction, and no case with an effective corrective opportunity. It does not make the error structural, displace a separate effect inquiry, or abolish the substantive/new-procedure distinction.

### III. Retroactivity does not by itself establish a present sentencing writ

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's Teague disposition must be vacated with that bar unavailable under the preceding holdings, but an operative sentencing writ requires actual legal parole ineligibility, denial of Simmons's effective opportunity, the applicable error consequence, and resolution of genuinely applicable unresolved habeas conditions. The District Court's conditional order is not automatically reinstated, and the conviction and unrelated claims remain undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, directly adopted by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This five-Justice coalition supplies the formal vacatur and bounded-remand mandate; Stone-Zsela seeks the different conditional-restoration direction stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** Simmons permits accurate defense presentation as well as an adequate instruction. The refused instruction therefore does not establish every element of violation. O'Dell's remarks about sixteen years on a prior parole violation and never leaving prison differ from accurate legal information about this conviction's sole alternative, but remain part of the complete presentation. The verdict's vileness finding and evidence of an earlier prison murder do not automatically cure an influence on sentence selection. Prison dangerousness remains a lawful State argument, without creating a categorical exception to the parole-information protection.

Brecht applies Chapman to preserved Doyle error, and O'Neal extends that treatment to its identified personal-intent claim. Gray found the capital preparation injury harmful under both competing approaches and selected no universal collateral standard. The necessary standard here must be resolved under those holdings, or a supported outcome reached under both; no general prisoner-prejudice rule is assumed. The Court makes no present harm finding.

Gray also preserves genuinely applicable unadjudicated statutory conditions, each with its own temporal and substantive predicates; the Court invents no condition, waiver or satisfaction. If an available violation ultimately warrants relief, Virginia may provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital disposition. No new factual hearing, reinstated or expired deadline, release or execution order follows from this remand.

## Law After Decision

The confined Simmons opportunity-to-answer claim is available against the Teague defense through two independently majority-supported grounds: it was dictated at finality, and, even if new, it satisfies the watershed exception on Part II's exact conjunction. The alternative does not label Simmons new as Court law, extend watershed treatment to sentencing procedures generally or make relief automatic. Eligibility, effective opportunity, error effect and actually applicable habeas conditions remain separate; the universal collateral harmless-error question remains unresolved.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the confined watershed holding in Part II and the rejection of the Teague bar, but concludes that Simmons was not dictated in 1988 and therefore does not join Part I. The new procedure qualifies, in his view, only when the State's future-danger case, actual legal parole ineligibility and loss of an effective means to correct the release premise combine to distort the sentencing choice. He would reverse and restore the District Court's conditional sentencing-relief framework. Restoration would still require proof of legal ineligibility, denial of an effective opportunity through accurate presentation or an adequate instruction, and the applicable error analysis. Neither instructional refusal alone nor the retroactivity holding proves entitlement. He would leave guilt undisturbed and permit Virginia a compliant new penalty proceeding or lawful noncapital sentence if the conditions warrant relief.

**Justice Thomas, joined by Justices Scalia and Kennedy, dissenting.** They would affirm under Teague. Gardner concerned undisclosed adverse information and Skipper excluded historical evidence; neither compelled in 1988 a legal-information obligation concerning the postsentencing regime. Ramos's state latitude and Caldwell's distinct responsibility rule support that substantive distinction. The rule may validly govern cases where it is available without having been dictated at finality. It regulates selection of an otherwise authorized penalty, so it is not substantive; its defined rebuttal protection also lacks the systemic character required for a watershed procedure. The dissenters preserve both exceptions but find neither satisfied. They decide no actual parole status, violation, harm, statutory condition or prison-danger exception.
