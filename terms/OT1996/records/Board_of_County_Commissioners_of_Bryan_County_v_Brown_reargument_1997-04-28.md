## Event, posture, and chronology

The case was argued November 5, 1996, on writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). The present action retains the supplied April 28, 1997 event date. The Court orders reargument on that date; reargument has not occurred, and no additional event or future calendar date is created by this Record.

The reviewed municipal question concerns whether Sheriff Moore's selection of reserve deputy Stacy Burns can establish the County's own policy, fault, and causal responsibility under 42 U.S.C. §1983 for Jill Brown's constitutional injury. The County seeks relief from municipal liability; Brown seeks preservation of her judgment. The operative appellate opinion, 67 F.3d 1174 (October 23, 1995), withdrew 53 F.3d 1410 in full and affirmed on the federal hiring ground. Rehearing was denied November 29, 1995. The distinct federal training and state negligent-hiring and negligent-training verdicts were not resolved in that substituted opinion. The withdrawn opinion is not an operative disposition of those grounds.

The district court's September 20, 1993 judgment awards $711,302 actual damages and $65,000 trial attorney fees against the County and Burns jointly and severally, $20,000 punitive damages against Burns alone, costs, and 3.43 percent annual interest. Conditional appellate fees are separate: $12,500 for the Fifth Circuit appeal, $5,000 for a certiorari application, and $5,000 if certiorari is granted. This procedural order alters none of those allocations and does not adjudicate their ultimate municipal basis. It neither reopens Burns's separate liability or qualified immunity nor revisits the earlier dismissals concerning Moore, Morrison, the Eighth Amendment, or intentional infliction of emotional distress.
