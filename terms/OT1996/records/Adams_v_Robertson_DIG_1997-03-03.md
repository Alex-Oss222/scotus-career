## Event

Adams v. Robertson, No. 95-1873. Argued January 14, 1997; decided March 3, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Alabama, which approved a mandatory settlement of policyholders' claims against Liberty National Life Insurance Company. The granted question is whether due process required an opportunity for every class member to exclude himself or herself from the class and settlement.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated. No Justice was excluded.

## Public Action

The writ of certiorari is dismissed as improvidently granted. The Court issues a per curiam explanation; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment.

The Court declines to decide the constitutional question in this vehicle. The state appellate presentation leaves substantial doubt whether the broad federal claim now advanced was adequately framed for decision below. The due-process heading, another party's issue statement, and the later rehearing presentation are relevant, but they do not require the Court to resolve every dispute about proper presentation in order to retain this writ. The state opinion did not decide the broad federal question, and petitioners' developed arguments focused on the state jury guarantee and nonresident concerns. Respondents' failure to raise the objection at the certiorari stage does not compel use of this doubtful grant. The Court leaves the constitutional issue for a case that permits its proper decision.

## Judgment & Remedy

Dismissal leaves the Alabama judgment undisturbed. It is not an affirmance of that judgment's constitutional correctness. No remand or affirmative relief is ordered.
