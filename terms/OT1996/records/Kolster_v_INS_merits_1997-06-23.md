### I. The saved proceeding remains subject to AEDPA's independent restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** Corrected IIRIRA §§306(c)(1) and 309(c)(1) preserve the former review scheme for this proceeding begun before April 1, 1997, subject to their particular exceptions; §242(g) reaches old proceedings but does not itself withdraw review of this legal objection to a completed adjudication. Section 306(c)(2) protects an actual judicial judgment or order entered under former INA §106, applying that section as amended by AEDPA §440, against an operation of §306(c)(1) that would invalidate it or require reconsideration; that protection neither supplies jurisdiction, reopens merits review nor bars ordinary appellate review of the judgment's correctness.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with this transition disposition without joining its reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** Yang supplies the April 1 commencement rule; Reyes-Hernandez, Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro distinguish the executive decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases and execute orders from review of a completed legal adjudication. Section 309(c)(2) permits election of new procedures only before the evidentiary hearing has commenced and with notice at least thirty days before it. Paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Paragraph (4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; the January 30, 1996 order does not meet that condition. Public Law 104-302 made §306(c)(1)'s general operation subject to §309 while retaining §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach; it left §306(c)(2) unchanged. ### II. Section 440(a) governs this ordinary petition

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA §440(a) withdraws ordinary appellate review of Kolster's final deportation order based on the admitted drug and aggravated-felony grounds, independently supported by the conviction documents, notwithstanding his February 28, 1996 petition. On the established facts, application of the channel restriction imposes no supported new legal disability on his plea, concession or another protected completed event; this conclusion establishes neither a universal actual-reliance requirement nor a rule that pleas or eligibility for discretionary relief can never support nonretroactivity.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the dismissal on a different temporal ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf requires statutory temporal direction first and, absent sufficient direction, an inquiry into new consequences of completed acts. Rivers preserves that provision-specific method. Section 440(f)'s express timing clause concerns subsection (e), not a saving clause for subsection (a). Reyes-Hernandez protected an actual surrendered colorable defense; Kolster identifies none, and conviction documents independently supported deportability. Even under his preferred domicile calculation, his September 1992 plea preceded seven-year eligibility by three years. The Court does not infer his subjective motives from those facts. They establish no lost legal option of the kind asserted. Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro establish that filing and an expectation of review alone do not preserve this channel; their holdings do not make surrender of a defense the only possible protected event. Hughes Aircraft concerns creation of a previously unavailable private enforcement action, a new consequence not established here. ### III. Independent habeas jurisdiction survives for substantial legal and constitutional objections

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 440(a)'s replacement of a particular INA review channel does not clearly repeal the independent jurisdiction enacted in 28 U.S.C. §2241. When custody, proper respondent, forum, exhaustion and other applicable predicates are satisfied, that jurisdiction permits consideration of a substantial legal or constitutional claim that executive custody or deportation lacks lawful authority, including a claim that an unlawful eligibility rule prevented consideration of discretionary relief, and permits effective relief if the claim warrants it.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this construction.

**Controlling explanation:** Felker distinguishes repeal of a specified appellate mechanism from the separate statutory habeas authority that Congress leaves in place. McCardle and Yerger likewise require attention to the channel actually withdrawn; Heikkila distinguishes restricted ordinary immigration review from habeas. The replacement of former §106(a)(10) does not identify or clearly repeal §2241's separate grant. Unlike Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro, this case presents a developed legal objection: the agency refused to consider a waiver because of its construction of eligibility. Judicial reweighing of discretionary equities is different. A detention-only remedy required to assume the challenged legal rule valid would not answer this objection. Congress may channel presentation and impose valid procedural conditions, but the retained court must be able to determine the substantial authority claim actually presented and afford effective relief when warranted. This construction permits withdrawal of ordinary review without approving insulation of that claim from meaningful judicial consideration. The objection that no collateral petition is before the Court limits the disposition: the Court decides statutory capability, not whether Kolster satisfies its predicates or deserves relief. No freestanding jurisdiction arises from §1651, and no constitutional violation follows automatically from every statutory error. The Court reserves a demonstrated failure of this retained route and does not define every constitutional minimum or reopen factual and discretionary questions generally.
