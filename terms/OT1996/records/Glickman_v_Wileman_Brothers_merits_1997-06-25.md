## Law of the decision

### Germane generic promotion may be funded as part of this collective marketing program

**Holding and operative rule:** A comprehensive agricultural marketing program may require regulated producers to finance generic, nonideological product promotion germane to its substantially collective economic functions when the message is not attributed to dissenting producers and they remain free to advertise and criticize independently. On this combination of facts, Central Hudson's framework for restrictions on a speaker's commercial message does not require invalidation of the assessment; common benefit or majority approval alone would not suffice.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt this program-specific ground, its qualifications and reversal with remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The orders already displace important individual decisions concerning price, quality, supply and marketing. Generic commodity promotion is a germane expense of that genuinely collective system. Abood and Keller distinguish permissible common-function charges from compelled support for unrelated ideological activity; Lehnert's germane-function inquiry supports the distinction, while Gibson preserves chargeable common activity and safeguards disputed nongermane dues. Respondents contend that advertising remains expression even when government requires payment rather than personal speech. The Court accepts that compelled funding can implicate expression without personal attribution or ideology. It nevertheless finds this integrated, germane expense permissible, rather than treating every expressive assessment as a restriction on respondents' own message. Respondents remain free to promote their products, criticize the campaign and speak independently; the advertisements do not identify them as authors. Hurley's protection of private expressive composition is therefore not infringed on these facts. Rosenberger expressly reserved compelled-fee questions and supplies no independent authorization for this assessment. Central Hudson, 44 Liquormart and Turner preserve scrutiny of supported restrictions on speech; they do not require an effectiveness trial for every germane administrative expense in this collective program. The Court makes no finding that generic advertising is superior to individual advertising. Its holding does not approve advertising-only arrangements in an otherwise independent market, ideological advocacy, false statements, viewpoint penalties, personal attribution or interference with independent expression. A materially different showing concerning those features would remove the factual basis for this disposition. The Court neither denies commercial and corporate speakers First Amendment interests nor holds that compelled funding is always ordinary nonexpressive regulation. It reverses only the reviewed speech judgment and supplies no ruling on unchallenged regulatory provisions.

## Public Projection

## Event

Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc., No. 95-1184. Argued December 2, 1996; decided June 25, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, whose June 27, 1995 opinion, amended September 18 and October 3, held the generic advertising assessments unconstitutional under Central Hudson's advancement and fit requirements.

The Agricultural Marketing Agreement Act marketing orders replace important aspects of ordinary competition with collective decisions about price, quality, quantity, inspection, research, packaging and promotion. Federally supervised producer committees assess regulated California nectarine, peach and plum growers and handlers for program expenses, including generic product advertising. Respondents remain free to advertise or criticize independently and are not identified as the generic message's authors. The question is whether these compulsory assessments violate the First Amendment and whether the Ninth Circuit applied the correct framework. The Secretary seeks reversal; respondents seek relief from the advertising assessments.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate in the argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the reviewed First Amendment judgment and remands for proceedings consistent with its holding sustaining the generic advertising assessments in this comprehensive collective marketing program.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Souter and Thomas.

The challenged assessments survive the First Amendment objection on this record. The mandate concerns the reviewed speech judgment; it does not reopen resolved or unreviewed statutory and administrative issues or itself order a collection award. Claims involving materially different false or ideological content, personal attribution, discrimination, interference with independent speech or stand-alone advertising remain outside the holding.

- Abood retains the distinction between chargeable common functions and compelled ideological support; the Court does not create a universal exception for commercially useful speech.

## Law After Decision

The challenged generic advertising assessments are valid as germane expenses of this substantially collective marketing scheme on the stated nonideological, nonattribution and independent-speech facts. The decision does not validate stand-alone compulsory advertising or all expressive spending within a regulated industry. Compelled funding may still implicate speech, and materially different content, attribution, discrimination or speech restrictions require their own analysis. No precedent is overruled, and no new efficacy finding is made.
