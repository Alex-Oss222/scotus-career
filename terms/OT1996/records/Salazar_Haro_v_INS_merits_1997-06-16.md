## Decision kernel and chronology

Salazar-Haro v. INS; Third Circuit No. 96-3007, 95 F.3d 309. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). Submitted and decided June 16, 1997.

Pedro Alejandro Salazar-Haro, a Peruvian citizen who entered in 1978 and obtained permanent-resident status in 1983, was convicted in June 1993 of cocaine-distribution conspiracy and aiding and abetting and received forty-two months' imprisonment. His wife and codefendant was deported in 1994; their three sons were United States citizens. The immigration judge considered rehabilitation, remorse, education, employment, care for the children and cooperation with the Government, but denied discretionary relief in light of sustained and renewed trafficking and the admission that he sold more than ten kilograms of cocaine. The Board of Immigration Appeals rejected the appeal.

The petition for judicial review was filed January 17, 1996, before AEDPA's April 24 enactment. On September 13, 1996, the Third Circuit dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. Its observation that it would have denied relief on the merits if it had jurisdiction was hypothetical, not a merits judgment. The final administrative order predates September 30, 1996.

The questions are whether §440(a) withdraws jurisdiction over this pending qualifying deportation petition, whether withdrawal of ordinary review violates due process or constitutional limits on jurisdiction, and what review outside that channel remains available when the Constitution requires it. Those questions are addressed within this record, which identifies no distinct legal defect apart from disagreement with the discretionary balance.

At submission and decision, all nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No nonparticipation is established; a quorum is present.

## Controlling holdings

### I. IIRIRA's transition provisions do not decide AEDPA's separate restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** The saved former procedure governs this old deportation proceeding, subject to applicable transition exceptions, while §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not itself withdraw review of the completed-adjudication claims presented here. That conclusion preserves no exemption from AEDPA §440(a), whose separate temporal operation must be decided.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, has eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this transition disposition and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Yang supplies the April 1, 1997 commencement rule; Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto apply the corrected §§306 and 309 and distinguish the three identified executive decisions or actions—commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing orders—from review of a completed legal adjudication. Corrected §309(c)(1) preserves proceedings begun before the new scheme's commencement subject to the particular exceptions. Paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only when the evidentiary hearing has not commenced, with notice at least thirty days before it; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; this earlier order does not meet that trigger. Section 306(c)(2) protects specified existing §106 judgments and orders from invalidation or required reconsideration under paragraph (1), but creates no jurisdiction. The date of this Court's consideration does not apply the permanent scheme wholesale, and these transition savings do not nullify AEDPA's independent bar.

### II. Section 440(a) withdraws this pending ordinary-review channel

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA §440(a) applies to this qualifying final deportation order despite the January 17, 1996 petition because withdrawal of future ordinary appellate review attaches no new legal disability to a protected completed event established by this record. Filing, prior conviction, family hardship and an expectation of favorable review alone establish no such disability; the agency had already considered discretionary waiver under the former law.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf first asks whether Congress prescribed temporal reach and otherwise examines the actual new legal consequence attached to a completed event; a jurisdictional label does not substitute for that inquiry. Rivers preserves the provision-specific method. Section 440(f)'s express timing rule concerns subsection (e), not a saving clause for subsection (a). Hincapie-Nieto applies §440(a) to pending ordinary review where filing and expectation alone identify no protected completed choice. Reyes-Hernandez differs because an uncontested surrender of a colorable defense acquired a new consequence; that is one possible protected predicate, not a universal prerequisite or a rule excluding every other completed event. No such distinguishing event is established here. Past convictions already made Salazar-Haro deportable, and the agency actually considered his waiver request under the earlier law. This is not a new §440(d) eligibility denial, and that subsection's separate operation is not decided. The Court adopts neither a blanket exemption for all pending petitions nor a categorical rule that every jurisdictional amendment is free from retroactivity limits.

### III. The present record does not require deciding an unfiled collateral case

**Holding and operative rule:** This record does not require preservation of ordinary appellate review for another weighing of discretionary equities, and a constitutional label or §1651 alone does not restore that jurisdiction. Affirmance neither approves eliminating every meaningful judicial forum for an actually presented legal or constitutional defect nor decides the availability, scope or custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates of immigration review under §2241.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's Court opinion has eight Associate joins. Stone-Zsela also joins the present-record conclusion against mere discretionary reweighing, while proposing an affirmative §2241 construction rather than joining its reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hincapie-Nieto distinguishes this ordinary channel from a concrete challenge that executive restraint exceeds legal authority. Here family hardship and rehabilitation were considered against repeated serious drug dealing; dissatisfaction with that balance identifies no distinct constitutional or statutory defect. The Court does not decide whether that balance was the best exercise of discretion. Felker sustained a restricted review channel alongside an actual retained original proceeding that received independent consideration. It does not establish the existence or adequacy of an imaginary immigration substitute. Flores preserves actual individual safeguards at its custody and facial-review scope without settling these different collateral questions. Because no developed legal defect demands resolution here, the Court need neither pronounce §2241 available nor declare every other forum foreclosed. Section 1651 assists existing jurisdiction rather than creating a free-standing source. No custody status, collateral filing or adequate substitute is presumed.

### IV. The mandate is jurisdictional, not a waiver judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's ordinary-review dismissal is affirmed without adopting its hypothetical merits footnote or adjudicating an unfiled collateral claim. No waiver, reopening, new hearing, transfer, stay or execution direction follows from this channel-specific decision.

**Authority:** Part IV of Breyer's Court opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The withdrawn jurisdiction cannot support an agency-merits adjudication. A prediction of what the lower court would have done had jurisdiction existed does not become a judgment for this Court to affirm. Nor does an abstract possibility of another remedy establish that a new petition has been filed, that its predicates are met or that relief should issue. Hincapie-Nieto supplies that confined disposition while leaving genuinely presented future claims to their proper statutory and constitutional analysis.

## Continuity, published separate positions and remedy

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.** The relevant activity is the judicial review now requested, which the present jurisdictional enactment governs. The earlier filing cannot preserve a future ordinary appellate forum. These Justices retain the approach expressed in their Landgraf and Felker writings and their current Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto positions; they do not join the proposition that surrender of an earlier defense can preserve this future channel. They join the transition construction, the bounded constitutional and collateral reservation and the actual mandate. Their position leaves §440(d)'s distinct eligibility question unresolved and adopts neither a waiver-merits judgment nor elimination of every meaningful forum.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The completed-event inquiry permits withdrawal of direct review here: convictions already supplied deportability, the agency considered the waiver and no surrendered defense or comparable completed choice acquired a new substantive disability. But he would construe §440(a), in its INA setting and with §2241, affirmatively to preserve qualifying collateral review of substantial legal or constitutional objections to custody or deportation beyond lawful authority. Section 2241 supplies that source when custody and its other statutory requirements are satisfied; neither free-floating constitutional jurisdiction nor §1651 alone is sufficient. A retained forum must be able to test the actual legal basis of restraint and grant effective relief, rather than review detention conditions alone.

Felker supports attention to an actual retained route but does not itself settle this immigration construction. Flores's safeguards and the antecedent due-process protection reinforce the need for effective review in the case presented. Stone-Zsela would not decide every future habeas boundary or assume its predicates here, and would leave open a future showing that the retained route cannot hear a substantial unlawful-restraint claim. The adverse family-hardship, rehabilitation and trafficking balance alone supplies no such claim; he does not reweigh it or adopt the lower court's hypothetical footnote. He agrees with the present dismissal and orders no collateral relief or new procedure. His affirmative construction has no additional join.

The ordinary petition ends for lack of direct-review jurisdiction. No collateral petition is converted, transferred or adjudicated; no custody or release date is assumed. The mandate neither stays nor commands execution of deportation and does not reopen the agency proceeding. ## Holdings

### I. IIRIRA's transition provisions do not decide AEDPA's separate restriction

**Holding and operative rule:** The saved former procedure governs this old deportation proceeding, subject to applicable transition exceptions, while §242(g)'s cross-temporal reach does not itself withdraw review of the completed-adjudication claims presented here. That conclusion preserves no exemption from AEDPA §440(a), whose separate temporal operation must be decided.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, has eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this transition disposition and adds no ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Yang supplies the April 1, 1997 commencement rule; Reyes-Hernandez and Hincapie-Nieto apply the corrected §§306 and 309 and distinguish the three identified executive decisions or actions—commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing orders—from review of a completed legal adjudication. Corrected §309(c)(1) preserves proceedings begun before the new scheme's commencement subject to the particular exceptions. Paragraph (2) permits election of new procedures only when the evidentiary hearing has not commenced, with notice at least thirty days before it; paragraph (3) permits termination and reinitiation only before a final administrative decision. No qualifying election is established. Section 309(c)(4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; this earlier order does not meet that trigger. Section 306(c)(2) protects specified existing §106 judgments and orders from invalidation or required reconsideration under paragraph (1), but creates no jurisdiction. The date of this Court's consideration does not apply the permanent scheme wholesale, and these transition savings do not nullify AEDPA's independent bar.

### II. Section 440(a) withdraws this pending ordinary-review channel

**Holding and operative rule:** AEDPA §440(a) applies to this qualifying final deportation order despite the January 17, 1996 petition because withdrawal of future ordinary appellate review attaches no new legal disability to a protected completed event established by this record. Filing, prior conviction, family hardship and an expectation of favorable review alone establish no such disability; the agency had already considered discretionary waiver under the former law.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Landgraf first asks whether Congress prescribed temporal reach and otherwise examines the actual new legal consequence attached to a completed event; a jurisdictional label does not substitute for that inquiry. Rivers preserves the provision-specific method. Section 440(f)'s express timing rule concerns subsection (e), not a saving clause for subsection (a). Hincapie-Nieto applies §440(a) to pending ordinary review where filing and expectation alone identify no protected completed choice. Reyes-Hernandez differs because an uncontested surrender of a colorable defense acquired a new consequence; that is one possible protected predicate, not a universal prerequisite or a rule excluding every other completed event. No such distinguishing event is established here. Past convictions already made Salazar-Haro deportable, and the agency actually considered his waiver request under the earlier law. This is not a new §440(d) eligibility denial, and that subsection's separate operation is not decided. The Court adopts neither a blanket exemption for all pending petitions nor a categorical rule that every jurisdictional amendment is free from retroactivity limits.

### III. The present record does not require deciding an unfiled collateral case

**Holding and operative rule:** This record does not require preservation of ordinary appellate review for another weighing of discretionary equities, and a constitutional label or §1651 alone does not restore that jurisdiction. Affirmance neither approves eliminating every meaningful judicial forum for an actually presented legal or constitutional defect nor decides the availability, scope or custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other predicates of immigration review under §2241.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's Court opinion has eight Associate joins. Stone-Zsela also joins the present-record conclusion against mere discretionary reweighing, while proposing an affirmative §2241 construction rather than joining its reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Hincapie-Nieto distinguishes this ordinary channel from a concrete challenge that executive restraint exceeds legal authority. Here family hardship and rehabilitation were considered against repeated serious drug dealing; dissatisfaction with that balance identifies no distinct constitutional or statutory defect. The Court does not decide whether that balance was the best exercise of discretion. Felker sustained a restricted review channel alongside an actual retained original proceeding that received independent consideration. It does not establish the existence or adequacy of an imaginary immigration substitute. Flores preserves actual individual safeguards at its custody and facial-review scope without settling these different collateral questions. Because no developed legal defect demands resolution here, the Court need neither pronounce §2241 available nor declare every other forum foreclosed. Section 1651 assists existing jurisdiction rather than creating a free-standing source. No custody status, collateral filing or adequate substitute is presumed.

### IV. The mandate is jurisdictional, not a waiver judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The Third Circuit's ordinary-review dismissal is affirmed without adopting its hypothetical merits footnote or adjudicating an unfiled collateral claim. No waiver, reopening, new hearing, transfer, stay or execution direction follows from this channel-specific decision.

**Authority:** Part IV of Breyer's Court opinion is joined by all nine Justices.
