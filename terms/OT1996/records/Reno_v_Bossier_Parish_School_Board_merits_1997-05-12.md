## Controlling holdings

### I. Distinct statutory inquiries

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5's effect inquiry compares the proposed voting change with the operative benchmark for retrogression; a violation of §2 does not itself establish prohibited §5 effect. A proved §2 violation is also not an independent ground for withholding §5 preclearance, but clearance leaves a separate §2 challenge available.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part I. All nine join the effect-benchmark and independent-action propositions. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer supply six direct votes for the complete nonincorporation rule; Stone-Zsela does not join that additional proposition, and Stevens and Souter oppose it.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court keeps the two statutory tasks distinct. *Beer* supplies the retrogression inquiry, and *Lockhart* preserves comparison with the operative voting practice. Section 2 separately asks whether minority voters have equal electoral opportunity. *De Grandy* requires the statutory predicates and totality inquiry and rejects a command to maximize majority-minority districts. The objection that Congress cannot permit clearance of an unlawful plan mistakes clearance for a determination that the plan is lawful for every purpose. Section 2 enforcement remains available, with its own standards and burdens. Neither the stipulated lack of retrogression nor the possibility of two additional majority-Black districts decides §2 liability. The Court makes no original §2 finding and requires no proportional representation.

### II. Relevant evidence remains relevant to purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** Evidence bearing on racial dilution may also bear on the purpose of a voting change and may not be categorically excluded from §5 purpose review merely because it supports a §2 theory. When such an exclusion may have affected the purpose determination, the ultimate preclearance judgment must be vacated for consideration of the relevant evidence without treating it as conclusive proof or altering the jurisdiction's existing burden to establish absence of prohibited purpose.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by all nine Justices, controls directly.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that evidentiary relevance and substantive liability are different questions. *Arlington Heights* makes impact, historical background, the sequence of events, departures from ordinary procedure or substantive criteria, contemporaneous explanations and possible pretext relevant to intent. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* preserve purpose as an inquiry distinct from nonretrogressive effects. The Board's selection of the Police Jury plan, rejection of the alternative, parish history and disputed explanations therefore require consideration for what they show about its actual purpose. They are not presumptive proof. The contrary argument—that evidence of a permissible result cannot show an impermissible purpose—does not justify excluding evidence capable of supporting the required inference. The district court's apparent categorical separation leaves uncertainty whether it applied the correct inquiry. The Court remands rather than finds purpose itself and creates neither a same-decision defense nor a new causation rule or burden shift.

### III. Unconstitutional purposeful racial dilution is not confined to intended retrogression

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5 prohibits preclearance of a covered voting change adopted with an unconstitutional purpose to dilute voting opportunity on account of race, even when that purpose does not include worsening the existing benchmark. Nonretrogressive effect therefore does not resolve that purpose inquiry; the court must determine actual purpose, preserving the existing preclearance burden and without treating every consideration of race or rejection of a feasible alternative as unlawful.

**Authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, has five direct votes and controls the remand. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not decide this larger scope question.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives the independent purpose prohibition its own work. The statutory bar addresses a purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race, not merely a purpose to worsen the existing arrangement. *Beer* defines effect through retrogression; it does not replace the separate purpose language. *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* confirm that benign comparative effects do not dispose of unlawful intent. Intentional unconstitutional dilution consequently cannot obtain clearance simply because the resulting plan does not make an already deficient arrangement worse. This holding does not incorporate every §2 results violation. *Shaw* preserves the distinction between purposeful racial dilution and racial consideration in territorial design alone; *De Grandy* rejects automatic liability from the feasibility of additional districts. The Board's actual purpose remains for the factfinder. No same-decision defense, altered allocation of proof, maximization duty or proportional-representation guarantee follows.

**Stevens, joined by Souter, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** They agree with the effect benchmark, evidentiary remand and broader unconstitutional-purpose rule, but would also deny clearance when the Attorney General or a challenger proves a §2 violation. Their approach does not require the jurisdiction routinely to prove §2 compliance as an additional condition, and a feasible additional district does not establish a violation. In their view, a limited benchmark inquiry does not require approval of a racial abridgment independently established under the Act. *De Grandy*'s actual-opportunity inquiry and *Morse*'s effective statutory protection support that distinct enforcement conclusion. They find no §2 violation themselves and prescribe no map.

**Thomas, concurring:** Thomas maintains his objection to judicial statutory vote-dilution adjudication, reflected in his *De Grandy* position, while accepting the bounded remand under existing dilution precedent. He also withholds any general rule that the district court owes normal deference to the Attorney General's own preclearance standards. The Court adopts no such deference rule. His concurrence does not decide that purpose is limited to retrogression or endorse every dilution theory; he joins only Parts I–II.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring:** The Chief Justice reads the enacted purpose language to forbid a proved purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race even without an intent to worsen the benchmark. He joins the Court's unconstitutional-purposeful-dilution holding while preserving that text-based formulation. A nonretrogressive result rules out neither an unsuccessful intent to retrogress nor a different purpose to deny equal voting opportunity. The same record evidence may bear on the actual purpose without proving it presumptively. His reasoning preserves *Beer* for effect, independent §2 enforcement and the existing burden; it supplies no same-decision defense, maximization rule or finding against the Board. His concurrence does not adopt the Court's additional categorical refusal to treat proved §2 illegality as an independent clearance bar.

## Procedure After Action

Both appeals return to the three-judge District Court for a purpose determination under the evidentiary rule and the controlling nonretrogression-independent purpose rule. The ultimate clearance judgment is vacated. The Court directs neither a particular purpose finding nor a replacement district plan; lawful supplementation of the existing record remains available within ordinary procedure.
