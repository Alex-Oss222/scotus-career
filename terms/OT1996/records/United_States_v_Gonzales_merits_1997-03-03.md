### Existing state imprisonment is within the concurrency prohibition

**Holding and operative rule.** A federal district court imposing a term under 18 U.S.C. §924(c) may not direct it to run concurrently with an already imposed state or federal term of imprisonment. The statute's unqualified reference to any other term includes state imprisonment; the general sentencing discretion in §3584 supplies no exception to that specific command.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress expressly confined the predicate crime to one prosecutable in federal court but repeated no federal limitation in the distinct concurrency prohibition. The reference that includes the predicate sentence gives an example within the comprehensive class rather than limiting that class. The specific notwithstanding command therefore controls over general discretion. Respondents' strongest argument is that mandatory addition can depend on whether state or federal proceedings occurred first. That concern does not insert a federal-only qualification Congress omitted, and reserving the later-state question preserves the limits of this case rather than answering that different question. Witte's sentence coordination remains conditional on governing statutes and Guidelines; it does not authorize overriding this statutory restriction. Deal construed the separate second-or-subsequent-conviction language and does not independently decide this phrase. Text and context leave no residual ambiguity requiring lenity. Aggregate punishment concerns cannot create an unprovided exception, though lawful choices concerning other terms remain available.

### Other sentencing choices and service sequence remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 924(c) does not otherwise withdraw the federal court's lawful authority to arrange non-§924(c) sentences under §3584 and applicable law, and it does not require the firearm term to be served first. The erroneous federal-only concurrency premise must be removed on remand without indiscriminately reinstating every original sentence or disturbing unrelated appellate rulings.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 3584 generally makes simultaneously imposed terms concurrent unless the court orders otherwise or a statute requires consecutiveness, and makes terms imposed at different times consecutive unless the court orders concurrency. Its separate prohibition of consecutive terms for an attempt and the offense that was the attempt's sole objective remains intact; no such pair is found here, and that provision supplies no firearm exception. Section 3584(b) requires attention to §3553(a)'s factors as to each offense when exercising the discretion the law permits. Administrative aggregation under §3584(c) does not establish which term must be served first. The District Court's concurrent treatment of the drug terms therefore presents a separate inquiry from the mandatory firearm addition. The Court does not decide a later state sentencing court's authority, credit calculations, the administrative order of service, the validity of underlying convictions or any independent constitutional or duplicative-enhancement challenge.

Witte's attention to coordinated punishment supports preserving that choice. Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Stevens, also dissents. His distinct emphasis is the practical coordination of punishment for related state and federal conduct.
