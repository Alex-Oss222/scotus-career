### Issue 1: Best-interests bypass for parental notification

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only whether the stated best-interests criterion is constitutionally sufficient. The statute's confidentiality, timing, and maturity routes are undisputed for this issue.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a direction to waive notice when notification is not in the minor's best interests authorizes the same constitutionally required decision as a direction to waive when abortion without notice is in her best interests.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies simulated Planned Parenthood v. Miller. The required inquiry is practical: can the independent decisionmaker authorize the abortion without notice when involving the parent would be contrary to the minor's best interests? Montana's text says yes. Read in context, it does not authorize a court to deny waiver after finding that proceeding without notice is best for the minor.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A confidential and expeditious bypass satisfies the best-interests component of the parental-notification rule when it requires waiver upon a finding that notification is not in the minor's best interests. Montana's formulation meets that requirement.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The constitutional concern is not a verbal formula but whether the minor can obtain an independent decision that notice should not occur. Montana supplies that authority, adds a maturity route, permits an abuse finding, seals the proceeding, appoints counsel, and grants relief automatically if the court misses the deadline. The Ninth Circuit's distinction would invalidate a functionally adequate route based on wording that changes no legal outcome.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide whether clear-and-convincing proof is valid in every application, whether the emergency provisions are adequate, or whether implementation makes the nominally expedited route unavailable.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved result reads the best-interests language to require waiver when parental notice would harm the minor’s interests. He applies that concrete bypass to the practical-interference concerns in Casey and Miller, while leaving any other preserved defect to its own inquiry. He does not replace simulated Casey with the historical undue-burden framework.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

The following clarification is user-approved except for the preserved source result. For the narrow wording claim, Stone identifies the compulsory notice duty, the independent maturity and best-interests exceptions, and the confined affected group whose parental notice is adverse. He reads the challenged words to require waiver when nonnotification serves the minor’s interests, rather than to authorize a parental veto. On this limited textual issue the challengers have not shown that the phrasing adds an obstacle or meaningful interference compared with the constitutionally adequate wording they seek. That defeats this particular objection at Casey’s interference step; it does not presume that timing, proof requirements, or actual administration satisfy the remaining review. Any properly preserved separate interference must receive the applicable justification and fit inquiry, clinical safeguards, and tailored relief. The proposed mandate therefore removes only the wording-based injunction and leaves unresolved claims for their own decision.
