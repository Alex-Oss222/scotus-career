## Controlling holdings

### Independent failure of traceability

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming sufficient personal aesthetic injury from Lota's absence, the Society has not shown that the challenged August 1991 ESA certificate amendment caused the lost zoo access. The independent earlier donation produced that loss, and this record establishes no mechanism connecting the later amendment to its continuation.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient jurisdictional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires support for the plaintiff's own injury and its connection to the challenged action at the litigation stage presented. A person can have a concrete aesthetic interest in observing a particular animal; species-wide depletion, ownership and a scientific profession are not prerequisites. The Court nevertheless assumes actual injury here rather than finding that the hypothetical regular observer described in the question exists in the evidence. The zoo's donation preceded the amendment, which governed later uses by Hawthorn. The Society abandoned its separate appeal concerning the original transfer and did not challenge the CITES certificate. Neither action can be restored to the case by calling this a challenge to continued absence. Government permission can be causally effective through supported, predictable private conduct; a government command is not invariably necessary. But this record supplies no such link between the later amendment and the lost Milwaukee observation. Sweet Home's treatment of actual injury to individual animals under a habitat regulation does not supply the missing human standing link for this different transportation permission.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the actual members' affidavits establish injury in fact. It adopts no categorical rule against third-party causation, no purchased-ticket requirement, and no general prohibition on standing to observe one animal.

### Independent failure of likely beneficial relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Society also fails independently to show that suspending the challenged certificate or declaring the transportation interpretation unlawful would likely benefit a member's impaired observation. Complete restoration is unnecessary, but a regulatory change must offer some likely concrete improvement to the member's interest.

**Authority:** Part II of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices independently adopt this remedial standing ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Invalidating the amendment would leave Hawthorn able to retain Lota at its Illinois premises. It would not annul the donation, compel the zoo to receive her, or make renewed Milwaukee access substantially likely. No supported alternative benefit to an established observer appears in the affidavits or requested relief. Church of Scientology permits meaningful partial relief even when full restoration is impossible, but changing another person's legal options is not beneficial relief to a member by definition. Even with favorable reasonable inferences from the existing materials and assumed injury, the missing connection remains. The Winikoff letters were considered because the objection to their unsworn form was not preserved; considering them does not supply absent facts. The Owens letter addresses the Society's access, not its authors' personal observation. A remand to create a new return mechanism or observer would not correct an established error in this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical conclusion is reached about remedies in another properly supported action. The Court orders no return, certificate cancellation, damages or other substantive relief.

### Procedural and representative claims retain the concrete-interest connection

**Holding and operative rule:** An asserted ESA procedure does not cure the missing connection between this challenged amendment and a represented member's concrete interest. Assuming that the asserted conservation-permit procedure exists, a claimant need not prove that proper procedure certainly changes the ultimate agency decision, but must still show the threatened personal interest that the procedure protects; representative litigation does not dispense with that constitutional requirement.

**Authority:** Part III of the same unanimous opinion; all nine Justices reject these proposed ways around the causal and remedial defects.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan preserves procedural protection for threatened concrete interests without requiring certainty of a different final result. It does not authorize general judicial supervision based on an interest in lawful conservation administration. No individual entitlement to Lota's Milwaukee residence or separate informational right is established here. Assuming the asserted section 1539 procedure for this analysis does not convert that section's permission into a found statutory command. Brown Group preserves concrete injury, traceability and redressability for represented members; its displacement of Hunt's prudential participation limit through WARN's express authorization supplies no ESA exemption. The procedure asserted in this case does not connect the later amendment to the completed transfer or establish likely benefit to the zoo observer. The Court therefore need not construe the commercial-activity provision or the asserted permit duty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether section 1539 actually imposes the claimed procedure, the commercial-activity interpretation, the unchallenged CITES certificate, the abandoned transfer claim and Hawthorn's Chaney objection remain undecided.
