## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit’s reversal and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case to the District Court for source-authorized Title VII relief under current staffing and employment facts, including consideration of placement, reinstatement or hiring feasibility, back pay, mitigation, declaratory or injunctive relief, and fees. The court should not prescribe one permanent schedule unless necessary after the employer has a lawful opportunity to choose among effective accommodations.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual undue hardship

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory merits because the District Court completed trial and the Ninth Circuit’s judgment rests on the sufficiency and legal significance of the employer’s hardship evidence.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply simulated *Beadle* and the then-governing *Hardison* rule unchanged.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On this record California did not substantiate more-than-de-minimis hardship from the materially available accommodations. Coworker resentment and generalized morale assertions do not meet the burden. The absence of an implemented accommodation is not independently decisive: Beadle permits supported prospective operational judgments and does not require trial implementation or exhaustion of every alternative.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory burden belongs to the employer after notice of the conflict. A continuously operating inspection station has legitimate scheduling and safety needs, but those needs must connect to the proposed accommodation. Opuku-Boateng did not demand exemption from all undesirable work. The record did not establish unavoidable overtime, violation of a seniority rule, inadequate coverage, or a concrete safety deficit. The Department’s failure to evaluate feasible combinations does not automatically create liability, but it leaves its asserted hardship unsupported where the alternatives were practical enough to require an evidence-based answer.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone creates no universal trial-period requirement and does not require an employer to incur material overtime, violate collective-bargaining or seniority rights, reduce necessary staffing, or transfer a substantial recurring burden to identified coworkers. A materially different record may establish hardship.

### Issue 2: Remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Some remedy is necessary after statutory liability, but the current employment setting may differ from the original vacancy.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Title VII’s remedial provisions and ordinary equitable discretion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Relief must place Opuku-Boateng as nearly as lawful and practicable in the position he would have occupied absent discrimination, without forcing a particular staffing design if the Department can supply another effective accommodation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Liability does not itself prove that the exact 1993 position remains open, the amount of back pay, or that one schedule is uniquely required. Those issues depend on updated facts, mitigation, and the remedies authorized by Title VII.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reaches no Free Exercise claim, no individual constitutional liability, and no generalized right to a preferred shift.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s user-approved affirmance should rest on the insufficiency of the asserted hardship for the materially different supported accommodations, not a categorical duty to experiment. His result preserves Hardison’s then-effective threshold and Ansonia’s rule that an effective reasonable accommodation need not be the employee’s preferred one.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1–2 — retained user-approved proposal, clarified to avoid changing Beadle.** Stone would affirm because the supplied trial record did not substantiate more-than-de-minimis hardship from the materially available combinations of shifts, swaps and other arrangements. He would not require actual experimentation, a realized staffing failure or rejection of every imaginable alternative. Reliable predictions can suffice, but unsupported morale concerns and assumptions about the alternatives here do not.

**Question 3 — new explicit answer, proposed to the Court and user-approved.** The reasonable-accommodation duty applies to this applicant’s known religious conflict on the same statutory terms as an employee’s conflict. Public employment creates neither an exemption nor enhanced accommodation. Relief remains tied to lawful hiring, mitigation and current feasibility, with the employer free to choose an effective arrangement rather than a court-selected permanent schedule.
