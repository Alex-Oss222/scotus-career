## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fair warning does not require a fact-identical Supreme Court case

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the standard because the Sixth Circuit’s categorical rule controlled the judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether criminal liability under 18 U.S.C. § 242 requires a prior Supreme Court decision applying the constitutional right to fundamentally similar facts, or whether pre-existing law gives adequate fair warning when the unlawfulness of the charged official conduct is apparent.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply due-process fair warning and § 242’s willfulness requirement without converting civil qualified immunity into the criminal test. The complete pre-existing legal landscape may supply notice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 242 does not require a prior Supreme Court decision involving fundamentally similar facts. Criminal liability is consistent with fair warning when statutory text, authoritative constitutional law, and their plainly apparent application made the charged official deprivation unlawful at the time; liability remains barred when the theory is novel or unforeseeable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** General legal rules often give clear notice at their core even before every application has been litigated. A state judge cannot demand a precedent involving the same office, victim, and method if established law already makes a coerced sexual assault under color of state authority an obvious invasion of protected liberty. At the same time, criminal condemnation is distinct from civil damages immunity and requires rigorous attention to notice and willfulness. The Sixth Circuit’s Supreme-Court-only and fundamental-similarity requirements were categorical additions unsupported by due process.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that broad invocations of substantive due process always suffice, decide each count’s legal notice, lower the Government’s burden on willfulness, or import every qualified-immunity formulation into criminal law.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's user-approved rejection of a Supreme-Court-only factual-twin rule does not convert a civil notice decision into a conviction. The remand must evaluate contemporaneous fair warning and willfulness for the charged acts and rights.
