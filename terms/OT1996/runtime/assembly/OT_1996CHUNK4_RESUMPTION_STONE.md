### Issue 1: Retroactive cancellation of release credits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone joins the threshold disposition and states the merits view below only in his concurrence.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether actual reincarceration after cancellation is an increase in punishment despite the credits' overcrowding purpose.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Morales and Weaver. The objective operation, not the legislature's original motive for creating credits, determines punishment. Florida awarded the credits, used them to calculate release, then retrospectively nullified them for Lynce's offense and returned him to prison. The increase is actual, not speculative. A credit affecting the date of lawful release is part of the effective measure of confinement for ex post facto purposes.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Florida violated the Ex Post Facto Clause by retrospectively canceling release credits already awarded and used to release Lynce when the cancellation lengthened his confinement. The administrative purpose of relieving overcrowding does not negate the punitive effect of reincarceration.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Simulated Morales invalidated the demonstrated loss of meaningful mandatory reconsideration despite the Board’s initial individualized prediction; proof that the missing hearing certainly would have produced parole was unnecessary. Here the State changed Lynce's release date by years and physically returned him to custody. The offense-based cancellation was expressly backward-looking in operation. The Constitution does not permit a State to avoid the result by calling the determinant administrative after it has used that determinant to calculate and end imprisonment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not freeze every prison-administration rule, protect credits never awarded or lawfully unavailable at the offense date, or bar prospective changes that do not increase punishment for completed conduct.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved reversal is supported by the direct deprivation, without adopting a new requirement that every prisoner prove with certainty that an omitted hearing would have yielded release. Morales expressly rejected that requirement. The remedy restores the lawful credit/release calculation on the established chronology rather than announcing immunity from every prospective change in prison administration.
