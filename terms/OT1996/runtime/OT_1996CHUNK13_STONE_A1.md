## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the District Court judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss the complaint for lack of Article III standing.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not reach the Line Item Veto Act’s constitutionality, delegation, Presentment, or ripeness. Preserve a later challenge by a plaintiff suffering a concrete cancellation injury or another legally cognizable injury.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Abstract institutional dilution is not a personal Article III injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches standing first and stops because it disposes of the action.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do individual Members of Congress who voted against the Line Item Veto Act have Article III standing to challenge it based on alleged dilution of their legislative power before the President has used the cancellation authority against a measure producing a personal, concrete injury?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received injury requirement strictly and distinguish Coleman’s complete nullification of decisive votes.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Individual legislators lack Article III standing when their votes on the challenged law were fully counted, they lost through the ordinary legislative process, and they allege only that the law dilutes institutional power or changes the practical effect of future votes shared by all Members. A statutory authorization to sue cannot create the missing constitutional injury.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The appellees were not excluded, deprived of office, or denied the effect of votes sufficient to determine an outcome. Their claimed injury belongs to Congress as an institution and is contested by the Houses they do not represent. Political and legislative remedies remain available, and an actual cancellation can produce a conventional plaintiff. Reaching the merits now would turn a disagreement among officials into an advisory structural judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not foreclose standing for personal exclusion, lost salary, decisive vote nullification, an authorized institutional suit satisfying Article III, or a person concretely harmed by an actual cancellation.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

### Application to the proposed jurisdictional disposition

Stone's user-approved proposed vacatur and dismissal follows the actual Lujan injury rule even though he did not join every part of that opinion. The claimed shift in Congress's institutional leverage does not identify these Members' own lost office, salary, counted-vote entitlement or concrete cancellation injury. Statutory expedition and access cannot cure that deficiency. He therefore would stop before Presentment or delegation, preserving a later suit with actual concrete injury and leaving the distinct standing of a properly authorized institutional plaintiff unresolved. No stricter rule that every legislator injury is nonjusticiable is proposed.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the three-judge district court.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Enforcement of the invalid content restrictions remains enjoined. The mandate preserves severable laws and statutory applications governing obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation, direct harmful contact with minors, and other independently unprotected conduct.

### Issue 1: Content-based suppression of protected Internet speech

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial First Amendment challenge. The severe criminal sanctions, uncertain content categories, and unavoidable effect on third-party speakers create the present chilling injury. Because the First Amendment grounds are sufficient, he does not announce a separate freestanding Fifth Amendment holding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone classifies the provisions as content based and applies the most searching review. Protecting children is compelling, but the Government must prove narrow tailoring in the actual medium. The terms indecent and patently offensive leave speakers uncertain about a national audience and community standard, reach serious discussion of health, art, sexuality, politics, and personal experience, and force many speakers either to abandon anonymity, incur prohibitive verification costs, reduce their audience, or remain silent. Scienter does not cure uncertainty about what content is criminal. User-controlled filtering, precise regulation of unprotected material, and targeted rules for direct communication with identified minors are materially less speech-restrictive on this record.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when vague and substantially overbroad categories suppress a large amount of protected adult speech and the Government fails to prove that the restriction is narrowly tailored in light of practical, materially less speech-restrictive means of limiting minors' access.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute makes the speaker responsible for every minor who might gain access, even when adults are the intended audience and the speaker lacks a feasible age gate. The record shows that many noncommercial speakers cannot use credit-card verification and that available controls either destroy anonymity, exclude lawful adults, or remove the speaker from the medium. The structural defect parallels Denver Area's invalidated default blocking: protected adult speech is suppressed first and restored only through burdensome mechanisms. The Internet's open and non-scarce architecture supplies no broadcast rationale for lesser protection.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation, direct harmful contact with minors, or a future precisely drawn rule using genuinely effective and nonexclusionary age-assurance methods. It does not require one constitutional classification for every Internet service.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's preserved adult-access objection follows Denver Area's actual cumulative-burden rationale. His Internet application is supported by the record's feasibility and anonymity limits; it does not rest on a categorical holding that all age verification is unconstitutional.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's broader content-based Internet analysis remains his existing individual position. His separate Denver Area §10(c) ground was not Court law; the proposed CDA result must follow the actual Internet statute and record together with applicable inherited speech rules, rather than treating his Denver writing or the fractured component judgments as a settled universal test.
