## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 1 — the alleged invitation violates the Fourth Amendment.** Stone would decide the media-intrusion merits on the assumed facts before considering the personal defense. The warrant authorizes an evidence-gathering intrusion with defined objects and purposes. Taking outside observers into the home to produce a broadcast adds persons and exposure that do nothing to locate the specified evidence or protect the search. The family's compelled submission to officers is not consent to that additional audience. Public interest in law enforcement does not enlarge the officer's warrant authority, and press status supplies no special permission to enter a private home.

**Question 2 — the warrant supplies no authority for these nonassisting third parties.** Stone preserves practical assistance: a technician, interpreter or person identifying sought property can sometimes aid lawful execution when reasonably needed and controlled. Section 3105 recognizes assistance, not attendance at the officer's unrestricted invitation. On this record the media purpose is independent of execution. The constitutional conclusion rests on the warrant's scope and reasonableness; breach of the statute alone neither creates the Bivens claim nor proves a constitutional violation.

**Question 3 — the alleged observation and recording add an unreasonable intrusion.** Lawful official inspection of a document does not entitle an unrelated camera crew to inspect and preserve its contents for a different purpose. The added exposure of the family and domestic materials exceeds the justification supporting the warrant. Stone would not make liability turn on whether recording is called a separate property seizure: the government's procurement of the extra privacy invasion during a home search is sufficient under the Fourth Amendment. Necessary official evidentiary photography or appropriate documentation of execution has a different justification and remains open to its own reasonableness analysis. No prior restraint on eventual publication is requested or ordered.

**Question 4 — deny the pleaded immunity defense through a distinct conduct-date inquiry.** Stone would affirm under the received Harlow–Anderson standard, as preserved in simulated Elder and Pinder. Before March 5, 1992, the warrant's purpose-bound authority and protection against needless documentary exposure were established by Bivens and Andresen, with Matias applying that discipline in the Second Circuit. Rivera made clear that entry authority does not erase the rights of household members outside the investigation. These propositions converge on a specific limit: officers executing an evidence warrant cannot gratuitously admit strangers to expose private home life for a purpose unrelated to the search. The alleged media invitation is outside that limit without requiring an earlier television-crew case. Section 3105 corroborates what execution authority permits, but he does not equate a statutory violation with clearly established constitutional liability.

Stone would not reason that because he now finds a violation immunity necessarily fails. He separately identifies the earlier sources, the relevant level of specificity and why an officer could not reasonably read this warrant to authorize the alleged conduct. Later Soldal, Wilson, Bills and the pending Richardson position do no retroactive notice work. Mottola does not claim that the crew was actually assisting the search, so no such factual allowance is available on this appeal.

Stone's Master favors one complete merits inquiry and a narrower defense based on genuine, reasonable reliance on identified authoritative law. Here no binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing this media intrusion is identified; the warrant's general search authorization cannot supply the missing permission. The same denial follows under that preferred approach. Because the presented claim fails even under the broader received defense, Stone need not overrule Harlow or impose a new immunity regime to decide this case. This preserves the latest Richardson distinction between eligibility and an individual's actual defense and does not repeat factual reasonableness as a second shield.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the Second Circuit's judgment insofar as it rejects Mottola's pleading-stage qualified-immunity defense to the media-invitation and associated intrusion claim. Remand for ordinary adjudication of his actual participation, causation and compensable injury on the lawful record. The merits ruling is conditional on the allegations the Court must assume; the disposition awards no damages, resolves no excluded force or detention claim, and adjudicates no liability of CBS or other agents.
