## Controlling propositions supported by the eight commitments

**Federal burden rule, unchanged:** Marriage is a fundamental liberty, but an employment rule does not require heightened scrutiny merely because marital status triggers an assignment consequence. The asserted burden must directly and substantially interfere with the decision to marry. The actual effects matter; completing the marriage does not by itself defeat a constitutional challenge, and absence of a completed discharge is not a universal safe harbor. Loving and Zablocki protect the decision to marry; Jobst permits rational incidental marital-status consequences at its stated scope.

**Policy text, corrected:** Paragraph C permits the affected employees to elect which spouse moves. If they make no election within thirty calendar days after notice, the less senior hospital employee moves. The hospital promises all reasonable attempts to provide comparable responsibility and pay. That is a qualified effort obligation, not an unconditional guarantee against reduced responsibility or compensation. No commitment rests on an absolute textual protection. The policy's general employee-choice/default language is not rewritten into a factual finding that both Wrights were hospital employees: John worked for PHI.

**Concrete burden, reconsidered:** The separate reported offer to Martha was transfer to any available position of the same grade and pay in another unit, which she refused. That actual offer is relevant independently of the general policy's weaker assurance. It is not converted into a finding identifying a particular unreported vacancy, guaranteeing every future placement, or establishing that any future reduction would be lawful.

The Court must also acknowledge the more substantial alternative actually threatened. After the marriage, MetroHealth requested John's transfer, and PHI announced relocation to Louisiana effective January 19, 1993. A threatened geographic separation can burden a married couple even if no move is completed. Here, however, the same-grade/pay hospital-transfer offer provided a reported alternative to the paired LifeFlight assignment before the relocation proposal, and the record establishes no completed move, discharge or demonstrated pay reduction. Preference for retaining both current assignments does not alone establish a substantial obstacle to marrying.

After filing, defendants agreed that John would not move and Martha would instead transfer within the hospital. During trial pendency, they further agreed that Martha would stay in LifeFlight while their schedules prevented shared flights. The Court does not treat these later accommodations as the original offer or a permanent promise never to transfer either spouse. Their temporary character does not erase the earlier threat. Equally, the threatened relocation is not treated as an unavoidable completed deprivation despite the reported alternative and subsequent sequence.

On the corrected record as a whole, the burden remains an assignment consequence rather than direct and substantial interference with entering marriage. The rule's trigger, the Louisiana proposal and the qualified policy protection have all been considered. The holding does not depend solely on the couple having married, the interim arrangement, or an invented guarantee of unchanged employment. A different record of forced separation, actual loss of livelihood or unavailable alternatives would require its own assessment; no outcome for that record is decided.

**Rational basis, unchanged in rule and confined in application:** Once that burden determination is made, the policy's close-interaction restriction rationally relates to supervision, conflicts, safety and effective emergency teamwork. The operational connection concerns the same LifeFlight team, notwithstanding John's employment by a contractor. The Court finds no new contract term and relies on no continuing PHI-party status. Ordinary review permits reasonable predictions and imperfect categories; it does not require an actual accident, proof that every married pair is unsafe or the least restrictive schedule.

Heller and Romer continue to require a relationship between the actual disadvantage and the asserted interest. Tracy illustrates attention to a supported operational distinction without supplying a new marriage rule. The fact that separate flight schedules worked during litigation is relevant to the plaintiffs' argument, but the existence of a narrower arrangement does not itself invalidate the assignment classification under ordinary review. The holding does not declare that any conceivable family restriction is valid or that marital animus is a legitimate objective. It approves this constitutional application at the corrected record's scope.

## Adversarial reconsideration of the changed premises

**Heightened-review path:** The strongest case for reversal now expressly includes the threatened Louisiana relocation and the absence of an unconditional policy guarantee. A State cannot necessarily evade marriage protection by acting through assignments or a contractor; neither successful marriage nor interim forbearance is conclusive. The countervailing record is the actual same-grade/pay offer within another hospital unit and the absence of demonstrated unavoidable geographic separation, lost pay or employment. Under Jobst and the surviving direct-and-substantial-interference boundary, those facts continue to support ordinary scrutiny. The Court must not describe the burden merely as disappointment over a schedule while omitting the relocation proposal.

**Ordinary-review invalidation or fact-remand path:** The couple's workable interim schedules and distinct employers provide the strongest argument that the classification exceeds its operational justification. Heller and Romer forbid resting on irrelevant class labels. Here, however, both spouses served the same close-interaction emergency unit; nominal payroll separation does not remove that relationship. Ordinary scrutiny does not require adoption of the plaintiffs' less burdensome alternative or proof of this couple's unsafe conduct. The qualified effort clause, read with the actual offer, supplies no established major deprivation demanding new factfinding merely to resolve the granted legal challenge. No unsupported vacancy, permanent accommodation or future safe placement is assumed to reject this path.

**Affirmance with limited nonreview:** The supported federal disposition remains affirmance. Its proper state-claim consequence is bounded nonreview of judgments outside the grant. Affirmance does not convert those existing state merits dismissals into discretionary jurisdictional dismissals or independently endorse Ohio privilege, emotional-distress or public-policy analysis. No formal remand or claim restoration is warranted by the supplied federal result.
