## 17. DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association

### Questions, legal alternatives, and record limits

The grant permits review of hostile-environment sufficiency, the preserved as-applied First Amendment defense, and retaliation. Coverage as a labor organization is established by the lower opinion's rejection of the Association's objection. The evidence is reviewed favorably to the verdict, with reasonable inferences for DeAngelis; the appellate opinion's description of her causation proof as weak is not a binding fact.

**Statutory path A — affirm for insufficient objective severity or pervasiveness.** The ten reproduced columns, including the April 1990 item, are assessed together, including comments about women generally, four references associated with DeAngelis, extensive distribution, humiliation, mocking, alleged supervisory difficulty, and the satirical/editorial setting. Harris makes none of physical advances, supervisory power, psychological injury or reduced output indispensable. The sufficient ground for A is the character, intermittency and degree of the actual treatment as a whole, not an absent mandatory factor. Its strongest objection is that a jury could regard recurrent widely distributed sex-based attacks on the first female sergeant and their workplace effects as an alteration of working conditions. An affirming Justice must answer that verdict-protective objection, rather than simply prefer the appellate view of credibility.

**Statutory path B — set aside the hostile-environment reversal and remand.** Harris forbids a requirement that employment opportunity be destroyed or that work performance palpably deteriorate. The lower opinion's repeated emphasis on actual work impairment, no advances, no superior harasser, and successful performance can have applied that forbidden threshold to an otherwise triable totality. Its strongest objection is that the panel also recited Harris correctly and gave an independent contextual assessment of these intermittent columns. A Justice taking B therefore identifies why the legal threshold materially affected the verdict review rather than treating every mention of those relevant circumstances as error. B does not reinstate damages: the preserved speech defense was not decided below and must be addressed before liability can stand, together with any remaining properly preserved damages issues.

**Distinct constitutional path C — affirm on a defined as-applied expressive ground even if the statutory threshold could be satisfied.** Hurley and the protected-expression authorities prevent treating every offensive editorial message as independently unlawful conduct merely by calling it harassment. The strongest objection is Mitchell's preservation of antidiscrimination regulation and relevant expressive evidence, coupled with Harris's recognition that verbal abuse can change working conditions. C would require a sufficiently specific account of what is being punished and why its connection to workplace discrimination does not remove protection. No Justice below makes C an independently sufficient present commitment; the affirmed statutory ground avoids it, and the reversing commitments require its lawful resolution on remand. An assembly may not convert those reservations into a constitutional join.

The offered response channel is neither consent nor a prerequisite to suit. Neither the missing columns nor the unrecovered transcript supplies new facts. No Landgraf damages defense is introduced: it was not raised below. Neither an actual retaliatory lawsuit nor an unreported job or membership sanction is assumed.

## 18. United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra

### Petition posture and independent components

January 6 is consideration of a single Rule 12.4 petition concerning two judgments. There is no assumed earlier grant. Every Associate would grant review; their choices between immediate summary merits action and plenary review are recorded separately below. Any public order must state only the action and published positions, not this internal poll.

**Merits path A — reject the categorical acquitted-conduct bar.** A general acquittal establishes failure of proof beyond reasonable doubt, not that each underlying fact was affirmatively disproved. Section 3661 permits sentencing information; the relevant Guidelines direct consideration of the firearm connection or related quantity. Dowling supports the difference in proof burdens. Witte supplies a bounded characterization of relevant-conduct punishment but expressly reserved acquitted conduct. McMillan independently supports ordinary judicial sentencing findings within existing statutory authority. A complete A opinion must address double jeopardy, due process and jury allocation separately. Its strongest objection is that mandatory Guidelines findings change the binding range, so using the acquitted episode can impose a concrete additional punishment notwithstanding the unchanged statutory ceiling. Neither the statutory text nor the different proof burdens answers that objection without the sentencing characterization and constitutional limits.

**Merits path B — sustain relief against these mandatory increases.** A judge may consider information about an acquitted episode without making it the legal predicate for additional mandatory imprisonment. The defendants' strongest distinction is between information relevant to discretion and a compulsory range increase for the very episode charged and acquitted. The strongest objection to B is McMillan's acceptance of mandatory sentencing consequences within the authorized punishment and Dowling's refusal to turn every acquittal into factual nonoccurrence. A B commitment must state why mandatory use here implicates the acquittal or substantive offense protection rather than presume that Gaudin decided the sentencing classification.

**Procedural path P — plenary review before a merits decision.** Witte and Gaudin reserve material questions; one may regard the mandatory Guidelines/acquittal combination as requiring full treatment even if its likely legal resolution favors the Government. The objection is that the Ninth Circuit's categorical rule can be corrected without settling every extreme increase, special verdict, statutory-maximum issue or exact calculation. P is not an affirmance of either sentence-vacatur rationale.

No Justice finds a special acquittal determination of factual nonoccurrence, a particular final sentence, criminal-history category or extraordinary sentence magnification. Watts's gun-possession connection is distinct from the full §924(c) offense, but no Justice reconstructs the acquittal's basis. Putra's second transaction is distinct from her conviction transaction; its relevant-conduct treatment cannot be defended merely by pretending it was the convicted sale. No exact resentencing calculation is supported.

## 19. Old Chief v. United States

### Admissibility alternatives and distinct remedial law

**Path A — Rule 403 error.** The prior judgment is relevant under Rule 401 despite the offer. Rule 403 evaluates probative value in context, including a legally adequate available admission. Where the sole legitimate purpose is the qualifying prior-conviction status, an admission establishing that legal status supplies the needed proof without disclosing a similar violent offense. The prior assault's name and nature add little legitimate value for this status element while presenting a substantial propensity danger in this assault/firearm prosecution. The strongest objection is the Government's ordinary right to offer coherent, credible evidence rather than a defendant's abstract substitute, and the trial judge's Rule 403 discretion. A is limited to the status proof and this unfair-prejudice comparison; ordinary narrative proof of the charged conduct remains different.

**Path B — no Rule 403 abuse.** Congress made a qualifying conviction part of the offense; an authentic judgment is direct evidence of that element. An offer does not delete the element or render its normal proof irrelevant. The trial judge may rely on ordinary limiting safeguards and the prosecution's interest in proving its case, without being compelled to take a defendant's preferred substitute. The strongest objection is that an element's existence does not place every manner of proving it beyond Rule 403, especially where the judge treats prosecutorial choice as conclusive and the unnecessary information resembles the pending violent charges. A B commitment must defend admissibility in this specific balance, not assert that relevant evidence is always admissible.

For both paths, the offer must be clarified to admit a conviction satisfying §922(g)(1) as legally defined in §921(a)(20), including the exclusions and conviction-status qualifications reproduced in the validated packet. No excluded or extinguished conviction is found here. The wording problem was not the trial court's stated ground and is not an invented independent basis for affirmance. A legally adequate admission does not allow the defendant to withdraw status from the jury without proof or stipulation/instruction establishing that element.

**Remedy is separate.** Williamson's controlling Part III already governs preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error: Rule 52(a)/Kotteakos require the Government to establish harmlessness through whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness. Roy concerns a different constitutional personal-intent error and supplies no automatic Chapman rule here. The Ninth Circuit never assessed harmlessness, and the supplied record cannot support a final harmlessness decision. Error therefore leads to vacatur and remand for that inquiry and lawful consequences, not automatic retrial. The separate credibility instruction's request and withdrawal do not waive the preserved objection to admitting the judgment.

## 20. Walmer v. Department of Defense

### Defined posture, scrutiny alternatives, and independent attainder issue

The January 13 event is appellate review of a preliminary-injunction denial under the earlier executive Army regulation. The approved live-imminent-discharge calendar is a scheduling premise, not evidence that a historical stay persisted. No later discharge, reinstatement, service-status change or Supreme Court interim order is inferred. The Board proceeded on the admitted acts and expressly excluded the other misconduct allegations. Walmer stipulated that the regulation's retention criteria did not apply to her. Those exceptions are real; no ruling on their content, validity or availability to a different claimant is proposed.

**Equality path A — affirm denial under ordinary rational-basis review.** Romer preserves conceivable legitimate grounds and reasonable predictions while forbidding bare harm or an irrational class disadvantage. A conduct-based personnel rule within military service is materially different from Amendment 2's sweeping foreclosure of ordinary civil protection. Maintaining military discipline, morale and workable service relationships is legitimate; the military can make reasonable classwide judgments about those interests without proving actual impairment by each officer. The absence of an individualized impairment finding and the availability of more individualized rules do not alone negate that relationship. The strongest objection is that generic references to morale can disguise prejudice, and Rich's fraudulent-entry record is materially different. A requires judicial examination of the actual rule and its plausible relation to military personnel administration; it cannot rest on the word military, on Rich as a factual identity, on missing documents as a failure by Walmer, or on an automatic inference from Bowers.

**Equality path B — vacate the equality-based denial and remand for renewed preliminary-relief consideration under Romer.** Rich's fraudulent-entry judgment does not itself answer the present acts-based classification. Romer and Heller require a relation between the actual exclusion and a permissible purpose; military deference does not convert the older decision into categorical foreclosure. The lower opinion's failure to assess that distinct relation after treating Rich as conclusive warrants renewed consideration of whether a serious merits question is shown and, if necessary, the other equitable factors. Its strongest objection is that ordinary review permits conceivable reasons, requires no governmental evidentiary presentation, and places the preliminary-relief burden on Walmer. B therefore cannot order an injunction because the Government failed to produce individualized impairment evidence, and cannot silently adopt Pruitt's distinct actual-justification burden.

**Heightened-scrutiny alternative C.** An orientation-based classification could be subjected to a newly adopted more demanding standard, after a supported justification for that constitutional change. Neither Romer, Kelley nor Virginia already supplies that holding; Bowers does not decide the equality tier either. None of the eight Associates commits to adopt a new tier in this preliminary, stipulated-conduct case. Their views below retain ordinary rationality and differ over its application and the legal adequacy of the lower analysis. No actual-objective proof burden, least-restrictive-means requirement or entitlement to individual performance adjudication is introduced.

**Attainder component D.** All eight reject the present extension of the Bill of Attainder Clause to this executive regulation. The Clause's legislative-action predicate, reflected in Nixon, Selective Service System and Ambassador Books, is not met by the regulatory action presented. Walmer did not claim on appeal that this was legislation; she sought extension to executive military regulation. This conclusion does not immunize executive action from other constitutional restraints and does not decide whether separation would constitute punishment if the missing legislative predicate existed. No unpreserved APA or independent procedural claim is revived.

The other preliminary-relief factors—irreparable injury, balance of harms and public interest—were never found satisfied. The court of appeals' assumption served only its likelihood inquiry. Even B therefore permits only lawful reconsideration, not automatic entry of an injunction or a final declaration of unconstitutional separation.
