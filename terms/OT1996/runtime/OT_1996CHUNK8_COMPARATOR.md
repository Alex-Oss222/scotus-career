# 84. Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board; Price v. Bossier Parish School Board

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded with all nine Justices agreeing to the remand. The 7–2 division concerns whether a § 2 violation independently establishes prohibited § 5 effect; Stevens and Souter also concurred in remanding the case. Preserve the issue-specific division separately from the formal judgment.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court. Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas joined in full; Justices Ginsburg and Breyer joined except insofar as Part III conflicted with Justice Breyer's concurrence. Justice Thomas concurred. Justice Breyer concurred in part and in the judgment, joined by Justice Ginsburg. Justice Stevens dissented in part and concurred in part, joined by Justice Souter.
- **Historical Holding:** Section 5 effect is limited to retrogression and does not independently incorporate § 2. Evidence of dilution and less dilutive alternatives may nevertheless be relevant to discriminatory purpose. The Court left open whether the purpose prong extends beyond retrogressive intent.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court at the joined scope, with separate writings on the unresolved purpose boundary.
- **Historical Sources:** Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board, 520 U.S. 471 (1997).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/520bv.pdf#page=566).

# 86. Reyes-Hernandez v. INS

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR

No historical Supreme Court merits decision in this matter is established by the verified record. The user-authorized grant is counterfactual; no historical vote or opinion is supplied.

## B. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT RESULT

The Seventh Circuit held §§440(a) and 440(d) inapplicable to the stated pre-enactment concession and colorable-defense situation, exercised jurisdiction, and denied review on the §212(c) merits. The lower court's historical reasoning is a comparator and a litigant position, not a simulated Supreme Court holding. # 89. Edwards v. Balisok

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia delivered the Opinion of the Court for a unanimous Court. Justice Ginsburg concurred, joined by Justices Souter and Breyer, to distinguish procedural defects that would not necessarily imply invalidity.
- **Historical Holding:** Claims based on hearing-officer deceit and bias were not cognizable because success necessarily implied invalidity of the good-time deprivation. Prospective relief ordinarily could proceed and required remand; the district court should dismiss, not stay, barred claims.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Edwards v. Balisok, 520 U.S. 641 (1997).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/520bv.pdf#page=736).

# 92. Clinton v. Jones

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. Justice Breyer concurred in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** The Constitution does not grant a sitting President temporary immunity from federal civil litigation concerning unofficial conduct that occurred before office. - **Historical Sources:** Clinton v. Jones, 520 U.S. 681 (1997).
