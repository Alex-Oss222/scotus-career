## 54. Wright v. MetroHealth Medical Center

**Reconciled disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the Sixth Circuit judgment **insofar as it resolves the granted federal marriage and equal-protection questions**. They leave the ungranted Ohio-law Rule 12(b)(6) dispositions undisturbed without reviewing or adopting their merits. There is no new judgment against PHI, which settled during the Sixth Circuit appeal and ceased to be a party to that appeal. The Court neither reopens nor construes that settlement.

**Common rule and corrected application:** Marriage is a fundamental liberty. A public-employment rule does not require heightened scrutiny merely because marriage triggers an assignment consequence; the asserted interference must directly and substantially burden the decision to marry. Actual operation matters. Completing the marriage, avoiding a completed discharge or obtaining temporary litigation protection is not by itself dispositive of the constitutional claim.

The policy addresses immediate family members serving in positions requiring close interaction. Paragraph C permits the affected employees to choose which spouse moves, provides a thirty-calendar-day default under which the less senior hospital employee moves if no choice is made, and promises all reasonable attempts to furnish comparable responsibility and pay. That promise is an effort obligation, not an unconditional guarantee. John worked for PHI rather than the hospital, so the policy's general employee-choice/default language does not establish that both Wrights were hospital employees.

The particular reported offer to Martha was a transfer to any available position of the same grade and pay in another unit, which she declined. This actual offer is distinct from the policy's qualified general promise. It supplies no finding about an unreported specific vacancy, guaranteed future compensation or the validity of every possible transfer.

The burden analysis also includes the proposed Louisiana relocation. After the marriage, MetroHealth sought John's transfer and PHI announced a Louisiana transfer effective January 19, 1993. That was a threatened geographic disruption, not mere disappointment over a schedule, but the record establishes no completed move, discharge or demonstrated pay reduction. The previously offered same-grade/pay hospital transfer provided a reported alternative to preserving both LifeFlight assignments. After suit was filed, defendants agreed not to transfer John and instead to transfer Martha; during trial they agreed that Martha would remain in LifeFlight, with schedules preventing shared flights. These later arrangements neither erase the original threat nor become a permanent promise against transfer.

Taken in its actual sequence, this record does not establish direct and substantial interference with entering marriage. A different record of unavoidable separation, lost livelihood, absent alternatives or a coercive marriage barrier requires its own assessment; none is approved here.

**Ordinary-review holding:** The close-interaction assignment restriction rationally relates to conflicts, supervision, safety and effective emergency teamwork. The spouses' work in the same operational unit matters despite their nominally different employers. Rational-basis review allows reasonable predictions and imperfect classifications without demanding an accident, proof that every married pair is unsafe or adoption of the least restrictive schedule. The successful interim scheduling alternative does not itself establish irrationality. Heller and Romer still require a relationship to the actual disadvantage and do not permit bare harm or irrational prejudice; Tracy illustrates a supported operational distinction without establishing a new rule about marriage.

**Precedent and scope:** Loving and Zablocki remain controlling protections for the decision to marry; Jobst supplies the distinction between substantial marriage interference and incidental marital-status consequences. No general immunity for anti-nepotism rules, suspect marital-status classification, right to a particular joint assignment, compulsory individualized exception, mandatory safety study or constitutional approval of livelihood loss follows. No new First Amendment association, career-liberty, sex-discrimination or Ohio-law holding is entered.
