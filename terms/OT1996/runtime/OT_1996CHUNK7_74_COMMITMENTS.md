## Reach, question and common legal ground

All eight Associate Justices reach the municipal hiring question on §1254(1) review of the final appellate judgment. All nine seated Justices are supplied as participating at argument and decision; no recusal or other exclusion is established. Ordinary majority and six-member quorum rules apply. The October 23, 1995 substitute opinion, 67 F.3d 1174, is the judgment under review, not the withdrawn 53 F.3d opinion.

The County stipulated that Moore was the final policymaker for the relevant hiring decision. The underlying officer injury stands for this granted municipal inquiry. The Court does not retry Burns's force, false-arrest, false-imprisonment or immunity verdicts, restore the separate earnings award, adjudicate dismissed individual claims against Moore, or resolve ungranted state-law issues. The earnings preservation problem is not imported as waiver of the County's hiring sufficiency objection.

All eight accept the following legal ground, while dividing on its application:

- Monell requires the municipality's own attributable act, the applicable fault and an actual causal connection to the federal deprivation; employing the tortfeasor is insufficient. The already established injury and stipulated final policymaker do not themselves establish those remaining elements.
- Pembaur allows a final policymaker's single deliberate choice to constitute municipal policy in appropriate circumstances. A single hire is neither automatically immune nor automatically actionable. No new requirement of a repeated hiring pattern follows solely from the act's being facially lawful.
- Canton rejects limiting §1983 to policies unconstitutional in their terms. For this facially lawful hiring decision, deliberate indifference must concern the constitutional risk presented by selecting this applicant for these duties, and liability must rest on a close causal connection to the actual injury. Negligent screening, even serious administrative shortcomings, is insufficient by itself.
- Tuttle prevents inferring a culpable municipal policy solely from one subordinate violation. Here the hiring act is independently identified; that distinction removes a policy-inference shortcut but does not remove fault or causation.
- Collins separates underlying federal deprivation from municipal attribution. Leatherman's ordinary pleading standard does not relax trial proof. Plakas preserves separately necessary deprivation, attribution, culpability and causation; it establishes no universal hiring rule. Swint supplies no municipal immunity. Nabozny preserves separate municipal proof even when the individual wrong is shown. Blessing addresses individually enforceable statutory rights and does not replace this constitutional municipal framework.

The record includes a long course of traffic/alcohol-related matters and specified assault-and-battery and resisting-arrest entries; it does not turn each entry into a conviction or establish unreported particulars of a prior assault. Moore obtained a printout and did not examine it adequately or pursue further information. Both experts supported further inquiry; their testimony about probable abuse is evidence, not a judicially established prediction. Burns was the son of Moore's nephew. Firearm and squad-car restrictions do not eliminate the actual assignment to coercive law-enforcement duties. State law's felony-or-moral-turpitude limit is retained, without deciding whether it was violated or treating it as the federal standard.

## Enhanced issue-specific alternatives

**Attribution versus actionable fault.** The strongest County path invokes Tuttle and Pembaur's distinction between directly ordering unlawful conduct and a facially lawful employment choice. It properly insists that merely identifying the final hiring policymaker cannot settle municipal liability. Its categorical variant fails for all eight because Canton permits a facially lawful policy to cause a constitutional injury through deliberate indifference, and Pembaur does not immunize every single choice. The strongest plaintiff path therefore identifies the decision independently and still proves fault and cause; it cannot stop at the stipulation. All eight agree at that level.

**Sufficiency: applicant-specific constitutional risk versus general unsuitability.** The four reversal votes accept the complete favorable account and all reasonable factual inferences, including deliberate inattention and familial favoritism. Their legal objection is that an obvious need to investigate and a prediction of general unfitness do not by themselves establish the necessary obvious constitutional-force risk and close causal connection. In their application, the cited traffic, drinking, identification and unelaborated assault/resistance history, even in combination, supports the former without sufficiently establishing the latter. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy's Canton writing is the direct personal source for refusing that step; Thomas's actual Plakas join preserves the same separation. They do not demand proof of a prior identical incident or decide that a misdemeanor history can never suffice.

The strongest objection to reversal is that it underweights the history in its actual setting: force is a recurring part of arrest work, the applicant's documented disregard of restraint included violent/resisting behavior indicators, and the sheriff knowingly bypassed the check used for others. Stevens's full Canton join and the other affirming Justices' actual Plakas/Nabozny positions permit attribution without a literal order to injure. Those four therefore allow the jury's specific risk inference. They do not establish the truth of unreported prior offenses, but rely on the record entries, the sheriff's treatment of them and the expert testimony as evidence of the risk in the entrusted duties. They require a close connection to coercive misuse rather than an undifferentiated risk of employee misconduct. Their ruling would not sustain the verdict merely because Brown would have escaped injury had Burns never been hired.

**Causation as an independent requirement.** County policy could not authorize Burns to commit unconstitutional force merely by authorizing arrests. That is the strongest objection to the lower court's causation phrasing, and every Justice rejects that phrasing as an automatic rule. The four affirming votes read the evidence as supporting more: the very abuse risk created by deliberately overlooking this applicant's history in assigning coercive duties came to pass. The four reversing votes find the evidence too general to establish that link at Canton's required constitutional specificity. Neither group attributes every deputy action to Moore or replaces the federal injury with a right to good screening.

**Remand alternative.** Clarifying the demanding relationship between the particular applicant and the particular constitutional risk could support vacatur and a fresh application if the case had been tried only under a materially lesser, unavailable standard. The strongest support is Canton's majority remand, which Stevens joined. The opposing consideration is that this jury was expressly charged with deliberate indifference, an obvious scrutiny need, and likely constitutional violations, and Brown had a full trial and expert opportunity directed at those issues. The four reversal votes therefore resolve legal sufficiency and require County judgment rather than invite a second evidentiary opportunity. They do not rest their result solely on an unpreserved instruction objection. The four affirming votes find the actual verdict sufficient under the demanding rule and have no reason to vacate; none provisionally votes for a new trial.
