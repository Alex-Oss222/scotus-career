## Supported facts and common legal limits

### Actual work and relationship

Contribution is distinct from substantial connection. Painting and chipping may contribute to a vessel's mission; Wilander does not require traditional navigation duties. A docked vessel with idle engines is not automatically withdrawn from navigation. The declaration supports operational readiness subject to its knowledge limits, and no Associate makes a new conclusive finding against navigation.

The known injury assignment was one day of dockside painting with no planned sailing and no commitment to return. The three or four specifically recalled prior Pt. Barrow jobs were dockside chipping and painting; the immediately preceding identified Sea Fox job was maintenance. Those facts do not prove that every other Harbor Tug assignment was maintenance. Twelve prior Harbor Tug occasions are supported, but the thirteen-day formulations do not authorize a reconstructed calendar or sea-voyage count.

Secchitano specifically describes replacement of a more regularly assigned deckhand, staffing the two-deckhand complement with Papai and Low, operational-crew dispatch rather than a separate maintenance gang, and contractual separation from shoreside work. These are material assertions about staffing and assignment; her federal-status characterization is not binding. The conflicting dispatch times are not resolved. Low's move to Sea King does not prove Papai's sea duties. Dawson's disputed descriptions and later trial citations do not themselves expand Papai's obligations.

The general 1987–1989 testimony that approximately seventy percent of the work was deckhand work, mostly on moving boats, concerns the broader hiring-hall period. It is actual-work testimony, not merely a contractual possibility, but is not a proved Harbor Tug percentage. Respondent's assertion that all qualified-deckhand work from January 1, 1989 was for Harbor Tug remains attributed; the unproduced cited trial pages and payroll do not become an uncontested fact. The forty-day Golden Gate engagement was shore maintenance, not a forty-day sea assignment; the ALJ's different short-job description remains an attributed administrative characterization.

The agreement's line handling, helmsmanship, lookout, winch, maintenance and underway engine-check provisions describe qualifications and possible duties. They may illuminate the employment relationship but do not prove performance of each duty. No majority or dissent rests on twelve assumed voyages, a fabricated temporal percentage, a categorical requirement of permanent employment, or the proposition that established crew members lose their status whenever they paint a docked vessel.

### Competing status rules and their precise joins

All eight accept the controlling requirement of contribution plus substantial connection in nature and duration and the conclusive-record boundary, subject to their recorded different reasons for judgment in Chandris. The approximately thirty-percent guide remains nonabsolute at its actual five-Justice scope, with justified departures and genuine changed assignments; no result here is reached by calculating an unsupported percentage.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas adopt the present clarification that the aggregate fleet connection requires common ownership or common operational control. A common hiring hall alone supplies labor, not unitary direction of vessels. No multiemployer bargaining or operational arrangement beyond the validated record is found. The clarification does not disqualify every hiring-hall seaman or every one-day assignment. The same-employer theory must still be considered separately.

Those five regard the nature of this engagement as conclusive: accepting the declaration's factual staffing account does not alter the limited duties actually undertaken. Papai was hired for a discrete maintenance job, not for an existing continuing sea-service obligation temporarily including painting. The specifically identified prior tasks and absence of a supported different ongoing obligation do not establish a substantial sea-based relationship through repeated employer dispatches. General occupational sea experience and contract qualifications do not fill that gap. The issue is the whole accepted engagement, not an isolated instant of injury. No adverse finding about every unidentified prior job is needed. Thomas agrees with this present application of governing law and also regards actual ship's-company service as insufficiently shown; no retroactive full-framework Chandris join is recorded.

Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer reject making unitary ownership/control indispensable in this hiring arrangement. The supported history of recurring deckhand work through the market from which the operators obtain their crews can identify the relevant employment relationship without requiring permanent assignment to one owner. A vessel owner who uses that labor source is not dealing with an unknown occupation merely because daily dispatch varies. This is not the proposition that any collection of vessels a worker happens to visit becomes a fleet, or that maritime contribution alone proves status. They must still identify actual seaman work and a substantial relationship under the applicable rule; the ordinary dockside maintenance task cannot alone erase a supported status relationship.

The strongest objection is that this approach loses a workable employer/group boundary and makes coverage depend on unrelated assignments an employer may not know. They answer that the known character of the shared crew market and actual employment at the time of injury supply a relevant connection, while a rigid owner boundary may systematically exclude workers doing the same seaman work solely because operators use daily hiring. They also preserve the narrower supported alternative: the specific replacement-role evidence and repeated Harbor Tug service permit, without compelling, an inference of actual crew integration. Souter and Ginsburg's initial common full-framework reasoning cannot be treated as a continuing two-Justice join after Souter's correction; Ginsburg retains her own full-framework application, while Stevens and Breyer retain their actual-crew rationale. No Marks majority is manufactured from different grounds.

## Actual administrative award and distinct legal theories

The award is real and final. The June 2, 1992 hearing involved counsel, evidence, an additional medical deposition and briefs. Jurisdiction/status was expressly disputed. The ALJ addressed Harbor Tug's crew-exclusion contention, noted the contrary civil positions, refused preclusive force to the interlocutory district orders and applied Wilander with a Robison-style permanent-assignment-or-substantial-work formulation. He found random, sporadic and transitory assignments and no qualifying connection. This was not a mere voluntary check or a compromise without a status determination. Neither a finding that the order used only an absolute permanence test nor a conclusive finding of identity under every aspect of Chandris is made here.

The order awarded some benefits and denied others, supplied credit, interest and calculation procedures, and did not grant every requested injury or wage item. It was signed August 27 and filed/served August 31, 1992; no appeal was taken and finality is accepted. Section 921(a) uses filing and thirty-day finality absent prescribed review; §921(c)'s sixty days concern a final Board order, not the initial ALJ appeal. No October 27 initial deadline, exact last-day calculation, absolute inability to appeal, missed modification deadline or last-payment date is invented. The February 2/February 5 internal medical-date discrepancy is not resolved because it does not decide this appeal.

**Separate statutory question.** Section 905(a) makes prescribed LHWCA employer liability exclusive when it applies, including the enumerated derivative claimants. Section 902(3)(G) excludes crew members. Harbor Tug claims that a formal final order conclusively activates exclusivity without ordinary issue-preclusion elements; Papai denies that legal consequence. Gizoni decided voluntary unadjudicated payments and expressly reserved formal awards. Its reservation is not an already controlling answer either for or against the employer. Mutual exclusivity of ultimate coverage does not by itself determine the collateral effect of an order. The final-order theory must be analyzed separately from initial election, judicial estoppel and issue preclusion.

**Separate administrative question.** Astoria favors final administrative adjudication subject to the actual issue, judicial character, adequate opportunity and statutory purpose. Congress can displace that presumption implicitly; no clear-statement rule is required. Astoria's ADEA outcome neither guarantees a second judicial trial after every federal award nor establishes this award's bar. The employer's strongest ordinary-preclusion case is substantial: the same parties actually litigated crew status as a necessary coverage predicate, the ALJ decided it, and finality followed. Possible error or later legal clarification alone does not nullify finality. Conversely, a hearing count does not automatically decide issue identity, adequate opportunity, review objections or congressional displacement.

**Five Associates reserve both questions.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas do not need either theory to deny seaman remedies on their status ground. Their reservation is not agreement with the lower court's reasoning, a new preclusion holding, a finding that the ALJ was wrong, or a vacatur requiring reconsideration of award effects as a condition of their status disposition. Souter's authorship of Astoria makes the contrary alternative particularly important but does not require an unnecessary ruling. Thomas receives no fictional Astoria or Gizoni join. None of these five treats the ALJ's findings as adverse summary-judgment facts merely because its existence can be judicially noticed.

**Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer reject foreclosure of this claim.** They would retain the court of appeals' result on this unreviewed formal award while preserving the pending judicial status contest. Their legal ground extends, rather than merely repeats, Gizoni: the statutory accommodation of compensation during a disputed seaman claim and of credits upon an overlapping recovery indicates that Congress did not make this administrative route an unintended surrender of the pending Jones Act determination. Section 905(a)'s exclusive-liability command does not, in their view, independently turn the existence of this order into an immutable classification outside the ordinary status and statutory inquiry. Under Astoria's contextual-intent approach, the same statutory structure counsels against giving this award the ordinary collateral effect the employer requests. The initial availability of two routes would be materially diminished if access to adjudicated subsistence compensation itself disposed of the pending seaman controversy, although voluntary payments left it open.

They acknowledge the strongest objection: the statutes can prohibit duplicate recovery while still honoring final adjudication, and §903(e) expressly credits other compensation or Jones Act amounts against LHWCA liability rather than providing a universal reciprocal preclusion exemption. They nevertheless regard the credit mechanism and Gizoni's overlapping-pursuit reasoning as evidence of the statutory accommodation in this posture, not just an arithmetical anti-double-recovery rule. Ordinary preclusion is a default rule of statutory construction, not an independent command immune to that context. They reject both asserted routes to foreclosure here without finding that the hearing was fictitious or that all common-law elements failed.

Their reasoning does not depend on a factual finding that Harbor Tug maliciously forced this hearing. Years of voluntary payments and the disputed added-back-injury account are preserved. The role of inconsistent positions is limited: the schemes place the parties on opposing sides of crew status, which informs their assessment of the statutory arrangement and incentives; it does not automatically establish judicial estoppel or inadequate litigation. The employer's disclaimer of affirmative election is respected. This is not a universal rule about a consensual §8(i) settlement, a prior judicially reviewed status judgment, every subsequent claim, or every compensation provision. The present award remains operative in its own system; no benefit cancellation or collateral modification follows. The historical dissent's express case-specific agreement, the actual lower grounds and each Justice's own statutory approach support this position; missing personal votes before Ginsburg and Breyer joined the Court are not invented.
