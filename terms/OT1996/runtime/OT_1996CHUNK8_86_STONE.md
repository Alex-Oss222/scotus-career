## A. STATUS AND PROPOSED ANSWERS

**User-approved position, reasoning and conditional fallbacks — October 11, 2026.** These remain proposals to the Court, not completed holdings or votes. The answers apply Stone’s documented source-based commitments to the actual record.

**Question 1:** Stone would decline to apply either provision to extinguish this already invoked waiver-and-review path on the narrow, uncontested premise accepted below: the pre-enactment concession surrendered a colorable defense in order to pursue the then-available relief. He would analyze the provisions separately. Section 440(d) would remove the avenue of discretionary eligibility; §440(a) would remove the review that formed part of the asserted completed choice. Neither provision contains the clear temporal direction needed for that retrospective consequence. This is a proposed application of simulated Landgraf, not a universal exemption for everyone convicted or placed in proceedings before April 24.

**Question 2:** Yes, when the identified legal change genuinely attaches a new disability to a completed litigation act. Landgraf requires examination of the act and its consequences, not an automatic pending-case exception. Felker enforced a review restriction governing new post-enactment authorization proceedings; Reyes's completed concession presents a different temporal fact. Stone would reject both a categorical immunity for pending proceedings and a categorical rule that a jurisdictional label defeats reliance.

**Question 3:** A colorable defense matters to this particular surrender-of-defense theory. If the alien had no legally available defense, merely admitting an objectively established ground ordinarily gives up nothing relevant, and the same inference of detrimental reliance cannot follow. A colorable defense is not a universal element that Stone would add to Landgraf for every plea, transaction or statutory remedy. The opinion says the Government did not contest the premise in this case; Stone would decide on that record rather than invent a particular defense or order factfinding simply to reopen an uncontested premise.

**Additional vehicle question 4:** Stone would reject Reyes's challenges to the reasoned waiver denial and the Board's treatment of the supplemental brief. The immigration judge considered the family hardship and rehabilitation, but permissibly assigned greater weight to repeated serious drug violations. The Board's adoption of that reasoned decision did not dispense with analysis. The subsequently tendered evidence had not been admitted into the adjudicatory record; requiring the established motion to reopen or remand permits orderly fact development rather than disregarding evidence already admitted. The Board expressly described that channel as remaining available. Abudu supplies its existing limits; Doherty supplies no majority reopening rationale, and Stone would not treat his own separate writing there as governing precedent. If the record instead showed admitted material evidence ignored by the agency, the procedural analysis would differ, but that is not the reported record.

## B. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

**Affirm the Seventh Circuit's judgment denying the petition for review**, on the bounded temporal premise and merits grounds above. Do not grant §212(c) relief, order a new hearing automatically, or adjudicate a later reopening motion. The user-approved fourth question supplies the adverse merits issue necessary for this complete disposition; the first three questions remain antecedent jurisdictional objections rather than an appeal solely from favorable reasoning.
