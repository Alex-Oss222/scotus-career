## A. APPROVAL AND INDIVIDUAL COMMITMENTS

The user approves these answers, reasoning and stated fallback as Stone’s proposed position. The Master treats aggregation as procedure governed by its text, measures commonality by a materially useful common answer, assesses predominance claim by claim and issue by issue, and preserves substantive elements and genuine jury questions. It rejects guilt or special protection merely from an entity’s size. Those commitments require scrutiny both of this plan and of the writ stopping it.

## B. COMPLETE ANSWERS

**Question 1 — issue certification is available, but the Court cannot use an invented average negligence law.** Stone rejects a rule that mass torts or differing state laws invariably prevent a useful issue class. Rule 23(c)(4)(A) permits particular-issue treatment. The judge must identify a genuinely common question and show that its answer advances affected claims under their actual law. A dated fact about one manufacturer’s knowledge may qualify; a generalized finding of negligence under multiple different duties may not.

This nationwide negligence proposal lacks demonstrated legal equivalence for its intended use. Foreseeability is especially consequential to the theory that hepatitis precautions would have prevented a different, then-unidentified disease. Whether professional custom fixes or merely informs reasonable care can also change the result. Gasperini and O’Melveny require actual state rules. Stone would demand supported governing-law analysis, correctly defined issues or coherent subclasses before such a verdict could operate; inconvenience cannot supply missing substantive law. He would not decide the underlying hepatitis or HIV negligence claims.

**Question 2 — certification cannot sacrifice defenses or jury rights, but stakes, settlement pressure and differing verdicts are not independent prohibitions.** Predominance and superiority require a workable lawful plan, not a count of repeated facts. Stone would identify what the first jury resolves and what later juries must still resolve. A broad negligence finding that later federal juries must partly reassess in comparative fault or foreseeability-based proximate cause cannot become conclusive merely through labels. Separability must be demonstrated by the actual questions, applicable law and instructions. Genuinely independent factual findings remain possible; this record does not establish that a sufficient revised plan already exists.

Lawfully different verdicts under different law or evidence are not themselves unconstitutional inconsistency. Defendants have no general right to repeat every common issue before many juries. Twelve favorable outcomes are not a valid adjudication of strangers’ claims or an established representative sample proving meritlessness. Aggregate exposure can warrant careful review of fairness, but joining valid claims does not weaken them. Stone would recognize neither punishment for corporate scale nor a bankruptcy-risk exemption from otherwise proper liability. He would apply Matsushita’s representation protection without treating notice as its substitute and preserve actual defenses without assuming either collective responsibility or collective innocence.

**Question 3 — ordinary error and predicted settlement do not establish mandamus; a demonstrated exceptional jury deprivation may.** Stone would enforce the writ threshold despite identifying serious certification concerns. The appellate court expressly assumed eventual final judgment for the named plaintiffs, allowing certification review. Its alternative inadequacy account depended substantially on estimated exposure and a predicted settlement. Digital Equipment and Swint do not themselves decide mandamus, but their governing finality rules reject a general immediate-review entitlement arising from litigation pressure; Allied Chemical requires more than ordinary reversible error.

Blodgett reinforces Stone’s obligation to examine concrete ordinary relief before approving an extraordinary writ. He applies that discipline to the district court’s capacity to revise this plan and the assumed final review, not to an invented failure to request reconsideration. The question remains whether those actual avenues adequately protect the right asserted; Blodgett does not foreclose a properly demonstrated jury-protection writ.

The strongest contrary argument is the jury right. Beacon Theatres permits protection before a jury entitlement is destroyed; Gasoline Products makes inseparable successive-jury factfinding a real concern. Stone nonetheless distinguishes an actually established, unavoidable deprivation from a forecast about a revisable trial plan. No jury has returned a verdict, the questions and instructions remain capable of correction, and ordinary review after the named plaintiffs’ judgment exists. On this supplied record he would find the showing inadequate for a writ commanding complete decertification. He does not require litigants invariably to await a completed violation: a definitively ordered inseparable scheme with no adequate ordinary protection could justify focused mandamus on a supported record.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION AND APPROVED FALLBACK

Reverse the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand with instructions to deny this mandamus petition. Certification remains subject to the district court’s ordinary power and duty to revise it before trial consistently with the clarified law. This disposition does not approve the nationwide negligence instruction, decree liability, validate future issue preclusion or prevent a properly supported narrower class. Explain the Erie and jury limits without supplying a judicially invented replacement trial plan or reopening individual judgments.

If the Court concludes that the already-ordered bifurcation establishes an unavoidable jury violation sufficient for mandamus, Stone’s approved fallback is to support only the focused relief needed to remove that inseparable scheme and permit reconsideration of a lawful issue definition. He would not join a categorical mass-tort ban, inferred defense victory from unrelated trials or settlement pressure as a stand-alone writ ground. The fallback identifies the factual and legal condition on which it depends; it does not pronounce that condition established.

* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged conduct may powerfully support discriminatory purpose, but legal consequences do not transfer between doctrines. Nabozny’s due-process theories remain centered on omissions and a climate of nonenforcement. Any preserved allegation of discouraging police protection must be assessed for actual obstruction and causal contribution, not dismissed merely because it lacks a promise.

The District remains subject to *Monell* and receives no immunity. This denial also accords with Stone's narrower personal-defense commitment: the officials identify no binding precedent or enacted authority directly permitting deliberate discriminatory withholding of ordinary protection, and an absence of a factual twin is not authoritative permission. Because the assumed conduct defeats the received defense as well, this case need not announce a general replacement of Harlow. If trial changes the material facts, the court must decide the actual violation and any properly available defense separately; no second factual-reasonableness shield or automatic liability follows.

### Issue 1: Title VII religious accommodation

- **Stone's Legal Position:** Polk County need not permit Brown to divert a secretary or other public resources to Bible-study work, open a workspace otherwise closed to all personal use, or use supervisory authority for official worship or religious counseling that objectively burdens subordinates. It must consider accommodation for genuinely voluntary employee-initiated prayer, private religious conversation, and personal religious items not used as official directives, because speculative polarization, generalized discomfort, or offense is not undue hardship.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VII requires reasonable accommodation of private religious practice unless the employer proves a real, more-than-de-minimis operational burden. It does not require diversion of public resources, official worship, coercive supervision, or unequal personal use of the workplace.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The County's legitimate operational limits can be enforced through neutral rules. The record does not support a categorical conclusion that every private religious conversation, voluntary prayer, or personal item creates the burden Hardison requires. The en banc remand is therefore proper without converting statutory accommodation into an unlimited workplace privilege.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize favoritism, compulsory prayer, use of subordinates for private devotion, harassment, or religious personal use where comparable secular personal use is forbidden.

### Issue 2: Free Exercise and religion-specific directives

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial breadth of the directives.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The express order to cease anything that could be considered proselytizing, witnessing, or counseling and to remove every religious item substantially burdens exercise and is neither neutral nor confined to a demonstrated operational problem. Neutral limits on work time, employees, resources, coercion, and attributed official worship remain valid.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public employer may enforce neutral limits on resources, duties, harassment, and coercion, but a religion-specific ban on all witnessing, counseling, proselytizing, and personal religious items is unconstitutional.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The challenged language suppresses the religious character of private expression rather than isolating misuse of public authority. Narrower neutral rules address the County's lawful interests without treating religious viewpoint itself as the workplace harm.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ruling does not protect conduct that coerces subordinates, consumes public resources, or is delivered as an official directive.

### Issue 3: Employee speech and Establishment attribution

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing attribution and speech rules but leaves application to particular disputed episodes for remand.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The County may control expression delivered through Brown's supervisory duties as its own. Genuinely private citizen speech on public concern receives Pickering balancing against actual or reasonably forecast operational disruption, and the breadth of a prospective ban requires the stronger justification recognized in NTEU. Avoiding an objectively grounded Establishment violation is a legitimate reason for tailored restrictions, especially for supervisor-led prayer in mandatory meetings. Offense or abstract fear is insufficient.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Public-employer control turns on attribution and actual function. The County may regulate official, coercive, or resource-dependent religious expression but may not suppress genuinely private religious speech through an undifferentiated prospective ban.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Authorship, voluntariness, time, location, control, resource use, and supervisory pressure separate private expression from government worship. That source-specific inquiry preserves both constitutional neutrality and ordinary public-employer authority.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The decision does not require identical treatment of official worship and private conversation, and it does not make a statutory accommodation violation automatically dispositive of a constitutional claim.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved remand should compare each protected practice and materially distinct proposed accommodation with the actual operational justification. He need not require the County to try a measure already shown to impose Hardison hardship, create a universal negotiation duty, or regard an asserted Establishment concern as self-proving.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved answer.** Stone would permit the statutory protected-practice claims to proceed because the blanket treatment failed to distinguish feasible private observance from operationally burdensome uses of staff, resources or authority. The employer need not grant a preferred practice when an effective reasonable accommodation exists.

**Questions 2–3 — preserved approved answers.** Voluntary private expression does not automatically become governmental endorsement. Supervisor-led prayer at mandatory meetings and diversion of employees pose materially different coercion, attribution and resource concerns. Stone would sustain tailored neutral restrictions supported by those concerns but invalidate the undifferentiated religion-specific directives. His remand keeps statutory burden, speech attribution and Establishment analysis separate and does not require a trial accommodation or actual accident before a reasonable supported prediction can establish hardship.

Tax Court Rule 142(a) placed the burden of proving this disputed administration-expense adjustment on the Commissioner because that challenge was absent from the original deficiency notice; this is a case-specific allocation, not the ordinary burden for every estate-tax dispute. Stone adopts a material-burden inquiry only to the extent supported by the existing regulatory language; he does not create a free-floating equitable tax test. The opinion rests on the ground common to the competing readings, that a reduction of the deduction requires a material limitation on the qualifying interest, and it does not decide whether the deductions are measured as of the date of death or whether postmortem events may bear on their valuation.

The Court does not decide whether the deduction is fixed as of the date of death.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The taxable estate and qualifying interests are anchored to the property passing from the decedent, while postmortem income and fiduciary administration are treated through separate Code provisions. The Commissioner litigated a categorical position and did not develop a record or alternative calculation demonstrating material diminution under the Tax Court’s approach. Stone will not manufacture that showing or rewrite the regulations. The Commissioner remains able to promulgate a clearer prospective rule within statutory authority.

A governing instrument or actual administration that materially depletes or limits what passes to the spouse or charity may require adjustment. Section 642(g), principal-funded expenses, valuation disputes, and a properly preserved calculation method remain governed by their own provisions. Stone neither adopts nor rejects the date-of-death valuation theory, and the holding rests on materiality alone.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the due-process merits. The material features of Harper's release and summary return are undisputed.
