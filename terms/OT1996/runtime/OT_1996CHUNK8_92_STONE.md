# 92. Clinton v. Jones

# SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Current approval status:** Approved by the user on October 13, 2026 (Version 2). It replaces Stone's previously approved October 11, 2026 position in full. It records Stone's position for the judicial simulation, not a Court decision, completed vote, or finding that the allegations are true.

## A. STATUS AND REACH

- **Historical decision baseline:** May 27, 1997. Use only the record and precedent available to the simulated Court at that time.
- **Proposed disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment, allowing the federal civil action to proceed under ordinary procedural law.
- **Primary approach:** Reject a constitutional shield based on presidential status, explain the official/unofficial distinction through an objective functional framework, and reject a term-long stay unsupported by a concrete showing of interference with Article II duties.
- **Conditional fallback:** If the Court will not adopt an articulated objective framework or broader language concerning unofficial acts, decide the two questions actually presented: no temporary constitutional immunity for the pleaded pre-presidential conduct in this federal civil case, and no justification on this record for the categorical trial stay. Preserve the same judgment.
- **Limits:** The judgment does not determine Clinton's liability, resolve the merits of Jones's federal or state-law claims, decide a separate immunity defense to the post-inauguration defamation allegations, or announce binding rules for criminal prosecution or state-court jurisdiction.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment. The district court's rejection of the claimed constitutional immunity stands, and its advance order staying trial until the end of the presidential term cannot stand on this record. Stone reaches the proper result without necessarily adopting the Eighth Circuit's characterization of a discretionary trial stay as the functional equivalent of constitutional immunity.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The action returns to the district court. Discovery and trial may be scheduled under ordinary procedural law. The court may use protective orders, sequencing, deposition accommodations, written or remote procedures where authorized, or tailored delay if the party requesting relief establishes an actual and material conflict with a particular presidential function. The need, length, consequences, and available alternatives must be assessed specifically. There is no automatic stay for the President's term, and nothing in this judgment requires him to attend a specified court session in person.
- **No merits decision:** The allegations are taken as pleaded solely to adjudicate this immunity question. The district court must independently decide jurisdictional, merits, evidentiary, and other defenses in the ordinary course.

### Issue 1: Presidential status does not immunize unofficial civil misconduct

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves whether Article II or separation of powers gives a sitting President temporary immunity, or a presumptive suspension of federal civil litigation, for events preceding the Presidency. The litigation concerns conduct alleged to have begun when Clinton was Governor of Arkansas, not an exercise of his presidential functions.
- **Stone's Primary Legal Position:** The Constitution protects the independence of the Presidency in discharging its assigned functions. It does not create a personal privilege to avoid judicial adjudication of private conduct merely because the defendant later assumes the office. The concern that damages liability could distort future official decisionmaking, which explains Nixon v. Fitzgerald, has no comparable force when the cause of action does not arise from a presidential decision. The court exercises Article III power over a private controversy; it does not take over a presidential responsibility by adjudicating it.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A sitting President has no constitutional temporary immunity from a properly brought federal civil action seeking damages for alleged unofficial conduct that occurred before taking office. The Presidency alone neither extinguishes the claim nor constitutionally requires it to be held until the term ends.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Jones alleges private misconduct, retaliation, and other injuries arising out of events in 1991. Her allegations also refer to Clinton's gubernatorial position and state personnel. For purposes of presidential immunity, none of those 1991 acts could have been performed in execution of the presidency Clinton did not yet hold. Whether some alleged conduct constituted action under color of state law, and whether other state-office or personal defenses exist, are different questions. The Court neither accepts the allegations as established facts nor determines that any claim will ultimately succeed.
- **Broad explanatory principle, beyond the necessary holding:** The basis for presidential official-act immunity is the function, not the officeholder's personal identity. Stone's view is that a President's purely private acts do not become presidential functions by occurring during a presidential term. The facts of this appeal, however, require a binding ruling only on the pre-presidential conduct properly presented.

### Issue 2: Objective functional identification of official and unofficial acts

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The distinction between presidential official and unofficial conduct is indispensable to rejecting immunity here. Because the principal challenged events predate Clinton's presidency, the Court need not decide a contested classification of a presidential act. Stone nonetheless proposes the following objective framework to explain, and constrain, the distinction for future cases. Its detailed formulation is proposed guidance, not an independently necessary holding about hypothetical presidential acts.
- **Stone's Proposed Framework:** First, identify the conduct with appropriate factual specificity and the presidential authority or responsibility claimed to support immunity. Second, ask whether, on objectively established circumstances, the conduct performs, directs, supervises, or is otherwise genuinely related to a function entrusted to the President by the Constitution or federal law, including the outer perimeter recognized in Fitzgerald. Third, as a check against a purely self-serving characterization, ask whether a reasonably informed holder of the Presidency, knowing the powers and circumstances of the office, would understand the conduct to be an exercise of presidential responsibility rather than a private act. The judge decides the legal classification on the relevant record; the actor's assertion or subjective good faith does not decide it.
- **Role of a Reasonable Person:** An ordinary reasonable observer's assessment of the visible circumstances may help expose a claimed official function that is only a label. It cannot itself supply, enlarge, or remove constitutional power. Stone therefore favors an objective reasonable-officeholder check within a functional legal test, not a public-opinion test or a free-standing jury verdict about whether conduct seems presidential.
- **What Does Not Decide Classification:** The President's title, location, access to aides or security, use of public resources, or declaration that an activity was official does not by itself establish presidential function. Nor does the allegation that a decision was unlawful, unconstitutional, retaliatory, or motivated by personal considerations automatically make an otherwise official exercise of presidential authority unofficial. An objectively connected presidential function may remain an official act even if judicial review finds the act invalid. Whether personal civil damages are available is a separate immunity question.
- **Illustrative Application:** Directing military operations or supervising a federal department ordinarily concerns presidential functions, even when the legality of a particular direction is challenged. A private interpersonal encounter does not become presidential government merely because it occurs at an official event or involves an official's staff. The Jones allegations concern events when Clinton held a different office; the Court does not convert any disputed state-official conduct into presidential conduct or decide its separate treatment under civil-rights law.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding, limited to this record:** Acts alleged to have occurred before Clinton assumed the Presidency are not official presidential acts, and the federal immunity recognized in Fitzgerald cannot be invoked solely because he subsequently became President.
- **Boundary:** This framework does not overrule Fitzgerald, create a qualified-immunity standard for Presidents, classify the post-inauguration defamation claim, or decide what relief is available for a proven unconstitutional official act. It distinguishes whether an act is official from whether it is lawful and whether a particular remedy is permitted.

### Issue 3: No automatic postponement; demonstrated interference warrants accommodations

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides whether the district court permissibly directed that trial await the end of the presidential term while permitting proceedings to move in other respects. The Court does not decide any specific compulsory attendance order or future request supported by new evidence.
- **Stone's Primary Legal Position:** Courts owe serious respect to the President's constitutional functions, not automatic deference to a status-based request for delay. Any claimed interference must identify a presidential function and a substantial, case-specific conflict. The party requesting a stay must substantiate the need; the judge should consider the claimant's interest in timely adjudication, loss of evidence, available accommodations, and the duration of relief.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Article II does not require a categorical stay of private federal civil litigation against a sitting President, and the district court abused its discretion by imposing an advance, presidential-term-long trial stay without a developed showing of need and without sufficiently accounting for Jones's interest in timely resolution.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Trial could not yet be assessed against a concrete presidential scheduling conflict, while a years-long delay risked loss of evidence and prejudice. The district court retains real docket-management authority under Landis v. North American Co. That authority is not immunity; a later, properly supported request for limited postponement remains available. The Eighth Circuit's judgment should therefore be affirmed even if Stone declines to adopt its reasoning that any trial stay is automatically equivalent to a grant of immunity.
- **Boundary:** A reasonable accommodation is not a personal exemption from law. Neither the President's office nor the plaintiff's demand creates an absolute right to a particular date, place, or method of testimony.

### Issue 4: Broader presidential accountability principles that Stone announces but does not decide

- **Stone's Express Constitutional Position:** Stone rejects the idea that holding Article II office, by itself, licenses private misconduct or forecloses ordinary legal accountability. Courts may assess the legality of official executive action even when Fitzgerald bars a particular personal-damages remedy. Personal civil immunity, review of official actions, and criminal prosecution are different questions.
- **Official misconduct and constitutional violations:** Stone would be prepared, in a case properly raising the issue, to examine whether Fitzgerald should be reconsidered or limited in cases involving proved treason or grave, deliberate constitutional wrongdoing. A charge that an act is unlawful is not, standing alone, an exception to Fitzgerald, and this record cannot support a binding exception.
- **Federal and state criminal law:** Stone's broader principle is that private criminal conduct is not transformed into constitutionally authorized executive conduct merely because a President commits it. But criminal liability requires an applicable offense, jurisdiction, proof, and an answer to any relevant immunity or supremacy question. This civil appeal cannot decide the timing of prosecution of a sitting President, the full scope of federal or state criminal jurisdiction, or whether immunity protects a specified criminally charged official act.
- **State courts:** Whether a comparable civil suit may proceed in a state tribunal raises federalism and federal-officer concerns different from the federal separation-of-powers argument decided here. Stone reserves that question.
- **Post-inauguration defamation:** The complaint includes statements attributed to presidential agents after Clinton took office. Whether Fitzgerald protects any claim concerning those statements is not presented for resolution and remains open.
- **Status of this issue:** These are declared Stone views and reserved questions. They do not become holdings through placement in the opinion, and Stone does not require agreement with them as the price of joining the judgment.

## C. CONDITIONAL FALLBACK FOR THE COURT

If a majority will not join Stone's broader objective framework or the propositions concerning private conduct during an incumbent President's term, Stone will accept the following narrower grounds without abandoning his separate position:

1. **Federal pre-presidential civil conduct:** Reject temporary immunity because the claimed injury did not arise out of an official presidential function and therefore is not governed by Fitzgerald.
2. **No categorical term-long stay:** Hold the actual stay premature and an abuse of discretion, preserving the district court's ordinary power to issue later, tailored accommodations on a demonstrated need.
3. **No new universal classification test:** State only that immunity depends upon official function rather than personal status; leave precise tests for mixed or disputed presidential acts to cases that present them.
4. **Express reservations:** Decide neither the post-inauguration defamation immunity defense nor liability, state-court jurisdiction, criminal prosecutions, or a proposed constitutional or treason exception to official-act civil damages immunity.

**Fallback judgment and remedy:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment and remand for federal district-court proceedings. Stone may write separately to explain his broader doctrine if the Court will join only the fallback.

## D. PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

- Nixon v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 731 (1982), recognizes absolute personal civil damages immunity for presidential conduct within the outer perimeter of official responsibility. Allegedly improper motives or violations of law do not automatically put otherwise official conduct beyond that perimeter. Stone does not silently overrule that precedent.
- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219 (1988), supports evaluating immunity through the nature of the function rather than the actor's identity. Stone's particular reasonable-officeholder check is his proposed formulation, not a holding already made in Forrester.
- Landis v. North American Co., 299 U.S. 248 (1936), preserves district-court authority to manage its docket and grant justified stays; it does not authorize a status-based presumption that litigation involving the President must await the end of a term.
- Clinton v. Jones, 520 U.S. 681 (1997), presents the federal civil pre-presidential immunity and discretionary-stay questions. The actual controversy does not provide a vehicle for comprehensive civil and criminal presidential immunity doctrine.
- **No imported future law:** Later Supreme Court decisions on presidential criminal immunity are not part of Stone's 1997 simulated precedent baseline.

## E. FINAL PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm and remand. The federal civil action is not barred or automatically suspended because the defendant holds the Presidency. The district court shall proceed under ordinary law while giving concrete presidential functions tailored procedural protection when justified. Stone's primary functional account and his reservations about the full breadth of presidential immunity are stated explicitly; the fallback preserves the same disposition if the Court accepts only the minimum grounds required by this record.
