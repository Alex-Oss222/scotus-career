## 137. Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — No. 96-270

### Exact components

**A1 — Order of decision.** The proposed settlement class fails certification before it can become the vehicle for binding the absent claimants. This nonmerits certification issue is logically antecedent to the class-related standing questions presented here and may be resolved without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate substantive claims. No named or absent person's Article III standing, future injury, or individual right to sue is affirmatively decided. Actual Steel Co. forbids merits adjudication on assumed jurisdiction; it does not force a jurisdictional ruling unnecessary to denial of this representative procedure.

**A2 — Settlement and Rule 23.** A settlement class must meet Rule 23's requirements. Because no trial is proposed, difficulties managing a trial need not be examined. Settlement may be relevant to assessing the real class and bargain, but the desirability of settlement or assurance of overall fairness cannot replace an unsatisfied certification requirement. Rule 23(a)'s protections remain essential when a bargain would bind people absent from its negotiation; the settlement does not make unlike substantive rights identical.

**A3 — Adequacy on this record.** Those currently injured and those exposed who may become injured later have materially different bargaining interests in immediate payments, future protection, inflation, limits and timing. The supplied representative structure does not adequately protect those interests through independent representation. Notice, the opportunity to opt out and the economic attractions of a settlement do not cure the deficiency. This is a record-specific conclusion, not a mandatory architecture for every class or a prohibition on settling future claims. The five affirming Associates adopt this conclusion; the two remanding Associates accept the legal protection but withhold this appellate factual application.

**A4 — Predominance on this record.** Common asbestos exposure and the wish for settlement do not predominate over the materially different applicable state laws, exposure histories, injuries and individual claims in the proposed class. Settlement removes trial manageability from consideration, not these substantive differences. This does not forbid nationwide, mass-tort, issue or settlement classes categorically, nor deny an appropriately supported later proposal. The five affirming Associates adopt A4; the remanding Associates do not.

**AR — Distinct remand position.** Vacate the Third Circuit's judgment and remand for settlement-sensitive Rule 23 appellate review of the District Court's findings. Settlement remains subject to all certification requirements, but the appellate court must account for its consequences beyond the absence of trial and assess the actual bargain and findings under the correct standard. Serious future-claimant representation concerns warrant careful review; they do not justify replacing that review with the Supreme Court's first assessment of a large record. AR neither reinstates the certification or settlement nor orders that either be approved. Notice and ultimate fairness remain open.

## 138. City of Boerne v. Flores — No. 95-2074

### Exact components

**B1 — Reach.** The accepted §1292(b) route presents Congress's §5 authority to impose RFRA on State and local government. Actual Church of Scientology and Swanner reserved constitutionality; their statutory applications and Agostini supply no prior §5 resolution. The Court does not decide whether the particular permit denial imposes a substantial burden, is neutral, is justified, or must be overturned.

**B2 — Section 5 merits.** Congress may prevent and remedy violations of Fourteenth Amendment rights, including through measures reaching some conduct that a court would not itself hold unconstitutional. The means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury Congress seeks to prevent or remedy; Congress has substantial latitude but may not replace the judicially defined substantive right with a different one under the name of enforcement. Smith remains the constitutional baseline for genuinely neutral, generally applicable laws. RFRA's general substantial-burden trigger and compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means command, imposed on State and local government across the field, substitute a materially different standard without the required enforcement connection. Reverse the Fifth Circuit's State/local §5 holding and remand. No prior judicial violation is required for every prohibited act; no mandatory geographic limitation, expiration date, case-by-case finding, or exclusive form of legislative evidence is created. The reasoning does not assert a fabricated empirical inventory or that preventive enforcement is unavailable.

**B3 — Reargument.** Obtain focused briefing and reargument on Smith, the constitutional treatment of neutral burdens, Sherbert/Yoder, and the consequences for the §5 inquiry before deciding the merits. This neither upholds RFRA nor overrules Smith on the present submission. B3 contains distinct O'Connor, Souter and Breyer qualifications set out below.

**BS — Stevens's additional personal ground.** RFRA affords a claimant a statutory exemption argument because the claimant acts from religion, while an atheist or agnostic asserting the otherwise comparable secular interest receives no such legal advantage. Stevens views that preference for religion over irreligion as violating the Establishment Clause under Wallace. This is his independent additional reason, not a holding shared by the B2 coalition, a claim that the Church automatically qualifies for an exemption, or a disposition of the statute's federal application in this State/local case. It is not a categorical objection to every lawful religious accommodation.

## 139. Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc. — No. 95-1184

### Exact components

**G1 — Protected interests.** Compelled funding may implicate expression even without personal attribution, ideological content or restriction of one's own advertising. The absence of those additional burdens remains important to the actual program. No categorical premise that commercial advertising is unprotected, or that attribution is always required, controls the result. No holding on false or ideological advertisements, unchallenged regulatory provisions or stand-alone promotion programs is made.

**G2 — Integrated collective program.** The challenged generic commodity promotion is germane to the otherwise lawful and substantially collective marketing scheme that already displaces important aspects of independent price, quality, quantity and other market decisions. Participants can be required to bear this common marketing expense while retaining independent speech and without being made personally to endorse the generic message. On these terms the Central Hudson restriction-of-message framework does not require invalidating the assessment. This sustains the reviewed program; common benefit or majority approval alone would not suffice, and an advertising-only arrangement in an otherwise independent market is not decided. G2 adopts no finding that generic advertising is more effective than individual advertising and no universal commercial-speech immunity.

**G3 — Dissenting compulsory-subsidy ground.** Commercial expression does not lose First Amendment protection because the speaker pays instead of speaks personally. Abood, Keller and Lehnert permit fees justified by the legally required representative function, not compulsory financing of every beneficial public-relations campaign. The program's regulatory breadth and shared commercial benefit do not establish the necessary justification for this separate forced expressive expense. Affirm the judgment against these advertising assessments, leaving independently valid nonexpressive regulation untouched. This position does not require a finding that the advertisements are false, ideological or personally attributed.

**G4 — Distinct alternative framework, not a newly adjudicated factual application.** Souter and Scalia accept Central Hudson scrutiny as an additional route for testing the government's substantial interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit; the burden is the government's, and no least-restrictive-means or mandatory-study requirement follows. Thomas rejects the discounted Central Hudson framework while maintaining that failure even under that lesser standard also demonstrates failure under his stronger protection. The historical writings applied this alternative to detailed coverage, geography, efficacy and credit facts. Those additional facts are not inserted into the frozen neutral record. G3 is the supported sufficient ground for the three affirmers; no new independent G4 factual holding is frozen here. Removing Scalia's provisional categorical rejection of G4 preserves his actual historical framework position without inventing record findings.

## 141. United States v. O'Hagan — No. 96-842

### Exact components

**O1/O2 — Section 10(b) misappropriation.** For counts 21–37, a person who owes the source a duty of loyalty and confidentiality, feigns fidelity, and secretly uses material nonpublic information entrusted in that relationship for securities trading commits deception in connection with the transaction. The deception is of the information source, and is consummated by the securities use; the source need not be the trading counterparty. The requisite fiduciary/confidential relationship and deception remain indispensable. This is primary liability, not equal access to information or automatic accessory liability. Full disclosure of the trading plan to the person or persons owed the duty removes this theory's deception; disclosure to only one of multiple principals need not discharge duties to the other, and eliminating this securities theory does not authorize a breach of other law. No novel private-aider rule follows from actual Central Bank. The six supporters reverse the categorical §10(b) rejection and remand without deciding every trial objection or evidentiary sufficiency challenge.

**O3 — Distinct §10(b) rejection grounds.** Scalia would affirm on criminal lenity: the statute and rule are insufficiently clear to punish deception of a source other than a party to the securities transaction. He does not adopt every Thomas criticism of the agency theory. Thomas would affirm because the source fraud is complete independently of the eventual trade and the government's attempted distinctions do not supply a coherent statutory connection; a court may not replace the agency's actual theory with its own improved rationale. Acceptance that information is property or that a fiduciary committed fraud does not itself answer the separate securities nexus question.

**O4 — Rule 14e-3(a), counts 38–54.** Section 14(e)'s express authority to prescribe means reasonably designed to prevent fraud supports a prophylactic rule in the present tender-offer misappropriation setting without requiring proof of a fiduciary breach as an additional element. The seven supporters sustain the rule in that scope and reverse the categorical invalidity ruling. The statute does not authorize redefining every informational disparity as fraud; the rule must remain reasonably designed to prevent fraud. A source-duty proof problem in secret tender-offer trading supplies the preventive connection for these supporters. The ruling does not resolve principal-authorized warehousing or every application of the rule.

The actual rule's conditions remain separate: a person has taken a substantial step or steps to commence, or has commenced, a tender offer; the trader is someone other than the offering person; the trader possesses material information relating to the offer; the trader knows or has reason to know both that it is nonpublic and that it was acquired directly or indirectly from the offering person, the issuer, or an officer, director, partner, employee or other person acting on behalf of either. It reaches the covered purchase or sale, causing such trading, and the specified securities convertible or exchangeable into the sought securities and options or rights concerning them. The public-disclosure alternative requires the information **and its source** to be publicly disclosed a reasonable time before trading. The tender-offer rule is independent of the source-disclosure escape from §10(b) deception; those are not interchangeable exceptions.

Section 78ff(a) separately requires a willful violation for criminal punishment. Proof of lack of knowledge of the rule bars imprisonment under that provision; it does not erase the rule, supply a general immunity from fines, or mean that the government must prove knowledge of the rule as part of every violation. The Court leaves preserved pre-offer scope and due-process arguments, trial objections and other unaddressed challenges to the appellate remand; it does not reach an unpreserved contention in the first instance. No categorical rejection of those claims is inferred from upholding prophylactic authority.

**OT — Thomas's narrower disagreement with O4.** Thomas accepts that the statutory language permits some prophylactic rules reaching nonfraudulent conduct, but rejects this rule as justified and applied to the source-misappropriation theory presented. The agency cannot enlarge the definition of fraud or have a court supply a preventive rationale different from its own. Even assuming the §10(b) theory valid, he finds no demonstrated reason why establishing the bidder source would leave proof of the relevant fiduciary breach so difficult as to justify dispensing with it; the knowledge-of-source conditions are not themselves the missing breach. This is not a declaration that every possible §14(e) prophylactic rule, classic target-insider application, or differently justified tender-offer protection is invalid. He would affirm the challenged §14(e) ruling in this case.

**O5 — Mail fraud and untouched laundering.** Reverse the Court of Appeals' disposition of mail-fraud counts 1–20 and remand for consideration of preserved alternate grounds that court did not reach. The securities-supporting route removes the premise used below to reverse mail fraud. Thomas separately rejects the claimed dependence: Carpenter permits prosecution of the fraudulent taking/use of confidential information as property under the mail-fraud law even when the same conduct does not violate the securities statutes. Scalia joins the mail-fraud result without adopting Thomas's entire analysis. No automatic reinstatement free from remaining objections, new jury finding, or final sufficiency conclusion follows. Money-laundering counts 55–57 were not included in the government's review petition and remain undisturbed; the mail-fraud mandate cannot silently reverse them.

## 142. Akins v. Federal Election Commission — D.C. Circuit No. 94-5088

### Exact components

**K1 — Article III.** The six supporters of standing accept the alleged continuing denial of particular campaign-financing information for these voters' own electoral participation as a concrete injury, not mere dissatisfaction with government compliance. Public Citizen does not require an informational injury to be unique; Lujan still requires a personal concrete interest appropriate to the stage. The actual legal interpretation supplied a ground of the political-committee dismissal, and correcting it can restore a process capable of supplying information still sought. Bennett supports examination of the real remedial chain; discretionary enforcement does not categorically destroy redressability. No guarantee of prosecution, a particular classification or full disclosure is made. Steel Co.'s already-current reports and completed injury do not describe this claim. No statement that a finding actually made in Bennett occurred here is imported.

The contrary view is that the supported connection remains the public's undifferentiated interest in electoral oversight, expressed as a demand to classify and regulate a third party. Statutory complaint status alone cannot supply a personal stake. Public Citizen's direct denial of specifically requested access does not eliminate this inquiry. That view does not find an absent affidavit, impose an invented documentation requirement, deny every widely shared informational injury, or use Findley's former candidacy as a substitute for analysis. Neither side decides the ultimate statutory entitlement as a prerequisite to standing.

**K2 — Review and exact remedy.** Under §437g(a)(8), an aggrieved complainant may seek review of dismissal within 60 days. The separate 120-day inaction route is not this case. The court **may** declare the dismissal contrary to law and **may** direct the Commission to conform within 30 days; if the Commission fails to conform, the complainant **may** bring an action to remedy the violation involved in the original complaint. Those permissions do not become mandatory prosecution, penalties, settlement terms or disclosure orders. The challenged political-committee dismissal rests on a legal interpretation reviewable through this channel. A separate corporate-prohibition nonenforcement explanation based on the close membership question cannot silently replace the actual ground for that dismissal. No general rejection of lawful agency enforcement discretion follows.

**K3 — Political-committee definition and the candidate-financing distinction.** Section 431(4)(A) covers the specified group that receives contributions exceeding $1,000 in a calendar year **or** makes expenditures exceeding $1,000 in a calendar year, subject to the governing definitions and exclusions. It does not literally provide that every group making contributions automatically becomes a political committee. Actual candidate-directed transfers may be expenditures under §431(9); amounts, covered purpose, candidate connection and exceptions require their own findings. Buckley/MCFL's constitutional narrowing for independent electoral expression does not establish a categorical major-purpose exemption from political-committee treatment for otherwise covered candidate financing. The five full supporters reject that categorical exclusion and remand for application of the actual text and exclusions, without deciding that AIPAC crosses the threshold. Review of a constitutional narrowing is not an instruction to ignore all agency expertise or deference on every separate question.

Actual Colorado Republican protects genuinely independent expression and requires a real candidate connection for the alternative contribution treatment. Ideological alignment, policy cooperation, lobbying or partisan identity does not alone establish coordination. Kennedy joins the rejection of an across-the-board major-purpose shield **only for actual candidate cash transfers or genuine contribution conduits**. He withholds agreement that every alleged coordinated expression of the association itself may be treated in that same way before actual transactions are identified. His qualification concerns a genuine legal boundary, not just different phrasing of full K3 support.

**K4 — Separate exclusions, open record and lawful remand.** Section 431(9)(B)(iii) excludes communications by a membership organization or corporation to its members, stockholders, or executive or administrative personnel if the organization or corporation is not organized primarily to influence the nomination or election of an individual to federal office. Its separate proviso requires reporting costs directly attributable to communications expressly advocating the election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate when those costs exceed $2,000 **per election**, except a communication primarily devoted to subjects other than that express advocacy. The proviso applies to a membership organization, including a labor organization, or a corporation; reporting follows §434(a)(4)(A)(i) and, for a general election, §434(a)(4)(A)(ii). It is not the $1,000 calendar-year political-committee threshold or a general §441b exception.

Section 441b(b)(2) separately excludes communications on any subject by a corporation to its stockholders and executive or administrative personnel and their families, or by a labor organization to its members and their families. Its nonpartisan registration/get-out-the-vote exclusion covers a corporation's campaigns aimed at those same corporate recipients or a labor organization's campaigns aimed at those same labor recipients. Its segregated-fund exclusion covers establishment, administration and solicitation of contributions to a separate political fund by a corporation, labor organization, membership organization, cooperative or corporation without capital stock. The separate loan exception covers money lent by a national or State bank in accordance with applicable banking law and regulations in the ordinary course of business. The cross-references in §431(8)(B)(vi) and §431(9)(B)(v) to activity allowed by §441b do not import the $2,000 qualification from §431(9)(B)(iii). Membership, covered transactions, candidate connection, thresholds, exclusions and resulting duties must be determined on the actual administrative record. No final membership classification, contribution/coordination finding, donor list, reporting liability, penalty, later membership rule or compulsory enforcement disposition is supplied.
