## 119. Fletcher v. Kalina

### Component propositions and remedy

**F1 — Protected advocacy:** Preparing and filing the information and warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case are protected advocacy under Imbler and Burns when performed in that capacity. This protection is not lost merely because the complaint alleges bad purpose or false evidence. It does not immunize a distinct evidentiary undertaking by the same person.

**F2 — Personal attestation:** When the prosecutor personally swears to the truth of factual assertions to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant, she performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness rather than the advocate's function of presenting another person's evidence. Absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect that personal certification. The functional difference, not proof that the statement was false, determines the absolute-immunity classification. No general withdrawal of immunity for courtroom witness testimony follows.

**F3 — Remaining elements and defenses:** Denial of absolute immunity for the oath establishes no damages liability. Fletcher must establish a constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality and causation under the governing Fourth Amendment law; Kalina retains qualified immunity and all other available defenses. A judicially issued warrant does not categorically sever causation when material deception procured it. A corrected truthful account may nevertheless establish probable cause, and neither culpability nor that account is decided in this interlocutory appeal. Elder and Lanier concern the legal review method, not retroactive notice from their decision dates.

**F4 — Judgment and stage:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's denial of absolute immunity for the personally executed certification, with the foregoing functional limitation, and return the case for the unresolved proceedings. No reversal of an actual damages judgment, determination of qualified immunity, new mandatory trial, or award of damages is ordered. If implementation requires separating claims resting only on protected preparation/presentation from claims resting on the personal oath, the lower court must observe that distinction; the affirmance does not authorize liability for all acts in the charging transaction.

### Enhanced comparison

The protection path treats the certificate as one component of initiating prosecution and stresses that separating preparation from presentation can expose protected advocacy through pleading. Its strongest support is Imbler's protection of initiation and Burns's protected appearance at a probable-cause hearing. Its obstacle is that the official personally furnished the evidentiary truth assurance that a separate witness could supply. Malley denies comparable absolute protection to the warrant affiant, and Antoine and Buckley require a historically supported function rather than institutional importance.

The function-specific denial path preserves the advocate's preparation and presentation but denies absolute immunity for the oath. Its strongest objection is an artificial division of one charging event or a special rule triggered by alleged dishonesty. F1 and F2 answer both: actual function, rather than timing, title, alleged motive or the truth ultimately found, supplies the line. Every Associate adopts this distinction. Kennedy and Souter do not surrender their Buckley view that genuine preparation for testimony can precede probable cause; the personal oath is different from consultation with a prospective trial expert. Scalia's historical method supports denial without revisiting Imbler or making every witness answer in damages for testimony.

## 120. Piscataway Township Board of Education v. Taxman

### The statutory fork and component definitions

**Common propositions T0:** Race determined which equally qualified, equally senior employee lost an existing job, so the employer needs statutory permission; equal qualifications do not remove the discrimination question. Weber and Johnson authorize some voluntary affirmative action without an adjudication that the employer itself violated Title VII. Neither the constitutional diversity discussion in Bakke nor the current federal constitutional rule in Adarand supplies an employment-law exemption. No constitutional claim was before the lower court, and none is decided here. All eight Associates adopt these propositions.

**T1 — Remedial boundary, supporting affirmance:** The statutory accommodation recognized by Weber and Johnson does not authorize this concededly nonremedial race-selected discharge merely to preserve department diversity. A manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category may support voluntary action without proof of the employer's own actionable past discrimination; that distinction does not make every beneficial or integration-minded purpose sufficient. On the admitted absence of that remedial premise, Title VII prohibits this decision. This is not a constitutional holding that diversity can never be important.

**T2 — Burden, independently supporting affirmance:** Making one existing tenured employee bear the loss of her employment because of race, under this nonremedial diversity policy without a remedial limiting goal, unnecessarily trammels the interests Title VII protects. The necessary staff reduction and equal seniority do not make the racial selection burdenless; the board's available nonracial tie procedure could allocate the loss without that classification. This case-specific holding does not settle every remedial layoff plan or import a rule that any hiring preference is permissible.

**T3 — Narrow educational permission, supporting reversal of federal liability:** Weber and Johnson do not make their sufficient remedial premises the exclusive permissible grounds for every voluntary decision. Title VII permits this genuinely educational choice, confined to an actual equality of qualifications and seniority in a necessary reduction, to preserve the business department's sole Black teacher and the integrated instruction that presence supplies. The employer has not displaced a superior qualification or seniority entitlement, chosen an unqualified employee, or established an absolute exclusion from employment. The serious layoff burden is not automatically unnecessary merely because the same loss falls on one of two otherwise tied employees. This rule does not permit racial balancing for its own sake, fixed representational quotas, derogation of superior seniority or qualifications, or a general diversity defense for all jobs. An expressly nonremedial objective need not have a remedial endpoint that would be conceptually incompatible with its continuing educational purpose; the limiting circumstances are the actual tie and the particular integration interest, not an asserted right to maintain a racial fraction indefinitely.

**T4 — Federal/state remedy separation:** An affirmance leaves the reviewed judgment in place: the federal award of $134,014.62 for backpay, benefits and prejudgment interest; the $10,000 state-law emotional-suffering award; and restored seniority. It creates no new reinstatement order because Taxman was already rehired, and does not disturb the denials of a broader injunction or punitive damages. A reversal of federal liability does not itself reverse the distinct NJLAD judgment. On that path, remand is necessary to identify whether the parallel state claim independently sustains liability and which monetary/seniority relief remains justified; no assumption is made that every component is federal or that all existing relief disappears. All eight accept this separation; they differ on which federal judgment it implements.

### Enhanced alternative-path test

For affirmance, the strongest argument is that Title VII protects individuals from discharge because of race, and Weber/Johnson's departure from a literal prohibition is tied to overcoming employment exclusion. The existing job loss and absence of a remedial benchmark make this plan materially different from both precedents. The strongest objection is that those decisions expressly left outer boundaries open and did not hold that a good-faith educational integration choice between fully tied teachers must be forbidden. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas resolve that objection by declining a new statutory exception whose supposed limit would depend on the employer's desirable purpose rather than the statute's established remedial accommodation.

For reversal, the strongest support is the distinction between eliminating discrimination and forbidding every voluntary integration choice, together with the absence of any superior individual claim to the remaining position. Stevens's Johnson concurrence, 480 U.S. 642–647, expressly addresses forward-looking reasons, including workforce diversity; his Wygant dissent, 476 U.S. 313–320, specifically explains integrated public instruction and rejects an automatic constitutional hiring/layoff divide. The strongest objection is that a tenured employee suffers a concentrated loss and that the nonremedial purpose provides no temporal endpoint. The reversing Associates answer by restricting permission to the actual equal-qualification/equal-seniority educational tie, acknowledging the harm, and rejecting a perpetual quota or general benign-purpose license. They do not claim that Adarand has changed Title VII, that its congressional-competence rationale applies to this board, or that Stevens's earlier separate writings were already controlling law.

## 122. Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey

### Reachability and common law

**D0 — Reach and finality:** Decide the three granted questions on the stipulated grant/abeyance posture. The City's definitive position, as established in the earlier finality litigation and the present record, permits consideration of the claimed period; Suitum confirms that definitive permissible use and later valuation are distinct. The absence of an adequate state compensation route for the challenged period is the packet's supplied predicate, not a general abolition of Williamson County. No separate equal-protection merits question or substantive-due-process appeal is invented. The City did preserve challenges below to jury allocation and legal sufficiency on both tried claims; the Ninth Circuit left its equal-protection objections undecided. Its observation that there was no objection to the wording of the taking instruction does not waive those allocation objections.

**D1 — Exactions and use restrictions:** Dolan's requirement that the government establish nexus and individualized rough proportionality applies to the identified property interests demanded as a permit condition. It is not a universal proportionality burden for an ordinary development refusal. The Ninth Circuit erred insofar as its rationale treated this final-denial claim as subject to that exactions rule. The trial instruction itself imposed the received Agins reasonable-relationship formulation and did not impose Dolan's special burden; an appellate rationale's error therefore does not automatically invalidate the instructions or require a new trial.

**D2 — Existing taking standards and evidence:** A complete deprivation of economically beneficial use invokes Lucas subject to its background-property/nuisance limitation; partial deprivation remains governed by Penn Central. The owner must establish the applicable predicate. Agins's distinct substantial-advancement formulation remains received law at this date, and this grant does not replace it with later doctrine. Neither repeated rejection nor environmental purpose alone proves or defeats a taking. Sale to California for more than the purchase price is relevant evidence of value, not conclusive proof of a productive use during the claimed deprivation. Finality likewise does not itself prove complete economic loss. These are existing-law and evidentiary-limit propositions, not a unanimous finding that either taking theory has been proved.

**D3 — Authorized damages:** Section 1983 supplies an existing action against a municipality for its own qualifying policy or final decision; Monell and the current function-specific McMillian rule still require attribution and causation. First English permits compensation for the actual period of a proved temporary taking when the constitutional and procedural predicates are met. Prospective or administrative relief alone does not extinguish that completed injury. This decision need not imply a new action, guarantee expected profits, order a permit, find an unidentified official a policymaker, or award a second damages sum. Franklin distinguishes remedial availability from creation of the action and proof of legally attributable loss. All eight accept availability at this scope, whatever their liability or jury position.

### Disputed jury propositions

**D4 — Jury right and factual application, supporting affirmance:** The presented §1983 action seeks compensatory legal damages for a completed unconstitutional interference with property. It resembles a property tort for Seventh Amendment purposes sufficiently to carry a jury right; the mere fact that its constitutional predicate concerns taking does not make it a sovereign's ordinary condemnation proceeding. The judge states the legal taking standards and identifies legally legitimate governmental interests. On this record the existence of economically viable uses and the relationship of this particular final denial to those stated purposes are factual applications the jury may decide under those standards. There is no general jury power to define property rights, select the constitutional test, set community land-use policy or determine every mixed constitutional question. Markman preserves the distinction between an action's jury status and subsidiary tasks without converting this record-specific application into patent-claim construction.

**D5 — Judicial taking determination, supporting vacatur:** The remedy's monetary form and §1983's general provision for legal and equitable proceedings do not themselves establish that the historically distinct determination whether government has effected a compensable taking belongs to a jury. The closer analogue is the sovereign-taking/compensation proceeding, not ordinary trespass liability, and the ultimate constitutional evaluation of a land-use restriction remains judicial. Markman requires attention to the actual task even within a damages action. On this view the judge must independently determine the constitutional taking under the correct legal standard, using properly determined facts; the jury verdict cannot by itself supply that legal determination. No separate constitutional jury entitlement to the factual predicates is recognized merely because this inverse-condemnation determination is brought through §1983; jury rights on a distinct independently legal claim remain a separate question. This conclusion neither makes disputed historical facts intrinsically questions of law nor forbids a jury's advisory use, and it does not require a fresh evidentiary trial if findings and legal conclusions on the existing record can properly resolve the issue. It rejects D4's allocation of the ultimate liability application without deciding every possible §1983 damages claim or taking procedure.

### Record application and remedy alternatives

**D6 — Affirmance path:** Applying the actual instructions rather than Dolan's special burden, the supported evidence permits the jury's verdict on both undifferentiated taking theories. The owners offered evidence that the combined beach/access, viewshed and habitat restrictions left no feasible development area, and that further proposals could not satisfy the City's fixed position. They also offered evidence of compliance with the fifteen conditions, earlier approval of grading/restoration, favorable habitat evidence, and the City's own role in the access difficulty. Crediting that evidence and the reasonable inferences available from it, the City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law. ### Competing statutory paths

**Integrated future-capacity construction.** Section 8(h)'s direction to consider natural future effects makes prospective injury-related economic vulnerability part of the capacity assessment. Actual wages establish capacity only when they fairly and reasonably represent it. A supported significant potential of later loss can therefore justify a nominal award even though the existing economic effect cannot yet be measured as an ordinary periodic award. Section 22 explains the importance of maintaining a lawful modification route; it does not itself create the risk predicate. The strongest objection is the payment formula: a nominal dollar must not become a judicial exception to the requirement of economic disability. The six answer that the significant future vulnerability is the legally relevant capacity impairment expressly contemplated by §8(h), rather than a payment for bodily impairment or administrative convenience.

**Present-loss/formula construction.** Scalia and Thomas read §§2(10) and 8(c)(21) as requiring an earning incapacity that supports compensation under the enacted formula; a possibility of future incapacity is not present incapacity. Section 8(h) helps estimate actual capacity and future effects within that statutory determination, but does not authorize an arbitrary nominal payment after capacity has been found undiminished. Their strongest difficulty is the combination of the future-effects text and §22's limit, which may close the route before the loss occurs. They regard that limit as a legislative choice rather than authority to create compensation whose only operative work is preserving jurisdiction. Their position does not treat high wages as conclusive when they misrepresent capacity or eliminate the employer's burden under Rambo I.

**Proof quantum.** Both statutory positions accept Greenwich Collieries as current law. For the six who adopt the new significant-risk category, persuasion concerns whether that category has been established, not whether its possible future event is itself more probable than not. ### Adversarial comparison

**Old-rule route.** Gardner and Skipper protected the ability to answer material reasons advanced for death. When danger is the asserted reason and ordinary parole is legally impossible under the only noncapital sentence, withholding the accurate explanation defeats that protected function. The strongest contrary argument is that the proposed response describes the law of punishment rather than a fact about the defendant, and that Ramos preserved state latitude over sentencing information. The five answer that the difference does not change the function: this is an accurate, legally fixed response to the State's own future-danger presentation, not a demand for speculative release forecasting or a universal instruction. Ramos permitted truthful commutation information; it did not authorize depriving the defendant of this responsive opportunity. Gray and Lambrix reinforce the required functional comparison without independently supplying 1988 law.

**New-rule route.** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the force of the rebuttal analogy but see a material legal extension. Gardner concerned undisclosed adverse information, and Skipper excluded factual evidence bearing on future conduct. Neither fixed the boundary between a State's sentencing-law instructions and a defendant's evidentiary response. Ramos's recognition of latitude makes that distinct obligation not compelled at finality. Their conclusion rests on the substantive legal/factual distinction, not the mere later date of Simmons or disagreement among courts. Current Simmons remains binding prospectively within its actual scope. Gray's last-minute evidentiary preparation injury and Lambrix's consequential jury-guidance holding do not eliminate all distinctions among later procedural applications.

**Exceptions if new.** The rule regulates selection of an otherwise permitted punishment rather than removing conduct, persons or punishment from state power. Accurate parole information may materially improve sentencing deliberation, but that importance alone does not make this defined disclosure/rebuttal protection a watershed procedure essential to the fundamental accuracy and fairness of the criminal process under Teague, Sawyer, Gilmore and Caspari. The three reject both exceptions on this claim; they do not abolish the watershed category. The five need no alternative exception holding because their old-rule conclusion is independently sufficient.

### Competing paths and exact qualifications

**Fringing-island practice.** Alaska's best argument is the accumulated public federal treatment of enclosed waters, including the Chapman Line, Webb letter, Gulf litigation submissions and 1961 Solicitor General letter, reinforced by the general-practice language in the 1985 opinion. That material cannot be dismissed by demanding a Stefansson-specific historic-title claim Alaska does not make. **Dinkum.** Alaska's best textual argument is that Article 10(1) adds no explicit permanency adjective and normal coastal boundaries can change with natural conditions. The competing reading gives effect to the definition's high-tide condition as an ordinary characteristic, while separating tide level from seasonal movement of the formation itself. ## 127. Agostini v. Felton; consolidated Nos. 96-552 and 96-553

### Constitutional rule, Rule 60 channel and limits

Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test. Its purpose, effects and entanglement inquiries supply evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives attribution-and-function analysis under ordinary procedure. These are routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test.

Religious-school location, presumed indoctrination from location, incidental institutional benefit and ordinary secular monitoring do not alone establish prohibited sponsorship, financing or institutional control. Public control, independently secular work and enforceable protection against devotional use and diversion remain material. The rule overrules Aguilar's categorical treatment of this public Title I service and displaces Ball's corresponding onsite rationale to that extent, without reopening Ball's judgment or approving its separate Community Education program. Specific prohibitions, distinct doctrines and the actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain intact. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding.

Rufo requires a significant change in fact or law making continued prospective enforcement inequitable and relief suitably tailored to that change. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a relevant risk can impose a heavy burden; foreseeability alone is no absolute bar. Costs, a cheaper alternative or anticipated judicial disagreement alone do not suffice. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply Aguilar. The Court's decision supplies the present legal change; no second motion is required solely to invoke it. Rule 60(b)(5) does not generally reopen completed judgments or extinguish independently supported statutory, contractual or decree obligations.

### Two lawful paths and their limits

**Affirmance path.** Section 1681(b) does not require preferential treatment simply to eliminate numerical imbalance. The regulation and 1979 interpretation nonetheless may use relevant participation evidence and three independent means of showing effective accommodation. Substantial proportionality is one sufficient route; continuing responsive expansion and full effective accommodation are others. Brown failed the first two on this record, and the supported findings of competitively viable interest and ability in the women's gymnastics, fencing and ski teams and water-polo club supply the third-route failure. This is not liability from percentages or from the loss of one team by itself.

Brown's strongest objection is that requiring full accommodation of the underrepresented sex while allowing unmet interest among the overrepresented sex makes proportionality coercive in practice. That objection would have force if the latter routes were illusory, comparative interest were excluded from every relevant factual inquiry, or viable interest were simply presumed from enrollment. The current Kelley rule, joined by all eight, makes the routes genuinely independent. Actual unmet demand, not a compelled assumption about identical preferences, defeats the defense here. Evidence measuring real interest and ability remains relevant within prong three; a comparison with men's aggregate preferences does not authorize leaving proved women's opportunities unserved merely because a larger share of men might participate. No new fixed participant-count formula, survey method, roster-number threshold or presumption of equal interests is adopted.

**Brown's narrowing/reversal path.** A court could require reconsideration if the liability findings rested on a quota, an unsupported demand estimate, an invalid counting rule material to the result, or an interpretation contradicting §1681(b). That route is legally real, and each Justice rejects a contrary administrative command. The supplied lower record instead contains actual competitively viable unmet interests and failure of the independent expansion route. Brown's objections remain arguments; the packet supplies no adopted correction of those findings that would make remand necessary. The shared rule does not authorize this Court to invent a contrary finding, later survey or later compliance event.

**Independent constitutional issue.** Brown's federal funding does not make it a public university. The identified governmental conduct is federal implementation and judicial enforcement of the sex-conscious accommodation rule. The objective is effective athletic opportunity in the face of demonstrated inequality; the alternative routes and the actual unmet-demand findings substantially connect the application to that objective. The principle works symmetrically for the actually underrepresented sex and neither declares women inherently less interested nor treats all numerical disparities as constitutional injuries. Kelley requires that analysis independently of statutory compliance. The Associates need not decide every imaginable application or reopen Kelley's result.

**Remedial alternative.** The original preliminary relief restoring gymnastics and volleyball is distinct from the later final direction to fund gymnastics, fencing, skiing and water polo. The First Circuit preserved the former while vacating the latter and returning meaningful choice of means, including lawful reductions. Effective correction may ultimately require specific relief if a permissible institutional plan fails or narrower alternatives cannot cure the violation. But liability alone does not prove that the four-team choice is necessary now. The planned remand preserves that distinction, permits no indefinite postponement, fixes no budget or roster, and supplies no damages or new funding sanction. Kinman's recipient-damages threshold for harassment is not imported into this prospective athletic-accommodation action.

### Adversarial comparison

**Officer-route path.** Young permits prospective relief from an ongoing violation of federal law by officials who actually exercise the challenged regulatory authority. The federal title question may be a premise of their lawful conduct without becoming a judgment binding the immune State's title. Tindal and the officer-property reasoning in Treasure Salvors supply the relevant distinction; Edelman and Green preserve the boundary against retrospective compensation or declarations about completed violations. Pennhurst excludes compelled compliance with state law, not this pleaded federal-law claim. Current Seminole reinforces the availability of bounded officer relief without creating a general rule for all statutes or deciding the land question.

The strongest objection is practical: an order giving the Tribe exclusive use and preventing the State's officers from governing the beds, banks and waters would transfer the operative control even if the decree declares that the State is not formally bound. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would enforce the Ninth Circuit's express limits and require a proved federal violation and an appropriate final decree. They would not assume at Rule 12 that title is already established, or let the State's assertion of an unusually important interest decide the federal merits indirectly. The width of the prayer supports careful remedial review, not dismissal of otherwise proper officer claims before those questions can be resolved.

**Functional-sovereign-relief path.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would treat the requested exclusive occupation and sweeping exclusion of State regulation as effectively the direct sovereign-property decree that the lower court has already barred. The comprehensive federal-title premise and displacement of sovereign control distinguish this prayer from an ordinary order stopping a particular enforcement act. Their rule rests on the actual scope and effect of relief; it is not a general assertion that every suit involving State land or every important State interest defeats Young.

Its strongest objection is that Young commonly restrains important official conduct and often requires deciding whether asserted State authority conflicts with federal rights. If the State can prevail by describing the affected interest as sovereign, the exception would swallow federal supremacy. These four retain ordinary Young suits and demand the unusual equivalence between this prayer and the barred title/control remedy. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas will not join an independent generalized balance of federal need, State importance and alternative forums. Kennedy may explain why the waters' regulatory sovereignty matters to that equivalence, but no additional balancing proposition is necessary to the shared disposition.

**Narrower remedial possibility and limits.** A demonstrably narrower claim against a connected officer could present a different case; no amended or severed complaint is found here. Conversely, the affirmance votes do not approve the prayer in every requested respect. Both camps reject retrospective treasury relief, a binding quiet-title decree against Idaho, automatic immunity waiver and a merits ruling on the separate United States action. That action covers only part of the geography, so its existence supplies neither complete substitute relief nor a basis to presume the Tribe has no practical need for this proceeding. The four reversal votes may not be enlarged into the broad Article I immunity rule they proposed in Seminole; current law supplies no such controlling rationale.
