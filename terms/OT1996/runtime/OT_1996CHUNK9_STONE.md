**Question 1:** Yes, on the supported prima facie record here. Patterson undertook a continuing provider relationship with an identified Ohio service, repeatedly sent software to its Ohio system for distribution, used its marketing and fee-collection arrangements and communicated about the resulting commercial dispute. The availability of a hypothetical trademark dispute after a different distribution arrangement does not eliminate the connection in the actual arrangement pleaded.

Stone would separately find the present exercise reasonable. Patterson's burden as an individual defending away from Texas counts in full; CompuServe's size creates no preferential entitlement. But the deliberate multiyear Ohio-centered relationship, the forum's connection to the disputed dealings and the parties' contemplated Ohio law supply adequate justification on the motion record. No fact establishes a compelling unfairness that defeats the purposeful, claim-linked connection. The fractured Asahi stream-of-commerce theories need not be converted into a new universal test to decide those concrete contacts.

**Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment reversing the Rule 12(b)(2) dismissal and remanding for further proceedings.** CompuServe has made the required prima facie showing; disputed facts remain subject to the applicable later procedure, and other defenses and the intellectual-property merits remain undecided. ## A. STATUS

- **Approval Status:** Approved by the user on October 11, 2026 as Stone's proposed position, reasoning, and stated fallback. It remains a proposal to the Court, not decided simulated law.

## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Questions 1 and 2 - recipient responsibility.** Stone would reject automatic vicarious liability and the proposition that constructive notice alone establishes the intentional recipient discrimination for which Franklin authorizes compensation. For damages he proposes, by analogy to the actual-awareness rule of Farmer and Doe, a rule for this open question, because Franklin expressly left recipient attribution unsettled; no completed decision adopts it for Title IX. The recipient is liable for its own conduct. That means actual awareness by an official with authority to take corrective action, defined by function and not by title, and a response that is unreasonable disregard of what the official knows. A danger the recipient merely should have discovered does not suffice, and a reasonable response defeats liability even if the harm continues. The rule has these parts:

1. Awareness may be proved circumstantially, including by obviousness and willful avoidance. Obviousness permits but does not compel a finding of actual awareness. Deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected abuse, including by deliberately diverting complaints, may establish awareness; a deficient complaint system by itself does not establish it, and separate officials' information cannot simply be pooled into collective knowledge. The evidence need not include a written complaint.
2. The response must be unreasonable disregard, not a bad outcome.
3. A teacher's delegated authority is evidence of the connection to the program, not vicarious liability. Title VII agency rules are guidance and not an enacted wholesale transfer.
4. Failure to keep required complaint channels is evidence, not liability by itself.
5. Administrative enforcement and injunctive relief are governed by their own sources: the statute's enforcement procedure, which requires notice and an opportunity for voluntary compliance, and the Department's guidance, which applies agency principles to harassment by school employees. That question is reserved for a later case, and nothing here lowers or raises the standard for it.
6. Harris supplies the severity analogy: the conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to create an objectively and subjectively abusive educational environment.

This is Farmer's actual-awareness analogy extended to a setting Franklin left open, and Stone states it as his proposal. The reported evidence warrants further recipient-specific consideration, not a damages award.

**Question 3 — separate constitutional claims.** Stone would apply Doe to Whitehouse and Mackiel separately. Their investigative steps are legally relevant, yet a misdirected trace, one-sided testing and delayed confrontation do not conclusively establish a reasonable response where supported evidence attributes knowledge of ongoing abuse and culpable nonresponse to the particular official. The inquiry must identify which reports each received, when, what each actually understood, the authority and opportunity each possessed, the response each made, and what subsequent injury that response allegedly caused. It cannot transfer Lankford's assumed knowledge to these defendants or treat every investigation as equivalent to Caplinger's distinct circumstances. Stone proposes vacating their §1983 summary judgments for renewed review of supported actual-knowledge, unreasonable-disregard and causal-injury disputes, followed by an individualized examination of the remaining personal defenses. This does not treat negligent investigation as deliberate acquiescence. He would affirm the District's §1983 dismissal absent supported identification of an institutional policy or final District decision causing the abuse; the conduct of supervisors alone does not satisfy Monell. The teacher's own claim remains in its distinct posture.

**Question 4 — same-sex harassment.** He rejects a categorical exclusion. Title IX protects persons against discrimination on the basis of sex; it does not condition protection on the offender's sex. Evidence of sex-selective pursuit and misuse of educational authority may satisfy that element. Welcome, the relevant educational effects, attribution and causation still require proof. Sexual orientation alone is neither an automatic Title IX element nor a disqualification from protection.

**Personal-immunity boundary and express departure.** The received rule is Harlow's objective clearly-established-law defense as retained in Elder and Doe. Elder requires examination of all relevant law on the preserved defense and creates no mandatory merits-first sequence. Stone joined Elder. Doe's Caplinger ruling separately granted immunity for lack of particularized conduct-time notice without deciding constitutional merits; Stone concurred only in that judgment on his own merits ground, but the Court's contrary rationale remains governing law.

Stone nevertheless retains his approved proposal: decide the constitutional merits first, then allow a distinct defense only for genuine reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, with the official bearing that burden. His legal reason for the change is the relationship between §1983's remedy for a proved personal deprivation and the justification for an official's separate protection from damages. In a claim requiring actual awareness and deliberate unreasonable disregard, a reasonable protective response already defeats the merits. The received notice rule does not demand an identical reported case. Stone's objection is to a distinct clearly-established-law screen that can nevertheless protect an official after a personal culpable deprivation has been proved, without identified authoritative law permitting the conduct or genuine reliance on it. He considers that broader protection capable of defeating the statute's remedy for knowing constitutional wrongdoing beyond what protection of justified legal reliance requires. That is his stated criticism of the received rule, not a claim that Elder or Doe already adopted it.

The new boundary is the identified-authority, genuine-reliance defense, including reasonable reliance affected by an unexpected change in controlling law. Legal silence, general supervisory discretion, or ordinary investigation policy supplies no independent permission. A factual allowance already resolved against the official under the merits rule is not repeated as a second shield. The proposal leaves personal responsibility, culpability, causation, ordinary pleading and proof burdens on the underlying claim, distinct institutional liability, and other applicable immunities untouched. Stone gives concrete reliance on authoritative legal permission weight through the retained defense, rather than presuming such reliance from the official's position or from uncertainty alone. No such reliance is found or rejected conclusively on this record, and no completed judgment is reopened. On this summary-judgment record he would remand for defendant-specific analysis without declaring personal liability or inventing reliance facts.

**Independent fallback under unchanged immunity.** If the Court retains the received defense, Stone's approved alternative is the same remand under that defense. The court must fix each challenged omission's date and each supervisor's supported assumed knowledge and response before assessing particularized notice. Kinman's school attendance from 1986 to May 1990 does not establish one continuous notice date for both supervisors; the later 1992 corroboration and termination efforts do not retroactively prove their earlier awareness. Preconduct bodily-integrity and personal-attribution law, including Ingraham, Rizzo and Monell, supplies relevant principles but does not automatically answer whether the specific supervisory omission was clearly unlawful. Harlow supplies the retained defense. Anderson supplies the particularized framework for present review but cannot itself give notice for conduct preceding its 1987 decision. The May 1987 Jefferson and Lopez authorities discussed in Doe can inform only conduct after they became available, with attention to their distinct direct-action and supervisory facts and their actual legal weight. Farmer (1994) and Doe (1995) cannot create notice for 1986–1990 conduct. Apply all relevant available authority, including authority not previously cited, without selecting disputed facts or inventing a new claim. The remand permits ordinary resolution of each actual defense; it does not order an automatic trial or prejudge immunity.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the rejection of a categorical same-sex bar and the availability of a Title IX damages claim; modify the Title IX remand to apply the recipient-responsibility rule stated above, which requires actual awareness by an official with authority to act, provable circumstantially, and unreasonable disregard, and which reserves administrative and injunctive enforcement for a later case. Vacate the personal supervisory §1983 summary judgments for Doe-compliant review; affirm the District's §1983 dismissal on this record. Remand without final liability, compensation, a finding of qualified immunity, or revival of the state tort claim. If the record cannot distinguish each supervisor's knowledge, seek ordinary focused factual development rather than inventing collective notice.

## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 1 — the alleged invitation violates the Fourth Amendment.** Stone would decide the media-intrusion merits on the assumed facts before considering the personal defense. The warrant authorizes an evidence-gathering intrusion with defined objects and purposes. Taking outside observers into the home to produce a broadcast adds persons and exposure that do nothing to locate the specified evidence or protect the search. The family's compelled submission to officers is not consent to that additional audience. Public interest in law enforcement does not enlarge the officer's warrant authority, and press status supplies no special permission to enter a private home.

**Question 2 — the warrant supplies no authority for these nonassisting third parties.** Stone preserves practical assistance: a technician, interpreter or person identifying sought property can sometimes aid lawful execution when reasonably needed and controlled. Section 3105 recognizes assistance, not attendance at the officer's unrestricted invitation. On this record the media purpose is independent of execution. The constitutional conclusion rests on the warrant's scope and reasonableness; breach of the statute alone neither creates the Bivens claim nor proves a constitutional violation.

**Question 3 — the alleged observation and recording add an unreasonable intrusion.** Lawful official inspection of a document does not entitle an unrelated camera crew to inspect and preserve its contents for a different purpose. The added exposure of the family and domestic materials exceeds the justification supporting the warrant. Stone would not make liability turn on whether recording is called a separate property seizure: the government's procurement of the extra privacy invasion during a home search is sufficient under the Fourth Amendment. Necessary official evidentiary photography or appropriate documentation of execution has a different justification and remains open to its own reasonableness analysis. No prior restraint on eventual publication is requested or ordered.

**Question 4 — deny the pleaded immunity defense through a distinct conduct-date inquiry.** Stone would affirm under the received Harlow–Anderson standard, as preserved in simulated Elder and Pinder. Before March 5, 1992, the warrant's purpose-bound authority and protection against needless documentary exposure were established by Bivens and Andresen, with Matias applying that discipline in the Second Circuit. Rivera made clear that entry authority does not erase the rights of household members outside the investigation. These propositions converge on a specific limit: officers executing an evidence warrant cannot gratuitously admit strangers to expose private home life for a purpose unrelated to the search. The alleged media invitation is outside that limit without requiring an earlier television-crew case. Section 3105 corroborates what execution authority permits, but he does not equate a statutory violation with clearly established constitutional liability.

Stone would not reason that because he now finds a violation immunity necessarily fails. He separately identifies the earlier sources, the relevant level of specificity and why an officer could not reasonably read this warrant to authorize the alleged conduct. Later Soldal, Wilson, Bills and the pending Richardson position do no retroactive notice work. Mottola does not claim that the crew was actually assisting the search, so no such factual allowance is available on this appeal.

Stone's Master favors one complete merits inquiry and a narrower defense based on genuine, reasonable reliance on identified authoritative law. Here no binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing this media intrusion is identified; the warrant's general search authorization cannot supply the missing permission. The same denial follows under that preferred approach. Because the presented claim fails even under the broader received defense, Stone need not overrule Harlow or impose a new immunity regime to decide this case. This preserves the latest Richardson distinction between eligibility and an individual's actual defense and does not repeat factual reasonableness as a second shield.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the Second Circuit's judgment insofar as it rejects Mottola's pleading-stage qualified-immunity defense to the media-invitation and associated intrusion claim. Remand for ordinary adjudication of his actual participation, causation and compensable injury on the lawful record. The merits ruling is conditional on the allegations the Court must assume; the disposition awards no damages, resolves no excluded force or detention claim, and adjudicates no liability of CBS or other agents.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: ERISA does not preempt the testamentary transfer of a deceased spouse's community interest

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches preemption because the sons' asserted entitlement depends on the state-law transfer.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does ERISA preempt application of Louisiana community-property law that permits a deceased nonparticipant spouse to transfer by will her community one-half interest in pension benefits accrued during the marriage?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Family, property, and probate are traditional state domains, and federal law does not displace them absent a clear and manifest congressional purpose. Stone applies the text and purposes of ERISA sections 1144(a) and 1056(d)(1) and the domestic-relations-order exception without an unlimited "relate to" test, consistent with Travelers' rejection of indiscriminate remote-effect preemption.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** ERISA does not preempt Louisiana community-property law as applied to permit a deceased nonparticipant spouse to transfer by will her community one-half interest in pension benefits accrued during the marriage. The surviving spouse's joint-and-survivor annuity under section 1055 remains protected.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Louisiana rule does not mention ERISA or pensions, and any effect on a plan arises from applying a general rule of ownership. It conflicts with no identified ERISA purpose, and the canon against displacing traditional state authority beyond what Congress fairly expressed applies. The anti-alienation provision protects participants against premature loss of retirement funds by assignment. Dorothy's interest arose by operation of community-property law and not by assignment, and ERISA does not regulate what a spouse does with her property after death. The domestic-relations-order exception addresses divorce-related orders between living spouses and does not define probate transfers as alienation. It would be anomalous to read into ERISA an implied bar on a testamentary transfer when a divorce court could have awarded the same interest. No holding of Hisquierdo is relied on; the text and purposes of sections 1055 and 1056(d)(1) control. The sons seek an accounting after both deaths, not a share of the survivor annuity. The stock and cash were distributed to Isaac in 1985 and the cash was placed in an individual retirement account. The sons are not plan participants or beneficiaries, and the plan's fiduciary duties would not change if the suit proceeds.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Apart from the survivor-annuity claim resolved in Issue 2, no section 1055 question is decided, including whether any other asset falls outside section 1055 or was waived. Sandra's annuity remains protected, and the accounting is structured to respect it. Stone does not hold that ERISA never preempts a community-property claim.

### Issue 2: The sons' claim to Sandra's survivor annuity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the survivor-annuity claim because the sons expressly seek part of Sandra's survivor-annuity entitlement, including through an accounting or a lump-sum equivalent.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May the deceased nonparticipant spouse's legatees obtain a share of the surviving spouse's annuity protected by ERISA section 1055, directly, through an accounting, or through an equivalent lump-sum award?
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Stone would hold that §1055 protects Sandra’s survivor annuity against the sons’ claim. They cannot obtain a share directly, through an accounting, or through an equivalent lump-sum award replacing that annuity share.
* **Preserved Claims:** Stone would preserve the approved accounting for Dorothy’s community one-half interest in the pension installments paid to Isaac, the IRA, and the distributed ESOP assets. Protecting Sandra’s annuity does not extinguish those separate claims.

Ownership changes the route of the economic burden, not its legal character. It should not be expressed as though Travelers had already sustained every tax collected from a plan or as though only taxes formally naming ERISA could ever be preempted.

## C. EQUITIES AND LIMITED CONSEQUENCES

Stone weighs finality and the cost of another review against correction of a material federal prediction of state law while Supreme Court review remains available. Counsel's prompt motion after learning of the decision supports reconsideration; the stated chronology supplies no basis for finding tactical withholding. The Fourth Circuit remains free to determine Sheets's proper scope and other dispositive policy provisions. A confined remand therefore respects its independence while reducing the risk that an obsolete legal premise controls the judgment.

These probability and equitable grounds support the approved GVR under §2106. Stone does not substitute his separate reasonable-possibility standard from Lawrence. The order finds no coverage, resolves no exclusion, awards no damages, and revives no forfeited claim. ## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An Alabama sheriff acts for the county in law enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the policymaker issue because municipal liability cannot be assessed without attributing the challenged policy to the proper government.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** For purposes of section 1983 municipal liability, is an Alabama sheriff a final policymaker for the county or for the State when conducting law-enforcement investigations and related prosecutorial functions?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Monell, Pembaur, Praprotnik and Jett to the function-specific question whether Alabama law shows that the sheriff acts for the county when he sets law-enforcement policy. An official whose acts may fairly be said to represent official policy is the final policymaker by virtue of the office he holds. Swint leaves this merits issue open, and Bryan County is a pending packet whose proposed reasoning is not a Court rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An Alabama sheriff represents the county when setting and executing law-enforcement policy within the county, and Monroe County may therefore be liable under section 1983 for that policy if it was the moving force behind the violation.
* **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The sheriff independently exercises law-enforcement authority for the county. The county pays his salary from its treasury like other county employees, and county voters elect him on a county ballot. Statutes charge him with law enforcement in his county, and the county must furnish his quarters, supplies, and vehicles, which gives the commission practical leverage over him. He reports collected money to the county treasurer. State supervision is limited. The Governor's power to direct investigations was not invoked here, and impeachment is an ultimate check on flagrant misconduct, not a tight rein on policy. The Alabama Constitution places sheriffs in the executive department, but Alabama law is inconsistent and its own cases and statutes also call them county officers. A state label does not control for section 1983 purposes, because otherwise States could nullify federal liability by relabeling officials. Enforcing state criminal law does not make a sheriff a state policymaker, since most criminal law is statewide. American sheriffs were made elective so that they would be controlled locally. Parker v. Williams, decided by the Eleventh Circuit, held that an Alabama sheriff acts for the county in hiring a chief jailer. Treating the sheriff as a state policymaker would let a State shield counties from Monell liability by label.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide that every act of a sheriff is county policy, find an underlying violation, impose respondeat superior liability, or decide individual liability. The County is liable only for its own policy that was the moving force behind the injury.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone reads Alabama law as showing a county law-enforcement policymaker, applying Monell, Pembaur, Praprotnik and Jett and treating state labels as non-controlling under section 1983. Hess is an Eleventh Amendment structure analogy only and is not relied on for the holding. The earlier correction in this entry, which affirmed the judgment for the County, is superseded by the disposition above.

### Issue 1: User-added equipment is other property

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the classification because it is the legal basis of the judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Under the maritime economic-loss rule of East River, is equipment added to a product by the initial user after purchase “other property” when a defect in the original product later destroys both the product and the added equipment after resale?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply East River to the product the manufacturer supplied and avoid converting resale bundling into retroactive expansion of that product.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Equipment added by the initial user after the manufacturer sold the vessel is “other property” for the maritime economic-loss rule when a defect in the original vessel later destroys that equipment, even if the equipment is transferred with the vessel to a subsequent purchaser.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The rule rests on the original commercial bargain: warranty law governs the manufacturer’s product, while tort law protects outside property. The manufacturer did not supply or price the added equipment and the initial purchaser could have recovered for its loss. Resale does not transform the nature of the property or reduce the later owner’s protection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not classify equipment supplied by the manufacturer, replacement components integrated into the original product, or items whose provenance is disputed.
