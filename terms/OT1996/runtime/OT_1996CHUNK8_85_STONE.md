### Issue 1: Retrieval of stored pager data incident to arrest

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether activating the pager was a search and whether the search-incident exception justified the retrieval. The lawful arrest and physical seizure of the pager do not themselves resolve access to its stored content.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether pressing a control to reveal stored pager messages is a search distinct from seizure and whether immediate warrantless retrieval is justified by a concrete evidence-preservation need.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Activating the pager to expose numerical messages stored in its memory reveals concealed content and is a Fourth Amendment search distinct from the physical seizure. Search incident to arrest does not categorically open secured electronic memory. Its evidence-preservation rationale permits immediate retrieval limited to volatile data only when the known operation of the particular device and the circumstances establish a concrete, imminent risk that incoming transmissions will overwrite evidence before a warrant can reasonably be obtained and the information cannot be preserved without viewing it. General propositions about finite pager memory and what some pagers may do do not satisfy that showing.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Activating a pager to retrieve stored messages is a search distinct from seizure of the device. Incident to a lawful arrest, officers may make an immediate retrieval limited to volatile data when the particular device and circumstances establish a concrete overwrite risk before a warrant can reasonably be obtained and no nonsearch means will preserve the data. The general observations offered here do not satisfy that showing.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Seizure restrained Ortiz's possession and preserved the device; pressing the control disclosed information that was not exposed to observation. Robinson and Belton protect officer safety and prevent destruction of evidence, but their categorical physical-container treatment cannot be extended beyond those justifications to secured electronic memory. The record did not establish this pager's storage capacity or overwrite behavior, an imminent incoming page, or the inability to preserve the data by leaving the device secured while seeking a warrant. The Government therefore failed to establish the preservation necessity it invoked.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize exploratory review, delayed examination after an overwrite risk ends, access to unrelated or nonvolatile data, or a warrantless search based only on the generic possibility that electronic information can be altered. When officers can preserve the information without inspecting it, the warrant requirement governs.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved Ortiz answer.** Stone would retain the pager-search reversal and remand for the proper evidentiary consequences. General overwrite possibilities do not prove this device presented an imminent loss risk with no nonsearch preservation method; physical seizure and access to its stored content remain distinct.

**Questions 2–3 — new companion answers, proposed to the Court and user-approved.** Stone would affirm the Seventh Circuit’s vacatur of Hurtado’s sentence and its resentencing direction under §2J1.5 on this record. The enacted guideline chain calls for an actual best analogy, not an automatic obstruction/accessory classification whenever testimony is withheld. The limited testimony assisted identification, no purpose to aid acquittal was established, and aversion to informing does not itself establish accessory-after-the-fact conduct. The material-witness analogy therefore better captures the conduct found. Conduct, proved purpose and effect matter to selecting the analogy; actual obstruction, where established on different facts, may support a different guideline. He would leave lawful adjustments and the resulting sentence for resentencing, disturb no contempt conviction, and invent no fixed replacement term.

**Combined proposed disposition.** Reverse and remand as to Ortiz under his preserved approved search position; affirm the appellate vacatur/remand of Hurtado’s sentence under the expressly user-approved companion proposal. The two appeals remain distinct within one consolidated matter.
