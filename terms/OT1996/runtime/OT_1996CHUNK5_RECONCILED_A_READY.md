**Common legal ground and remedy:** Title VII responsibility for a supervisor-created hostile environment is not confined to higher management's actual or constructive notice and negligent failure to correct. Delegated supervisory authority may support attribution when it actually aided the creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment. A supervisor's title or mere workplace proximity is insufficient; the connection between the authority delegated and the misconduct must be established. Hiring, firing, discipline or threatened employment action can supply that connection, but an express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not indispensable in every agency-supported case. Daily control of working conditions remains relevant. Negligent notice-and-response liability remains a distinct route.

Meritor rejects both automatic imputation of every supervisor's misconduct and universal no-notice immunity. Harris and the effective DeAngelis decision establish the hostile-environment threshold without resolving corporate attribution. The administrator's actual supervision and hiring/firing authority must be assessed with permissible verdict-favorable inferences; reporting to a corporate officer does not independently defeat agency. Remand preserves ordinary Rule 50 sufficiency review, properly preserved alternative grounds and any genuine instruction/preservation question. It neither deems every allegation true nor supplies the missing connection itself.
