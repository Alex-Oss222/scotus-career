### Issue 1: Residency-based charitable tax exemption

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory appointment authority

* **Reasoning Below:** The military appellate court held that the Secretary possessed authority to appoint the civilian judges.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The UCMJ authorized assignment but did not confer appointment authority, and § 323(a) was too general or unrelated to the judicial offices.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** Section 323(a) authorizes the Secretary, as department head, to appoint departmental officers, while Article 66 expressly contemplates civilian appellate judges; the provisions operate together.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** If statutory authority is absent, simulated Ryder would require new appellate review without deciding inferior status.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** A department head may appoint only officers Congress has permitted that department head to appoint.
### Issue 2: Principal or inferior officer

* **Reasoning Below:** The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces treated the judges as inferior officers.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Their adjudicatory responsibilities and decisional independence make them principal officers requiring presidential nomination and Senate confirmation.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** They are supervised, removable from assignments, governed by rules prescribed by the Judge Advocate General, and subject to review by presidentially appointed officers; they lack final executive authority.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** A narrower holding may rely on the combined supervision and review in this specific military system.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Appellate review alone does not automatically make every lower federal adjudicator an inferior officer.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance follows if § 323(a) authorizes the Secretary’s appointments and the judges are inferior officers because their work is supervised and their decisions lack finality absent review or permission by superior executive officers. Reversal would follow from either statutory absence or principal-officer status, with simulated Ryder supplying the remedy. The Court should state the institutional features doing work and avoid a universal test based on one factor.
