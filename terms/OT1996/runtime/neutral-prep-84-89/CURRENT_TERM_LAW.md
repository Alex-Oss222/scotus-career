### 1996-11-13 — INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang, No. 95-938; October Term 1996.

Authority: [Record](../records/INS_v_Yueh_Shaio_Yang_merits_1996-11-13.md).

#### Holdings

#### I. Review under the corrected 1996 transition provisions

**Holding and operative rule.** Review of this existing deportation proceeding remains available under former INA §106 and 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) on November 13, 1996. Corrected IIRIRA §306(c)(1) gives new §242(g) reach across past, pending and future proceedings when that provision commences; it does not advance commencement of the new jurisdictional rule from April 1, 1997, and the other review restrictions establish no present bar on this record.

**Authority.** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies an eight-Justice controlling rationale. Stone-Zsela concurs only in this threshold resolution and does not join its reasons.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court reads the enacted timing instructions together. Section 309(a) sets April 1, 1997 as the general commencement date, expressly excepting §309 and §§303(b)(2), 306(c), 308(d)(2)(D) and 308(d)(5). Corrected §306(c)(1) applies the amendments as provided in §309, then separately extends §242(g) to old as well as new proceedings. The immediate-operation argument relies on those exceptions and the unrestricted reference to pending claims. But the exceptional clause specifies covered proceedings without supplying a replacement commencement date. Its present operation as an applicability instruction has independent work: after commencement, §242(g) reaches proceedings otherwise protected by grandfathering. This reading does not presume that jurisdiction can never change during litigation.

Corrected §309(c)(1), subject to its succeeding provisions, preserves the earlier proceedings and judicial review. No paragraph (2) or (3) agency election is established. Paragraph (4) requires a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996; it lists §§212(c), 212(h), 212(i), 244 and 245. The 1995 posture supplies no new triggering order, and that list omits former §241(a)(1)(H). Permanent §242(a)(2)(B)(i) instead lists §§212(h), 212(i), 240A, 240B and 245; clause (ii) separately addresses other statutorily discretionary authority, except §208(a) grants. Neither permanent clause governs this event.

Section 306(c)(2) protects existing §106 judgments and orders from invalidation or compelled reconsideration by paragraph (1); it does not independently establish appellate jurisdiction. Section 306(d)'s AEDPA-related criminal amendment supplies no bar without a covered conviction, which this record does not establish. The Court leaves open §242(g)'s scope after commencement, including its three subjects—commencing proceedings, adjudicating cases and executing removal orders—and its exception for review provided in §242. *Landgraf* and *Rivers* supply attention to Congress's actual temporal directions, not a new unanimous adoption of every earlier temporal rationale.

#### II. Eligibility does not exclude the continuing fraud pattern from discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** Former INA §241(a)(1)(H), 8 U.S.C. §1251(a)(1)(H), permits the Attorney General to consider a continuing pattern of immigration fraud when choosing among eligible waiver applicants, even though particular acts are connected with entry. Eligibility does not erase that conduct or compel a waiver; the Court neither permits automatic denial to every eligible applicant because of the qualifying fraud nor decides whether the minimum qualifying fraud alone could sustain a particular individualized denial.

**Authority.** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute makes eligibility and favorable discretion distinct. It reaches covered entry fraud or misrepresentation, whether willful or innocent. An applicant must be the spouse, parent, son or daughter of a citizen or lawful permanent resident, have possessed an immigrant visa or equivalent document, and otherwise have been admissible at entry. The exceptions for labor-certification and immigrant-documentation grounds under §§1182(a)(5)(A) and (7)(A) apply only when those grounds directly resulted from the fraud. The separate exclusion for assistance in Nazi persecution or participation in genocide remains. An actually granted waiver also reaches entry-inadmissibility grounds directly resulting from the fraud. These provisions define eligibility and the consequence of relief; they do not remove conduct from the discretionary decision.

*Jay v. Boyd* supplies the distinction between statutory eligibility and discretionary selection. *INS v. Errico* construed a mandatory predecessor; its family-preservation concern does not convert the later discretionary provision into an entitlement. *National Center for Immigrants' Rights* supports discretion exercised within the statute while preserving required individual consideration.

The Board accepted eligibility and expressly disregarded the initial entry fraud. It considered the wife's false-identity scheme and sham marital arrangements, the later naturalization fraud, further marital arrangements and lack of candor, together with favorable residence, business, property, family and health considerations and Taiwan connections. Those are the Board's findings and assessments. The Ninth Circuit allowed dishonest testimony to count but wrongly excluded collaboration and naturalization conduct because of their connection with entry. The argument that waiver discretion must remain meaningful therefore does not justify its categorical rule. A continuing pattern distinguishes applicants without making every eligible applicant lose. The Court does not reweigh the balance.

#### III. Permissible factors remain subject to reasoned and consistent administration

**Holding and operative rule.** Statutory permission to consider a factor does not excuse arbitrary decisionmaking, pretext, disregard of a binding rule, or an unexplained departure from a genuinely settled agency policy. Here the Board rationally distinguished continuing immigration fraud from the initial entry fraud it disregarded; the record supplies no policy reversal requiring a new administrative balance.

**Authority.** Part III of Justice Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court separates statutory authority from the lawfulness of its exercise. *Smiley*, applying the administrative-change principles preserved from *Good Samaritan*, requires examination of the agency's actual reasons, prior guidance and legitimate reliance. A court cannot supply a litigation explanation for an unexplained change. An agency may revise a policy openly with adequate explanation, subject to binding law; the existence of discretion alone supplies no answer.

The Board retained its initial-fraud distinction and explained the significance of the continuing pattern. Its actual reasons defeat the claim that this decision irrationally abandoned that distinction. The Court neither finds a different binding policy nor commands a new explanation for an unproved reversal. The mandate removes the Ninth Circuit's compelled reweighing based on categorical exclusion. Any genuinely remaining preserved objection is for ordinary proceedings within the record and mandate; the Court directs neither a waiver nor its denial, reopening, a new hearing, or a fresh Board balance. This decision concerns the statutory immigration function presented, not classifications outside it.

#### Precedent Treatment

- *Landgraf v. USI Film Products* and *Rivers v. Roadway Express*: applied to the actual congressional timing instructions; their distinct temporal rationales are not combined into a broader unanimous framework.
- *Jay v. Boyd*, 351 U.S. 345: applied for eligibility's separation from discretionary selection; it remains authority for that distinction.
- *INS v. Errico*, 385 U.S. 214: distinguished because its mandatory predecessor differs from the discretionary text here; its own construction is not overruled.
- *INS v. National Center for Immigrants' Rights* (December 16, 1991): applied by analogy for statutory boundaries and legally required individual consideration; its release-condition holding is not transformed into a waiver decision.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* and *Smiley* (June 3, 1996): applied for reasoned administrative interpretation and explained treatment of settled policy and reliance; statutory discretion does not displace those limits.
- *INS v. Doherty* (January 15, 1992): its fractured disposition supplies no controlling substantive reopening rationale for this waiver question; no separate rationale is promoted to Court law.

#### Law After Decision

Effective November 13, 1996, courts may not exclude a continuing immigration-fraud pattern from former §241(a)(1)(H) discretion merely because its acts are connected with entry. The eligibility conditions, the distinct consequence of a granted waiver, meaningful individual discretion and ordinary review of actual agency reasons remain operative. The Court leaves the minimum-fraud-only individualized case unresolved and permits no universal eligibility-based denial. The specific transition construction preserves review at this event; it decides no post-April §242(g) scope question. No waiver is awarded or denied by this Court, and the agency balance is not judicially redone.

### 1997-03-18 — Young v. Harper; No. 95-1598.

Authority: [Record](../records/Young_v_Harper_merits_1997-03-18.md).

#### Holdings

Part I is directly joined by all eight Associate Justices. Parts II–IV are directly joined by all nine Justices. These are majority holdings at their stated scopes; no separate-rationale synthesis is involved.

###### Part I — Accepted rehearing makes this petition timely

**Holding and operative rule:** When the appellate court expressly permits a late rehearing filing, treats it as timely and keeps the proceeding open pending its resolution, the accepted rehearing’s denial is the relevant finality point for the ensuing certiorari period. This petition, filed within 90 days of denial on that sequence, is timely.

**Controlling explanation:** The governing Rule 13.3 measures the period from denial of timely rehearing, and FRAP 40 permits enlargement by court order. Bowman distinguishes an unauthorized late submission from one the court permits and entertains. Jenkins similarly respects the lower court’s actual treatment as substantive rehearing without allowing manipulation of this Court’s statutory period. Here leave was granted, the filing was treated as timely and the mandate was withheld. A merely unauthorized late paper, an en banc suggestion not treated as rehearing, a mandate date alone or a later order entered solely to extend this Court’s filing time would not establish the same result. The Court waives no statutory period and announces no rule that every late submission restarts it.

###### Part II — Existing parole-like community liberty requires protection

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State releases a prisoner to live at home, work and conduct ordinary community life under parole-like supervision and conditions, termination of that existing conditional liberty requires Morrissey’s protections. Retained legal custody, an overcrowding objective, the preparole label and a separate decision about formal parole do not eliminate that interest.

**Controlling explanation:** Morrissey protects substantial conditional freedom already possessed while accounting for a continuing sentence and lawful grounds for return. Greenholtz distinguishes a request for initial release, and Meachum’s ordinary internal-transfer setting does not describe this community life. Sandin expressly preserves directly constitutional liberty alongside its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement inquiry; no exhaustive program-entitlement finding is needed here. Residence, employment and supervision establish the relevant difference, without making five months a minimum constitutional duration.

The Governor’s denial is not automatically an adequate revocation proceeding. The August 1991 procedure cannot establish a return condition communicated at Harper’s October 1990 release or validate his March return by itself. Yet the earlier procedure’s silence on parole denial does not establish the whole program’s terms, prove compliance with every condition or make release irrevocable. The Court does not invalidate the later procedure prospectively or decide every lawful ground for return. Formal parole, short furloughs, work details and ordinary internal transfers remain distinct.

###### Part III — Morrissey’s ordinary stages and qualifications govern

**Holding and operative rule:** Termination of this community liberty requires the ordinary preliminary and final revocation protections described below. Final revocation must rest on a constitutionally adequate determination, but a completed final hearing is not invariably required before initial arrest or detention.

**Required process and operative qualifications:** When the participant is arrested and detained pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the place of the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground for detention for a release-condition violation. A person not directly involved in the case decides; that person need not be a judge and may be a different parole officer. The participant receives notice of the inquiry, its purpose and the alleged violations, may appear and speak, and may present relevant documents and witnesses. On request he may question an adverse informant in his presence. If the officer determines that identifying the informant would expose that person to harm, confrontation and cross-examination of that informant may be withheld. The officer summarizes the proceedings and states the reasons and evidence supporting the determination; formal judicial findings are unnecessary. Supported probable cause permits continued detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final revocation hearing, if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation. It must resolve contested relevant facts and whether the circumstances warrant revocation, allowing denial and mitigation. Minimum protections are written notice of the claimed violations or asserted grounds; disclosure of adverse evidence; an opportunity to be heard personally and present witnesses and documents; confrontation and cross-examination unless the officer specifically finds good cause to withhold them; a neutral and detached decisionmaker, whose members need not be judges or lawyers; and a written statement of the evidence relied on and reasons.

Informal evidence, including letters and affidavits, may be considered. Issues already determined adversely in another forum, including a relevant criminal conviction, need not be relitigated. Morrissey’s discussion of two months establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor. Preliminary informant risk and final-stage specific good cause are distinct exceptions, each permitting rather than requiring withholding upon the necessary finding. No such finding, valid waiver, dispositive admission, prior determination or supported probable-cause decision is established here. This case decides no separate counsel question.

**Controlling explanation:** Morrissey’s flexible administrative process protects reliable identification of a lawful ground and the judgment whether revocation is warranted. Calling the program preparole cannot replace those safeguards with a telephone return instruction. Conversely, procedural protection does not convert revocation into a criminal trial or require a judicial decisionmaker. Temporary detention remains distinct from final deprivation and must receive the appropriate prompt preliminary and final protections. The Court adds no evidentiary burden, fixed deadline, automatic-release consequence or new adjudicatory code.

###### Part IV — Affirmance preserves lawful grounds and bounded relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tenth Circuit correctly reversed denial of habeas relief because the termination of Harper’s community liberty required Morrissey process. Proceedings must apply that determination, while Oklahoma retains authority to establish a legally available revocation ground through adequate procedures or another independent lawful basis for custody.

**Controlling explanation:** The procedural violation does not establish formal-parole entitlement, expiration of sentence, absence of every possible release-condition violation or a particular release date. The original telephone instruction does not itself supply an adequate revocation determination. The Court neither directs unconditional release nor prescribes a new deadline for state proceedings; any subsequent revocation or continuing custody must be assessed under the governing law and actual record. Lynce supplies no hypothetical exhaustion defect and no implied waiver here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Morrissey v. Brewer: applied to existing parole-like community liberty with both stages, distinct confrontation exceptions, informal evidence and bounded relief intact.
- Greenholtz v. Inmates of Nebraska Penal and Correctional Complex: its initial-release distinction is preserved; this case concerns freedom already conferred.
- Meachum v. Fano: its ordinary internal-transfer setting is distinguished from actual community release.
- Sandin v. Conner: its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule and preservation of direct liberty are applied without an exhaustive independent state-entitlement finding.
- Bowman v. Loperena: applied to rehearing the lower court permits and entertains, rather than an unauthorized late submission.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, 495 U.S. 33 (1990): actual rehearing treatment determines finality within the statutory limit; orders merely extending this Court’s filing time remain insufficient.
- Lynce v. Mathis: genuine habeas threshold predicates remain necessary; no exhaustion defect or waiver is inferred from missing papers.
- United States v. Gonzales: its sentence-concurrency construction is distinguished from conditional-release process and supplies no authority for summary revocation.

#### Law After Decision

Parole-like community freedom receives Morrissey protection by its actual character, irrespective of the preparole name. Existing safeguards retain their ordinary flexibility and limitations, and lawful revocation remains possible. Initial parole eligibility and complete state-program entitlement remain separate questions. Accepted rehearing on the stated sequence makes this review timely without a general enlargement of the certiorari period.

### 1997-03-31 — Young v. Fordice; No. 95-2031.

Authority: [Record](../records/Young_v_Fordice_merits_1997-03-31.md).

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court has all nine joins; every following proposition controls at its stated scope.

##### Federal implementation does not exempt discretionary voting changes

**Rule:** A covered jurisdiction must preclear new voter-registration practices embodying state policy choices even when adopted to comply with federal legislation. The NVRA preserves Voting Rights Act obligations; this decision does not decide an act compelled in every detail by federal law and leaving no state discretion.

**Controlling explanation:** Voting Rights Coalition preserves the valid federal-registration duties and distinct state-election questions; it supplies no automatic preclearance. Presley locates this case within practices directly governing registration and voting. Lopez prevents state-law compulsion from excusing a covered jurisdiction's administration of voting changes, but the Court need not mechanically extend that ruling to the different wholly federal hypothetical. Mississippi retained choices about instructions, forms, agencies, assistance, reporting and how applicants understand federal-only registration. Federal compliance does not exempt those choices from the statutory sequence.

##### This Provisional Plan never became the §5 comparison baseline

**Rule:** The Provisional Plan did not become a practice in force or effect for §5 comparison on the combined circumstances of forty-one days of registration use, adoption by only about one-third of registration officials, no election held or imminent, and prompt abandonment when anticipated state legislation would not be enacted. The Old System supplies the benchmark for this New System challenge; no single listed circumstance creates a general threshold.

**Controlling explanation:** Perkins and Lockhart preserve the principle that a practice may be operative despite a state-law defect. State illegality alone therefore cannot exclude a baseline. Here the limited use and uptake, nonelectoral setting and prompt abandonment together establish the tentative character of the arrangement, rather than a settled registration practice displaced later. Actual registrations are not erased from the facts: the Court finds neither that nobody registered nor that nobody relied on the plan. It determines the system-level §5 comparison, not whether every individual registration was legally valid, and adopts no forty-one-day, one-third-adoption, intent or election-use bright line.

##### The New System still changes the Old System

**Rule:** Retention of old state-election qualifications does not remove §5 coverage where the New System changes the Old System's registration instructions, agency designations, manuals, reporting, assistance or communication of federal-only and combined registration status. Both minor and major covered changes require preclearance without prior proof that they harm minority voters.

**Controlling explanation:** Allen and Hampton County require submission of changes in voting practices, not just changes deemed major or discriminatory in the enforcement suit. Mississippi's New System introduces discretionary procedures absent from the Old System; calling it a return to old state qualifications does not restore the former machinery. The changes can affect who registers, the information received and the administration of forms and voter categories. This supplies an independent coverage ground even though the Provisional Plan itself never became the benchmark. Substantive purpose and effect belong to the designated preclearance process, not this coverage decision.

##### Approval covers the identified submission, not every later contingency

**Rule:** Preclearance of a submitted registration arrangement does not approve materially different later choices that the submission did not identify. The submitting jurisdiction must describe its changes sufficiently; ambiguity does not require the Attorney General to infer and approve every possible later arrangement.

**Controlling explanation:** McCain and Lopez require accurate identification of what was submitted and approved. The earlier submission proceeded on an arrangement allowing NVRA registration to serve federal and state elections; the later dual arrangement's new communications and administration were not submitted as such. Neither federal enactment nor the Attorney General's knowledge of legislative uncertainty supplies blanket approval of unpresented choices. Particular genuine submission questions remain for the district court on the actual terms. The Court does not rescind the earlier approval or make it subject to judicial review merely because it fails to cover a later change.

##### Enforce nonuse through lawful, fitted relief

**Rule:** The enforcement court determines coverage and submission status and enforces nonuse of covered unprecleared practices; only the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia supplies substantive preclearance. Relief must preserve lawful NVRA duties and remain within independent remedial authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Clark, Morse, Shaw and Lopez preserve that division of responsibility. Calling a State-selected plan court ordered does not exempt it. Under Connor and McDaniel, a necessary temporary plan wholly formulated by a court may operate without separate §5 approval only when independent lawful authority supports it; it must comply with the Constitution and Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessary and yield to a lawful precleared arrangement. No such plan or emergency exception is selected here. The district court must identify the covered changes, enforce nonuse and permit expeditious use of the designated channels without blocking compliance with a federal command. The Court orders no identical rolls, racial-purpose or retrogression finding, damages, cancellation of registrations or completed-election consequence.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson:** Preserved federal registration duties and distinct state-election administration; no automatic submission exemption or compulsory identical-roll remedy follows.
- **Presley v. Etowah County Commission:** Applied the direct-voting boundary to registration machinery, without extending §5 to every allocation of governmental power.
- **Perkins v. Matthews and City of Lockhart v. United States:** Preserved actual operative practice as potentially controlling despite state-law defects; mere illegality does not eliminate a baseline.
- **Allen v. State Board of Elections and NAACP v. Hampton County:** Applied coverage to minor as well as major voting changes and kept substantive discrimination separate.
- **McCain v. Lybrand:** Applied specific-submission requirements; an earlier approval does not encompass unpresented later choices.
- **Lopez v. Monterey County:** Applied administration, specific submission and nonuse principles with its lawful judicial-remedy qualifications; the wholly federal ministerial hypothetical remains undecided.
- **Clark v. Roemer:** Applied nonuse pending preclearance; no new emergency or retrospective equitable rule is announced.
- **Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia and Shaw v. Reno:** Preserved designated preclearance channels and the distinction between coverage and substantive review.
- **Connor v. Johnson and McDaniel v. Sanchez:** Preserved the distinction between a wholly judicial temporary remedy and judicial adoption of governmental policy.

#### Law After Decision

Discretionary NVRA implementation remains subject to §5. The Old System, not this short and incompletely adopted Provisional Plan, is the benchmark; the New System includes unapproved changes from it. Earlier approval remains submission-specific. Neither federal duties nor the possibility of separate federal and state rolls authorizes unprecleared discretionary details, and no rule declares every temporary or state-law-defective practice ineligible as a baseline.

### 1997-04-15 — Chandler v. Miller, No. 96-126

Authority: [Record](../records/Chandler_v_Miller_merits_1997-04-15.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for each rule and qualification below.

**1. Candidacy does not waive bodily privacy.** Government-required urine collection and chemical analysis are Fourth Amendment searches. Choosing to seek public office does not waive that protection; candidacy's public setting may inform reasonableness but does not itself authorize a compelled bodily search.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner establishes the search character of compelled urinalysis, and Vernonia preserves that conclusion even where participation in an activity is voluntary. A candidate's exposure to public scrutiny of qualifications is different from consent to bodily collection and medical analysis. Georgia's use of a personal physician and initial disclosure to the candidate reduce the intrusion, but do not eliminate the protected interest. The Court evaluates those actual safeguards and attributes no unsupported public disclosure, observed collection, police use or unrelated medical testing to the program.

**2. Georgia has not supplied a special need sufficient to justify this candidate-testing program.** A suspicionless search justified by a need beyond ordinary law enforcement must be reasonable in light of the actual governmental need, the program's operation, and the affected privacy interests. General desires for drug-free officials and public confidence do not supply the substantial, concrete need required for this broad advance-scheduled candidate program; its reduced intrusion cannot make up that deficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Skinner and Von Raab permit special-needs searches when the protective or integrity interests and the program's operation justify the departure from individualized suspicion. Vernonia sustains its particular school-athletic program through custodial responsibility, contextual privacy and protective need. Political candidates do not share that setting. Georgia's covered offices have no common duty supplying a comparable protective risk, and the record identifies neither a concrete drug problem nor another substantial operational need connecting this broad class to the search. Sound judgment, integrity and deterrence remain legitimate interests, but do not explain why a negative certificate from one predictable test, scheduled within thirty days before qualification, adequately answers the asserted concern about future service. Advance scheduling limits what the certificate establishes; it does not make every scheduled test useless or unconstitutional. Nor must a State wait for a catastrophe, quantify efficacy, exhaust every alternative, or show safety-sensitive duties in every conceivable valid program. The holding rests on this program's combined deficiencies, not a mandatory checklist of missing facts. Georgia's strongest argument—the importance of trustworthy officials and the modest intrusion—therefore fails because a modest bodily search still needs an adequate governmental justification.

**3. The live prospective challenge requires relief against enforcement, without altering past elections.** Chandler's stated and uncontested intention to run again preserves the controversy despite completion of the 1994 election. The judgment sustaining the challenged requirement is reversed, and the case is remanded for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief barring its enforcement.

**Controlling explanation:** The continuing qualification condition affects Chandler's future candidacy, so resolving that live Fourth Amendment dispute does not require setting aside a completed election. The Court prescribes no replacement testing regime or new candidate qualification, and decides no damages, drug use, test result or individualized disqualification. The holding leaves materially different programs to their own supported special-needs justification. Independent First Amendment or state-law qualification claims, medical-fitness examinations, financial disclosure, particularized-suspicion testing, security screening tied to an actual safety risk, and private-sector testing are not decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association: Applies the rule that compelled urine collection and analysis are searches and its attention to substantiated protective need and actual privacy safeguards.
- Treasury Employees v. Von Raab: Preserves contextual special-needs analysis, including integrity interests; the doctrine is not confined to preventing a physical catastrophe.
- Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton: Applies the search and no-waiver holding and distinguishes the actual school-athletic merits holding on custodial, privacy and protective facts. Its five-Justice program rule, eight-Justice facial-prescription ruling and unanimous search rule remain distinct and unchanged.
- New Jersey v. T.L.O.: Its school-responsibility rationale does not transfer wholesale to adult political candidates. No earlier special-needs decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Georgia's challenged candidate-testing condition violates the Fourth Amendment. Suspicionless testing remains available under the existing contextual special-needs doctrine when a materially different program has an adequate supported justification. Public prominence and reduced intrusion alone do not provide it; neither a proven catastrophe nor exhaustion of all alternatives becomes a universal condition.

### 1997-04-21 — Blessing v. Freestone, No. 95-1441

Authority: [Record](../records/Blessing_v_Freestone_merits_1997-04-21.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority for all holdings:** The Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each following proposition has nine direct votes. Scalia and Kennedy also state a separate, noncontrolling reservation about the original meaning of §1983; it changes none of these joins or the mandate.

**1. A §1983 statutory claim must identify an individual right.** A claimant must identify the particular statutory provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding rather than merely precatory obligation, and defining the asserted individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than leaving it vague or amorphous. The text, statutory setting and actual entitlement asserted govern; violation of a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish the right.

**Controlling explanation:** Wright and Wilder recognize defined individual entitlements within funding statutes; they do not make an entire program an indivisible promise. Livadas separately identifies the federal right, the §1983 channel, congressional foreclosure, defendant and remedy. Suter likewise preserves provision-specific adjudication rather than one answer for all state-plan provisions. The families must therefore identify what each particular command entitles them to demand. Program importance, mandatory wording and remedial usefulness cannot take the place of that inquiry; qualitative language is not universally unenforceable merely because applying it requires judgment.

**2. Title IV-D supplies no individual right to general administrative adequacy.** The federal substantial-compliance measure does not give each beneficiary a personal right to compel systemwide compliance. The same general theory fails when restated as an entitlement to sufficient agency staffing or satisfactory computerized administration as such; rejection of those theories does not decide whether a distinct, actually asserted provision creates a particular individual right.

**Controlling explanation:** The substantial-compliance measure evaluates aggregate performance for federal oversight and funding consequences. A State may meet that benchmark while failing one family; aggregate success cannot extinguish an independently existing personal right. Section 654(3) directs a single, separate organizational unit meeting prescribed staffing requirements, but identifies no beneficiary's right to a particular staff complement, caseload or assigned worker. Former and amended §654(16), their associated requirements and §654a's management and performance functions similarly organize program administration. Their technical detail does not make the system itself an individual entitlement. Mandatory duties important to effective collections therefore do not sustain the general claim recognized below. Alleged shortages, backlogs and accounting defects may still bear on a proved violation or fitted remedy, but create no right by themselves. No separately pleaded claim to each standardized computer requirement, confidentiality protection or individual transmission deadline is established here. The Court adjudicates no individual clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 and announces no categorical rule against regulatory elaboration of an established statutory right. A genuinely distinct claim must receive its own provision-specific inquiry, rather than automatic approval or rejection because it appears in an administrative section.

**3. General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983.** Once a provision creates an individual federal right, that right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes the channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Title IV-D's federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions do not alone foreclose every otherwise existing individual claim; any specific incompatible remedy must be assessed for the actual provision and claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Livadas separates enforceability from whether a right exists. Funding sanctions regulate the federal-state relationship and offer no general administrative forum for a family to obtain case-specific relief. Their detail and practical importance do not create the incompatibility found in the different remedial arrangements of Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson. Wright and Wilder likewise reject automatic foreclosure from federal administrative enforcement. The District Court's chapterwide preclusion ground cannot support dismissal of every possible concrete right. This holding does not establish a particular right, excuse ordinary defendant or relief requirements, or resolve a hypothetical provision-specific remedy not identified here.

**4. State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 prohibit rejecting enforceability merely because a Social Security Act provision appears among requirements for a state plan or its contents. They do not dispense with the individual-right inquiry or alter the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not enforceable in a private action.

**Controlling explanation:** These provisions apply to actions pending on their respective October 20 and October 31, 1994 enactment dates and actions brought thereafter, including this action. Their text leaves other established private-action grounds intact except for overturning any state-plan ground applied in Suter but not in earlier enforcement decisions, and expressly saves the §671(a)(15) result. The Court applies Suter's actual provision-specific rights and separate implied-action holdings, including its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) case-plan claim. No broader state-plan immunity is imported through the statutory reference, and no every-provision entitlement follows from removing that categorical obstacle.

**5. Madrid's concrete distribution allegation and the operative law require separate examination.** A broad prayer for programwide relief does not erase Madrid's allegation that collected support was not distributed under former §657(b)(1). The District Court must identify each asserted provision, conduct, remedy, governing version and effective date before deciding right, violation and relief; neither a superseded duty nor a duty not yet effective may silently support an injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** Former §657(b)(1) is materially more specific than an aggregate administrative benchmark, but specificity does not decide Madrid's unadjudicated eligibility, payment facts, legal right, defendant or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not promise a fresh $50 for each installment regardless of timing. Subsection (b) covers collections under an approved plan in fiscal years beginning after September 30, 1976, subject to subsection (d)'s distinct foster-care rules. Those rules first reimburse current foster-care maintenance, direct qualifying excess up to ordered support to the placement agency for the child's interests, then reimburse unrepaid past foster-care or family-assistance payments from the remainder, with the federal financing share reimbursed appropriately and any balance going to that agency for the child's interests. The agency may reserve its payments for future needs or make them available to the person meeting the child's daily needs. No relevant exception or payment fact is established for Madrid. The 1996 changes likewise have separate operative dates and transitions; compilation together does not make all duties presently applicable. The court below must apply them to identified claims and dates, without inferring Arizona implementation or default.

**Statutory timing limits on the remand:**

- Pub. L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.
- Under §395(b), the applicable date is the later of the otherwise specified date or the effective date of implementing state legislation, but no later than the first day of the first calendar quarter after the close of the first regular legislative session beginning after August 22, 1996. Each year of a two-year session counts separately. Under §395(c), a State unable to comply without amending its constitution is protected from a noncompliance finding until the earlier of one year after the necessary amendment takes effect or five years after August 22, 1996. Neither rule establishes an Arizona event or compliance fact here.
- The former §654(24) operational-computer deadline was changed from October 1, 1995 to October 1, 1997 by the October 12, 1995 amendment; the old date establishes no present default. The 1996 text separately requires compliance by October 1, 1997 with requirements enacted on or before October 13, 1988, and by October 1, 2000 with requirements enacted on or before August 22, 1996. The latter deadline extends one day for every day the Secretary misses the final-regulations deadline of two years after August 22, 1996. Implementation deadlines remain distinct from enactment and general effective dates.
- Section 654a(g), added by §312(c), takes effect October 1, 1998. Section 312(d)(2) separately permits a State processing receipts through local courts on August 22, 1996 to continue that receipt processing through September 30, 1999, at its option, through the courts already processing those payments by enactment. That narrow exception concerns §654b(b)(1)'s unit receipt-handling requirement; it is not a wholesale extension of every computer-system obligation. No Arizona qualification for it is found, and these future duties are not April 1997 mandates merely because they appear in the compilation.

**6. Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation.** On remand, the District Court must identify and assess the actual individual claims, then any §1983 foreclosure, proper defendant, breach, causation and lawful relief. Neither general administrative failure nor rejection of the programwide theory authorizes an undifferentiated substantial-compliance injunction, blanket dismissal, specified payment, damages award or prescribed staffing or computer redesign.

**Controlling explanation:** General administrative shortcomings can be evidence relevant to an established right and a remedy fitted to its violation. They are not a general commission to supervise Arizona's program. The Court decides no directly implied Title IV-D action through the §1983 inquiry. The Eleventh Amendment argument and request to overrule Maine v. Thiboutot were neither presented below nor included in the certiorari questions and are not reached; that preservation limit does not resolve every future defendant-specific question. The Ninth Circuit's undifferentiated right cannot stand, but the State's requested dismissal of every possible particular claim does not follow.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Suter v. Artist M.: Preserves its actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights and separate implied-action holdings and its unanimous preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim for specific adjudication. State-plan placement is not a chapterwide answer.
- Livadas v. Bradshaw: Applies the separate inquiries into individual federal right, §1983, congressional foreclosure, defendant and relief; statutory duty or preemption alone is insufficient.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority: Preserves its defined rent-and-utility entitlement and independent foreclosure analysis; no programwide Title IV-D right follows.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association: Preserves its identified reimbursement obligation and rejection of automatic foreclosure through funding withdrawal; it does not resolve the unparsed claims here.
- Pennhurst: Applies the need for clear notice of an asserted funding obligation without categorical immunity for state-plan provisions.
- Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association and Smith v. Robinson: Preserve foreclosure through comprehensive incompatible remedies; Title IV-D's general audits and funding measures alone do not supply that incompatibility.
- Maine v. Thiboutot: Remains undisturbed; the unpreserved request to overrule it is not reached.

#### Law After Decision

Title IV-D's aggregate compliance measure and general administrative machinery create no individual entitlement to systemwide adequacy. Specific actual claims remain subject to the individual-right test and a separate congressional-foreclosure inquiry. General federal oversight does not alone displace §1983, and state-plan location does not alone defeat enforcement. Remedy must fit an established right and violation under the applicable statutory version; no individual distribution right or statewide structural remedy is newly established.

### 1997-04-28 — Richards v. Wisconsin; No. 96-5955.

Authority: [Record](../records/Richards_v_Wisconsin_merits_1997-04-28.md).

#### Holdings

##### I. The felony-drug category cannot replace the required execution inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Amendment does not permit officers to dispense with announcement solely because a warrant seeks evidence of felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver. A valid search warrant authorizes the search but does not itself establish that an unannounced forcible entry is reasonable.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine in Parts I, II and IV; Part III has the distinct seven-Justice coalition specified below.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson v. Arkansas makes announcement of identity and authority part of reasonable dwelling entry while preserving supported exceptions. Wisconsin's category assumes danger and threatened destruction in every included case despite differences in occupants, evidence and execution. General associations among drugs, guns and easy disposal cannot do the work of a particular justification. Announcement protects voluntary admission, property, privacy and occupant safety even when officers hold a warrant. Buie rejects an automatic offense premise for a broader protective sweep; its permission to inspect immediately adjoining attack spaces rests on a different immediate risk. Maryland v. Wilson likewise permits a limited passenger-exit direction through its specific safety and incremental-intrusion comparison, not a general license for surprise dwelling entry. The Court therefore rejects this category without declaring every categorical Fourth Amendment rule invalid. Chandler's attention to the actual justification in its different testing context is consistent with, but does not enlarge, this holding.

##### II. Reasonable suspicion governs a supported execution exception

**Holding and operative rule:** Officers must identify particular circumstances and rational inferences supporting reasonable suspicion that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including by allowing destruction of evidence. The threshold is below probable cause but requires more than the offense label or an unsupported hunch, and the relevant facts must be known when the challenged force begins.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson preserves announcement exceptions but leaves their precise quantum open. Buie's use of articulable suspicion for a limited protective step supports a practical threshold by analogy: execution of an independently authorized search may require a prompt response without awaiting probable cause of imminent destruction. This rule supplies no authority to search without a warrant or another lawful basis and does not transfer every protective-sweep rule to evidence searches. For the destruction branch, suspicion must connect delay for announcement and voluntary admission to a threat to preservation of the evidence. Officers need not hear destruction or wait until evidence is lost. A drug label or unsupported general weapons claim remains insufficient, and later discovery or flight cannot retroactively justify earlier force. The standard applies to the identified execution exceptions; the Court fixes no universal waiting time and does not equate seeing a uniform with knowing the officers' purpose. The independent useless-gesture contention need not be decided here.

##### III. Historical findings and constitutional application have different review roles

**Holding and operative rule:** In reviewing this execution under the reasonable-suspicion standard, the historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, with grounded experience given real weight; the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of those facts is independently assessed. That allocation permits review of the supported record without manufacturing new facts.

**Authority:** Breyer, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven direct joins. Scalia withholds this method; Stone-Zsela joins the substantive holdings and application without an additional position on it.

**Controlling explanation:** Ornelas separates historical factfinding from ultimate reasonable suspicion in its stop and warrantless-search setting. The same functional distinction applies here: the trial court decides what officers observed and whose testimony to credit, while the constitutional execution threshold remains a legal question. Ornelas did not previously decide every warrant-execution question; this is its reasoned application to the present one. Robinette demonstrates that correcting a general rule does not invariably decide its application or require remand. Here the original hearing already supplies the material observations and credibility determination. The Court respects those findings while testing their constitutional significance, and need not supplement them with assumed purpose knowledge or later events.

##### IV. The credited contemporaneous facts justified this entry

**Holding and operative rule:** The credited look toward a nearby uniformed officer and surprised reaction, combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, supplied particularized reasonable suspicion of destruction risk before the first attempt to enter; the subsequent rapid closure and locking strengthened that justification for the later force. The entry was therefore reasonable despite the invalid categorical rule and the judge's advance refusal of no-knock authorization.

**Controlling explanation:** Pharo's maintenance ruse brought Richards to the door. The trial judge rejected the material portion of Richards's contrary account and inferred awareness of the uniformed officer from the credited observations. Recognition is an inference on disputed evidence, not a concession. The look toward the uniform and surprise preceded the first attempted entry and supported the inference that the ruse had exposed the police; rapid closure and locking strengthened it for later force. Combined with the specifically sought disposable cocaine, these facts supplied more than a general drug assumption. Closing against a supposed late-night maintenance intruder is a possible innocent account, but it does not eliminate reasonable suspicion from the credited police-recognition response. Neither intended destruction nor certainty of recognition had to be proved. Identity was shouted two or three seconds after closure, possibly while force was already underway; purpose was recalled as announced when the door opened. A belated announcement cannot cure prior unlawful force, but a destruction exception already justified this execution. Later window flight, drugs found above ceiling tiles, unproved destruction noises and an unproved weapon play no part in supplying pre-force justification. The original hearing suffices without deciding the objection to supplemental testimony. Advance refusal required attention to new facts, not disregard of the judge's ruling on the same general premise; the doorway developments supplied those new facts. The ruse was not consent to forced entry.

The Court does not decide exclusion after a violation, an independently sufficient futility ground, the cross-petition objection concerning the alternative remedy argument, or a general doctrine for every police-created exigency. A valid warrant alone is not declared an independent evidentiary source or proof of inevitable lawful discovery.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Wilson v. Arkansas is applied to reject the offense category and given a reasonable-suspicion execution threshold; its constitutional exclusion question remains open.
- Maryland v. Buie supplies a practicable articulable-suspicion analogy and distinguishes a justified limited category from an automatic offense premise for a greater intrusion.
- Ornelas is applied by functional analogy to the review allocation in Part III, with the existing factual protections intact.
- Ohio v. Robinette supports review of interwoven federal/state reasoning and separates correction of a rule from application and remedy; it imposes no obligatory remand on an adequate record.
- Michigan v. Long supplies the independent-state-ground inquiry without displacing genuinely independent Wisconsin law.
- Maryland v. Wilson retains its limited passenger-exit holding, distinguished from forcible dwelling entry.
- Chandler retains its actual-justification analysis in the different bodily-testing setting and supplies no universal prohibition of categories.

#### Law After Decision

Wisconsin may no longer rely on this felony-drug category alone. Unannounced execution requires reviewable reasonable suspicion grounded in the particular circumstances, and the time of the challenged force controls the justification. The Court applies a distinct factual/legal appellate allocation by seven direct joins. The existing conviction stands on the destruction-risk ground; no new constitutional exclusion rule, automatic valid-warrant immunity or universal waiting period follows.
