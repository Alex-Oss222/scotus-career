Five Arizona mothers, some receiving AFDC, sued the state agency director. They alleged that, after applying for child-support services and cooperating, the agency failed to take adequate collection steps. Alleged structural causes included staff shortages, heavy caseloads, backlogs, accounting problems and inadequate recordkeeping. They sought a class of present and future eligible Arizona children and custodial parents, a declaration, and an injunction requiring programwide substantial compliance. The complaint's requested relief appears at Appendix 42; allegations of staffing problems at Appendix 11 and 14–16.

One plaintiff, Madrid, alleged that the agency collected payments from her former husband but failed to distribute the first $50 of each payment (Appendix 13, paragraph 48), invoking the pre-1996 distribution provision, §657(b)(1). That allegation must be kept distinct from a judgment establishing a right, a proved failure, or a specific monetary remedy actually demanded. The general complaint's precise scope and the date-governing text require analysis.

The District Court treated the director's dismissal motion as summary judgment. Its ground was that federal audit and fund-withholding oversight precluded a private §1983 remedy. The divided Ninth Circuit panel reversed, recognizing an enforceable programwide right and rejecting implied displacement by that administrative oversight. The panel left the appropriateness of injunctive or declaratory relief to the District Court. Judge Kleinfeld's dissent disputed individual rights and treated enforcement as entrusted to the Secretary. The lower judgment is 68 F.3d 1141.
