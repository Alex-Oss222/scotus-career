## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment while rejecting Wisconsin's categorical exception.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The evidence remains admissible because the particular entry was reasonable. Wisconsin may not rely on a felony-drug category alone in future cases.

### Issue 1: Case-specific justification for unannounced entry

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both the categorical rule and the case-specific application. The suppression record supports review without new factfinding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone rejects Wisconsin's blanket exception and adopts reasonable suspicion for dispensing with announcement. Officers must possess particular facts supporting a reasonable suspicion that announcement would create danger, be futile, permit escape, or materially risk destruction of evidence. The threshold is lower than probable cause but cannot rest solely on the charged offense.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not permit a categorical no-knock exception for felony drug investigations. The entry here was reasonable because Richards recognized police, immediately closed and secured the door, and the officers reasonably suspected that further delay would permit destruction of readily disposable drugs or escape.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A category-wide exception would make Wilson's protection vanish whenever officials selected a drug offense and would substitute legislative or police generalization for judicial review of the entry actually made. At execution, however, the factual setting changed from the one before the magistrate. Richards's apparent recognition and abrupt retreat made additional announcement substantially futile and created a concrete destruction risk. The officers' brief response was tied to that development rather than to a per se rule.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The disposable character of drugs alone does not justify every unannounced entry. Advance denial of no-knock authorization does not forbid officers from responding to later exigent facts, and advance authorization does not excuse review of the actual execution.
