## Historical comparator and its actual grounds

The historical Court reversed and remanded, six to three. Thomas's opinion was joined by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Breyer's dissent was joined by Stevens and Ginsburg. Rehnquist is not a participating Justice in this timeline and supplies no transferable vote.

The historical Court did more than invoke a general rule against agency revision of statutes. It construed the relevant maximum as the highest term the applicable penalty statute authorizes for the offender. Section 841(b)(1)(C)'s twenty-year ordinary ceiling and thirty-year enhanced ceiling illustrated why an actually applicable enhancement matters. The category argument did not justify merging defendants who had received the required §851 notice with those who had not. The Court expressly separated the identity of the maximum from the flexibility allowed by at or near. It reserved the numerical proximity boundary and acknowledged lawful adjustments and departures. Its footnote 1 made the enhancement dependent on the statutory information filed before trial or acceptance of the plea; footnote 4 distinguished calculating a term from arranging multiple terms under §3584; footnote 5 rejected use of a guideline maximum to test a command governing the writing of those guidelines; footnote 6 left general Chevron deference to the Commission undecided because this statute was considered unambiguous. Neither a mandatory maximum sentence nor an automatic enhancement follows.

The historical dissent agreed that the benchmark is statutory, but disputed which statutory features Congress directed the Commission to incorporate into its career-offender guideline. It treated the offense/offender distinction and the Commission-directed statutory scheme as material to the referent of maximum term authorized. Its examples of probation and consecutive sentences challenged a purportedly self-defining reference to every possible source of imprisonment. It considered the overhaul of sentencing, abolition of parole and legislative history as evidence that more than one statutory implementation was possible, not as proof that Congress affirmatively chose the lower ceiling. It answered the Court's claim that enhancements would become meaningless: enhanced statutory ceilings still permit otherwise lawful sentences above the ordinary ceiling, including appropriate departures or other guideline calculations. Having found interpretive room, it regarded the Commission's uniformity, proportionality and administrability judgments as permissible implementation. This is not a claim that policy defeats statutory text already found clear.

## Reconciled proposition-level support and join boundaries

**Path A — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas.** For the defendants covered by §994(h), the Commission must use the legally applicable statutory maximum, including an applicable recidivist enhancement, as the benchmark for its at-or-near instruction. Amendment 506 is invalid insofar as it categorically excludes an increase that applies because of the defendant's prior criminal record. Categories identify covered offenders and do not authorize ignoring the penalty statute's applicable enhancement. A distinction Congress creates through §851 is not removed by the Commission's desire for uniformity. Without the required timely enhancement information, the enhanced maximum is unavailable; mere potential eligibility does not make it the benchmark. These respondents received the required notices, so no new notice hearing is ordered.

The five Associates directly support the same statutory rule and its application, with these limits: the Commission retains lawful implementation discretion; at or near permits lawful adjustments and departures and supplies no numerical minimum here; the Court does not require prosecutors to seek enhancements or approve an improper charging basis; Amendment 506's specific prior-record exclusion is the question, not every possible guideline or statutory enhancement; and §3584's arrangement of separate terms supplies no rule aggregating all possible punishment into one maximum. General Chevron treatment of the Commission need not be decided to reject commentary inconsistent with the statute. Stinson supplies that consistency limit; Neal and MCI are preserved at their own statutory scope; Auer and Gonzales do not independently decide §994(h).

**Path B — Stevens, Ginsburg, Breyer.** The statutory reference must be read in its Commission-directed, categorical sentencing setting. The words do not unambiguously require incorporating recidivist increases into the career-offender table's benchmark. The Commission may use the unenhanced offense maximum as that benchmark while maintaining the enhanced statutory ceiling and any applicable floor. Its choice reasonably serves the delegated task of developing uniform, proportionate and administrable sentencing categories. Permissible interpretation is distinguished from policy overriding a command already established as unambiguous. The benchmark question must be answered before statutory inconsistency is declared.

These three directly support the common rationale, not merely the same judgment. Their independent proximity conclusion remains bounded: on the scheme and record presented, examining adjusted final sentences alone does not establish that the career-offender guideline fails the separate near requirement. They uphold this implementation without prescribing a universal percentage, licensing every remote sentence, eliminating lawful adjustments, or guaranteeing a reduction. The full separate-writing explanation must address the actual ambiguity-and-delegation ground, the preserved force of enhanced statutory limits, and why uniformity is an implementation consideration rather than authority to disregard clear text. No additional separate writing is required by the reconciled positions; no author is selected here.

All eight agree on the granted scope, the three respondents, the difference between benchmark and proximity, and the distinct §3582(c)(2) gateways and discretion. Those shared principles are not eight votes for either conflicting construction. The two constructions are incompatible at the operative benchmark; no Marks synthesis combines them.
