## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 1 — coverage.** Stone would hold that the shared sex of offender and employee does not exclude Title VII coverage. The statute asks why working conditions were imposed on the claimant, not whether offender and claimant occupy different sex categories. Meritor already recognizes that abusive conditions can constitute discrimination; the new application follows the enacted individual protection. Stone's broader equality commitments support taking the alleged injury seriously but do not create statutory liability independently of the text.

**Question 2 — proof and sexual desire.** Desire is one possible factual explanation, not a required statutory element. Sex-based hostility, selective conduct and other competent circumstances can prove that sex caused the discriminatory conditions. Stone would neither require evidence that a harasser identifies as gay nor treat a sexual act as conclusively establishing discriminatory causation. The trier must evaluate the actual inference and alternatives. Hicks preserves the ultimate burden; it does not demand a fictitious comparator or bar circumstantial proof.

**Question 3 — severity and workplace context.** An all-male workforce does not make sex-based treatment legally impossible. Stone would apply Harris's objective and subjective inquiry in that workplace's actual circumstances, without excusing forced contact and rape threats simply as rough camaraderie. Evidence of severe physical humiliation can meet the threshold without psychiatric injury, but it must still be unwelcome, because of sex, and attributable to an employer under the applicable rule. Ordinary offensive banter and interpersonal hostility unconnected to sex remain outside this statutory cause.

Stone would not announce universal employer responsibility, treat coworkers as employers by fiat, or award relief under a free-standing dignity theory. He would preserve a distinct inquiry into any quid-pro-quo claim: the alleged demand, supervisor's use of employment power, and actual employment consequence must be identified. His proposed interpretation is not a finding that any allegation is true or that all defendants owe relief.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Reverse the judgment insofar as it rests on a categorical same-sex exclusion and remand for adjudication under the correct statutory and Harris standards. Retain independently unchallenged grounds concerning individual coworker employer status. The lower courts should address employer attribution, preserved quid-pro-quo matters, proof of causation and available relief in their ordinary order. No final harassment finding, reinstatement, backpay, punitive award or constructive-discharge determination follows from this mandate.

## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **The disclaimer does not save governmental religious sponsorship.** Stone would assess the actual mechanism, including official events and required religious-content screening. Under Lee's sponsorship majority, a school cannot avoid responsibility merely by assigning delivery to a student or announcing voluntariness. An independently attributable private forum use is a different application and must remain protected.

2. **Student initiation is relevant but not decisive.** Selection, control, audience, and attribution determine whether the speech remains private. Stone would apply Pinette and Chabad to genuine private access, while applying Lee where the school incorporates prayer into its ceremony. Rosenberger does not convert school-directed religious observance into private expression merely because the selected speaker is a student.

3. **Official sponsorship can suffice without a separate quantification of coercion.** Compulsory attendance and institutional pressure strengthen the constitutional concern, but Stone would rely on the actual sponsorship holding he joined. He would not retroactively claim the separate Lee coercion rationale as his own holding. Nor would he presume that hearing a fellow student's private religious viewpoint constitutes unconstitutional coercion.

4. **These enrolled students have standing to challenge the imminent official-event mechanism, with parents asserting their children's interests through the proper representative route.** Stone would draw the concrete-threat inference from their impending attendance at public-school events, the statute's operative requirement that prayer be permitted, and the school's necessary control of event access and the statutory religious-content limits. The challenge arose immediately before the school year, so exposure was not a hypothetical remote contingency. He would acknowledge the contrary argument that districts had not yet implemented a policy; the mandatory mechanism and imminent event participation supply the credible threat despite that uncertainty. He would not grant standing to an abstract objector elsewhere or infer final unconstitutional implementation merely from this jurisdictional conclusion.

5. **Do not enjoin lawful private expression merely because other applications fail.** Stone would require provision-specific and event-specific severability analysis. He would remove the categorical graduation safe harbor insofar as it authorizes the very official sponsorship Lee forbids, while preserving genuinely private, independently attributable access. The interim order should restrain the unconstitutional statutory mechanism, not prohibit every religious statement by a student.

6. **Intervention is not automatic, but the shared desired outcome is not conclusive adequacy.** Stone would require a Rule 24 determination addressing whether the students' private-speech interest is actually protected by the State's litigation position and the contemplated injunction. If that distinct interest may be impaired and inadequately represented, intervention should be allowed on appropriate terms. Amicus participation alone does not answer the rule's entitlement inquiry.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Find Article III standing for the enrolled-student challenge on the concrete impending-exposure basis above. Affirm preliminary protection against school-sponsored religious exercises supported by that record; vacate the categorical graduation exception and any blanket reach into protected private speech; remand for an appropriately severed interim order and a specific Rule 24 adequacy determination. Resolve no final damages claim and impose no general ban on student religious speech. This follows Stone's documented distinction between institutional religious action and equal treatment of genuinely private expression.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Vacate the injunction insofar as it rests on the asserted defect in the best-interests formulation. Remand for proceedings on any preserved issue not resolved by this judgment.

### Issue 1: Best-interests bypass for parental notification

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only whether the stated best-interests criterion is constitutionally sufficient. The statute's confidentiality, timing, and maturity routes are undisputed for this issue.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a direction to waive notice when notification is not in the minor's best interests authorizes the same constitutionally required decision as a direction to waive when abortion without notice is in her best interests.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies simulated Planned Parenthood v. Miller. The required inquiry is practical: can the independent decisionmaker authorize the abortion without notice when involving the parent would be contrary to the minor's best interests? Montana's text says yes. Read in context, it does not authorize a court to deny waiver after finding that proceeding without notice is best for the minor.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A confidential and expeditious bypass satisfies the best-interests component of the parental-notification rule when it requires waiver upon a finding that notification is not in the minor's best interests. Montana's formulation meets that requirement.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The constitutional concern is not a verbal formula but whether the minor can obtain an independent decision that notice should not occur. Montana supplies that authority, adds a maturity route, permits an abuse finding, seals the proceeding, appoints counsel, and grants relief automatically if the court misses the deadline. The Ninth Circuit's distinction would invalidate a functionally adequate route based on wording that changes no legal outcome.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide whether clear-and-convincing proof is valid in every application, whether the emergency provisions are adequate, or whether implementation makes the nominally expedited route unavailable.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved result reads the best-interests language to require waiver when parental notice would harm the minor’s interests. He applies that concrete bypass to the practical-interference concerns in Casey and Miller, while leaving any other preserved defect to its own inquiry. He does not replace simulated Casey with the historical undue-burden framework.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

The following clarification is user-approved except for the preserved source result. For the narrow wording claim, Stone identifies the compulsory notice duty, the independent maturity and best-interests exceptions, and the confined affected group whose parental notice is adverse. He reads the challenged words to require waiver when nonnotification serves the minor’s interests, rather than to authorize a parental veto. On this limited textual issue the challengers have not shown that the phrasing adds an obstacle or meaningful interference compared with the constitutionally adequate wording they seek. That defeats this particular objection at Casey’s interference step; it does not presume that timing, proof requirements, or actual administration satisfy the remaining review. Any properly preserved separate interference must receive the applicable justification and fit inquiry, clinical safeguards, and tailored relief. The proposed mandate therefore removes only the wording-based injunction and leaves unresolved claims for their own decision.

- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The City shall cease using the cross-bearing seal as its official imprimatur within a reasonable implementation period set by the district court. The lower court may preserve records and historical displays with accurate contextual treatment. Individual damages and immunity remain separate.

### Issue 1: Christian cross in the official city seal

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of the municipal seal. The record conclusively establishes government authorship, pervasive use, and the cross's placement.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether secular images elsewhere in the seal and a historical explanation prevent the City's own use of the cross from communicating official religious adoption.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies existing Establishment Clause principles. The seal is government speech: it authenticates official correspondence, property, uniforms, vehicles, and civic action. An unmistakable Latin cross placed among the City's selected civic emblems identifies Christianity as part of the City's official self-description. The surrounding secular images do not convert the cross into a neutral graphic, and longevity does not cure governmental religious adoption.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A municipality violates the Establishment Clause when it incorporates an unmistakable Christian cross into its official seal and uses that seal pervasively as the City's own imprimatur. Edmond's seal has that operation.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The attribution question is decisive. Unlike Pinette, no private speaker selected or owns the message, no neutral forum admitted it, and no disclaimer can plausibly separate the seal from the City. The City may teach its history, preserve archival material, and identify the role of churches in settlement. It may not make the Christian symbol an official mark borne by every resident's government.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not invalidate the City's name, private religious displays in neutral forums, incidental architectural depictions in a genuinely historical exhibit, or ceremonial references that do not adopt a faith as the government's own.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved answer.** Stone would affirm because the cross functions as part of the City’s own recurring official identity on uniforms, bills, vehicles and other governmental instruments.

**Question 2 — preserved approved answer clarified.** Purpose and history inform the objective contextual inquiry; age is neither a constitutional safe harbor nor proof of endorsement by itself. Here control and pervasive official use establish governmental attribution, and the accompanying secular images do not convert the cross into a private or neutral message. Stone would preserve accurate historical records while ending use as the current official imprimatur through the existing bounded remand.

### Issue 1: Record support and fit of must-carry

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the completed merits left open by the 1994 remand. He does not reopen the content-neutral classification fixed by controlling simulated law and does not reach the separately underdeveloped low-power-station issue.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the expanded record now establishes a real threat to local broadcast carriage and a burden proportionate to the statutory response.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the existing intermediate-scrutiny rule. Congress could reasonably conclude that cable bottleneck control and vertical integration threatened carriage of local broadcasters and the free over-the-air service on which many households relied. Less burdensome imagined alternatives need not be adopted when the chosen rule is reasonably fitted and materially more effective.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On the developed record, the must-carry provisions materially advance the important interests recognized in simulated Turner I and do not burden substantially more cable editorial discretion than necessary. The provisions satisfy content-neutral intermediate scrutiny.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The evidence closes the gap identified in 1994. It supports both the likelihood of noncarriage without the rule and the connection between carriage, audience reach, advertising revenue, and broadcast survival. The actual added-carriage burden occupied a small portion of total cable capacity, most systems displaced no service, and Congress confined the obligation rather than commandeering entire lineups. Intermediate scrutiny does not require proof that the broadcasting industry as a whole would collapse or that every compelled station individually faced failure.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not apply broadcast scarcity doctrine to cable, immunize a materially more burdensome future rule, or foreclose a system-specific challenge supported by actual disproportionate displacement.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fair warning does not require a fact-identical Supreme Court case

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the standard because the Sixth Circuit’s categorical rule controlled the judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether criminal liability under 18 U.S.C. § 242 requires a prior Supreme Court decision applying the constitutional right to fundamentally similar facts, or whether pre-existing law gives adequate fair warning when the unlawfulness of the charged official conduct is apparent.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply due-process fair warning and § 242’s willfulness requirement without converting civil qualified immunity into the criminal test. The complete pre-existing legal landscape may supply notice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 242 does not require a prior Supreme Court decision involving fundamentally similar facts. Criminal liability is consistent with fair warning when statutory text, authoritative constitutional law, and their plainly apparent application made the charged official deprivation unlawful at the time; liability remains barred when the theory is novel or unforeseeable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** General legal rules often give clear notice at their core even before every application has been litigated. A state judge cannot demand a precedent involving the same office, victim, and method if established law already makes a coerced sexual assault under color of state authority an obvious invasion of protected liberty. At the same time, criminal condemnation is distinct from civil damages immunity and requires rigorous attention to notice and willfulness. The Sixth Circuit’s Supreme-Court-only and fundamental-similarity requirements were categorical additions unsupported by due process.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that broad invocations of substantive due process always suffice, decide each count’s legal notice, lower the Government’s burden on willfulness, or import every qualified-immunity formulation into criminal law.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's user-approved rejection of a Supreme-Court-only factual-twin rule does not convert a civil notice decision into a conviction. The remand must evaluate contemporaneous fair warning and willfulness for the charged acts and rights.

The district court shall identify the covered components and enter prospective relief consistent with lawful federal-registration duties.

### Issue 1: Baseline and coverage of NVRA implementation changes

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches § 5 coverage and baseline. He does not decide the preclearance merits, discriminatory purpose, retrogression, or the validity of Mississippi's state-registration choices.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the brief Provisional Plan became the operative baseline and whether policy-laden implementation choices in the New System were changes from the Old System.

Federal compulsion to comply with the NVRA does not preclear state choices the federal statute leaves open.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A temporary and partially implemented registration plan that never became the State's lawful operative system does not displace the prior system as the § 5 baseline. Discretionary state choices used to implement the NVRA are voting changes requiring preclearance when they differ from that baseline. Mississippi's New System contains such unprecleared changes.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 5 is triggered by change, not by proof that the change is harmful. The Provisional Plan's forty-one-day use, limited adoption, absence of an election, and prompt abandonment prevent treating it as a settled practice. Comparing the New System to the Old reveals numerous nonministerial choices capable of affecting who registers, what applicants understand, how forms move, and how officials distinguish federal and state voters. Those choices belong in preclearance even when made under a valid federal mandate.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require preclearance of a purely ministerial act dictated with no state discretion, decide whether separate federal and state rolls are substantively lawful, or prevent immediate compliance with a federal command while the State seeks expedited preclearance for discretionary details.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall require the plaintiffs to identify each concrete statutory right asserted, determine whether that provision creates an individual right, and only then address § 1983 preclusion, defendant, causation, and relief. ### Issue 1: Identification of enforceable Title IV-D rights

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether the asserted systemwide right exists. He does not decide unparsed claims under particular payment, distribution, or service provisions.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statute's substantial-compliance yardstick grants each eligible family a judicially enforceable right to systemwide program performance.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the existing rights-versus-law distinction. A plaintiff must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the plaintiff, impose a binding and judicially manageable duty, and define the claimed entitlement with sufficient specificity. Title IV-D's systemwide substantial-compliance standard measures aggregate state performance for federal oversight and tolerates nonperformance in some cases; it does not promise each family that the entire program will satisfy every requirement.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title IV-D does not create an individual federal right to compel a State to achieve systemwide substantial compliance. Any § 1983 claim must be analyzed under the specific statutory provision said to create the individual right.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower court's blanket holding collapses a complex administrative program into one undefined entitlement. Data-system requirements, staffing rules, audit measures, collection duties, and payment-distribution commands perform different legal work and cannot be treated alike. The aggregate compliance metric is addressed to the Secretary and may be met even while a particular family receives no service, which is incompatible with the asserted individual right.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not foreclose a claim under a specific rights-creating provision or decide whether prompt distribution or another concrete command is enforceable.

### Issue 2: Statutory displacement of § 1983

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the categorical preclusion argument because Arizona seeks dismissal of every possible § 1983 claim under the title.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone rejects categorical preclusion. Title IV-D gives the Secretary oversight and funding tools but no general administrative forum in which an individual family can obtain case-specific relief. That scheme is not so comprehensive and incompatible that it displaces § 1983 for every concrete right the statute may create.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title IV-D's general audit and funding-enforcement scheme does not categorically foreclose § 1983 actions to enforce a specific individual federal right.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Funding reduction measures regulate the federal-state relationship and do not provide the claimant a hearing, order, or remedy for her own case. Allowing a properly established individual-right claim would not necessarily conflict with the Secretary's aggregate supervision. The preclusion inquiry must follow, not precede, identification of the claimed right.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not create a right, authorize programwide structural relief, or prevent a more specific statutory remedial scheme from displacing § 1983 for a particular claim.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved remand should require the same separate identification of right, action, defendant, breach, and remedy that Suter preserves. A right belonging to a particular family cannot be inferred merely from a federal audit threshold, and Suter’s reserved concrete plan-and-review provision demonstrates why the lower court must examine any narrower Title IV-D entitlement rather than stop at aggregate noncompliance.

### Issue 1: Findings and standard for terminating the foster-care decree

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the governing standard and adequacy of findings. He does not decide whether New Mexico has substantially complied.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a court can dissolve a decree requiring sustained systemic performance when the adopted report states ultimate conclusions without the subsidiary facts and reasoning needed to test material compliance.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 52 and the decree's text. Substantial compliance means performance that satisfies the decree's essential purposes despite only immaterial deviations. Because the decree specifies fair, reasonable, and timely decisionmaking and durable procedures for permanent placement, the court must address each essential objective, explain the significance of deviations from enumerated criteria, state the methodology used to weigh system data and case evidence, and make enough subsidiary findings for review. Termination is not punishment or reward; it follows when the agreed legal condition is proved.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal court may dissolve this institutional consent decree only on reviewable findings that the Department maintained substantial and continuous compliance with the decree's essential purposes for twelve consecutive months. Conclusory findings that do not explain material deviations, methodology, or subsidiary facts do not satisfy Rule 52(a).
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The report acknowledged serious low-compliance figures and cases in which children languished, yet often declared substantial compliance without explaining why the shortfall was immaterial. It did not identify how unreliable data were treated or how the essential purposes of fairness, timeliness, and durable permanent-placement procedures were satisfied. Appellate judges may not reconstruct those missing findings from a voluminous record. Retaining the decree pending lawful findings respects both state administration and the final judgment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require perfect compliance, convert every numerical target into an indispensable element, authorize indefinite federal control after the termination condition is met, or permit the decree to expand beyond its terms.
