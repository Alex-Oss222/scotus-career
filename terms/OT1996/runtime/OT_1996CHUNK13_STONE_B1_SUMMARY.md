## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: County discretion does not exempt a changed appointment practice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal coverage premise because it independently infected the dismissal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do discretionary county changes in the partisan formulas used to appoint election judges require § 5 preclearance, did Texas’s 1985 submission preclear those methods, and what disposition is required when the record does not establish the appointment procedure in force on the coverage date?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Presley's direct voting nexus and Morse's functional electoral coverage and baseline requirements to the actual appointment practice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A covered county’s discretionary change in the method used to select officials who administer elections is subject to § 5 when it differs from the coverage-date practice. Exercise of authority under a precleared state statute does not exempt the new local practice.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 5 reaches the standard or procedure the County administers, whether formal or informal. The appointment method's concrete connection to polling, not the bare presence or absence of discretion, supplies the required voting nexus. Election judges supervise polling, so the method governing their selection is connected to the conduct of voting. The statutory question remains change from the baseline, which the present record does not establish.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that partisan appointment is substantively unlawful or that an unchanged 1972 practice requires new preclearance.

### Issue 2: The 1985 submission did not identify the local formulas

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the scope of the submission because it was the second basis for dismissal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do discretionary county changes in the partisan formulas used to appoint election judges require § 5 preclearance, did Texas’s 1985 submission preclear those methods, and what disposition is required when the record does not establish the appointment procedure in force on the coverage date?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the notice-based preclearance rule without inventing knowledge for the Attorney General.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Preclearance of a state recodification that identifies only changes to appointment timing does not preclear undisclosed county partisan-selection formulas. A change is approved only when the submission fairly identifies it for review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The summary and statutory text directed attention to start dates and terms, not to the County’s evolving allocation formulas. Blanket approval would defeat the preclearance process by making silence carry every local practice. Morse's requirement of a legally relevant unprecleared change preserves the distinction between the actual submitted measure and another local practice whose approval is not shown.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves any evidence of a separate, sufficiently specific submission or approval.

### Issue 3: Remand is required for the missing baseline

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches disposition and appellate jurisdiction but not the ultimate comparison.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do discretionary county changes in the partisan formulas used to appoint election judges require § 5 preclearance, did Texas’s 1985 submission preclear those methods, and what disposition is required when the record does not establish the appointment procedure in force on the coverage date?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply § 1253 finality and Stone’s prohibition on supplying missing facts.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The interlocutory appeal must be dismissed, the final dismissal vacated, and the matter remanded because the Court cannot determine whether a change occurred without evidence of the procedure in force on the coverage date.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The legal errors below do not prove the plaintiffs’ ultimate factual premise. Section 5 operates by comparison, and the baseline is absent. Remand preserves both the County’s opportunity to show no change and the voters’ right to nonuse if a changed method lacks preclearance.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not select the baseline, decide completed-election consequences, or adjudicate discriminatory purpose or effect.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

### Application to Stone's three proposed answers

Stone's user-approved disposition remains dismissal of the interlocutory appeal, vacatur of the final dismissal and remand. His coverage answer applies Presley's voting nexus to officials who administer polling and Morse's functional, baseline-dependent §5 inquiry; he proposes applying those existing principles to county partisan-selection formulas, without claiming Lopez or Young already decided it. His submission answer rests on the actual 1985 documents identifying timing changes and not the distinct local formulas; a separate sufficiently specific approval, if proved, would matter. His remand answer follows because the legal error does not prove the missing 1972 comparison. The local court may decide coverage and preclearance status, not substitute its view of discrimination for the designated process or order new elections automatically.

# 148. Pounders v. Watson

## A. APPROVED POSITION AND PRECISE DISPOSITION

Stone's approved merits position is to affirm habeas relief from the summary contempt conviction on the narrower due-process ground below, while rejecting a rule that summary contempt requires repeated or pervasive misconduct. California may pursue ordinary contempt proceedings with adequate notice and opportunity to defend if state law permits. A different judge is required only if the governing neutrality or embroilment rule requires one. Stone leaves the underlying trial order and the finding of deliberate violation intact.

Habeas eligibility remains distinct from that merits position. The petition predates AEDPA. Stone would apply the same provision-specific construction he proposes in Lindh: Congress's express pending-case instruction for chapter154 and its omission for chapter153 support applying the older chapter153 framework to this already-filed application. He reaches that statutory conclusion independently here, without treating the prepared Lindh packet as a Court holding.

## B. SUMMARY ADJUDICATION AND ACTUAL TRIAL CONTROL

The judge repeatedly prohibited punishment references. Watson raised the subject, was admonished during the exchange, and then asked about life without parole. Stone accepts the state findings that she knew the order and deliberately violated it, and he takes the finding of serious prejudice to the jury seriously. She explained her conduct at the bench before the contempt finding and again through counsel later; his objection is to the authority for summary adjudication, not a claim that she received no warning or opportunity to speak. A single such event can warrant contempt; repetition is neither a constitutional element nor a prerequisite to decisive trial control.

The distinct question is whether those facts justified dispensing with ordinary criminal-contempt procedure. Stone would require a particularized connection between summary adjudication and protecting the ongoing proceeding or preserving authority that cannot practicably await ordinary process. Observation settles what the judge saw; it does not alone establish why a prompt ordinary hearing cannot supply reliable punishment. On this record the questioning had ended, the trial continued for roughly two weeks without another violation, and confinement was deferred. A command stopping the questioning, appropriate trial-management measures, and later contempt adjudication could address the completed defiance. Summary punishment could not undo the prejudice already caused. Stone concludes that the record establishes serious misconduct but does not establish why adjudicating guilt summarily was necessary to preserve the trial.

## C. RECEIVED PRECEDENT AND THE PROPOSED LIMIT

Terry, Oliver, McConnell and Wilson preserve the exceptional power to address direct contempt and the requirement to use no more power than the occasion warrants. Wilson involved refusal of immunized testimony that obstructed the ongoing proceeding. Stone distinguishes the need to obtain testimony from punishment for Watson's completed questioning. Sacher permits deferring punishment in a case where direct contempt and the requirements of trial control justify summary treatment; delayed imprisonment therefore does not independently establish a due-process violation. Stone relies on the entire record above, not on a categorical delay or repetition rule.

To the extent the received authorities permit summary conviction for this completed, directly observed defiance without the additional necessity showing, Stone candidly proposes narrowing them. His legal reason is the DueProcessClause's protection of notice and an effective opportunity to defend: an exception justified by preserving proceedings should not swallow those protections whenever a judge can later punish witnessed misconduct. His new boundary requires an actual trial-control reason for dispensing with ordinary adjudication. It preserves immediate action against violence, refusal, ongoing obstruction and comparably urgent threats, and preserves ordinary punishment for all proved contempt. Courts' preference for summary procedure supplies no concrete reliance comparable to a completed private transaction; completed contempt judgments are not reopened merely by this proposal.

## D. RETROACTIVITY GATE AND BOUNDED FALLBACK

The preceding proposed narrowing cannot automatically support a collateral writ. Under actual simulated Caspari, a preserved and fairly included Teague defense requires comparison with the law at finality. Stone would not pretend that a rule requiring this additional necessity finding was dictated if the earlier cases reasonably allowed the summary procedure used here. On that new-rule characterization, the change is procedural and supplies neither a substantive immunity from contempt nor a watershed accuracy rule; a properly available Teague bar would require rejection of relief on this ground.

The supplied packet does not establish preservation of that defense. Under actual simulated Schiro, Stone would not introduce an omitted nonjurisdictional defense on his own merely to defeat the claim; he makes no invented finding of waiver. If the actual review record establishes a preserved, fairly included Teague defense, his fallback is to reverse the grant of relief on this proposed new-rule ground, without approving every feature of summary contempt. If no such bar is properly available, he adheres to the approved affirmance and the ordinary-proceedings remedy above. This conditional answer identifies the controlling consequence of the missing procedural fact; it supplies no final Court outcome or unsupported custody deadline.
