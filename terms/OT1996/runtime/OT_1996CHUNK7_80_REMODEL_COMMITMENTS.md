## Shared status rules and the materially different record

**Contribution, navigation and the vessel group.** Painting and chipping can contribute to a vessel's function or mission. That contribution does not by itself establish seaman status, and traditional navigation duties are unnecessary under Wilander. Idle engines, docking and absence of permanent crew on the injury day do not establish withdrawal from navigation. The union declaration supplies concrete contrary evidence about continuing operational status, subject to its stated knowledge limits. No Associate makes a new conclusive navigation finding against Papai.

The five Associates who joined Chandris's full framework—O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg—would clarify that an identifiable fleet for the aggregate employment-connection inquiry requires common ownership or common operational control. A common hiring hall alone does not provide that relationship among otherwise unrelated employers' vessels. That clarification is a present interpretation, not an already settled entering-law holding. Stevens, Thomas and Breyer agree with the narrower rejection of this hall-wide aggregation: a common labor source does not itself establish service in one ship's company or a coordinated vessel group. They need not adopt a universal fleet-duration theory to reject the lower court's stated hall rationale. No categorical exclusion follows for hiring-hall seamen, one-day assignments, a replacement crew member or legitimate service aboard an employer's own vessel group.

**The relevant competing characterizations.** The specific injury task was known to be dockside painting, expected to last one day; Papai would not sail on the tug's departure and had no commitment to return next day. His earlier Pt. Barrow jobs—three or four by his account—were chipping and painting at the dock, and the identified immediately prior Sea Fox engagement was maintenance. These facts support an assignment distinct from the tug's ongoing sea service. They are not proof that all earlier Harbor Tug jobs were identical. Twelve earlier Harbor Tug deckhand occasions in 1989 are supported, despite the differing thirteen-day formulations; no precise reconstructed calendar or sea-voyage count is supplied.

The contrary evidence is now substantially more specific than an occupational designation. Secchitano identifies replacement of a regularly assigned Pt. Barrow deckhand, staffing the two-deckhand complement with Papai and Low, dispatch into the operational crew rather than a separate maintenance gang, and contractual separation from shoreside classifications, pay and seniority. Those are assertions about the actual assignment and relationship that must be evaluated favorably at summary judgment to the extent competent; her conclusion cannot determine federal legal status, and her knowledge limits remain. The competing inference is that a regular crew assignment's ordinary maintenance work retained its nature despite a short replacement engagement. Neither the absence of sailing that day nor the work's contribution alone resolves that inference.

Papai's general 1987–1989 account supplies actual moving-vessel work, including handling lines, and an estimated seventy percent deckhand work mostly on moving boats. It is not a proved percentage of Harbor Tug service in 1989 and cannot be used as though it accounts for every one of the twelve earlier engagements. Respondent's narrower assertion that all qualified-deckhand work since January 1, 1989 was for Harbor Tug remains an attributed merits assertion, with its later trial-page support not reproduced; it is not promoted into an uncontested summary-judgment fact. The replacement declaration and verified repeated Harbor Tug work supply the concrete narrower theory independently of that unsupported numerical conversion. The contrary forty-day Golden Gate shore job also remains in the account; the ALJ's different short-job characterization is attributed, not substituted for the deposition.

The contract's steering, lookout, winch, line, maintenance and underway engine-check provisions identify possible obligations and qualifications, not proof that Papai performed each on any particular job. They can illuminate whether the replacement dispatch placed him in a crew position; they cannot create sea service or fill missing payroll. The conflicting dispatch time accounts, Low's move to Sea King and Dawson's differing descriptions are not resolved by invention. Low's reassignment does not establish Papai's sea duties. Dawson's title alone supplies no decisive change-of-assignment fact.

**Two available status paths.** Under the conclusive-exclusion path, the specific actual assignment and known earlier vessel work remain transitory maintenance despite staffing through crew positions; the additional replacement evidence does not establish the kind of continuing vessel-work relation or seagoing responsibility required in nature. There is no automatic permanence condition or rule that a crew member loses status when painting. Under the factfinding path, the replacement-complement evidence plus repeated service for Harbor Tug permits an inference of actual crew integration or a substantial employment relationship; choosing the maintenance-only characterization would resolve a material employment inference against the nonmovant. No ultimate status finding follows. A genuine new essential assignment is evaluated on its own duties; a routine maintenance task within an unchanged crew assignment is not automatically a reassignment. The parties dispute which description applies here.

The approximate thirty-percent guide, with justified departures and changed-assignment qualifications, controls at its actual five-Justice scope. Neither group derives a reliable percentage from the supplied 1987–1989 estimate or unitemized twelve engagements. The three conclusive-status Associates rely on nature, not a fabricated duration shortfall; the two full-framework factfinding Associates identify a supported issue about both the employment relationship and the proper period without finding that a numerical guide has been met. The three actual-crew Associates do not silently acquire a full-framework join.

## The actual award: four separate legal questions

**What is established.** Benefits were paid voluntarily before the formal proceeding, but the present issue is the later adjudication. The June 2, 1992 hearing involved counsel, evidence, an additional medical deposition and briefs. Jurisdiction/status was one of five disputed questions. The ALJ expressly addressed Harbor Tug's contention that Papai might fall within the master/crew exclusion, recognized the parties' contrary civil positions, refused preclusive effect to the interlocutory district status orders, applied Wilander and a Robison-style connection formulation, and determined the crew exclusion did not apply. The order awarded some benefits, denied others and provided credit, interest, calculation and fee procedures. It was signed August 27 and filed and served August 31, 1992; neither side appealed, and its finality is accepted in the supplied litigation. This is neither an unadjudicated payment nor an imagined automatic award with no status decision.

Section 921(a) makes filing the effectiveness trigger and provides thirty-day finality absent its prescribed review proceeding. Section 921(b) supplies Board review; the sixty days in §921(c) follow a final Board order, not the initial ALJ decision. No October 27 ALJ-appeal deadline, actual last-day computation, categorical inability to appeal or claimant appellate standing ruling is invented. The conflicting February 2/February 5 benefit-date language is left intact because it does not decide this status or preclusion question. No new last-payment date or failed modification attempt is assumed.

**Statutory exclusivity.** Section 905(a) replaces other employer liability where the prescribed LHWCA employer liability applies, subject to its failure-to-secure exception and separate contractor qualification. The crew exclusion in §902(3)(G) defines the coverage boundary. Harbor Tug's independent submission is that a final formal compensation disposition conclusively activates §905(a) without needing ordinary issue-preclusion elements; Papai disputes that legal consequence. The statutory text does not say simply that receiving any payment makes a person a covered employee, and the controlling Gizoni holding forecloses such a rule for voluntary unadjudicated payments. Its reservation, however, leaves the final-order question open rather than deciding it against the employer. The issues include whether the final order conclusively establishes coverage for this purpose, what §921's prescribed review path makes binding outside compensation enforcement, and whether Congress's preservation of overlapping pursuit and credits changes that consequence. A formal-award rule must answer those questions rather than derive itself from the phrase mutually exclusive.

The strongest statutory-foreclosure argument is that final determination of LHWCA liability invokes an express exclusivity command and prescribed review scheme, with no failure to secure payment alleged. Continued payments, a lien or a second recovery's credit would not undo a final statutory classification. The strongest contrary argument is that §905(a)'s liability trigger and §902(3)(G)'s exclusion do not expressly create a new conclusive-status category based simply on the form of payment, while Gizoni preserves alternate pursuit and the underlying schemes contemplate overlap. The answer cannot be assumed from ordinary issue preclusion or from the absence of an affirmative election. Settlements raise additional issues and are not decided merely because the employer includes them in its argument. None of the eight adopts an independent final-award statutory bar here; the three status-reversal Associates reserve it, and the five remanding Associates require its distinct legal consideration without assuming either result.

**Ordinary administrative issue preclusion.** Astoria supplies a contextual presumption favoring preclusion for final administrative adjudication, subject to congressional purpose, the agency's role, the issue actually and adequately litigated, and the relevant procedural opportunity. Its statutory-intent inquiry permits implied displacement and imposes no clear-statement demand. It rejected preclusion under the ADEA's particular scheme; that outcome does not establish a universal second judicial trial after every federal administrative award. Conversely, a general preference for repose does not remove the statutory and adjudicatory predicates. Astoria is independently verified primary law and an own-writing/actual-join anchor for Souter and the four other present Associates then participating.

Applied here, the employer has a substantial claim: the same parties litigated the crew exclusion as an express prerequisite to compensation, the ALJ decided it after an evidentiary hearing and briefs, the earlier district orders remained interlocutory, and the award became final. Papai cannot answer merely that compensation began voluntarily, that counsel changed positions or that the ALJ might have erred. Neither later clarification of law nor possible §922 modification automatically destroys finality. But issue identity and an adequate opportunity on the precise status issue require more than counting hearings: the actual connection standard used, necessity and scope of the determination, contrary litigation incentives, claimed limitations on review and any applicable statutory displacement must be addressed. The ALJ cited both permanent-assignment and substantial-work alternatives; no simple finding that it applied a permanent-only rule is adopted. Nor is a material legal difference under Chandris conclusively disproved merely because Wilander was cited. The preserved objections require a source-specific comparison, not a generic assumption that every compensation hearing is the same as a Jones Act trial.

The recovered order eliminates the old missing-order reason for declining analysis. The remanding Associates do not find its predicates unknowable or demand the whole hearing transcript as a condition of decision. They reject the Ninth Circuit's categorical extension of Gizoni's voluntary-payment reasoning to a formal status adjudication, and return the actually disputed predicates and statutory question for their proper first application below. The record already establishes real adjudication and finality; an alleged defect in opportunity or issue identity requires its actual legal or evidentiary basis. A new first-instance conclusive preclusion holding would require that completed comparison and a properly supported individual judicial position; this model does not supply one by inference from the Justices' status opinions. Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer are not attributed personal joins in Astoria or Gizoni that they did not cast.

**Election, inconsistent positions and credits.** Harbor Tug disclaims an affirmative election-of-remedies defense. Initial alternative pursuit, judicial estoppel, ordinary issue preclusion and §905(a) exclusivity therefore cannot be collapsed into one election label. Years of voluntary payments and the dispute about added back symptoms prevent the lower court's forced-litigation rationale from becoming an established finding of employer manipulation. At the same time, a final adjudication does not by itself refute the claimed effects of incentives or establish that a favorable award recipient could obtain review of every disputed premise. Those objections must be analyzed on their actual grounds.

Section 903(e) expressly credits specified other compensation or Jones Act payments against LHWCA liability. Current Gizoni preserves its own overlapping-recovery credit rule. Neither provision's direction nor that holding automatically settles the collateral effect of a formal award. The employer's settlement-and-credit examples and nonsymmetry argument remain arguments, not findings about what happened here. No double recovery, refund, fee consequence or new lien amount is adjudicated.

Section 913(d) supplies tolling only after denial of tort recovery on its specified covered-employee, employer and secured-compensation grounds. It is not protection following every failed lawsuit. Section 922 permits specified factual-mistake or changed-condition modification within the last-payment or rejection periods, preserves its limits on prior payments and excludes settlement modification. It does not establish an expired one-year period measured from the award's signature. No actual stay request, withdrawal right, promised sequencing protection or unused modification opportunity is invented. These are legal alternatives to test if material, not presumed conduct by Papai.
