### Issue 1: Affirmative creation or intensification of private danger

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional merits. Because no constitutional violation is established, he does not reach qualified immunity.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the named administrators personally contributed to a specific foreseeable danger through affirmative action or obstruction of protection, with the required knowledge, culpability and causation.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone preserves his approved no-liability position using Collins’s workplace-duty boundary and a proposed, expressly nonprecedential evaluation of the reserved affirmative-danger theory; Pinder supplies no controlling merits test. Closing the unit and delegating placement were affirmative policy acts, but the record does not show a concrete assurance to Uhlrig, induced abandonment of available protection, concealment, or a defendant-specific decision directing Waddell into her care. She was warned of general and specific risk, trained for violent patients, and had access to his file. The named officials used a clinical placement process rather than deliberately overriding a known immediate threat to her.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The supplied record does not establish a defendant’s affirmative contribution to a specific foreseeable danger, conscious disregard of a known serious risk with time and practical ability to respond, and resulting causation under Stone’s newly articulated proposed rule. Assurances, reliance and imminence are evidence where relevant, not universally mandatory elements. The approved affirmance is preserved; this clarified formulation is user-approved.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The death is grave, and the decision to close a specialized ward may have been negligent or poor policy. Due process does not make senior officials insurers of every later act by a patient. Stone would require an official affirmative deprivation creating the specific vulnerability, rather than imperfect regulation of a dangerous institution; Pinder reserved that merits rule. The warnings and delegated clinical process undermine the asserted contribution, concealment and conscious disregard; the record must connect each official’s own action to the injury rather than infer responsibility from supervisory status. Summary judgment was proper.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect an official who conceals a known imminent threat, affirmatively orders an employee into a uniquely dangerous encounter after promising safety, or personally overrides clinical safeguards with deliberate indifference.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Pinder adopted no assurance/reliance/imminence elements. The reconciled proposal below follows the Master’s affirmative contribution, specific foreseeable danger, conscious disregard and causation formulation; the former assurance/imminence chain is not retained as universally necessary. His approved affirmance must also leave independently pleaded federal rights and genuinely different compulsory or intentionally harmful conduct outside Collins’s no-general-duty holding.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved result; refined merits analysis User-approved Stone position.** Stone would affirm no liability on the actual defendant-specific record. His Master Jurisprudence proposes liability outside custody when affirmative authority creates or materially increases a specific foreseeable danger, or knowingly blocks available protection, with conscious disregard of a known serious risk despite time and practical opportunity. Assurances and reliance can show an official contribution; they are not universal additional elements. Here the warnings, access to the file, delegated clinical process and absence of a demonstrated personal override do not establish that affirmative culpable contribution and causal connection. **Question 2 — new answer, User-approved Stone position.** With time for considered action, conscious disregard of a known serious risk can supply the culpability for the proposed affirmative-danger claim once personal contribution and causation are proved. Emergency constraints bear on knowledge and practical ability, not a blanket excuse.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required, subject to completion of the reimbursement and prospective-compliance steps required by the then-effective correction provision. Stone proposes applying the interpretive rule below in this case; its application leaves the judgment unchanged.

### Issue 1: Validity and interpretation of the salary-basis rule

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the salary-basis regulation's validity, the governing interpretive standard, and its application. He does not reach the separately asserted affirmative evidentiary-burden argument because it was not adequately preserved. Agency participation as amicus does not by itself establish either controlling force or disqualifying litigation invention.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** What judicial weight an agency's interpretation of its own regulation receives, whether the public-sector salary-basis regulation validly implements the executive exemption, and what “subject to reduction” means.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone expressly proposes replacing the controlling-weight component of simulated Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala, which he joined. That five-Justice holding made the administering agency's interpretation controlling unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation; it did not reserve the question. Stone instead would have courts determine regulatory meaning independently using ordinary interpretive tools. If genuine uncertainty remains, an authoritative, considered agency reading earns special persuasive weight when consistent or adequately explained, fairly noticed, within delegated authority, grounded in relevant expertise, and more than a restatement of the statute. Weight must follow those reasons rather than authorship alone. An agency cannot enlarge the statute or regulation through interpretation or impose liability without the notice governing law requires.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The FLSA permits the Secretary to define the executive exemption through a salary-basis requirement applicable to public employees. Courts interpret that regulation independently, with the bounded persuasive weight described above. “Subject to reduction” reaches an actual practice of improper deductions or a clear, particularized policy creating a significant practical likelihood of deductions inconsistent with salaried status; a merely theoretical disciplinary power is insufficient.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 213(a)(1)'s delegation to define and delimit the exemption supports the regulation: fixed salary reasonably distinguishes exempt executive compensation from pay varying with the quantity or quality of work. The words “subject to” can describe practical susceptibility as well as formal legal exposure. Treating every broadly written disciplinary code as disqualifying would disregard actual compensation arrangements; requiring only completed deductions would disregard a communicated policy that already changes those arrangements. The Secretary's middle reading fits the regulatory text, structure, and salary concept. Stone accepts it for those reasons under independent review, rather than merely because it is a permissible agency reading.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The proposed interpretive rule does not displace expressly assigned policy choices within a lawful delegation, authorize courts to substitute their policy preference, or determine the validity of every agency litigation interpretation. Fair notice, explanation, consistency, authority, and text retain their distinct work.

### Issue 2: Application, correction, and duties

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved application and duties questions on the supplied record.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the manual and isolated deduction defeat salary status, whether correction is available, and whether the officers perform exempt duties.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The general disciplinary manual did not clearly and particularly communicate that these officers' salaries would vary with ordinary work quality or quantity, and the record showed no recurring improper-deduction practice. The isolated improper deduction is curable through reimbursement and the prospective commitment required by the then-effective correction mechanism. The officers' supervisory responsibilities satisfy the duties component on the supplied undisputed findings.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The petitioners satisfy the salary-basis requirement under the practical-exposure construction; the isolated deduction is subject to the regulation's correction mechanism, and their duties satisfy the executive exemption. The judgment is affirmed subject to completing the required correction steps.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A manual covering a broad range of employees and disciplinary possibilities does not itself establish the particularized likelihood that these petitioners' fixed salaries will vary improperly. The correction provision governs the isolated error without excusing a recurring practice or a clear contrary pay policy. The duties findings supply the independent basis for treating these officers as executives; the interpretive rule does not eliminate that separate requirement.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The proposed result does not protect a recurring deduction practice or a clear policy specifically threatening improper reductions, and it does not waive reimbursement or prospective-compliance conditions.

## C. PRECEDENT, LEGAL REASON FOR CHANGE, AND RELIANCE

Stone acknowledges the precise rule he would change, including his own prior join. Thomas Jefferson University already polices plain text, inconsistency, explanation, and the actual agency ground. His objection concerns the remaining step: treating the agency's selection among permissible meanings as legally controlling because it administers and wrote the regulation. He regards that step as inconsistent with the distinction between delegated authority to prescribe a rule and the court's responsibility to determine the rule's legal meaning. The FLSA's power to define and delimit an exemption supplies rulemaking authority; it does not itself make a later agency interpretation conclusive on the reviewing court. Expertise can make a reading persuasive, and a lawful delegation can assign policy discretion, without regulatory ambiguity alone transferring interpretive responsibility. This is Stone's legal reason for candid change under Master Jurisprudence Section 16, not a finding that agency expertise has failed or that the Auer record proves systemic abuse.

The proposed overruling is confined to mandatory own-regulation interpretive weight in Thomas Jefferson University and the corresponding general rule of Seminole Rock and its applications. Stone preserves lawful delegated policy discretion, the regulation's operative text, ordinary reasoned-decisionmaking requirements, review of the agency's actual ground, and the earlier decisions' independent substantive holdings. Statute-specific allocations of administrative authority require their own analysis. He does not reopen Thomas Jefferson University's completed reimbursement judgment or decide that its actual regulatory construction was substantively wrong.

Concrete reliance on settled transactions, compensation arrangements, and fairly communicated legal obligations deserves weight; administrative preference for controlling deference is not itself such reliance. The supplied record identifies no concrete reliance requiring a different disposition here, where independent review accepts the Secretary's construction and the existing correction provision. Stone makes no finding that reliance is absent across other programs. Any distinct retrospective liability or settled-transaction claim must receive the applicable notice, finality, and remedial analysis in its own vehicle. No new transition machinery, general reopening authority, or Court majority is inferred.

### Issue 1: Retroactive cancellation of release credits

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone joins the threshold disposition and states the merits view below only in his concurrence.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether actual reincarceration after cancellation is an increase in punishment despite the credits' overcrowding purpose.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Morales and Weaver. The objective operation, not the legislature's original motive for creating credits, determines punishment. Florida awarded the credits, used them to calculate release, then retrospectively nullified them for Lynce's offense and returned him to prison. The increase is actual, not speculative. A credit affecting the date of lawful release is part of the effective measure of confinement for ex post facto purposes.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Florida violated the Ex Post Facto Clause by retrospectively canceling release credits already awarded and used to release Lynce when the cancellation lengthened his confinement. The administrative purpose of relieving overcrowding does not negate the punitive effect of reincarceration.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Simulated Morales invalidated the demonstrated loss of meaningful mandatory reconsideration despite the Board’s initial individualized prediction; proof that the missing hearing certainly would have produced parole was unnecessary. Here the State changed Lynce's release date by years and physically returned him to custody. The offense-based cancellation was expressly backward-looking in operation. The Constitution does not permit a State to avoid the result by calling the determinant administrative after it has used that determinant to calculate and end imprisonment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not freeze every prison-administration rule, protect credits never awarded or lawfully unavailable at the offense date, or bar prospective changes that do not increase punishment for completed conduct.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved reversal is supported by the direct deprivation, without adopting a new requirement that every prisoner prove with certainty that an omitted hearing would have yielded release. Morales expressly rejected that requirement. The remedy restores the lawful credit/release calculation on the established chronology rather than announcing immunity from every prospective change in prison administration.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the categorical question and rejects it. He does not reach the unpreserved alternative justification.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether officer safety permits a suspicionless order directed to every passenger, rather than an order based on any objective circumstance in the actual stop.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone proposes the narrow rule required by the Fourth Amendment's demand for reasoned seizure. - **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not authorize a categorical passenger-exit order in every traffic stop. An officer must identify an objective circumstance making the order reasonable for safety or lawful investigation. Because Maryland preserved and presented only the categorical rule, the suppression judgment stands.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Mimms rests partly on the driver's own offense and control of the vehicle. A passenger has not supplied that predicate. Officer safety is weighty, and Stone's rule gives officers broad practical latitude without requiring probable cause or a formalized suspicion label. It requires only a reason connected to the stop. That modest discipline prevents Whren's broad stop authority from becoming arbitrary control over millions of passengers. The actual movements and nervousness might support the order, but the State did not preserve that ground and the Court cannot rewrite the litigated case.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent an officer from ordering a passenger out on minimal objective safety grounds, controlling occupants during a lawful search, or arresting on probable cause. It does not decide a command to remain in the vehicle.

### Issue 1: Fixed zones and the counseling exception

### Issue 2: Floating zones and the post-refusal condition outside fixed zones

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both the unconditional floating zones and the linked automatic retreat requirement.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A mobile fifteen-foot zone around every person or vehicle is operationally uncertain, prevents ordinary conversational distance and leaflet transfer, and burdens peaceful speakers far beyond the access points. - **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Direct conduct rules can address obstruction, touching, threats, and harassment without forcing every speaker to calculate a moving fifteen-foot boundary.
