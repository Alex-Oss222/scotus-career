# 3. Lopez v. Monterey County

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by all participating Justices.
- **Historical Holding:** The historical Court reversed because the district court ordered elections under the County's own unprecleared countywide, at-large arrangement and thereby exceeded the limited role of a § 5 enforcement court. It left unresolved whether later California law had made the arrangement a state plan, whether laches applied, whether the County's coverage designation was constitutionally proper, and whether the consolidations were changes affecting voting.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Lopez v. Monterey County, 519 U.S. 9, 19-25 (1996).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=179).

# 7. In re Gaydos

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** IFP and requested relief denied; the Court allowed a December 23, 1996 fee-and-compliance cure and imposed prospective fee-and-Rule 33.1 conditions on both certiorari petitions and extraordinary-writ petitions in noncriminal matters. Stevens dissented; no Justice was recorded as nonparticipating.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Per curiam opinion with a Stevens dissent.
- **Historical Holding:** Repeated frivolous noncriminal filings justified ending IFP access for future noncriminal certiorari and extraordinary-writ petitions, preserving paid rule-compliant access. The historical condition covered both channels, not extraordinary writs alone.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; it is not simulated precedent.
* **Historical Sources:** [In re Gaydos, 519 U.S. 59](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-5831.ZPC.html).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=229).

# 11. Humane Society v. Babbitt

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL APPELLATE COMPARATOR

On February 14, 1995 the D.C. Circuit vacated the defendants’ summary judgment and directed jurisdictional dismissal. It questioned the evidence of aesthetic injury and future use but assumed sufficient animal-specific injury when deciding causation and redressability. The later certificate did not cause the earlier zoo transfer, and invalidation would not likely return Lota. It did not sustain the district court’s commercial-activity interpretation on the merits and preserved the transfer count’s waiver.

[Historical appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/46/93/591773/).

## B. HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT STATUS

No historical Supreme Court merits disposition or verified certiorari order for this particular appeal is established by the recovered sources. Babbitt v. Sweet Home is a different case. The timely petition, hold, October 7, 1996 grant and accelerated December consideration are authorized simulation setup, not historical Supreme Court events or completed simulated holdings.

# 12. Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg delivered the Opinion of the Court for a unanimous Court.
