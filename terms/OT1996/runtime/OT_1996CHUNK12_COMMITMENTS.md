## 131. Lindh v. Murphy — No. 96-6298

### Components and rival paths

All eight reach the temporal question. None reaches the underlying confrontation claim, determines chapter 154 State qualification, or treats Felker as settling the application of all Title I amendments.

**L1 — application-date construction:** The deliberate contrast between §107(c)'s express pending-case instruction for chapter 154 and the absence of that instruction for the related ordinary-habeas amendments indicates that the chapter 153 amendments at issue govern applications filed after enactment. This is an inference from the statute's organization, not a requirement that Congress use prescribed words. It resolves the case at Landgraf's statutory-direction stage. Reverse and remand for pre-AEDPA consideration of this 1992 application, without awarding a writ. Independently directed provisions retain their own rules.

**L2 — provision-specific present-adjudication construction:** Chapter 154's express reach concerns its distinct capital/State-qualification system and does not prescribe the opposite rule for chapter 153. Amended §2254(d) governs the federal court's present authority to grant relief, without changing the criminal prohibition, trial entitlement or punishment. Apply that standard to the still-pending application and affirm the temporal ruling at the reviewed scope. This does not adopt every related amendment without examining its operation.

The strongest objection to L1 is that different subjects explain the different clauses; its answer is the unusually proximate, integrated enactment and Congress's express selection of pending applications in one part. The strongest objection to L2 is that it makes that selection largely uninformative and mistakes a changed condition of relief for a mere housekeeping rule; its answer distinguishes the ongoing judicial remedy from Hughes Aircraft's creation of a previously unavailable private action for completed conduct. A label alone supplies neither answer.

## 132. Metro-North Commuter Railroad Co. v. Buckley — No. 96-320

### Separate gates, injuries and remedies

All eight reach both questions on the legal review of the Second Circuit's vacatur and remand of a Rule 50 judgment entered at the close of Buckley's case. Negligence is conceded, not injury or causation. The evidence must receive the nonmovant's lawful inferences; the Court may not convert testimony or corroborating worker complaints into findings or declare there was literally no corroboration.

**E1 — bounded impact:** Gottshall's impact branch does not encompass symptomless contact with asbestos whose feared harm is disease developing later. The common-law injury boundary cannot be satisfied by every particle contact, even an extensive negligent exposure. This interprets the reserved toxic-contact category; it does not move the threatened-impact branch's fear-for-self requirement into the actual-impact branch. The alternative immediate-risk branch likewise is not established merely by an increased chance of future disease. Reject this emotional-distress claim without prescribing a universal clinical-treatment or physical-manifestation condition.

**E2 — actual bodily contact:** Extensive bodily exposure supplies the actual-impact branch; the statute and Gottshall do not state that an impact must itself produce contemporaneous disease. Apply the remaining genuine-injury and causal requirements on the supported Rule 50 record. Evidence of exposure, medical risk and Buckley's described distress permits further proceedings; no liability finding follows. Strongest objection: microscopic contact becomes a formal gateway for unbounded fear litigation. Answer: actual serious exposure, genuine injury and causation remain independent, and this is not ordinary work stress or a bystander claim. E1's strongest objection is that it imports traumatic/immediate-injury restrictions into an independently phrased branch; E1 answers that Gottshall adopted a common-law legal category, not a dictionary contact test, and expressly reserved the disease category.

**M1 — no demanded stand-alone lump sum:** FELA's injury requirement and common-law guidance do not support the requested independent lump-sum monitoring recovery for this asymptomatic exposure. Conceded negligence and future risk do not themselves establish every compensable injury category. Reverse that portion of the appellate judgment. Leave employer-provided surveillance, reimbursement for actually incurred necessary costs, a suitably constituted monitoring program, later disease and materially different records undecided. Do not proclaim that every monitoring arrangement is forbidden.

**M2 — present incremental surveillance loss:** Medically necessary additional surveillance can be a present pecuniary consequence of negligent exposure, distinct from compensation for a feared disease. Permit further proceedings requiring proof of materially increased risk, reasonable medical necessity, incremental costs and causation; avoid speculative or duplicative recovery and account for actual comparable services if proved. No amount, schedule, fund or offset is established. Strongest objection: this creates an injury from preventive spending where statutory injury is missing. Answer: the obligation to incur reasonable additional care is itself the present loss, not the risk alone. M1's strongest objection is withholding reasonable preventive costs until illness develops; M1 answers that a useful precaution does not automatically create the demanded statutory damages category.

## 133. Richardson v. McKnight — No. 96-318

### Eligibility, legal appeal and alternative grounds

All eight accept legal collateral-order review of immunity eligibility, assume state action only for this appeal and leave the alleged excessive-restraint violation unadjudicated. Behrens, Elder and Johnson v. Fankell separate substantive immunity, legal appeal and factual sufficiency. Neither a threshold eligibility ruling nor failure of immunity determines liability, damages, corporate responsibility or a distinct good-faith defense.

**R1 — deny Harlow immunity in this operational setting:** Wyatt requires an independent historical and functional justification, not state-action attribution alone. The full-time employees of this commercially organized, operationally independent prison contractor do not establish the same immunity need as public officials merely because they perform delegated custody. Competition and ordinary private employment/insurance arrangements matter to the functional comparison; they are not guarantees that litigation has no cost or that every employee is indemnified. Affirm and continue the unresolved action under ordinary rules.

**R2 — recognize eligibility by custodial function:** These employees perform the State's coercive custody responsibilities, not a private litigant's attempt to collect a private debt. Harlow's protection against litigation-induced hesitation in discretionary public duties can apply to the individual officers regardless of the private employer. Reverse the categorical denial and remand for ordinary qualified-immunity analysis; do not grant ultimate immunity. Market pressure on the company need not protect the guard's individual decision at the moment of force.

The strongest challenge to R1 is that identical governmental force becomes differently protected by outsourcing. R1 answers that Wyatt distinguishes attribution from immunity and that the actual institutional setting is part of the functional need. The strongest challenge to R2 is that “governmental task” collapses that distinction and ignores the absence of a demonstrated common-law entitlement to this modern immunity from suit. R2 answers that coercive custodial discretion supplies an independent functional ground, unlike privately initiated process. Neither path rests on a fabricated universal historical practice of private jailers; the supplied sources support no such factual assertion.

## 134. Plyler v. Moore — Fourth Circuit No. 96-6884

### Components and applicable exception

All eight reach the four authorized legal questions and affirm the termination judgment against the presented statutory, Article III and due-process objections. This is not a finding that all South Carolina conditions satisfy federal law. The broader equal-protection objection described below is not independently included in the assigned questions; no separate general equal-protection holding is committed here.

**P1 — statutory source and scope:** Original §802 applies to existing federal prison-conditions prospective relief. The actual §3626(b)(2) motion invokes termination where relief was approved without the required need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings; it does not require waiting for (b)(1)'s two-year route or separately meeting Rufo's modification test. Subsection (b)(3) prevents termination upon written record-based findings that relief remains necessary to correct a **current OR ongoing** federal violation, extends no further than necessary, and is narrowly drawn and least intrusive. Existing substantive findings can suffice without ceremonial wording. A term exceeding federal minima can still be necessary to correct a proved federal violation; excess alone is not present compliance or a finding of dispensability.

**P2 — independent Article III adjudication:** Congress changes the legal authority for future enforcement and leaves the court to determine the statutory facts and preservation exception. It does not command a false finding, decide liability under unchanged law, or reverse an accrued damages adjudication. Robertson, Rufo and Wheeling Bridge support that distinction. The controlling Morgan Stanley/Plaut holdings are preserved at their bounded reinstatement scope, not expanded into unrestricted legislative power over judgments.

**P3 — decree rights:** A decree creates enforceable judicial obligations while lawful enforcement authority persists, but its additional negotiated terms are not themselves the independent federal violation that makes every term immune from §3626(b). Otherwise the statutory standard could never operate. This does not extinguish independent federal rights, decide a separate private agreement or state-law remedy, or permit leaving a proved violation without lawful relief.

**P4 — due process and remedy:** No immutable entitlement to unchanged future federal equitable enforcement arises from this modifiable decree. The supported motion, opposition and judicial determination supply a procedure for the legal objections actually presented; no unrecorded hearing or evidentiary finding is presumed. Reject the demand for restoration based solely on vested-decree status. An actual failure to apply (b)(3) could warrant correction, but the approved record identifies no preserved finding error requiring a new evidentiary remand. Affirmance therefore resolves the presented legal challenge rather than declaring all statutory predicates factually satisfied by this Court anew.

The competing path would treat the decree as a completed judicial entitlement whose negotiated protections Congress cannot withdraw, or would remand because actual federal violations might persist. Its strongest support is the judicial character of a consent judgment and Rufo's refusal to reduce promises automatically to constitutional minima. Its decisive difficulty here is that Congress expressly changes prospective authority, preserves independently necessary relief and retains judicial application; mere possibility of an unaddressed violation supplies no record-based remand ground. Conversely, P1–P4 cannot be defended as automatic enactment-based dissolution, ritual-word analysis, or constitutional approval of every PLRA section.

## 135. Princeton University Press v. Michigan Document Services — Sixth Circuit No. 94-1778

### Independent liability and remedial components

All eight reach the supplied merits questions. The operative en banc judgment affirmed infringement, vacated statutory damages and remanded for damages reconsideration and a separately entered, more precise injunction. The $5,000-per-work award is not an intact appellate award for this Court to affirm.

**C1 — integrated fair-use application:** Affirm infringement on the six excerpts. The commercial sale of verbatim copies performs the original works' assigned-reading function, although teachers' selection and students' educational use genuinely favor the defense within factor one. Published scholarly/factual character favors access but does not eliminate protection of expression. The substantial 17-to-95-page excerpts, 5%–30% of the books, must be assessed in relation to that same reading function, not by a numerical ceiling. On the supplied undisputed record, the established permissions market supplies actual substitutive licensing harm: the product supplies the very excerpts otherwise licensed for coursepacks. It is not merely a publisher's assertion of a fee for criticism. These considerations together defeat this defense without making transformation indispensable, profit dispositive or classroom guidelines binding maximums.

**C2 — damages distinct from copying:** Affirm the vacatur and damages remand. Deliberate copying is not itself knowledge or reckless disregard of infringement. The publishers did not contest Smith's actual good faith; his legally arguable fair-use position on this record does not support the enhanced willfulness finding merely because it loses. Ordinary statutory damages remain for lawful determination, including any properly supported innocent-infringer question under its distinct terms. This conclusion is not a universal rule that sincere belief defeats objective recklessness, and it fixes no award.

**C3 — precise prospective restraint:** Affirm the direction for a separately entered, sufficiently specific injunction tied to infringement. An injunction cannot prohibit lawful future fair use by implication; whether a particular future use is fair retains §107's actual inquiry. That does not categorically forbid a properly defined injunction from protecting later copyrighted works, or immunize later coursepacks. No historical publisher cross-petition, exact replacement wording or amount is invented.

The strongest alternative to C1 treats the noncommercial educational end use and factual works as decisive, or requires further factfinding before assessing market harm. Its force is §107's express teaching example and Campbell's rejection of commercial presumptions. Its difficulty is that the copying sells substitute assigned excerpts in a demonstrated licensing market; a teacher's selection does not supply the criticism-specific justification Campbell recognized. C1's strongest objection is circular licensing harm. Its answer requires the established substitutive market and the actual copying function, rather than any hypothetical licensing fee or assumption that all six books would otherwise be purchased. A future materially different classroom practice remains open.

The strongest alternative to C2 treats a deliberate commercial decision to copy despite known permissions practice as reckless. Knowledge of a publisher's demand, however, is not necessarily knowledge that a disputed statutory defense is untenable. The genuine legal uncertainty and uncontested good faith defeat that enhanced finding on this record. The alternative broader injunction would simplify enforcement, but cannot remove the statute's lawful-use boundary; specificity must identify prohibited conduct rather than suppress all copying.

## 136. Kolster v. INS — First Circuit No. 96-1194

### Threshold, temporal operation and real legal-review issue

All eight accept review of the appellate jurisdictional judgment under the approved grant, without treating the ordinary petition as a habeas filing. They distinguish the agency's legal refusal to consider eligibility from a discretionary weighing of hardship. They neither resolve the seven-year domicile issue nor treat the sentencing recommendation as binding.

**K1 — transition:** Apply the saved former proceeding, the relevant §309 exceptions and the limited §242(g) construction supplied by Reyes-Hernandez. The January 30, 1996 final administrative order does not meet §309(c)(4)'s later-order trigger; no qualifying election is established. Section 306(c)(2) protects an actual §106 judicial judgment/order applying §106 as amended by AEDPA §440 only against §306(c)(1)'s asserted invalidation or required reconsideration. It supplies no jurisdiction and bars no ordinary correctness review. The October 11 correction left paragraph (2) unchanged. No transition rule alone saves this petition from AEDPA.

**K2 — ordinary review withdrawn:** Apply §440(a) to this pending qualifying petition. Under Landgraf for the five Associates using its general framework, no established completed act acquires the asserted protected new consequence: Kolster surrendered no asserted colorable defense, documentary proof independently established deportability, and even his preferred domicile computation left him three years short of eligibility at his 1992 plea. Those facts support this result without establishing a universal actual-reliance requirement or proving subjective motives. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their relevant-activity ground: current judicial review is the governed activity. All leave §440(d)'s distinct eligibility operation open.

**K3B — narrower channel judgment, collateral question reserved:** Section 440(a) withdraws this particular ordinary appellate proceeding; no due-process entitlement to that particular mode of review is shown. ## 137. Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — No. 96-270

### Components and competing lawful paths

**A1 — Reach and sequencing.** Rejecting the proposed certification is logically antecedent to the standing objections produced by including putative class members within this proposed settlement. That is a refusal to authorize representative adjudication, not an assumed exercise of Article III power to resolve asbestos liability or release absent persons' claims. Steel Co. forbids assumed jurisdiction for contested statutory merits; its nonmerits-sequencing reservation permits this limited order of decision. This commitment neither establishes each named person's standing nor permits a settlement to be approved without the necessary jurisdiction. Individual injury and notice objections unnecessary to rejection remain undecided.

**A2 — Rule 23 and settlement.** Settlement eliminates the need to assess manageability at a trial that will not occur. It does not replace Rule 23(a), predominance or the actual governing-law inquiry with an overall assessment of a bargain's desirability. Castano requires a demonstrated basis for representative treatment; Matsushita and Richards separately preserve genuine adequate representation. Those cases do not make every Rule 23 defect constitutional.

**A3 — Adequacy.** Presently injured people seeking current payments and exposure-only people needing secure future protection have materially different negotiating interests under this capped, nonindexed arrangement. Nine undifferentiated representatives and common counsel do not supply the separate advocacy these interests require. A favorable aggregate bargain, notice and an opportunity to exclude oneself cannot alone cure this structural defect. The defect is sufficient to leave certification, settlement approval and its injunction vacated. This does not decide that every injured/exposure-only combination is inherently impossible, order particular subclasses or approve a replacement proposal.

**A4 — Predominance.** Common exposure to asbestos and the offer of one settlement do not establish that common legal questions predominate over materially different governing state law, exposure, injuries and claims in this proposed class. The record supplies no demonstrated lawful grouping that performs the work Castano requires. A settlement can remove proof-management obstacles; it cannot manufacture a common underlying legal entitlement. A4 is an additional ground only for Justices expressly joining it below.

**Adversarial comparison.** The strongest path to reversal/remand is that the lower court used litigation manageability or demanded identical claims despite a settlement's real common benefits. A2 rejects those errors without validating this proposal. The strongest objection to affirmance is excessive appellate substitution for the district court's evaluation of an innovative bargain. A3 answers it through the supported conflict in who bargains for future protection, rather than a different valuation of the settlement. A narrower adequacy-only affirmance avoids unnecessary predominance generalization; a broader ban on settlement-only or nationwide tort classes has no support here.

## 138. City of Boerne v. Flores — No. 95-2074

### Components and competing lawful paths

**B1 — Reach.** The accepted §1292(b) appeal properly presents Congress's authority to impose RFRA on States/localities. Prior Church of Scientology and Swanner applied the statute while expressly reserving constitutionality. Neither their application nor Agostini resolves §5. The permit's boundaries, actual substantial burden and asserted justification have not been adjudicated and are not decided here.

**B2 — Enforcement-power path.** Congress may use preventive as well as remedial means to secure the Fourteenth Amendment's guarantees, including measures reaching some constitutionally permissible conduct. The limit is that the means must enforce the constitutional right rather than substitute a new substantive standard for the right itself. With Smith still governing genuinely neutral, generally applicable laws, RFRA's universal person-specific compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means command reaches State/local burdens without a tether sufficient to identify it as prevention or remedy for unconstitutional treatment. B2 would reverse the Fifth Circuit on State/local RFRA authority and remand accordingly. This is not a requirement of a prior judicial violation for every statutory protection, nor a holding that Congress lacks predictive or preventive power.

**B3 — Constitutional-baseline path.** O'Connor's Smith concurrence applied compelling-interest review while agreeing with the judgment; Souter's public Lukumi writing called for reconsideration in a case that required it. If Smith's underlying constitutional rule is unsound, an invalidity judgment built upon its restrictive baseline may decide the wrong antecedent issue. This path calls for focused further briefing/reargument on Smith, its treatment of neutral burdens, its relation to Sherbert and Yoder, and the resulting §5 inquiry before a final merits judgment. It does not presume an unbriefed overruling, uphold RFRA now, or grant the permit.

**Adversarial comparison.** The strongest objection to B2 is the breadth of Morgan and South Carolina v. Katzenbach: constitutional enforcement is not confined to judicially identified instances, and difficult-to-prove discrimination can justify prophylaxis. The answer must preserve that discretion while distinguishing enforcement of an existing right from a general replacement standard; no invented legislative inventory or absence of discrimination is used. The strongest objection to B3 is that Smith is binding and the granted question concerns §5, so the Court can answer it now. B3's proponents regard the constitutional baseline as sufficiently consequential to require a properly framed reconsideration rather than implicit endorsement. A third path sustaining RFRA merely because Congress can expand rights beyond constitutional minima is rejected; broader statutory protection needs a valid source of federal authority.

**Relevant primary sources:** [Smith, including O'Connor's concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/494/872); [Morgan](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/384/641), which applies broad appropriate-means review and does not require case-by-case antecedent judicial invalidation; [Gregory majority](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/90-50.ZO.html) and [White/Stevens concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/90-50.ZC.html), whose federalism/interpretation dispute does not itself decide an expressly stated §5 enactment. The actual earlier public Lukumi writing is in [OT1992 chunk 8](../../OT1992/output/OT_1992CHUNK8.md). The original RFRA source is linked in preflight B; the statutory qualifications there are retained, not replaced by a general immunity.

## 139. Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc. — No. 95-1184

### Components and competing lawful paths

**G1 — Reach and characterization.** The challenged item is compelled financing of generic commodity advertising in comprehensive collective marketing orders. Compelled funding can burden expression without attribution, ideological content or suppression of a producer's own advertising. The absence of those additional burdens informs the analysis; it is not a categorical answer. Separate statutory challenges to the orders remain separate.

**G2 — Integrated-program ground for reversal.** On the supported scheme, generic promotion is germane to an otherwise lawful, substantially collective marketing arrangement governing product characteristics and common marketing decisions. A producer may be charged its share of that common economic activity without choosing each message, where its own speech remains free and no personal endorsement is demanded. This ground would reverse the First Amendment invalidation and remand the remaining statutory matters. It does not immunize every agricultural assessment, every commercial message or a stand-alone advertising association imposed on an otherwise independent market.

**G3 — Compelled-subsidy ground against the assessment.** Mere benefit, majority approval or extensive regulation does not establish the justification necessary to compel dissenters to finance an expressive campaign. Abood/Keller/Lehnert distinguish expenditures made necessary by a legally imposed representative function from promoting interests that beneficiaries could pursue voluntarily. On this record the generic advertising charge is not shown to be a necessary incident of a duty to perform an otherwise unavoidable representative service. The asserted common economic benefit does not suffice. G3 supports affirming the judgment against the advertising assessments, without treating every nonexpressive order requirement as invalid.

**G4 — Commercial-speech alternative.** If review proceeds under commercial-speech advancement and fit rather than G2/G3, the government must justify the actual compelled-support burden. The lower judgment addresses both advancement and fit. Neither a mandatory empirical-study rule nor least-restrictive-means review may be imposed. These commitments do not turn the lower court's conclusions into new Supreme Court findings about effectiveness; no Justice below relies on invented advertisements, brand dilution or a new evidentiary failure. G4 is reserved unless a row specifies an operative ground.

**Adversarial comparison.** G2's strongest objection is that one cannot define the government's project to include advertising and thereby make compelled speech self-justifying. Its proponents therefore depend on the already comprehensive economic integration and limit their conclusion to germane common marketing. G3's strongest objection is that it improperly separates one shared marketing expense from collective obligations which dissenters may lawfully be required to finance. Its proponents respond that regulation does not eliminate the independent First Amendment question and that the exclusive-representation cases require more than shared commercial benefit. Hurley concerns control of an actual private expressive product; Rosenberger expressly left compulsory fees open. Neither resolves this funding dispute. Turner II retained independent speech scrutiny, not a general economic-regulation exception.

**Direct primary anchor:** [Lehnert, 500 U.S. 507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/500/507). Stevens's separate opinion permitted broader germane public-relations expenditure. Scalia's separate opinion, joined by O'Connor and Souter and by Kennedy except its strike-preparation section, confined compulsory fees to the legally imposed representative function; Kennedy's separate writing resisted an unduly rigid treatment of reasonably incurred costs. These are different rules, not interchangeable votes for either result here. Current Gibson, 44 Liquormart and Turner II provide the later individual commitments used below.

## Decisional components and common limits

### A. Reachability and personal stake

All eight reach the appeal. The decree grants injunctive districting relief after a required three-judge proceeding. The supported resident-assignment relationship is sufficient under the actual Miller threshold holding and Thomas's unanimous Associate opinion in Vera. Lawyer contests the decree that governs his own district and the disposition of his own claims; he is not appealing merely as a stranger dissatisfied with a settlement. The continuing decree and requested relief supply a live controversy. Neither agreement that the original map should change nor receipt of some desired relief settles the lawfulness of the judicial means or disposes of a properly retained claim.

The rejection of a substantive racial-design theory does not itself eliminate Article III standing or make the controversy wholly insubstantial. Miller, Vera, Bell v. Hood and the limited Goosby rule distinguish those questions. Steel Co. requires attention to actual constitutional power, not conversion of every merits defect into a jurisdictional one. No jurisdictional dismissal is supported here.

### B. Consent, state choice and the nonconsenter

All eight accept these limited propositions from Firefighters, 478 U.S. 501, 525–531: a prior adjudication of liability is not invariably required for a consent decree; lawful consent can support obligations broader than relief obtainable after trial; withholding consent does not give one plaintiff a veto over the other parties' lawful compromise. The decree must nevertheless arise from a dispute within jurisdiction, remain within the pleadings' general scope, advance the objectives of the underlying law and remain lawful. Other parties cannot settle away a nonconsenter's properly raised valid claim or impose duties on him through their consent. The opportunity to object is relevant but does not independently supply authority to extinguish a claim.

The record supports state officials and both chambers acting through authorized litigation representatives. Their participation is a genuine state settlement choice, and Florida was not denied an opportunity merely because its political branches declined formal legislation. That is different from finding that Plan 386 became state law through a joint resolution or a superseding state judgment. Neither event is supplied. The March 19 order itself immediately substituted the six district descriptions. Growe's duty to respect an operative state judgment cannot be applied to a proposal as though the proposal were such a judgment.

No general holding that state officials can never settle districting litigation, or that every such settlement requires a legislative session, is supported. Conversely, litigation authority and a lawful map do not alone furnish the federal judicial basis for compulsory replacement of the operative plan.

### C. Why this approval cannot stand on its stated basis

The district court required a fairly contestable federal dispute, then identified that dispute by applying racial predominance and the related shape and composition inferences as the underlying constitutional wrong. The defendants made a settlement-only concession and expressly denied liability; the concurrence's contrary characterization is not a majority adjudication or an unconditional admission. The court's only accepted predicate thus uses the very substantive theory that current Shaw, Miller and Vera reject. It did not identify a distinct established wrong or explain an independently sufficient settlement basis under the governing rule.

Firefighters does not require a plaintiff to prove that the settled claim would win at trial. Nor does the fact that prospective terms exceed constitutional minima invalidate them. The defect here is more specific: the operative approval expressly depends on the legal sufficiency of a territorial-design theory foreclosed by the governing cases, and the supplied judgment does not identify a different preserved federal dispute supporting the judicial substitution. A reasonable compromise of contested facts, valuation or remedy is different from treating an unadopted legal rule as the rule already governing the court. The possibility of asking this Court to reconsider precedent is not an intervening holding on which the lower court could act.

The four Associates who joined the earlier design-only merits holdings support that analysis as their own substantive position. The four who dissented retain their objection to those holdings. They support vacatur of this approval at the narrower level that the court must identify and decide its legally available basis, distinguish the authorized compromise from a purported state enactment, and dispose of the nonconsenter's claims under the law it is bound to apply. They do not join an opinion announcing renewed personal endorsement of the substantive design-only boundary. Their present procedural vote does not adopt their proposed replacement rule as law or silently abandon it.

### D. The actual remaining allegations and the confined remand

An unconditional direction to dismiss every conceivable claim is not justified by the account of the record. Complaint paragraph 9 alleges that street-side boundaries prevented potential opponents from opposing the plan's author. Paragraphs 12–13 assert fragmented interests and deficient representation. Those allegations must be described accurately. Paragraph 9 is not simply erased as a racial-percentage allegation, but it also is not an established individual ballot-access, candidate-exclusion or partisan-dilution claim. The complaint identifies the plaintiffs as residents and registered voters; it does not identify a plaintiff as one of those excluded candidates. The final order addresses the racial-design theory and supplies no resolution of a separately preserved claim on that allegation.

The representation allegations, insofar as they infer incapacity to represent a shared constituency from its racial composition and geometry alone, are within the merits rule of Miller. Actual discriminatory treatment, if properly within this case, would have its own standards. No such treatment is found. The declarations describing effective representation cannot become a universal finding of equality either.

Polarization evidence is present. It concerns comparative electoral opportunity under the proposed remedy and alternatives and does not establish the Gingles prerequisites and totality inquiry for a Plan 330 claim. Bossier preserves independent §2 enforcement but neither creates such a claim here nor turns preclearance into an affirmative decree predicate. The 1992 election-administration mistakes were actual harms, including wrong elections and candidate-qualification problems. They are not merely hypothetical inconvenience; neither the source memorandum nor the final order, however, connects them to a preserved plaintiff's continuing federal claim that supports this decree.

The remand therefore concerns the existing case: identify whether any distinct claim was actually preserved within the complaint, operative pleadings and pretrial definition; determine its legal sufficiency, personal basis and relation to the relief, if it exists; and reconsider decree authority and the disposition of Lawyer's claims accordingly. The quoted pretrial insistence that only a Miller remedy remained bears on preservation but does not permit invention of the complete pretrial statement's contents. No new claim, amendment entitlement, proof failure, waiver or hearing is presumed. The remand neither orders a search for a different injury nor directs the parties to supply materials solely to fill an operator's archive gap.

If no legally sufficient, properly preserved basis supports this federal decree, the approval and dependent compulsory-redistricting relief must end, and the rejected design-only claims must receive their appropriate merits disposition. Other properly presented matters follow ordinary procedure. The court may not revive dismissed or abandoned claims merely because the rejected theory no longer works. Any separate state adoption must be established through its own lawful source; it is not inferred from the federal settlement.

### E. Plan 386, evidence and remedy

Vacating approval of the decree does not adjudge Plan 386 unconstitutional. The plan's shape, crossing of water or county boundaries, percentages, racial awareness and negotiation context do not themselves supply a recognized violation under the current rule. The accepted ordinary review rule applies to actual findings of districting purpose. The four earlier merits-majority Associates would reject a freestanding predominance-only invalidation; the four dissenters retain their different proposed scrutiny rule. No majority or individual commitment is inferred from historical Lawyer reasoning or from a historical Lawyer join.

The renewed decree-authority ground makes a conclusive appellate decision about Plan 386's predominance finding and the disputed limits on examining DOJ counsel unnecessary. The supplied declarations and hearing are not treated as nonexistent, and counsel's participation in settlement is not presumed to prove racial predominance. No Justice commits here to reversing a factual finding from shape alone, requiring examination of that attorney, finding a new injury, or certifying the plan under every constitutional and statutory provision.

**Formal provisional judgment:** All eight vote to vacate the March 19, 1996 approval and decree and remand for the confined current-law proceedings above. There is no instruction to find Plan 330 unconstitutional, no new map, no judicial racial target, no election cancellation, special election, deadline or special mandate date. Ordinary mandate procedure applies. The legal consequences of vacatur and any properly supported request concerning transition must be addressed under the actual operative instruments and applicable law, without inventing an election schedule or treating vacatur as an independent grant of continuing supervisory authority. No automatic stay, preservation order or restoration of an unverified later map is supplied. Fees and costs remain subject to their own lawful predicates; retained fee jurisdiction establishes no entitlement.

## 141. United States v. O'Hagan — No. 96-842

### Components and competing lawful paths

**O1 — Scope and actual deception.** Review covers mail fraud (counts 1–20), §10(b)/Rule 10b-5 (21–37) and §14(e)/Rule 14e-3 (38–54). The separate laundering disposition (55–57) is outside review. A fiduciary's secretly disloyal conversion of entrusted confidential information while feigning fidelity can deceive the source; unfairness, unequal market knowledge or occupational status alone cannot. Disclosure to the source may remove this theory's deception but is not a universal exemption from securities or other law.

**O2 — §10(b) nexus supporting reversal.** When the concealed conversion is accomplished through the fiduciary's securities trades, source deception is sufficiently connected to those purchases or sales even though the source is not the trading counterparty. This is primary deceptive conduct, not a general information-parity duty or automatic criminal aiding-and-abetting liability. Chiarella's duty requirement remains; its reserved misappropriation question is answered on this confined theory. The in-world Central Bank does not forbid it and supplies no criminal scienter shortcut.

**O3 — Competing §10(b) nexus ground supporting affirmance.** Deception of the employer/client concerning misuse of its property does not necessarily deceive a securities counterparty or concern the transaction in the sense required by §10(b). The fact that securities trading is the means of profiting from a general property fraud is insufficient on this reading. Source fraud can remain criminal under the mail-fraud statute even when the securities nexus fails. Carpenter's equally divided securities disposition settles neither path nor any current Justice's vote.

**O4 — Independent §14(e) authority.** Section 14(e) expressly authorizes reasonably designed preventive means. A rule need not make every covered trade independently fraudulent under §10(b). Rule 14e-3(a)'s specified tender-offer setting, source and knowledge restrictions reasonably prevent deception around confidential tender information; no separate fiduciary-duty element is necessary to its validity as applied to the alleged source-informed trading. Schreiber's footnote 11 recognizes latitude to regulate nondeceptive activity as a means of prevention while preserving the underlying statutory meaning of manipulation. MCI requires genuine delegation; this express preventive text supplies what a mere power to modify did not.

**O4 limits.** The offeror must have taken substantial steps to commence, or have commenced, a tender offer. The trader other than the offeror must possess material tender information, know or have reason to know it is nonpublic, and know or have reason to know that it came directly or indirectly from the offeror, issuer, or their officer, director, partner, employee or agent. Covered trades include the identified securities, convertible/exchangeable securities and related options or rights, including causing covered purchases or sales. Trading is not barred under this provision when the information and its source are publicly disclosed a reasonable time beforehand. This commitment neither prohibits all advantageous information trading nor adjudicates irrelevant regulatory exceptions. Criminal willfulness and any lack-of-knowledge limitation remain distinct from rule validity and are preserved for remand.

**O5 — Mail fraud and mandate.** Carpenter recognizes confidential business information as property and permits mail fraud for its deceptive appropriation when the required use of the mails furthers the scheme. It does not make any professional breach mail fraud automatically. The lower court's dependency on rejection of the securities theories cannot remain conclusive when O4, or O2, is sustained. Vacate the mail disposition and remand to examine the actual charged theory and preserved instructions, sufficiency, causation/use of mails and error-effect questions. Do not automatically reinstate convictions or decide that an independent property-fraud theory was necessarily submitted correctly. Preserve the unreviewed laundering disposition.

**Adversarial comparison.** O2's strongest objection is that the transaction may merely follow a completed source fraud, leaving §10(b) without a limiting nexus. Its proponents require deceptive appropriation through the securities use itself, not any later expenditure of stolen money. O3's strongest objection is that the enacted language does not restrict the victim to a buyer or seller and the deception here accomplishes its end in the trades. O4's strongest objection is that prevention could become an unlimited equality-of-information code; the answer must rest on the express delegation and the rule's concrete tender, source and knowledge limits, not market fairness alone. O5 separately rejects both automatic reinstatement and automatic invalidity of mail fraud whenever a securities count fails.

**Primary anchors:** [Chiarella, including Stevens's concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/445/222); [Carpenter](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/484/19); [Schreiber, including note 11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/1); [Eighth Circuit judgment](https://openjurist.org/92/f3d/612/united-states-v-ohagan), whose historical Central Bank premise is displaced in-world. For Breyer, [SEC v. MacDonald, 699 F.2d 47](https://openjurist.org/699/f2d/47) confirms his participation in statutory insider-liability and bounded disgorgement decisions without deciding source misappropriation; [Mayburg, 740 F.2d 100](https://openjurist.org/740/f2d/100/mayburg-v-secretary-of-health-and-human-services), his own opinion, distinguishes actual delegated authority from deference merely because an agency adopted a position. Neither is attributed to a Justice who did not participate.

## Defined components and bounded alternatives

### K1. Article III reach: injury, causation and redress

**Affirm-standing path.** These politically active voters seek particular campaign-financing information for their own electoral participation, rather than a declaration that the government must generally obey the law. The asserted denial is continuing: no source establishes that the sought information has already been disclosed. Public Citizen permits a concrete informational-access injury even when many other people could make the same request. Lujan requires a personal concrete interest and support appropriate to the stage, not an injury unique to one citizen. The supported connection to the voters' electoral activity is sufficient here; the precise transactions may be unknown because of the alleged nondisclosure. This conclusion neither makes filing an administrative complaint an injury nor presumes the ultimate statutory entitlement correct.

The FEC's legal interpretation is an actual ground of the political-committee dismissal. Correcting it can restore the statutory process for obtaining information still withheld. The Commission's retained authority to make lawful findings and enforcement choices does not make that remedial link categorically speculative. Bennett requires examination of the actual link; its announced agency-compliance fact is not imported here. Lujan's procedural-right rule permits review tied to a concrete interest without certainty of a changed ultimate outcome. Steel Co. is different because the requested reports there were already current and no remedy repaired the completed injury. Here useful information remains the object of relief.

**Jurisdictional-dismissal path.** The competing view treats the supported interest in understanding and opposing AIPAC's influence as the public's generalized interest in informed electoral oversight, insufficiently particular to these litigants. The injury is not established merely by alleging that a regulator should classify and regulate a third party differently. On this view, Public Citizen's denial of specifically requested records and access does not remove the requirement to establish these voters' own concrete use of the information the different FECA process may produce. Congressional permission to challenge dismissal does not supply that connection. This is a legal assessment of the supported personal stake, not a new finding about undisclosed records, absence of an affidavit or failure to meet an invented document requirement.

**Strongest objections and limits.** The strongest objection to affirming standing is that almost any voter could convert an interest in enforcing election law into an information claim. The answer is the particular legally protected information sought for actual electoral activity, not complaint status alone. The strongest objection to dismissing is that numerical breadth and nondisclosure-caused inability to identify each affected candidate do not make a real access injury abstract; Public Citizen and Lujan's preserved congressional power require a substantive distinction. The dismissal proponents maintain that the present connection remains an undifferentiated electoral interest, rather than declaring every widely shared informational injury nonjusticiable. No row uses Findley's past candidacy or an agency failure to challenge it as a substitute for Article III.

The strongest redressability objection is the sequence of future Commission action, third-party compliance and useful disclosure. An affirm-standing vote accepts likely redress through the actual statutory channel, not a guarantee that the Commission must prosecute or that any possible settlement without the desired information is unlawful. The separate corporate-prohibition nonenforcement ground cannot be silently reassigned as the actual basis of the political-committee dismissal.

### K2. Express review and available remedy

Section 437g(a)(8) permits an aggrieved administrative complainant to challenge a dismissal within sixty days. Its separate 120-day inaction trigger is not the route invoked here. A court may declare the dismissal contrary to law and may direct the Commission to conform within thirty days; failure to conform may permit the complainant's own action addressing the original violation. These permissions do not automatically command prosecution, a penalty, a settlement or wholesale disclosure.

This express channel allows correction of the legal premise actually used. Ordinary nonenforcement discretion does not insulate that premise from all judicial review. Conversely, statutory review does not erase lawful agency factfinding and discretion or authorize courts to substitute an unexercised discretionary rationale. Brown Group distinguishes enacted enforcement authority from the separate constitutional minimum. Bennett requires the claim to fit its actual statutory channel. Thomas's Legi-Tech opinion preserves actual authorized Commission decisionmaking and statutory prerequisites; it supplies neither an automatic enforcement remedy nor a generic bar to review.

The competing path sustaining a lawful discretionary dismissal would require a legally permissible ground actually supporting this dismissal. The separate close membership question for the §441b corporate prohibition is not, without more, that ground for the political-committee decision. The present commitment does not invalidate the separate corporate enforcement choice.

### K3. Major purpose and genuinely covered financing

**Corrected-construction path.** Section 431(4)(A) identifies a group receiving contributions exceeding $1,000 during a calendar year or making expenditures exceeding $1,000 during a calendar year. A group's payment to a candidate must be analyzed through the relevant definitions; the definition does not literally say that every group making any kind of contribution above $1,000 is automatically a political committee. Covered candidate-connected payments can constitute expenditures under §431(9), subject to each applicable exclusion. Threshold amount, calendar-year aggregation, candidate connection and exclusions remain independent predicates.

Buckley's major-purpose discussion addresses the danger of sweeping issue discussion and independent expression into election regulation. MCFL likewise concerns independent spending, while recognizing the distinct treatment of actual contributions. Those decisions do not establish an organization-wide exemption for otherwise covered candidate financing merely because lobbying occupies most of the organization's budget. The Commission cannot use that categorical premise to terminate the inquiry. Colorado Republican requires actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion where the asserted statutory contribution theory requires it; lobbying, shared policy views or political sympathy do not supply that connection.

**Strongest competing construction.** Political-committee status entails organizational and associational burdens beyond disclosure of one transaction. Buckley's language and MCFL's concern with the full regulatory structure can therefore be read to require a candidate-controlled or primarily electoral organization even when some transactions are candidate-connected. This is a serious constitutional-avoidance argument, not merely a policy preference. The corrected-construction path answers that actual candidate resources have the constitutionally material connection missing from independent issue speech, while preserving genuine as-applied objections to particular obligations and preserving exclusions. It does not uphold every organizational burden whenever $1,001 is alleged or require disclosure of every contributor regardless of the statute.

The extent of a judicial constitutional narrowing is for the court to determine. Deference to agency administration does not make the agency's reading of Buckley or MCFL conclusive. This commitment establishes no universal denial of Chevron deference to the FEC on genuinely delegated statutory questions.

### K4. Membership, other exclusions and remand

The Commission must apply the effective provisions separately before a final threshold or organizational determination. Section 431(9)(B)(iii) excludes qualifying communications to members, stockholders or executive/administrative personnel by an organization not primarily organized to influence federal nomination or election. It nonetheless requires reporting of costs directly attributable to express advocacy of a clearly identified candidate when those costs exceed $2,000 for an election, except a communication primarily devoted to subjects other than that express advocacy; the specified §434 reporting schedules govern. Exclusion from expenditure status therefore does not necessarily erase this distinct reporting proviso.

Section 441b(b)(2) separately excludes the specified corporate communications to stockholders, executive/administrative personnel and their families, labor communications to members and families, the specified nonpartisan registration/get-out-the-vote activity, and establishment, administration and solicitation of a separate segregated fund by the named types of organizations. Its ordinary-course bank-loan qualification remains distinct. Section 431(8)(B)(vi) and (9)(B)(v) separately cross-reference payments that would not constitute expenditures under §441b(b). The §431(9)(B)(iii) $2,000 proviso is not transplanted into those other exclusions.

No final AIPAC membership finding, actual coordination finding, amount finding after exclusions, comprehensive classification, compulsory donor disclosure or penalty is supplied. No later proposed membership rule is operative law. Correcting the major-purpose premise permits a remand under the actual statutory provisions; it does not decide that any original violation is proved.
