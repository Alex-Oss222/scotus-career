# 107. Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT COMPARATOR

The Seventh Circuit treated timely filing as part of EPCRA's incorporated requirements, found no language extinguishing citizen enforcement after belated compliance, distinguished Gwaltney's different statute and reversed the dismissal. It regarded notice as facilitating agency participation and voluntary compliance rather than a categorical liability amnesty. The lower opinion did not supply the independent standing analysis required by the expanded simulation questions. Its statutory judgment is the historical comparator, not governing simulated Supreme Court law.

This records the historical lower-court disposition only. It does not establish a completed simulated Supreme Court outcome or supply governing post-divergence Supreme Court law.

[Primary opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/90/1237/593842/)

## B. LATER HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR — OUTSIDE GOVERNING SIMULATION LAW

On March 4, 1998, the historical Supreme Court vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss in Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment, 523 U.S. 83, No. 96-643. The Court's controlling reasoning rejected hypothetical Article III jurisdiction and found the requested relief could not redress the past reporting injury. It did not decide whether EPCRA authorized citizen suits for wholly past violations. Separate opinions differed over jurisdictional sequencing and the statutory route.

[Historical Supreme Court opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/96-643.ZO.html). This subsequent historical result supplies no simulated holding, vote, or effective precedent for this packet.

# 115. Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 8-1.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer, and by Justice Scalia except as to footnote 4. Justice Stevens dissented.
- **Historical Holding:** When a Chapter 13 debtor retains collateral, § 506(a) values the secured claim by the cost of obtaining like property for the debtor's proposed use, adjusted for the collateral's age and condition, rather than by net foreclosure proceeds.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash, 520 U.S. 953 (1997).



## Historical reasoning references preserved from the source

- **Other Authority Used:** The historical Rash opinion is persuasive on the relationship between retained use and replacement value. BFP is distinguishable because it concerns completed foreclosure and reasonably equivalent value, not prospective retained use under § 506(a).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/520bv.pdf#page=1048).

# 116. Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 9-0; the Court held that the action should be dismissed under the pre-1986 government-possession bar.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Thomas delivered the Opinion of the Court for a unanimous Court.
- **Historical Holding:** The 1986 amendment does not apply to pre-enactment false claims because it eliminates a complete defense and permits a private action previously barred, thereby attaching new legal consequences to completed conduct.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Hughes Aircraft Co. v. United States ex rel. Schumer, 520 U.S. 939 (1997).



## Historical reasoning references preserved from the source

- **Other Authority Used:** Landgraf and Winfree support treating a newly available private enforcement action and eliminated complete bar as substantive for retroactivity. The historical Hughes opinion is persuasive on the statutory operation.

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/520bv.pdf#page=1034).
