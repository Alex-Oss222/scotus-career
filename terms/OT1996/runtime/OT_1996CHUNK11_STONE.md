## C. LEGAL REASONING

A prosecutor retains absolute immunity for legitimate charging decisions and prosecutorial advocacy, but not when personally swearing to factual assertions as a complaining witness. Immunity follows the function performed, not the official's title.

Stone would additionally recognize a narrow exception to absolute immunity for knowingly fabricated evidence or deliberately false material assertions causing a constitutional deprivation. Honest mistakes and disputed factual judgments do not satisfy this exception.

Under Stone's proposed **Contextual Fair-Notice and Reasonable-Conduct Test**, qualified immunity protects genuinely reasonable mistakes assessed according to the official's responsibilities, available information, and governing law. It does not protect knowing constitutional violations or objectively indefensible conduct. The official bears the burden of establishing the defense.

## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **First independent holding: this nonremedial layoff does not fall within Title VII's recognized affirmative-action permission.** The Board chose who would lose an existing position because of race without connecting the decision to a demonstrated remedial imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category. Preserving one department's racial composition does not itself establish that statutory connection. Weber and Johnson require the remedial connection they describe, and Stone would not read their accommodation as authority for any beneficial racial objective. An employer need not confess a proved violation, and a plan addressing manifest imbalance remains available, but that distinction does not assist a board expressly relying on diversity alone to choose who loses an existing job.

2. **Second independent holding: the layoff unnecessarily trammels the interests of the employee.** Assuming without deciding that a diversity purpose could count, discharging an incumbent from an existing job is central to the no-unnecessary-trammeling inquiry, and a tie between equally senior and qualified employees does not erase that burden. A race-neutral lottery was available to resolve the equal-seniority problem and the Board had used it before. Stone would reject a universal rule that every race-conscious employment plan involving burdens is invalid, but finds this burden unjustified on this record. Either holding independently supports affirmance.

3. **Weber and Johnson remain intact.** Nothing here overrules them, requires proof of a past violation by the employer, or forbids a remedial layoff arrangement supported by a manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated category.

4. **Separate part: statutory and constitutional standards differ.** Title VII is not the Equal Protection Clause, and this decision rests on the statute. It holds nothing about whether diversity can ever justify race consideration by a public institution and decides no constitutional question. A university's educational mission and an employer's workforce layoff present different questions. If the Court has by then recognized an educational-benefit interest for a public university, that recognition rests on the institution's academic function and does not transfer to a decision to discharge an incumbent. Simulated Adarand's actual holding provides no exemption for this local employment decision, and historical Adarand is not imported.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the Title VII liability judgment and the associated relief to the extent properly before the Court, on either independent statutory ground. Reject a constitutional holding that diversity can never justify consideration of race, and decline to decide unpresented equal-protection questions. This resolves the tension with Stone's documented diversity views through the distinction between constitutional permission and statutory restriction, rather than concealing or discarding those views.

**Question 1 — recognize the jury right for this damages action and these factual tasks.** Stone would treat this §1983 claim for compensation for an alleged unconstitutional deprivation as a legal action with a property-tort analogue, protected by the Seventh Amendment. He does not extend the ruling to every condemnation proceeding, a challenge to general zoning legislation, or every subsidiary legal issue in a damages action. **Question 2 — repeated denials can produce a taking, but repetition is evidence rather than the test.** Stone would uphold the verdict on the properly confined, supported theories submitted here. For complete deprivation, the relevant question is productive use of the parcel during the actual period, not merely whether this preferred project could proceed. The owners' evidence of mutually constraining requirements and the asserted practical exclusion of development supports the jury's conclusion; the City's legitimate environmental aims remain relevant but do not themselves establish the background-title exception required by simulated Lucas. No substantiated antecedent limitation is identified that would independently require the entire deprivation found here. A later sale to the State above the original purchase price is material evidence, but does not conclusively establish that a productive option existed during the earlier period or that its loss has already been compensated. Stone would not presume total deprivation from a permit rejection alone.

On the alternative substantial-advancement instruction, Stone applies received Agins and the unchallenged instruction's reasonable-relationship formulation to this particular final decision. He would not ask a jury whether a different land-use policy is preferable. The evidence permitted it to assess whether the reasons actually proffered bore the required relationship to this denial after the owners' responses to the City's conditions. This preserves legitimate environmental judgment while permitting adjudication of the concrete constitutional claim. Agins's distinct formulation has not been displaced by the supplied simulated authorities. Stone does not silently replace it with later historical law, and an affirmance under the submitted, supported grounds need not announce that every poorly fitted regulation appropriates property. His own property framework continues to distinguish physical appropriation, complete deprivation, partial-loss analysis and exactions; this disposition applies the binding formulation and its presentation limits without expanding it.

Stone expressly rejects the Ninth Circuit's suggestion that the final denial itself must satisfy Dolan's rough-proportionality burden. Simulated Dolan concerns demanded property conveyances, and its actual limits remain. The jury was not instructed on that enlarged standard. The evidence supporting the narrower reasonable-relationship inquiry and complete-deprivation ground allows affirmance without endorsing the appellate overextension. He would uphold a supported verdict, not independently resolve witness credibility.

**Question 3 — permit compensatory damages through the established §1983 municipal action.** A City decision satisfying Monell's institutional connection and the constitutional taking requirements can support a damages judgment; the owner is not confined to requesting another administrative decision or prospective invalidation after the injury has occurred. ### Issue 1: Nominal compensation for a significant potential future loss of wage-earning capacity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved statutory question. He does not decide whether this record proves the required future risk because the administrative law judge did not make the necessary finding under the governing standard.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone adopts a narrow statutory reconciliation. A nominal award is available when a permanent work injury causes no current wage loss but the claimant proves a significant and nonconjectural potential that the injury, interacting with reasonably foreseeable labor-market conditions, will reduce future wage-earning capacity. The employer seeking termination must first establish the change in conditions supporting termination of actual benefits. Once current earnings fairly represent current capacity, the claimant seeking nominal compensation carries persuasion on the significant future potential. The possibility may not be presumed as an administrative convenience.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The LHWCA permits nominal compensation when a work-related injury creates a significant, proved potential for future diminished wage-earning capacity despite no current loss, and the administrative law judge must decide whether that condition exists.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 8(c)(21) compensates lost earning capacity rather than physical impairment alone. Section 8(h), however, expressly requires attention to the future effect of disability. Reading the Act to require an outright denial whenever current wages equal or exceed preinjury wages would make § 22's one-year limitation prevent later relief even when the anticipated economic consequence materializes. A nominal award gives effect to both provisions without paying present compensation for a merely physical loss. Metropolitan's proof of much higher current market wages supports termination of actual benefits. It does not answer the separate future-risk question, and the Ninth Circuit could not make that factual finding for the administrative law judge.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no nominal award for every permanent impairment, does not assume future unemployment, and does not permit an appellate court to substitute its view of probability for supported administrative findings.

- **Stone's Legal Position:** Rufo requires a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable, followed by a modification suitably tailored to the change. Mere dissatisfaction, a cheaper alternative, or an expected shift in votes does not suffice. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a relevant risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. The principles governing voluntarily accepted consent obligations do not transform this adjudicated injunction into a negotiated bargain.

  The lower courts remained bound by Aguilar. Kiryas Joel did not overrule it; the simulation's Zobrest did not decide the establishment merits; and the bounded access, attribution, and publication-benefit decisions did not establish that this onsite Title I program had already become lawful. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to follow the directly applicable precedent rather than predict its reversal.

  That obligation does not prevent this Court, in a case presenting Aguilar's continued validity, from expressly changing its own rule for a sufficient legal reason. If the Court adopts the change proposed in Issue 2, the change exists when the Court directs prospective relief. Stone does not require the City to return in a second motion solely to invoke the decision this Court has just rendered.

- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A ruling expressly displacing the constitutional premise of this continuing onsite prohibition supplies a significant change in law for Rule 60(b)(5). Continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The proper response is tailored modification under the new rule, not affirmance on the ground that the lower courts lacked authority to anticipate the change.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The injunction governs future provision of services. Its identified constitutional basis is Aguilar's treatment of public remedial teachers working inside religious schools and the supervision associated with that arrangement. Stone would reject that categorical premise for the legal reasons stated in Issue 2. The City would therefore establish a change pertinent to the obligation it seeks to modify if the Court adopts his proposed rule.

  The expense of off-premises delivery and the practical difficulty of reaching eligible children reinforce the need for prompt, tailored consideration of prospective relief. They do not establish constitutional validity by themselves. Stone does not need to find an independently sufficient factual change in those expenses to reach the proposed disposition, and he makes no unsupported finding about the size or timing of a factual change.

  Nor does he find the lower courts culpable for following Aguilar. The proposed vacatur follows the Court's newly announced law, not a conclusion that the lower courts should previously have treated unresolved doctrinal tension as overruling.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Rule 60(b)(5) remains a standard for modifying continuing relief, not a general license to reopen completed judgments. If the Court declines the relevant doctrinal change, Stone's proposal alone supplies no changed law. Any modification must rest on a rule actually adopted by the Court or another independently established ground for relief.

### Issue 2: Whether to update Lemon's general method and retain Aguilar's categorical rule

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The continued validity of the injunction requires the Court to decide whether Aguilar's governing constitutional rule should remain authoritative. Stone addresses the defect in the general method insofar as it produces that rule, proposes the update to that general method, and specifies its application to the program before the Court. He does not treat a desire for a different test as a reason to decide unpresented disputes about vouchers, monuments, legislative prayer, or accommodations.

- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone would keep Lemon as the governing test and update how it operates: its independent purpose, primary-effect, and excessive-entanglement prongs are restructured into the inquiry below. The constitutional concern is governmental exercise or sponsorship of religious authority and public financing of religious functions, while preserving private religious liberty and independently secular services. Purpose, effects, and institutional relationships remain evidence of the governmental act and its operation. They no longer operate as freestanding prongs or as an alternative test for ambiguous cases.

  The updated Lemon test has three routes within one inquiry. A specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct. An established permissible relationship defeats the establishment claim on that ground. A legally sufficient but unresolved allegation of prohibited conduct receives the attribution-and-function inquiry under ordinary procedure. Clear cases are applications of the updated test, not exemptions from review.

  In the residual inquiry, the court identifies the challenged governmental act and determines what function that act performs. Attribution may concern an official message, an organized event, the use of supervisory power, or an expenditure. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; it is unnecessary to make all of the recipient's religious conduct government speech. Official authorization of private access, religious identity, and physical location are relevant in context but do not individually determine the answer.

  Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, use public authority to solicit religious adherence, exercise theological governance, or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Government may purchase independently identifiable secular work in religious settings, with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of access to private exercise restricted by governmental custody or comparable control remains subject to a defined accommodation inquiry rather than an automatic financing prohibition.

  Actual conversion, religious success, or a completed penalty is unnecessary when the prohibited governmental act is established. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, the provider's religious motivation does not make its secular service devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular work is not an establishment merely because the contacts are repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain prohibited institutional relationships.

- **Stone's Legal Reason for Change:** Aguilar converts measures intended to preserve secular instruction into a categorical reason to exclude that instruction from religious premises. The resulting rule makes location and ordinary supervision substitutes for identifying religious sponsorship, devotional financing, or theological control. Stone concludes that this is an internal defect in the doctrine's legal boundary: an independently secular service can be condemned because it is supervised to remain secular, without a finding that the supervision governs religious affairs.

  That conclusion does not depend on proving that no religious school or public employee could ever divert a service. Nor does Stone claim that new empirical evidence has conclusively disproved every concern expressed in Aguilar. He rejects the legal sufficiency of the categorical inference. The proper inquiry retains protection against actual or necessarily designed devotional activity and improper institutional control, while distinguishing them from the administration of separately controlled secular work.

  The correction traces to the same constitutional concerns Lemon sought to implement: sponsorship, financial support of religious functions, and intrusion of civil and religious authority into each other's proper responsibilities. Stone changes the method because its categorical treatment of this relationship defeats that distinction. The costs and access difficulties make the defect consequential, but inconvenience alone is not the reason for overruling.

- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Religious-school location and ordinary secular supervision do not, without more, establish unconstitutional sponsorship, financing, or institutional control. Aguilar is overruled as the constitutional basis for categorically excluding the described public Title I remedial instruction from religious-school premises. Ball's corresponding onsite-instruction rationale is displaced to the extent it treats location, presumed inculcation, or incidental institutional benefit as sufficient without the prohibited governmental function. The Court does not reopen Ball's completed judgment or approve every program considered there.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The supplied record identifies children selected by educational need, public teachers using public materials, and public staffing and supervision of remedial services. It identifies no finding of actual indoctrination, transfer of public funds to the schools, or religious control of the public teachers. The governmental act to be examined is therefore the provision and supervision of the remedial instruction, not the surrounding institution's religious mission as a whole.

  Those affirmative features permit the service to be analyzed as independently secular. Absence of an adverse finding is not proof that every conceivable defect has been investigated and disproved. It is, however, insufficient to sustain a categorical religious-location ban after the Court rejects the inference on which that ban rested. Public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize devotional conduct if supported evidence establishes it; equally, religious premises do not establish such conduct without the relevant legal connection.

  Monitoring public teachers' performance and the content of the purchased service does not, on the supplied showing, establish governmental direction of religious doctrine or leadership. The court must distinguish supervision of the public service from regulation of the school's private religious affairs. It may examine any supported contention that the two are inseparable, but may not presume inseparability merely because the service occurs inside the school.

  The earlier supplement's universal demand for separation in governance, personnel, content, and accounts is refined here. The updated test requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for the funded work; it does not invariably require separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared premises or organizational structures cannot excuse devotional financing, but neither automatically defeats a genuinely severable public service. This is an express revision of Stone's stated position, not a claim that the earlier language already meant the same thing.

  The proposed disposition removes the categorical basis of the injunction and permits the district court to implement the new boundary. It does not authorize an inquiry into hypothetical violations unsupported by the record or require a guarantee that future misconduct is impossible. Any further fact development must address a specified issue material under the updated test and proceed through ordinary procedure.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The case does not approve government purchase of devotional instruction. It does not decide a tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme. Stone's broader funded-use position, under which parental selection does not alone legitimate public purchase of religious formation, is part of his broader position but is not a merits holding on an absent program.

  Witters and Mueller retain their actual pre-divergence holdings unless expressly reconsidered in a case presenting their scope or validity. Stone acknowledges that his general funded-use position may require such reconsideration; he does not announce that those holdings already adopted it. Rosenberger's actual vendor-funded private-publication holding remains financing, not merely access, and is preserved on its specific terms. A religious editorial viewpoint in that program is not automatically classified as purchased devotional instruction. Pinette and Chabad retain their actual attribution and access rules; the categorical proposition that a symbol's central governmental location always establishes attribution is not carried into the updated test. Kiryas Joel's restrictions on denominational allocation of civil authority remain intact.

  Other specific doctrines, including denominational scrutiny, private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, and applicable accommodation rules, retain their own sources and burdens. Broad statements in this proposal do not silently overrule their concrete holdings.

Stone proposes a candid doctrinal change. The five-part statement required by his law of precedent is:

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the First Circuit's judgment affirming Title IX liability, vacating the team-specific remedy, and remanding for a lawful compliance plan.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Brown remains liable under Title IX. The district court shall allow Brown to choose a lawful means of effective compliance and may prescribe more specific relief only if a supported finding shows that institutional choice will not cure the violation.

### Issue 1: Title IX's alternative compliance routes and prong three

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory framework and its application to liability.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone states the three routes and section 1681(b) in the Court's own words from Kelley. Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, and any one route is sufficient. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Stone reads prong three to ask whether the actual, competitively viable unmet interests and abilities of the underrepresented sex are fully and effectively accommodated. On that reading, aggregate comparisons between men's and women's interest levels cannot excuse proved unmet demand or freeze a disparity that unequal opportunity may itself have produced.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Brown has not established effective accommodation under any of the three routes. Prong three requires full accommodation of demonstrated, competitively viable unmet interests and abilities of the underrepresented sex rather than a comparison with the other sex's aggregate interest level. Numerical imbalance alone establishes neither liability nor a quota.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Brown materially underrepresented women, showed no continuing practice of responsive expansion, and left proved viable interests unaccommodated, including existing women's teams with demonstrated interest and ability. The conclusion rests on program operation and demonstrated unmet demand, not enrollment arithmetic alone. The Court applies the agency's interpretation as Kelley did and decides no broader question of deference.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not preserve any particular team, make proportionality the exclusive route, or require accommodation of unsupported or infeasible interest.

### Issue 2: Equal protection of prong-three enforcement

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional challenge because federal enforcement expressly treats the underrepresented sex differently.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Under Kelley, a public university may consider sex to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure, or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient, and stereotypes, pretext, and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden. Correcting demonstrated program-wide inequality in participation opportunities is an important governmental objective, and accommodating proved, competitively viable unmet interests is substantially related to it. Applying that standard to the federal enforcement rule extends Kelley, whose rule concerned a public university's own sex-conscious restructuring, and the classification examined here is the federal enforcement rule and not Brown's conduct as a private university.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** As applied to prong-three enforcement, the sex-conscious rule survives intermediate scrutiny because it is substantially related to correcting demonstrated program-wide inequality without relying on stereotype or numerical imbalance alone.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The classification responds to an established opportunity deficit and ends when effective accommodation is achieved. It does not assign athletic preferences by assumptions about what men or women ordinarily want; it responds to the interests and abilities actually shown in the program.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve a future sex-conscious remedial order without applying intermediate scrutiny to the order actually entered.

### Issue 3: Remedial scope

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the limits of the team-specific decree.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Title IX fixes the equality obligation but does not initially select the university's lawful means of compliance. The district court may reject an ineffective plan and later enter necessary specific relief, but it must first preserve institutional choice when another effective route remains available.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The liability judgment remains, but the order elevating identified teams was properly vacated so Brown could submit a lawful compliance plan.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Liability is established independently of the first remedial proposal. Vacating the particular team order does not dilute the obligation; it preserves the ordinary sequence between a proven duty and a remedy no broader than necessity requires.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The district court retains authority to impose effective relief if Brown's chosen plan fails to cure the violation.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1-3.** Stone would sustain three genuinely alternative compliance paths, any one of which is sufficient, and full accommodation of proved, viable unmet interests under prong three, which is Stone's reading of Kelley's third route, with section 1681(b) expressly invoked. General claims that women are less interested do not answer existing demand or justify freezing inequality shaped by prior opportunity. The framework is not a mandatory numerical quota because proportionality is one route and disparity alone does not establish failure of all routes. The supplied application satisfies the stated equality scrutiny on demonstrated program-wide inequality.

**Question 4.** Restoration of opportunities can be lawful relief, but the district court must initially permit an effective institutional compliance choice. Stone would affirm liability, affirm vacatur of the specified-team mandate, and remand for a lawful plan; necessary more specific relief remains available if supported findings show the institution's choice will not cure the violation.
