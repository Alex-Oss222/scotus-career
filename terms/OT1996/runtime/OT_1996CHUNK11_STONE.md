## C. LEGAL REASONING

A prosecutor retains absolute immunity for legitimate charging decisions and prosecutorial advocacy, but not when personally swearing to factual assertions as a complaining witness. Immunity follows the function performed, not the official's title.

Stone would additionally recognize a narrow exception to absolute immunity for knowingly fabricated evidence or deliberately false material assertions causing a constitutional deprivation. Honest mistakes and disputed factual judgments do not satisfy this exception.

Under Stone's proposed **Contextual Fair-Notice and Reasonable-Conduct Test**, qualified immunity protects genuinely reasonable mistakes assessed according to the official's responsibilities, available information, and governing law. It does not protect knowing constitutional violations or objectively indefensible conduct. The official bears the burden of establishing the defense.

## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **First independent holding: this nonremedial layoff does not fall within Title VII's recognized affirmative-action permission.** The Board chose who would lose an existing position because of race without connecting the decision to a demonstrated remedial imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category. Preserving one department's racial composition does not itself establish that statutory connection. Weber and Johnson require the remedial connection they describe, and Stone would not read their accommodation as authority for any beneficial racial objective. An employer need not confess a proved violation, and a plan addressing manifest imbalance remains available, but that distinction does not assist a board expressly relying on diversity alone to choose who loses an existing job.

2. **Second independent holding: the layoff unnecessarily trammels the interests of the employee.** Assuming without deciding that a diversity purpose could count, discharging an incumbent from an existing job is central to the no-unnecessary-trammeling inquiry, and a tie between equally senior and qualified employees does not erase that burden. A race-neutral lottery was available to resolve the equal-seniority problem and the Board had used it before. Stone would reject a universal rule that every race-conscious employment plan involving burdens is invalid, but finds this burden unjustified on this record. Either holding independently supports affirmance.

3. **Weber and Johnson remain intact.** Nothing here overrules them, requires proof of a past violation by the employer, or forbids a remedial layoff arrangement supported by a manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated category.

4. **Separate part: statutory and constitutional standards differ.** Title VII is not the Equal Protection Clause, and this decision rests on the statute. It holds nothing about whether diversity can ever justify race consideration by a public institution and decides no constitutional question. A university's educational mission and an employer's workforce layoff present different questions. If the Court has by then recognized an educational-benefit interest for a public university, that recognition rests on the institution's academic function and does not transfer to a decision to discharge an incumbent. Simulated Adarand's actual federal holding provides no exemption for this local employment decision, and historical Adarand is not imported.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the Title VII liability judgment and the associated relief to the extent properly before the Court, on either independent statutory ground. Reject a constitutional holding that diversity can never justify consideration of race, and decline to decide unpresented equal-protection questions. This resolves the tension with Stone's documented diversity views through the distinction between constitutional permission and statutory restriction, rather than concealing or discarding those views.

**Question 1 — recognize the jury right for this damages action and these factual tasks.** Stone would treat this §1983 claim for compensation for an alleged unconstitutional deprivation as a legal action with a property-tort analogue, protected by the Seventh Amendment. The judge determines the governing constitutional standards, legally relevant property interest and sufficient evidence to submit a claim. The jury determines supported disputes about what uses remained, what the City's final decision did in this particular setting, and the resulting compensable injury. The City cannot remove those factual functions by labeling the entire taking question constitutional.

Markman's actual majority rule is function-specific, including its assignment of technical facts embedded in patent meaning to the judge. Stone would apply that method without converting his separate writing into law: public patent construction is a different task from determining this permit denial's practical effects. He does not extend the ruling to every condemnation proceeding, a challenge to general zoning legislation, or every subsidiary legal issue in a damages action. Section 1983's recognition of legal relief supports the character of the action; the constitutional jury source supplies his answer without requiring a universal statutory jury rule.

**Question 2 — repeated denials can produce a taking, but repetition is evidence rather than the test.** Stone would uphold the verdict on the properly confined, supported theories submitted here. For complete deprivation, the relevant question is productive use of the parcel during the actual period, not merely whether this preferred project could proceed. The owners' evidence of mutually constraining requirements and the asserted practical exclusion of development supports the jury's conclusion; the City's legitimate environmental aims remain relevant but do not themselves establish the background-title exception required by simulated Lucas. No substantiated antecedent limitation is identified that would independently require the entire deprivation found here. A later sale to the State above the original purchase price is material evidence, but does not conclusively establish that a productive option existed during the earlier period or that its loss has already been compensated. Stone would not presume total deprivation from a permit rejection alone.

On the alternative substantial-advancement instruction, Stone applies received Agins and the unchallenged instruction's reasonable-relationship formulation to this particular final decision. He would not ask a jury whether a different land-use policy is preferable. The evidence permitted it to assess whether the reasons actually proffered bore the required relationship to this denial after the owners' responses to the City's conditions. This preserves legitimate environmental judgment while permitting adjudication of the concrete constitutional claim. Agins's distinct formulation has not been displaced by the supplied simulated authorities. Stone does not silently replace it with later historical law, and an affirmance under the submitted, supported grounds need not announce that every poorly fitted regulation appropriates property. His own property framework continues to distinguish physical appropriation, complete deprivation, partial-loss analysis and exactions; this disposition applies the binding formulation and its presentation limits without expanding it.

Stone expressly rejects the Ninth Circuit's suggestion that the final denial itself must satisfy Dolan's rough-proportionality burden. Simulated Dolan concerns demanded property conveyances, and its actual limits remain. The jury was not instructed on that enlarged standard. The evidence supporting the narrower reasonable-relationship inquiry and complete-deprivation ground allows affirmance without endorsing the appellate overextension. He would uphold a supported verdict, not independently resolve witness credibility.

**Question 3 — permit compensatory damages through the established §1983 municipal action.** A City decision satisfying Monell's institutional connection and the constitutional taking requirements can support a damages judgment; the owner is not confined to requesting another administrative decision or prospective invalidation after the injury has occurred. Here the challenged final municipal decision and the action's adequate-remedy posture supply the relevant channel. Stone does not use Franklin to invent one or treat the taking alone as dispensing with all jurisdictional and remedial predicates.

Compensation remains tied to the proved duration and extent of deprivation, with sale proceeds and other recovery accounted for to avoid duplication. First English and simulated Lucas do not confer anticipated profits automatically or make every period of ordinary permit processing compensable. The selected questions identify no basis to enlarge the existing $1,450,000 award or order the City to approve construction. The legal availability of damages is distinct from the supported amount and from the already rejected substantive-due-process theory.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Ninth Circuit shall remand to the Benefits Review Board with instructions to return the matter to the administrative law judge. The administrative law judge shall determine whether Rambo has proved a significant, nonconjectural potential that his work injury will reduce his future wage-earning capacity. ### Issue 1: Nominal compensation for a significant potential future loss of wage-earning capacity

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved statutory question. He does not decide whether this record proves the required future risk because the administrative law judge did not make the necessary finding under the governing standard.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone adopts a narrow statutory reconciliation. A nominal award is available when a permanent work injury causes no current wage loss but the claimant proves a significant and nonconjectural potential that the injury, interacting with reasonably foreseeable labor-market conditions, will reduce future wage-earning capacity. The employer seeking termination must first establish the change in conditions supporting termination of actual benefits. Once current earnings fairly represent current capacity, the claimant seeking nominal compensation carries persuasion on the significant future potential. The possibility may not be presumed as an administrative convenience.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The LHWCA permits nominal compensation when a work-related injury creates a significant, proved potential for future diminished wage-earning capacity despite no current loss, and the administrative law judge must decide whether that condition exists.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 8(c)(21) compensates lost earning capacity rather than physical impairment alone. Section 8(h), however, expressly requires attention to the future effect of disability. Reading the Act to require an outright denial whenever current wages equal or exceed preinjury wages would make § 22's one-year limitation prevent later relief even when the anticipated economic consequence materializes. A nominal award gives effect to both provisions without paying present compensation for a merely physical loss. Metropolitan's proof of much higher current market wages supports termination of actual benefits. It does not answer the separate future-risk question, and the Ninth Circuit could not make that factual finding for the administrative law judge.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no nominal award for every permanent impairment, does not assume future unemployment, and does not permit an appellate court to substitute its view of probability for supported administrative findings.

Stone’s approved allocation preserves the employer’s burden to establish actual-benefit modification and places the nominal-order predicate on Rambo. The proposed nominal-award mechanism resolves a point expressly reserved in Rambo I; it is not a claim that the earlier mandate guaranteed ongoing compensation or required a new hearing regardless of the existing record.

- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Simmons applies retroactively when the prosecution seeks death through future dangerousness, state law makes parole legally impossible, and the jury is left uninformed after the trial court refuses an accurate instruction.

Stone’s approved conclusion that this new procedure falls within the narrow watershed exception is an express proposed extension, not a completed holding of Simmons. No broader capital-retroactivity exception or automatic reversal rule is imported.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in both dockets.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The permanent injunction remains in force. New York City may continue to provide Title I remedial services through the off-premises and other arrangements the injunction permits. The City may again seek relief under Rule 60(b)(5) on a showing of a significant change in fact or law, or may adopt a materially different program, which would be reviewed on its own terms. Nothing in the judgment reopens a completed matter or prohibits a program that does not fall within Aguilar's rule.

### Issue 1: Relief from the continuing Aguilar injunction

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Rule 60(b)(5) question first because it decides the judgment. The denial of relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, and the question is whether the City carried its burden of showing a significant change in fact or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable. Stone does not decide whether a differently designed program would be valid, because this motion fails at the threshold of change.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Rule 60(b)(5) permits relief from the prospective effect of a decree on a significant change in circumstances, as the Court held in Rufo. Under Rufo, actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden; mere foreseeability does not bar relief, and an unforeseeable catastrophe is not required. Rufo's protection of voluntarily accepted consent obligations does not convert this adjudicated injunction into a bargain. Increased expense and practical access difficulties must be assessed for significant change and continued inequity; the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice. A change in law must be an actual change in the rule that governs, not tension between later decisions and an earlier one. Aguilar was binding on the lower courts and remains the controlling rule: Kiryas Joel expressly left Aguilar and Ball in place, Zobrest produced no Establishment Clause merits holding, and Rosenberger and Chabad decide program-specific questions of access and attribution while retaining Lemon. Tension is not change. Only this Court may overrule its decisions, and it should do so only for a legal reason under the law of precedent, not because the motion invites reconsideration.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The City did not establish a significant change in fact or law. The district court did not abuse its discretion in denying relief, and the Second Circuit correctly affirmed.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The decree governs the City's future administration, so Rule 60(b)(5) applies, and the burden was the City's. The cost of mobile units and off-site service, and the reduced practical access that followed, are relevant to the motion. Stone does not reject them merely because they were foreseeable. He concludes that the showing identifies the burden of operating under the injunction but does not establish a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable. Nor does the record identify a material change in the program's design, the schools' relationship to it, or the facts on which Aguilar rested. The claim that later opinions undermine Aguilar requires this Court to consider its continued authority under the law of precedent; it does not establish that the lower courts were already free to disregard it. A change in the Court's expected vote is not a change in law, and the opinion says so plainly. The lower courts did what Rodriguez de Quijas required.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide that a differently designed program is valid or invalid and does not make the decree immune from modification on a qualifying showing. This Court may reconsider Aguilar in a proper vehicle without requiring another decision already to have displaced it; Stone addresses and rejects the present request for reconsideration in Issue 2.

### Issue 2: Whether Aguilar and Ball should be retained

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the question because it was granted and because the City's claim of changed law depends on it. He decides it only as far as that claim requires: whether a legal reason justifies overruling.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Aguilar and Ball are retained. Stone changes precedent only for a stated legal reason: conflict with superior text or structure, internal contradiction, unworkability, a collapsed factual premise, or systematic defeat of the doctrine's own protection. None is shown. First, the monitoring that Aguilar found entangling follows from placing public employees inside religious classrooms, and delivery at neutral sites avoids both that monitoring and the symbolic union, so the rule can be satisfied and has been for years; Wolman preserves properly administered neutral-site services. Second, Aguilar did not rest on proof of actual indoctrination, so the absence of such a finding does not disprove the structural risk it identified. Third, the Funding Wall permits secular instruction at a religious institution when the funded work is separated from religious activity in governance, personnel, content and accounts, meets the same regulation as other recipients, and allows no diversion. Onsite delivery or incidental cost savings do not by themselves establish a breach. Neutral eligibility and public personnel are relevant but do not alone resolve every separation question. Stone retains Aguilar's directly applicable rule here because he finds no sufficient legal reason to displace its treatment of this program; he does not add a categorical Funding Wall rule against all onsite secular instruction. Fourth, the actual costs, inefficiency and reduced access bear on the asserted unworkability and continued equity. Stone concludes that the showing establishes serious administrative burdens, but not failure of the rule's operation or a collapsed factual premise sufficient to justify the requested overruling.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Aguilar and Ball remain good law. A publicly staffed and supervised Title I program delivered inside religious schools is not taken outside their rule because its eligibility criteria are neutral and its teachers are public employees.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The children are selected by educational need, and the teachers remain public employees using public materials. The record identifies no finding of actual indoctrination, a transfer of public funds to the schools, or religious control of those teachers. Stone does not turn the absence of such findings into proof that the schools' own instruction was displaced or that fiscal diversion occurred. He nonetheless regards the monitoring and institutional relationship addressed by Aguilar as sufficient reasons to retain its rule for this program: public staffing and secular materials do not, in his view, supply the legal reason required to overrule that structural judgment. This conclusion applies Aguilar; it is not an independent factual finding that every onsite secular service is inseparable from religion. Transportation, health services, meals, publicly controlled disability services and genuinely separated secular instruction remain subject to their own governing rules.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not forbid all public benefit that reaches religious-school settings. Witters and Mueller retain their actual pre-divergence holdings. Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger and Chabad are untouched, Lemon is neither revised nor replaced, and no new test is adopted. Stone joined Rosenberger's actual vendor-funded private-publication holding; he neither recasts it as access without financing nor derives a ban on all incidental institutional cost savings from it. Whether a materially different remedial-instruction program satisfies the applicable separation requirements and falls outside Aguilar's retained rule is reserved.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the First Circuit's judgment affirming Title IX liability, vacating the team-specific remedy, and remanding for a lawful compliance plan.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Brown remains liable under Title IX. The district court shall allow Brown to choose a lawful means of effective compliance and may prescribe more specific relief only if a supported finding shows that institutional choice will not cure the violation.

### Issue 1: Title IX's alternative compliance routes and prong three

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory framework and its application to liability.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone states the three routes and section 1681(b) in the Court's own words from Kelley. Title IX does not guarantee preservation of a particular varsity team or sport-for-sport counterparts. Effective accommodation may be established through substantial proportionality, a history and continuing responsive expansion for the underrepresented sex, or full and effective accommodation of that sex's interests and abilities, and any one route is sufficient. Section 1681(b) rejects required preference merely because of imbalance while permitting relevant statistics. Stone reads prong three to ask whether the actual, competitively viable unmet interests and abilities of the underrepresented sex are fully and effectively accommodated. On that reading, aggregate comparisons between men's and women's interest levels cannot excuse proved unmet demand or freeze a disparity that unequal opportunity may itself have produced.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Brown has not established effective accommodation under any of the three routes. Prong three requires full accommodation of demonstrated, competitively viable unmet interests and abilities of the underrepresented sex rather than a comparison with the other sex's aggregate interest level. Numerical imbalance alone establishes neither liability nor a quota.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Brown materially underrepresented women, showed no continuing practice of responsive expansion, and left proved viable interests unaccommodated, including existing women's teams with demonstrated interest and ability. The conclusion rests on program operation and demonstrated unmet demand, not enrollment arithmetic alone. The Court applies the agency's interpretation as Kelley did and decides no broader question of deference.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not preserve any particular team, make proportionality the exclusive route, or require accommodation of unsupported or infeasible interest.

### Issue 2: Equal protection of prong-three enforcement

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional challenge because federal enforcement expressly treats the underrepresented sex differently.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Under Kelley, a public university may consider sex to preserve opportunities against demonstrated inequality only when the choice serves an actual important objective and is substantially related to it. Ratios, budget pressure, or attempted federal compliance alone are insufficient, and stereotypes, pretext, and measures materially broader than the demonstrated need are forbidden. Correcting demonstrated program-wide inequality in participation opportunities is an important governmental objective, and accommodating proved, competitively viable unmet interests is substantially related to it. Applying that standard to the federal enforcement rule extends Kelley, whose rule concerned a public university's own sex-conscious restructuring, and the classification examined here is the federal enforcement rule and not Brown's conduct as a private university.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** As applied to prong-three enforcement, the sex-conscious rule survives intermediate scrutiny because it is substantially related to correcting demonstrated program-wide inequality without relying on stereotype or numerical imbalance alone.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The classification responds to an established opportunity deficit and ends when effective accommodation is achieved. It does not assign athletic preferences by assumptions about what men or women ordinarily want; it responds to the interests and abilities actually shown in the program.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve a future sex-conscious remedial order without applying intermediate scrutiny to the order actually entered.

### Issue 3: Remedial scope

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the limits of the team-specific decree.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Title IX fixes the equality obligation but does not initially select the university's lawful means of compliance. The district court may reject an ineffective plan and later enter necessary specific relief, but it must first preserve institutional choice when another effective route remains available.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The liability judgment remains, but the order elevating identified teams was properly vacated so Brown could submit a lawful compliance plan.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Liability is established independently of the first remedial proposal. Vacating the particular team order does not dilute the obligation; it preserves the ordinary sequence between a proven duty and a remedy no broader than necessity requires.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The district court retains authority to impose effective relief if Brown's chosen plan fails to cure the violation.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1-3.** Stone would sustain three genuinely alternative compliance paths, any one of which is sufficient, and full accommodation of proved, viable unmet interests under prong three, which is Stone's reading of Kelley's third route, with section 1681(b) expressly invoked. General claims that women are less interested do not answer existing demand or justify freezing inequality shaped by prior opportunity. The framework is not a mandatory numerical quota because proportionality is one route and disparity alone does not establish failure of all routes. The supplied application satisfies the stated equality scrutiny on demonstrated program-wide inequality.

**Question 4.** Restoration of opportunities can be lawful relief, but the district court must initially permit an effective institutional compliance choice. Stone would affirm liability, affirm vacatur of the specified-team mandate, and remand for a lawful plan; necessary more specific relief remains available if supported findings show the institution's choice will not cure the violation.
