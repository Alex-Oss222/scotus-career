### Recreation component

The presently rejected contention that ending a private benefit can never justify such a condition is a completed legal ruling, but that alone does not complete the case.

Cox's first category does not follow from the finality of that proposition: the substantive remand is neither shown preordained nor practically concluded. The second does not require immediate decision of the recreation issue because the remand could make the federal challenge unnecessary. ### Art component and the retained-grant alternative

The art obligation was sustained, compliance occurred under an express reservation, and recreation proceedings cannot simply eliminate that obligation. This is the strongest route to component-specific review under Cox II; its survival must be acknowledged rather than dismissed by calling the entire judgment interlocutory. Cox describes dissociated federal issues and also emphasizes avoiding multiple federal appeals; its Radio Station WOW example involved a remaining accounting that could not itself present another federal issue. Here the unfinished recreation dispute concerns related development conditions and could produce a later constitutional appeal. That distinction is relevant to retaining this grant, but these commitments do not resolve whether it necessarily defeats art jurisdiction.

Assuming the art component could be separately reviewed, the granted controversy would change materially. The individualized recreation charge presents the monetary-exaction question; the art program instead imposes a valuation-based requirement across defined classes with several methods of compliance. The successor used onsite art, retained title, and remained subject to maintenance, insurance and public-accessibility terms. No particular compelled-entry easement or mandatory physical occupation of the site is established. A court could decide a suitably bounded general challenge without the precise site arrangement; its absence is not a litigant default or a jurisdictional fact found against Ehrlich. **Chronology gate:** This is a durable provisional handoff against the pre-February-18 baseline. ## Result and exact ground

All eight Associates provisionally support **reversing the denial of appellate habeas relief and remanding for further proceedings** on the erroneous administrative-purpose ground. The reviewed Eleventh Circuit order denied a certificate of probable cause without explanatory merits reasoning; the district court adopted the magistrate's categorical treatment of overcrowding credits as administrative. No reasoned appellate merits opinion is invented.

The Court should hold that the population-management purpose of a statutory credit does not exclude it from the Ex Post Facto Clause when its operation shortens punishment and retrospective withdrawal lengthens custody. A prisoner must still establish the legally available offense-date release framework, the adverse retrospective change, and the resulting punishment increase. If later enactments merely implemented or continued that framework, the State cannot avoid the constitutional inquiry by calling the later credits administrative. Conversely, the Clause does not automatically freeze every new benefit first granted after the offense. The Court should return the material statutory-continuity and protected-credit question to the lower courts under that rule.

This commitment does **not** establish that all 1,860 provisional days are protected, award an unconditional refund of days, reverse the separate 335-day cancellation, or command immediate release. ## Enhanced competing paths and narrower alternatives

**Taxpayer path, taken at its strongest.** Once Congress permits refund suits, Irwin makes equity presumptively available even against the sovereign. Incapacity that actually prevents a claim is materially different from counsel's ordinary neglect. Scott has an adjudicated inability to act, followed by prompt filing after the incapacity ended, and an absence-of-prejudice finding. **Government path selected, with its strongest objection answered.** The disputed question is not whether the taxpayers deserve relief or whether Congress used the word “equity.” Unlike an ordinary deadline appended to an authorized claim, §6511 separately specifies both when a claim may be made and which payments may be returned. Suspending that payment interval and constructing a corresponding Government assessment extension would change two statutory operations. The repeated allowance restriction and tailored statutory adjustments reinforce that conclusion. Irwin allows rebuttal from the enacted scheme without an express anti-equity sentence; the assumption that equity must preserve the practical usefulness of a tollable filing deadline cannot first establish that either provision is tollable. Administration of a national tax scheme corroborates this reading but does not independently establish it, and the Government's volume assertion is not a finding that every return entails an incapacity dispute.

**Narrower route actually tested.** All eight could dispose of the tax-payment claims solely under L while reserving F. **Broader route rejected as a common opinion.** Dalm plus sovereign immunity cannot, without more, make Irwin irrelevant.
