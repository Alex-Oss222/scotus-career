# 152. Faragher v. City of Boca Raton

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT OUTCOME

The en banc Eleventh Circuit rejected liability because it found neither employment-scope conduct nor agency aid, and held the trial's constructive-notice reasoning insufficient. It distinguished actionable hostility from what management should have known. Dissenting reasoning treated delegated command power as a sufficient agency connection. This was the reviewed lower judgment, not governing simulation Supreme Court law. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/111/1530/630363/).

## B. LATER HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR ONLY

Historically, Faragher v. City of Boca Raton, 524 U.S. 775, No. 97-282, decided June 26, 1998, reversed and ordered reinstatement of the district judgment. The Court recognized employer vicarious responsibility for a supervisor's actionable hostile environment. Without a tangible employment action, it allowed an employer to prove both reasonable preventive/corrective care and the employee's unreasonable failure to use available protection or avoid harm; a tangible employment action precluded that defense. The City's complete failure to communicate its policy defeated the defense, leaving no reason for further litigation over the nominal award. Thomas, joined by Scalia, opposed vicarious liability absent an adverse employment consequence and would remand on negligence.

That later rule, including its affirmative defense and disposition, is comparison material only. Stone's proposal in Section II independently reaches a different rule on the defense and requests a confined attribution remand; no historical result is imported into the simulation. [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/97-282).

# 153. Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc.

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT OUTCOME

The Tenth Circuit reversed the employer's Title VII judgment because the jury instructions omitted liability for abuse of delegated power and imposed inappropriate managerial-control/apparent-authority requirements. It affirmed rejection of the employer's exhaustion argument, distinguishing collective contractual grievances from statutory claims. Its broader discussion of labor arbitration is lower-court reasoning, not an inherited Supreme Court holding. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/112/1437/585308/).

Historically, under the reversed caption Eddy Potash, Inc. v. Harrison, No. 97-232, 524 U.S. 947 (June 26, 1998), the Supreme Court granted certiorari, vacated the appellate judgment and remanded for reconsideration in light of the newly decided historical Faragher. This was a GVR, not an independent Supreme Court merits ruling adopting Harrison's agency or arbitration reasoning. The historical 1998 Faragher rule and this dependent order postdate the simulation review; neither supplies entering law or a simulated result. [Official United States Reports, page 947](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/524bv.pdf).
