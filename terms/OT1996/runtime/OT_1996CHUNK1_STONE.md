# 1. Department of State v. Legal Assistance for Vietnamese Asylum Seekers, Inc.

## A. APPROVED POSITION AND DISPOSITION

Vacate the D.C. Circuit judgment and remand for reconsideration in light of IIRIRA §633. Stone does not decide visa eligibility, order asylum, find the controversy moot, or decide the old-law merits while the new provision's application remains unresolved.

## B. INTERVENING LAW AND THE SPECIFIC REMAND

The judgment below construed §1152(a)(1)'s nationality and residence nondiscrimination rule as limiting the Department's treatment of screened-out Vietnamese applicants seeking immigrant-visa processing in Hong Kong. Section633, enacted September30,1996, addresses the Secretary's authority over immigrant-visa processing procedures and locations. That is the precise activity the judgment regulates. Stone therefore finds a reasonable probability that consideration of the new provision will alter an outcome-material statutory premise or the permissible continuing relief; its relationship to the judgment is direct, not merely thematic.

Simulated Lawrence requires independent equitable reasons as well as that probability. This case remains on timely review, the enactment arrived after the appellate decisions, and the parties have had supplemental briefing on its possible effects. The substantial delay already borne by applicants weighs against an unfocused reopening, but also favors obtaining a decision under the legally operative framework rather than an appellate merits ruling that may immediately require revision. Congress's intervening enactment, rather than a litigant's changed preference or strategic withdrawal, supplies the reason to reconsider. A confined remand respects the lower court's task without prejudging its answer. Those circumstances justify vacatur despite the costs of another review.

Landgraf still requires the court to identify express temporal direction, then the provision's operation and any new legal consequences for completed conduct. Stone does not infer retroactivity merely from a pending case, nor conclude that all prospective relief remains governed by the superseded understanding. The lower court should decide §633's meaning and temporal reach, the effect on the challenged location policy, what claims remain live, and what relief its lawful construction permits. Existing favorable judgments and reliance interests must be addressed through those rules; vacatur is not an instruction to deny the applicants relief.

## C. LIMITS

The remand may produce the same judgment on a lawful ground. It revives no forfeited defense, opens no independent asylum proceeding, and requires additional factfinding only for identified matters that cannot be resolved on the existing record. Stone's result applies the Court's reasonable-probability and equities requirements, not his noncontrolling reasonable-possibility formulation from Lawrence.

# 3. Lopez v. Monterey County

# SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Current approval status:** All Stone answers, reasoning, proposed dispositions and stated conditional fallbacks in this supplement are approved by the user on October 11, 2026. They are Stone’s positions for the simulation, not Court decisions. Approval does not establish missing evidence, participation or an intervening Court holding.

## A. STATUS

- **Approval Status:** Source-designated approved position, preserved subject to the express precedent conflicts identified below. This packet does not enter a Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The order authorizing the March 1996 countywide, at-large election shall be vacated. The district court shall enjoin Monterey County from administering the countywide, at-large system unless and until every operative post-1968 voting change necessary to that system has received § 5 preclearance. It shall determine the exact scope of any preclearance produced by California's 1983 submission, identify the remaining unprecleared changes, and require their expeditious submission through one of the forums Congress designated. If no lawful electoral arrangement can operate in time and an independent source of remedial authority permits judicial action, the court may adopt a wholly court-developed, necessary, and temporary plan. Such a plan need not itself receive § 5 preclearance, but it must comply with the Constitution and the Voting Rights Act, displace state law no further than necessity requires, and terminate when a lawful precleared arrangement becomes available. The enforcement court may not decide whether the County's plan is substantively entitled to preclearance.

### Issue 1: State-mandated voting changes

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the state-compulsion question because it is expressly within the granted coverage question and presents a legal issue independent of the unresolved state-law characterization. He need not decide whether California law actually displaced the County ordinances because the answer is the same even if the arrangement is now wholly state mandated.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a covered political subdivision "seek[s] to administer" a postbaseline voting change when it gives the change operative effect solely because the superior law of a noncovered State requires it to do so.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone draws a bounded analogy from Morse’s legally operative nomination function and Presley’s direct-voting distinction; he proposes the answer to local discretion as a new statutory construction, rather than claiming that Morse already adopted a general delegation test. Section 5 separately reaches a covered jurisdiction that enacts a change and one that seeks to administer a change. "Administer" concerns execution and operation, not authorship or freedom to choose. Requiring local discretion would add a condition the enacted text does not contain and would permit a noncovered State to route voting changes around § 5 throughout its covered subdivisions. Stone therefore holds that the County's duty turns on its administration of the changed election system, not on whether California or the County selected the policy. The State is not required to preclear its law in the abstract; the covered County must obtain preclearance before giving that law operative effect within Monterey County. This interpretation changes no burden or substantive preclearance standard and leaves the designated forums' authority intact.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** "Administer" means giving a change operative effect. A covered political subdivision therefore seeks to administer a voting change when its officials put it into operation in the subdivision, and Section 5 requires Monterey County to obtain preclearance before it runs a postbaseline judicial-election change, even if California law compels the change and the County has no discretion over it. The duty rests on the County. It imposes no duty on California itself, and it decides nothing about retrogression or discriminatory purpose.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 5's paired verbs distinguish lawmaking from implementation. Monterey County conducts the elections, defines the operative electorate through the system it administers, and would put the countywide arrangement into effect. The change experienced by voters is identical whether it originated in a County ordinance or a California statute. Earlier treatment of state reapportionment affecting covered counties and the statute's preventive design reinforce the textual conclusion, but the holding rests on the enacted command. Because the County has not obtained preclearance for all operative changes, it may not administer the countywide, at-large system.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding imposes no duty on California itself and no statewide preclearance obligation, and it does not reach state laws with no operative effect in a covered jurisdiction. It does not decide whether the countywide system has a discriminatory purpose or retrogressive effect, or whether preclearance should be granted. Those questions belong to the Attorney General and the District Court for the District of Columbia.

### Issue 2: Covered change, designation, and laches

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal sufficiency of the change, designation, and laches defenses because the undisputed baseline and operation permit decision without new factual findings. He remands only the exact scope of the 1983 submission and any backward-looking remedial question.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the consolidation altered a voting standard, practice, or procedure; whether the County may collaterally defeat its published § 4(b) designation in this enforcement action; and whether delay can bar prospective enforcement of § 5's no-use command.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** First, replacing nine district electorates with one countywide electorate changes district boundaries, the constituency entitled to elect each judge, and the method by which votes translate into office. It is a covered voting change under § 5 and § 14(c)(1). Second, Monterey County's published coverage designation remains operative unless the County obtains statutory bailout. The underlying administrative coverage findings may not be collaterally relitigated here, and the facial constitutional objection is foreclosed by South Carolina v. Katzenbach and City of Rome. Third, laches cannot convert an unprecleared change into an enforceable one or bar an injunction against its future use. Delay and reliance may bear on retrospective relief, treatment of completed acts, or the timing and form of a lawful transition, but they do not dispense with preclearance.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The shift from district-based judicial elections to a countywide, at-large system is a change affecting voting. Monterey County remains covered until released through the statutory bailout process, and laches does not authorize prospective administration of an unprecleared change.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The 1968 system allowed voters in each district to elect the judges serving that district. The challenged system places every judgeship before the countywide electorate. That is a direct alteration of electoral boundaries and voting power, not an internal governmental reallocation outside § 5. The County's designation was published and remains legally effective, while Congress supplied bailout rather than collateral relitigation as the route out of coverage. The County's long noncompliance and the plaintiffs' later filing cannot make the statutory precondition disappear. California's 1983 submission may have reached the final 1983 ordinance, but it did not identify the earlier ordinances; the district court must determine its exact scope without treating one submission as blanket approval of the full consolidation sequence.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide a factual bailout claim, damages, relief concerning completed elections, or the precise administrative effect of the 1983 submission. Those matters require their own lawful record and remedial source.

### Issue 3: Enforcement-court authority and temporary remedy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the enforcement court's remedial authority because the challenged judgment ordered an election under an unprecleared plan. He does not reach substantive preclearance, discriminatory purpose, or retrogression because Congress assigned those questions to the Attorney General and the United States District Court for the District of Columbia.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the enforcement court may authorize the County's or the State's unprecleared policy choice, and when a genuinely court-developed temporary arrangement falls outside § 5's preclearance requirement.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** An enforcement court may determine coverage, determine whether preclearance occurred, enjoin nonuse, and select a temporary remedy authorized by law. It may not decide substantive preclearance or obtain the same practical result by ordering elections under an unprecleared governmental plan. A narrow exception applies to a plan formulated wholly by the federal court in the first instance, because such a plan is a judicial remedy rather than a voting change enacted or administered as the policy of the covered jurisdiction. The exception does not apply when the court adopts material policy choices proposed, required, or previously selected by the County or the State. A court-developed plan must be supported by necessity and independent remedial authority, must be temporary, and must yield promptly to a lawful precleared governmental plan.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A § 5 enforcement court may not authorize elections under an unprecleared County or State plan. When an independently authorized temporary remedy is indispensable, a plan wholly formulated by the federal court may operate without separate § 5 preclearance, but a court order does not exempt a plan that carries forward governmental policy choices.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The March 1996 order reinstated the same countywide, at-large arrangement Monterey County had adopted and allowed judges elected under it to serve ordinary six-year terms. The plan therefore remained a governmental policy choice, not a wholly judicial stopgap. Years of notice and the County's abandonment of its D.C. preclearance action also defeat any claim that an extreme last-minute circumstance required use of that plan. The district court's administrative difficulty does not enlarge its authority or permit it to adjudicate the plan's discriminatory purpose or effect. Its task is to enforce nonuse, require prompt submission, and employ only a separately authorized temporary remedy if one is truly necessary.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A wholly court-developed plan remains subject to all other constitutional and statutory constraints. Stone does not select the interim arrangement, approve the countywide system on the merits, or state any view on whether the Attorney General or the D.C. District Court should grant preclearance.

* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not invariably require a free-to-go warning before an officer requests consent. Consent is valid only if the encounter was lawfully consensual or otherwise justified and the consent was voluntary under the complete objective circumstances.

**Current approval status:** All Stone answers, reasoning, proposed dispositions and stated conditional fallbacks in this supplement are approved by the user on October 11, 2026. ## A. STATUS

* **Version:** OT1996 supplemental draft 1.0
* **Approval Status:** proposed to the Court and user-approved. The revised position stated below was user-approved 2026-10-30.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** November 25, 1996, subject to same-term and Standing State revalidation.
* **Conditions:** The exact Standing State grant wording was not supplied. This position answers the reconstructed merits questions only; any narrower grant controls.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand. Affirm the determination that the 1992 admissions system was unconstitutional and that punitive damages were unavailable on the supplied record. Vacate the Fifth Circuit's categorical rules that a public university may never consider race to pursue concrete educational benefits and that remediation is confined to the Law School's own proved present effects, and vacate any prospective command extending beyond the challenged 1992 process. Remand claim-specific causation, compensatory relief, proper defendant, any remaining lawful remedy, and the question whether any traceable remnant of Texas's former dual system persists.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve declaratory and nominal relief for the proven unequal process and fee-free consideration under a lawful system if still practically available. Compensatory relief requires proof, under the governing cause of action, that the unconstitutional process caused the claimed loss; defendants may establish that the same decision would have followed under a specified lawful process. No injunction may categorically prohibit every future consideration of race on this obsolete record. The opinion is organized in separate parts, one for each holding, so that each stands on its own reasoning.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Part I: The 1992 system is invalid

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The undisputed design and trial findings permit application without new factfinding.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the common substantial-justification standard that simulated Adarand adopted for federal, state and local racial classifications to the State's admissions classification, together with the surviving admissions authorities; simulated Adarand created no two-lane rule. Admissions decisions distribute limited seats to identified applicants, and race effectively determined access in the 1992 system.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The 1992 system fails narrow tailoring because it used materially different presumptive thresholds, separate review machinery, racial coding, separate waiting-list practices, and numerical aspirations that made race decisional across broad categories without a sufficiently supported remedial predicate, genuinely individualized comparison, demonstrated necessity, or adequate consideration of workable alternatives.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The process did not merely widen access to consideration. It altered the legal and practical terms on which identified applicants competed for particular seats. The record does not connect the magnitude and operation of those preferences to identified present effects within the Law School, and the separate tracks undermine the individualized educational judgment the defendants invoke. The State cannot replace missing fit with the general truth that Texas once maintained de jure exclusion.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not invalidate outreach, barrier removal, race-neutral contextual review, collection of demographic information, or a materially different individualized program supported under the standard in the following parts.

### Part II: Educational diversity is a cognizable objective

* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Fifth Circuit's categorical rule that educational diversity can never be compelling is inherited law.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Bakke prevents treating that categorical prohibition as inherited law, while the Reconstruction guarantee forbids treating race as an administrative proxy for merit, viewpoint, or entitlement. As a source-grounded extension in a question squarely presented, a public university may pursue concrete and demonstrated educational benefits associated with meaningful institutional inclusion, but it must explain the benefit, the mechanism by which the classification advances it, and the actual burdens imposed. Racial arithmetic, generalized societal discrimination, reputation, hostility outside the institution, and assumptions that racial identity predicts viewpoint are insufficient.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A public university's interest in the concrete educational benefits of a genuinely diverse student body is cognizable and, on a proper record, capable of being an important objective. It is not categorically forbidden. The university must establish the objective and a fitted design; racial balance does not suffice.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone's rule does not presume validity or invalidity from a program's label. It demands a supported objective and a design fitted to actual operation, and it identifies this recognition as an extension stated openly and not as a holding that any program is valid.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide the validity of any later Texas admissions program, prescribe a universal admissions model, require race-conscious action, or convert broad educational aspiration into an important objective without evidence and fit.

### Part III: Operation controls, not labels

* **Question Stone Resolves:** How a court assesses an admissions program that claims individualized consideration or a small formal weight for race.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The university carries the justification burden. A court examines the program's actual operation: decision sequence, thresholds, separate tracks, numerical aims, discretion, the breadth of beneficiary categories, genuine rebuttability, and workable alternatives. A small formal weight is no defense where race decides the outcome for applicants clustered near the decision threshold, and an individualized label is no defense where race in fact decides access. A program without quotas, tracks, or numerical aims is not invalid merely because race is one consideration among several.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The validity of a race-conscious admissions practice turns on how it actually operates, not on its description. Unequal opportunity to compete is a cognizable injury without proof that the applicant would have been admitted.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The 1992 system fails under this inquiry because race altered thresholds, review channels, and waiting lists across broad categories. This part supplies a method for later programs. It approves and rejects none.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The part adopts no universal admissions model and no numerical test.

### Part IV: Repair and the relevant institution

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches this question because the Fifth Circuit's confinement of remediation to the Law School's own proved present effects is a categorical rule within the granted question on remediation and must be reviewed to vacate the prospective command. This part is separable from Parts I through III.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether remediation is confined to the Law School's own proved present effects, or whether the State that maintained a legally segregated university system can be the relevant actor for repair.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Under Fordice, which eight Justices joined, a State that maintained a legally segregated university system does not discharge its duty by adopting good-faith neutral policies. It must eliminate policies traceable to that system that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed, and the State bears the burden of showing dismantlement. The relevant actor is the State that operated the system, not only the particular school, and the Fifth Circuit's categorical confinement is not supplied by the authorities. Fordice's own limits apply. The feature must be traceable to the dual system and continuing; current racial imbalance alone is insufficient; and the remedy must be fitted to the violation and cannot reach beyond it. Generalized societal discrimination also remains insufficient under the Croson line of authority. Stone's repair conditions also apply: objective support for the persisting effect, a scope reasonably fitted to it, meaningful continuing opportunity for others, respect for legally protected existing interests, and no continuation after the basis ceases. The government carries the burden of establishing any corrective justification it invokes.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fifth Circuit's rule confining remediation to the Law School's own proved present effects is vacated. A State that operated a legally segregated university system may be the relevant actor for repair of traceable, continuing, practicably removable remnants, within Fordice's limits. No finding of remnants is made. The remand decides whether any traceable continuing remnant exists and whether any admissions practice is fitted to it.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The record does not establish whether any remnant of Texas's former dual system persists in a form that would justify a particular admissions practice, and a court may not supply missing findings about present effects, necessity, or alternatives. The 1992 system fails under Part I whether or not any remnant exists, because its thresholds, tracks, and numerical aims were not fitted to any identified one.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not treat Texas's history of exclusion as itself justifying any admissions practice, create a presumption that remnants exist, approve numerical goals or any particular program, or decide that a remnant exists. It does not authorize remediation of generalized societal discrimination.

### Part V: Causation and relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches relief because the judgment below remanded damages and stated a prospective categorical rule.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary source-specific causation and remedial discipline.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Unequal racial processing supports declaratory and nominal relief without proof that a plaintiff would certainly have received a seat. Admission, backpay-equivalent loss, or compensatory damages require the causal showing prescribed by the governing claim. Defendants may avoid admission-related compensatory relief by proving that a specified lawful admissions process would have produced the same decision, but that showing neither erases the constitutional violation nor validates future racial sorting.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A merits defect and a monetary remedy are separate. The record establishes unconstitutional differential treatment but does not conclusively establish each applicant's lawful counterfactual outcome. The discontinued system also supplies no basis for a nationwide or perpetual rule governing every future university program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Proper defendants, waiver, personal immunity, statutory damages, fees, and the exact form of remand relief remain for source-specific resolution below.

### Part VI: Questions reserved

* **Stone's Position:** The Court does not decide the validity of any later Texas program; whether holistic individualized review with race as one factor is valid; admissions in other levels of education; or whether race-conscious employment decisions can be justified on a similar basis. This case concerns a public university's admissions. Employment decisions are governed by the employment statute and the cases construing it.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's rationale openly uses the governing Adarand standard and identifies the recognition of an educational-benefit objective as an extension. It does not rely on nonexistent Adarand lanes or on an unverified later Ayers disposition. Fordice applies at its actual scope, with its traceability and practicable-removal limits, and supplies a continuing duty and a relevant actor, not a permission for any particular preference. The parts are written to be severable, so that disagreement with one does not defeat the others. His individual causation inquiry for compensatory relief remains distinct from equal-competition standing and nominal recognition of unequal process.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1 and 2.** Part II recognizes the concrete educational benefits of a genuinely diverse institution as a cognizable objective that may qualify as important on a proper record, not a bare diversity label or racial balancing. Part IV permits remediation of traceable continuing remnants of a former dual system by the State that operated it, within Fordice's limits, and makes no finding that any remnant exists. Generalized societal disadvantage does not suffice.

**Question 3.** Part I holds the separate presumptive thresholds, committee, coding, and wait lists invalid because they made race decisional without the required individualized fit. Stone would affirm that invalidity, vacate the categorical ban on every future consideration of race, and preserve nominal and declaratory relief and a causally supported damages inquiry. He would not award admission or compensatory damages from the unequal process alone or adjudicate an unidentified replacement program.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the former-Title-III construction, reverse the categorical Fourth Amendment ruling, vacate the civil-contempt judgment, and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** On remand, the court shall determine whether Askin knew of or knowingly exposed the cordless retransmission, whether each compelled question derived from the interception, and whether independent source, attenuation, objectively reasonable good-faith reliance, or another lawful basis permits the question. If the governing premises establish lack of knowledge and direct derivation without a lawful exception, the contempt judgment must be set aside.

### Issue 1: Former Title III coverage

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory construction because it bears directly on asserted just cause.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether former Title III covered the radio segment acquired from the cordless handset.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the definitions effective when the surveillance occurred. Former Title III excluded the relevant cordless radio segment, and that segment carried both parties' words. The exclusion left acquisition at that point outside the statutory prohibition; it did not affirmatively authorize interception at another point or answer the Fourth Amendment question.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Former Title III excluded the cordless radio segment intercepted here, including both speakers' words. The 1994 amendment may not be applied retroactively to completed surveillance.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The place of acquisition controls the former statute. Congress later removed the exclusion, but Landgraf prevents the amendment from attaching new legal consequences to the earlier monitoring. Statutory noncoverage and constitutional permission remain separate questions.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding concerns only the superseded statutory text and does not govern interceptions occurring after the 1994 amendment.

### Issue 2: Fourth Amendment protection of the noncordless caller

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal rule but cannot finally apply it because Askin's knowledge or knowing exposure is unresolved.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a landline or cellular caller automatically loses Fourth Amendment protection because the other participant unknowingly uses a vulnerable cordless handset.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Absent participant consent, a willing informant, or knowing exposure, a caller does not assume the risk that unknown government agents will independently intercept both sides of a private conversation through the other endpoint. White assigns the risk that an interlocutor will reveal or transmit what was said; it does not eliminate privacy when neither speaker consents and one caller is unaware of the vulnerable retransmission. A contrary rule would make protection turn on technology the caller neither selected nor knew was in use.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A caller using a landline or cellular telephone does not lose Fourth Amendment protection merely because the other participant uses a vulnerable cordless handset without the caller's knowledge. Knowing exposure or consent remains a different case.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Government acquired the contents of Askin's own calls through the other endpoint. That acquisition is not justified merely by the former statute's omission or by the technical accessibility of the radio signal. Because the record does not establish Askin's knowledge, the categorical ruling against him must be reversed and application returned to the lower court.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide the cordless user's own expectation, protect a speaker who knowingly exposes the call, or disturb participant-consent cases.

### Issue 3: Just cause and the contempt remedy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the source of just cause because the contempt judgment depends on whether Askin could lawfully refuse questions derived from the interception.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A Title III violation supplies just cause through § 2515 and Gelbard. Independently, Stone would apply the Fourth Amendment exclusionary rule narrowly when the Government seeks to imprison the intercepted speaker for refusing questions that are direct fruits of the unlawful interception of his own calls. The rule does not extend beyond direct fruits and remains subject to independent source, attenuation, and objectively reasonable good-faith reliance.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When compelled questions are direct fruits of an unlawful interception of the witness's own calls, and no recognized exception supplies an independent lawful basis, refusal constitutes just cause under § 1826(a).
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The contempt sanction makes the intercepted material the direct means of coercing testimony from the person whose own communication was acquired. That core use supplies substantial deterrent value, while question-by-question derivation and the ordinary exceptions keep the remedy bounded. The unresolved factual and derivative questions require vacatur and remand rather than final discharge.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no automatic immunity from questioning, excuses no independently sourced question, and does not expand the remedy beyond the procedural setting and direct-fruit use presented here.

Stone’s proposed Fourth Amendment just-cause rule goes beyond Pinson and Wellons. Those decisions direct correction of an erroneous categorical search ruling while preserving other available grounds and do not order automatic exclusion. The corrected posture is refusal of testimony at the related criminal trial. His approved Askin position must therefore justify its narrow rule for using the witness’s own unlawfully intercepted communications to coerce his trial testimony; it neither applies nor creates an exception to Calandra’s grand-jury rule.
