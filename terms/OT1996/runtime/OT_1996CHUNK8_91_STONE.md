### Issue 1: Religious viewpoint exclusion from the senior-center access program

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the as-applied First Amendment question. The City created the relevant access program and denied these speakers because their treatment of a permitted religious subject was devotional. The record presents no disputed operational fact requiring remand.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies existing simulated law. A city that permits private treatment of religion, morality, history, literature, and philosophy in a voluntary adult program may not exclude an otherwise eligible presentation because it affirms a faith, invites prayer, or distributes religious text. Neutral access does not become governmental worship where attendance is voluntary, the speaker and materials are privately identified, no public funds produce the message, and the City retains no editorial control over its content. The federal funding condition may be read to prevent governmental or center-sponsored sectarian instruction, not to compel unconstitutional viewpoint exclusion of private speakers.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Albuquerque violated the First Amendment by excluding the privately sponsored film and New Testaments because they presented an affirmative religious viewpoint on subjects the senior-center program otherwise allowed, and the voluntary, privately attributed program created no Establishment Clause justification for that exclusion.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The centers invited private speakers and offered broad programming, including nondevotional discussion of the Bible and religion. The line the City drew therefore turned on viewpoint, not subject matter. Adults chose whether to attend and could leave at any time, so the record supplies neither coercion nor a captive audience. A neutral notice identifying Church on the Rock as sponsor would address any residual attribution concern without suppressing the speech. The injunction fits the proven denial and leaves ordinary facility administration intact.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require the City to sponsor worship, finance devotional materials, permit disruption, or open the centers outside their defined program. It does not bar a genuinely subject-neutral rule excluding all private instruction or a neutral rule against conduct that interferes with other users.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved access remedy is grounded in the actual subject matter and private adult presentation. To the extent his language protects worship as such beyond an admitted comparable subject or activity, that is an explicit proposed extension, not a universal rule decided by Good News. No new funding entitlement follows from permission to use the center.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1–2 — preserved approved answers.** Because Albuquerque already allowed the relevant subjects in privately organized activities, Stone would treat exclusion of this affirmative religious film and literature as viewpoint discrimination. He would not require a senior center to open every otherwise excluded subject or surrender neutral administration.

**Question 3 — preserved approved answer.** Voluntary adult attendance, private identification, lack of public production funding and absence of City editorial control prevent neutral access here from becoming the City’s own religious exercise. The Establishment Clause therefore does not justify this exclusion. Stone would affirm the bounded access injunction and fee proceedings without deciding direct devotional subsidies.
