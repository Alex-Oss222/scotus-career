### Issue 1: Indigent access to a first appeal from permanent termination

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The record fee is the direct barrier to the first appeal, and the case does not require review of the termination evidence itself.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Mississippi may offer appellate correction of a permanent termination decree while making the indispensable record unavailable solely because the parent is indigent.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies a narrow access rule. A State need not create a civil appeal, subsidize ordinary private litigation, or furnish every transcript. Once it creates a first appeal from a judgment that permanently and exclusively alters the legal parent-child relationship, however, it may not make meaningful review turn on an indigent parent's ability to buy the only usable record. The State may use a transcript, an agreed statement, a settled narrative, or another adequate equivalent; it may not make wealth the condition of access.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Mississippi violates due process and equal protection by denying an indigent parent meaningful first appellate review of a permanent termination decree solely because she cannot prepay the cost of the record necessary for that review.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Termination is an irreversible exercise of state power over a civil status the State exclusively defines and enforces. The risk of error is serious, the underlying burden of proof is elevated, and the appellate court cannot test the sparse decree without an adequate account of the evidence and rulings. The requested accommodation does not require Mississippi to finance all civil litigants. It requires the State to make its own first-level error-correction process real in the narrow class of permanent parental-status judgments. The record establishes indigency and necessity, so no factfinding is required on those points.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not create a general constitutional right to a civil appeal, free discovery, appointed appellate counsel, or publicly financed litigation over property, money, or ordinary custody scheduling.

### Issue 1: Duration, safeguards, and state placement under § 4246

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory, due-process, and equal-protection objections to commitment beyond the hypothetical maximum sentence. He does not reach the separately asserted enumerated-power question because preservation is not established.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether present illness-caused dangerousness, rather than the maximum possible criminal sentence, governs continued civil commitment and what continuing safeguards the statute requires.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 4246 does not contain a maximum-sentence cap. It permits continued civil custody only while current mental disease or defect causes the danger specified by the statute, proved by clear and convincing evidence, and while meaningful review and practical routes to unconditional or conditional release remain available. The Attorney General must make and periodically renew reasonable efforts to transfer responsibility to an eligible State, and transfer becomes mandatory if a State accepts. The custody-defined federal class and the interests in safely winding up lawful custody, treatment, and public protection are sufficiently related even under intermediate scrutiny; Stone does not select an ultimate tier.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 4246 permits confinement beyond the maximum possible sentence because the confinement is civil and rests on current mental illness causing current dangerousness, not punishment for the dismissed charge. Continued custody requires meaningful review and release routes and reasonable, periodically renewed efforts to transfer responsibility to a willing State. The Court does not decide the unpreserved enumerated-power question.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Sentence length does not measure recovery from a present disorder, but neither does an old finding authorize indefinite custody. Sections 4246 and 4247 require counsel, a neutral hearing, clear-and-convincing proof, periodic reporting, renewed judicial review, release when the predicate ends, and continuing state-placement efforts. Those safeguards distinguish commitment from custody based on incompetence alone and require remand for current application rather than unconditional release or permanent federal retention.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit confinement based on incompetence alone, dangerousness without qualifying mental disease, a stale finding, nominal review, or federal retention after a State accepts responsibility. It establishes no freestanding federal civil-commitment power.

Foucha’s four-vote equality discussion supplies no controlling tier. - **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Judgment for the Association remains in force on the alternative First Amendment ground as to hostile environment and on statutory insufficiency as to retaliation. No separate remedial instruction is required.

### Issue 1: Statutory hostile-environment sufficiency

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory sufficiency question under the verdict-protective standard.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A rational jury could rely on the repeated sex-derogatory columns distributed to more than seven hundred colleagues, the links to DeAngelis, the recurring comments and workplace incidents, the evidence of humiliation and lost authority, promotion deterrence, and the membership's refusal to identify the columnist. Harris makes neither physical threat, supervisor status, measurable performance decline, nor the written medium indispensable.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Viewing the evidence favorably to the verdict, a rational jury could find the Association's publications severe or pervasive under Harris. The Fifth Circuit's contrary reweighing and any indispensable work-performance requirement are rejected.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Harris requires the complete circumstances, not a comparison with a single model of harassment. The institutional distribution, duration, repeated sex-based content, workplace audience, and claimed professional effects permit the statutory inference. That conclusion makes the First Amendment question necessary because the verdict rests on the Association's own publication.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The statutory ruling does not make every insult, offensive opinion, or isolated article actionable and does not resolve the separate constitutional limit.

### Issue 2: First Amendment limit on publication-based liability

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional question because statutory law otherwise permits liability on this record.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Title VII damages may rest solely on the protected viewpoint of opinion or satire published in the Association's newsletter.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Civil damages imposed solely because protected editorial opinion or satire expresses a sex-derogatory viewpoint are content based. Title VII may regulate discriminatory decisions, quid pro quo demands, true threats, authority-backed directives, and speech integral to or used as evidence of independently unlawful employment conduct. Here, however, the publication itself is the only independently asserted wrong, and the judgment is not narrowly tailored to the compelling interest in eradicating sex discrimination.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VII damages may not rest solely on protected opinion or satire in an association newsletter without an unprotected category or independently discriminatory employment conduct to which the expression is integral or evidentiary. The hostile-environment judgment therefore cannot stand.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The record permits the statutory hostile-environment inference, but constitutional review asks what the damages judgment punishes. It punishes the newsletter's viewpoint without a separate discriminatory employment act, threat, directive, or denial of employment or associational rights. The First Amendment therefore supplies the alternative ground for affirming judgment for the Association.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not immunize discriminatory employment decisions, quid pro quo demands, true threats, authority-backed directives, or evidentiary use of expression to prove independently unlawful conduct.

### Issue 3: Retaliation

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved sufficiency question.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The complaint-related publications and the conditional statement that the Association might sue if the action proved groundless did not deny membership, representation, pay, promotion, assignment, authority, or another material employment or associational advantage.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The retaliation claim fails because the record establishes no material employment or membership disadvantage caused by DeAngelis's protected complaint.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Hostile references and litigation rhetoric may show opposition to the complaint, but the retaliation provision requires a cognizable adverse consequence. None was proved on the verdict record.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not define every actionable retaliation as an ultimate employment decision or foreclose liability for threats, harassment, or public shaming that materially deter protected activity on a stronger record.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved First Amendment ground is a proposed application beyond Harris, not an existing Harris exception. He first accepts that the jury could find statutory hostility, then explains why the particular award, on his characterization of the publication and absence of an independently unlawful directive or act, impermissibly punishes protected expression. That characterization must not be generalized into a rule that speech can never itself constitute actionable workplace harassment.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved answer.** The evidence permits a rational severe-or-pervasive finding under Harris; Stone would reject appellate reweighing or an indispensable performance-decline rule.

**Question 2 — preserved approved answer.** On the source’s independently expressive-newsletter premise, Stone would sustain judgment for the Association because liability rests solely on protected opinion/satire rather than an unprotected category or expression integral to independently discriminatory action. He would not extend that answer to threats, discriminatory employment decisions, coercive authority or a different record. The statutory answer and constitutional limit therefore remain distinct.

**Question 3 — preserved approved answer.** The supplied record lacks a material employment or membership disadvantage caused by the complaint; disparagement and the contingent threat of a groundless-suit action do not establish that missing element here. Stone would affirm for this statutory reason without announcing that publication can never implement actionable retaliation.

### Issue 1: Acquitted conduct in Guidelines sentencing

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the categorical legal rule. He does not find the underlying conduct himself or decide any ordinary evidentiary objection not resolved below.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a general acquittal categorically prevents a sentencing court from considering the underlying conduct under the lower burden governing sentencing facts.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A general acquittal establishes that the prosecution failed to prove the charged offense beyond a reasonable doubt. It does not establish that every underlying fact was false or unavailable for every later purpose. Section 3661 and the Guidelines permit a sentencing court to consider reliable relevant conduct proved by a preponderance when the resulting sentence remains within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. Gaudin remains fully operative for elements; sentencing may not be used to remove an element from the jury or exceed the conviction-authorized range. Stone adds no special findings mandate and no separate acquittal-preclusion rule.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An acquittal does not bar a sentencing court from considering the underlying conduct proved by a preponderance when the resulting sentence remains within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. Ordinary evidentiary and sentencing procedures continue to govern the finding.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Ninth Circuit treated acquittal as a categorical factual determination, but a general verdict may rest on any failure of proof required for the offense. Witte and Dowling permit the same evidence to carry different consequences under a different burden and legal question. The constitutional jury guarantee remains protected by keeping elements and conviction-authorized punishment with the jury, not by disabling ordinary sentencing factfinding within that range.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit accusation to substitute for evidence, authorize punishment beyond the conviction-authorized range, remove an element from the jury, or excuse noncompliance with Rule 32 and the Guidelines.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved position extends Witte’s characterization from uncharged to acquitted conduct and accepts preponderance factfinding within the punishment legally authorized by the conviction. Witte’s separate conditional §5G1.3 sentence-coordination holding supplies no automatic aggregate cap or double-counting remedy here.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Vacate the conviction and remand for a new trial. At retrial the Government may prove the status element through Old Chief's admission or another sanitized means and may introduce the prior offense's details only if a distinct lawful purpose arises and Rule 403 is satisfied.

### Issue 1: Rule 403 treatment of a conceded status element

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The issue was preserved and the judgment is final.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an equally conclusive admission changes the Rule 403 balance when the only disputed proof concerns legal status.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 403 and holds that available evidentiary alternatives matter when they prove exactly the same discrete proposition. The prosecution ordinarily controls the narrative of its case and need not accept a defendant's preferred stipulation. That principle has limited force when the element is a formal status, the admission conclusively supplies it, and the detailed prior offense adds no narrative link to the charged conduct. In that setting the incremental probative value is negligible and the status-propensity risk is acute.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a defendant offers an unconditional admission to the complete prior-felony status element and the Government identifies no other permissible purpose, Rule 403 requires exclusion of the prior judgment's name and nature if their incremental probative value is substantially outweighed by the danger of unfair propensity prejudice.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant comparison is not between some evidence and none. It is between two means of proving the same legal status. Old Chief's admission would have allowed an instruction establishing the element beyond dispute. The assault judgment added vivid information likely to invite judgment about character while doing no additional work on possession. The district court therefore exercised discretion under an erroneous conception of probative value. Because the prior offense closely resembled violent conduct and the Government cannot show the error harmless on this record, a new trial is required.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not let defendants stipulate away the narrative of a charged event, require acceptance of partial or qualified admissions, or govern prior acts offered for a genuinely disputed nonpropensity purpose.

The judgment rests on Part A of Issue 1 and does not depend on the standard of review stated in Part B, which is written to be separable.

### Issue 1: Equal protection and the military discharge rule

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the classification and the error in the lower courts' treatment of it. He does not finally decide whether the rule survives on the incomplete preliminary record.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** What equality inquiry governs a military rule mandating separation specifically for homosexual acts, and how military judgment operates within it.

**Part A: the classification, the burden, and the inquiry**

- **Stone's Legal Position:** A rule selecting homosexual acts as the mandatory basis for separation intentionally sorts by sexual orientation, whether described as conduct or as a status proxy, and Bowers does not exempt it from equal-protection review. Romer supplies the rational-basis floor and a warning against unexplained disadvantage to a class, and it does not itself place a burden on the Government. Stone's own inquiry requires the Government to show a real relationship between the rule and an actual military objective, without reliance on moral disapproval, private bias, or stereotype. Professional military judgments and reasonable contemporaneous predictions receive substantial weight on discipline, cohesion, readiness, and command, but deference does not carry the Government's burden by assertion.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A military rule mandating separation for homosexual acts classifies by sexual orientation and must be justified by a real relationship to an actual military objective, with substantial but nonconclusive weight given to professional military judgment. Applying his proposed allocation of the justification burden to this express classification, Stone would vacate and remand the equal-protection component because the lower courts treated the claim as foreclosed, placed the practical burden on Walmer to disprove the rule, and did not resolve the military predicate his proposed inquiry requires.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Under Stone's proposed Government justification requirement, the lower courts' allocation cannot stand. Identifying the classification alone did not shift that burden under Romer's existing rational-basis rule; Stone is proposing the change explained below. The Court cannot replace the missing military record with its own judgment or convert an interlocutory appeal into final adjudication. Remand permits the Government to make the showing the governing inquiry requires and permits the ordinary preliminary-injunction factors to be applied to that record.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide that all military regulation of sexual conduct is invalid, overrule Bowers, constitutionalize a right to serve, or substitute unsupported judicial prediction for professional military judgment.

**Part B (separable): the standard of review**

- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone openly assigns intermediate scrutiny to this classification. The Government must identify an actual important military objective and establish a substantial relationship to it, with substantial weight to supported military judgment. This is an express proposed doctrinal step and is not imported from Romer or from the public-university sex cases.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The classification receives intermediate scrutiny. Part B is an additional, separable proposed ground; Part A independently supplies Stone's proposed judgment and remand.

### Issue 2: Bill of Attainder Clause

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves the institutional threshold and does not reach the punishment element.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Article I's Bill of Attainder Clause restrains legislative punishment. AR 635-100 is an executive military regulation. Any executive abuse remains reviewable under equal protection, due process, statute, and ordinary administrative law, but not under the Bill of Attainder Clause.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Bill of Attainder Clause does not govern an executive military regulation, so the punishment element is not reached.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Clause's institutional allocation is dispositive. Recharacterizing executive regulation as legislative punishment would detach the prohibition from the branch-specific act the Constitution identifies and is unnecessary because other legal sources remain available.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ruling does not insulate an executive regulation from equal-protection, due-process, statutory, or administrative review.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone proposes two distinct doctrinal changes: Part A places the justification burden on the Government for the challenged intentional sexual-orientation classification; Part B additionally assigns intermediate scrutiny. Part A remains the independent ground for his proposed vacatur and remand. Separating Part B does not turn Part A's changed burden into received rational-basis law.

**Prior rule and Stone's earlier position.** Simulated Romer, which Stone joined, applies ordinary rational-basis review to Amendment 2's combined withdrawal and foreclosure of ordinary protection. It permits reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds, supported predictions and imperfect classifications without requiring formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone does not invalidate a rule. Beach and Heller retain those ordinary-review limits at their actual scopes. Romer did not assign a sexual-orientation tier, and Kelley's actual-important-objective requirement concerns sex classifications. Neither decision already supplies Part A's Government burden or Part B's tier.

**Legal reason for the proposed changes.** Stone concludes that an express or deliberate-proxy rule making homosexual acts a mandatory ground for separation requires public justification tied to military function. His reason is the equal-protection guarantee against intentional class disadvantage justified by stereotype or private hostility: in this setting, accepting a merely conceivable explanation would allow the classification to operate without the Government establishing its actual relationship to the military objective invoked. He therefore requires the Government to identify the actual objective and establish the relationship in Part A. Part B states his additional conclusion that the classification requires an important objective and a substantial relationship. These are proposed adjustments of the received inquiry, not claims that Romer's outcome alone dictates them.

**New boundary and law retained.** The proposed rules concern the intentional orientation classification challenged here. Professional military judgments and reasonable contemporaneous predictions retain substantial but nonconclusive weight; experience and institutional knowledge may support the showing without scientific certainty or a universal demand for formal findings. Neither part requires the least restrictive means or an individualized exception in every case. Stone leaves Bowers's specified-conduct holding, the absence of a constitutional entitlement to military service, ordinary regulation of materially relevant conduct, and the separate preliminary-injunction requirements intact. He does not transfer Kelley's holding to a different classification by analogy alone.

**Reliance and disposition.** Any reliance on concrete personnel commitments or settled arrangements must be identified in the actual record; Stone makes no finding here that such commitments exist. Institutional preference, administrative convenience, command responsibilities in the abstract and an expectation of continued class exclusion do not establish protected reliance. Concrete commitments, if shown, receive the weight warranted by the record and governing remedial law, without creating a new transition requirement. Stone does not infer that every military concern is unsupported from the absence of an individualized performance finding, and he does not order an injunction, final invalidation or immediate change in personnel status. The district court must assess the military showing under the proposed inquiry and apply every required preliminary-relief factor. Those limits preserve the approved disposition while allowing the Government to present the justification the proposed rule requires.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1-2.** The classification and Bowers are answered in Part A. Stone openly proposes Part A's Government justification burden and Part B's intermediate scrutiny, giving supported professional military judgments substantial but nonconclusive weight. Romer adopted neither change. Under Part A's proposed allocation, he would vacate the equal-protection component and remand for the actual justification and fit inquiry together with all preliminary-relief requirements; he would neither order an injunction nor finally invalidate the regulation on this record. Part B remains a separable additional proposed ground.

**Question 3.** The challenged rule is executive rather than a legislative attainder, so Stone would affirm rejection of that claim without reaching the punishment element. This does not decide every possible due-process or statutory objection to the discharge.

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory and federal-common-law merits. He does not decide whether Atherton was negligent or whether New Jersey law ultimately imposes liability.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether federal chartering and insurance justify a judge-made uniform negligence rule, and what legal effect § 1821(k) gives state law.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies O'Melveny and the enacted text. Federal chartering, nationwide supervision, and receivership convenience do not themselves authorize federal common law. Section 1821(k) makes gross negligence actionable under federal law and expressly preserves liability under other applicable law. The best reading is a federal floor, not a ceiling: state law may impose ordinary negligence or another stricter standard, while a State may not reduce liability below the federal gross-negligence minimum.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** State law governs the ordinary standard of care for officers and directors of a federally chartered savings association unless a specific federal rule displaces it. Section 1821(k) establishes gross negligence as a minimum federal basis of liability and preserves stricter applicable state law.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The asserted duties concern lending judgment, fiduciary conduct, and corporate governance, fields traditionally supplied by state law. The FDIC identifies no operational conflict that requires a federal ordinary-negligence rule. Section 1821(k) does not merely mention gross negligence; its preservation clause confirms that other law continues to operate. Reading the section as a ceiling would erase that clause and create the broad preemption Congress did not write. The lower court therefore used the wrong source of law.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent Congress or a valid federal regulation from supplying a specific duty, decide the standard for federal enforcement actions resting on distinct statutory obligations, or resolve New Jersey's business-judgment rule.
