## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Lawyer v. Department of Justice
* **Citation or Docket:** 521 U.S. 567 (1997); No. 95-2024
* **October Term:** 1996
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued direct appeal from a three-judge district court approving a settlement-based Florida legislative redistricting plan over one plaintiff’s objection.
* **Simulated Event Date:** June 25, 1997 (historical)
* **Question Presented:** Could the District Court approve the State-supported settlement plan without first adjudicating the old plan unconstitutional, notwithstanding one plaintiff’s objection, and was the finding that race did not predominate in the new district clearly erroneous?
* **Question Granted:** Whether approval of Plan 386 respected state primacy, settlement limits, and equal-protection districting doctrine.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** A three-judge District Court approved Plan 386 after a fairness hearing, rejected the objector’s demand for a liability judgment against Plan 330, and found that traditional districting principles rather than race explained the new District 21. Lawyer v. Department of Justice, 920 F. Supp. 1248 (M.D. Fla. 1995).
* **Relief Requested:** Lawyer seeks reversal, adjudication of the original plan, and rejection of Plan 386 as a racial gerrymander. The state and federal appellees seek affirmance of the settlement and remedial plan.
* **Material Record:** After the 1990 census, Florida’s original Senate plan encountered § 5 objections in covered counties. The Florida Supreme Court adopted Plan 330 after legislative impasse. Lawyer and other residents challenged District 21. All parties except Lawyer later agreed to Plan 386. Florida’s Attorney General proposed the settlement, counsel for both legislative chambers participated, and the political branches continued to decline a special session. The District Court heard Lawyer’s objections and found that county boundaries, geography, communities, incumbency, and other traditional considerations explained Plan 386; race did not materially subordinate them.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court reviews state opportunity, authority to settle, effect on a nonsettling plaintiff, and the clear-error status of the predominance finding separately. Settlement cannot impose obligations on a nonconsenting party or insulate an unconstitutional plan, but it may supply lawful relief without an unnecessary liability adjudication.
* **Relevant Dates:** The District Court approved the settlement in 1995; argument occurred February 19, 1997; the Supreme Court event date is June 25, 1997.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None identified as a separate matter requiring adjudication.

### Issue 1: State primacy and settlement before liability adjudication

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Relief requires a lawful basis and respect for state choices; actual Miller preserves otherwise lawful voluntary inclusion without requiring federal compulsion, while an imposed remedy must fit the established wrong. Prepared Abrams supplies no holding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Give the State a genuine opportunity to choose a lawful redistricting remedy. A settlement may resolve a disputed plan without a formal liability judgment when authorized state representatives select it, the court independently reviews legality, and a nonsettling party retains the right to challenge the replacement plan.

### Issue 2: Racial predominance in Plan 386

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Actual simulated Shaw, Miller/Johnson and Vera reject predominance alone as the asserted constitutional injury or scrutiny trigger. Independent deprivations retain their own proof and remedial standards; districting-purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether race materially subordinated traditional districting principles. Racial awareness, demographic forecasting, and participation in a voting-rights settlement do not alone establish predominance.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Settlement authority and state opportunity

* **Reasoning Below:** The District Court found that Florida selected the settlement through authorized representatives and that Lawyer could not demand adjudication of a plan the State agreed to replace.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Only the legislature or state court could make the State’s districting choice, and a settlement over his objection could not eliminate his right to a liability judgment.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** The Attorney General and legislative counsel represented the State, the political branches deliberately declined to act formally, and the settlement gave Lawyer the core relief sought while preserving his challenge to the new plan.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** The Court may affirm only after confirming that the settlement neither imposes obligations on Lawyer nor forecloses review of Plan 386.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Settlement cannot authorize a court to enter an unconstitutional district or bind a nonparty beyond lawful adjudicative power.
### Issue 2: Predominance

* **Reasoning Below:** The District Court found race did not subordinate traditional criteria.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** District 21’s shape, crossing of water and county lines, and racial composition show race drove the design.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** Unrefuted evidence showed the boundaries followed traditional Florida practices and geography; no racial target displaced those considerations.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** If the findings are inadequate, remand for district-specific analysis rather than presume predominance from demographics.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** A settlement label does not immunize a cognizable constitutional or statutory wrong, but racial composition or sorting alone is not the independent deprivation required by the actual simulated territorial-design rule.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may affirm if Florida exercised its opportunity through authorized settlement participation, the decree did not extinguish Lawyer’s individual claim without review, and Plan 386 survives independent constitutional examination. On predominance, clear-error review applies to the District Court’s supported factual finding. Race predominance alone does not trigger the historical Shaw strict-scrutiny inquiry under the actual simulated rule. An independently cognizable deprivation must be addressed under its own law whether or not predominance is established.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Reconciled entering-law application — simulated territorial-design rule

The completed simulated Shaw, Miller/Johnson (June 29, 1995), and Vera (June 13, 1996, corrected October 7, 2026) reject racial predominance alone as the asserted equal-protection deprivation or automatic strict-scrutiny trigger in a shared territorial electorate. An independently cognizable deprivation—exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, inferior civic status or another recognized wrong—retains its own elements; it need not arise from another governmental act or later loss. Miller distinguishes lawful voluntary state inclusion from federally compelled remedies and imposes no maximization duty; it affirmed judgment for Georgia and denial of relief, without a redrawing order. Vera reversed the sole design-only invalidations and dependent relief. Miller preserves ordinary clear-error review of actual districting-purpose findings while separating their legal consequence. Here Lawyer's shape, county-line and racial-composition evidence may bear on a legally cognizable claim but cannot alone revive the historical Shaw predominance theory. State-authorized settlement and any voluntary replacement still require lawful judicial authority and protection of nonconsenting parties; statutory §§2 and 5 claims keep their independent channels and predicates. No completed Abrams holding is identified; the completed Bossier decision is incorporated in the dated preflight supplement below.
