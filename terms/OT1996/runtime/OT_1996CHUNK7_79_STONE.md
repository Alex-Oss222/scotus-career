### Issue 1: Governmental use of identified medical information

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional standard and applies it under Rule 50's verdict-protective posture. He does not decide the ungranted liability and remedy questions identified above.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether access to and internal use of identified prescription information must remain proportionately connected to the actual public function asserted, and whether the evidence permitted the jury's finding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Identifiable prescription records, especially records revealing HIV status, fall within the Fourteenth Amendment's limited protection against unjustified governmental acquisition, use, and disclosure of highly intimate information. Stone’s proposed application beyond Sheets’s decided disclosure setting asks whether personally identifying access is genuinely and proportionately connected to the public function asserted, considering sensitivity, the need for identifiers rather than aggregate or coded data, who receives access, and the safeguards actually in place. SEPTA's interest justified utilization monitoring, but it did not establish justification as a matter of law for continued identified review.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Governmental access to and internal use of identified prescription information must remain proportionately connected to an actual public function, with access and safeguards fitted to the information's sensitivity. The evidence permitted the jury to find that SEPTA exceeded that need when it continued to use Doe's identified prescription record beyond a function that required only de-identified utilization data.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** A reasonable jury could find that employee names were unnecessary for the relevant utilization monitoring, that Pierce deliberately examined and researched the identified prescriptions after their sensitivity became apparent, and that no established safeguard limited unnecessary identified review. The Third Circuit could not replace those verdict-supported inferences with its own characterization of the intrusion as minimal.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not bar aggregate or coded auditing, identified review after a concrete fraud or eligibility concern, disclosures required by law, or confidential retention alone. It does not decide damages, personal immunity, municipal liability, or private-employer duties.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved proposed extension.** Stone would recognize a limited constitutional interest in identified prescription and HIV information, while acknowledging that Sheets and Hodge did not already decide this employer setting.

**Question 2 — preserved approved answer.** Fiscal oversight is legitimate but must explain identified rather than aggregate or coded review and the actual access allowed. Absence of consent is not automatically dispositive, and a legitimate program does not justify every identified use.

**Question 3 — preserved approved answer.** Internal circulation can exceed the function and safeguards even without public dissemination; on the verdict-favorable record the jury could find such an excess. Stone would reverse the Rule 50 direction and remand unresolved Rule 59/damages issues, not establish personal immunity, municipal liability or a universal prohibition on benefits monitoring.
