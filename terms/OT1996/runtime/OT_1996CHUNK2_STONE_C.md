## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The court of appeals shall direct application of New Jersey law subject to § 1821(k)'s federal gross-negligence floor. The lower courts shall decide breach, causation, defenses, and remedy under the correct sources.

### Issue 1: Governing standard of care

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory and federal-common-law merits. He does not decide whether Atherton was negligent or whether New Jersey law ultimately imposes liability.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether federal chartering and insurance justify a judge-made uniform negligence rule, and what legal effect § 1821(k) gives state law.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies O'Melveny and the enacted text. Federal chartering, nationwide supervision, and receivership convenience do not themselves authorize federal common law. Section 1821(k) makes gross negligence actionable under federal law and expressly preserves liability under other applicable law. The best reading is a federal floor, not a ceiling: state law may impose ordinary negligence or another stricter standard, while a State may not reduce liability below the federal gross-negligence minimum.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** State law governs the ordinary standard of care for officers and directors of a federally chartered savings association unless a specific federal rule displaces it. Section 1821(k) establishes gross negligence as a minimum federal basis of liability and preserves stricter applicable state law.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The asserted duties concern lending judgment, fiduciary conduct, and corporate governance, fields traditionally supplied by state law. The FDIC identifies no operational conflict that requires a federal ordinary-negligence rule. Section 1821(k) does not merely mention gross negligence; its preservation clause confirms that other law continues to operate. Reading the section as a ceiling would erase that clause and create the broad preemption Congress did not write. The lower court therefore used the wrong source of law.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent Congress or a valid federal regulation from supplying a specific duty, decide the standard for federal enforcement actions resting on distinct statutory obligations, or resolve New Jersey's business-judgment rule.

## A. APPROVED POSITION AND DISPOSITION

Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. Leave the Ninth Circuit judgment undisturbed without adopting its RICO accrual rule or deciding the alternative new-injury and tolling theories. This is a discretionary decision about the vehicle, not a jurisdictional dismissal or a finding that the lower court failed to address the issues.

## B. REASON FOR THE LIMITED DISPOSITION

The May1989 bankruptcy complaint already alleged the loss of Siragusa's interest; the November1994 RICO complaint relied on asserted discovery of a wider pattern in December1990, later injuries, concealment, and the bankruptcy proceedings. The Ninth Circuit separately analyzed those routes. Stone does not replace that actual history with a supposed absence of lower-court analysis.

Stone retains his approved judgment that this combination is an unsuitable vehicle for selecting a universal civil RICO accrual trigger. A ruling on discovery of a pattern would not by itself settle the logically separate arguments about a later independent injury, reasonable diligence, or the effect of pursuing the bankruptcy claim. Resolving the case broadly would require selecting and applying those additional rules on a record assembled around several distinct theories. He chooses to discharge the writ rather than use it to formulate an omnibus limitations rule. Complexity does not deprive the Court of authority or require dismissal in every case; this is his confined exercise of certiorari discretion.

## C. QUESTIONS LEFT OPEN

No merits answer is rendered on injury discovery versus injury-and-pattern discovery, separate accrual, fraudulent concealment, or bankruptcy-related tolling. The four-year period supplied by Agency Holding remains intact. The later prepared Klehr packet is not law and supplies neither the reason nor authority for this dismissal. The historical Court's unexplained DIG also supplies no reason of its own; the vehicle assessment above is Stone's approved reasoning.

## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 1.** Propose recognizing a confined common-law civil action for this established prohibition on personally committed official torture. Section1350 is jurisdictional in wording; Stone should not misdescribe it as an express damages cause. His proposed construction treats Congress's express reception of law-of-nations torts in §1350, read against the statute's common-law adjudicative setting, as authority to recognize this narrow received action. The obligatory international prohibition supplies the substantive rule; it does not alone supply domestic remedial authority. This statutory construction is Stone's answer to the source-of-action objection, limited to determinate norms of the kind that jurisdiction was designed to receive. This requires evidence of a specific, obligatory prohibition, rather than a judge's preferred human-rights policy. The findings of deliberate official torture meet that proposed boundary. Suter's source/action distinction remains intact; Central Bank's received-action reasoning is an analogy, not an ATS holding.

**Question 2.** Propose compensatory and punitive relief for the proved intentional personal tort, grounded in that confined common-law action and its ordinary intentional-tort remedies. Franklin confirms that a recognized action's remedies must be analyzed rather than assumed unavailable, but does not settle punitive authority. The proposed punitive authorization is therefore an express additional answer, not inherited law. Deliberate cruelty supports a punitive category; any preserved objection to amount remains governed by its own record and law. No universal punitive remedy for every international wrong follows.

**Question 3.** Reject categorical political-question dismissal. Applying a determinate torture prohibition to individual acts neither decides which government should rule Ethiopia nor directs United States diplomacy. A concrete immunity or contrary political-branch determination would require its own consideration; none is invented.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the judgment on the three selected questions, with the narrow common-law and remedy rationale stated above. Preserve ordinary jurisdictional, personal-responsibility and amount-of-damages limits; do not reopen forfeited limitations or declare that all customary international law creates private actions. The user approved this as Stone's position on October 11, 2026; the proposed ATS rule remains for the Court to consider and is not an inherited Court holding.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for application of the payroll method.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Count each person on every working day during which a genuine employment relationship existed. If the threshold is met, the lower courts may proceed to the unresolved merits.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Employment relationship, not daily labor, controls the count

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the counting rule because it is the sole basis for dismissal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an employer counts an individual as an employee for each working day on which an employment relationship exists and the individual appears on the payroll, or only on days the individual is actually working or paid.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the statutory text to the legal employment relationship and use payroll records as the ordinary administrable evidence of that relationship.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For Title VII’s fifteen-employee threshold, an individual counts on each working day within the period of an existing employment relationship, whether or not the individual worked or was paid that day.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute asks whether the employer “has” employees, not how many labor units it purchased each day. The payroll method treats full-time, part-time, leave, and variable schedules coherently and is anchored in records employers already maintain. It does not count a person before hiring or after termination.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether every listed individual was in fact an employee, whether the threshold is jurisdictional, or the discrimination claim.
