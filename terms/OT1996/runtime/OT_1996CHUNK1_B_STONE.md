## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required. The later IEP remains in force, and Tyler's future IEPs remain subject to IDEA's individualized procedures, integration command, proximity regulation, and ordinary review.

### Issue 1: Neighborhood-school presumption under IDEA

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the preserved legal question. He treats the old placement dispute and the request for post-hearing evidence about that superseded IEP as moot. He does not decide unexhausted supplementary-aid theories or the factual appropriateness of Northside.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statute establishes a categorical or rebuttable neighborhood-school presumption apart from its integration mandate.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the enacted distinction between education with nondisabled children and geographic proximity. IDEA strongly prefers education with nondisabled peers to the maximum extent appropriate and requires placement as close to home as practicable, but the text does not make the neighborhood building the presumptive legal endpoint. Proximity is a mandatory placement consideration under the regulations, not a burden-shifting rule that overrides an individualized IEP when another school is needed to deliver the required education and integration.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** IDEA creates no independent rebuttable presumption that the least restrictive environment is the neighborhood school. The responsible team must consider proximity and ordinarily use the neighborhood school when the IEP can be implemented there, but the controlling statutory question remains the child's individualized education with nondisabled children to the maximum extent appropriate.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory command identifies the form of segregation Congress sought to reduce: removal from regular education with nondisabled children. It does not equate every transfer between public schools with increased restriction. The regulations make closeness to home and the school the child would otherwise attend important operational constraints, but they expressly permit a different placement when the IEP requires it. Tyler's later IEP kept him at Olathe, so the Court has no live record on which to choose between Olathe and Northside or to prescribe aids. The only enduring dispute is the proposed legal presumption, which the statutory structure does not support.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit convenience-based transfers, diminish the duty to use supplementary aids before removing a child from regular classes, or make travel distance irrelevant. It resolves no claim that a particular placement denied Tyler a free appropriate public education.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved answer.** No independent burden-shifting neighborhood-school presumption follows from IDEA’s LRE requirement. Proximity and the regulatory neighborhood default remain mandatory considerations; a team cannot ignore them merely because centralization is convenient.

**Question 2 — clarification proposed to the Court and user-approved.** A different building is permissible when the individualized IEP actually requires services there and the applicable proximity and integration requirements are met. A district’s assertion that its specialized program is better does not prove those requirements. Because the disputed regular/special-class mix was accepted and the later IEP placed Tyler at Olathe, Stone would not decide an obsolete individualized placement question or bless every specialized placement.

**Question 3 — newly explicit answer, proposed to the Court and user-approved.** Stone would recognize the statutory additional-evidence channel rather than restrict review categorically to the agency record. Relevant subsequent progress can inform a live dispute, with its temporal relevance explained; the court must give due weight without surrendering independent legal judgment. Here he would order no new evidence: the aids proffer does not cure the failed geographic-presumption theory and the later IEP removes the requested placement/progress remedy. He preserves the approved affirmance and declines to resolve the full inter-circuit evidentiary disagreement unnecessarily.

## A. APPROVAL AND METHOD

The user approves these answers, reasoning and conditional limits as Stone’s proposed position. The Master distinguishes personal aesthetic use from ideological disagreement and requires separate proof of causation and each remedy’s likely benefit. ## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

**Question 1 — a particular animal can be the subject of concrete aesthetic injury.** Stone answers yes where established observation, study or other personal use is interrupted. Neither ownership, a scientific profession nor species-wide depletion is required. Another elephant need not substitute for a continuing study of this one. He rejects a categorical denial merely because three Asian elephants remain. That follows Lujan’s preservation of aesthetic and professional use, not a judicial right to choose an animal’s custodian.

These affidavits do not establish every fact the question assumes. Stone would not transform occasional visits over many years into a continuing study, invent return plans or treat a summary of concern as proof of each member’s activity. Local residence can support an inference together with a proved practice; it does not compel one here. He would assume a cognizable observational injury for the independently decisive causal and remedial inquiry rather than unnecessarily settle injury’s outer boundary.

**Question 2 — permission can cause injury through predictable private conduct, but this permission did not cause the asserted loss.** Stone rejects a compulsion-only rule. Removing a legal barrier can predictably enable harmful conduct, and traceability does not require a government command. The Master permits supported institutional and market inferences for protected beneficiaries as for regulated parties. Here, however, Lota had already left Milwaukee before the challenged amendment. Without it she could remain at Hawthorn’s Illinois premises. Suspending it neither annuls the donation nor makes renewed zoo access substantially likely. The waived transfer count cannot conceal that gap. Even assuming injury, this record fails the causal and redressability requirements.

**Question 3 — a procedural right needs a concrete protected interest and the relevant connection.** Stone requires no proof that correct process would certainly change the agency’s result. His Master treats invasion of an individually held right as injury when the governing source supplies that right and remedy, but expressly rejects procedure alone. No individual entitlement to Lota’s residence at Milwaukee or separate informational right is established here. Assuming §1539 provides the asserted procedure, its use cannot redress an unrelated completed transfer merely because the Society favors conservation. Lujan’s controlling rule and Stone’s own commitment yield this limited result.

Brown Group does not eliminate the injured-member requirement, and Church of Scientology does not make a constraint on Hawthorn beneficial to these members by definition. Stone would not adjudicate the commercial-activity interpretation after finding no standing. His nonjoin in Sweet Home’s separate deference rationale does not diminish that rationale’s controlling status or change the threshold result.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the D.C. Circuit judgment vacating the district court’s merits judgment and directing dismissal for want of Article III jurisdiction. Rest on the missing causal and remedial connection while recognizing that interference with proved personal observation of a particular animal can qualify as injury. Decide no commercial-activity merits question and order no custody transfer, damages or permit procedure. The dismissal imposes no merits preclusion on a genuinely different claim with adequate standing; it does not reopen the waived transfer challenge or authorize manufacture of missing evidence.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and direct reinstatement of the District Court's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Sixth Circuit shall reinstate the federal jury judgment. The disposition does not erase the earlier violation of the removal and remand statutes or foreclose any independently authorized fee or sanction remedy arising from that violation.

### Issue 1: Effect of a removal defect cured before trial

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory consequence because Lewis timely sought remand, complete diversity later arose lawfully before trial, and subject-matter jurisdiction remained complete through judgment.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the erroneous refusal to remand requires destruction of a completed diversity judgment when jurisdiction existed before any merits trial and through final judgment.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1447 required remand while the nondiverse party remained, and the District Court erred by refusing Lewis's timely motion. That error did not eliminate the subject-matter jurisdiction supplied by § 1332 after the nondiverse party left through an ordinary, noncollusive settlement before trial. Finn and Grubbs do not require vacatur of a judgment rendered with jurisdiction merely to punish an earlier statutory error. The remand duty and any authorized sanction remain separate from the validity of the completed trial.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An erroneous refusal to remand does not invalidate a later diversity judgment when complete diversity arises lawfully and noncollusively before trial and remains through judgment. The district court must still remand while the defect exists.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Lewis timely invoked the remand statute, so the District Court's initial ruling was wrong. Before trial, however, the nondiverse party was dismissed through an ordinary settlement, complete diversity arose, and the federal court thereafter possessed adjudicatory authority throughout the merits proceeding. Requiring a second trial would not enforce a jurisdictional limit that remained absent at judgment; it would convert the earlier procedural violation into a judgment-destroying sanction Congress did not supply.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Finality does not cure absent subject-matter jurisdiction, collusive manufacture of diversity, or improper manipulation, and it does not extinguish any separately authorized fee or sanction request.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Willy supports keeping the erroneous remand refusal, any independently authorized sanction, and the later merits jurisdiction analytically distinct. Stone’s approved reinstatement rests on the actual pretrial cure described in the record, not on a power to excuse continuing jurisdictional absence for efficiency.
