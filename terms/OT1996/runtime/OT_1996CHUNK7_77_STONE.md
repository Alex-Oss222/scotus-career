## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The tribal court lacks jurisdiction over this nonmember highway accident

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the federal jurisdictional boundary because exhaustion is complete and it controls the requested injunction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a tribal court has civil adjudicatory jurisdiction over a personal-injury action arising from an accident between nonmembers on a North Dakota public highway maintained under a federally granted right-of-way through reservation land.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Montana and the retained-sovereignty cases narrowly and source by source. Territorial location inside reservation boundaries is relevant but not alone dispositive.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Absent treaty or statutory authorization, a tribal court may not adjudicate a tort action against nonmembers arising from a nonmember-on-nonmember accident on a state-maintained public highway held under a federal right-of-way, when the accident is unrelated to any consensual tribal relationship and does not directly threaten tribal political integrity, economic security, or health or welfare.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State’s public right-of-way opens the road to general traffic and removes the conduct at issue from the Tribe’s ordinary internal governance. A-1’s separate subcontract with a tribal corporation does not constitute consent to tribal regulation of every unrelated activity, and the victim’s family relationships do not change the nonmember conduct being adjudicated. Montana’s second exception preserves what is necessary to tribal self-government; it cannot become a general reservation tort power without erasing the rule it qualifies.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide jurisdiction over members, accidents on tribal or closed internal roads, claims arising directly from a consensual tribal transaction, explicit forum consent, treaty-based authority, or nonmember conduct posing a direct and substantial threat to tribal self-government.
