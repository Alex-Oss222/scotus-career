## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The authorized settlement respected state primacy and Lawyer’s rights

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the settlement objections because they are threshold challenges to the decree.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Could the District Court approve the State-supported settlement plan without first adjudicating the old plan unconstitutional, notwithstanding one plaintiff’s objection, and was the finding that race did not predominate in the new district clearly erroneous?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply state primacy as an allocation of remedial choice, not a requirement that the State use one formal organ after it has authorized litigation representatives and legislative parties to settle.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal court may approve a State-supported redistricting settlement without first adjudicating the superseded plan unconstitutional when authorized state representatives have selected the replacement, the State has had a genuine opportunity to act, the settlement imposes no unlawful obligation on a nonsettling plaintiff, and the court independently considers the new plan’s legality.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Florida did not lose its remedial opportunity; it exercised it through the Attorney General with counsel for both legislative chambers, after the political branches chose not to convene. Lawyer sought elimination of Plan 330 and received that relief. He retained a full hearing and appeal on Plan 386, so the settlement did not purchase immunity from his constitutional claim. A liability declaration about a discarded plan was unnecessary to the disposition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not approve unauthorized executive redistricting, consent decrees binding objectors to new duties, or settlements that evade proof of a still-operative violation.

### Issue 2: Race did not predominate on the supported findings

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the equal-protection challenge because Plan 386 remains operative.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Could the District Court approve the State-supported settlement plan without first adjudicating the old plan unconstitutional, notwithstanding one plaintiff’s objection, and was the finding that race did not predominate in the new district clearly erroneous?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply simulated Miller's separation of districting-purpose facts from constitutional deprivation, together with ordinary clear-error review.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A district is not race predominant merely because it crosses political boundaries, has an irregular shape, or contains a higher minority percentage than surrounding counties. The supported purpose finding remains, but under actual simulated Shaw, Miller and Vera, predominance alone would not establish this design-only constitutional claim even if proved. An independent deprivation would retain its own test.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lawyer’s indicators are relevant but not self-proving. Florida districts commonly cross water and county lines, and the trial record supplied nonracial explanations for the particular boundaries. No rule requires each district to mirror countywide racial percentages. Completed Miller and the distinct statutory voting cases supply no entitlement to racial maximization; the prepared OT1996 Abrams position adds no law. Florida's authorized choice to improve minority electoral opportunity also needs no federal compulsion: as all nine Justices held in Miller, a State's otherwise lawful consideration of racial conditions to improve electoral opportunity is not unconstitutional merely because no prior adjudication or federal statute required the precise district chosen.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not immunize any independently cognizable racial deprivation. Such a claim retains its own elements, burden, justification and remedy; predominance alone is insufficient under the actual simulated rule.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

### Corrected application to the proposed affirmance

Stone's proposed affirmance remains user-approved and now rests on the actual simulated law. Florida may select an otherwise lawful replacement through authorized settlement without proving that federal law demanded that exact map. Lawyer received an opportunity to contest Plan 386; an objector is not bound to new duties merely by other parties' consent. The supported finding that ordinary geography and districting practices explain the plan is accepted under clear-error review, but it is not indispensable to defeating a claim based solely on shared-electorate racial design: even proven predominance alone would not establish that claim under Shaw, Miller and Vera. The supplied challenge identifies no independent exclusion, dilution or unequal-participation injury; any preserved such claim would require its own adjudication. Stone therefore leaves Plan 386 effective on the presented record without imposing historical strict scrutiny, inventing a federal compulsion to create a district, or deciding unpresented statutory claims.
