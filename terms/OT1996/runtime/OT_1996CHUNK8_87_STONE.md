## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The discriminatory residency condition may not be enforced against the camp. The Maine courts shall provide the exemption and any refund or prospective relief authorized by state remedial law. Maine may replace the scheme with a nondiscriminatory charitable policy.

### Issue 1: Residency-based charitable tax exemption

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial operation of the residency condition. The camp's national recruitment and predominantly out-of-state clientele are undisputed.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the existing nondiscrimination rule. Maine conditions a valuable tax exemption on serving residents and withdraws it when the same charitable service is sold principally to nonresidents. That classification disadvantages interstate customers and the provider that attracts them. The State may support charity, but it can do so through resident benefits, direct public programs, general-revenue grants, or neutral exemption criteria.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Maine's charitable property-tax exemption violates the dormant Commerce Clause because it denies exemption based on the nonresident identity of the institution's principal beneficiaries and thereby burdens interstate commerce in charitable and recreational services.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The camp markets a service nationally and brings children across state lines. The tax rule raises the cost of that service precisely because the users are nonresidents. Calling the exemption a subsidy does not change the discriminatory condition attached to the tax system. Maine is not purchasing a service as a market participant, and it has not shown that resident assistance requires penalizing a charity serving outsiders. Reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives are evident.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require a State to subsidize charitable work everywhere, invalidate direct resident public benefits, or prevent neutral exemption criteria based on nonprofit status, use, finances, or regulatory compliance. It forbids conditioning a tax exemption on the out-of-state identity of the persons served.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved result rejects enforcement of the residency condition. His instruction to provide the exemption should be understood as the selected proposed remedy on the presented state-law scheme, not a general federal rule that every discriminatory exemption must be extended. Any refund still requires the proper state-law and due-process analysis; no amount is invented.
