# 13. O'Gilvie v. United States

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 6–3. The punitive damages were taxable; the Court also rejected the erroneous-refund limitations objection and the objection to the Government's appeal.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer wrote for the Chief Justice and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Justices O’Connor and Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** Former § 104(a)(2) did not exclude punitive damages. The two-year erroneous-refund period ran from the children's receipt of the checks, making the Government's suit timely. The Court accepted the Tenth Circuit's explanation that the operative final judgment made the Government's appeal timely.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; it is not simulated precedent.
* **Historical Sources:** [O'Gilvie v. United States, 519 U.S. 79](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/519/79/).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=249).

# 19. Old Chief v. United States

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice O'Connor dissented, joined by the Chief Justice, Scalia, and Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** When felony status is the only evidentiary purpose and the defendant offers to admit the full status element, Rule 403 requires the court to compare the offered record with the admission and exclude the name and nature of the offense when its added prejudice substantially outweighs its negligible added probative value.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Old Chief v. United States, 519 U.S. 172 (1997).

# 21. Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer delivered the Opinion of the Court. Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg joined in full. Justices O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas joined except to the extent the opinion relied on legislative history. Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, concurred in part and concurred in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** State law ordinarily governs the standard of care for officers and directors of federally chartered savings institutions. Section 1821(k) creates a federal gross-negligence floor and preserves stricter state standards; no special federal common-law ordinary-negligence rule is justified.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Atherton v. FDIC, 519 U.S. 213 (1997).



## Historical reasoning references preserved from the source

- **Other Authority Used:** The historical Atherton opinion is date-eligible persuasive authority on the interaction between § 1821(k) and preserved state standards.
