## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate both judgments and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The three-judge court shall keep Section 5 effect separate from Section 2, shall not exclude evidence from the purpose inquiry merely because it also supports a dilution theory, and shall determine on the existing record and any lawful supplementation whether the Board adopted the plan with a purpose to deny or abridge the right to vote on account of race. It shall apply the rule that this purpose is not limited to an intent to worsen minority voting strength compared with the existing plan. The Court does not decide whether the Board acted with prohibited purpose and does not create a same-decision burden or defense.

### Issue 1: Relationship between Section 5 effect and Section 2

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal relationship between the statutes but not whether the plan violates Section 2.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 5 effect remains a comparison with the lawful operative baseline. A Section 2 violation does not independently establish prohibited Section 5 effect, and preclearance does not immunize a plan from a separate Section 2 action.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Section 2 violation does not itself establish prohibited Section 5 effect, which remains a comparison with the lawful operative baseline.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Sections 2 and 5 protect distinct statutory injuries through different benchmarks and procedures. Treating Section 2 liability as automatic Section 5 effect would erase that enacted distinction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require proportional representation or maximization and does not decide a separate Section 2 action.

### Issue 2: Relevance of dilution evidence to discriminatory purpose

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the evidentiary error necessary to the disposition. The scope of the Section 5 purpose prong is decided separately in Issue 3, and any mixed-purpose causation or same-decision rule is reserved.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether evidence may be excluded from the Section 5 purpose inquiry solely because it also supports a Section 2 dilution theory.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Evidence of dilutive impact, rejected alternatives, sequence, procedural departures, and historical context may be relevant to why a jurisdiction selected a plan. It may not be categorically excluded merely because it also bears on Section 2. That conclusion does not establish prohibited purpose or alter the governing burdens and causation rules.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Evidence relevant to dilution may also be relevant to discriminatory purpose and may not be categorically excluded merely because it supports a Section 2 theory.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The court below treated the statutory labels as mutually exclusive evidentiary compartments. The same facts can bear on different legal questions without transferring the conclusion from one doctrine to another. Because the exclusion may have distorted the purpose inquiry, the preclearance judgment cannot stand.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not decide whether the plan was adopted with prohibited purpose or create an identical-plan same-decision defense or burden.

### Issue 3: The scope of the Section 5 purpose prong

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the scope of the purpose prong because it is within the question granted and because the remand cannot be given a legal standard without it. The three-judge court must know what kind of purpose it is looking for. Stone decides only that scope, not whether this Board acted with such a purpose.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Section 5's bar on a purpose to deny or abridge the right to vote on account of race is limited to a purpose to cause retrogression.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The text controls. Section 5 forbids preclearance of a change that has the purpose or will have the effect of denying or abridging the right to vote on account of race. The statute does not say a purpose to retrogress. Beer defines effect as retrogression against a benchmark. Purpose asks why the jurisdiction acted, and Stone reads the enacted prohibition as reaching a purpose to deny or abridge the vote on account of race even when that purpose does not include worsening the benchmark. The Court has treated discriminatory purpose as a requirement separate from retrogression. Miller preserves that distinction but does not itself decide the full scope of nonretrogressive-purpose liability. Stone's proposed construction prevents a jurisdiction from intentionally denying or abridging equal voting opportunity on account of race merely because it does not intend to make an existing arrangement worse. This construes the open statutory question and overrules nothing; it does not equate every consideration of race with prohibited purpose.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Section 5 bar on a purpose to deny or abridge the right to vote on account of race is not limited to a purpose to cause retrogression. A covered jurisdiction's adoption of a nonretrogressive plan with a discriminatory purpose cannot be precleared. Beer remains the rule for effect.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Board adopted the Police Jury's recently precleared plan with no majority-Black district, and the parties stipulate that it is not retrogressive. Nonretrogressive effect does not rule out an unsuccessful intent to cause retrogression. Stone's broader construction also reaches a proved purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race that does not include an intent to worsen the existing benchmark; stipulated nonretrogression therefore cannot resolve that separate purpose inquiry. On remand the three-judge court should weigh the evidence the lower court excluded, including the dilutive effect, the rejected alternative plan with two majority-Black districts, the sequence of decisions, departures from ordinary practice, and the history of discrimination in the parish. None of it is presumptive proof, and the court must find the Board's actual purpose. The existing allocation of the preclearance burden is not altered.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require maximization or proportional representation and does not treat a rejected feasible alternative as proof of purpose. It leaves Beer's rule for effect untouched and Section 2 independent. It does not decide a same-decision rule or any shift in the burden, and it does not decide whether this plan was adopted with a discriminatory purpose.
