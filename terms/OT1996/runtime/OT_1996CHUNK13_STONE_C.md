## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 1 — Yes, when delegated authority materially facilitates the harassment; supervisory status alone is insufficient.** Stone proposes this construction of Title VII's employer-and-agent language through Meritor's agency instruction. The second branch of Restatement §219(2)(d) concerns aid from the agency relationship and is not absorbed into scope of employment or permission to harass. Authority over assignments, discipline and access to an employee can make conduct possible or coercive in a manner that ordinary coworker proximity does not. Congress's protection of working conditions would be incomplete if an employer could delegate control over those conditions yet disclaim every abuse because it prohibited sexual misconduct.

This is a proposed clarification of Meritor's open attribution issue, consistent with Stone's approved Andrade answer, not a declaration that Andrade has become law. He preserves Meritor's limit by requiring a demonstrated connection between actual delegated power and the discriminatory conditions. Apparent authority separately requires manifestations attributable to the employer and reasonable reliance; the victim need not believe the employer lawfully authorized assault. Mere employment, a supervisor's unsupported claims or the parties' proximity does not establish either route.

**Question 2 — Higher-management knowledge is not an additional element of proved agency-based attribution.** It remains material to the independent negligence route. Otherwise attribution through a supervisor's power would collapse into notice-and-response negligence, depriving the agent language and agency-aided principle of their distinct work. Stone would reject the appellate requirement of a firing threat or completed adverse decision as the necessary evidence of aid. He would also reject equating Harris severity with management notice: Harris concerns the employee's conditions, while constructive notice asks what reasonable employer care would have discovered.

**Question 3 — Do not adopt the proposed freestanding affirmative defense once materially aided misuse of delegated authority has been proved.** Stone's answer is not automatic liability for all supervisory acts. Its boundary is the agency nexus in Question 1. Reasonable care can defeat a negligence theory; demonstrated facts may defeat apparent authority, causation or recoverable avoidable loss where the governing remedial law permits. They do not erase attribution already established through actual delegated power. Neither Title VII nor Meritor supplies a general immunity from that attributable discrimination merely because the victim failed to complain. Turning those matters into a new complete defense would alter the legal consequence of established agency responsibility; Stone rejects that additional rule on this presented question. This is his approved proposal, not an inherited holding and not the historical 1998 framework.

**Question 4 — No.** A written policy's actual operation matters to the legal ground for which it is offered. A policy never communicated to the relevant unit cannot establish that employees unreasonably failed to use its protections. Prompt correction after actual notice also does not retroactively erase earlier attributable misconduct. Conversely, the failed dissemination alone is not a finding of every element of a negligence claim, and Stone does not invent negligence per se.

## C. APPLICATION AND PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Propose vacating the Eleventh Circuit's Title VII judgment and remanding for application of the agency rule just stated to the existing bench findings, with further findings only if a material factual issue remains. The trial findings about concentrated command power and repeated conditions imposed by those commanders are substantial evidence of the relevant nexus. But its stated inference from being “agents” to liability does not explicitly distinguish actual facilitation from status alone. Stone does not silently convert that incomplete legal inquiry into a conclusive finding under his clarified rule. The appellate court's contrary insistence on a firing threat or adverse employment decision is equally insufficient.

If the existing findings, properly reviewed, establish material aid through delegated power, restore the one-dollar Title VII judgment without reopening the established hostile-environment issue. If they do not, the lower courts must resolve the preserved negligence and notice grounds under their own requirements before entering judgment for either party. A new evidentiary trial is not required merely because the rule is clarified. This limited remand addresses an actual unresolved attribution determination, rather than requiring an invented compliance program or hypothetical litigation of a new defense.

Landgraf forecloses expanding this remedy into post-1991 damages. The personal constitutional and state claims remain outside the disposition, and any collateral fee question requires its own statutory and reasonableness analysis. Stone's result protects Faragher's statutory claim from a mistaken categorical bar while respecting the trial court's factfinding role and the actual limits of the available relief.
