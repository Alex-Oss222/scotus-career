# 13. O'Gilvie v. United States

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 6–3. The punitive damages were taxable; the Court also rejected the erroneous-refund limitations objection and the objection to the Government's appeal.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer wrote for the Chief Justice and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Justices O’Connor and Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** Former § 104(a)(2) did not exclude punitive damages. The two-year erroneous-refund period ran from the children's receipt of the checks, making the Government's suit timely. The Court accepted the Tenth Circuit's explanation that the operative final judgment made the Government's appeal timely.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; it is not simulated precedent.
* **Historical Sources:** [O'Gilvie v. United States, 519 U.S. 79](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/519/79/).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=249).

# 14. Greene v. Georgia

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Court vacated and remanded in a per curiam opinion; no roll-call vote was reported.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Unsigned per curiam disposition with no separate writing.
* **Historical Holding:** Witt’s federal habeas standard did not control the Georgia Supreme Court’s direct review; the state court had to reconsider the ruling under the proper standard.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; it is not simulated precedent.
* **Historical Sources:** [Greene v. Georgia, 519 U.S. 145](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/519/145/).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=315).

# 15. M.L.B. v. S.L.J.

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 6-3.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Ginsburg delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Breyer. Justice Kennedy concurred in the judgment. Chief Justice Rehnquist dissented. Justice Thomas dissented, joined by Justice Scalia and, except as to Part II, by the Chief Justice.
- **Historical Holding:** A State may not condition an indigent parent's first appeal from a decree permanently terminating parental rights on prepayment of record fees she cannot afford.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court at the controlling scope, with an additional concurrence in the judgment.
- **Historical Sources:** M.L.B. v. S.L.J., 519 U.S. 102 (1996).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=272).

# 16. United States v. Sahhar

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No historical Supreme Court merits comparator. The historical anchor is the Ninth Circuit's reversal and remand.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Judge Schroeder wrote for a panel joined by Judge Kozinski and Judge Wilfred Feinberg of the Second Circuit, sitting by designation.
- **Historical Holding:** The maximum sentence for the dismissed charge does not cap a valid § 4246 civil commitment based on continuing mental illness and dangerousness, though the statute requires continued attention to state custody.
- **Historical Authority:** Lower-court judgment only.
- **Historical Sources:** Sahhar, 56 F.3d 1026.
