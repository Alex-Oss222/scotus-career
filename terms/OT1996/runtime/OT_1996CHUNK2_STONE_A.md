**Government appeal.** Stone reaches the notice objection first because appellate authority precedes affirmance. He proposes that December 7, 1993, rather than the withdrawn August 1992 order or the November 30 interest-only order, supplies final judgment on the punitive claim. The February 1, 1994 notice falls within the governing sixty days. This applies finality to what the orders actually disposed of; it does not hold that every amended judgment restarts time. *Ibarra*'s criminal-motion scope and *Smith*'s insistence on timely notice remain intact.

**Children's refund recovery.** Stone proposes that the refund was made for § 6532(b) when the children received payment July 9, 1990. Section 7405(b) recovers an erroneous benefit actually delivered; the traditional mistaken-payment cause accrues at receipt, and neither writing nor mailing necessarily supplies that benefit. The July 9, 1992 suit is timely. A postmark may be easier evidence but cannot overcome the statutory relationship between making a refund and recovering it. He decides these undisputed receipt dates only and announces no tolling rule or new exception to a tax deadline.

**Taxability.** Stone applies *Schleier*'s independent receipt-specific causation requirement to the Kansas punitive component. Personal injury occasioned the litigation, but the legal function of this distinct award was punishment and deterrence, not restoration of injury loss. The tort-type character of the action cannot by itself satisfy the second requirement. He therefore proposes inclusion under § 61 and denial of exclusion under the pre-amendment § 104(a)(2). He candidly extends *Schleier*'s causal reasoning to this different punitive award; he does not claim *Schleier* had already decided every punitive-damages question or manufacture a majority rule from fractured *Burke*. Actual compensatory awards and mixed allocations are reserved.

**Judgment and remedial precision.** Affirm the Tenth Circuit judgment sustaining taxability and rejecting the two procedural objections. Do not reverse the taxpayer-adverse judgment while adopting its legal conclusion; do not independently compute tax, recover an unstated amount, or order a new factual remand unless an identified unresolved matter requires it. The two case-specific procedural grounds are answered, not relegated to an unexplained reservation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal habeas deference does not bind state direct review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the source error because it controlled the reasoning below and makes the juror’s qualification unnecessary now.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a state appellate court reviewing a trial court’s exclusion of a capital juror must apply the deferential federal habeas standard associated with Wainwright v. Witt, or may independently review the trial-court ruling under state appellate law.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Keep federal habeas review and state direct appellate review separate.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Wainwright v. Witt does not require a state supreme court on direct review to apply the deference used by a federal habeas court. The state court must exercise the review assigned by state law while enforcing the federal constitutional floor.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The same trial ruling can receive different review in different institutions because the source and posture differ. Importing collateral-review deference into direct review obscures who is responsible for correcting trial error and may lower the state court’s own legal duty without authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Georgia may adopt a deferential state standard, and Stone expresses no view on the excluded juror, the ultimate capital judgment, or harmlessness.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Mississippi shall permit M.L.B.'s first appeal without prepayment and shall provide an adequate record or equivalent means of review. The Mississippi courts shall decide the termination appeal under ordinary law; this Court makes no finding on parental fitness or the ultimate decree.

### Issue 1: Indigent access to a first appeal from permanent termination

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The record fee is the direct barrier to the first appeal, and the case does not require review of the termination evidence itself.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Mississippi may offer appellate correction of a permanent termination decree while making the indispensable record unavailable solely because the parent is indigent.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies a narrow access rule. A State need not create a civil appeal, subsidize ordinary private litigation, or furnish every transcript. Once it creates a first appeal from a judgment that permanently and exclusively alters the legal parent-child relationship, however, it may not make meaningful review turn on an indigent parent's ability to buy the only usable record. The State may use a transcript, an agreed statement, a settled narrative, or another adequate equivalent; it may not make wealth the condition of access.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Mississippi violates due process and equal protection by denying an indigent parent meaningful first appellate review of a permanent termination decree solely because she cannot prepay the cost of the record necessary for that review.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Termination is an irreversible exercise of state power over a civil status the State exclusively defines and enforces. The risk of error is serious, the underlying burden of proof is elevated, and the appellate court cannot test the sparse decree without an adequate account of the evidence and rulings. The requested accommodation does not require Mississippi to finance all civil litigants. It requires the State to make its own first-level error-correction process real in the narrow class of permanent parental-status judgments. The record establishes indigency and necessity, so no factfinding is required on those points.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not create a general constitutional right to a civil appeal, free discovery, appointed appellate counsel, or publicly financed litigation over property, money, or ordinary custody scheduling.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment reversing the release order and remanding for further proceedings.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall conduct a contemporaneous inquiry into the statutory predicates and Arizona's response to the Attorney General's reasonable, periodically renewed placement efforts. The unappealed dismissal of the indictment remains undisturbed. Continued federal custody must end if the current illness-caused danger is not proved, if the statutory release route requires discharge, or if an eligible State accepts responsibility.

### Issue 1: Duration, safeguards, and state placement under § 4246

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory, due-process, and equal-protection objections to commitment beyond the hypothetical maximum sentence. He does not reach the separately asserted enumerated-power question because preservation is not established.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether present illness-caused dangerousness, rather than the maximum possible criminal sentence, governs continued civil commitment and what continuing safeguards the statute requires.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 4246 does not contain a maximum-sentence cap. It permits continued civil custody only while current mental disease or defect causes the danger specified by the statute, proved by clear and convincing evidence, and while meaningful review and practical routes to unconditional or conditional release remain available. The Attorney General must make and periodically renew reasonable efforts to transfer responsibility to an eligible State, and transfer becomes mandatory if a State accepts. The custody-defined federal class and the interests in safely winding up lawful custody, treatment, and public protection are sufficiently related even under intermediate scrutiny; Stone does not select an ultimate tier.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 4246 permits confinement beyond the maximum possible sentence because the confinement is civil and rests on current mental illness causing current dangerousness, not punishment for the dismissed charge. Continued custody requires meaningful review and release routes and reasonable, periodically renewed efforts to transfer responsibility to a willing State. The Court does not decide the unpreserved enumerated-power question.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Sentence length does not measure recovery from a present disorder, but neither does an old finding authorize indefinite custody. Sections 4246 and 4247 require counsel, a neutral hearing, clear-and-convincing proof, periodic reporting, renewed judicial review, release when the predicate ends, and continuing state-placement efforts. Those safeguards distinguish commitment from custody based on incompetence alone and require remand for current application rather than unconditional release or permanent federal retention.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit confinement based on incompetence alone, dangerousness without qualifying mental disease, a stale finding, nominal review, or federal retention after a State accepts responsibility. It establishes no freestanding federal civil-commitment power.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved result rests on the continued civil predicate and §4246’s operative safeguards, not the prior criminal accusation. Foucha’s four-vote equality discussion supplies no controlling tier. His statement that this scheme survives even intermediate scrutiny is his own alternative reasoning, not a finding that Foucha imposed that level of review.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1–2 — preserved approved answer.** Stone would hold that dismissal of the indictment and expiration of the possible criminal maximum do not themselves end a separately lawful §4246 commitment. Current illness-caused danger and real review/release safeguards must do the work; historical dangerousness or the dismissed charge cannot substitute. He would sustain this limited custody-defined classification even under intermediate scrutiny without declaring that tier universally governing.

**Question 3 — preserved approved answer.** Federal custody may continue only within §4246 while reasonable efforts to secure an eligible State’s acceptance are made and periodically renewed. Actual acceptance triggers the required transfer; a suggestion of willingness requires a current inquiry. Stone would affirm the existing remand, leave the indictment’s unappealed dismissal intact, and require release or transfer when the governing predicate or statutory condition demands it. He would not reach an unpreserved general enumerated-power challenge.
