## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Seventh Circuit shall adjudicate Lindh's appeal under the habeas law governing when his application was filed. The Court expresses no view on the Confrontation Clause claim or ultimate entitlement to relief.

### Issue 1: Temporal reach of AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only statutory temporal reach. Because pre-AEDPA law governs, the court of appeals must address the merits in the first instance under that framework.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies enacted structure rather than a free-floating characterization of the amendments as procedural. Congress expressly directed chapter 154's new capital procedures to pending cases and omitted comparable language from chapter 153. The most coherent reading is that chapter 153 applies to applications filed after enactment. That application-based boundary is administrable and respects the legal proceeding already invoked.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The neighboring chapters were enacted together and address the same general subject. Congress's express pending-case instruction in chapter 154 shows that it considered temporal reach and knew how to state it. Giving chapter 153 the same pending reach would erase that deliberate contrast. The amendments also alter the standards governing an already-filed request for judicial relief, so the general Landgraf concerns reinforce the textual inference. Lindh filed four years before enactment and receives the law attached to that application.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern applications filed after AEDPA, chapter 154 cases covered by its express clause, successive petitions governed by Felker, or procedural rules whose enacted text supplies a different temporal command.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The emotional-distress proof is insufficient

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the claim because the Second Circuit reinstated it, but uses the narrower evidentiary ground rather than define every physical-impact boundary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May a railroad worker negligently exposed to asbestos but presently without disease recover under FELA for fear of future illness and for the incremental cost of medically reasonable monitoring?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gottshall's distinct physical-danger and injury predicates; assume the disputed toxic-impact question favorably only if an independently dispositive, properly reviewed injury-proof deficiency supports reversal.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An asymptomatic worker’s understandable fear of future disease does not support FELA emotional-distress damages on a record containing no objective evidence of severe emotional injury, even when the employer negligently caused a significant exposure.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Buckley proved risk and concern, not the serious emotional injury that separates compensable distress from the common anxiety accompanying hazardous exposure. The District Court heard the evidence and found the showing insufficient. That ground resolves this record without holding that physical contact with toxic material can never satisfy a different claimant’s zone-of-danger theory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone leaves distinct manifested-disease claims and emotional claims satisfying both Gottshall's applicable physical-danger gate and the required injury showing open. Severe distress alone does not satisfy the gate.

### Issue 2: Reasonable medical monitoring is a present economic injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the separate claim because the Second Circuit recognized it and the District Court did not resolve it.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May a railroad worker negligently exposed to asbestos but presently without disease recover under FELA for fear of future illness and for the incremental cost of medically reasonable monitoring?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Recognize a narrow FELA claim and state openly the proof and remedial boundaries necessary to keep it distinct from speculative future-disease damages.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A railroad worker may recover the incremental cost of medical monitoring under FELA when negligent occupational exposure materially increases the risk of a serious disease and competent medical evidence shows that a reasonable physician would prescribe monitoring different from ordinary care. Relief must correspond to the monitoring actually necessary and available.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The claimed injury is the present need to purchase additional medical surveillance, not cancer that may never occur. FELA’s text reaches injury resulting from employer negligence, and common-law reluctance toward fear damages does not erase a measurable cost undertaken to detect a risk the railroad wrongfully created. Requiring medical necessity, incremental cost, and tailored payment prevents windfalls and duplicate recovery without denying the injury altogether.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not authorize recovery for exposure alone, ordinary checkups, speculative tests, undifferentiated lump sums, or future disease damages before disease manifests.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

### Application and proposed development

Stone's split disposition remains proposed to the Court and user-approved. On fear damages he assumes without deciding the contested toxic-impact gate in Buckley's favor and rests his proposed reversal on the independently insufficient proof of compensable emotional injury. That approach does not treat seriousness as a substitute for Gottshall or make professional mental-health testimony invariably necessary. If Buckley's testimony and corroboration establish a legally sufficient injury under the applicable evidentiary standard, this narrower rationale fails and Stone must answer the impact issue rather than reweigh supported evidence. On monitoring, Stone openly proposes recognizing medically demonstrated incremental surveillance costs as present injury under FELA; no completed simulated holding already adopts that extension. His remand requires causation, increased risk, comparative ordinary care, medical necessity and nonduplicative costs. It awards no sum and does not force a fund, payment schedule or monitoring regimen that the record does not require.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Position:** Affirm and Remand.
- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit's denial of qualified immunity and remand for further proceedings concerning any properly unresolved state-action question, the constitutional merits, causation, and damages. Reserve the availability of any separate private good-faith defense.

## C. LEGAL REASONING

Private prison guards employed by a for-profit contractor operating a state prison under limited governmental supervision are not entitled to qualified immunity merely because they perform governmental functions.

Neither historical precedent nor the functional purposes of governmental immunity justify extending that protection here. Market competition, contractual accountability, insurance, and private employment arrangements distinguish these guards from public officials.

Stone's proposed **Contextual Fair-Notice and Reasonable-Conduct Test** applies only after a defendant establishes eligibility for qualified immunity. It does not independently confer immunity on private contractors.

A separate private good-faith defense remains an open question. The absence of qualified immunity does not itself establish a constitutional violation or personal liability.

**User-approved position, reasoning and conditional fallbacks — October 11, 2026.** These remain proposals to the Court, not completed holdings or votes. Stone’s recorded Rufo authorship and Lewis disposition are established commitments; this approved case-specific position remains a proposal to the Court.

**Question 1:** Stone would uphold §3626(b)(2) against the presented Article III challenge when applied with its full statutory safeguards. Congress changed the law governing the continuing availability and scope of federal prison-conditions relief. The judiciary still determines the existence and extent of a federal violation, the actual findings and whether §3626(b)(3) requires continued relief. Under Robertson, that is a change of governing law rather than a legislative instruction to find false facts or decide an unchanged rule for a chosen party. A reading that eliminates the court's independent statutory assessment would be a different case.

**Question 2:** The motion concerns the continuing prospective operation of a federal prison-conditions decree. Morgan Stanley and Plaut uphold a defined congressional procedure for reinstating qualifying completed private limitations dismissals while preserving independent judicial adjudication; they do not make that distinct reopening procedure the source of authority in this case.

This decree expressly retained supervision and modification, and the State previously invoked that continuing process. Rufo recognizes judicial modification of prospective relief upon a sufficiently significant change in fact or law, but Congress supplied an independent termination standard in §3626(b)(2).

The court must apply that standard without imposing an additional Rufo threshold and must preserve relief that satisfies the original current-or-ongoing-violation safeguard of §3626(b)(3).

The ruling neither authorizes termination without the statute's required judicial assessment nor adjudicates accrued monetary liabilities or retrospectively completed damages judgments.

**Question 3:** No. The parties' agreement and its incorporation into a federal decree do not turn every protection exceeding underlying federal law into the substantive federal right that prevents termination under §3626(b)(2). That reading would preserve every old decree simply because it was a decree. But the State must establish the actual statutory basis for termination, and the court must preserve narrowly necessary relief under the original **current or ongoing** §3626(b)(3) exception. Lewis's existing-findings rule prevents a purely formal search for missing phrases. Stone would apply its eight-Associate statutory rationale as governing law although he joined only the disposition there; his separate position cannot erase that Court authority.

**Question 4:** On the presented record, beneficiaries have no vested entitlement to perpetual federal enforcement of mutable prospective terms unaffected by later valid law. They received a judicial motion process and appellate review of the statutory and constitutional objections. Stone would reject the claimed automatic property-right bar to termination. This does not decide whether a court could terminate relief needed for an established federal violation without the findings the statute requires, nor adjudicate separately accrued or contractual rights. The related equal-protection/access argument below fails on this record because the statute confines prospective remedies while leaving a channel to vindicate actual federal rights; simulated Lewis would still forbid a system that in operation prevents meaningful court access. Mere invocation of state sovereignty cannot replace compliance with those limits.

## B. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

**Affirm the Fourth Circuit's judgment sustaining termination against these legal challenges**, making clear that the ruling concerns prospective federal enforcement and incorporates the full original §3626(b)(3) safeguard. The reported appeal supplies no preserved factual showing requiring a new hearing; Stone would not order one merely because conditions litigation is important. ## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **These commercial coursepacks do not establish fair use on this record.** Stone would give weight to instruction and scholarly content but conclude that substantial unaltered excerpts sold to satisfy assigned reading replace an established licensed means of obtaining that same expression. He would assess the six works and actual amounts rather than create a universal rule for educational copying. Campbell's integrated method, including its rejection of a transformation prerequisite, controls the analysis.

2. **Commercial character does not automatically outweigh education.** It is a material aspect of the copy shop's use. A professor's pedagogical selection gives the transaction an educational purpose, but does not make the supplier's reproduction legally identical to personal student study. Stone would reject either categorical extreme and find the particular substitution and substantial amounts decisive in combination with commercial exploitation.

3. **An established permissions market can establish cognizable harm, but a licensing demand cannot prove itself.** The publishers must identify a legitimate, ordinarily exploitable coursepack market and substitution for licensed copies. Stone would distinguish Campbell's protected critical use, where copyright cannot reserve a veto through a licensing market, from unaltered reading substitutes. He would not assume students would otherwise buy whole books. On the described record, the existing excerpt-licensing market supplies the relevant connection.

4. **Intentional copying with a good-faith belief is not automatically willful.** An honest belief supported by a tenable fair-use position can negate knowing or reckless infringement even when the defense ultimately fails. Merely announcing a belief cannot immunize deliberate disregard of known infringement. Stone would sustain rejection of willfulness on the record of a substantial legal dispute and the identified belief, while preserving ordinary statutory damages; unsuccessful fair use does not automatically establish innocent infringement either.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm rejection of fair use for the six challenged coursepack reproductions; affirm rejection of enhanced damages based on willfulness; remand for an ordinary statutory-damages determination unaffected by an erroneous willfulness premise and for a specific injunction preserving lawful fair use. Do not announce that every commercially supplied educational copy infringes. This proposal follows Stone's commitment to statutory method and realistic alternatives while treating education as evidence relevant to §107, not an exemption absent from the enacted scheme.
