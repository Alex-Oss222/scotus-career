## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

**Questions1–2.** Allow this prospective equitable challenge against the responsible implementing officer. Lack of a pleaded APA action does not by itself establish that Congress barred all ultra vires review. Stone's Franklin opinion requires attention to the actual official act and source of final authority; Dalton requires attention to a particular preclusion scheme. Neither permits treating the word “President” as a complete answer. Use §1331 for jurisdiction and traditional officer-directed equity for the action; apply §702's nonmonetary waiver to the implementing Secretary, with the Larson–Dugan ultra vires principle supporting the confined officer route. The waiver is not an implied damages action. The asserted conflict between the procurement order and the NLRA supplies a judicially manageable statutory limit, and no identified statutory scheme precludes this challenge. Confine relief to the Secretary's implementation, without resolving presidential APA status or creating damages liability.

**Question3.** Reject this executive-order policy under the general procurement delegation. Congress's treatment of economic weapons and replacement hiring cannot be displaced across federal contractors merely by describing the change as efficient purchasing. Boston Harbor protects a proprietor's supported transaction-specific bargain; it does not make all government labor policy proprietary. The breadth and object of this rule place it on the policy side of that boundary. This reasoning does not constitutionalize every employer practice or deny Congress authority to change the NLRA.

## C. PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the D.C. Circuit's judgment reversing dismissal and rejecting the challenged implementation, with any decree confined to the unlawful procurement restriction. Preserve ordinary review of a materially different proprietary condition. No nationwide redesign of labor law, presidential injunction or categorical presidential APA holding is proposed.

# SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Current approval status:** All Stone answers, reasoning, proposed dispositions and stated conditional fallbacks in this supplement are approved by the user on October 11, 2026. ## A. APPROVAL AND METHOD

The user approves these proposed answers and the stated fallback. The Master expressly directs ordinary patent law to govern without a special ideological overlay. Stone applies §103 and Graham, respects actual claim boundaries and evidentiary burdens, and invents neither a biotechnology preference nor a presumption against biological inventions.

## B. COMPLETE ANSWERS

**Question 1 — not on this showing, but absence of a printed nucleotide sequence is not a universal safe harbor.** Stone would affirm because the actual combination establishes a reason to investigate, not a supported conclusion that this claimed subject matter was within ordinary skill. Bohlen’s nineteen-amino-acid fragment is not a full protein sequence. The Board did not adequately justify equating the proteins or moving from brain-specific disclosure to the relevant placental and uterine materials. Redundancy expands the possible sequences but does not establish nonobviousness by arithmetic alone. The question is whether the art and ordinary skill supplied a reasoned path to the product, not whether later readers can reconstruct Deuel’s achievement.

For claims 5 and 7, the prima facie case therefore fails on this record. For claims 4 and 6, Stone requires independent treatment of their broader reach: breadth can make rejection easier to establish, and prior identification of every genus member is unnecessary. Here, however, no independent ground was articulated and the identification gaps remain unresolved. He would affirm reversal of their rejection too, without declaring the genera nonobvious against all possible art. Markman reinforces the need to use the actual claim boundaries before comparing them with evidence; it does not decide their patentability.

**Question 2 — a genuinely predictive method can be probative and sometimes sufficient with the relevant art; a familiar research method alone is not.** Stone rejects a categorical rule making methods essentially irrelevant whenever the claim describes a product. Section 103 asks what the subject matter as a whole would have made obvious to the skilled person. Evidence that a known process reliably yields a sufficiently identified product can answer that inquiry even if its exact sequence was not previously written down. The proof must connect the particular route, source material, target and expected result. Routine use of cloning in a field supplies none of those missing specifics by itself.

This qualifies the appellate opinion’s broad language rather than finding that the Office supplied the missing evidence. A target-specific route with predictably bounded results differs from general library screening plus an incomplete fragment and unresolved tissue assumptions. Stone rejects the Office’s use of the applicants’ failure to disprove those assumptions as a substitute for its initial burden. Ordinary skill includes what the evidence establishes a capable worker knew; it cannot be filled with the invention itself.

He would not decide §101 or the reserved §112 issue. Disclosure of only two cDNAs despite broad encoding claims may warrant examination, but a different statutory defect needs its own ground, notice and record. The proposed result protects the statutory examination process and does not award exclusivity merely for research effort.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the Federal Circuit judgment reversing the Board’s §103 rejection of claims 4–7 on the grounds and record presented. Decline categorical exclusion of predictive-method evidence from product obviousness. Decide no eligibility or §112 issue and order no patent issuance. Leave independently proper further examination to the PTO under ordinary procedures. If the Court concludes that an erroneous categorical exclusion prevented a necessary factual determination, Stone’s approved fallback is a limited remand for claim-specific application of the correct §103 standard, without directing rejection or allowance.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the Supreme Judicial Court of Massachusetts.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No separate remedial instruction is required.

### Issue 1: Parental liberty and familial privacy

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial merits. Waived state-law consent claims and hypothetical medical services are outside the case.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether mere availability of condoms in a compulsory-school setting is itself coercive interference with parental direction.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the received family-liberty cases without enlarging them. Parents retain primary authority to instruct their children on morality, religion, and sexual conduct. The State does not displace that authority by making a nonprescription preventive item voluntarily available, where students may ignore the program, no belief is demanded, and no benefit or discipline turns on participation. Compulsory attendance does not make every optional resource compulsory.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A voluntary school condom-availability program does not violate parental liberty or familial privacy merely because it lacks a parental opt-out or notice provision, when students are not required to obtain condoms, receive counseling, read materials, or affirm any message.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The challenged program changes availability, not legal authority within the family. Parents remain free to instruct their children not to participate, and the school imposes no sanction for compliance with that instruction. The condoms are not an abortion, sterilization, medication, or other procedure that itself changes bodily status. The program's presence in school may offend or make parental instruction more difficult, but constitutional interference requires more than proximity to an option the parent rejects.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide confidential medical treatment, prescription drugs, compelled sex education, distribution to a child unable to consent, or a program that affirmatively undermines parental authority through penalty or deception.

### Issue 2: Free exercise

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the free-exercise merits. The plaintiffs identify sincere religious objections but no required religiously prohibited act.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether exposure to and voluntary access to a school program substantially burdens religious exercise.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the substantial-burden threshold. Government neither requires students to obtain condoms nor conditions schooling, grades, discipline, or benefits on approval of the program. The school does not regulate belief or worship. Offense and peer influence do not become legal compulsion without state-created pressure tied to a consequence.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The program imposes no substantial burden on free exercise because participation is voluntary and no student or parent is penalized for religiously motivated refusal.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Constitution protects the family from coerced observance and targeted disability, not from every public program inconsistent with private faith. The record contains no state demand that the plaintiffs endorse premarital sex, use contraception, or surrender a benefit. The free-exercise claim therefore ends at the burden threshold.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not approve religious targeting, compelled participation in sexual instruction, denial of an exemption from a mandatory practice, or a program that conditions a benefit on conduct forbidden by faith.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1–2 — preserved approved answers.** Stone would affirm because the State neither displaces parental instruction nor coerces participation, belief or religious abandonment. Religious disagreement with a voluntary offering does not itself establish the asserted constitutional burden.

**Question 3 — newly explicit formulation of the preserved no-veto result; proposed to the Court and user-approved insofar as it adds reasoning.** A constitutional exemption is unnecessary where the child may already refuse the offered activity without consequence. A parent’s request to stop a child voluntarily obtaining the item is a different substantive veto, which the cited family and religion precedents do not supply here. The answer leaves compulsory instruction, medical treatment, disclosure and different consent laws for their own sources and facts; Stone proposes no general school-health immunity.

### Issue 1: Contract formation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches formation because the contract claim cannot proceed without an actual undertaking.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether continued use constituted acceptance where the buyer received advance package notice, encountered the full license before meaningful use, could reject the transaction, and deliberately continued using the software commercially.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary UCC formation rules without creating a technology-specific immunity or a universal shrinkwrap rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A packaged-software seller may invite acceptance by use after review when the buyer receives reasonable pre-purchase notice that terms govern, obtains the full terms before meaningful use, has a practical opportunity to reject and return the product, and objectively manifests assent by continued use. Those conditions are established here.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The UCC permits contracts formed through conduct and does not require every term to appear on the outside of a package. Here the transaction was not silent: the box disclosed the license, the software displayed it, continued operation required assent, and Zeidenberg used the consumer product to operate the very commercial service the covenant excluded. Enforcing that promise applies ordinary law to a new transaction form; it does not allow a seller to add unknown terms after acceptance is complete.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves a term first revealed after irrevocable use, a transaction without a practical return route, browsewrap lacking objective assent, unconscionability, fraud, illegality, and enforcement against downstream strangers.

### Issue 2: Section 301 preemption

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches preemption because it was an independent ground of the District Court’s judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply § 301’s equivalence requirement and simulated *Wolens*’s distinction between external regulation and enforcement of a self-imposed undertaking.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 301 does not preempt enforcement of this party-specific noncommercial-use covenant because liability depends on a voluntary promise in addition to copying or use of material within copyright’s subject matter, and the covenant creates no right against nonparties.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Copyright defines federal rights against the public. This action instead asks whether these defendants broke a promise they accepted as the price of the lower-cost consumer transaction. The promise changes the legal relation between the parties and is not equivalent to a copyright owner’s exclusive right against everyone. *Feist* still prevents ProCD from claiming copyright in facts merely because they were costly to collect, and contract law cannot bind persons who never assented.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether § 301 preempts a nominal contract claim lacking a genuine additional promise, whether another federal copyright rule affirmatively forbids a particular covenant, or whether first-sale, misuse, public policy, or competition law defeats a different license.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — retained user-approved proposal.** Terms disclosed after payment can become part of this transaction where the buyer receives reasonable advance notice of license terms, can review them before acceptance by meaningful use, and has an effective return/refund opportunity. Stone would not validate hidden or practically nonrejectable terms categorically.

**Question 2 — retained user-approved proposal made explicit.** Continued use is objective acceptance here because the communicated offer invited it after review and the software required assent. Mere possession or use without adequate notice would not establish the same agreement.

**Question 3 — retained user-approved proposal.** The particular party-specific noncommercial-use promise supplies an obligation beyond copyright’s general rights and does not bind nonassenting persons. Stone would affirm enforcement on that record, preserve genuine generally applicable defenses, and reserve other licenses or remedies that effectively create rights against the public.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Mrs. Yates's death-benefit claim may proceed without a § 33(g) forfeiture based on the predeath settlements. The Director may remain a respondent. Amount, causation, and any other defenses remain for the agency process.

### Issue 1: Predeath spouse and § 33(g)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory merits. The future-benefit question is ripe because Ingalls asserts the settlements as a present forfeiture defense.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Mrs. Yates satisfied the legal prerequisites for death benefits when she signed the settlements before her husband's death.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the statutory phrase at the time of settlement. A person is entitled when the legal prerequisites for the benefit are satisfied, not merely when future events could make entitlement likely. Before Mr. Yates died from a work-related cause, Mrs. Yates had no death-benefit claim and might never have acquired one. The phrase 'would be entitled' compares settlement value to compensation once a person already entitled enters the settlement; it does not convert every potential future beneficiary into a present one.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An injured worker's spouse is not a 'person entitled to compensation' for LHWCA death benefits before the worker's work-related death. A predeath third-party settlement therefore does not trigger § 33(g)'s employer-approval forfeiture for the later death-benefit claim.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute uses entitlement, not expectancy. Mrs. Yates's present consortium claim and contingent wrongful-death release do not establish the distinct federal prerequisites for LHWCA death benefits. Ingalls's double-recovery concern is a policy argument that cannot add a forfeiture Congress did not write. Other offset provisions remain available only within their text.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern a settlement entered after the worker's death, an already vested disability claim, or an employer's right to an offset expressly authorized elsewhere.

### Issue 2: Director as respondent

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the procedural issue because both private parties remain adverse and the Director participated below.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Rule 15(a)'s agency-respondent requirement permits the Department to appear through its designated litigating officer.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the Rule to the Department whose statutory program and internal adjudicative components produced the review proceeding. The Secretary may designate the Director to represent the Department. Respondent status does not make the Director the author of the Board's decision or require defense of it; it permits the Department to state its position within an existing Article III controversy.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Director of OWCP, as the Secretary's designated representative of the Department of Labor, is a proper respondent under Rule 15(a) in review of a Benefits Review Board order when a private party has invoked appellate jurisdiction.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The LHWCA divides adjudication and administration within one Department and separately authorizes the Secretary's litigation designation. Rule 15(a) requires the agency to be named, and the Director is the Department's lawful representative. Newport News limits who may initiate review; it does not bar participation once another party creates the appeal.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide whether the Director may initiate an appeal in a setting Newport News forbids or whether an agency representative alone can preserve a controversy after all private adversity disappears.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Section 704(a) includes former employees

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory coverage because it is the sole ground of dismissal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the term “employees” in Title VII § 704(a) includes former employees alleging retaliatory postemployment conduct by a former employer.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret the term in the anti-retaliation provision’s text, structure, and enforcement function. This is ordinary statutory construction, not a new cause of action.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VII § 704(a) protects a former employee against postemployment retaliation by a former employer when the challenged act is taken because the person engaged in activity protected by Title VII.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute contains no current-employment qualifier. A person alleging discriminatory discharge necessarily pursues protection after the employment relationship ends, and an employer’s reference or blacklisting power can directly punish that protected resort. Reading “employees” differently in another provision does not control because Title VII itself uses the word in context-dependent ways. The coverage rule preserves the enacted anti-retaliation mechanism without deciding what degree of harm the claimant must prove.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether Shell retaliated, whether every unfavorable reference is materially actionable, or what damages or equitable relief may be available.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's user-approved coverage proposal is an interpretation of §704(a), not an application of a nonexistent same-term Walters holding or a finding that the reference was retaliatory.
