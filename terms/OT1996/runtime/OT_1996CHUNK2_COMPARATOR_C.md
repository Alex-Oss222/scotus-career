# OT1996 chunk2 COMPARATOR C

# 21. Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer delivered the Opinion of the Court. Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg joined in full. Justices O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas joined except to the extent the opinion relied on legislative history. Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, concurred in part and concurred in the judgment.
- **Historical Holding:** State law ordinarily governs the standard of care for officers and directors of federally chartered savings institutions. Section 1821(k) creates a federal gross-negligence floor and preserves stricter state standards; no special federal common-law ordinary-negligence rule is justified.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** Atherton v. FDIC, 519 U.S. 213 (1997).



## Historical reasoning references preserved from the source

- **Other Authority Used:** The historical Atherton opinion is date-eligible persuasive authority on the interaction between § 1821(k) and preserved state standards.

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=383).

# 24. Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc.

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Court reversed unanimously.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for a unanimous Court.
* **Historical Holding:** Title VII uses the payroll method: a person counts on each working day during an employment relationship, regardless of whether the person worked or was paid that day.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; it is not simulated precedent.
* **Historical Sources:** [Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, 519 U.S. 202](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/519/202/).
