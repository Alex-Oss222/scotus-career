## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit judgment for Monroe County and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate the County claim premised on Sheriff Tate's law-enforcement policymaking for further proceedings. The Court enters no finding of an underlying violation and does not decide whether the challenged conduct reflected county policy that was the moving force behind the injury. Personal-capacity claims remain in their own posture.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An Alabama sheriff acts for the county in law enforcement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the policymaker issue because municipal liability cannot be assessed without attributing the challenged policy to the proper government.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** For purposes of section 1983 municipal liability, is an Alabama sheriff a final policymaker for the county or for the State when conducting law-enforcement investigations and related prosecutorial functions?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Monell, Pembaur, Praprotnik and Jett to the function-specific question whether Alabama law shows that the sheriff acts for the county when he sets law-enforcement policy. An official whose acts may fairly be said to represent official policy is the final policymaker by virtue of the office he holds. Swint leaves this merits issue open, and Bryan County is a pending packet whose proposed reasoning is not a Court rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An Alabama sheriff represents the county when setting and executing law-enforcement policy within the county, and Monroe County may therefore be liable under section 1983 for that policy if it was the moving force behind the violation.
* **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The sheriff independently exercises law-enforcement authority for the county. The county pays his salary from its treasury like other county employees, and county voters elect him on a county ballot. Statutes charge him with law enforcement in his county, and the county must furnish his quarters, supplies, and vehicles, which gives the commission practical leverage over him. He reports collected money to the county treasurer. State supervision is limited. The Governor's power to direct investigations was not invoked here, and impeachment is an ultimate check on flagrant misconduct, not a tight rein on policy. The Alabama Constitution places sheriffs in the executive department, but Alabama law is inconsistent and its own cases and statutes also call them county officers. A state label does not control for section 1983 purposes, because otherwise States could nullify federal liability by relabeling officials. Enforcing state criminal law does not make a sheriff a state policymaker, since most criminal law is statewide. American sheriffs were made elective so that they would be controlled locally. Parker v. Williams, decided by the Eleventh Circuit, held that an Alabama sheriff acts for the county in hiring a chief jailer. Treating the sheriff as a state policymaker would let a State shield counties from Monell liability by label.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide that every act of a sheriff is county policy, find an underlying violation, impose respondeat superior liability, or decide individual liability. The County is liable only for its own policy that was the moving force behind the injury.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone reads Alabama law as showing a county law-enforcement policymaker, applying Monell, Pembaur, Praprotnik and Jett and treating state labels as non-controlling under section 1983. Hess is an Eleventh Amendment structure analogy only and is not relied on for the holding. The earlier correction in this entry, which affirmed the judgment for the County, is superseded by the disposition above.
