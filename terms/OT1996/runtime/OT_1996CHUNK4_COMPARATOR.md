# 38. FEC v. Legi-Tech, Inc.

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT COMPARATOR

The D.C. Circuit reversed dismissal, accepting the reconstituted Commission's ratification and allowing enforcement to continue. It treated lawful reconsideration as a sufficient cure without requiring every investigation and conciliation step to begin again, and rejected the premise that retroactivity necessarily demanded the same dismissal remedy as in the earlier structural case.

This records the historical lower-court disposition only. It does not establish a completed simulated Supreme Court outcome or supply governing post-divergence Supreme Court law.

# 44. Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. The fixed buffer zones were upheld 6-3; the floating buffer zones were invalidated 8-1.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Chief Justice Rehnquist wrote for a unanimous Court in Parts I and II-A; for eight Justices in Part II-C, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; and for six Justices in Parts II-B and II-D, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia concurred in part and dissented in part, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas. Justice Breyer concurred in part and dissented in part.
- **Historical Holding:** The fixed zones and fixed-zone counseling condition survived, while the floating zones around people and vehicles burdened more speech than necessary.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court at the stated scope.
- **Historical Sources:** Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network, 519 U.S. 357 (1997).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf#page=527).
