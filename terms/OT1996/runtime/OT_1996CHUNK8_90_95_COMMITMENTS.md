## 90. United States v. Thomas — May 19, 1997

### Components and supported disposition paths

**T1 — offense-date §1465 coverage.** The challenged acts are knowing interstate transportation for sale or distribution of the charged obscene pictures/images through the controlled bulletin-board service. The computer encoding is the means of moving and reproducing the visual material, not a distinct object beyond the statute merely because the signal is binary. This conclusion uses the offense-date terms, including picture, image and electrical transcription; it does not borrow a later computer amendment. The statute's distribution purpose and applicable culpability requirements remain elements, not presumed merely from ownership of a computer.

**T2 — receiving-community obscenity standard.** Miller and Hamling permit the receiving community's standards for prurient appeal and patent offensiveness in this knowing, controlled commercial distribution. Pope keeps serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value a reasonable-person inquiry, not a referendum by the receiving community. Sable supports applying established obscenity doctrine to an electronic service able to select its audience. None of those propositions adjudicates an uncontrolled global publication or a recipient's concealed location. The constitutional answer does not require proof that defendants knew the law or the jury's eventual legal classification.

**T3 — venue.** Article III, the Sixth Amendment and §3237(a) permit trial where the charged continuing interstate transportation reaches its authorized recipient. The record establishes a screened and approved Memphis subscriber, the operator's contact with that subscriber, and delivery of the charged files there. The recipient's act of downloading completes the distribution offered and authorized by the service; it does not alone manufacture an unrelated district. Knowledge of Memphis is a supporting record fact, not a newly imposed universal statutory knowledge-of-destination element. This holding decides neither venue in every district traversed electronically nor venue for a passive page with an unknown audience.

**T4 — remedy and scope.** The seven Associates accepting T2 would affirm the Sixth Circuit on the three granted questions. They would not decide new forfeiture, sentencing, trial-instruction or counsel issues. Robert Thomas was acquitted of Count 11; no child-pornography conviction may be reinstated or assumed. Stevens would reverse on the First Amendment ground described below as to the obscenity convictions within review and remand for implementation confined to that ground. His position does not disturb the Count 11 acquittal or decide the separate non-granted challenges.

Two statutory paths were examined: applying the ordinary enacted media terms to transmitted visual files, or limiting the offense to transported physical objects and invoking lenity/fair warning. The strongest objection to coverage is Carlin's treatment of recorded telephone messages and the absence of a computer-specific term. Alpers rejects freezing a comprehensive media description at familiar technologies, but purpose alone cannot create a crime. Here the affirmative textual terms and the actual conveyance of the pictures supply the coverage; Carlin's voice-message construction does not erase those visual terms. Lanier's conduct-time rule means the Court may explain the earlier text but may not create earlier notice with a later amendment or later case. No general technology exception or principle that every new medium is covered is adopted.

Two constitutional paths were examined separately: apply the controlling local-standard doctrine to this known destination, or protect consenting adult receipt against a criminal distribution prohibition. The first must answer the chill from geographically different standards; the actual screened membership and knowledge of Memphis defeat the asserted inability to choose the jurisdiction served. The second draws real support from Stevens' own Sable position, but does not command the other Associates: the controlling Sable, Hamling and Miller rules permit criminal regulation of obscenity distributed to willing adults. This is a substantive disagreement about adult distribution, not a finding that statutory elements disappear. A national standard for all online expression is unnecessary under either conclusion on these facts.

The alternative venue path treats the recipient's unilateral download as the sole operative act and confines prosecution to California. Its difficulty is that the service authorized and provided the very interstate transfer charged. Venue follows the offense's actual beginning and completion; mere effects, a remote viewer or official forum selection would not independently suffice.

## 91. Church on the Rock v. City of Albuquerque — May 27, 1997

### Components and supported disposition paths

**C1 — actual private forum and viewpoint exclusion.** The City's broad private-program forum admits movies and instruction about religion, history, literature and philosophy. It may not exclude the proposed film and accompanying literature because they affirm Christianity rather than treat religion nondevotionally. The film's concluding conversion invitation and short prayer are part of the protected presentation considered here; they are neither omitted from the record nor converted into an official exercise. Good News/Good Sports applies the admitted-subject rule despite prayer and Bible instruction; Lamb's Chapel supplies the foundational distinction between an excluded subject and a religious perspective on an admitted one. A center's subject/activity limits remain lawful when genuine and viewpoint neutral.

**C2 — concrete Establishment Clause defense.** Equal access for this private sponsor, on the voluntary nonresidential adult-program terms, does not establish religion. The City does not select this religious message as its own, conduct the conversion prayer or compel attendance. Neither the building's public ownership nor the film's devotional content makes the expression governmental. This is a conclusion about the actual arrangements, not a categorical immunity for every nominally private activity.

**C3 — contextual explanation.** Under Lemon's retained purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry, the actual broad private-program use serves the secular forum purpose, leaves the message attributable to its private presenter and does not require official theological control. Staff's present practice of screening and stopping content by an undefined sectarian line does not justify retaining the discriminatory exclusion. Eliminating that line does not require officials to supervise doctrine. Chabad's contextual rule governs actual attribution; Pinette supplies no comprehensive majority test. The comparison with Lee, Ingebretsen and Robinson turns on the absence here of the official organization, adoption or institutional religious identification present there, not on voluntary attendance as a universal cure.

**C4 — funding and relief.** The invoked Older Americans Act condition does not justify this unconstitutional as-applied denial of equal private access. The Court decides neither the Act's facial validity nor federal grant administration, funding withdrawal, an entitlement to devotional subsidies or relief against the United States. Affirm the Tenth Circuit's reversal of City summary judgment, its as-applied injunction against barring this film and accompanying literature, and appellate fee entitlement/remand for determination. No fee amount, damages award, new attribution hearing or mandatory disclaimer is prescribed. Neutral eligibility, scheduling, capacity, safety and genuinely prospective viewpoint-neutral forum changes remain available.

The lawful alternatives are (a) protect religious advocacy within the subjects and forms actually admitted, or (b) treat devotional participation as a separate activity outside a forum limited to nondevotional instruction. Path (a)'s strongest objection is that every worship service discusses religion, so defining the subject at that breadth could erase real activity limits. The answer here rests on this submitted movie-and-literature event and the City's wide private movie/class program, not on an unconditional admission rule for worship in every forum. The concluding prayer is not sanitized, but does not create a categorically different activity in an admitted film merely because the speaker invites religious commitment. Good News rejects that reclassification where prayer and Bible instruction are the religious means of addressing the admitted subject.

Path (b)'s strongest support is the real difference between academic treatment of a tradition and recruitment into its exercise, and Stevens' own Good News dissent makes that a serious question. Its principal difficulty is this forum's actual breadth and its admitted film, Bible, Theosophy and religious-oratorio programs, combined with content monitoring that excludes affirmation rather than a demonstrated neutral movie/activity class. No finding of an unknown written criterion is invented. The federal condition cannot supply an independent license to violate the applicable speech rule. The common conclusion does not hold that every government line between worship and discussion is invalid.

The Establishment Clause has two compatible but distinct supporting paths. The contextual Associates apply the existing purpose/effect/entanglement and actual-attribution rules. The three private-access Associates accept C2 on genuine private equal access and refuse the complete contextual observer methodology. Both must answer the actual religious invitation; both can do so without treating it as governmental prayer. Their different rationales cannot be pooled into a comprehensive replacement test.

## 93. Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency — May 27, 1997

### Components, alternatives and common operative position

**S1 — regulatory finality.** The responsible agency has definitively prohibited development of this parcel, denied the variance and completed administrative review. The owner need not try to sell transferable development rights to obtain a final decision concerning the use of the regulated parcel. No remaining agency choice capable of permitting its development is identified in the approved record.

**S2 — valuation is distinct.** The possible economic value or other constitutional significance of transferable rights does not make that no-build determination provisional. Penn Central's economic-impact inquiry explains why evidence of value may matter later; it does not make every evidentiary uncertainty a failure of regulatory finality. The Court need not determine here whether these rights bear on liability, compensation or both. Nor does it hold them valueless or establish that the parcel suffered complete deprivation.

**S3 — remedy and limits.** Vacate the Ninth Circuit's unripe dismissal and remand without its attempted-sale prerequisite. Resolve only the final-decision ground presented. Preserve any independently applicable state-compensation prerequisite, liability, parcel denominator, relevant valuation, the rights' ultimate treatment, defenses and remedy. No compensation award, building permit, new parcel rule or merits finding follows.

The claimant's path separates a fixed use prohibition from the valuation of transfer rights. Its strongest counterargument is that a court cannot adjudicate economic injury without knowing what those rights can yield. That is an argument about evidence and the applicable merits framework; it identifies no further land-use decision. The agency's path requires an attempted sale or additional transfer-related process first. Its strongest support is Williamson County and MacDonald's insistence on concrete final decisions rather than hypothetical regulatory burdens. Those cases do not require useless applications after an agency has definitively fixed the parcel's permissible use, and the supplied record identifies no further parcel-use discretion. Simulated Lucas' earlier-period reviewability holding preserves ordinary finality while demonstrating that reviewability and ultimate loss remain distinct. It does not automatically establish Suitum's taking.

## 94. United States v. Hyde — May 27, 1997

### Components, alternatives and common operative position

**H1 — accepted plea and deferred agreement.** After the district court has accepted a guilty plea following the required Rule 11 inquiry, former Rule 32(e)'s fair-and-just-reason requirement governs the defendant's presentence withdrawal motion even when the court has deferred acceptance of the associated plea agreement. Acceptance of the plea and acceptance of the agreement are distinct acts under the then-effective rules; deferral of the latter does not create an unconditional right to undo the former.

**H2 — retained protections.** The plea must still be knowing, voluntary and supported by the required factual basis. A supported involuntariness, coercion or other fair-and-just-reason claim remains available. If the court rejects an agreement in circumstances to which former Rule 11(e)(4) applies, it must afford the withdrawal opportunity that rule supplies, together with its rejection advisements. An accepted plea is not irrevocable. A court's actual acceptance statement and the agreement's lawful conditions remain controlling facts; this record supplies acceptance, not merely a tendered plea.

**H3 — mandate.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's categorical free-withdrawal rule and remand for application of the proper standard and consideration of any properly preserved remaining objection. The district court held an evidentiary hearing and rejected the asserted duress; the Supreme Court need not independently resolve every challenge to those findings to reject the categorical rule. No automatic new evidentiary hearing, new trial or predetermined renewed sentence is ordered.

The Government's path gives the accepted plea its separate legal effect. Its strongest objection is that the defendant is bound while the court remains free to reject the inducing agreement. Rule 11's express rejection protection and Rule 32(e)'s continuing fair-and-just route answer that asymmetry without making deferral equal rejection. The opposing path treats plea and agreement as an inseparable transaction until both are accepted. Its strongest support is the real importance of the bargain to the defendant's decision, recognized in Santobello and Mabry. But a due-process requirement that the actual promise and plea be honored does not erase the rules' separate acts or the express consequence when rejection actually occurs. An agreement-specific condition or an actual nonacceptance would present a different record; none is assumed to negate the approved acceptance fact here.

Godinez supplies competence and the independent requirement of a valid waiver, not an automatic finding of voluntariness. Mezzanatto preserves the actual scope and voluntariness of limited plea-related agreements, not universal enforceability of every bargain. Libretti distinguishes the substantive guilty plea from sentencing matters and preserves statutory and waiver limits. Each is applied at that scope, and none is represented as already deciding this deferral interval.
