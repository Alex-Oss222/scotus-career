## Revised component commitments

### R1 — Retain the counting rule and its actual qualifications

Retain original W1–W3. An individual counts on each working day on which a genuine employment relationship exists, although the individual neither works nor receives compensation that day. Payroll evidence ordinarily identifies that relationship; it does not create it, extend it before hiring or after termination, or conclusively turn an independent contractor or expressly excluded person into an employee. The daily phrase remains operative because the relationship must exist on the relevant days. Payment on a recurring part-time day off is not an additional statutory condition.

The statutory threshold remains fifteen or more employees on each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year. The industry-affecting-commerce requirement, agent language and express employer exclusions remain. Those exclusions concern the United States, wholly federally owned corporations, Indian tribes, the specified District of Columbia competitive-service departments or agencies, and qualifying tax-exempt bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations. The historical first-year twenty-five-person transition does not govern this period. The separate employee exclusions for elected state or local officers, their selected personal staff, policymaking appointees and immediate constitutional or legal advisers retain their civil-service qualification; the citizen-employee foreign-employment sentence changes no domestic count here.

Darden supplies the ordinary common-law relationship approach to a circular definition at its ERISA scope, including consideration of all relevant incidents rather than a single decisive factor. Town & Country supplies the compatible-service and actual-control approach at its NLRA scope. Neither independently decided Title VII's daily numerical language. Their proper interpretive use does not require inventing an individual-status dispute in a stipulated case.

### R2 — Resolve the 1990 numerical objection on the stipulated minimum

Under R1, the stipulated record establishes satisfaction of the fifteen-employee, twenty-week numerical requirement for 1990. The weekly payroll method initially counted employees appearing during each of 47 weeks without checking every working day. That possible overstatement is fully accommodated for the present legal issue by excluding all nine weeks with midweek employment changes: **47 − 9 = 38**. Thirty-eight is a supported minimum, not an exact total, and it exceeds twenty without deciding whether any of the excluded weeks also qualifies.

This conclusion rests on the stipulated relationships and the actual scope of Metropolitan's objection. It does not presume that a payroll listing is always conclusive or adjudicate the identity of every person in an imaginary status contest. It does not count all 52 weeks or supply precise hiring and termination dates. No further tally or new employee-classification inquiry is needed merely to answer this 1990 objection. Further proceedings may address genuinely remaining issues; the general legal qualifications in R1 are not a direction to reopen the resolved stipulation without an independently proper basis.

The separate nine weeks satisfying the compensation method do not reduce this 38-week minimum. That first figure measures payment every working day; the nine weeks subtracted in the calculation measure possible disruption of relationships by midweek changes. Neither is substituted for the other.

### R3 — Decline the unpresented promotion-year issue here without converting nonreview into a merits or waiver holding

Treat the correct-year discrepancy as a distinct question from whether an existing employee counts on an unpaid working day. The counting-method question can be fully answered, and the 1990 dispute resolved, without adjudicating the additional promotion claim's selection of calendar years. Walters omitted the discrepancy from both her petition and merits brief. The record establishes no appellate decision expressly resolving that discrepancy, deliberate relinquishment, or exceptional circumstance requiring this Court to add it to the granted question.

Yee distinguishes a supporting argument from a related independent question and preserves the prudential question-presented limit. Williams permits review of a question actually passed upon under its specified circumstances; the District Court's use of two years does not establish those circumstances in the court of appeals. Zafiro supports ordinary petitioner-specific nonreview without treating it as a merits rejection or intentional waiver. The absence of a petition or merits-brief challenge is affirmative evidence here, not a consequence inferred from unrecovered files.

Accordingly, decline to decide the additional year-selection dispute and its procedural consequences at this Court. This is an exercise of review discretion. It is not a declaration that 1990 is a legally proper coverage year for a September 1989 promotion, that the promotion claim satisfies the threshold, that 1988 failed it, that all objections have been waived, or that the year question is jurisdictionally unavailable. No 1988 count is supplied. The fact that the text points to 1988 and 1989 explains why the Court's 1990 application cannot settle the promotion claim; it does not supply the missing application or foreclose proper lower-court examination.

### R4 — Reverse the erroneous dismissal and remand only genuinely remaining matters

Support reversal of the compensation-based judgment in the consolidated cases and remand for further proceedings consistent with R1–R3. The fifteen-employee, twenty-week numerical objection concerning 1990 is resolved on the supported minimum. The remand is therefore not a commission to discover whether that minimum reaches twenty.

The promotion component is included in setting aside the compensation-based dismissal, but receives no affirmative finding of statutory coverage and no direction to enter judgment for either party. Its calendar-year question and any proper lower-court preservation issue remain for ordinary proceedings, as does any necessary application to 1988. The mandate neither commands that a particular unpresented argument be entertained below regardless of ordinary procedural rules nor bars its consideration merely because this Court declined it. The lower courts must not treat the 1990 count as a Supreme Court determination that the promotion claim satisfies the numerical requirement in 1988 or 1989. The stipulated failure in 1989 remains, while no result for 1988 is invented.

There is no supported independent promotion ground established in this packet that permits affirming its dismissal outright: its alternative relevant year is unknown, and the omission in this Court's papers is not itself proof that the underlying claim lacks coverage. Conversely, reversal of an erroneous counting rationale does not guarantee the promotion claim's success. This bounded return gives the lower court the unresolved claim-specific work without deciding that work here.

Retaliation, discriminatory discharge or promotion, other liability elements, defenses properly remaining and damages are not adjudicated. The lower courts' jurisdictional terminology is reported accurately, but the Court need not decide whether numerosity is jurisdictional to reverse an erroneous numerical dismissal and return unadjudicated issues. No employee-identity or statutory-exclusion controversy is invented. The court must still apply any independently proper remaining requirements rather than equate a successful numerical showing with a discrimination judgment.

## Supported alternatives and their limits

**A. Apply the relationship rule but remand the 1990 count.** This was the original commitment on the old source premise. Its strongest argument is that the original weekly count was not daily. The correction answers that problem with a conservative exclusion of every potentially affected week. An exact total or all original payrolls are unnecessary to decide whether a supported minimum of 38 exceeds twenty. No additional status objection is established. All eight commitments therefore reject a counting-only remand based on the superseded source uncertainty.

**B. Retain compensation counting.** The strongest statutory argument emphasizes “for each working day” and the readily checked payment rule. Paid leave's inclusion means that rule is not simply an actual-work test. The answer remains that Congress counted employees, whose relationships persist during unpaid off-days; the temporal words govern the existence of the relationships rather than a daily compensation requirement. The new application facts do not change this legal assessment.

**C. Reach the promotion-year issue now and direct an inquiry using 1988 and 1989.** The strongest argument is that the correct years follow directly from the statutory text and an undisputed promotion date, and that an orderly remand should not perpetuate an apparent error. The Court has lawful discretion to consider genuinely included legal arguments, as the previously consulted United States National Bank and Lebron decisions explain. But this dispute can be separated from the granted counting-method question, was not presented in the petition or merits brief, and cannot establish the promotion claim's fate without the unknown 1988 application or further procedural analysis. No exceptional need for a first-instance Supreme Court decision is established. The selected remand preserves proper lower-court resolution without endorsing the District Court's year selection.

**D. Affirm or terminate the promotion claim because Walters omitted the discrepancy.** This path confuses appellate nonreview of an added issue with a new merits ground defeating a pending claim. The granted compensation-method challenge undermines the dismissal rationale actually supplied. The record identifies no intentional waiver or independently sufficient adverse 1988 determination. Declining to resolve the year dispute here consequently does not justify entering a new adverse coverage judgment or treating missing 1988 data as default.

**E. Declare the promotion claim covered because the general judgment is reversed.** This would improperly apply the 1990 result to the September 1989 event and collapse the distinct years. All eight refuse that expansion. The remand must expressly preserve the distinction even if the ultimate opinion states the judgment compactly.
