## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the First Circuit judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Invalidate Amendment 506 to the extent it excludes an applicable statutory recidivist enhancement from the maximum term authorized under § 994(h), and direct lawful resentencing.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The enhanced statutory maximum controls

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory issue because it is preserved and dispositive.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does 28 U.S.C. § 994(h) require career-offender guideline sentences at or near the maximum term authorized after applicable statutory recidivist enhancements, or may the Sentencing Commission define the maximum by ignoring those enhancements?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted words and reject the Commission’s contrary policy construction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For an offender subject to a lawfully invoked statutory recidivist enhancement, the “maximum term authorized” in § 994(h) is the enhanced maximum. The Sentencing Commission may not define the term to exclude that legally operative penalty.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** “Authorized” refers to the sentence the governing statute permits for the offender, not a hypothetical maximum for a first offender. Congress knew how enhancements operate and gave the Commission no power to erase them. Concerns about charging leverage may inform legislation or prosecutorial policy, but they cannot make an applicable maximum disappear from the statute.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not require an enhancement to be sought, approve arbitrary charging, or decide constitutional challenges not preserved in these cases.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's proposed interpretation follows statutory authority before guideline policy, preserving applicable notice and statutory predicates for the enhancement rather than treating a hypothetical maximum as automatically applicable to every defendant.
