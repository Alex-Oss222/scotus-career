- **Case Name:** Atherton v. Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation
- **Citation or Docket:** 519 U.S. 213 (1997); No. 95-928; useful lower-court citation: 57 F.3d 1231 (3d Cir. 1995).
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** January 14, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether federal common law supplies a nationwide standard of care for officers and directors of federally chartered savings associations, and how 12 U.S.C. § 1821(k) interacts with state law.
- **Question Granted:** Whether state law or a federal common-law negligence rule governs the FDIC receiver's claims, subject to § 1821(k)'s gross-negligence provision.
- **Legal Field:** Federal courts, federal common law, financial institutions, and statutory interpretation.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit applied a federal common-law standard of ordinary negligence to claims brought by the FDIC as receiver for City Federal Savings Bank.

- **Material Facts and Record:** After City Federal failed, its federal receiver sued former officers and directors for gross negligence, simple negligence, and breach of fiduciary duty arising from allegedly imprudent loans. The institution was federally chartered and insured, but the asserted duties were traditional corporate-governance duties. Section 1821(k) expressly authorizes liability for gross negligence and preserves other applicable law.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The receiver's standing and federal jurisdiction are not disputed. The case presents choice of governing law, not whether the alleged conduct satisfies any particular standard. No factual finding of liability is before the Court.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Application and limits of O’Melveny

O’Melveny’s unanimous pre-FIRREA ground applies state imputation law to the receiver’s acquired California claims because fund protection, recovery, and general uniformity do not establish a particular significant federal-policy conflict. Its FIRREA construction is conditional, not a retroactivity holding. Atherton concerns a different duty and an express federal gross-negligence provision. State corporate-governance law consequently remains the starting point, but §1821(k)’s actual federal minimum and savings language must be given their own effect; O’Melveny does not supply either the minimum or a blanket rule that federal chartering can never matter.

- **Case Name:** Grimmett v. Brown
* **Citation or Docket:** 519 U.S. 233 (1997); No. 95-1723
* **October Term:** 1996
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case concerning accrual and limitations in a civil RICO action.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 14, 1997 (historical)
* **Question Presented:** When a civil RICO limitations period begins where the plaintiff discovers an injury, later learns of a broader racketeering pattern, and alleges concealment or additional predicate acts.
* **Question Granted:** The preserved question stated above, within the posture and limits described in this packet.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit held the civil RICO claims untimely under its accrual analysis and rejected the asserted tolling or later-accrual theories.
* **Relief Requested:** Grimmett seeks reversal and a rule tying accrual to discovery of the pattern or to later injury-producing acts. Brown seeks affirmance under injury discovery and ordinary tolling principles.
* **Material Record:** Joanne Siragusa and the bankruptcy trustee alleged a fraudulent reorganization of her former husband’s medical practice and related deception. A May1989 bankruptcy complaint already asserted the injury; the November1994 RICO action asserted later discovery of a wider pattern, later independent injury, concealment and bankruptcy-related tolling. The Ninth Circuit separately addressed those theories and affirmed the time bar, treating the record as summary judgment because materials outside the pleadings had been considered. These are alternative timeliness theories in a particular record, not interchangeable accrual rules.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The vehicle may not cleanly isolate a single universal RICO accrual rule. Claim-specific injury dates, concealment, and preservation may dispose of the matter or produce multiple answers.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Accrual and tolling

* **Reasoning Below:** The Ninth Circuit treated the claims as accruing when the injury was or should have been discovered and found the action late.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** RICO’s pattern element means the claim cannot accrue until the plaintiff discovers or should discover the pattern; alternatively, later predicates or concealment preserve the claims.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** The limitations period begins with discovery of injury, while pattern discovery and continuing effects do not postpone accrual; tolling requires separate proof.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** The Court may resolve a narrow claim-specific issue or dismiss the writ if the record and preserved questions do not support a coherent general rule.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** A limitations rule should not revive damages for old injury merely because later acts occur, but neither should it erase a distinct new injury.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A merits path could select injury discovery, pattern discovery, or separate accrual, then remand for application. A narrower path could decide only tolling or a discrete claim. Because the record and question combine several doctrines and a later same-term case presents related RICO timing issues, dismissal as improvidently granted is institutionally available if no clean rule can be applied without deciding unpreserved matters.

# 23. Abebe-Jira v. Negewo

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether 28 U.S.C. §1350 merely confers subject-matter jurisdiction over specified international-law torts, or also authorizes federal courts to recognize and enforce private civil causes of action for violations of customary international law.

2. Whether federal courts possess authority to fashion compensatory and punitive remedies for established international-law violations in an action under the Alien Tort Statute.

3. Whether the political-question doctrine bars adjudication of individual claims for torture committed abroad by officials of a foreign government.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

Three Ethiopian women sued a former local official for his personal participation in torture during the 1977–78 Red Terror. Their federal action followed his discovery in Atlanta. After a bench trial the court awarded each plaintiff $200,000 compensatory and $300,000 punitive damages; the Eleventh Circuit affirmed. The selected appeal concerns §1350's remedial authority and political-question objections. The unpreserved limitations argument does not become an additional granted issue. The defendant seeks reversal of the damages judgment; the plaintiffs seek affirmance. This is a civil tort judgment, not criminal prosecution or an immigration-removal proceeding.

## C. ENTERING LAW APPLIED TO THE RECORD

The text of §1350 supplies district-court jurisdiction over an alien's tort committed in violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty. Subject-matter jurisdiction, a legally enforceable substantive norm, a private action and the available remedy require separate analysis. Pre-divergence Baker v. Carr requires a concrete textual commitment or judicially unmanageable decision, not merely a foreign setting. Official personal torture presents a materially different question from reviewing the legitimacy of an entire foreign government.

Simulated Suter rejects deriving an individual right and an implied statutory action merely from an administrative statutory obligation; its majority, despite Stone's disagreement, requires a source-specific inquiry here. It does not decide §1350 or abolish historically received common-law actions. Simulated Franklin v. Gwinnett permits appropriate relief only once a cognizable action exists and specifically reserves punitive damages. It cannot itself authorize either this cause or this punitive award. Simulated Central Bank preserves a confined received securities remedy for culpable substantial assistance; that holding supports attention to the historical received action, not an unrestricted judicial power to enforce every international norm. The question therefore turns on the meaning and historical function of §1350 and the specificity of the asserted prohibition, with remedial authority separately justified. Later historical ATS decisions, including Sosa, are not entering law.

## D. PARTY POSITIONS, AVAILABLE DISPOSITIONS AND LIMITS

The plaintiffs characterize the statute as recognizing judicial enforcement of a narrow, established international tort and emphasize the defendant's proved personal conduct. The defendant distinguishes a jurisdictional grant from congressional creation of damages liability and invokes foreign-relations limits. An affirmance must identify the action and remedies independently; jurisdiction alone is insufficient. Reversal on the action issue would not approve torture. A limited remand could be needed if an alternative pleaded statute requires temporal or preservation analysis, but no unverified TVPA claim or retroactivity premise is inserted to save this judgment. No foreign sovereign is made a defendant by this packet.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc.
- **Citation or Docket:** 519 U.S. 202 (1997); Nos. 95-259 and 95-779
* **October Term:** 1996
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case concerning Title VII’s fifteen-employee coverage threshold.
* **Simulated Event Date:** January 14, 1997 (historical)
* **Question Presented:** Whether an employer counts an individual as an employee for each working day on which an employment relationship exists and the individual appears on the payroll, or only on days the individual is actually working or paid.
* **Question Granted:** The preserved question stated above, within the posture and limits described in this packet.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit used a compensation-based approach and held that the employer did not meet Title VII’s fifteen-employee threshold for the required period.
* **Relief Requested:** Walters seeks reversal and application of the payroll method. The employer seeks affirmance and dismissal for lack of statutory coverage.
* **Material Record:** The dispute turns on employees who remained in an employment relationship during portions of a week but did not work or receive pay every working day. The number counted under the chosen method determines whether the employer had fifteen or more employees for twenty or more calendar weeks. The discrimination merits were not decided.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The employee-numerosity requirement is a statutory coverage element. The Court can decide the counting method without resolving liability, damages, or the disputed employment action.
* **Relevant Dates:** January 14, 1997 (historical).
- **Companion or Related Matters:** No. 95-779, Equal Employment Opportunity Commission v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises, Inc., is consolidated with Walters, No. 95-259. Both arise from the same Seventh Circuit litigation and remain one combined matter.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Counting employees for statutory coverage

* **Reasoning Below:** The Seventh Circuit focused on whether an individual was compensated on the particular day.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The statute counts employment relationships, and payroll records supply an objective method; part-time, leave, and intermittent schedules should not create artificial daily fluctuations.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** The words “for each working day” require day-by-day proof that the person actually worked or was paid.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** The Court may adopt the payroll method while allowing proof that a payroll listing did not reflect a genuine employment relationship.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** A name on payroll is evidence of the relationship, not an irrebuttable rule when the person had been terminated or not yet hired.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may adopt the payroll method and remand for the threshold count. An actual-work method would affirm dismissal but creates instability for leave, part-time work, and midweek hiring. The discrimination merits remain for later proceedings if coverage exists.
