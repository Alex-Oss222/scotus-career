## A. APPROVED POSITION AND DISPOSITION

Grant certiorari, vacate the Fourth Circuit judgment and remand for consideration of Sheets. No plenary insurance-coverage grant or merits holding is proposed. The receiving court may reach the same judgment on a lawful independent ground.

## B. THE ACTUAL TIMING AND GOVERNING GVR STANDARD

Sheets was decided July26,1996, eleven days before the August6 federal appellate judgment. Counsel learned of it September17 and moved September20 to recall or stay the already issued mandate. Stone does not premise relief on the false assertion that Maryland changed its law only after the federal judgment or during certiorari review.

Simulated Lawrence reaches sufficiently recent as well as intervening developments. Sheets rejected key authorities used to predict Maryland's treatment of an accident. That direct connection creates a reasonable probability that the federal judgment rests on an outcome-material premise the Fourth Circuit would reconsider under the State's authoritative law. The denial of recall as without merit does not establish that the court ignored Sheets, but it leaves unclear whether the substance or the procedural stage controlled. Simulated Stutson supports limited reconsideration where the record leaves a substantial question about whether a materially relevant rule received the appropriate treatment; it does not make unexplained denials automatically erroneous.

## C. EQUITIES AND LIMITED CONSEQUENCES

Stone weighs finality and the cost of another review against correction of a material federal prediction of state law while Supreme Court review remains available. Counsel's prompt motion after learning of the decision supports reconsideration; the stated chronology supplies no basis for finding tactical withholding. The Fourth Circuit remains free to determine Sheets's proper scope and other dispositive policy provisions. A confined remand therefore respects its independence while reducing the risk that an obsolete legal premise controls the judgment.

These probability and equitable grounds support the approved GVR under §2106. Stone does not substitute his separate reasonable-possibility standard from Lawrence. The order finds no coverage, resolves no exclusion, awards no damages, and revives no forfeited claim. Additional proceedings are required only to the extent the lower court identifies a material issue after applying current Maryland law.
