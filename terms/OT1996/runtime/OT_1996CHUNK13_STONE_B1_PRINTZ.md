# Mechanically scoped approved position subset

# 149. Printz v. United States; Mack v. United States

# SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Current approval status:** Stone's answers, reasoning and proposed disposition in this supplement are approved. They are Stone's position for the simulation, not a Court decision or a recorded Court vote. Approval does not establish missing evidence, participation or an intervening Court holding.

## A. STATUS

- **Approval Status:** Approved position: Stone would join in full an opinion for the Court holding that Congress may not command unwilling state executive officers to administer the interim federal firearm-transfer checks, and he would reverse in both consolidated dockets. The position applies Stone's categorical personal commitment against compelled executive administration. Its ground is his New York v. United States position that compulsory state ownership and compulsory state administration under federal direction exceed ordinary commerce authority, extended here from compulsory legislation and ownership to compulsory executive administration. Stone states that extension as an extension.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgments in Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503 insofar as they sustain the compulsory duties that 18 U.S.C. §922(s)(2) imposes on chief law-enforcement officers.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Declare that the federal government may not compel Printz and Mack to perform the interim checking obligations of §922(s)(2), and remand for entry of conforming judgments protecting both officers against enforcement of those duties. No damages or reimbursement award follows. The officers need not prove an amount of fiscal loss to establish the structural violation, and there is no remand for a budgetary hearing. The judgment does not invalidate independent federal duties imposed on firearm dealers, private purchasers or federal personnel; it does not decide unpresented dealer claims or rewrite the Act's complete severability scheme.

### Issue 1: Mandatory state-executive administration of a federal program

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional question presented by the actual statutory commands. Printz and Mack challenge duties imposed directly on them because of their offices, not as regulated private persons, and that concrete official burden supports review. The duties' asserted modesty, temporary duration and statutory limits do not remove the question, and no express constitutional source comparable to the Elections Clause governs firearms administration.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone extends the structural rule of New York from compulsory state legislation and ownership to compulsory executive administration; he does not treat the extension as an application New York already made. Structural federalism carries decisive weight, and a categorical distinction between direct federal regulation and compulsory state executive administration implements it. Historical understanding corroborates the distinction, and political accountability explains why it matters. The rule is not a cost-balancing test. The question is whether Congress may make state executive personnel responsible for administering federal regulation of private parties without state consent or a specific constitutional source authorizing the assignment. Congress may regulate private conduct within its enumerated powers, preempt conflicting state law, establish federal officials, use federal appropriations and invite state cooperation. It may not ordinarily use its commerce authority to turn state executive officials into an involuntary federal administrative workforce.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses do not authorize Congress to command unwilling state executive officers to perform affirmative investigative, screening, record-processing or enforcement duties as administrators of a federal regulatory program directed at private persons. The constitutional defect is compulsory assignment of executive responsibility, not the number of hours required or the absence of federal reimbursement. Section 922(s)(2)'s mandatory duties for chief law-enforcement officers are unconstitutional insofar as they compel Printz and Mack to administer the interim federal firearm-transfer system.
- **Stone's Statutory Construction:** Section 922(s)(2) directs the chief law-enforcement officer who receives the dealer's notice to make a reasonable effort within five business days to ascertain whether receipt or possession would violate the law, including research in available state and local recordkeeping systems and the designated national system. Reasonableness governs the effort required; it does not make the officer's participation optional, and the designee option does not let the office decline the assigned responsibility. The statutory limits on duration, effort and use of information confine the duty without making it voluntary. Stone does not read “shall” as “may” or insert a limitation Congress did not enact; no permissible construction converts the mandatory inquiry into optional cooperation, so the constitutional question must be decided.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Supremacy Clause makes constitutionally valid federal law controlling; it does not erase the division between the federal government and the States as separately accountable governments. The distinction from direct regulation of a State is not semantic. When federal law regulates a State's own use of information, its employment practices or another activity Congress may validly reach, the State is the regulated actor. When federal law directs an officer to investigate an unrelated private purchaser for a federal program, the officer has been assigned federal administrative responsibility. A State's existing law-enforcement capacity does not become a general federal resource because it makes federal implementation convenient, and the Necessary and Proper Clause does not make proper a means that destroys the distinction between federal command and state executive responsibility merely because the means is useful. Section 922(s)(2) commands the officers to undertake federal transaction checks. The State is not complying with a direct law regulating its own activity, and the limits on duration, effort and use of information do not make the duty voluntary. The commands fall within the prohibited category.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The rule reaches affirmative federal conscription of unwilling state executive personnel to administer federal regulation of private actors. It does not prohibit direct federal regulation of state activity, generally applicable laws, duties assigned to state courts as courts, voluntary cooperation or otherwise valid conditional federal spending. Once the mandatory checks are removed, the conditional confidentiality, destruction and written-reasons provisions compel no participation from these officers, and they are not separately declared invalid. Reserved because they are not presented: duties under constitutional grants specifically involving state governmental action, including federal elections; actual consent to cooperative arrangements, including the operation of those conditional provisions after voluntary participation; statutory compensation agreements; and the complete rights of nonparty dealers. This is not a physical-occupation case, an individual-rights scrutiny dispute or a totality test.

### Issue 2: Severability of the interim commands

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches severability only so far as it fixes the lawful relief. Printz and Mack may obtain relief from the duties imposed on them. No dealer or purchaser is a party, and the officers' challenge does not support an adjudication of every nonparty's obligations.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the ordinary severability rule of New York and Alaska Airlines without changing it. The invalidity of the compulsory officer duties does not by itself invalidate the Act's independent federal duties on firearm dealers, private purchasers or federal personnel.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The judgment relieves Printz and Mack of the compulsory §922(s)(2) duties. It does not invalidate independent federal duties imposed on dealers, purchasers or federal personnel, and it does not decide whether dealers must continue transmitting purchaser statements or observing the waiting period, or any other consequence for nonparty dealers and unchallenged provisions.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** New York's rule asks whether a remainder is fully operative and whether Congress evidently would have rejected it; that inquiry belongs to a case in which a party bound by a remaining provision presents it. Deciding it here would rewrite the Act's complete severability scheme on a challenge confined to the officers' own duties. Relief limited to those duties protects the officers fully without using the structural defect to disable regulation not imposed on them.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unpresented dealer claims, the continuing operation of the Act's waiting-period provisions and statutory alternatives for nonparties, and every other application of the remaining Act are reserved. The judgment requires no State to provide voluntary assistance.

## C. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND CANDOR

**New York v. United States, June 19, 1992.** The completed decision holds that Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal direction and taking their waste into state ownership with its liabilities. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion controls; Stone agreed separately, without a formal join, on the ground that compulsory state ownership and compulsory state administration under federal direction exceed ordinary commerce authority. New York did not decide commands to state executive officers. Stone's position extends its structural rule from compulsory legislation and ownership to compulsory executive administration of federal regulation of private parties. He identifies that extension openly and does not claim that New York already decided the Brady duties.

**Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority (1985) and South Carolina v. Baker (1988).** Preserved. Generally applicable federal regulation of state activity is distinct from directing States to govern private actors for Congress, as New York itself recognized.

**Testa v. Katt (1947) and Howlett v. Rose (1990).** Preserved for state-court adjudication under the Supremacy Clause. State courts applying federal law as courts are not executive officers administering a federal program.

**FERC v. Mississippi (1982).** Preserved within its bounded regulatory and adjudicative setting. It supplies no general authorization for compulsory executive field administration.

**United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995.** Preserved. Stone's opinion for the Court rejected the school-zone possession offense's remote chain of effects and refused to insert a missing commerce element. Authority to regulate the underlying transaction does not independently establish authority to select state executive personnel as its administrators; the present rule concerns the means Congress chose, not the reach of the commerce power over the transactions.

**Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson, February 26, 1996.** The unanimous congressional-registration holding rests on Article I, §4, and the presidential-registration holding on a distinct national protective authority. Both remain intact at their source-specific scope. Neither makes low cost, administrative simplicity or state participation a general commerce-power exception. Federal elections and other express constitutional assignments are reserved.

**Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock (1987).** Preserved on severability. The Court need not determine every consequence for nonparty dealers or other unchallenged provisions.

**Consistency with Stone's personal commitment.** Stone's standing commitment rejects compulsory executive administration and gives mandatory eligibility administration as an example. This position applies that commitment to the Brady checks without narrowing it. Its basis in current law is the stated extension of New York, not a claim that the Court has already adopted the categorical rule.
