## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the District Court judgment and remand with instructions to dismiss the complaint for lack of Article III standing.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Abstract institutional dilution is not a personal Article III injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches standing first and stops because it disposes of the action.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do individual Members of Congress who voted against the Line Item Veto Act have Article III standing to challenge it based on alleged dilution of their legislative power before the President has used the cancellation authority against a measure producing a personal, concrete injury?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received injury requirement strictly and distinguish Coleman’s complete nullification of decisive votes.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Individual legislators lack Article III standing when their votes on the challenged law were fully counted, they lost through the ordinary legislative process, and they allege only that the law dilutes institutional power or changes the practical effect of future votes shared by all Members. A statutory authorization to sue cannot create the missing constitutional injury.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The appellees were not excluded, deprived of office, or denied the effect of votes sufficient to determine an outcome. Their claimed injury belongs to Congress as an institution and is contested by the Houses they do not represent. Political and legislative remedies remain available, and an actual cancellation can produce a conventional plaintiff. Reaching the merits now would turn a disagreement among officials into an advisory structural judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not foreclose standing for personal exclusion, lost salary, decisive vote nullification, an authorized institutional suit satisfying Article III, or a person concretely harmed by an actual cancellation.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

### Application to the proposed jurisdictional disposition

Stone's user-approved proposed vacatur and dismissal follows the actual Lujan injury rule even though he did not join every part of that opinion. The claimed shift in Congress's institutional leverage does not identify these Members' own lost office, salary, counted-vote entitlement or concrete cancellation injury. Statutory expedition and access cannot cure that deficiency. He therefore would stop before Presentment or delegation, preserving a later suit with actual concrete injury and leaving the distinct standing of a properly authorized institutional plaintiff unresolved. No stricter rule that every legislator injury is nonjusticiable is proposed.

- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Enforcement of the invalid content restrictions remains enjoined. The mandate preserves severable laws and statutory applications governing obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation, direct harmful contact with minors, and other independently unprotected conduct.

### Issue 1: Content-based suppression of protected Internet speech

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial First Amendment challenge. The severe criminal sanctions, uncertain content categories, and unavoidable effect on third-party speakers create the present chilling injury. Because the First Amendment grounds are sufficient, he does not announce a separate freestanding Fifth Amendment holding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone classifies the provisions as content based and applies the most searching review. Protecting children is compelling, but the Government must prove narrow tailoring in the actual medium. The terms indecent and patently offensive leave speakers uncertain about a national audience and community standard, reach serious discussion of health, art, sexuality, politics, and personal experience, and force many speakers either to abandon anonymity, incur prohibitive verification costs, reduce their audience, or remain silent. Scienter does not cure uncertainty about what content is criminal. User-controlled filtering, precise regulation of unprotected material, and targeted rules for direct communication with identified minors are materially less speech-restrictive on this record.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when vague and substantially overbroad categories suppress a large amount of protected adult speech and the Government fails to prove that the restriction is narrowly tailored in light of practical, materially less speech-restrictive means of limiting minors' access.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute makes the speaker responsible for every minor who might gain access, even when adults are the intended audience and the speaker lacks a feasible age gate. The record shows that many noncommercial speakers cannot use credit-card verification and that available controls either destroy anonymity, exclude lawful adults, or remove the speaker from the medium. The structural defect parallels Denver Area's invalidated default blocking: protected adult speech is suppressed first and restored only through burdensome mechanisms. The Internet's open and non-scarce architecture supplies no broadcast rationale for lesser protection.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not protect obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation, direct harmful contact with minors, or a future precisely drawn rule using genuinely effective and nonexclusionary age-assurance methods. It does not require one constitutional classification for every Internet service.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's preserved adult-access objection follows Denver Area's actual cumulative-burden rationale. His Internet application is supported by the record's feasibility and anonymity limits; it does not rest on a categorical holding that all age verification is unconstitutional.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's broader content-based Internet analysis remains his existing individual position. His separate Denver Area §10(c) ground was not Court law; the proposed CDA result must follow the actual Internet statute and record together with applicable inherited speech rules, rather than treating his Denver writing or the fractured component judgments as a settled universal test.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Remand with instructions to reject the equal-protection challenge to New York's conduct distinction. The judgment does not disturb independent rights to refuse unwanted treatment, receive lawful pain relief, or obtain good-faith palliative care.

### Issue 1: Equal-protection distinction between treatment refusal and assisted suicide

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the equal-protection merits. The law does not classify by disability, terminal illness, or another listed status. It distinguishes conduct and legal roles. No threshold defect prevents adjudication, and the record supports no finding of animus.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies ordinary rational-basis review. A competent patient's refusal directs that an unwanted bodily intervention stop or not begin, after which the underlying condition takes its course. Assisted suicide requires another actor intentionally to provide or administer the means of death. The State may recognize those differences in consent, causation, purpose, and professional role. Its interests in preserving life, preventing suicide, protecting vulnerable patients, and maintaining a medical system oriented toward care are legitimate and rationally served by the line.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State does not violate equal protection by permitting a competent person to refuse unwanted medical treatment while prohibiting intentional assistance in suicide, because the distinction rests on legally meaningful differences in bodily consent, causation, intent, and professional role and rationally serves legitimate protective interests.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Equal protection does not require the State to collapse different acts merely because death may follow both. The treatment-refusal rule protects the patient's authority over bodily intervention and does not direct a physician to make death the intended means or object. The assisted-suicide prohibition applies across persons and does not withdraw an ordinary civic protection from a disfavored status group. The asserted protective purposes therefore explain the line without resort to condemnation or civic subordination.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize forced treatment, override a valid advance directive, permit discriminatory denial of pain care, punish good-faith palliation because death is foreseen, or foreclose an as-applied claim supported by a materially different record of discriminatory administration.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's preserved rejection of this equal-protection claim aligns with his express jurisprudence allowing States to regulate or prohibit assisted death while protecting valid refusal and good-faith palliation. Requiring consent before bodily treatment protects the patient against an unwanted intervention; forbidding another person’s intentional provision of lethal means addresses a different risk of error, coercion and misuse of professional authority. Those differences rationally connect the line to the State’s protective purposes, independently of the conduct-based label or the absence of proven animus. The claim is permission for willing assistance, not an asserted entitlement to force a doctor or obtain public payment. His refusal/palliation protections state his position at their actual governing-law scope; they do not expand Cruzan's assumed premise into a completed assisted-death holding.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Ninth Circuit shall direct entry of judgment for Washington on the asserted substantive-due-process right to physician-assisted suicide. Claims involving forced treatment, valid treatment refusal, pain relief, good-faith palliation, or discriminatory administration remain outside this disposition.

### Issue 1: Asserted fundamental right to physician-assisted suicide

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits. The plaintiffs present a concrete challenge to Washington's prohibition, but the right must be stated at the legally relevant level. The record does not require a ruling on forced treatment, palliation, or a patient who lacks practical access to lawful pain care.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Due Process Clause protects a competent terminally ill adult's access to willing physician assistance in suicide against this criminal prohibition.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone declines to recognize the asserted right as fundamental. Treatment refusal protects bodily integrity by allowing a person to decline intervention. Physician-assisted suicide requires another actor to provide the means of death and asks the Constitution to disable state regulation of that affirmative assistance. Text, structure, precedent, and the legal tradition do not establish that entitlement. Rational-basis review therefore applies. Washington's interests in preserving life, preventing suicide, protecting vulnerable persons from pressure and error, maintaining medical ethics, and permitting cautious democratic experimentation are legitimate and rationally served by the prohibition.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Due Process Clause does not protect a fundamental right to obtain intentional physician assistance in suicide, and Washington's prohibition survives rational-basis review.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The broad language of personal autonomy cannot decide the case without a manageable boundary. Many important choices remain subject to ordinary law, and the Constitution's protection against unwanted bodily intervention does not itself decide whether a willing physician's intentional assistance in death must be protected against this criminal prohibition. The State may account for diagnostic error, coercion, depression, unequal access to care, and difficulty policing a line once assistance is normalized. Those concerns need not be proved with certainty under rational review, and the prohibition leaves States free to authorize a different policy through legislation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit forced treatment, override competent refusal or valid directives, criminalize good-faith pain relief merely because death is foreseen, or use the assisted-suicide ban to deny lawful palliative care. It leaves States free to authorize and regulate assisted death.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's preserved proposed reversal follows his express Master Jurisprudence commitment: protected competent refusal and good-faith palliation coexist with no freestanding constitutional right to assisted death. His legal reasoning must distinguish the content of the acts and protected interests rather than invoke the word affirmative as dispositive: abortion and many protected choices also involve willing professional assistance. Here he treats authorization of intentional lethal assistance as different from ending an unwanted bodily intervention, and finds no established source extending the asserted fundamental liberty to that specific act. He then evaluates the stated protection against error, coercion and vulnerability under ordinary rational review. No claim that the Constitution requires payment or conscripts an unwilling doctor is attributed to the plaintiffs, and no new universal fundamental-rights methodology is presented as entering law.

## A. APPROVED POSITION AND PRECISE DISPOSITION

Stone's approved merits position is to affirm habeas relief from the summary contempt conviction on the narrower due-process ground below, while rejecting a rule that summary contempt requires repeated or pervasive misconduct. California may pursue ordinary contempt proceedings with adequate notice and opportunity to defend if state law permits. A different judge is required only if the governing neutrality or embroilment rule requires one. Stone leaves the underlying trial order and the finding of deliberate violation intact.

Habeas eligibility remains distinct from that merits position. The petition predates AEDPA. Stone would apply the same provision-specific construction he proposes in Lindh: Congress's express pending-case instruction for chapter154 and its omission for chapter153 support applying the older chapter153 framework to this already-filed application. He reaches that statutory conclusion independently here, without treating the prepared Lindh packet as a Court holding.

## B. SUMMARY ADJUDICATION AND ACTUAL TRIAL CONTROL

The judge repeatedly prohibited punishment references. Watson raised the subject, was admonished during the exchange, and then asked about life without parole. Stone accepts the state findings that she knew the order and deliberately violated it, and he takes the finding of serious prejudice to the jury seriously. She explained her conduct at the bench before the contempt finding and again through counsel later; his objection is to the authority for summary adjudication, not a claim that she received no warning or opportunity to speak. A single such event can warrant contempt; repetition is neither a constitutional element nor a prerequisite to decisive trial control.

The distinct question is whether those facts justified dispensing with ordinary criminal-contempt procedure. Stone would require a particularized connection between summary adjudication and protecting the ongoing proceeding or preserving authority that cannot practicably await ordinary process. Observation settles what the judge saw; it does not alone establish why a prompt ordinary hearing cannot supply reliable punishment. On this record the questioning had ended, the trial continued for roughly two weeks without another violation, and confinement was deferred. A command stopping the questioning, appropriate trial-management measures, and later contempt adjudication could address the completed defiance. Summary punishment could not undo the prejudice already caused. Stone concludes that the record establishes serious misconduct but does not establish why adjudicating guilt summarily was necessary to preserve the trial.

## C. RECEIVED PRECEDENT AND THE PROPOSED LIMIT

Terry, Oliver, McConnell and Wilson preserve the exceptional power to address direct contempt and the requirement to use no more power than the occasion warrants. Wilson involved refusal of immunized testimony that obstructed the ongoing proceeding. Stone distinguishes the need to obtain testimony from punishment for Watson's completed questioning. Sacher permits deferring punishment in a case where direct contempt and the requirements of trial control justify summary treatment; delayed imprisonment therefore does not independently establish a due-process violation. Stone relies on the entire record above, not on a categorical delay or repetition rule.

To the extent the received authorities permit summary conviction for this completed, directly observed defiance without the additional necessity showing, Stone candidly proposes narrowing them. His legal reason is the DueProcessClause's protection of notice and an effective opportunity to defend: an exception justified by preserving proceedings should not swallow those protections whenever a judge can later punish witnessed misconduct. His new boundary requires an actual trial-control reason for dispensing with ordinary adjudication. It preserves immediate action against violence, refusal, ongoing obstruction and comparably urgent threats, and preserves ordinary punishment for all proved contempt. Courts' preference for summary procedure supplies no concrete reliance comparable to a completed private transaction; completed contempt judgments are not reopened merely by this proposal.

## D. RETROACTIVITY GATE AND BOUNDED FALLBACK

The preceding proposed narrowing cannot automatically support a collateral writ. Under actual simulated Caspari, a preserved and fairly included Teague defense requires comparison with the law at finality. Stone would not pretend that a rule requiring this additional necessity finding was dictated if the earlier cases reasonably allowed the summary procedure used here. On that new-rule characterization, the change is procedural and supplies neither a substantive immunity from contempt nor a watershed accuracy rule; a properly available Teague bar would require rejection of relief on this ground.

The supplied packet does not establish preservation of that defense. Under actual simulated Schiro, Stone would not introduce an omitted nonjurisdictional defense on his own merely to defeat the claim; he makes no invented finding of waiver. If the actual review record establishes a preserved, fairly included Teague defense, his fallback is to reverse the grant of relief on this proposed new-rule ground, without approving every feature of summary contempt. If no such bar is properly available, he adheres to the approved affirmance and the ordinary-proceedings remedy above. This conditional answer identifies the controlling consequence of the missing procedural fact; it supplies no final Court outcome or unsupported custody deadline.

## A. STATUS

## B. PREFERRED JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgments in Nos. 95-1478 and 95-1503, sustaining the challenged interim requirements of 18 U.S.C. §922(s) as limited records-based eligibility assistance incidental to the independently valid regulation of handgun transactions.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** Deny the requested injunction against those limited duties. No provision is severed under the preferred disposition. This affirmance authorizes no compulsory field investigation, arrest, prosecution, permanent state enforcement apparatus, or general transfer of federal enforcement responsibility to state officers. It does not adjudicate an unpresented application of those powers or impose an obligation to notify a dealer that a transaction is unlawful where Congress left notification discretionary.

### Issue 1: Mandatory state-executive administration of a federal program

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional question presented by the actual statutory commands. The Act imposes a mandatory reasonable-effort inquiry, not merely passive reporting or voluntary cooperation. Limited examination of records involves an eligibility judgment; describing the supporting duties as ministerial does not erase that inquiry. The constitutional issue is whether this bounded assistance transfers governmental responsibility in the way forbidden by simulated New York, not whether every task takes little time.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Congress acting within an enumerated power and the Necessary and Proper Clause may require temporary, narrowly confined assistance using lawfully available government records when it is incidental to direct federal regulation of private transactions. Federal law must supply the substantive rule and retain responsibility for coercive enforcement. Here the permissible duties include receiving prescribed forms, consulting available state, local and designated federal records, making the limited eligibility assessment those records support, and performing the statute's associated explanation and record-management functions. Duration and convenience support the fit but do not independently supply constitutional power. This is Stone's proposed answer to the executive-assistance question left open by New York, not an existing general exception established by Voting Rights Coalition.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The challenged interim duties of §922(s), construed to require limited records-based eligibility screening and its associated administrative tasks, are permissible means of implementing the otherwise valid federal handgun-transaction rules. Congress may not use that permission to require a State to enact legislation, assume independent responsibility for administering federal regulatory policy, create a permanent federal enforcement organization within state government, conduct independent field investigations, or exercise coercive police powers against private persons for the federal program. The holding necessary here concerns the listed Brady duties; the precise treatment of materially different commands awaits a case presenting them.
- **Stone's Statutory Construction:** Section 922(s)(2) requires a reasonable effort within five business days and expressly identifies available record systems. Stone reads that enacted reasonable-effort limitation in the context of an interim transaction-screening scheme, its specified records tasks, and the absence of a command to prevent sales or arrest purchasers. The word “including” does not itself mean “only”; the confined construction therefore rests on the whole provision and its bounded assignment, not on replacing one word with another. Ordinary checking and interpretation of existing records remain mandatory. The construction does not add a missing commerce element, excuse all inquiry, or invent an administrative power. If the text instead requires broader compulsory investigation and cannot bear this construction, Stone uses the original fallback rather than rewriting the statute.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Unlike the regulate-or-take-title arrangement, the Brady scheme directly governs commercial transfers by federally licensed dealers and fixes the relevant federal consequences. Local officers neither choose a federal regulatory policy nor assume the continuing obligation to enforce it. Limited access to existing records helps implement that rule while Congress and federal authorities retain responsibility for the transaction regime. The officer's mandatory inquiry remains real, but it does not become an obligation to conduct an independent investigation or stop a sale. A notification of an unlawful transaction is discretionary; a requested explanation of an ineligibility determination and the applicable confidentiality and destruction duties follow the statute's own conditions. Stone regards this defined assistance as constitutionally different from assigning the State an independent enforcement program. He acknowledges the resulting state effort and cost instead of treating either as nonexistent.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The rule does not authorize general commandeering because a federal purpose is useful, temporary, inexpensive, or valid under the Commerce Clause. Ordinary firearms policing, compulsory field investigation and a State's broader staffing and enforcement priorities do not pass to federal control. State-court obligations, Elections Clause duties, valid preemption, generally applicable regulation of a State's own activity, voluntary agreements and lawful spending conditions retain their separate sources and limits. No ruling is made on a permanent compulsory records program or an unrelated information demand.

### Issue 2: Severability of the interim commands

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Severability is conditional on finding an invalid command. Under the preferred construction, the challenged limited duties survive and no severance is necessary.
- **Stone's Legal Position and Proposed Holding:** Affirm the judgments sustaining the limited interim duties; preserve the Act's dealer and purchaser rules and federal implementation without alteration. The Court need not decide how a hypothetical, materially different command would be severed.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The plaintiffs' challenge does not establish a basis to invalidate independent federal obligations merely because state officers participate in the interim records inquiry. If the commands cannot constitutionally or textually be confined as stated, the approved fallback applies New York's actual severability rule to the independently operative remainder and confines relief to duties imposed on these plaintiffs.

## C. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND CANDOR

**New York v. United States, June 19, 1992.** The completed decision invalidates the compulsory choice between state regulation and ownership/liability. Its majority holding governs although Stone's agreement with the structural result was in a separate writing rather than a formal join. Stone retains that prohibition and the actual independently operative remainder rule. New York did not decide the precise limited executive duties presented here. Stone must therefore defend the records-assistance distinction as a new boundary, not claim New York already approved it or overrule a nonexistent Brady holding.

**United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995.** Stone joined the controlling opinion rejecting the original school-zone possession offense's remote chain through crime costs, education and productivity; the Court also refused to insert a missing commerce element. The Brady duties assist regulation of commercial handgun transfers through licensed dealers, rather than a stand-alone possession prohibition justified by generalized social costs. This distinction preserves Lopez's commercial-connection requirement and its no-rewriting rule. The Necessary and Proper Clause implements an independently established power; it does not supply the missing power by itself.

**Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson, February 26, 1996.** The unanimous congressional-registration holding rests on Article I, §4; the distinct presidential-registration holding has its own national protective source. Neither makes low cost, administrative simplicity or state participation a general commerce-power exception. Stone accordingly relies on his separately reasoned limited-assistance proposal here, not on importing the election holding into firearms regulation. The express constitutional assignments remain intact.

**Express refinement of Stone's personal commitment.** The Master currently rejects compulsory executive administration and gives mandatory eligibility administration as an example. The user's present instruction narrows that personal categorical position for this case; it is not portrayed as unchanged application. Following Section 16's candor discipline, Stone identifies: (1) the earlier categorical prohibition; (2) the legal reason for refinement—an otherwise valid direct transaction rule may have necessary incidental records assistance without transferring independent regulatory and coercive responsibility; (3) the new boundary—this temporary, specified records inquiry and related tasks; (4) the law retained—New York's compulsory-legislation prohibition, Lopez's enumerated-power limits, and the separate sources governing courts, elections, preemption and voluntary cooperation; and (5) reliance—the actual state staffing and budgeting burden must be acknowledged, while this limited affirmance creates no permanent duty, reopens no completed judgment, and establishes no retroactive officer liability.
