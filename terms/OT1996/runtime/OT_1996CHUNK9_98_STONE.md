## A. STATUS AND ANSWERS

**User-approved position, reasoning and conditional fallbacks — October 11, 2026.** These remain proposals to the Court, not completed holdings or votes.

**Question 1:** Stone would apply §440(a) to the pending direct-review petition, giving Hincapie-Nieto the benefit of the original February 16 filing date. The provision withdraws authority to undertake future ordinary appellate review of the specified deportation order. It does not retrospectively change the elements of the conviction or the agency's completed consideration of the waiver. An assumed pre-enactment filing is relevant but not dispositive under simulated Landgraf.

**Question 2:** No categorical presumption saves every pending judicial proceeding. Stone would test actual consequences. Hincapie-Nieto identifies no colorable defense surrendered for this avenue, and §440(d) did not deprive him of the administrative consideration he actually received. The review-channel change therefore may operate now. Stone would not endorse the lower court's suggestion that an immigration concession can never involve real reliance or that discretionary eligibility is categorically beyond Landgraf; neither proposition is necessary here. Felker supports provision-specific analysis, not automatic retroactivity for every AEDPA amendment.

**Question 3:** Stone would construe the replacement of the INA's particular review mechanism as leaving the general §2241 channel available, subject to its actual predicates, for substantial constitutional and statutory objections to the legal authority for custody and deportation. This construction and its application to immigration review are proposed answers to an open issue, not an already binding extension of Felker. Section 2241 supplies an enacted jurisdictional source; §1651 or a policy preference for review would not. A court capable only of deciding the length of detention cannot be assumed sufficient for an objection that the deportation order rests on an unlawful legal rule. Stone would therefore expressly reject the Government's narrow concession as the complete constitutional justification.

Stone's position permits Congress to withdraw ordinary review of the agency's lawful discretionary balancing. It preserves a real judicial opportunity to test whether the executive possesses the authority it claims and complied with constitutional requirements. The minimum does not entitle every disappointed waiver applicant to a fresh judicial weighing of rehabilitation and hardship. Flores's particular safeguards and Felker's actual independent review show why practical availability matters; neither already resolves this statute's full scope.

## B. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

**Affirm the dismissal of the direct petition for lack of jurisdiction**, with an express construction preserving qualifying §2241 legal and constitutional review. Do not affirm the BIA's merits, adjudicate an unfiled habeas case, or order a new waiver hearing. An as-applied showing that the retained channel is unavailable or cannot address a substantial challenge remains open; the present record does not establish that failure.
