## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The associations are not the United States for § 1341

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the channel question first because it disposes of the federal action.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Are federally chartered Production Credit Associations “the United States” for purposes of the Tax Injunction Act’s exception permitting the United States to sue in federal court to restrain state taxes, despite Congress’s separate treatment of those associations and the absence of the United States as a party?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply § 1341 according to institutional identity rather than transfer an immunity label into a jurisdictional exception.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Production Credit Association suing in its own name and interest is not “the United States” for the Tax Injunction Act’s exception merely because it is federally chartered and may possess federal instrumentality status. With an adequate state remedy, § 1341 bars the federal injunction suit.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress created separate cooperative entities, gave them capacity to sue, and did not make each litigation choice an act of the sovereign. Instrumentality status may matter to the merits, but under Stone’s nonconversion principle it does not automatically create the United States’ forum privilege. The state court remains bound by federal law and can decide immunity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether the tax is valid, whether the associations are immune, or whether an action by the United States would fit the exception.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS
