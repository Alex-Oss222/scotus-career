### Nixon v. United States, No. 91-740

Merits/threshold decision, January 13, 1993

#### Chronology and Posture

506 U.S. 224. Argued October 14, 1992. On certiorari to the D.C. Circuit, 938 F.2d 239 (1991), affirming dismissal of Walter Nixon’s challenge to Senate Impeachment Rule XI. Nixon, a removed federal district judge, seeks a declaration invalidating his removal and recognizing his entitlement to office-related salary and privileges. The question is whether courts may review his claim that receiving testimony through a committee rather than before the whole Senate failed to satisfy the constitutional duty to try impeachments.

A committee of twelve Senators heard ten witnesses over four days and supplied a record and report to the full Senate without resolving disputed facts or recommending a disposition. The full Senate received the evidence, heard arguments including Nixon’s presentation, deliberated and convicted him on two articles by the required two-thirds vote on November 3, 1989. His complaint concerns allocation of evidence-taking, not failure to meet the express voting requirement.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the judgment, 9–0. Six Justices adopt the Court’s ground that this claim is not open to judicial decision. Three concur in the judgment separately; two of those would reach the constitutional merits.

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy and scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Judgment dismissing Nixon’s Rule XI challenge | Affirm, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas | No opposing Justice | Dismissal stands. No declaration invalidating removal, reinstatement, salary award or supervisory remand. |

The judgment coalition does not make the alternative constitutional-merits rationale controlling. The Court resolves the review channel for the presented evidentiary objection.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The presented committee-evidence challenge is not judicially revisable

**Controlling proposition:** The Senate’s use of a committee to receive testimony, while the full Senate receives the record, hears argument and retains deliberation and judgment, falls within the impeachment-trial procedure committed to the Senate and is not judicially revisable on this claim. This holding does not decide whether courts may enforce the oath, presidential-trial presiding-officer or two-thirds requirements, or review a purported proceeding that abandons adjudication altogether.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Six of nine Justices adopt this exact ground and its limits; White and Blackmun’s merits ground is noncontrolling, and Souter does not formally join the Court’s opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this challenge asks the Judiciary to choose the Senate’s evidentiary method rather than enforce an independently prescribed constitutional procedure. Baker requires examination of the particular constitutional commitment and the standards available for judicial decision. Article I gives the Senate sole responsibility to try impeachments but does not require every Senator to receive live testimony. The committee assembled evidence; the full Senate retained deliberation and judgment. Powell enforced qualifications independently fixed by the Constitution and therefore does not authorize appellate supervision of this method. Commerce v. Montana likewise permits review of a defined population constraint, not judicial selection of another branch’s trial procedure. Political importance alone would not foreclose adjudication. Here, however, imposing the ordinary courtroom model would transfer supervisory authority the constitutional text assigns elsewhere. The Court accordingly affirms dismissal without deciding whether the procedure was constitutionally sufficient on the merits, or whether violation of an express command or abandonment of adjudication would present a reviewable claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 — applied through its issue-specific textual-commitment and manageable-standards inquiry; political subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486 — distinguished because independently fixed legislative qualifications differ from the evidentiary method left to the Senate.
- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, decision — preserved and distinguished; its apportionment review holding does not create impeachment-procedure review.
- United States v. Muñoz-Flores, 495 U.S. 385 — preserved; interbranch disagreement alone does not bar constitutional review.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

No precedent is overruled. Baker’s inquiry is applied to this claim. Powell, Muñoz-Flores and Commerce v. Montana retain their holdings permitting judicial enforcement of the constitutional constraints they addressed.

Effective January 13, 1993, the Rule XI evidentiary-allocation objection presented here is nonjusticiable. This adds an impeachment-specific application of Baker without a general immunity for politically significant action. The Senate’s procedure receives no controlling merits approval. Judicial enforceability of independently determinate impeachment commands and the consequences of a proceeding abandoning adjudication remain open. White and Blackmun’s contrary reviewability rule and Souter’s exceptional-case formulation are noncontrolling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring, no joins.** Stevens joins the Court’s opinion and emphasizes the constitutional decision to place impeachment in the Legislative Branch. That allocation deserves respect in evaluating this ordinary procedural objection. He sees no occasion to assume that the Senate would abandon its constitutional responsibility. His additional institutional emphasis supplies no holding concerning a future violation of an express command.

**White, joined by Blackmun, concurring in the judgment.** They would decide the claim on the merits. Powell requires courts to interpret the limits of a constitutionally assigned power; assigning the Senate the trial does not necessarily make the Senate the final interpreter of the duty to conduct one. Review does not require imposing every criminal-trial formality. The committee developed a record, the full Senate received it and heard the parties, and the full Senate made the decision. In their view that process satisfies the constitutional requirement to try Nixon. They reject the premise that possible disruption or interbranch disagreement independently removes ordinary constitutional interpretation from the courts. Their ground supports affirmance but commands only two votes.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment, no joins.** Souter regards the present allocation of evidentiary work as falling within the Senate’s broad responsibility for impeachment trials. The institutional disruption of judicial review reinforces restraint here. He would leave room for judicial intervention in an extraordinary proceeding whose arbitrariness seriously threatens the integrity of the result, such as conviction unrelated to adjudication. This reservation is a separate, noncontrolling position; the Court itself leaves that outer boundary undecided.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court review is complete. The affirmed dismissal remains in place; Nixon receives no relief from removal. No supervisory remand, retained Supreme Court matter or later filing is established.

### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., No. 93-1121

Merits decision, April 18, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 30, 1994; decided April 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, reviewing its August 3, 1993 judgment, 1 F.3d 1487. The question is whether §27A(b) may require reopening this final, unappealed private federal securities dismissal.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining denial of reinstatement under §27A(b).

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Section 27A(b) reinstatement of final private dismissal | Affirm denial, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer | Stevens and Ginsburg | Final dismissal remains binding; no reinstatement or merits remand. |
| Subsection (a), broader applications, due process and securities merits | Not reached | No new merits vote | No new merits vote | No fraud or damages determination. |

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer | Affirmance | Complete confined Morgan Stanley application; seven rationale votes. |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins Court and judgment | Broader private-finality formulation with express reservations; noncontrolling beyond Court holding. |
| Dissent | Stevens | Ginsburg | Would reverse and remand | Express limited reconsideration of Morgan Stanley; no liability determination. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Section 27A(b) cannot reopen this completed private federal judgment

**Controlling proposition:** Article III prevents §27A(b) from requiring reinstatement of this private §10(b) action after its dismissal with prejudice became final without appeal before December 19, 1991. Satisfaction of the statute's reinstatement conditions does not overcome that constitutional limit; the denial of reinstatement is affirmed without deciding pending cases, judgments still on direct review, continuing prospective relief, or independently lawful judicial reopening.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer: seven direct rationale and judgment votes. Stone-Zsela also concurs separately; his broader formulation is not the Court's holding. Justices Stevens and Ginsburg dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Morgan Stanley governs this completed private limitations judgment. That decision accepts Congress's genuine amendment of limitations law but rejects this command to reopen a dismissal already final without appeal. Robertson permits changed law in pending litigation; it does not erase the completed-judgment boundary. McCullough recognizes completed adjudication, and Klein protects the judicial function without prohibiting genuine statutory amendment. The plaintiffs satisfied §27A(b)'s conditions and timely sought reinstatement, so their motion fails on Article III grounds, not statutory ineligibility. Their strongest argument is that Congress restored earlier filing expectations while leaving fraud and defenses for judicial resolution. Morgan Stanley considered that distinction: further merits work cannot make this completed dismissal nonfinal. No altered statutory command, relevant record difference, intervening displacement, or demonstrated unworkability warrants reconsideration here. Wheeling Bridge concerns continuing prospective relief, and Sioux Nation concerns the Government's waiver of its own defense. The Court applies the existing confined rule, leaving broader remedial legislation, other statutory applications, due process, and fraud liability undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: applies its as-applied Article III rule to the same completed, unappealed private-dismissal setting without enlarging it.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: preserves genuine legislative changes governing pending cases; it does not authorize the present reopening of a completed judgment.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: applies the distinction between changing law and disturbing completed adjudication.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: preserves protection of the judicial function without treating actual legislative amendment as forbidden.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes adjustment of the continuing prospective operation of equitable relief.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes sovereign waiver of the Government's own final-judgment defense from compulsory reopening between private parties.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304: leaves its due-process revival rule intact; it does not resolve the Article III question presented.

**Statutory conditions and reservations:** Section 27A(b) addresses a private §10(b) action commenced on or before June 19, 1991, dismissed as time barred after that date, and timely under the law of the jurisdiction, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991. It directs reinstatement on a plaintiff's motion within sixty days after December 19, 1991. These conditions were satisfied here, including the February 11, 1992 motion. Subsection (a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, constitutional due process, fraud, damages, federal waiver, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. The Court adopts no comprehensive prohibition governing every later legislative effect on every final order.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Morgan Stanley controls without enlargement. Robertson retains legislative amendment of pending-case law; McCullough and Klein protect completed adjudication without denying that genuine amendment occurred. Wheeling Bridge and Sioux Nation remain distinct prospective-relief and sovereign-waiver settings. Chase Securities remains a due-process revival authority. No precedent is overruled.

The existing May 23, 1994 Article III rule remains operative: §27A(b) cannot compel reopening of the completed, unappealed private dismissal presented here. This decision applies that rule rather than establishing a universal prohibition on every legislative effect on every final order. Stone-Zsela's broader formulation and the two dissenters' proposed reconsideration are noncontrolling. Statutory timeliness is satisfied, and neither fraud nor liability is adjudicated.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the Court's opinion and judgment and writes separately to state the broader finality principle he would apply. Congress may change substantive law, govern pending cases, alter the rules for prospective relief, create generally applicable judicial remedies, and waive federal claims or defenses when otherwise constitutionally permissible. It may not command an Article III court to reopen and replace a final judgment in a private civil action after the judicial process for revising that judgment has ended. The defect is another department's assertion of authority to revise a conclusive judicial disposition, not principally unfairness or disappointed reliance. Morgan Stanley supplies the controlling rule for this statute, and §27A(b) orders restoration of cases already finally dismissed. Wheeling Bridge concerns continuing prospective relief, which remains distinct. His position preserves prospective legislation, nonfinal cases, lawful alteration of continuing equitable relief, federal waiver, and generally applicable reopening rules administered by courts consistently with Article III. No other Justice joins this separate broader formulation.

Justice Stevens dissents, joined by Justice Ginsburg. They expressly advocate reconsidering Morgan Stanley's treatment of this limited statutory class; they do not deny that it is binding, recent, and directly applicable. Section 27A(b) changes the limitations law and restores actions timely under the earlier filing regime while leaving fraud, defenses, and relief for courts to adjudicate. Robertson supplies the genuine-law-change principle, and Schor supports attention to the actual judicial function retained. They acknowledge completed finality and McCullough's concern but regard the confined correction as preserving essential judicial adjudication. Chase Securities supplies a revival analogy, not a controlling Article III answer; Sioux Nation illustrates changed legal effect without pretending that the Government waived a private defendant's defense here. They would reverse the denial and remand for reinstatement and adjudication, without finding fraud, awarding damages, deciding every due-process question, or recognizing unrestricted legislative reopening power.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Denial of the reinstatement motion remains in force and the final dismissal remains binding. No separate remand, restored securities action, fraud trial, or damages determination is ordered. The decision does not adjudicate other applications of §27A or independently authorized judicial reopening.
