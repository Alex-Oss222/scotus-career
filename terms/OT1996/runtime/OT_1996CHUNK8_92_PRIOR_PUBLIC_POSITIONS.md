### Nixon v. United States, No. 91-740

Merits/threshold decision, January 13, 1993

#### Chronology and Posture

506 U.S. 224. Argued October 14, 1992. On certiorari to the D.C. Circuit, 938 F.2d 239 (1991), affirming dismissal of Walter Nixon’s challenge to Senate Impeachment Rule XI. Nixon, a removed federal district judge, seeks a declaration invalidating his removal and recognizing his entitlement to office-related salary and privileges. The question is whether courts may review his claim that receiving testimony through a committee rather than before the whole Senate failed to satisfy the constitutional duty to try impeachments.

A committee of twelve Senators heard ten witnesses over four days and supplied a record and report to the full Senate without resolving disputed facts or recommending a disposition. The full Senate received the evidence, heard arguments including Nixon’s presentation, deliberated and convicted him on two articles by the required two-thirds vote on November 3, 1989. His complaint concerns allocation of evidence-taking, not failure to meet the express voting requirement.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the judgment, 9–0. Six Justices adopt the Court’s ground that this claim is not open to judicial decision. Three concur in the judgment separately; two of those would reach the constitutional merits.

The judgment coalition does not make the alternative constitutional-merits rationale controlling. The Court resolves the review channel for the presented evidentiary objection.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The presented committee-evidence challenge is not judicially revisable

**Controlling proposition:** The Senate’s use of a committee to receive testimony, while the full Senate receives the record, hears argument and retains deliberation and judgment, falls within the impeachment-trial procedure committed to the Senate and is not judicially revisable on this claim. This holding does not decide whether courts may enforce the oath, presidential-trial presiding-officer or two-thirds requirements, or review a purported proceeding that abandons adjudication altogether.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Six of nine Justices adopt this exact ground and its limits; White and Blackmun’s merits ground is noncontrolling, and Souter does not formally join the Court’s opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this challenge asks the Judiciary to choose the Senate’s evidentiary method rather than enforce an independently prescribed constitutional procedure. Baker requires examination of the particular constitutional commitment and the standards available for judicial decision. Article I gives the Senate sole responsibility to try impeachments but does not require every Senator to receive live testimony. The committee assembled evidence; the full Senate retained deliberation and judgment. Powell enforced qualifications independently fixed by the Constitution and therefore does not authorize appellate supervision of this method. Commerce v. Montana likewise permits review of a defined population constraint, not judicial selection of another branch’s trial procedure. Political importance alone would not foreclose adjudication. Here, however, imposing the ordinary courtroom model would transfer supervisory authority the constitutional text assigns elsewhere. The Court accordingly affirms dismissal without deciding whether the procedure was constitutionally sufficient on the merits, or whether violation of an express command or abandonment of adjudication would present a reviewable claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 — applied through its issue-specific textual-commitment and manageable-standards inquiry; political subject matter alone remains insufficient.
- Powell v. McCormack, 395 U.S. 486 — distinguished because independently fixed legislative qualifications differ from the evidentiary method left to the Senate.
- United States Department of Commerce v. Montana, March 31, 1992, decision — preserved and distinguished; its apportionment review holding does not create impeachment-procedure review.
- United States v. Muñoz-Flores, 495 U.S. 385 — preserved; interbranch disagreement alone does not bar constitutional review.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

No precedent is overruled. Baker’s inquiry is applied to this claim. Powell, Muñoz-Flores and Commerce v. Montana retain their holdings permitting judicial enforcement of the constitutional constraints they addressed.

Effective January 13, 1993, the Rule XI evidentiary-allocation objection presented here is nonjusticiable. This adds an impeachment-specific application of Baker without a general immunity for politically significant action. The Senate’s procedure receives no controlling merits approval. Judicial enforceability of independently determinate impeachment commands and the consequences of a proceeding abandoning adjudication remain open. White and Blackmun’s contrary reviewability rule and Souter’s exceptional-case formulation are noncontrolling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring, no joins.** Stevens joins the Court’s opinion and emphasizes the constitutional decision to place impeachment in the Legislative Branch. That allocation deserves respect in evaluating this ordinary procedural objection. He sees no occasion to assume that the Senate would abandon its constitutional responsibility. His additional institutional emphasis supplies no holding concerning a future violation of an express command.

**White, joined by Blackmun, concurring in the judgment.** They would decide the claim on the merits. Powell requires courts to interpret the limits of a constitutionally assigned power; assigning the Senate the trial does not necessarily make the Senate the final interpreter of the duty to conduct one. Review does not require imposing every criminal-trial formality. The committee developed a record, the full Senate received it and heard the parties, and the full Senate made the decision. In their view that process satisfies the constitutional requirement to try Nixon. They reject the premise that possible disruption or interbranch disagreement independently removes ordinary constitutional interpretation from the courts. Their ground supports affirmance but commands only two votes.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment, no joins.** Souter regards the present allocation of evidentiary work as falling within the Senate’s broad responsibility for impeachment trials. The institutional disruption of judicial review reinforces restraint here. He would leave room for judicial intervention in an extraordinary proceeding whose arbitrariness seriously threatens the integrity of the result, such as conviction unrelated to adjudication. This reservation is a separate, noncontrolling position; the Court itself leaves that outer boundary undecided.

### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., No. 93-1121

Merits decision, April 18, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 30, 1994; decided April 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, reviewing its August 3, 1993 judgment, 1 F.3d 1487. The question is whether §27A(b) may require reopening this final, unappealed private federal securities dismissal.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

### Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. The court independently determines those cumulative conditions and, when they are satisfied, must reinstate without discretionary balancing, retaining independent responsibility for all claims, defenses, and remedies; this expressly retrospective entitlement does not authorize legislative findings, dictated liability, or unrestricted reopening of completed merits judgments.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and remedy stated here. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas dissent; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

Sixth Circuit reversed, 5–4; remanded for reinstatement under §27A(b) and further proceedings on the securities claims.

The February 11, 1992 motion satisfied the statutory conditions. Reinstatement therefore follows, with the securities merits left for the courts. The dissent's objection treats completion of this limitations dismissal as an absolute bar despite the retained judicial work; Morgan Stanley forecloses that rule in this setting. Other completed merits judgments, individualized legislative commands, and independent constitutional protections remain outside this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may not command findings contrary to the record, impose liability on identified defendants by legislative declaration, or disguise an individualized award as a procedural reopening law. The Court decides no general power to set aside completed judgments adjudicating the underlying merits, no constitutionality of every retrospective statute, and no independent due-process question; applicable due-process protections and other constitutional restrictions remain. Section 27A(a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, federal waiver, continuing prospective relief, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. The decision neither adjudicates other parties' completed cases nor creates criminal or innocence remedies. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses remain for the courts; reinstatement establishes none of them.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Morgan Stanley controls the constitutional question and mandatory statutory remedy. Robertson and Klein retain the distinction between legislation governing adjudication and legislative performance of adjudication. McCullough continues to protect completed judicial determinations within the distinction Morgan Stanley draws. Beam's rule of judicial retroactivity alone creates no reopening entitlement. Wheeling Bridge, Sioux Nation, and Chase Securities remain authorities for their distinct prospective-relief, sovereign-waiver, and due-process settings. Lampf remains the intervening limitations decision to which §27A(b) responds. No precedent is overruled.

A completed, unappealed private securities limitations dismissal within §27A(b)'s objective criteria must be reinstated on a timely qualifying motion. Courts determine eligibility independently and retain all adjudicative responsibility for the underlying claims, defenses, and remedies. A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability. The rule does not establish a general power to reopen completed merits judgments or remove independent constitutional protections. The dissent's contrary finality rule is noncontrolling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, and Thomas. A dismissal with prejudice that has become final without appeal is a completed exercise of judicial power even when it rests on limitations. In their view, Congress may change the law for pending cases but may not require an Article III court to undo its conclusive disposition after ordinary review has ended. Leaving fraud and damages for a later judicial decision does not preserve the conclusiveness of the dismissal itself. The dissent acknowledges that Morgan Stanley governs the statutory setting, but rejects its distinction between a new entitlement to adjudication and interference with the completed judgment.

McCullough supplies the dissent's protection of completed adjudication, and Klein protects judicial decision from legislative control. Robertson permitted changed law in pending litigation; it did not decide completed judgments. Beam preserves finality despite later judicial doctrine. Wheeling Bridge concerns prospective equitable relief and Sioux Nation the Government's own defense. The dissent treats neither setting as authority for reopening a private final dismissal. Congress's use of objective criteria and an express temporal command does not answer the dissent's objection: every judgment within the defined class has already conclusively disposed of its case. Narrow and wholly retrospective coverage strengthens the concern about legislative interference, although the dissent's objection also reaches a broader command to reopen such completed private judgments. The dissent would affirm denial of reinstatement, without deciding §27A(a), independent due-process claims, fraud, liability, or damages.
