## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Questions 1 and 2 - recipient responsibility.** Stone would reject automatic vicarious liability and the proposition that constructive notice alone establishes the intentional recipient discrimination for which Franklin authorizes compensation. For damages he proposes, by analogy to the actual-awareness rule of Farmer and Doe, a rule for this open question, because Franklin expressly left recipient attribution unsettled; no completed decision adopts it for Title IX. The recipient is liable for its own conduct. That means actual awareness by an official with authority to take corrective action, defined by function and not by title, and a response that is unreasonable disregard of what the official knows. A danger the recipient merely should have discovered does not suffice, and a reasonable response defeats liability even if the harm continues. The rule has these parts:

1. Awareness may be proved circumstantially, including by obviousness and willful avoidance. Obviousness permits but does not compel a finding of actual awareness. Deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected abuse, including by deliberately diverting complaints, may establish awareness; a deficient complaint system by itself does not establish it, and separate officials' information cannot simply be pooled into collective knowledge. The evidence need not include a written complaint.
2. The response must be unreasonable disregard, not a bad outcome.
3. A teacher's delegated authority is evidence of the connection to the program, not vicarious liability. Title VII agency rules are guidance and not an enacted wholesale transfer.
4. Failure to keep required complaint channels is evidence, not liability by itself.
5. Administrative enforcement and injunctive relief are governed by their own sources: the statute's enforcement procedure, which requires notice and an opportunity for voluntary compliance, and the Department's guidance, which applies agency principles to harassment by school employees. That question is reserved for a later case, and nothing here lowers or raises the standard for it.
6. Harris supplies the severity analogy: the conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to create an objectively and subjectively abusive educational environment.

This is Farmer's actual-awareness analogy extended to a setting Franklin left open, and Stone states it as his proposal. The reported evidence warrants further recipient-specific consideration, not a damages award.

**Question 3 — separate constitutional claims.** Stone would apply Doe to Whitehouse and Mackiel separately. Their investigative steps are legally relevant, yet a misdirected trace, one-sided testing and delayed confrontation do not conclusively establish a reasonable response where supported evidence attributes knowledge of ongoing abuse and culpable nonresponse to the particular official. The inquiry must identify which reports each received, when, what each actually understood, the authority and opportunity each possessed, the response each made, and what subsequent injury that response allegedly caused. It cannot transfer Lankford's assumed knowledge to these defendants or treat every investigation as equivalent to Caplinger's distinct circumstances. Stone proposes vacating their §1983 summary judgments for renewed review of supported actual-knowledge, unreasonable-disregard and causal-injury disputes, followed by an individualized examination of the remaining personal defenses. This does not treat negligent investigation as deliberate acquiescence. He would affirm the District's §1983 dismissal absent supported identification of an institutional policy or final District decision causing the abuse; the conduct of supervisors alone does not satisfy Monell. The teacher's own claim remains in its distinct posture.

**Question 4 — same-sex harassment.** He rejects a categorical exclusion. Title IX protects persons against discrimination on the basis of sex; it does not condition protection on the offender's sex. Evidence of sex-selective pursuit and misuse of educational authority may satisfy that element. Welcome, the relevant educational effects, attribution and causation still require proof. Sexual orientation alone is neither an automatic Title IX element nor a disqualification from protection.

**Personal-immunity boundary and express departure.** The received rule is Harlow's objective clearly-established-law defense as retained in Elder and Doe. Elder requires examination of all relevant law on the preserved defense and creates no mandatory merits-first sequence. Stone joined Elder. Doe's Caplinger ruling separately granted immunity for lack of particularized conduct-time notice without deciding constitutional merits; Stone concurred only in that judgment on his own merits ground, but the Court's contrary rationale remains governing law.

Stone nevertheless retains his approved proposal: decide the constitutional merits first, then allow a distinct defense only for genuine reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, with the official bearing that burden. His legal reason for the change is the relationship between §1983's remedy for a proved personal deprivation and the justification for an official's separate protection from damages. In a claim requiring actual awareness and deliberate unreasonable disregard, a reasonable protective response already defeats the merits. The received notice rule does not demand an identical reported case. Stone's objection is to a distinct clearly-established-law screen that can nevertheless protect an official after a personal culpable deprivation has been proved, without identified authoritative law permitting the conduct or genuine reliance on it. He considers that broader protection capable of defeating the statute's remedy for knowing constitutional wrongdoing beyond what protection of justified legal reliance requires. That is his stated criticism of the received rule, not a claim that Elder or Doe already adopted it.

The new boundary is the identified-authority, genuine-reliance defense, including reasonable reliance affected by an unexpected change in controlling law. Legal silence, general supervisory discretion, or ordinary investigation policy supplies no independent permission. A factual allowance already resolved against the official under the merits rule is not repeated as a second shield. The proposal leaves personal responsibility, culpability, causation, ordinary pleading and proof burdens on the underlying claim, distinct institutional liability, and other applicable immunities untouched. Stone gives concrete reliance on authoritative legal permission weight through the retained defense, rather than presuming such reliance from the official's position or from uncertainty alone. No such reliance is found or rejected conclusively on this record, and no completed judgment is reopened. On this summary-judgment record he would remand for defendant-specific analysis without declaring personal liability or inventing reliance facts.

**Independent fallback under unchanged immunity.** If the Court retains the received defense, Stone's approved alternative is the same remand under that defense. The court must fix each challenged omission's date and each supervisor's supported assumed knowledge and response before assessing particularized notice. Kinman's school attendance from 1986 to May 1990 does not establish one continuous notice date for both supervisors; the later 1992 corroboration and termination efforts do not retroactively prove their earlier awareness. Preconduct bodily-integrity and personal-attribution law, including Ingraham, Rizzo and Monell, supplies relevant principles but does not automatically answer whether the specific supervisory omission was clearly unlawful. Harlow supplies the retained defense. Anderson supplies the particularized framework for present review but cannot itself give notice for conduct preceding its 1987 decision. The May 1987 Jefferson and Lopez authorities discussed in Doe can inform only conduct after they became available, with attention to their distinct direct-action and supervisory facts and their actual legal weight. Farmer (1994) and Doe (1995) cannot create notice for 1986–1990 conduct. Apply all relevant available authority, including authority not previously cited, without selecting disputed facts or inventing a new claim. The remand permits ordinary resolution of each actual defense; it does not order an automatic trial or prejudge immunity.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the rejection of a categorical same-sex bar and the availability of a Title IX damages claim; modify the Title IX remand to apply the recipient-responsibility rule stated above, which requires actual awareness by an official with authority to act, provable circumstantially, and unreasonable disregard, and which reserves administrative and injunctive enforcement for a later case. Vacate the personal supervisory §1983 summary judgments for Doe-compliant review; affirm the District's §1983 dismissal on this record. Remand without final liability, compensation, a finding of qualified immunity, or revival of the state tort claim. If the record cannot distinguish each supervisor's knowledge, seek ordinary focused factual development rather than inventing collective notice.
