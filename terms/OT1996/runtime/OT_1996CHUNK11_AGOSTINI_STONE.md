# 127. Agostini v. Felton

# SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT


## A. STATUS

Stone would write the Opinion of the Court.
- **Updated test:** The Lemon test, updated as stated in this supplement. Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test; its purpose, effects, and entanglement inquiries operate within the attribution-and-function structure described below rather than as freestanding prongs. This supplement states the propositions necessary to Stone's proposed vote and identifies the test's applications that remain unadjudicated.
- **Simulation baseline:** The supplied simulated holdings control the entering law. In that baseline, Kiryas Joel left Aguilar and Ball in place; Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding; and Rosenberger, Pinette, and Chabad adopted their stated program-specific rules while retaining Lemon. Historical opinions from a different timeline do not replace those simulated holdings.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgments in both dockets and remand for relief from the prospective injunction consistent with the updated Lemon test. Stone would adopt that update, overrule Aguilar's categorical treatment of this onsite public remedial-instruction program, and disavow the corresponding Ball rationale to the extent it makes onsite public instruction or its ordinary secular supervision constitutionally dispositive.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court must remove the injunction's prohibition on the described secular Title I services insofar as that prohibition rests on religious-school location, presumed indoctrination from that location, or ordinary monitoring of public personnel. Any genuinely unresolved issue concerning an independently supported prohibition must be identified and decided under the new rule; the court may not recreate Aguilar by requiring proof that misconduct is impossible or by treating ordinary supervision as itself unlawful. If no independent basis supports the onsite prohibition, that prohibition must be dissolved. Any remaining decree obligation must have an identified lawful basis. Existing services may continue during the prompt implementation of the mandate, and no administrative delay may become an indefinite continuation of the rejected categorical rule. Modification occurs through the district court's order under the mandate; the City may not disregard a still-operative injunction on its own initiative.

### Issue 1: Relief from the continuing Aguilar injunction

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone first identifies Rule 60(b)(5) as the channel governing relief from this continuing decree. The denial of relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with the governing legal premises subject to review as law. The significant-change requirement is not resolved finally before the Court addresses the continued authority of Aguilar, because Stone's proposed decision in Issue 2 would itself change the rule on which the prospective prohibition rests.

- **Stone's Legal Position:** Rufo requires a significant change in fact or law making continued enforcement inequitable, followed by a modification suitably tailored to the change. Mere dissatisfaction, a cheaper alternative, or an expected shift in votes does not suffice. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a relevant risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. The principles governing voluntarily accepted consent obligations do not transform this adjudicated injunction into a negotiated bargain.

  The lower courts remained bound by Aguilar. Kiryas Joel did not overrule it; the simulation's Zobrest did not decide the establishment merits; and the bounded access, attribution, and publication-benefit decisions did not establish that this onsite Title I program had already become lawful. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to follow the directly applicable precedent rather than predict its reversal.

  That obligation does not prevent this Court, in a case presenting Aguilar's continued validity, from expressly changing its own rule for a sufficient legal reason. If the Court adopts the change proposed in Issue 2, the change exists when the Court directs prospective relief. Stone does not require the City to return in a second motion solely to invoke the decision this Court has just rendered.

- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A ruling expressly displacing the constitutional premise of this continuing onsite prohibition supplies a significant change in law for Rule 60(b)(5). Continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The proper response is tailored modification under the new rule, not affirmance on the ground that the lower courts lacked authority to anticipate the change.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The injunction governs future provision of services. Its identified constitutional basis is Aguilar's treatment of public remedial teachers working inside religious schools and the supervision associated with that arrangement. Stone would reject that categorical premise for the legal reasons stated in Issue 2. The City would therefore establish a change pertinent to the obligation it seeks to modify if the Court adopts his proposed rule.

  The expense of off-premises delivery and the practical difficulty of reaching eligible children reinforce the need for prompt, tailored consideration of prospective relief. They do not establish constitutional validity by themselves. Stone does not need to find an independently sufficient factual change in those expenses to reach the proposed disposition, and he makes no unsupported finding about the size or timing of a factual change.

  Nor does he find the lower courts culpable for following Aguilar. The proposed vacatur follows the Court's newly announced law, not a conclusion that the lower courts should previously have treated unresolved doctrinal tension as overruling.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Rule 60(b)(5) remains a standard for modifying continuing relief, not a general license to reopen completed judgments. If the Court declines the relevant doctrinal change, Stone's proposal alone supplies no changed law. Any modification must rest on a rule actually adopted by the Court or another independently established ground for relief.

### Issue 2: Whether to update Lemon's general method and retain Aguilar's categorical rule

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The continued validity of the injunction requires the Court to decide whether Aguilar's governing constitutional rule should remain authoritative. Stone addresses the defect in the general method insofar as it produces that rule, proposes the update to that general method, and specifies its application to the program before the Court. He does not treat a desire for a different test as a reason to decide unpresented disputes about vouchers, monuments, legislative prayer, or accommodations.

- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone would keep Lemon as the governing test and update how it operates: its independent purpose, primary-effect, and excessive-entanglement prongs are restructured into the inquiry below. The constitutional concern is governmental exercise or sponsorship of religious authority and public financing of religious functions, while preserving private religious liberty and independently secular services. Purpose, effects, and institutional relationships remain evidence of the governmental act and its operation. They no longer operate as freestanding prongs or as an alternative test for ambiguous cases.

  The updated Lemon test has three routes within one inquiry. A specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct. An established permissible relationship defeats the establishment claim on that ground. A legally sufficient but unresolved allegation of prohibited conduct receives the attribution-and-function inquiry under ordinary procedure. Clear cases are applications of the updated test, not exemptions from review.

  In the residual inquiry, the court identifies the challenged governmental act and determines what function that act performs. Attribution may concern an official message, an organized event, the use of supervisory power, or an expenditure. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; it is unnecessary to make all of the recipient's religious conduct government speech. Official authorization of private access, religious identity, and physical location are relevant in context but do not individually determine the answer.

  Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, use public authority to solicit religious adherence, exercise theological governance, or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Government may purchase independently identifiable secular work in religious settings, with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of access to private exercise restricted by governmental custody or comparable control remains subject to a defined accommodation inquiry rather than an automatic financing prohibition.

  Actual conversion, religious success, or a completed penalty is unnecessary when the prohibited governmental act is established. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, the provider's religious motivation does not make its secular service devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular work is not an establishment merely because the contacts are repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain prohibited institutional relationships.

- **Stone's Legal Reason for Change:** Aguilar converts measures intended to preserve secular instruction into a categorical reason to exclude that instruction from religious premises. The resulting rule makes location and ordinary supervision substitutes for identifying religious sponsorship, devotional financing, or theological control. Stone concludes that this is an internal defect in the doctrine's legal boundary: an independently secular service can be condemned because it is supervised to remain secular, without a finding that the supervision governs religious affairs.

  That conclusion does not depend on proving that no religious school or public employee could ever divert a service. Nor does Stone claim that new empirical evidence has conclusively disproved every concern expressed in Aguilar. He rejects the legal sufficiency of the categorical inference. The proper inquiry retains protection against actual or necessarily designed devotional activity and improper institutional control, while distinguishing them from the administration of separately controlled secular work.

  The correction traces to the same constitutional concerns Lemon sought to implement: sponsorship, financial support of religious functions, and intrusion of civil and religious authority into each other's proper responsibilities. Stone changes the method because its categorical treatment of this relationship defeats that distinction. The costs and access difficulties make the defect consequential, but inconvenience alone is not the reason for overruling.

- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Religious-school location and ordinary secular supervision do not, without more, establish unconstitutional sponsorship, financing, or institutional control. Aguilar is overruled as the constitutional basis for categorically excluding the described public Title I remedial instruction from religious-school premises. Ball's corresponding onsite-instruction rationale is displaced to the extent it treats location, presumed inculcation, or incidental institutional benefit as sufficient without the prohibited governmental function. The Court does not reopen Ball's completed judgment or approve every program considered there.

- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The supplied record identifies children selected by educational need, public teachers using public materials, and public staffing and supervision of remedial services. It identifies no finding of actual indoctrination, transfer of public funds to the schools, or religious control of the public teachers. The governmental act to be examined is therefore the provision and supervision of the remedial instruction, not the surrounding institution's religious mission as a whole.

  Those affirmative features permit the service to be analyzed as independently secular. Absence of an adverse finding is not proof that every conceivable defect has been investigated and disproved. It is, however, insufficient to sustain a categorical religious-location ban after the Court rejects the inference on which that ban rested. Public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize devotional conduct if supported evidence establishes it; equally, religious premises do not establish such conduct without the relevant legal connection.

  Monitoring public teachers' performance and the content of the purchased service does not, on the supplied showing, establish governmental direction of religious doctrine or leadership. The court must distinguish supervision of the public service from regulation of the school's private religious affairs. It may examine any supported contention that the two are inseparable, but may not presume inseparability merely because the service occurs inside the school.

  The updated test requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for the funded work; it does not invariably require separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared premises or organizational structures cannot excuse devotional financing, but neither automatically defeats a genuinely severable public service.

  The proposed disposition removes the categorical basis of the injunction and permits the district court to implement the new boundary. It does not authorize an inquiry into hypothetical violations unsupported by the record or require a guarantee that future misconduct is impossible. Any further fact development must address a specified issue material under the updated test and proceed through ordinary procedure.

- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The case does not approve government purchase of devotional instruction. It does not decide a tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme. Stone's broader funded-use position, under which parental selection does not alone legitimate public purchase of religious formation, is part of his broader position but is not a merits holding on an absent program.

  Witters and Mueller retain their actual pre-divergence holdings unless expressly reconsidered in a case presenting their scope or validity. Stone acknowledges that his general funded-use position may require such reconsideration; he does not announce that those holdings already adopted it. Rosenberger's actual vendor-funded private-publication holding remains financing, not merely access, and is preserved on its specific terms. A religious editorial viewpoint in that program is not automatically classified as purchased devotional instruction. Pinette and Chabad retain their actual attribution and access rules; the categorical proposition that a symbol's central governmental location always establishes attribution is not carried into the updated test. Kiryas Joel's restrictions on denominational allocation of civil authority remain intact.

  Other specific doctrines, including denominational scrutiny, private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, and applicable accommodation rules, retain their own sources and burdens. Broad statements in this proposal do not silently overrule their concrete holdings.

## C. PROPOSITIONS SUBMITTED FOR THE SIMULATION'S VOTE

The simulation should record actual support for each proposition rather than treat a shared judgment as adoption of every reason:

1. **General method:** Update Lemon's independent three-prong method into a specific-rule, permissible-relationship, and residual attribution-and-function structure. Purpose, effects, and entanglement are evidence within that structure, not freestanding prongs or an alternative inquiry.
2. **Onsite secular services:** Reject religious location, presumed indoctrination from that location, and ordinary secular monitoring as sufficient grounds to prohibit the described public remedial-instruction service.
3. **Precedent treatment:** Overrule Aguilar's categorical rule and displace the corresponding Ball rationale to the extent specified, while preserving unaddressed holdings and the independent prohibitions on religious sponsorship and devotional financing.
4. **Prospective relief:** Treat the Court's adoption of the pertinent change as a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), vacate the judgments, and direct tailored removal of the injunction's now-unsupported onsite prohibition.

The full updated test states Stone's position on the larger field. The Court's law consists only of propositions actually adopted with the support required by the simulation. A Justice may support the judgment or the narrow onsite rule without joining the entire general method. The vote record must say so. No vote in this case automatically decides the test's unpresented examples.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone proposes a candid doctrinal change. The five-part statement required by his law of precedent is:

1. **Prior rule.** Lemon supplies the inherited general purpose, effect, and entanglement method. Aguilar applies that method to prohibit this onsite public Title I arrangement because of the institutional relationship and monitoring it entails. Ball supplies related reasoning concerning publicly provided instruction in religious schools. The supplied simulation does not contain an intervening merits holding that has already displaced the directly applicable rule.
2. **Legal reason for change.** The categorical treatment of location and ordinary secular supervision substitutes proxies for the religious sponsorship, financing, and institutional intrusion the Clause prohibits. It can condemn the means of preserving secular delivery without identifying government administration of religion. Stone corrects this internal defect openly, rather than asserting an unproved empirical collapse or a change justified by Court membership.
3. **New boundary.** The updated test identifies the governmental act and the prohibited or permissible function it performs. Government may provide genuinely separable secular instruction on religious premises and supervise its own service. It may not use that arrangement to conduct or finance religious formation or govern religious affairs. Specific prohibitions and distinct doctrines retain their force; ordinary procedure governs uncertain applications.
4. **What remains untouched.** Rufo's significant-change and tailored-modification requirements; Rodriguez de Quijas's rule for lower courts; the simulation's actual holdings in Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, and Chabad; the actual pre-divergence holdings of Witters and Mueller; the absence of an establishment merits holding in the simulation's Zobrest; independently supported restrictions on devotional financing, official worship, religious coercion, and improper allocation of civil authority; and all unrelated judgments and statutory duties. Preserving those specific holdings does not represent an adjudication that every possible future application of Stone's full updated test is compatible with them.
5. **Reliance.** Identifiable property, contractual, completed-transaction, and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. The supplied record identifies no concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement that requires retaining the categorical constitutional ban. The continuing protection against government-sponsored religion remains. Practical service continuity and any established contractual obligations are addressed in implementation, not converted into a permanent right to the discarded rule.

The First Amendment supplies the constitutional source. Stone's jurisprudence supplies his method for proposing the change, not legal authority binding the Court. The updated test is his proposal until the simulation adopts it. No later historical decision, later empirical study, or future Court vote is attributed to the Court sitting in 1997.

## E. CONCRETE RELIANCE AND LIMIT OF THE CHANGE

The proposed judgment changes the law governing prospective operation. It does not condemn the lower courts for following controlling precedent, validate past disobedience of the injunction, reopen completed claims, or cancel contracts by judicial declaration.

Children currently receiving services should continue to receive them while the lawful delivery arrangements are implemented. The City retains lawful discretion about delivery within statutory and constitutional limits. Any necessary implementation period must be tied to identified tasks and circumstances; the record supplies no basis for inventing a fixed number of days. Neither administrative convenience nor the desire for a transition permits indefinite enforcement of the rejected categorical prohibition.

The district court must identify the legal source of any obligation it preserves. This record concerns an adjudicated injunction; it supplies no basis for assuming additional negotiated obligations. If a genuinely independent statutory, contractual, or decree obligation is established, the change in the constitutional minimum does not automatically extinguish it.

Stone supports a narrow lawful alternative if the Court declines to adopt the full update: the Court may reject Aguilar's categorical treatment of this separately administered secular service, preserve Lemon outside the decided correction, and grant the same tailored prospective relief. That alternative is independently sufficient in Stone's view because the site-and-monitoring inference fails for the reasons stated here. He would describe the resulting decision as a narrow correction, not as an update of the Lemon test. No alternative assumes missing facts or attributes a holding to fewer than the required supporting Justices.
