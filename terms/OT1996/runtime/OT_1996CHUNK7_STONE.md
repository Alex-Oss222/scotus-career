## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

**Question 1 — permit these claims to proceed under a confined construction of §1350.** Stone would affirm rejection of categorical dismissal based on the defendant's nonstate status. He reads §1350's reception of law-of-nations torts against its common-law adjudicative setting as authorizing recognition of a narrow civil action for sufficiently determinate and obligatory norms of personal responsibility. The statute's jurisdictional wording is not an express damages cause; its historical statutory function supplies his proposed domestic authority for this received action. A grave wrong alone supplies neither that authority nor jurisdiction. This is the same proposed statutory reasoning he approved in Abebe-Jira, independently stated here because neither packet has become a Court holding.

That construction respects Suter's demand for an identified source and Central Bank's bounded received-action method. It does not convert Central Bank's securities assistance standard into the rule of command responsibility. The specific prohibition, the recognized basis for attributing responsibility to this defendant, causation and the claimed remedy each require their own support. At this stage the well-pleaded allegations are sufficient to defeat a categorical no-state/no-jurisdiction dismissal; the Court should not find the atrocities or Karadzic's participation proved.

**Question 2 — reject a universal state-action condition while preserving each offense's elements.** Stone would recognize that genocide can impose personal duties without official status and that the fundamental war-crimes rules applicable to parties to an armed conflict can bind an insurgent organization and responsible individuals. Common Article 3's minimum protections would be defeated by treating the absence of diplomatic recognition as permission to attack protected civilians. The allegations of a campaign against a protected population and conflict-related atrocities must, however, satisfy the particular offense's intent and contextual elements. Ordinary private assault does not become genocide merely because it is brutal.

For standalone torture or summary execution outside a proved genocide or war-crimes setting, Stone preserves the applicable official-action requirement. The alleged exercise of territorial governmental power and cooperation with an existing government require factual examination under the relevant norm; diplomatic nonrecognition alone does not decide functional color of law. He would not declare Srpska a diplomatically recognized state, dispense with TVPA conditions or invent a free-standing federal action for every private abuse. His Master commitment against legal validation of peremptory abuse does not create an action or defeat an immunity by itself.

**Question 3 — reject categorical political-question or foreign-affairs abstention dismissal.** Determining whether an individual committed a legally specified international tort does not require choosing Bosnia's government, fixing borders or setting the United States' diplomatic response. Unlike the particular Senate procedure in simulated Nixon, no identified constitutional commitment assigns adjudication of these personal tort elements exclusively to a political branch. The norm-specific inquiries above supply judicial standards. The actual United States Statement of Interest disclaims a political-question obstacle, reinforcing rather than replacing the Court's independent Baker inquiry. The State Department's invitee letter identifies no service immunity; Stone would not invent future head-of-state recognition as a present defense. The act-of-state argument was not preserved below, so he would not revive it as an additional ground for dismissal. A properly presented immunity or abstention rule retains its own source and conditions in a case that actually presents it; generalized diplomatic sensitivity supplies none here.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the Second Circuit's reversal of dismissal and remand of both Kadic and Doe actions. Permit the adequately pleaded genocide and war-crimes claims to proceed without a categorical state-action bar; permit development of the alleged official-action basis for claims that require it. Reject the presented categorical political-question objection. Leave personal responsibility, proof, remaining claim-specific defenses, alternative statutory requirements and relief for ordinary adjudication. No damages, class certification, civil command-responsibility formula or general §1331 cause is awarded by this disposition.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The jury's municipal-liability judgment remains in force. No ruling is made on individual-officer qualified immunity, which is not before the Court, or on any theory not included in the municipal judgment under review.

### Issue 1: Municipal liability for a final policymaker's single hire

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the county's preserved sufficiency challenge. The underlying excessive-force violation and Sheriff Moore's final policymaking authority are treated as established for this issue.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** When a final policymaker's single hiring decision may constitute deliberate municipal action that directly causes a later constitutional injury.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A final policymaker's single hire may support liability when the policymaker consciously disregards a substantial and highly predictable, applicant-specific risk that conferring the particular governmental authority will produce the kind of constitutional injury that occurs, and the hiring decision directly moves that injury. The standard does not require practical inevitability, which would collapse deliberate indifference into intentional authorization, but it requires much more than negligent screening, favoritism, or general unsuitability.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A final policymaker's single hiring decision may support municipal liability when the policymaker consciously disregards a substantial and highly predictable, applicant-specific risk that conferring the particular governmental authority will produce the kind of constitutional injury that occurs, and the decision directly moves that injury. This record permits that finding.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Moore conferred coercive arrest authority after obtaining but not meaningfully examining a record that included assault and battery, resistance to arrest, public drunkenness, and repeated lawbreaking. The jury could find that the risk closely corresponded to the authority granted and the excessive-force injury suffered, that Moore consciously disregarded it, and that the hire was the moving force. That is municipal fault by the County's own final policymaker, not respondeat superior.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Negligent screening, general unsuitability, or prior misconduct unrelated to the authority later abused is insufficient. The plaintiff must still prove an underlying violation, final policymaking authority, deliberate indifference, and direct causation. Individual qualified immunity cannot be transferred to the municipality.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

The approved single-hire rule is a proposed application of Monell, Pembaur, and Canton. Plakas supplies the distinct-elements discipline, not Stone’s proposed substantial-and-highly-predictable-risk formulation. The verdict may be retained only on evidence connecting this hiring choice to the kind of excessive force found, not merely because Moore was the final policymaker or related to Burns.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Third Circuit's judgment.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The judgment sustaining the Interstate Wagering Amendment remains in force. No separate remedial instruction is required.

### Issue 1: Pre-enforcement standing and ripeness

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone holds the challenge concrete and ripe. The statute forced Pic-A-State to stop the business Congress specifically targeted, resumption would expose it to serious criminal penalties, and the Government did not disavow enforcement.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A predominantly legal pre-enforcement challenge is justiciable when the statute directly targets the plaintiff's existing conduct, serious penalties attach to resumption, and the Government has not disavowed enforcement.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Pic-A-State's predominantly legal pre-enforcement challenge is fit for adjudication and presents a concrete Article III controversy.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The injury is neither hypothetical nor dependent on a discretionary chain. The company ceased its existing operation because the new offense expressly reached it, and the threatened penalty made resumption impracticable.

### Issue 2: Commerce power and the interstate-agreement exception

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the enacted offense and the legal effect of its interstate-agreement structure.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether § 1301 regulates interstate commerce itself and whether Congress may authorize state restrictions otherwise barred only by the dormant Commerce Clause.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The Amendment regulates a knowing interstate transmission in a commercial transaction to procure an interest in another State's lottery. It therefore reaches interstate commerce itself and need not be sustained by aggregating substantial effects. The interstate element performs real work. Congress may also unmistakably authorize state restrictions otherwise barred only by the dormant Commerce Clause, and § 1301's federal prohibition coupled with its interstate-agreement exception supplies that authorization.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Interstate Wagering Amendment regulates interstate commercial transmissions used to procure out-of-state lottery interests and therefore lies within Congress's commerce power. Its express interstate-agreement structure may authorize state restrictions that the dormant Commerce Clause alone otherwise would forbid.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Pic-A-State's business used interstate communications to consummate the economic substance of an out-of-state lottery purchase while leaving the paper ticket elsewhere. Congress regulated that cross-border procurement mechanism, not generalized gambling effects. The agreement exception permits States to alter the federally imposed rule by mutual authorization and thereby removes only the dormant implication.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not permit Congress to federalize local gambling through generalized effects, validate a nominal interstate element unrelated to the transaction, or authorize States to violate constitutional provisions other than the dormant Commerce Clause.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1–2 — preserved approved answers.** Stone would uphold the prohibition as direct regulation of the interstate procurement transmission. The operative interstate element supplies the connection that the original school-possession statute in Lopez lacked; no new substantial-effects aggregation or invented physical-ticket movement is needed.

**Question 3 — preserved approved answer.** State permission does not exempt an interstate commercial arrangement from a valid federal rule. Congress’s express agreement exception also permits the specified state restrictions against the dormant Commerce Clause objection. Stone would affirm without deciding ordinary local lottery administration or replacing the enacted federal exception.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Minnesota's absolute anti-fusion rule may not be enforced against a recognized party's nomination of a candidate who consents, where the other party has not objected and the party otherwise satisfies neutral ballot-access requirements. Relief is prospective and does not reopen the completed 1994 election. Minnesota may implement lawful ballot design, consent, counting, antifraud, deadline, and support rules. The Court does not decide whether a State may require the other party's consent as a condition of a fusion nomination.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Associational burden

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits because the law remains operative and directly governs the Party's nomination and ballot identity.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the absolute prohibition is justified as applied to a recognized party's nomination of a candidate who consents and whom another party has also nominated without objecting.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received Anderson-Burdick framework and assess the character and magnitude of the burden in operation. The burden is substantial but not severe, and no new scrutiny tier is created. The Eighth Circuit's strict-scrutiny reasoning is set aside. Burdick sustained Hawaii's write-in ban within a complete ballot-access system and separately reserved a genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate for contextual analysis. This case concerns a recognized party's choice of its own nominee, not access to the ballot.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Preventing a recognized political party from placing its own name beside a consenting chosen nominee imposes a substantial burden on the party's candidate-selection and ballot-communication interests, and on the ability of its voters to cast an effective vote for that candidate under that party's name. The burden is not severe, because the party remains on the ballot and may endorse and campaign for the candidate. It is more than slight, because endorsement outside the ballot does not replace the legally operative nomination the State has created.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A party acts through candidates and nominations. Minnesota lets the New Party onto the ballot but denies it the candidate it selected solely because another party also selected him. Voters who wish to support that candidate through that party cannot do so. The claim concerns vote power and effective choice, not the ballot as a forum for expression. Simulated Colorado Republican reinforces that shared candidate support does not erase a smaller party's independent identity, and Norman protects a party's authorized use of its name against a categorical bar broader than the State's concern required.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not recognize a right to a separate ballot line, to multiple vote credit, to a particular ballot format, or to use the ballot for general expression. Neutral support, deadline, format, and counting rules are untouched.

### Issue 2: Interests and fit

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Minnesota's asserted interests are necessary to the calibrated framework.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Weigh each asserted interest by its legitimacy, its strength, and the extent to which it makes the burden necessary. The evidentiary demand rises with the burden and does not require elaborate empirical proof. Munro and Jenness concern the winnowing of unsupported candidates. A recognized party with a consenting nominee has already met a support threshold.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** As applied, the absolute ban is not a reasonable, nondiscriminatory restriction justified by the interests Minnesota asserted. Candidate consent, recognized-party status, clear labeling, and single-vote counting address the integrity and confusion concerns, and the State offered no evidence of confusion, manipulation, or splintering. Political stability cannot mean preserving the competitive position of established parties. The Court does not decide whether a properly supported stability interest, tied to a particular restriction, could justify some limit on nominations.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The record identifies one consenting candidate, one qualified minor party, and no objection from the other party. It shows no fraud, duplicate counting, ballot congestion, or candidate objection. As Minnesota acknowledged at argument, ballot instructions and single counting are workable, fusion in other States has operated without major confusion, and a state legislative race has no effect on a major party's statutory status, which removes the claim that fusion lets a minor party bootstrap itself to major-party status. Consolidating support behind one consenting candidate does not splinter the vote. Minnesota pressed political stability only weakly and tied it to nothing in this record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially different system presenting a nominee's refusal, an objection by the other party, misleading labels, multiple vote credit, unmanageable ballots, or evasion of valid support rules may be regulated. The Court does not decide whether a State may require the other party's consent, a rule that would raise its own question about giving an established party a veto over a recognized party's nominee. Stone does not require a universal ballot format.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone applies the received Anderson-Burdick framework and the simulated Burdick holding. He distinguishes Burdick's sustained write-in ban as an access question inside a complete system, and relies on its reservation for a genuine interest in supporting an actual alternative candidate. Norman, in Souter's opinion joined by six other Justices including Stone, protects a party's authorized name and rejects categorical bars broader than needed. Colorado Republican protects a party's independent identity without deciding fusion. The operative burden is loss of the qualified party's legally effective nomination of a consenting candidate and its distinct ballot identity. Stone finds the absolute ban insufficiently fitted on this record. He does not treat his Burdick dissent as law and does not demand strict scrutiny for every ballot restriction. No precedent is overruled.

ERISA expressly defines plan to include welfare plans and uses narrower pension or nonforfeitable terminology elsewhere. Courts may not insert vesting as an additional element.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** ERISA § 510 reaches discharge intended to interfere with attainment of rights under an employee welfare benefit plan even though those benefits are not subject to ERISA's vesting requirements.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress knew how to distinguish pension from welfare plans and how to refer to nonforfeitable benefits. It instead used the inclusive term plan and the forward-looking phrase may become entitled. An employer's authority to amend or terminate a welfare plan does not authorize it to evade the formal plan process by discharging participants for the prohibited purpose. Whether these employees had already attained the particular welfare rights or can prove specific intent remains open.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not vest welfare benefits, freeze plan terms, establish prohibited purpose, or resolve liability of a defendant that was not the employer or plan administrator.
