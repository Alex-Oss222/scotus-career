## Common factual and statutory limits

CBE gave notice on March 16, 1995, to the Company, EPA and the appropriate Illinois authorities. The Company then supplied the overdue reports; the reports were current when the complaint was filed on August 7, 1995. C confirms no EPA enforcement during the notice interval. N supplies allegations that members live near or visit the facility and use the information for chemical-risk assessment and emergency preparation. N supplies no allegation of an ongoing or imminent future reporting violation. The members' interests are therefore more particular than an abstract demand that every company obey every law, but that distinction does not answer whether a judgment would redress a remaining injury.

The prayer seeks a declaration of past violation, periodic access to the facility and records at the Company's expense, copies of future compliance reports, Treasury penalties, and investigation/prosecution expenses through the litigation-cost clause. No damages entitlement, continuing withheld report or continuing information deficit is supplied. The old reporting pattern and annual obligations are relevant to the strongest deterrence argument; they are not converted into a pleaded imminent violation.

Section 11046(a)(1)(A)(iii) concerns completion and submission under §11022(a), with its tier-II alternative. Section 11022(a)(2)'s March 1 tier-I requirement does not apply when tier-II information for the same year goes to the same recipients by the same deadline. Section 11046(a)(1)(A)(iv) separately concerns the toxic-release form under §11023(a), ordinarily due July 1 for the preceding calendar year. Coverage, thresholds, reporting-frequency provisions and the information requirements retain their statutory qualifications; none is newly found satisfied beyond the supplied posture.

Section 11046(c) authorizes enforcement of the requirement concerned and imposition of its civil penalty. Section 11045(c)(1) imposes liability to the United States of no more than $25,000 for each §11022 or §11023 violation by a nongovernmental person; §11045(c)(3) counts each continuing day separately. The maximum is not a mandatory award and the recipient is not CBE.

The §11046(d)(1) notice-and-waiting condition and §11046(e) enforcement bar remain distinct: the latter requires that EPA have commenced and be diligently pursuing the specified administrative order or civil action. No defect under either provision is added. Section 11046(f) permits an appropriate award to a prevailing or substantially prevailing party when issuing a final order; it does not compel an award or expressly create damages for late information. Section 11046(g) preserves existing rights without creating a new claim. Section 11022(f)'s inspection entitlement belongs to the requesting fire department with jurisdiction; it is not an automatic CBE inspection right.

## Alternative-path assessment by component

### A. Constitutional jurisdiction and decisional sequence

**Path A1 — decide Article III first.** Lujan, Brown Group, Arizonans and Bennett preserve an independently necessary personal controversy, irrespective of statutory permission. Young distinguishes a substantial federal claim's merits from statutory adjudicatory power. The question whether this particular citizen cause reaches completed late filings therefore cannot simply be renamed lack of jurisdiction because §11046(c) uses that word. Its heading, structure and incorporation of subsection (a) must be assessed; a restriction on actionable conduct or obtainable relief remains capable of being a merits restriction.

**Strongest contrary path A2 — statutory threshold or limited merits-first decision.** Section 11046(c) expressly confers jurisdiction in actions brought under subsection (a), making a statutory-threshold reading arguable. Norton and Avrech also establish that the Court has reserved jurisdictional questions rather than exhaustively deciding them before every adverse disposition. The Company could seek resolution through a statutory ground before a difficult constitutional question.

**Assessment and limits.** Norton involved difficult appellate and statutory-route questions after a contemporaneous decision had already settled the merits; either route had the same practical effect. Avrech likewise had intervening merits authority. EPCRA's disputed reach is a live issue here, not a foreordained application. Neither case authorizes deciding this new substantive question merely because dismissal under a preferred construction seems convenient. McCardle supports the need for adjudicatory authority but involved withdrawal of a statutory appellate route; it is not silently enlarged into a decision of every possible ordering question. Arizonans preserves power to correct a lower court's jurisdictional error without adjudicating the merits. The bounded common conclusion is that Article III supplies the appropriate first and sufficient ground here. The broader sequencing positions of individual Justices appear below.

### B. Injury and traceability

**Path B1 — recognize a personal informational injury.** Required information about a facility near members' homes or activities can matter to their own preparations and assessment of risks. The loss of timely access need not be indistinguishable from an abstract interest in enforcement. Kennedy and Souter's Lujan concurrence allows Congress to identify concrete injuries and causal chains; Stevens's Lujan position and O'Connor's join in the dissent recognize concrete use interests and reasonable inferences. Bennett and Brown Group, joined by all eight Associates, preserve pleading-stage treatment of a concretely alleged interest. Section 11023(h) expressly identifies public informational uses.

**Strongest contrary path B2 — statutory entitlement is insufficient by itself.** Lujan does not permit Congress's permission to sue to replace injury. Proximity and a general desire for reports do not necessarily establish every claimed informational harm. Information already supplied before filing also distinguishes the availability of prospective relief from a past deprivation.

**Commitment and boundary.** Each Justice can assume for this disposition that the described past deprivation is concrete and fairly traceable to the Company's own nonfiling. No independent causation defect based on absent agencies is asserted. No Justice is committed here to finding informational injury categorically insufficient or to holding that every failure to meet EPCRA deadlines injures every resident. Because redressability is independently deficient, the judgment need not decide B1 versus B2 at a broader level. The distinction between a completed injury and a threatened injury remains necessary when examining particular relief.

### C. Redressability, each requested remedy, and remand

**Path C1 — jurisdictional dismissal.** Even assuming the personal past injury and statutory authority to pursue a penalty, the plaintiff must show a likely concrete benefit from the relief requested. The reports were current before filing. No alleged ongoing or imminent violation ties prospective coercion or deterrence to these members' future information needs. This conclusion does not rely on the noncontrolling three-Justice application of Lujan to absent agencies.

**Strongest contrary path C2 — allow the action or order bounded further proceedings.** Congress sought timely information and selected penalties, notice and potential costs to encourage private enforcement. A long history of nonfiling followed only by notice-induced compliance can support a practical concern about recurrence. Members retain their nearby activities and annual information interests. Church of Scientology requires only meaningful partial relief, while Bennett protects reasonable pleading inferences rather than certainty.

**Why C2 does not sustain this pleaded action.** Annual duties and past nonfiling do not, on the supplied allegations, identify a presently withheld report or an imminent repetition. The claim of concrete benefit cannot rest solely on the plaintiff's success in seeing public law enforced. Church of Scientology involved retained copies on which a judicial order could operate; Bennett involved an alleged continuing water constraint. A remedy could remove something concrete in each. Here, compliance preceded suit, and no comparable present object of relief is alleged. This is initial standing, not a postfiling voluntary-cessation case. A remand to invent recurrence, to presume a right to discovery, or to cure the operator's missing original documents is unwarranted.

### D. EPCRA temporal scope and statutory disposition

**Path D1 — missed deadlines remain within the citizen cause after late filing.** Subsection (a) addresses failure to complete and submit forms under §§11022(a) and 11023(a), whose deadlines are part of the obligation. Unlike the Clean Water Act formulation considered in Gwaltney, EPCRA does not use the same present-violation language. Notice can correct mistakes, end accumulation of additional daily violations, permit diligent EPA enforcement and encourage settlement even if it does not erase accrued liability. C develops these textual and structural arguments; the official text preserves their actual qualifications.

**Strongest contrary path D2 — citizen enforcement requires an unperformed reporting obligation when suit begins.** The specified act is completion and submission, which was accomplished before the complaint. Section 11046(c)'s enforcement language and the notice interval can be read as a supplemental compliance mechanism, leaving wholly past penalties to government enforcement. Meghrig requires attention to the enacted private cause and remedies rather than an assumed general enforcement purpose.

**Assessment and commitment.** D1 has substantial textual force; D2 has a genuine structural argument. Meghrig's current-endangerment language and remedy limitation do not determine EPCRA's distinct formulation, while Gwaltney's result cannot be transplanted without comparing its operative text. None of the eight commitments adopts D1 or D2 as an alternative holding after concluding that Article III is absent. A Justice's willingness to discuss their relative force does not authorize a vote for a statutory judgment. The statute's temporal reach, violations, penalty amount and EPA's available enforcement remain undecided.

The strongest argument for making D2 an independent jurisdictional ground is addressed under A2. The members' lack of a redressable stake makes it unnecessary to settle every classification issue surrounding §11046(c), and no Justice is committed to rewriting the statutory merits disagreement as a jurisdictional fact.

### Entering law and distinct components

Blessing, April 21, 1997, is controlling current-term law. Its unanimously joined individual-right inquiry requires a particular intended beneficiary, a binding obligation, and an entitlement sufficiently determinate for judicial enforcement; mandatory administrative duties alone do not suffice. A proved right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress forecloses it. General funding oversight does not alone foreclose enforcement.

Suter's March 25, 1992 holdings independently reject the asserted §671(a)(15) reasonable-efforts and §671(a)(9) reporting rights and direct implied actions. Its individual-right and remedy limits survive; it supplies no categorical state-plan rule. Livadas, June 13, 1994, keeps right, §1983 channel, foreclosure, defendant, and relief separate. Wright and Wilder remain operative. The Social Security Act's state-plan-location amendments do not directly apply to CAPTA.

The operative §5106a(b)(2) imposes two different commands: initiation of an investigation promptly after receipt of a report of known or suspected abuse or neglect, to substantiate the report's accuracy; and immediate protective steps after a finding of abuse or neglect, extending to other children under the same care who may be endangered. The asserted claim reviewed here concerns the first command. No preinjury abuse finding activates the second on this record. The limited statutory waiver machinery is retained, but no District waiver is established. The later four-year-plan requirement cannot be imposed on 1989 conduct. An implied CAPTA action is not asserted.

Sandin's June 19, 1995 substantive-constraint rule, Pinder's May 21, 1996 distinction between omissions and an official's own affirmative deprivation, and Uhlrig's February 18, 1997 conduct-time immunity holding do not create an investigative liberty interest. DeShaney denies a general affirmative protection duty outside custody while leaving independently created entitlements analytically distinct.

### Statutory alternatives and legal grounds

**Affirm the statutory dismissal.** The funding provision obliges the recipient to provide a system in which reports lead promptly to investigation, but does not confer the asserted individual federal entitlement to an investigative response. The particular report trigger is important, yet a mandatory administrative response can remain a public institutional duty. Suter's separate reporting holding is the closest controlling analogy: an identified child's predicament and a specific required official response do not alone establish an individual right. The conclusion cannot rest merely on funding, plan placement, a supposed absence of all mandatory duties, or an unasserted implied-action theory.

**Strongest objection to affirmance:** Unlike aggregate compliance or staffing, the command identifies a report, its child beneficiary, and a concrete act that must begin promptly. A complete failure to act can be evaluated without defining every investigative technique. The affirming ground answers that judicial manageability and intended benefit are necessary but not sufficient to turn an institutional reporting-and-investigation obligation into a private entitlement; both the text's recipient focus and Suter's analogous reporting limit remain material.

**Reverse the statutory dismissal and remand.** The provision can instead be read to confer the limited right of a child identified in a received abuse or neglect report to prompt initiation of an appropriate investigation directed to that report's accuracy. It does not guarantee a particular outcome, removal, universal numerical deadline, or freedom from all private violence. The duty to start a concrete investigation is different from a claim to general agency adequacy.

**Strongest objection to reversal:** A mandatory governmental duty is not necessarily an individual right, and Suter rejected a similarly child-specific reporting claim. The reversing ground distinguishes a limited claim to the service Congress expressly commands for the reported child from the broad caseworker relief supported by Suter's reporting theory. “Promptly” permits application to concrete alleged complete inaction, while choices about how to investigate remain discretionary. That is a proposed provision-specific distinction, not a holding that every beneficiary may enforce every grant condition.

**Remedy on reversal:** Only the CAPTA dismissal would be reversed. The lower court would address the conduct-time duty, actual reports and timing, breach, personal responsibility or lawful municipal attribution, causation, applicable immunity, and relief. No damages, injunction, staff complement, removal decision, or fixed deadline follows. On either path, ordinary federal oversight and local statutory duties remain.

**Event / research cutoff:** Merits decision, June 9, 1997; argument February 19, 1997. No later law is used. ### Entering law, components and disposition paths

Walmer's January 13, 1997 Part I controls the ordinary rational-basis standard in the conduct-based preliminary setting it decided. It permits conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable military predictions and imperfect categories; it requires a relation to the actual disadvantage and excludes bare harm or irrational prejudice. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer joined that part. Walmer expressly reserves the statutory statements rule and an orientation-only inference. Its preliminary disposition neither decides this final discharge nor creates an individualized-impairment requirement.

Romer's ordinary-review holding and the separate proof-burden and family-participation holdings in Heller remain operative. Neither Romer nor Virginia adopts heightened orientation scrutiny. Dawson and Mitchell permit speech to establish a constitutionally relevant fact; they do not transform an ordinary identity statement into an admission of a particular act. Weiss preserves substantial respect for military judgments with independent constitutional review. Waters supplies no automatic actual-disruption requirement, no mandatory investigation checklist and no least-severe-discipline rule.

Section 654(b)(2) is the route used here. It requires separation on the approved statement finding unless the further finding establishes that the member is not a person who engages in, attempts to engage in, has a propensity to engage in, or intends to engage in homosexual acts. The directive's reproduced definition makes propensity a likelihood of acts, beyond abstract preference or desire. The (b)(1) five-part actual-act retention exception is a different rule; (b)(3) marriage is not this finding. Section 654(e)'s exception to mandatory processing requires both a service-avoidance purpose and a determination that separation would not serve the military's best interest; neither premise is established here. Congress's subsection (a) findings are legislative judgments, not findings of Thomasson's conduct or impaired unit performance.

The two principal lawful paths are:

* **T-A — Affirm at the conduct-related, rebuttable-inference scope.** A voluntary statement of orientation may rationally indicate likely sexual conduct even though it proves no completed prohibited act. Congress may use that inference for preventive military personnel decisions, subject to genuine rebuttal. This Board received the submitted evidence; favorable general service evidence does not necessarily rebut the distinct conduct/propensity predicate, and the member declined further specific rebuttal. The First Amendment does not immunize a relevant statement from that evidentiary use. The strongest objection is that the policy permits private orientation but imposes a burden to disprove future noncompliance upon its disclosure, while an exemplary service history supports obedience. This path answers that likelihood need not be certainty, predictive personnel rules need not await misconduct, and the offered rebuttal remains materially different from evidence negating the predicate. It does not rest on military immunity or a universal civilian-employment rule.
* **T-R — Reverse this application under ordinary equal protection.** When the statement discloses identity and opposition to the policy without admitting an act or intent to disobey, the Government cannot sustain this discharge by treating that disclosure alone as an unrebutted likelihood of prohibited conduct. The service history supplies context, but the legal defect is the relation of this statement-based burden and discharge to the claimed conduct predicate, not a right earned by good performance. The strongest objection is that ordinary rationality tolerates classwide predictions and places the burden on the challenger, especially under Walmer. This path accepts those principles but finds the inference insufficient at its actual scope: a general attraction does not establish likely noncompliance merely because it is honestly disclosed. A statement admitting acts, plans, intent or otherwise contextually showing a likelihood of prohibited conduct is different. This application ruling neither invalidates all conduct rules nor creates heightened scrutiny.

An additional **T-S** path treats the same as-applied defect as an independent First Amendment violation: the Government may use relevant statements as evidence, but a protected disclosure cannot itself supply the missing disqualifying fact through an impermissible inference. Its strongest objection is the ordinary evidentiary relevance of orientation and the military's preventive role. A Justice selecting T-S must reject that connection here, rather than say that statements can never be evidence.

A purported construction-only remand is not selected. The reproduced directive already distinguishes likelihood from abstract preference, and the source does not show that the Board excluded evidence, demanded a denial of identity or applied an unreported different instruction. It would be improper to manufacture that legal-error premise to avoid choosing between T-A and T-R. Nor does this handoff adopt Luttig's separate invalidation of the directive: the scope and preservation needed for that additional disposition are not established by the grant.

**Event / research cutoff:** Merits decision, June 12, 1997. The supplied summary-judgment review and ordinary appellate channel govern. No later accommodation doctrine is used.

### Common law and competing applications

All eight Associates accept the controlling Title VII accommodation law they directly joined in Beadle (February 5, 1996), Brown (March 17, 1997) and Opuku-Boateng (April 7, 1997). Section 2000e(j) supplies an affirmative accommodation duty; §2000e(m) places production and persuasion on the employer for its hardship demonstration. Hardison's then-governing more-than-de-minimis standard remains; reasonable operational forecasts and concrete noncash burdens count. Ansonia ends the alternatives inquiry after an effective reasonable accommodation. No independent duty to negotiate, experiment, wait for injury, monetize every burden or exhaust every conceivable alternative is added.

The three separable questions are notice and timing; the independent hardship defense; and whether summary judgment survives. Neither ground is supplied merely by a legitimate nondiscriminatory disciplinary reason, because the disparate-treatment theory is outside this appeal. No constitutional employee-speech claim is introduced.

**N — Shared notice proposition:** Knowledge need not come through a formal request or particular words. Direct or indirect knowledge may sufficiently reveal the sincere religious practice and employment conflict in time to consider accommodation. Knowledge of someone's faith is not notice of every conceivable practice. Timing matters to the accommodation actually sought and the possibility of avoiding or limiting harm; neither all indirect notice nor all notice received before discipline is categorically insufficient. The lower majority itself reserved ordinary manifestations such as ashes and religious headwear. No Justice adopts the inaccurate premise that it categorically barred all indirect notice.

**H — Shared hardship proposition:** The employer must connect the practice and proposed accommodation to supported business burdens. Supervisory pressure, credible relationship damage, privacy effects and reasonable prevention of coercion may count; neither supervisory status, offense nor the possibility of a lawsuit automatically does so. Liability for harassment need not already have accrued. A disputed material inference must be taken favorably to Chalmers on Tulon's summary-judgment motion.

The competing applications are:

* **C-A — Affirm this judgment.** The previously tolerated religious conversations did not notify Tulon that Chalmers needed to send these unsolicited, personally corrective letters into coworkers' homes. By the time the letters supplied explicit knowledge, the particular intrusion and damage had occurred. Independently, the established distress, interruption of LaMantia's business presentation, reported breakdown of his working relationship with the center's only resident manager, and a supervisory letter to a convalescing subordinate support a more-than-de-minimis business burden. A warning does not undo the completed burden or establish an effective alternative; no absolute duty to impose lesser discipline follows. The strongest counterargument is that the employer knew the religious nature before discharge and could preserve employment under prospective limits. This path finds the requested leniency insufficient on this concrete record without adopting a categorical rule against religious letters.
* **C-R — Vacate and remand for proper accommodation adjudication.** Prior conversations and clear religious notice before discharge permit the accommodation claim to reach the employer's statutory defense; exact advance notice of each manifestation is not dispositive here. The letters' content, Chalmers's account of unchanged work relations, her explanation and apology, and the distinction between allowing future objectionable letters and retaining an employee with prospective boundaries make the lower court's categorical impossibility conclusion inadequate for summary judgment. The strongest objection is that the distress and business interruption are real, a manager says he cannot work with her, and the employer need not conduct an experiment or undo completed harm. This path accepts those facts and the forecast rule but requires the defense, rather than an adverse characterization or third-party ultimatum alone, to establish inability to accommodate under Rule 56. It does not find any particular warning feasible or require trial regardless of a properly supported renewed disposition.

**Record conditions binding both paths:** LaMantia's letter did not expressly accuse him of adultery; Chalmers says she meant inaccurate customer turnaround representations. His wife's distress and his reported marital strain remain real. The Combs letter criticizes adultery and refers to divinely caused sickness while expressly disclaiming an assertion that this explains Combs's illness. The supervisor/subordinate relationship is material. Combs's later no-offense/no-relationship-damage statement is unsworn, and the separate unsworn Faber account was expressly not used below. The alleged Bible-study invitation was not formally attached to the summary-judgment response. None is silently promoted into competent Rule 56 proof. The remanding position can stand on the letters, ordinary prior conversations and Chalmers's own admissible account without those disputed materials; the affirming position does not rely on the excluded Faber exhibit.

## 115. Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash — retained collateral

## 117. Mazurek v. Armstrong — preliminary relief under actual Casey

### Complete governing inquiry and alternatives

**M1:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand for application of the complete controlling Casey framework and ordinary preliminary-relief standards. The District Court's all-legislators purpose premise was erroneous; the Ninth Circuit correctly required objective attention to purpose and actual hardships, but its substantial-obstacle/purpose formulation is incomplete in this Court's law. This is a correction of the remand instructions, not a direction to enter an injunction or find an unconstitutional purpose.

The framework that M1 requires is:

1. Identify the actual physician-only duty, its authoritative construction, exceptions and sanctions, the regulated clinical work and the protected baseline. Previability choice may not be prohibited, placed under third-party control or deprived of timely care necessary for life or health.
2. Determine the duty's actual function and objectively supported obstructive purpose from text, design, context and operation. An established prohibited purpose independently invalidates the duty. An abortion opponent's sponsorship, opposition to abortion or knowledge that Cahill alone is affected is relevant context but not a conclusive substitute for that inquiry.
3. Require challengers to establish the actual added burden, stable affected population and causal connection through concrete, nontrivial evidence, including reliable prospective evidence. Evaluate genuinely usable alternatives, capacity, travel, cost and delay against actual conditions without double counting or merely assuming that another provider can absorb the work.
4. An established baseline violation, prohibited purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid and cannot be rescued by balancing benefits. Remaining severe interference requires compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful lesser interference requires the State to substantiate a permissible justification and fit and answer a concretely identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose and other independently preserved constitutional claims remain. No new-study, scientific-certainty, universal numerical facial threshold or perfect-legislation requirement applies.
5. Give competent medical evidence and professional judgment their appropriate weight without a professional veto. Clinical judgment and necessary care remain protected; prior approval cannot obstruct timely necessary care. The present case supplies no separate compelled-communication question.
6. Match preliminary or final relief to the duty, applications and population for which a violation and the relevant equitable predicates are supported. Preserve separable lawful duties; broader relief requires corresponding proof. This is not an automatic all-applications barrier or automatic facial invalidity.

Cahill's long-standing supervised, board-approved practice supplies a concrete alternative for examination under the applicable tier. It does not itself prove a meaningful patient burden, a feasible alternative constitutionally sufficient in every circumstance, absence of health benefit, or unconstitutional targeting. The absence of a proved substantial obstacle does not settle all other branches.

Casey's separate invalidation of exclusive physician delivery of routine consent information did not decide who may perform an abortion. Its protection of individualized diagnosis and professional responsibility likewise did not categorically approve every physician-only performance rule. The actual challenged clinical duty must be examined under the complete framework; neither that informational holding nor historical Casey wording supplies an automatic answer.

**M2, rival:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and reinstate denial of preliminary relief. The existing showing does not warrant disrupting a reasonable legislative physician-qualification requirement; individual targeting and opposition sponsorship are insufficient, and the asserted effects have not shown loss of practical access. Kennedy's published reasonable-regulation approach and Scalia's/Thomas's constitutional position support this result independently. Their method is not the governing complete framework. A narrower M2 under that framework would require a supported determination that every preserved ground fails, not merely absence of a substantial obstacle.

## 118. Crawford-El v. Britton — proof quantum, immunity and discovery

### Components and adversarial alternatives

**C1:** Reject a special clear-and-convincing burden for unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation action. Ordinary legally competent direct or circumstantial proof remains available. Kimberlin already separated the underlying constitutional state of mind from the objective immunity defense and preserved ordinary procedural burdens; it expressly did not adopt the alternative heightened quantum. Deciding this reserved quantum question now requires the additional conclusion that neither §1983, Harlow's objective defense, this underlying First Amendment claim nor the governing Rules supplies it.

**C2:** Reject a categorical rule demanding proof meeting that new quantum before the claimant can obtain necessary, properly limited discovery. The lower court's absolute no-discovery proposal was not a majority holding; its competing approaches must be evaluated accurately. Courts may resolve a dispositive legal immunity defense first, insist on specific nonconclusory facts identifying the challenged conduct, use rule-authorized clarification and replies, stage discovery, and enter protective orders. Rule 56's affidavit-based procedures and ordinary evidentiary duties remain real. No unlimited discovery or categorical right to depose an official follows.

**C3:** Vacate the lower judgment to the extent it imposes the heightened quantum and dependent directions, and remand with the existing retaliation/conversion remand otherwise preserved. No disputed factual account is selected; no final immunity, liability, motive, causal connection, injury or damages is adjudicated.

**Rival A, heightened proof:** Harlow protects against litigation burdens, not simply adverse liability. Motive allegations are cheap and subjective; a higher quantum could protect officials from costly meritless suits. New York Times and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby show that heightened substantive burdens can influence summary judgment. The answer is that the speech-protective constitutional basis of the defamation burden is absent here, and Liberty Lobby applies an independently valid burden rather than authorizing its creation. Ordinary rules plus early legal immunity rulings respond to the legitimate concern without changing what proves this constitutional wrong.

**Rival B, plausible lawful purpose:** Britton's handling could reflect safekeeping and transfer policy, so an objectively reasonable lawful explanation should end the case regardless of actual motive. Settle's unanimous causation ruling defeats that categorical alternative: a merely possible lawful reason is not a proved same-decision defense. Claimant must still establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; defendant may prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. Neither mixed motives nor an asserted bad purpose automatically establishes recovery.

**Rival C, immediate alternative dismissal:** The interval between alleged threats and property delivery, comparable treatment of other prisoners, small losses and alleged legitimate explanation may defeat the claim under ordinary rules. On this grant, however, the lower court's new-proof-dependent assessment is not an independent finding that permits this Court to resolve contested inferences and credibility. Neither the constitutional protection nor the injury issue is rewritten merely to avoid the granted legal question.
