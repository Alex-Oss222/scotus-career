## 2. Components and operative record

The operative Fifth Circuit opinion is 67 F.3d 1174, October 23, 1995, which withdrew the earlier 53 F.3d 1410 opinion in full. It sustained County liability on federal hiring, leaving the separate federal training basis and state issues unanalyzed. The withdrawn opinion's training treatment cannot itself dispose of the surviving verdict. Rehearing was denied November 29, 1995.

The special verdict found Burns liable for false arrest, excessive force, and false imprisonment, and rejected his qualified-immunity defenses. It separately answered for Brown on **federal deliberately indifferent hiring, federal deliberately indifferent training, state negligent hiring, and state negligent training**. Those four County grounds are not a single hiring finding. The federal charge required inadequacy, deliberate indifference, and direct causation; the state charge used ordinary negligence and proximate cause. The verdict does not supply special findings resolving each criminal-history entry or Moore's actual knowledge of it.

The fifteen damage items totaled $927,302. The court removed $36,000 past earnings and $180,000 future earning capacity, leaving **$711,302 actual damages against County and Burns jointly and severally**. Trial attorney fees were **$65,000 jointly and severally**. **$20,000 punitive damages were against Burns alone**. Conditional appellate fees were separate: $12,500 for the Fifth Circuit, $5,000 for a certiorari application, and $5,000 upon a certiorari grant. The resulting $87,500 combined fee figure is not a $87,500 trial-fee award. The judgment carries costs and 3.43 percent annual interest from September 20, 1993. The actual-damage items include $50,000 for violation of the excessive-force constitutional right and $50,000 for loss of liberty; the present handoff does not independently decide their treatment if only a state ground ultimately survives.

Brown's failure to object to a postverdict earnings reduction outside the County's motion led to the appellate plain-error treatment of that reduction. It does **not** establish that the County failed to preserve its municipal fault, policy, or causation challenges. The close-of-plaintiff Rule 50 motion expressly attacked those matters and the state-law exemptions; the County reports renewal at the close of all evidence; the September 20 order rejects the municipal sufficiency and single-incident grounds and the two state exemptions. The actual Rule 51 excerpts contain objections to treating this hire as policy, lack of a pattern, the deficiency and causal requirements, the hiring/training interrogatories, the charge's constitutional-needs terminology, and the treatment of restrictions on Burns's authority. Review of hiring sufficiency is available. There is no commitment that all instructions were unobjected to, that every possible later verbal formulation was preserved, or that a newly decisive uncopied preservation fact should be assumed.

The stipulation identifies Moore as County policymaker regarding the Sheriff's Department. The operative lower opinion treats his relevant final authority as stipulated, and the charge identifies him as policymaker. The County nevertheless disputes whether hiring discretion amounts to final employment policy and invokes state standards and the commissioners' role. These are separate questions. For this case's presented hiring decision, each Justice proceeds on the stipulation, trial treatment, and appellate posture attributing Moore's final selection to the County; none announces that every discretionary act of every sheriff is County policy. The objection that state eligibility standards necessarily exhaust all County policy fails: a minimum eligibility rule does not establish that the final selector lacks a choice among eligible applicants. This limited treatment does not independently settle Oklahoma's allocation of every personnel power or declare the County's broader policy objections waived.

The evidence must be evaluated in its actual form:

- The record enumerates **one assault-and-battery conviction**, multiple traffic convictions, false identification, public drunkenness, expired tags, suspended-license matters, and alcohol-related actual physical control. Actual physical control is not silently relabeled driving under the influence. Resisting arrest appears as a charge, not an established conviction. Four assault references on the OSBI printout may contain duplication; Barnes also acknowledged the possibility of more than one complainant. Neither four violent convictions nor a conclusive finding of one harmless pushing incident follows. Burns's own description of the campus encounter remains his testimony, not a jury finding excusing it.
- Moore obtained background information, knew traffic and alcohol-related history, and probably noticed suspensions. He denied noticing the assault, resistance, and false-identification entries. No finding establishes that he actually read every entry. The plaintiff's supported path concerns both information available to him and his failure to investigate it. The actual outstanding warrant does not prove notice through NCIC, because Moore testified that his check disclosed no warrant. Probation and noncompliance evidence remain subject to the limiting instructions; testimony about substituting undercover work for punishment was struck and is not used.
- Prior misconduct was admitted on the propriety of permitting Burns to serve, not as proof by propensity that he committed the May 12 acts. The jury's underlying constitutional findings supply the latter premise. Family connection, the grandfather's service, and unexplained failure to follow up are relevant context, not independent constitutional wrongs or conclusive proof of favoritism.
- Schweizer assessed the combined recent history as a risk for police abuse; Barnes identified reasons for inquiry and doubts about hiring, with qualifications. Those opinions support an inference but do not themselves establish the federal legal standard. State eligibility was not an adjudication of adequate constitutional screening. The state scheme included felony or moral-turpitude disqualification and other qualifications; no new violation of those provisions is found here.
- CLEET began May 6, six days before May 12. Barnes corrected the longer estimate. The claim that Burns had attended at most three classes, perhaps none, is counsel's inference rather than a special finding. His testimony about County instruction changed between deposition and trial; the County's recitation of ride-along instruction rests partly on omitted trial pages. Neither total absence of instruction nor a completed adequate program is stipulated.
- Moore restricted firearms and patrol-car driving and described a supervised, learning role with arrests on a deputy's direction. Burns understood another officer to be in charge, but no actual instruction to pursue, extract, take down, or cuff Brown was established in his testimony. This record supports neither an uncontested unrestricted commission nor an uncontested effective prohibition on the physical activities that occurred. Later uses of the arm-bar technique and lack of complaints do not establish Moore's preselection knowledge. An expert's description of an arm bar as a low-level technique does not undo the jury's finding about the force actually used.

## 3. Common legal commitments and procedural limits

All eight maintain the separation required by **Monell, Pembaur, Canton, Collins, Leatherman, and the municipal portion of Plakas**: an underlying federal deprivation; a decision properly attributable to the municipality; the necessary municipal fault; and a causal connection between that fault and the particular deprivation. Employment alone supplies none of the last three. Collins rejects an additional freestanding governmental-abuse element; it does not dispense with attribution. Leatherman rejects heightened pleading, not the proof demanded after trial. Plakas preserves a distinct municipal inquiry and requires evidence connecting the identified policy or deficiency to the deprivation. Nabozny likewise preserves actor-specific responsibility; it creates no relaxed hiring standard.

No Associate adopts the County's categorical immunity theory merely because this is a single final hiring choice or a facially lawful act. Their affirmative commitments differ: Stevens, Souter and Breyer hold that a sufficiently culpable and causative single selection can establish liability under existing law; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas assume that possibility without deciding it, because this record fails the demanding fault and causation inquiry in any event. Ginsburg seeks further argument rather than choosing a hiring-specific merits rule. Canton remains binding in its training setting and rejects a universal facial-unconstitutionality prerequisite; it does not by itself settle every isolated-screening claim. Tuttle bars inferring a municipal policy merely from a subordinate's single misconduct; it does not erase independent evidence of the final policymaker's selection and the information accompanying it. Conversely, identifying Moore's selection as attributable to the County does not prove deliberate indifference or direct causation. The question is the demonstrated risk of the relevant constitutional abuse, not whether an applicant was generally undesirable or whether declining to hire would have prevented all his later acts.

A prior pattern may establish notice but is not an invariant prerequisite in the face of an otherwise obvious constitutional risk. None requires proof that the policymaker intended the injury or could foresee its exact time, victim, and mechanics. The stricter Canton formulation requiring actual or constructive notice of an omission substantially certain to produce constitutional violations remains material for O'Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy; it is not a demand for metaphysical certainty or proof of the County's illustrative psychiatric hypothetical. The three hiring-affirmance Justices and the four hiring-insufficiency Justices disagree about what the proven background and proposed police duties permit a jury to find. Ginsburg withholds that merits choice in favor of further argument. No current commitment converts ordinary negligent selection into federal liability, and no proposed reconsideration itself changes governing law.

State permission to employ an applicant is not federal immunity and is not proof that hiring him was constitutionally indifferent. The other direction is equally important: a departure from prudent hiring practice or an arguable state-paperwork defect does not itself establish a federal constitutional injury. No Justice adopts the County's requested good-faith immunity from municipal liability.

## 4. Independent commitments and strongest alternative paths

The three merits-affirmance, four vacatur, and one further-argument commitments below are Associate-Justice commitments, not a forecast of the Court's final tally. Every Justice accepts the common record and procedural limits, while merits joins and the further-consideration component have the exact scope stated. Opposition to the County's requested disposition does not itself establish agreement on hiring sufficiency.

### John Paul Stevens

**Disposition and ground:** Affirm the County judgment on federal hiring. On the evidence favorable to the verdict, a jury could find deliberate indifference and the required causal relation. The combined recent history, the assault and resistance information requiring examination, known repeated disregard of legal restraints, and deployment to physical police work provide evidence about a constitutional-force risk beyond the bare fact of employment. The jury could reject Moore's account of adequate consideration without finding that he read every entry. It need not treat the four printout references as four convictions. The precautions and possible innocent explanation of some entries are contrary evidence, not facts the reviewing court must conclusively adopt.

**Own anchors:** Stevens joined the Canton majority, including its obvious-risk route and its refusal to make deficient performance alone sufficient; his Collins opinion separates the underlying right from municipal responsibility. His actual Plakas municipal join preserves that separation and evidentiary causation. These positions support applying the deliberate-indifference standard to the combined proof while leaving factual inferences to the jury. His historical joins to the sufficiency dissent and to the separate request for reconsideration independently corroborate the two distinct commitments; neither historical author assignment is carried forward.

**Strongest opposite path:** The County can characterize the evidence as youthful misconduct, ordinary unsuitability, and an expert's conclusion about potential aggression, with no earlier police-abuse complaint and some supervision. Canton forbids liability simply because better screening could have avoided the injury. Stevens rejects that application because the record includes information about resistance and force-related misconduct, the failure to examine it, and duties presenting the corresponding risk. His ground is the sufficiency of that combination, not a right to ideal screening or a rule that every assault conviction disqualifies a deputy.

**Instructions, alternatives, and remedy:** Read the federal charge as imposing the distinct inadequacy, deliberate-indifference, and direct-cause requirements; the preserved objections do not justify reversing this verdict on the County's proposed categorical grounds. No independent training or state-law holding is needed to affirm. Canton permits considering a further opportunity to prove a case when a genuinely new standard changes the trial task; no such retrial is needed on his selected application of the existing standard. He will not join an opinion that uses negligent state hiring alone, infers May 12 misconduct from propensity, or immunizes facially lawful selections categorically. He also will not join an immediate complete judgment for County that ignores the other verdicts.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Disposition and ground:** Vacate the County portion of the appellate judgment and remand as specified in section 5. Hold federal hiring legally insufficient on this trial record. Even crediting the evidence and permissible notice inferences, it establishes serious reasons to investigate and question fitness rather than the requisite exceptionally close connection between this selection and the later constitutional use of force. Actual or constructive notice must concern the particular constitutional danger; a foreseeable possibility of aggression is not enough. The full combined history is evaluated, including its unfavorable features, rather than reduced to an asserted harmless isolated fight.

**Own anchors:** Her Canton separate writing, joined there by Scalia and Kennedy, requires actual or constructive notice, a very close causal connection, and strong proof that the omission creates the constitutional risk. It expressly accepts obvious recurring-duty cases without a prior pattern. Her actual Plakas municipal join preserves separate fault and causation inquiries. Those positions permit assuming a possible single-selection claim while demanding more than this record supplies; she reserves whether an isolated screening failure can ever establish liability on another record.

**Strongest opposite path:** The final official had an available record containing force/resistance indicators and repeated law violations and chose a relative for duties predictably involving restraint. The jury could view failure to clarify the entries as deliberate disregard. O'Connor nevertheless distinguishes the obvious need to inquire into general fitness from sufficiently strong notice that hiring this applicant would lead to unconstitutional force. Neither the expert's hiring preference nor ordinary proximate foreseeability closes that gap. Her conclusion does not depend on believing Burns's exculpatory account, counting every entry as a duplicate, or requiring another injured police victim first.

**Instructions, alternatives, and remedy:** Her holding is trial-evidence insufficiency under the demanding existing standard, not a standalone reversal for an unpreserved new instruction. Her Canton remedy position supplies the reason to foreclose a second opportunity to improve the hiring proof after a full trial. It does **not** justify judgment for County on distinct verdict grounds left unresolved below. She therefore permits appellate consideration of the properly available training and state grounds, with their own preservation and record questions. She will not join negligent-selection liability, presumed actual knowledge of all entries, automatic state-compliance immunity, or an order that dismisses the entire County case on hiring insufficiency alone.

### Antonin Scalia

**Disposition and ground:** Vacate the County appellate judgment and remand under section 5, with federal hiring foreclosed on this record. Attribution of the final selection is not a substitute for proving that County's own deliberately indifferent choice caused the constitutional wrong. Moving from general disobedience and possible aggressiveness to the specific unlawful-force risk on this evidence leaves the connection too dependent on employment and hindsight.

**Own anchors:** His actual join in O'Connor's Canton writing supplies both the demanding notice/causation rule and its full-trial remedial consequence. His Plakas municipal join and Leatherman join retain substantive proof limits while rejecting procedural substitutes for them. His commitment rests on those actual adopted propositions, not an assumed generic opposition to § 1983 liability.

**Strongest opposite path:** This is not solely an inference from one subordinate's injury: the final selector had background information and failed to pursue significant warnings. Pembaur and Canton's obvious-risk route make that a legally cognizable claim. Scalia assumes the proposed route for deciding this case but finds the warnings inadequate to establish the requisite constitutional-force probability; he does not affirmatively decide its availability on another record. Hiring an imperfect person, without proof of the necessary connection, cannot make the municipality responsible for all unconstitutional acts he later commits. A force-related entry is relevant; relevance alone is not the required quantum.

**Instructions, alternatives, and remedy:** No required prior-pattern rule, blanket immunity for a lawful hire, or new state-eligibility exemption. Do not turn the County's moral-certainty formulation into an impossible proof requirement divorced from Canton. No independent instructional holding or training ruling is needed after the hiring sufficiency holding. The same full-opportunity principle forecloses retrying hiring, but the distinct federal training and state verdicts require appellate resolution before County's entire liability can be eliminated. He will not join an opinion allowing expert characterization to replace statutory municipal fault, making mere but-for causation sufficient, or treating an unexamined alternative verdict as already defeated.

### Anthony M. Kennedy

**Disposition and ground:** Vacate and remand the County judgment under section 5; reject federal hiring sufficiency. The combined background supports concern and further screening. It does not support the necessary high constitutional-risk inference and direct causal connection strongly enough to attribute this particular excessive-force deprivation to County's hiring choice. The restrictions on Burns's role are relevant countervailing circumstances, although neither wholly effective supervision nor disobedience of an absolute arrest prohibition is established as fact.

**Own anchors:** Kennedy joined O'Connor's Canton writing. His own operative Plakas municipal analysis separately identifies deprivation, municipal attribution, culpability, and causation and rejects the actual equipment/training claim for lack of the needed connection. That law also prevents substituting the individual officer's position for the County inquiry. The present result follows that component structure; it does not assume that Plakas decided negligent hiring.

**Strongest opposite path:** Jury deference and the correspondence between background indicators and police restraint favor affirmance; the selection itself supplies municipal action independently of Burns's injury. Kennedy accepts those premises but finds that general fitness, limited prior force evidence, and disputed knowledge do not establish the distinct municipal constitutional fault required by Canton. Family connection adds context, not the missing causal strength. The County's own future-violence hypothetical is illustrative advocacy, not the threshold every plaintiff must satisfy.

**Instructions, alternatives, and remedy:** The holding rests on insufficient hiring proof, with no need to adopt every charge objection. No pattern prerequisite and no rewriting of the stipulated policymaker posture. No County immunity follows from state minimum qualifications. The full trial forecloses a renewed hiring case, but Plakas's insistence on distinct components also requires preserving the separate training inquiry. State negligent hiring/training involve different fault standards and unresolved exemptions. He will join the limited remand; he will not join wholesale County exoneration, a general federal personnel code, or an opinion collapsing the training and hiring theories.

### David H. Souter

The actual force-related and resistance information need not be inflated to multiple convictions for the combination to permit the required inference. A reasonable jury could find more than an employee's poor performance or a remote causal chain.

**Own anchors:** Souter's actual Plakas municipal join demands proof of a distinct municipal wrong and its causal link rather than treating the officer's status as dispositive. **Instructions, alternatives, and remedy:** The federal charge's full requirements support the verdict; the County's preserved categorical objections do not warrant reversal. **Instructions, alternatives, and remedy:** Decide the preserved sufficiency issue rather than announce a new instruction prerequisite. ## 5. Formal disposition and mandate by group

**Stevens, Souter, and Breyer, if the Court reaches the present merits:** Affirm the operative appellate judgment sustaining County liability on federal deliberately indifferent hiring. The existing $711,302 joint-and-several actual judgment and its actual fee allocation are not enlarged or reallocated. No independent holding concerning training sufficiency, the state exemptions, or the two constitutional-damage items is necessary to that affirmance. Burns's separate punitive award and individual liability remain outside the municipal ruling.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas:** Vacate the portion of the Fifth Circuit judgment sustaining County liability and remand to that court. Their legal determination forecloses the federal hiring theory **on this completed trial record**; the mandate does not authorize a new trial merely to improve the hiring proof. The court of appeals must address the properly available alternative grounds and their preservation before directing a conforming district-court judgment. Specifically:

1. The federal deliberately-indifferent-training verdict remains to be considered. The parties dispute its availability within this appellate posture and petition; the withdrawn opinion cannot resolve that dispute. Its sufficiency requires attention to the duties actually assigned, instruction actually provided, notice, and causation. The six-day CLEET interval alone neither proves nor defeats it. The remand does not command a new training trial or decide whether its existing verdict must stand.
2. The state negligent-hiring and negligent-training verdicts remain separate. The district court used Oklahoma law for the County's conduct, distinguished discretionary planning from operational selection/deployment under § 155(5), and found recognized-standard conformity not established under § 155(28). The operative appellate opinion did not decide those issues. Their preserved appellate treatment must precede a final elimination of County liability; federal deliberate-indifference insufficiency does not answer ordinary negligence, comity, or either exemption.
3. Any resulting judgment must identify which County grounds survive and the consequent damages and fee treatment. This commitment supplies no automatic subtraction of the $100,000 constitutional-right items, no automatic destruction of joint-and-several liability, and no new fee award. Those questions depend on the legal and remedial effect of the surviving grounds. Burns's distinct liability and $20,000 punitive award are undisturbed by this County-only mandate.

The strongest alternative remedial path for the four rejecting hiring is immediate entry of judgment for County, using Canton's full-opportunity rationale. That rationale is sound only as to the theory found insufficient. It cannot decide the separately found training and state grounds that the operative appellate judgment did not analyze. The strongest alternative for the three affirming on hiring is a Canton-style clarification and remand, but those Justices find that the existing standard and actual charge permit affirmance on the evidence. Their separate willingness to seek further argument about Monell is not a finding that Brown requires another trial under a newly announced hiring standard.

**Ginsburg:** Request further argument before the Court disposes of the statutory municipal-liability issue. She supplies no independent affirmance mandate. If the Court orders that course, no merits mandate follows from this handoff before the further consideration; if the Court declines, her dissent remains on the procedural ground.
