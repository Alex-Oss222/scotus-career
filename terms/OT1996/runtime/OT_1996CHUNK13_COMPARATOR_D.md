# 153. Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc.

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT OUTCOME

The Tenth Circuit reversed the employer's Title VII judgment because the jury instructions omitted liability for abuse of delegated power and imposed inappropriate managerial-control/apparent-authority requirements. It affirmed rejection of the employer's exhaustion argument, distinguishing collective contractual grievances from statutory claims. Its broader discussion of labor arbitration is lower-court reasoning, not an inherited Supreme Court holding. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/112/1437/585308/).

## B. LATER HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT DISPOSITION ONLY

Historically, under the reversed caption Eddy Potash, Inc. v. Harrison, No. 97-232, 524 U.S. 947 (June 26, 1998), the Supreme Court granted certiorari, vacated the appellate judgment and remanded for reconsideration in light of the newly decided historical Faragher. This was a GVR, not an independent Supreme Court merits ruling adopting Harrison's agency or arbitration reasoning. The historical 1998 Faragher rule and this dependent order postdate the simulation review; neither supplies entering law or a simulated result. [Official United States Reports, page 947](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/524bv.pdf).
