## Eight-Justice common-ground commitment

**1. The particular public-road consequence.** Montana protects distinct authority over retained tribal/trust land and limits inherent authority over nonmember activity on alienated land, subject to its two exceptions and any specific superior federal source. Bourland applies an exclusion consequence to particular land opened to public use by federal acquisition legislation; it does not require treating all land instruments or rights-of-way alike. Here, the actual federal grant and public-road use mean the Tribe cannot condition ordinary motorists' highway passage on obtaining its discretionary permission. At that limited scope, the right-of-way has the relevant characteristic supporting Montana's nonmember framework.

This is a legal conclusion about this authorized public use, not a finding that the instrument conveyed fee title or expressly transferred every governmental power to North Dakota. Its trust-only provision, route, conditions and reservations remain effective. The easement's tenure is unlimited only while actually used for its specified purpose. It remains subject to valid existing rights or adverse claims. The United States grantor may terminate all or part after thirty days' written notice and failure to correct breach of a grant term or applicable regulation, two consecutive years of nonuse for the granted purpose, abandonment, or failure to file the required completion affidavit when construction ends. The grant conditions bind and benefit the grantee's successors as stated. Indian owners, lessees, successors and assigns retain reasonably necessary crossings, constructed and maintained at their risk with responsibility for resulting right-of-way damage. Those rights concern the grant's duration and compatible land use; they do not establish a present general power to exclude authorized public motorists or an adjudicatory entitlement over every accident. No termination trigger is found.

The acknowledged consideration is $5,546.50 plus other good consideration. The header's zero tribal acres and 87.48 individually owned acres is not a finding of the exact collision parcel's owner. The incorporated map/plats and separate consent document are not reproduced, but the grant is not attacked as invalidly issued. Neither their absence nor the trust-only clause decides a new title dispute. The result holds for the supported right-of-way setting without selecting a parcel owner, finding statutory noncompliance, construing every clause of §§323–328, or treating all reservation roads as alienated land. Sac and Fox's inclusion of qualifying rights-of-way within Indian country remains intact; territorial classification and this particular nonmember governing power are different inquiries.

**2. Forum power requires a sufficient source.** Subject to controlling treaties or statutes, including congressional enlargement of tribal-court jurisdiction, and Montana's two exceptions, a tribe's adjudicatory authority over nonmembers does not exceed its corresponding substantive governing authority. Adjudication and choice of law are analytically distinct, but compelled litigation and a binding tort judgment exercise sovereign authority over the defendants' conduct. A tribal court cannot supply the missing authority for this highway-negligence claim solely by saying it could apply state law. National Farmers and Iowa Mutual ensure tribal examination and preserve ultimate federal review; their exhaustion holdings are not independent grants of jurisdiction over every reservation occurrence. Their civil/criminal distinction remains effective: no automatic importation of Oliphant or Duro supplies this result. Williams involved an on-reservation trader's claim against resident members concerning their transaction, a concrete internal relationship missing from this claim between two non-Indian road users.

Merrion remains a substantial counterargument because it separates sovereign taxing power from a private landowner's ability to exclude and refuses to infer surrender from contractual silence. None infers a general sovereign waiver from an unreserved clause here. They distinguish its taxation of mineral lessees' reservation business and governmental-services relationship from the present ordinary highway claim. Montana's external limit and its exceptions, applied to the federally authorized public-road setting, do the work; the result is not a universal rule that every government power vanishes whenever an easement is granted.

**3. Consensual relationship.** A-1 voluntarily entered the established $12,490 subcontract with LCM, the tribally owned corporation, for reservation community-center work. The first Montana exception can support appropriate regulation of activities arising from consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements. It does not require an express forum-consent clause in every case, but it does require a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and the consensual activity; contracting with the Tribe does not submit the contractor to every conceivable reservation claim.

For this claim all eight assume, favorably to petitioners without making findings, that Stockert's trip served that project and that Fredericks resided on the reservation. She nonetheless asserts the ordinary duty owed by a public-road driver to another non-Indian road user, not a tribal contract entitlement, an obligation owed to a tribal participant in the work, or control of project performance. The road is used under the public right-of-way, rather than only by permission gained through the subcontract. The contract may explain why the truck was there without making adjudication of this third-party highway claim part of governing the consensual relationship. That distinction supplies the result; absence of contractual privity is relevant but not a universal rule that no third-party tort could ever fall within Montana's first exception. A different injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a person participating in the consensual activity presents a different question.

This reasoning does not find the trip unrelated. It makes the unresolved work connection legally insufficient even on petitioners' strongest supported version, so further factfinding about the load or assignment cannot change the commitments of all eight. No weight is given to the purported Utah forum/law clause, which was not accepted as part of the appellate record. Nor do attributed building/employment-law obligations establish blanket consent to this lawsuit. The holding neither enforces nor invalidates an unread contract provision.

**4. Tribal integrity, security, health or welfare.** Montana separately preserves civil authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. Nonmembership alone cannot eliminate every possible tribal interest. The seriousness of Fredericks's injury, her family ties to members, assumed residence, road safety and the importance of functioning tribal courts are all acknowledged. But ordinary individual injury and the resulting familial or community effects do not on this record establish the governmental threat or direct tribal effect sufficient to make this nonmember public-road litigation an exercise of necessary tribal self-government. Otherwise the exception would restore general territorial authority over essentially every local civil wrong that the external limit excludes.

No fiscal or service burden is invented, and no finding that the Tribe suffered no consequences is entered. The question is legal sufficiency of the supported claim and interests. The sons' membership does not change their mother's membership or bring their unreviewed consortium claims into the judgment. A filed protective state action is consistent with another forum being available, but forum availability is not the independent legal reason that tribal power is absent. No state merits or limitations outcome is decided.

## Souter's and Breyer's reconciled grounds

**Souter.** His actual join in Blackmun's Bourland dissent supplies the strongest objection to reasoning directly from lost exclusion to lost compatible regulation. His actual Chickasaw dissent join likewise protects a concrete governmental relationship under the source at issue. Those commitments require preserving this grant's conditions, the difference between public passage and a conveyance of title, and genuinely qualifying residual powers. They do not establish that this claim is a qualifying residual exercise. The controlling actual Bourland rule leaves Montana's exceptions open; here both are examined rather than presumed extinguished. The title retained beneath a lawfully open highway supplies no present gatekeeping power over ordinary passage, while the contractor's work trip, assumed in petitioners' favor, does not make the injured non-Indian motorist a participant in that undertaking. The ordinary road-duty claim remains outside the necessary relationship. His historical full join reached this result despite the same exclusion/regulation objection, a conditional easement and the same uncertainty about work purpose. No intervening adopted premise changes that analysis. He therefore accepts the common adjudicatory ceiling, both exception applications, and affirmance; he does not preserve the provisional compatible-forum objection as a live refusal to join.

**Breyer.** His actual Chickasaw income dissent depends on a particular treaty protection and the tribal member/employer/work relationship, rather than an unrestricted power over reservation activity. The subcontract here deserves genuine consideration, and the common grounds do not require privity for every conceivable claim. They assume the work-related trip and reservation residence and still distinguish an ordinary non-Indian road user's negligence claim from a claim regulating the project or a participant's relation to it. His historical full join expressly coexisted with an uncertain work purpose and treated residence as immaterial. No actual antecedent establishes a broader consensual-activity exception requiring resolution of those factual disputes for this claim. He therefore joins the common rule, the two exception applications and affirmance. He retains no first-exception remand, distinct disposition, or refusal of the common forum-power ground.

For each, this conclusion is reached after considering the strongest contrary path. It does not treat silence in the grant as a general sovereign waiver, presume the trip unrelated, invent a residence finding, or imply that every third-party tort necessarily falls outside the first exception. The source corrections constrain the reasoning but do not change the legally decisive assumed facts.
