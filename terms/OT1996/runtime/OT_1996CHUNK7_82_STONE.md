## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The unpreserved Gaudin error does not warrant discretionary correction here

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches all Rule 52(b) steps necessary to explain affirmance, but he may assume the substantial-rights prong because the fourth prong independently resolves this record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this unobjected judge-decided materiality element requires reversal under Rule 52(b).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gaudin's jury-element rule and Olano's four-step framework. Olano reserved error first clarified on appeal; Stone newly proposes measuring plainness at appellate review here because that is when the court exercises Rule 52(b)'s correction authority and Gaudin then unambiguously identifies the error. This resolution is user-approved. The third condition may be assumed without deciding it, and the fourth remains an independent, record-specific discretionary inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A trial court errs by deciding materiality rather than submitting it to the jury, and the error may be plain when controlling law changes before appellate review. Even assuming an effect on substantial rights, Rule 52(b) does not require correction where materiality was overwhelming, essentially uncontroverted, and no plausible jury dispute appears in the record, so reversal rather than affirmance would impair the fairness and integrity of the proceeding.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone does not excuse the constitutional violation: Gaudin establishes who must decide the element. But Rule 52(b) applies because Johnson made no objection, and its fourth step asks whether extraordinary appellate intervention is warranted. The grand jury was investigating the source of the money, and the charged falsehood addressed that subject directly. No trial strategy or evidence suggested immateriality. On this narrow record, a new trial solely to obtain an inevitable element finding would not protect the public reputation of adjudication.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not classify every Gaudin error as harmless, shift the Government’s burden on preserved error, resolve structural-error doctrine, or deny relief when materiality was contested, the evidence was not overwhelming, or the judge’s action affected trial strategy.
