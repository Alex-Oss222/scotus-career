# 96. CompuServe, Inc. v. Patterson

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR

No historical Supreme Court merits decision in this matter is established. The counterfactual simulation grant supplies no historical vote or holding.

## B. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT RESULT

The Sixth Circuit reversed the personal-jurisdiction dismissal and remanded. It found a prima facie purposeful, ongoing Ohio commercial relationship, a sufficient connection to the trademark and unfair-competition controversy and a reasonable forum. It expressly reserved other subscribers, third-party claims and states where software was merely purchased or used. The holding concerned personal jurisdiction, not copyright or trademark liability. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/89/1257/582901/).

# 98. Hincapie-Nieto v. INS

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR

No historical Supreme Court merits decision in this matter is established. The simulation grant creates no historical vote or reasoning.

## B. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT RESULT

The Second Circuit dismissed the direct petition under §440(a), assuming the original pre-enactment filing date survived withdrawal and reinstatement. It treated the restriction as jurisdictional, rejected the claimed temporal bar on these facts and relied on the Government's acknowledgement of possible detention habeas while reserving its nature and scope. It did not decide §440(d), and it did not hold that a district court could review the deportation order through §2241. The simulation must independently apply its different, limited Felker record. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/92/27/517073/).

# 99. Kinman v. Omaha Public School District

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT COMPARATOR

The Eighth Circuit allowed Title IX proceedings under a knew-or-should-have-known standard drawn from Title VII, found disputed welcome and institutional response, and rejected a same-sex exclusion. It affirmed the §1983 judgments because the officials investigated and the record did not show deliberate indifference or a widespread District practice. It thus used different standards for statutory and constitutional responsibility. The new Stone proposal would retain the same-sex conclusion but alter both the Title IX attribution rule and the treatment of personal supervisory summary judgment.

This records the historical lower-court disposition only. It does not establish a completed simulated Supreme Court outcome or supply governing post-divergence Supreme Court law.

[Primary opinion](https://openjurist.org/94/f3d/463)

# 100. Ayeni v. Mottola

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

The Second Circuit held that the pleaded media intrusion violated sufficiently established Fourth Amendment limits and affirmed the denial of dismissal. # 102. Boggs v. Boggs

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Court reversed and remanded, 5-4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy announced the judgment and wrote the controlling opinion. Justices Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas joined the principal preemption portion; Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justice Ginsburg joined the portion addressing the statutory conflict. Justice Breyer dissented, joined by Justice O’Connor and, except for one subsection, by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justice Ginsburg.

# 103. De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed, 7-2.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by the Chief Justice and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Justice Thomas.
- **Historical Holding:** A generally applicable state tax on health-care facilities is not preempted merely because an ERISA plan owns a taxed facility and therefore bears the charge directly. The supposed direct versus indirect economic distinction did not separate the case from Travelers.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court.
- **Historical Sources:** De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund, 520 U.S. 806 (1997).



## Historical reasoning references preserved from the source

- **Other Authority Used:** Travelers and Fort Halifax support the distinction between plan administration and ordinary economic regulation. The historical De Buono opinion is persuasive on the practical equivalence between direct ownership and purchasing taxed services.

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/520bv.pdf#page=901).
