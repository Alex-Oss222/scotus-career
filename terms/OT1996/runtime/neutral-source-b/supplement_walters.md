## Walters — ready for the counting question on the stipulated record

Research cutoff: January 14, 1997. Argument: November 6, 1996. Walters, No. 95-259, and EEOC, No. 95-779, are the combined matter on review of 60 F.3d 1225. The neutral baseline omits the argument date; the sanitized official recitation supplies it. The EEOC brought the action; Walters intervened with an additional promotion claim. The merits of retaliation, discrimination, liability and damages remain undecided.

The employer moved for dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, and the lower courts dismissed on the numerical threshold under their compensation-based method. That is the source-supported procedural treatment below. The baseline's categorical classification of numerosity as a 'coverage element' is not adopted as a new jurisdictional holding. The counting question can be resolved without deciding the consequences of that classification for forfeiture or later procedure.

The official recitation reports stipulated facts: no qualifying threshold in 1989; Monday–Friday working days; 15–17 payroll employees each working day for most of 1990; only nine weeks satisfying a method requiring compensation to at least fifteen persons on every working day; and two hourly part-time employees ordinarily not working one day each week. Paid leave counted under the compensation method. These are published recitations of stipulations, not independent inspection of original payrolls. Do not invent an employee-status dispute, continuous 15-person employment for all fifty-two weeks, or a specific hiring/termination date.

The operative statutory threshold remains fifteen or more employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year. The associated industry-affecting-commerce requirement, agency language and express employer exclusions are not erased by choosing a counting method. Section 2000e(f)'s circular employee definition and express public-office/staff exclusions are separately preserved. None of those exclusions is claimed to describe the two hourly part-time workers on the supplied record.

The employees' strongest construction counts a continuing employment relationship on working days despite a part-time schedule or unpaid absence, treating payroll evidence as an administrable indicator of the relationship. The employer's strongest construction gives 'for each working day' a day-specific compensation requirement and relies on the lower circuit's established rule. Paid leave's inclusion and hourly employees' regular off-days must be accounted for in assessing that distinction; a purely actual-work rule is not an accurate description of the compensation method below.

Under a relationship-based construction, a payroll entry supplies evidence rather than an irrebuttable legal classification. A person not yet hired, whose relationship has ended, or who is not an employee under the governing relationship test is not counted merely because a name remains on a list. Darden supplies a date-eligible common-law-agency interpretive framework at its ERISA scope; Town & Country shows that compatible employment and particular statutory exclusions require attention to enacted text and actual control. Neither independently holds how Title VII's daily numerosity phrase operates. A court must still apply the selected method to the stipulated period and qualifying relationships, without adjudicating the discrimination merits.

Supported paths include affirmance under the compensation construction or rejection of that construction with a confined remand for proceedings under the chosen method. The current record supports resolving the legal counting issue without new judicially invented payroll facts or a jurisdictional ruling. If a proposed final outcome depends on an exact number of qualifying weeks not established by the recitation 'most of 1990,' obtain the original stipulation or remand that application; do not replace it with an invented number.
