## A. STATUS AND ANSWERS

**User-approved position, reasoning and conditional fallbacks — October 11, 2026.** These remain proposals to the Court, not completed holdings or votes.

**Question 1:** Yes, on the supported prima facie record here. Patterson undertook a continuing provider relationship with an identified Ohio service, repeatedly sent software to its Ohio system for distribution, used its marketing and fee-collection arrangements and communicated about the resulting commercial dispute. These are his purposeful acts, not simply CompuServe's unilateral location. Stone would apply Burger King's actual-course-of-dealing inquiry. The standardized agreement and Ohio choice of law reinforce the relationship but do not independently constitute consent or settle jurisdiction. The small number of direct Ohio consumers does not erase the separate continuing Ohio intermediary relationship.

**Question 2:** Yes. Deliberate electronic commercial conduct can establish the legally necessary affiliation without physical travel. Stone's source-based treatment of new technology applies the received doctrine to what the communication actually does. Simulated Quill's eight-Justice due-process holding is binding at its collection-duty scope even though Stone did not join it; its reasoning supports rejecting a mechanical physical-entry requirement. Stone would neither import Quill's distinct Commerce Clause protection into this case nor treat its software discussion as deciding every Internet dispute. International Shoe and Burger King perform the direct doctrinal work.

**Question 3:** The operative facts of the claim must have a real connection to the defendant's purposeful forum dealings. That requirement is met provisionally because the asserted marks, allegedly competing software, marketing and licensing activities, and demand for relief concern the provider relationship carried on through Ohio. Mere communication of a demand to an Ohio resident would not, standing alone, make every unrelated claim amenable there. The availability of a hypothetical trademark dispute after a different distribution arrangement does not eliminate the connection in the actual arrangement pleaded.

Stone would separately find the present exercise reasonable. Patterson's burden as an individual defending away from Texas counts in full; CompuServe's size creates no preferential entitlement. But the deliberate multiyear Ohio-centered relationship, the forum's connection to the disputed dealings and the parties' contemplated Ohio law supply adequate justification on the motion record. No fact establishes a compelling unfairness that defeats the purposeful, claim-linked connection. The fractured Asahi stream-of-commerce theories need not be converted into a new universal test to decide those concrete contacts.

## B. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

**Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment reversing the Rule 12(b)(2) dismissal and remanding for further proceedings.** CompuServe has made the required prima facie showing; disputed facts remain subject to the applicable later procedure, and other defenses and the intellectual-property merits remain undecided. Stone would impose no new Internet-specific test, nationwide jurisdiction rule, numerical sales threshold or mandatory evidentiary machinery beyond what the actual dispute requires.
