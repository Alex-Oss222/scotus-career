### Exact entering law

**Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct** — **Present operation effective: Rivers v. Roadway Express, April 26, 1994**. The sequential rule comes from Landgraf of the same date, Stevens's five-Justice framework. Congress's temporal instruction controls subject to constitutional limits; otherwise the inquiry concerns the particular new legal consequences of completed acts. A jurisdictional label or pending case alone does not decide it. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's relevant-activity rationale remains distinct.

**Supreme Court original habeas after an authorization-review bar** — **Present operation effective: Felker, June 28, 1996**. The specified certiorari bar and retained original section 2241 channel are distinct; original relief actually received independent consideration. Felker does not establish an immigration habeas channel or uphold destruction of all meaningful review on the strength of a nominal remedy. Section 1651 assists existing jurisdiction; it creates none.

**Yang, November 13, 1996**, construed the corrected transition provisions and separated eligibility, permissible discretionary factors and reasoned administration. **Reyes-Hernandez, May 12, 1997**, now controls section 242(g)'s limited subject matter and the distinct new consequences attached to the accepted surrender of a colorable defense. Its five participating Associates supporting the temporal rationale were Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; the three remaining Associates expressly retained their relevant-activity objection. No outcome is inferred from the lower courts' historical disagreement. Flores preserves actual individual safeguards at its limited custody/facial-review scope; it does not answer the missing immigration habeas questions.

## Proposed legal propositions

**I. Transition and statutory route.** Corrected IIRIRA section 309(c)(1) preserves the former procedure for proceedings before April 1, 1997, subject to its stated exceptions; corrected section 306(c)(1) nevertheless extends section 242(g), after that commencement, across past, pending and future proceedings. Under Reyes-Hernandez, review of this completed adjudication is not a challenge to the identified executive decision or action to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute an order. Section 242(g) therefore does not itself eliminate this claim's otherwise saved route. That conclusion leaves AEDPA's separate restriction to be tested; it does not restore a route another applicable law withdraws.

The January 18, 1996 final order does not meet corrected section 309(c)(4)'s trigger of a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996. No later order is established. Paragraph (2)'s election requires an uncommenced evidentiary hearing and notice at least thirty days before it; paragraph (3)'s termination/reinitiation option requires the absence of a final administrative decision. No such election or predicate is established here. Section 306(c)(2) protects existing section 106 judgments and orders against invalidation or required reconsideration under paragraph (1); it supplies no jurisdiction. Permanent removal-review provisions do not replace this old proceeding merely because the Court acts after April 1.

**II. Section 440(a)'s pending-petition application.** Section 440(a) withdraws ordinary judicial review of the specified final deportation orders; Hincapie-Nieto's admitted drug and aggravated-felony grounds are covered. Section 440(f)'s express timing rule applies to subsection (e), not a saving provision for subsection (a). The later section 306(d) correction, effective as if included in AEDPA, preserves the dual-offense branch's qualification that both predicates are otherwise covered without regard to their commission dates; this case does not depend on that branch.

**Five-Associate temporal rationale:** Assume Hincapie-Nieto receives the full benefit of the February 16 filing despite the stipulated withdrawal and reinstatement request. The provision changes the court's future power to adjudicate that ordinary review; on this record it does not add a new consequence to an identified completed surrender of a real defense, withdraw eligibility the agency had not considered, increase criminal punishment or reopen a final Article III judgment. The administrative application was actually considered before enactment. The mere filing and expectation of continued direct review do not themselves establish a protected completed transaction under Landgraf. This is therefore not the Reyes-Hernandez lost-option situation. Other completed-event predicates remain open; no universal rule makes a colorable defense the only conceivable retroactivity predicate.

**Three-Associate alternative rationale:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas identify the future judicial review as the activity regulated by section 440(a), as in their Landgraf, Felker and Reyes-Hernandez positions. The restriction governs the judicial action requested after enactment; the date of the conviction, administrative concession or earlier filing does not fix perpetual ordinary reviewing power. They do not join the five-Associate formulation under which an earlier surrender of a defense can preserve this review channel. They leave section 440(d)'s separate substantive operation unresolved.

**III. Collateral review and constitutional limits.** Affirmance is confined to withdrawal of this ordinary appellate channel. The Government conceded only possible detention review upon INS custody and denied habeas review of the deportation order itself. That concession establishes neither the existence nor the adequacy of collateral review of a legal defect in the deportation order. The Court should expressly decline the lower court's suggestion that naming some possible relief is enough under Felker.

The supplied record presents an admitted ground of deportability and a denial after actual administrative consideration of discretionary relief. It identifies no distinct surrendered defense, legal defect in deportability, unlawful custody claim or other developed claim whose necessary judicial forum must be decided to resolve this petition. The Constitution does not on this record guarantee retention of this particular ordinary appellate channel for further review of the discretionary denial. That bounded conclusion does **not** approve eliminating every meaningful forum for a properly presented constitutional or legal challenge. Whether section 2241 survives for a challenge to an order's legal basis, the nature of any constitutionally required review, and its custody, respondent, forum, exhaustion and other requirements remain undecided. The appeal is not converted or transferred into an unfiled habeas proceeding. No fictional alternative remedy is a premise of the affirmance.

**IV. Mandate.** Affirm the direct-review jurisdictional dismissal. Do not reach the agency's alternative merits defense, award or deny a waiver, order renewed balancing or reopening, deem a habeas petition filed, or issue a stay or removal-execution command. No custody date or execution date is established. The original filing-date assumption is preserved; no unproved timeliness default supplies the disposition.

## Recipient attribution: incompatible proposed rules

### Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

**Rule and judgment.** A funding recipient may be responsible for a teacher's intentional sex-based harassment when actual educational authority delegated to the teacher materially aided the harassment, or when responsible school officials knew or reasonably should have known of the harassment and negligently failed to take appropriate corrective steps, causing injury or continuation. Teacher status or proximity alone does not establish agency attribution. These are distinct routes; proof of a forbidden environment does not itself prove either. They would affirm the removal of summary judgment on the recipient Title IX claim and remand under this clarified standard, without commanding a trial before ordinary remaining legal issues are addressed.

**Sufficient grounds.** Section 1681 regulates discrimination under the funded program, which the recipient operates through its teachers and administrators. Franklin recognizes intentional teacher harassment as capable of supporting compensation while reserving attribution. Meritor and Andrade supply a reasoned agency analogy: a recipient cannot necessarily avoid responsibility for intentional discrimination materially enabled by the authority it entrusted merely because senior management lacked notice. The teacher's intent remains the underlying discriminatory wrong; negligent failure to correct that known or reasonably knowable wrong is a separate institutional connection, not compensation for unintended disparate effects alone. Appropriate corrective efforts remain relevant to the negligence route, but do not automatically defeat a proved actual-authority route.

**Application.** Calling Kinman out of study hall and using the teacher relationship to obtain intimate disclosures and access can support the actual-authority connection; friendship alone could not. Repeated reports, the transfer request, the investigation's timing and asymmetry, the alleged wrong-line trace and lack of protective separation permit a dispute over reasonable institutional correction even if they do not establish constitutional deliberate disregard. The school's later termination does not conclusively answer responsibility for earlier preventable injury. The trier must identify the relevant period, knowledge, authority, response and causation.

**Strongest objection and answer.** Title VII expressly refers to employer agents, while Title IX concerns funding recipients and has a notice-and-voluntary-compliance administrative mechanism. These four do not disregard that distinction or import all employment rules. They conclude that ordinary program/agency responsibility is compatible with the existing private action and express nondiscrimination condition. Section 1682 governs federal administrative enforcement and does not expressly make its notice/hearing sequence a prerequisite to private compensation. Franklin prevents turning funding notice into a categorical compensation defense, while leaving the Court to identify the actual institutional connection. These four reject strict liability for every teacher's private act and an automatic award whenever a school policy was violated.

### O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas

**Rule and judgment.** Private compensation from a funding recipient for a teacher's harassment requires actual notice to an official empowered to take corrective action of the harassment or its substantial risk, followed by deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that known danger that causes injury or continuation. The wrongdoer's knowledge of her own conduct is not alone institutional notice to an independent corrective authority. Merely constructive notice and ordinary agency assistance are insufficient for this compensatory recipient action. They would reverse the Title IX remand and restore recipient summary judgment on this record because deliberate disregard is not established even assuming the relevant actual knowledge.

**Sufficient grounds.** Franklin reserved attribution; its compensation rule does not decide when intentional teacher misconduct becomes a recipient's intentional breach. Title IX's recipient-centered funding obligation lacks Title VII's express agent language. Section 1682's enforcement structure addresses the recipient's own noncompliance and provides notice and opportunity for voluntary correction before federal enforcement action. That scheme, together with Pennhurst's notice principle at the reserved attribution scope, supports requiring conscious institutional nonresponse rather than imposing private compensation for what the recipient should have discovered. These four do not import the administrative hearing, federal-agency notice or thirty-day congressional-report provisions as literal prerequisites to a private suit; they derive an attribution distinction from the recipient's role. Nor do they apply qualified immunity to the District.

**Application.** Whitehouse and Mackiel investigated the asserted relationship through interviews, attempted telephone monitoring, two polygraph examinations and confrontation of McDougall, followed by authentication and termination when the journal, cards and photographs arrived. The favorable account permits criticism of delay, technique and failure to use available interim protection. It does not supply deliberate refusal to address the known danger rather than an imperfect effort to ascertain and stop it. An allegation that the trace used the wrong line is not proof that the error was deliberately chosen. The officials were not entitled to demand conclusive proof before any response; they did undertake responses before such proof arrived. Their use of denials and adverse tests is not treated as conclusive truth or a finding that Kinman lied.

**Strongest objection and answer.** Entrusting educational authority can materially enable a teacher's intentional discrimination; refusing ordinary agency attribution may leave a student without recipient compensation even where that authority facilitated the injury. These four accept the concern but distinguish program authority from the recipient's own compensable noncompliance under the funding scheme. Andrade answers an express employer-agent statute, while Franklin and Brown reserved this different question. They do not approve teacher misconduct, eliminate direct personal remedies, require an exact prior warning of each act, or make the mere existence of an investigation conclusive in every case.

**B. This is an objective connection-to-authorized-conduct rule, not a motive-only invalidation of an otherwise lawful search. It does not prohibit genuine requested assistance, necessary investigative documentation or entry under valid occupant consent. It decides no media entitlement outside the home. Section 3105 permits service by the named or legally authorized officers and aid requested by the officer while the officer is present and executing the warrant. ## Component commitments and operative grounds

**A — Forum:** Section 1341 bars this federal declaratory and injunctive action. These borrower-owned production credit associations sue for their own financial interests. Congress's designation of an entity as a federal instrumentality, and the possibility that federal law exempts its obligations or activities from particular taxes, do not make its independent suit one by the United States. The Farm Credit Act provides the associations' own sue-and-be-sued capacity and distinct statutory privileges; it does not identify this litigation as litigation of the sovereign. The associations' lending role, regulation and statutory designation do not establish sovereign identity sufficient to defeat section 1341 here.

*Department of Employment* supplies the United States' exception where the sovereign sues to protect itself and its instrumentalities; the United States and Red Cross were plaintiffs there. The Farm Credit associations fail even the functional-sovereign-interest path described in the United States' argument. It is unnecessary to adopt an absolute rule requiring the United States as named plaintiff in every possible instrumentality action, to resolve the treatment of the Federal Reserve or FDIC, or to settle every possible congressionally created exception.

## Record and statutory precision

Dorothy died August 14, 1979. Isaac married Sandra April 5, 1980, before his September 1, 1985 retirement; he died February 16, 1989. Dorothy's will left Isaac one-third and a lifetime usufruct over the sons' two-thirds. The 1980 succession judgment recognizes that state-law arrangement; it does not stipulate its federal enforceability. The retirement lump sum was $151,628.94, rolled into an IRA without intervening withdrawals, valued at $180,778.05 at death. Sandra is the IRA beneficiary and receives the survivor annuity. Ninety-six AT&T shares were distributed under the ESOP. The stipulated monthly retirement payment was $1,777.67. The claim paragraph's September 1, 1995 date conflicts with the separately stipulated chronology and is not used to move retirement past Isaac's death.

Four claims require decisions: **P**, an accounting for Dorothy's asserted share of pension payments made during Isaac's life; **S**, a percentage of Sandra's survivor annuity; **I**, a percentage of the rollover IRA; and **E**, a share of the distributed ESOP stock. The stipulation mentions life insurance but identifies no separate proceeds claim. The federal judgment denies Sandra's declaration; it does not award an accounting amount or conclusively divide the assets.

Section 1055 requires the applicable joint-and-survivor protection, subject to its enacted plan categories, election and consent rules. A qualified joint-and-survivor annuity supplies a survivor annuity for the spouse's life, at least 50 percent and at most 100 percent of the annuity payable during joint lives, with the stated actuarial equivalence. The percentage definition is not permission to divert the promised survivor benefit to a deceased predecessor spouse's devisees. The statutory waiver requires the requisite written, witnessed spousal consent and beneficiary/election protection, subject to the enacted inability-to-obtain-consent exceptions; no such waiver is established here. The optional one-year marriage condition does not exclude Sandra on this chronology. Later amendments concerning specific consent details or present-value calculations are not retroactively used to manufacture a defect in a 1985 election.

Section 1056(d)(1) protects pension benefits against assignment or alienation. Its text is not an absolute ban without exceptions: subsection (d)(2) preserves the stated voluntary revocable assignment of no more than ten percent of a benefit payment, the pre-September 2, 1974 irrevocable-assignment qualification, and the qualified secured-plan-loan rule, with the administrative-cost limitation. None describes this testamentary transfer. Subsection (d)(3) preserves a qualifying domestic relations order: a qualifying support, alimony or marital-property order for a spouse, former spouse, child or other dependent under state domestic-relations law, including community-property law, that satisfies the statutory identification, amount/formula, duration and plan requirements and does not require a new benefit form, increased actuarial benefits or a conflicting previously ordered benefit. A qualifying alternate payee is treated as a beneficiary. The segregation, eighteen-month determination, prospective late-qualification and proper-payment protections do not establish that this succession judgment was recognized or administered as such an order.

REA §303(d) separately requires administrators to treat qualifying pre-January 1, 1985 orders under its mandatory rule when payments were already being made pursuant to the order on that date, and permits treatment of other earlier orders under the permissive rule even without all the amended requirements. The 1980 date does not itself establish the mandatory predicate. No stipulated payment or administrative recognition supplies it. The word may does not create a compulsory entitlement by itself. The Court may resolve the legal sufficiency of the testamentary theory presented without finding a litigant default from a missing exhibit or prohibiting a genuinely established applicable statutory exception. The present record supplies no independent basis to order qualification, new administrative proceedings or a distribution under this transition rule.

Section 408(g) directs how the IRA tax section applies without regard to community-property law. Its distinct divorce-transfer tax rule in subsection (d)(6) is not an inheritance rule. Neither independently supplies a universal federal property code for the IRA. The operative claimed transfer occurred on Dorothy's death in 1979, followed by the 1980 order; this is not a holding adjudicating a separate cause of action or act completed before January 1, 1975 merely because some employment preceded ERISA.

## Competing grounds and their exact reach

**J — Federal adjudicative route, all eight:** Sandra is a beneficiary receiving the survivor annuity and seeks clarification and protection of her asserted rights to benefits under the plan. Section 1132(a)(1)(B), with the related federal equitable route to the extent applicable, supplies the federal controversy; the Declaratory Judgment Act alone does not. The sons' claim that the eventual assets are outside ERISA goes to the reach of the asserted protection, not to the absence of any substantial federal benefits controversy. *Guidry* footnote 16 likewise distinguishes an uncertain ancillary remedial theory from jurisdiction over a beneficiary's proper benefits action. No jurisdictional dismissal is proposed.

**S — Survivor annuity, all eight:** Reverse the rejection of preemption insofar as it permits the sons to obtain a portion of Sandra's statutory survivor annuity through Dorothy's testamentary interest. A state-law ownership label cannot defeat the particular surviving-spouse protection, and waiting for each installment to reach Sandra does not make this claim independent of that protected annuity. No applicable consent, QDRO or transition entitlement is established. This is a protection of the actual federally required survivor benefit, not a conclusion that every asset Sandra owns is immune from state law. No actuarial amount is adjudicated.

**T — Antecedent-transfer ground for P, I and E, adopted by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg:** These claims depend on recognizing and transferring at Dorothy's death an interest in then-undistributed plan benefits. The sons do not identify a later bargain, tort, loan, gift or other obligation independent of that asserted ownership. Enforcing the same alleged transfer after payment would give legal effect to the excluded alienation and beneficiary substitution. Sections 1056(d), 1144(a) and the enacted domestic-relations exceptions therefore preempt these particular testament-derived claims as well. The holding does not impose perpetual immunity on money or stock traceable to a pension. It rejects this claim's antecedent legal source. *Guidry* supplies the substantive protection and the rule against judicially created equitable exceptions; *Mackey* preserves the pension/welfare distinction. *Board of Trade* demonstrates that separate administration does not save an obligation whose legal operation invades the protected plan entitlement. *Travelers* and *Dillingham* foreclose reliance on a limitless economic connection, but this ground is a specific statutory conflict rather than such a connection. *Inter-Modal* likewise gives an independent enacted protection effect even when a different statutory feature would not preserve the claimed interest.

For **P**, the required accounting is not an ordinary unrelated debt merely because Isaac first received the checks: its existence depends wholly on Dorothy's asserted ownership of the benefits before their distribution. For **I**, the rollover and later IRA designation do not validate that earlier testamentary transfer; section 408(g) is unnecessary to this result, and the IRA is not declared to remain an ERISA plan. For **E**, delivery of the stock similarly changes its custody and form without creating a valid antecedent testamentary interest. No independent claim to stock acquired outside the distribution is decided. Reverse the Fifth Circuit on each component and remand to implement the preemption declaration at this scope, with ordinary resolution of any genuinely unresolved statutory-exception issue actually presented. No new factual premise of administrative recognition is supplied.

**U — Recipient-obligation ground for P, I and E, adopted by Stevens, Souter and Breyer:** Affirm rejection of preemption for these three claims insofar as relief is confined to an accounting against the recipient or succession and to assets already paid out, without redirecting payments by the plan, changing plan duties or taking Sandra's protected survivor annuity. Louisiana's ownership and usufruct rules supply the asserted obligation; the plan's payment is a historical source of property rather than the command that defines a new plan benefit. Section 1056(d) protects delivery of benefits and forbids a transfer enforceable against the plan; it does not expressly erase every ownership or accounting obligation after delivery. The QDRO route permits demands upon the plan and changes in federally recognized payees; its detailed conditions do not necessarily occupy every later relationship among recipients and successors. *Mackey* requires attention to Congress's chosen statutory distinctions and permits ordinary enforcement outside the protected category. *Guidry* prohibits a constructive trust upon the pension itself and does not decide every obligation to account after distribution. *Travelers* and *Dillingham* require an actual interference with the plan's legal operation rather than the bare derivation of an asset from benefits. *Board of Trade* involved a state-required covered-plan benefit benchmark and does not resolve an ordinary succession obligation solely because its amount can be traced to former payments.

These three nevertheless reject a decree that would reach future pension payments in plan hands, alter the administrator's duties, or reduce Sandra's survivor annuity. They would not order the accounting amount, ownership fraction or any distribution. Section 408(g)'s tax instruction does not by itself preempt the IRA claim; ESOP stock already delivered is ordinary property for this limited analysis. Their strongest objection to T is that it converts a prohibition on plan-benefit alienation into an unstated federal rule extinguishing a spouse's otherwise existing ownership and every derivative recipient obligation. T's strongest reply is that calling the right preexisting does not answer whether federal law permits that very right in undistributed pension benefits. The disagreement is that legal characterization, not a factual difference or a general exemption for domestic relations.

## Presented facts and distinct components

The Fund operates two separately incorporated, licensed New York diagnostic and treatment centers and a New Jersey center. The New York centers provide services principally to covered workers, retirees and dependents, with additional outside-employer and PPGU arrangements described by the State's record account. The Fund is a self-insured multiemployer welfare plan, not thereby a federal sovereign or federal regulatory agency.

The HFA is a revenue assessment on licensed health-facility operations. The reviewed diagnostic-center rate is 0.6 percent of assessable gross cash receipts from patient care and other operations. Receipts reach the general fund. The centers reported $1,177,670 in assessable receipts for January through November 1991 and paid $7,066.02 before ceasing reports and payments. Neither figure is a final present assessment or an adjudicated refund. Transfers from the Fund to its centers were excluded as grants, and the State did not contest that exclusion. Other receipts include direct outside-employer payments, PPGU and workers' compensation payments, registration fees and copayments, and miscellaneous revenue. The Fund does not challenge the tax on unrelated miscellaneous receipts.

The April 16, 1992 complaint requests prospective declaration and injunction and repayment. The District Court identified but expressly left undecided the state-remedy dispute under the Tax Injunction Act. It also left the separate Eleventh Amendment objection to repayment undecided. The Court of Appeals reversed on ERISA and adhered after reconsideration. Neither court's merits discussion establishes an adjudicated refund entitlement. State silence in later appellate briefing does not waive section 1341 or prove adequate or inadequate state relief.

The components are **A**, the statutory tax-jurisdiction framework and absence of an automatic ERISA exception; **B**, adequacy of the actual New York route for this prospective preemption challenge; **C**, the jurisdictional disposition and separate treatment of repayment; and **D**, the ERISA merits commitment if a lawful federal vehicle remains after the distinct jurisdictional issues. D is recorded for reconciliation and compatibility only; it is not an alternative merits holding of a court dismissing for lack of jurisdiction.

## A and B — threshold grounds adopted by all eight

**A:** The HFA is a state tax for the present section 1341 inquiry: its gross-receipts assessment raises public revenue rather than imposing a regulatory sanction adjudicated against a particular violator. The Court need not adopt the lower court's broad suggestion that every revenue collection device is necessarily a tax. Section 1341 bars federal prospective restraint where a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy may be had. ERISA's federal fiduciary cause of action does not repeal that restriction; section 1144(d) expressly preserves other federal law. Nor does federal regulation of a plan make this private Fund the United States for purposes of the sovereign exception. The holding does not decide the position of a genuine federal agency or a different express statute.

**B:** New York's independent declaratory-judgment route affords a sufficiently plain and adequate opportunity to obtain adjudication of this federal preemption objection. The District Court itself identified that affirmative route. *Tully* establishes the operative New York practice of entertaining constitutional challenges to taxation through CPLR 3001 notwithstanding tax-review exclusivity language, with ordinary preliminary relief available in a proper case. *Morgan Guaranty* demonstrates that New York's courts actually adjudicate an ERISA preemption objection to a tax. Its different refund procedure is not treated as the HFA's procedure. Together these authorities answer the presented categorical objection that ERISA prevents the state court from considering the federal question at all. Section 1132(e)(1) reserves specified ERISA civil actions to federal courts; it does not withdraw the state court's authority to apply supreme federal law when deciding a different state tax action. The adequate state cause of action need not duplicate section 1132(a)(3).

This is a legal adequacy determination on the demonstrated declaratory route, not a presumption based on the Fund's failure to produce an exhibit or a guarantee of the Fund's substantive success. No special HFA barrier to that declaratory route is established in the presented dispute, and none is invented. The State need not concede that the tax is unlawful or promise a preliminary injunction for the route to be available. Conversely, a merely hypothetical or inaccessible remedy would not suffice. *Reich* forbids a State to hold out a postpayment remedy and then withdraw it when invoked; *National Private Truck Council* depends on an actually adequate channel. Neither decision permits substitution of a label for a real hearing. Their limit is satisfied by the identified existing state adjudicative route for prospective relief, not by importing Arkansas procedure or the amount paid in another case.

The strongest contrary authority is the Second Circuit's jurisdictional analysis in lower *Travelers*, reinforced by the Fund's direct section 1132(a)(3) claim. Its inference conflates exclusivity over that federal cause of action with exclusive authority over every federal preemption issue. It does not establish that New York must refuse its own declaratory action. The earlier Supreme Court *Travelers* merits holding did not adopt that jurisdictional analysis or create an ERISA exception to section 1341. The Court's present interpretation gives effect to both section 1132 and section 1144(d), while leaving the federal question fully available in state adjudication and ordinary review.

## D — conditional ERISA merits commitments, all eight

If merits are lawfully reached, all eight would reject this preemption challenge and reverse the Second Circuit's contrary merits conclusion at the following scope. This position is not an operative holding accompanying C.

The assessment operates on licensed medical-provider activity and assessable receipts independently of ERISA status. ERISA coverage neither triggers the tax nor supplies a mandatory benefit level, eligibility criterion or legal measure of a plan obligation. A plan's choice to provide care through its own centers does not convert every ordinary provider cost into regulation of the plan as a plan. *Travelers* preserves ordinary economic influence while leaving direct self-funded applications open; its reservation is addressed rather than treated as an earlier holding against the Fund. *Dillingham* supplies the current inquiry into actual reference or connection, and rejects the inference that an independently operating criterion refers to ERISA merely because a covered entity encounters it. *Board of Trade* involved a state-required covered-plan benefit benchmark, which is absent here. *Inter-Modal* preserves independently enacted ERISA protections; it supplies no tax exemption or special rule for self-provided welfare services.

The Fund's strongest objection is that the tax falls on its very delivery of benefits, reduces money for promised services and induces different fund-routing and accounting choices across state lines. Those effects are real economic consequences, but the presented law does not dictate the terms of benefits, eligibility, covered beneficiaries, funding promises, fiduciary standards or the administrator's adjudication of claims. Tax reporting and a choice to route otherwise exempt grants are not themselves legal commands to redesign plan entitlements. The grant exclusion is an actual accepted application here, not a finding that every receipt could be recharacterized to avoid tax. Direct incidence and reduction in available assets do not alone distinguish the tax from ordinary costs that a plan incurs when it operates a medical facility.

Neither general applicability nor the 0.6 percent rate is a categorical safe harbor. A discriminatory reference, mandatory covered-plan benchmark, competing benefit-enforcement system or sufficiently acute economic coercion that effectively dictates choices could present a different question. This record establishes no such operation. The Court would not require the Fund to prove insolvency or pretend that any financial burden is harmless; it would require the asserted connection to be one ERISA protects against. Section 1144(b)(5)(B)(i)'s reservation concerning tax laws in the special Hawaii provision preserves application of the ordinary preemption rule to taxes; it does not preempt all taxes touching plans. The insurance saving and deemer clauses do not change this provider-tax inquiry or turn self-insured status into general immunity.

The exact state statutory qualifications remain material. Section 2801's definition and exclusions, section 2807-d(1)(a)'s personal-needs and refund deductions and paragraph-(b) exclusion, and section 2807-d(2)(c)'s specified diagnostic-center rate are not collapsed into a tax on every dollar. Conditional nonpreemption decides neither an individual receipt's classification nor liability outside the challenged operations.

## Threshold component: reachability of the August judgment

The later denial of leave is part of that composite disposition; it does not conclusively establish that the merits were never entertained. This is a record-specific legal determination, not a rule that filing any second motion restarts the clock. With actual entertainment, the December 27 petition is timely from December 16.

The strongest contrary path applies Bowman's warning about denied leave literally: no formal allowance of a second rehearing appears, the December order can be read as declining reopening, and the ordinary deadline from September 3 expired December 2. That path would bar direct review absent an unestablished valid extension and would require a distinct, supported recall-review basis for any relief. They do not derive jurisdiction from §2106. No Justice relies exclusively on an independent recall route, and no indefinite power to revive expired review is adopted.

Actual entertainment for finality means the court considered the merits of the renewed request. It is not consistent with a claim that Sheets was never presented or wholly unconsidered. The separate remedial dispute is whether the existing unexplained denial, against the still-articulated August rationale, leaves a sufficiently probable unresolved error about the new state-law rule to justify this limited remedy. ## Common threshold and issue divisions

All eight Associates reach the granted County-attribution question. The published lower opinion establishes a permitted §1292(b) appeal; this is not the unauthorized pendant County appeal vacated in Swint. The fact that the old Swint merits reasoning was vacated requires fresh evaluation, not automatic reversal of a later judgment through a valid channel. No separate issue about hearsay, individual immunity, the ultimate truth of misconduct allegations, or jail staffing is granted here.

All eight apply Monell's separate institutional-attribution requirement. Kinman's unanimous May 27 institutional holding confirms the policy/custom or attributable final-decision route, including a single decision if final authority is established. Its personal-supervisor culpability and Title IX recipient rules do not determine this case. Collins likewise separates federal injury from attribution. Bryan County remains pending for reargument and supplies no merits rule. All reject ordinary respondeat superior, a label-only determination, and a requirement that every County policy come from the County commission.

Jett Part IV, 491 U.S. 737–738, supplies a majority rule for judicial identification of the official who speaks with final policymaking authority concerning the particular act, using state/local positive law and legally effective custom. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy directly joined it. Pembaur's executive single-decision holding and actual Ohio application must be distinguished from its four-Justice Part II-B formula. Praprotnik's O'Connor plurality, joined by Scalia, preserves genuine delegation, ratification and custom while respecting legal allocation. Neither case decides that every locally elected sheriff represents a county, or that a State constitutional classification invariably defeats County attribution.

The separable components are: (A) the legal method and granted scope; (B) application to the sheriff's criminal-investigation/law-enforcement function; and (C) remedy and reservation of other claims/functions. Each Associate accepts A and the bounded nature of C, but the application of B divides them.

## Two supported application paths

**Affirmance path.** Alabama's constitutional allocation places the sheriff in the State executive department; its courts treat the office as State employment in the relevant structural cases; the criminal-investigation statutes require service to courts and prosecutorial officers, with State executive authority to direct investigations. The county supplies money and facilities but the record establishes no County power to direct, review, or prescribe the challenged criminal-investigative policy. This is an allocation of authority, not merely a title. The County need not answer for a different entity's policy because it pays expenses.

The strongest counterargument is that an independently elected sheriff may himself embody County government. The absence of commission control does not show absence of County authority if the law placed local final authority directly in the sheriff. Local election, jurisdiction, revenue flows, office facilities and operating expenses are part of the same governmental arrangement; the State's immunity classifications concern a different question. Treating the commission as synonymous with the County would impose a control relationship incompatible with final policymaking.

**Reversal path.** The sheriff's final criminal-investigative choices within the county are exercises of the local governmental office the State created. The State's broad executive label and ultimate oversight do not displace the local authority demonstrated by election, jurisdiction, financing, and independent operational responsibility. The existing dismissal mistakenly treats the lack of commission control as a sufficient answer; final local policy can be assigned directly to a sheriff. Reversal does not prove a federal injury or impose vicarious liability for a subordinate's act.

Its strongest counterargument is that locality and financial support do not identify the entity represented: State officials can be elected by districts and serve a local territory. Jett requires respect for the actual positive-law allocation, not a federal presumption that decentralized law enforcement is County business. The reversal position must show why the arrangement establishes a County policymaker; it cannot merely count local features or ignore the investigation-directing statutes.

## Common threshold, prior findings and controlling framework

All eight Associates reach the granted other-property question under admiralty jurisdiction. The trial court found a defective hydraulic design and legal causation and imposed strict products liability; the circuit affirmed those findings and the two-thirds reduction for Saratoga's own fault. Footnote 8 of the circuit opinion does not support an independent negligence holding. None of the Associates reopens those matters or treats every causation, defense and liability issue as untried. Exxon protects legal causation as a distinct inquiry before comparative allocation; here its application respects the found nonsuperseding causal contribution rather than substitutes a new appellate finding.

East River controls the commercial product/self-damage rule. Its 867 discussion treats the integrated manufacturer-supplied turbine/propulsion package as one product, preventing component-by-component tort recovery for the machine's own damage. Its 871–875 discussion protects contract allocation, including the downstream charterers' actual repair and insurance obligations. It preserves other-property tort principles, subject to their elements and any valid applicable allocation, but does not already choose the product boundary after user additions and resale. No Associate finds a categorical fisherman exemption or treats every post-sale item as other property.

The remaining adjudicative components are: (A) classification of initial-user additions transferred at resale; (B) the scope of the disposition, preserving original and replacement components and the separate hull theory; and (C) implementation through the existing damages and interest posture. The disputed classification is A. Agreement to preserve B and C does not make the Associates agree on A.

## Supported competing rules

**Manufacturer-to-initial-user boundary.** Property independently added by an initial user after the manufacturer's delivery remains other property against that manufacturer merely notwithstanding its later transfer with the product to a subsequent user. The original product, its supplied integrated components, and its actual contractual allocation remain protected by East River. A user addition does not lose its identity only because the user later sells the bundle. This rule rests on who supplied the product and the actual sequence, not mere absence of privity.

The strongest objection is that the purchaser here bargained for a complete fishing machine, including the equipment necessary to fish, and accepted its commercial condition. East River took downstream bargains seriously. The response is that the manufacturer sold an identifiable product to an initial user who actually used it before resale; risk to additional property already existed during that user's ownership. The resale alone does not enlarge or shrink that manufacturer's existing tort duty. A resale warranty about product performance does not itself waive the manufacturer's duty concerning other property.

**Subsequent-purchase boundary.** The product is the integrated commercial vessel and equipment acquired in Saratoga's own bargain. Loss of that entire acquisition is disappointed product value governed by contract and warranty; the manufacturer's tort duty should not depend on which seller supplied each operational part before the plaintiff purchased the completed asset.

The strongest objection is that the original seller receives a liability reduction solely from a later title transfer and that ordinary external property can become product damage by contract with someone else. The response is that East River's boundary concerns the plaintiff's protected interest and allocation of product value, not only the defendant's manufacturing inventory; its downstream-charter analysis would lose significance if only the first sale mattered. This view must still leave newly acquired catch and separately retained external property distinguishable, rather than swallow all property aboard.

## Opinion compatibility and remedy

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer can directly join the manufacturer-to-initial-user rule and its application to independent additions transferred at resale. The common opinion should explain both East River's manufacturer-supplied integration and downstream-bargain passages; rejecting the second-purchase boundary does not ignore either. It can state that Saratoga's as-is resale terms do not by themselves establish a waiver of the original seller's other-property tort obligation, without holding that express tort allocations are impossible.

Scalia and Thomas can join a contrary opinion defending the integrated second-purchase boundary. Their disagreement is what counts as the product, not rejection of maritime products liability or an assertion that comparative fault was superseding cause. No author is assigned. The internal Associate alignment is six for reversal on A and two for affirmance; Stone's vote and grounds are not inferred.

All eight agree that the grant does not reopen the separate denial of hull recovery against Marco, the treatment of installed replacement components, the affirmed legal-causation findings, or the two-thirds fault allocation. The issue is not whether every item purchased later is forever other property; uninstalled spare inventory and replacement components require the correct distinct characterization. The circuit's preserved catch, crew-property, currency, rescue, and interest determinations are disturbed only to the extent proper calculation of the resulting award requires, not by a general retrial order.

A reversing disposition should reverse the challenged other-property exclusion and remand for further proceedings consistent with the rule, leaving the circuit/district courts to implement supported item classifications, damages and corresponding interest.
