## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The agency’s no-development determination is final

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches ripeness because it is the sole basis of dismissal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Is a facially complete regulatory-takings claim unripe because the owner has not attempted to sell transferable development rights, when the agency has definitively determined that the parcel may not be developed and the transfer rights affect compensation or economic impact rather than the permitted use of the parcel?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received final-decision doctrine and distinguish land-use finality from proof of economic impact.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A regulatory-takings claim is final for federal adjudication when the responsible agency has definitively prohibited development of the parcel and no further land-use procedure can alter that decision. The owner need not first sell transferable development rights that do not change the parcel’s permitted use.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agency has answered the institutional question assigned to it: this parcel cannot be developed. Marketing the transfer rights may show their worth, but the market cannot revise the land-use determination. Requiring a sale as a jurisdictional prerequisite would make a merits fact into a gate and could force the owner to dispose of a disputed asset before a court decides its legal significance.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone expresses no view on whether a taking occurred, how the rights affect economic impact or compensation, or what other remedial prerequisites may apply.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's proposed remand permits the takings claim to be heard; it supplies no Lucas total-loss finding and no damages award. The transfer rights remain relevant on the merits despite being unnecessary to obtain a final agency use determination.
