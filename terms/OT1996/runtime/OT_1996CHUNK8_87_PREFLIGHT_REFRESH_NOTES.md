## 1. Current-law correction

General Motors Corp. v. Tracy, February 18, 1997, is earlier-effective current-term law. Its exact public holdings, published positions and procedural consequence have been supplied in OT_1996CHUNK8_87_TRACY_NEUTRAL.md and are copied below. The packet's opening-only cutoff and absence-of-earlier-decision clauses cannot exclude this authority.

Tracy requires examination of the actual competitive relationship and materially different service obligations before treating the compared activities as equivalent. It sustained the particular comparison between regulated utility supply and independent commodity marketing because compulsory rate, reliability and service duties materially distinguished the offerings and the record did not establish the asserted separate industrial-sales category. Commodity overlap alone did not establish comparability on that record. This is neither a general public-service exemption nor a presumption that all transactions of a favored institution are different; the Court left a materially different proved sales category open. Oregon Waste and Fulton retain their demanding justification and cumulative compensatory requirements once discrimination is established. Tracy's distinct rational-basis holding does not lower Commerce Clause scrutiny or add an equal-protection question to this grant.

The original Camps packet's statement that the relevant comparison is the same service with resident and nonresident recipients should therefore be read as the camp's supported comparison to be tested, not a factual or legal stipulation that conclusively answers Tracy. The town may argue that resident-serving charities provide a different public service by relieving local obligations and that the exemption constitutes local public assistance. That argument must identify the actual distinction it invokes; no compulsory utility-like service, rate, reliability or universal-service duties have been found for either class of charities in this supplied record. The camp may respond that both classes provide the same private charitable service and that recipient residence alone creates the tax difference. That response must address the claimed local-service relationship rather than treat every charitable activity as necessarily comparable. Neither a charitable label nor bare overlap in services resolves the question by itself.

These remain lawful analytical paths, not votes: the actual service comparison may establish discrimination subject to the existing justification rule; material differences may defeat the asserted discrimination without reaching justification; or a genuinely unresolved material comparison may require a bounded remand if the governing law and existing record warrant it. A new evidentiary proceeding is not automatic merely because the parties dispute the legal significance of established facts. Market participation, subsidy characterization, comparison, justification, compensatory taxation and remedy remain distinct inquiries. The refresh neither finds an equivalence nor finds a materially different service obligation, and imports no Tracy factual finding into the camp's record.

## 2. Statutory completeness correction

The sanitized source supplies the entire published footnote quotation of Me. Rev. Stat. Ann., title 36, §652(1)(A) (Supp. 1996), officially recited at 520 U.S. 564, 568–569 n.2. It contains statutory text only, without the current case's result, rationale, lineup or commitments. The exact passage is copied below, including its source's line wrapping and typographic extraction artifact. It is a verified published recitation, not a claim that a separate original code supplement or session law was recovered. The quotation's concluding ellipsis is retained as part of that published quotation; no omitted language is inferred.

The operative qualifications, which must remain explicit in subsequent modeling and any eventual holding using them, are:

- The general paragraph concerns real estate and personal property owned and occupied or used solely for their own purposes by benevolent and charitable institutions incorporated by Maine. Its protection against deprivation because of fund source or limitations in beneficiary classes must be read together with the express subparagraph that follows.
- An institution actually conducted or operated principally for nonresidents receives an exemption **not exceeding $50,000 of current just value** only when its statutorily computed average weekly charge per person is **no more than $30**. This is a cap on exempt current just property value, not a $50,000 reduction in tax due or a universal dollar subsidy.
- The statute computes that rate by dividing all stipends or charges taken during the tax year for services, benefits or advantages by all persons receiving them during that year, then dividing that average annual per-person amount by the number of weeks in the tax year when the institution is actually operated principally for nonresidents. The definition of tax year points to §502. A quoted advertised weekly rate is not itself a substitute for the statutory calculation if a disputed computation matters.
- A principally nonresident-serving institution whose rate, computed under that provision, **exceeds $30** receives no tax exemption under the subparagraph. The low-charge capped exemption and the above-threshold exclusion must not be merged into one unqualified nonresident rule.
- **The subparagraph does not apply to institutions incorporated as nonprofit corporations for the sole purpose of conducting medical research.** This removes that subparagraph's restriction for a qualifying institution; it does not itself dispense with all other requirements of the general exemption. No finding establishes that this camp qualifies, and no broader exception for every charitable medical activity is inferred.
- The paragraph's illustrative benevolent/charitable categories and licensing/incorporation conditions remain as quoted. Its definition of nonprofit concerns a facility exempt under Internal Revenue Code §501(c)(3); nonprofit status alone is not a holding that every other statutory condition has been met.

The original packet states roughly $400 weekly camp charges and approximately 95 percent nonresident campers. This refresh preserves those statements while correcting the incomplete statutory description. It makes no new arithmetic calculation, tax-year finding, beneficiary tally, nonprofit-medical-research determination, tax amount, preservation conclusion or entitlement to the $50,000 cap. The exact amount of lawful retrospective relief remains outside the presented federal merits determination.
