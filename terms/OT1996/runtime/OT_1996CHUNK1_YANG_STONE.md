## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for lawful discretionary consideration.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Board may consider Yang’s broader pattern of immigration fraud, while remaining bound by the statute, any valid agency policy, and reasoned decisionmaking. The Court does not order denial of the waiver.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Eligibility, discretion, and agency policy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory scope of discretion because the Ninth Circuit imposed a categorical limitation that controlled the judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Attorney General, when deciding whether to grant a discretionary waiver of deportation under former 8 U.S.C. § 1251(a)(1)(H), may consider a pattern of immigration fraud connected with and extending beyond the entry fraud that made the alien deportable.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted distinction between eligibility and discretion. Permit consideration of the broader pattern, but retain ordinary review for arbitrariness, pretext, or unexplained departure from agency policy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Former § 1251(a)(1)(H) does not forbid the Attorney General from considering a pattern of immigration fraud when selecting among statutorily eligible applicants. Eligibility does not erase the conduct or compel relief.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text identifies who may be considered and expressly leaves the ultimate decision discretionary. Treating a later naturalization fraud and separate sham arrangements as legally nonexistent would add a restriction Congress did not enact. At the same time, discretion is not immunity from law: if the agency announces a policy, it must apply it rationally or openly revise it with an explanation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not approve automatic denial based solely on the waivable entry fraud, decide the waiver balance, or extend immigration discretion to interior classifications outside the native immigration function.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's distinction between statutory eligibility and lawful discretionary relief is consistent with his own four-Justice Doherty rationale, which remains a separate writing rather than Court law. His proposed application to Yang therefore rests on this waiver's text and actual fraud/policy record. National Center for Immigrants' Rights reinforces the need to preserve legally required individual consideration; it does not authorize automatic denial of every eligible waiver applicant or excuse an unexplained agency policy departure.
