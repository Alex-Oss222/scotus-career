# 72. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Robertson-Ceco Corp.

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether the Due Process Clause requires a separate inquiry into the reasonableness of exercising general personal jurisdiction after a corporation's contacts with the forum state have been found continuous and systematic.

2. Whether the absence of a substantial connection between the forum state and the underlying controversy may render general jurisdiction constitutionally unreasonable despite the defendant's substantial business contacts with that state.

3. How courts should determine the relevant period and nature of a corporation's contacts when evaluating general personal jurisdiction.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

A New York insurer sued a Delaware corporation headquartered in Pennsylvania over a Florida building's curtain-wall system, selecting Vermont's favorable limitations period. The district court dismissed for insufficient general contacts. The Second Circuit found continuous and systematic Vermont business contacts but affirmed because exercising jurisdiction was unreasonable. The plaintiffs' suit was unrelated to Vermont. The record included substantial multiyear sales, dealer or builder relationships and solicitation. Companion petitions challenge the contact and fairness rulings; they concern one lawsuit. The requested remedy is jurisdictional, not an adjudication of construction liability.

## C. ENTERING LAW APPLIED TO THE RECORD

International Shoe, Perkins, Helicopteros, Burger King and Asahi supply pre-divergence principles. The contacts inquiry distinguishes specific jurisdiction over connected claims from general jurisdiction over unrelated ones. The forum's interests, burden on the defendant, plaintiff's interest in effective relief and systemic fairness remain relevant; Asahi's international burdens cannot simply be presumed for domestic litigation. Neither later Goodyear nor Daimler is available to impose an “at home” limitation here.

No authenticated simulated holding displaces this general-jurisdiction framework. The inherited standards' source-first and record-specific method requires assessing the actual course of business over a reasonable period before filing. Sales totals alone, a manufactured single-day snapshot or a generalized interest in business cannot answer continuity and systematic activity. Conversely, if general contacts are constitutionally adequate, lack of a suit-related forum connection is expected in this category and cannot automatically nullify it. A genuinely exceptional burden may still matter. Venue, transfer, limitations choice and personal jurisdiction must not be collapsed into one convenience inquiry.

## D. PARTY POSITIONS, AVAILABLE DISPOSITIONS AND LIMITS

The insurer relies on deliberate sustained Vermont business and argues that ordinary domestic litigation burdens cannot defeat the resulting jurisdiction. The corporation disputes the sufficiency of the contacts and emphasizes the out-of-state parties, events and proof. Affirmance could rest on insufficient contacts or independently excessive burden if supported by the correct tests. Reversal or vacatur would restore litigation only to the extent other threshold issues permit; it would not resolve defect, damages or timeliness. Both companion-petition contentions remain visible.

# 73. Kadic v. Karadzic; Doe v. Karadzic

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether the Alien Tort Statute, 28 U.S.C. §1350, permits federal jurisdiction over civil claims alleging customary-international-law violations committed by private or nonstate actors, including genocide and war crimes.

2. Whether customary international law requires state action for every actionable violation, or whether particular offenses impose duties directly on private persons.

3. Whether civil claims against a foreign political or military leader for international-law violations must be dismissed under the political-question doctrine or a related abstention principle.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND AUTHORIZED TERM SETUP

Authorized counterfactual setup: timely petitions seeking review of the consolidated Second Circuit judgment were filed and held through July 1, 1996. The simulation grants review on October 7, 1996 under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), preserving both Kadic and Doe appeals in one matter, with the merits hearing scheduled April 21, 1997. The consolidated civil complaints by Bosnian victims allege rape, torture and killing connected with a campaign of genocide and armed conflict, and Karadzic's personal or command responsibility. He headed the self-proclaimed Republika Srpska. The district court dismissed for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction; the Second Circuit reversed and remanded. These are pleading-stage allegations, not liability findings. Claims depending on official action also allege governmental authority or cooperation with an existing state. [Reported opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/70/232/601336/).

The United States' actual Statement of Interest, signed by the Solicitor General and State Department Legal Adviser, disclaimed a political-question obstacle to these suits. The State Department also stated that Karadzic's United Nations invitee status conferred no immunity; the appellate court rejected the asserted service immunity, subject to proof of service outside the headquarters district. The act-of-state doctrine was not asserted below and was not before the appellate court. Those record facts narrow the actual objections; an imagined future executive recognition or immunity determination supplies no present bar.

The original merits decision is October 13, 1995; January 6, 1996 is the reported rehearing date, although the online page's date field uses the latter. Both companions remain distinct plaintiffs' actions within this single Supreme Court matter. Karadzic seeks restoration of dismissal; the plaintiffs seek continuation of their civil claims. The supplied grant reaches the three questions above, not a final tort judgment or the amount of recovery.

## C. ENTERING LAW APPLIED TO THE QUESTIONS

Section 1350 confers jurisdiction over an alien's tort violating the law of nations or a United States treaty. Jurisdiction, the norm's existence and personal scope, domestic authority for a private action, and recoverable relief remain separate. Genocide and the core laws of armed conflict require a norm-specific inquiry into duties of individuals; there is no logical necessity that every international obligation attach only to recognized governments. Conversely, recognition of privately committed war crimes does not eliminate an official-action requirement belonging to a different norm. The pleaded context and required intent, armed-conflict connection, protected persons and defendant's responsibility must eventually be proved. The Geneva Conventions and genocide instruments are evidence of the asserted norms; using them for that purpose does not decide that every provision independently creates a domestic civil action.

Simulated Suter refuses to infer a private action from a statutory obligation without the required source-specific justification. Its child-welfare holding neither decides §1350 nor prohibits every received common-law action. Simulated Central Bank preserves bounded development of a received securities action, including its own culpability and participation limits; it supplies an analogy for examining a received action, not an ATS cause or universal accessory-liability rule. Simulated Franklin permits appropriate relief within an established action but creates no missing cause and reserves punitive damages. Thus, even if a pleaded offense binds a private actor, the court must identify why domestic law permits this civil action before authorizing recovery.

The Torture Victim Protection Act supplies a separate enacted action for its defined torture and extrajudicial-killing claims, subject to actual or apparent authority or color of foreign law and its own conditions. It does not remove the state-action requirement from its own text or settle every §1350 claim. The grant requires no finding that every pleaded claim meets that alternative statute.

Simulated Nixon v. United States applies Baker's issue-specific textual-commitment and manageable-standards inquiry: its refusal to revise a Senate impeachment evidentiary procedure does not make politically sensitive cases categorically nonjusticiable. Here the court must identify any foreign-policy judgment the claims necessarily require, rather than equate adjudicating individual conduct with recognizing a state or directing diplomacy. The pre-divergence act-of-state rule, including W. S. Kirkpatrick, likewise turns on a necessary determination of an official sovereign act's validity; foreign connection alone is insufficient. Sovereign, official and service immunities remain separate questions. §1350 supplies no universal immunity exception.

## D. PARTY POSITIONS, POSSIBLE DISPOSITIONS AND LIMITS

Karadzic contests federal jurisdiction over a nonstate actor and invokes foreign-affairs restraint. The plaintiffs distinguish offenses that bind individuals from official-action norms and argue that applying established prohibitions need not resolve the legitimacy of a government. A court accepting that distinction could affirm the appellate remand while requiring proof of each norm's elements and the defendant's responsibility; a court rejecting the domestic action or concluding that a particular claim requires unpleaded official conduct could dismiss that claim without adjudicating the others. Any concrete immunity or justiciability obstacle requires its own analysis.

The prepared Abebe-Jira position presents a related source-of-action issue, but is not completed Court law.
