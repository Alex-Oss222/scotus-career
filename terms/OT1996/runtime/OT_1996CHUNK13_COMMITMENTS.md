## Raines v. Byrd — No. 96-1671

### Source addition, review and competing grounds

Before completing these commitments this context received and read the clean `OT_1996CHUNK13_RAINES_MERITS_NEUTRAL.md` supplement, including its enacted-text and lower-opinion support and the validator's complete-reading receipt. Its SHA-256 is `ba808e72356fda4a7521240b603e14535320629789fcd2b144cbe1f40d951018`. It fills the original merits-source gap without changing entering law or supplying a Supreme Court outcome. No actual cancellation is assumed.

All eight accept the direct appeal from the single district judge and the supplied dates. Article III must precede the disputed constitutional merits. The Houses did not authorize these plaintiffs to represent them; their amici defend validity without taking a standing position. Hatfield's retirement does not cure or defeat the five continuing Members' claims.

**No-standing path.** These Members' votes were counted and lost; none was excluded, deprived of a decisive vote, or subjected to personal financial or informational injury. The claimed diminution of future legislative influence is institutional. Coleman concerns votes whose nullification changed a result; Powell concerns personal exclusion and its incidents. Lujan and Bender require the plaintiff's own concrete stake. Akins establishes that sharing does not make an actual informational deprivation abstract, but supplies no similar deprivation here. Congress can remove prudential barriers within its enactment's scope, not Article III.

The strongest objection is that the Act changes the legal effect of the Members' present votes by making the resulting package subject to executive cancellation. The six no-standing Justices answer that equal voting weight and actual participation remain, while Congress holds the disputed institutional authority. A legislature's enactments often change the consequences of future legislative work. The availability of repeal or protection of later legislation reinforces the institutional character without becoming an exhaustion requirement or an independent automatic bar. They do not hold every official injury abstract, every institutional suit unavailable or every Article I dispute a political question.

**Standing path.** Stevens and Breyer regard the immediate alteration of a Member's own voting function as concrete official injury, with statutory authorization confirming protection of that interest. Judicial invalidation would remove the alleged alteration without awaiting a specific cancellation. Coleman protects effective votes; Akins rejects numerical exclusivity; Lujan requires concreteness, not money or loss of office. The complete district source supports current budgetary work and impending appropriations votes, making this injury theory ripe without invented subsequent events.

Their strongest difficulty is Stevens's own Bender opinion, which bars an individual member from appropriating his body's appeal. They distinguish an expressly authorized suit concerning the Member's present voting power from an unauthorized appeal imposing no individual obligation. Statutory permission alone does not create standing; the asserted functional injury supplies it. A chance to persuade a majority to repeal the law does not erase that injury.

### Merits alternatives for the standing Justices

The complete source supports two distinct legal paths. The repeal account treats cancellation after signature as a new removal of enacted legal force, subject to Chadha's prescribed legislative procedure irrespective of intelligible standards. The execution account treats the Act and later law together as a conditional enactment, with Field v. Clark, Hampton and ordinary delegation principles supporting bounded executive implementation. It must answer permanent cancellation and the distinct direct-spending and tax effects; permissive appropriations alone cannot resolve all categories.

Stevens adopts the repeal account and would affirm the declaration on Article I grounds. His pre-divergence Bowsher concurrence, Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority Court opinion and Chadha join distinguish the constitutionally allocated function from an efficient rearrangement. A signed bill becomes law regardless of private presidential reservations; later cancellation changes its legal effect. Discretionary rescission, extinguishment of the specified new spending authority or obligation, and removal of a qualifying tax provision's force differ in detail but share that challenged mechanism. Field's contingent implementation does not establish a general power to select which enacted provisions survive. His ground is structural, not insufficiency of the deficit standard or subjective lack of presidential approval.

Breyer reserves an Act-merits vote in his dissent from the jurisdictional dismissal. This is an independent scope decision: his disagreement is that the Court should adjudicate the merits, and deciding every category's validity is unnecessary to explain it. It is not a research-gap shortcut. The conditional-execution account is substantial under Field, Hampton and Mistretta and consistent with the functional separation analysis of his current Clinton concurrence; permanent removal of enacted obligations and tax provisions remains a serious contrary consideration. His frozen position is to reject jurisdictional dismissal and reach the merits, neither sustain the Act nor join Stevens's invalidation. No later position is imported.

## Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — No. 96-511

### Components, review and complete operative statutory boundaries

All eight Associates accept the supplied direct-appeal route under Act §561 and the live pre-enforcement speech controversy. The appeal concerns the preliminary order as clarified, not a new permanent injunction or damages claim. No disputed filing date, standing defect or expiration is inferred.

The separate judgment components are: (R1) §223(a)(1)(B)'s indecency transmission offense and §223(a)(2)'s associated knowing, intentional facility permission; (R2) §223(d)(1)(A)'s sending to a specified minor or minors and §223(d)(2)'s corresponding facility permission; (R3) §223(d)(1)(B)'s display available to minors and the corresponding §223(d)(2) liability; and (R4) severability and the retained scope of relief. R1 requires knowing making, creation or solicitation and initiation by a telecommunications device, with knowledge that the recipient is under eighteen. R2 and R3 involve knowing use of an interactive computer service and contextual depictions or descriptions of sexual or excretory activities or organs patently offensive under contemporary community standards. R1's device definition excludes an interactive computer service; the provisions cannot be collapsed into one generic Internet offense. The text applies regardless of who placed the call or initiated the communication. The facility offenses require both knowing permission and intent that the facility be used for prohibited activity. The challenged crimes permit a fine, imprisonment up to two years, or both.

The defense is not strict liability for mere carriage. Section (e)(1) protects access or connection to a facility, system or network outside the person's control, including incidental transmission, downloading, intermediate storage and access software, without content creation. Sections (e)(2) and (3) withhold that defense respectively from a conspirator actively involved with creation or knowing distribution, a knowing advertiser of the prohibited communications, and an access provider owning or controlling the offending network. Under (e)(4), employer liability requires within-scope conduct plus knowledgeable authorization or ratification, or reckless disregard. Section (e)(5), applying to (a)(1)(B), (d), and the corresponding (a)(2) activity, separately protects good-faith reasonable, effective and appropriate access-restriction measures under the circumstances, including feasible available technology, OR restricted access using a verified credit card, debit account, adult access code or adult PIN. A speaker need not satisfy both alternatives.

Under (e)(6) the FCC may describe suitable measures for (d), but may not enforce their use, approve or sanction them, or endorse specific products; described measures are evidence of good faith, not an exclusive license. Interactive computer services do not thereby become common or telecommunications carriers. Section (f)(1) protects otherwise lawful good-faith implementation of defenses or access restrictions; (f)(2) preserves the stated complementary intrastate and uncovered-conduct powers while barring inconsistent liability within its named commercial-entity, library and higher-education scope. Section (g) preserves other federal law. None of those savings terms narrows the challenged content categories to obscenity or cures their impact on speakers creating protected content.

### Issue-specific alternatives

**Path A: affirm the challenged injunction in full.** The criminal scheme selects protected content, suppresses lawful adult communication and demands impracticable age separation from many small or noncommercial speakers. The context-sensitive terms and knowledge requirements do not supply the missing fit. Sable distinguishes a compelling child-protection purpose from adequate means; ACT/Pacifica preserves adult speech and independent necessity review even in broadcasting; Denver's controlling compulsory-scheme holding addresses actual deterrence and access burdens. Internet communication is not justified by broadcast scarcity or unavoidable household intrusion. Turner and the March 1997 application concern content-neutral carriage, not permission to defer away a content-selected criminal burden.

The strongest objection is that targeted sending to known minors is textually distinct from public display and can operate without excluding any adult. The full-injunction Justices conclude that the actual offenses and defenses remain substantially overbroad as enacted: the transmission provisions reach communications with adult as well as minor recipients; the statutory categories do not adopt Ginsberg's variable-obscenity limits, including its serious-value protection; uncertainty backed by criminal penalties deters protected sexual, health, artistic and political discussion. The Court can preserve obscenity by excising the distinct indecency category, but cannot legislate a new minors-only, harmful-to-minors offense or an effective age-verification system. This is a judgment about substantial protected reach, not a claim that every conceivable application is unconstitutional.

**Path B: retain a separable direct-to-minor core.** O'Connor and Thomas distinguish a communication confined to one or more particular recipients known to be minors from mixed-age conversation and unrestricted display. Government may protect minors through the former restriction without reducing any adult's access. Ginsberg supplies support for age-differentiated restrictions; the intact good-faith and verified-access defenses are supplementary safeguards, not a substitute for evaluating the audience. The strongest objection is that the indecency definitions are broader than harmful-to-minors obscenity and that the Court would rewrite the Act. They answer only at the severability/remedial level: leave a valid direct-recipient application enforceable while barring the adult-burdening reach, and do not add new elements or change the statutory definition. They do not hold all protected speech to minors regulable, settle every minor's access claim, or reject a specific vagueness challenge to a later prosecution. Their disagreement is the demonstrated facial remedy's reach, not permission for general Internet censorship.

Neither path treats parental controls as already universal, free, perfectly effective or technologically implemented. The record supports the practical shortcomings of the enacted defenses for many speakers, not the impossibility of every future feasible age screen. No Justice here uses a public-forum designation as the necessary premise.

## Vacco v. Quill — No. 95-1858

### Enhanced comparison and common narrow proposition

All eight Associates vote to reverse the Second Circuit's equal-protection judgment and remand consistently with rejection of the asserted unequal-treatment theory. The physician controversy is live on the packet; the initial patients' deaths are not converted into total mootness. No Justice obtains a vote from same-day Glucksberg.

**Path A, accepted at least at the bounded proposition below:** New York may distinguish a patient's rejection of unwanted bodily intervention from a physician's intentional assistance in bringing about death. All competent persons have the same legally stated treatment-refusal option, and none has an entitlement under this classification to intentional suicide assistance. The patient comparison remains necessary: the fact that one dying patient is dependent on life support and another is not produces a different practical opportunity to hasten death. That difference is rationally connected to protecting bodily consent while restricting lethal assistance, reducing risks of mistake or pressure, preserving life and maintaining professional responsibilities. Equal protection does not compel elimination of one recognized liberty merely to equalize every practical consequence, or extension of its scope to every act producing death.

**Path B, rejected on this record but retained as an argument:** Competent terminally ill patients may seek the same end and physicians may anticipate death from either withdrawal or medication; therefore differential availability of assistance can look arbitrary. Under actual Heller and Romer, calling the categories conduct-based and reciting a legitimate purpose are insufficient without explaining their connection to the actual disadvantage. The answer is not a universally true physiological distinction. Consent to continued intervention is independently material, and law can distinguish an intended lethal act from cessation of unwanted intervention or good-faith pain relief despite foreseeable death. The risks attaching to a requested new lethal intervention are a rational reason to retain that distinction. The equality theory presented does not establish that patients in a concrete exceptional application have no legally material difference, nor prove that permitted palliation is covertly forbidden. Narrower regulation is possible; ordinary rational-basis review does not require its adoption.

The sufficient proposition is ordinary rationality of this treatment-refusal/intentional-assistance distinction in the presented claim. It does not depend on declaring all patients alike or unlike for every purpose, discovering animus, making intent conclusive about every medical act, finding every withdrawal unintended, denying the constitutional significance of bodily integrity, or declaring no future end-of-life application can fail. No suspect classification or independently established fundamental-right entitlement triggers heightened equality review here. Cruzan's assumed competent-refusal premise does not itself create a right to lethal assistance. Casey protects its defined reproductive liberty and does not constitutionalize every physician-assisted choice. Beach's permissible generalization operates with Heller's actual-disadvantage requirement and Romer's ordinary rationality constraint.

## Washington v. Glucksberg — No. 96-110

### Enhanced comparison: asserted liberty, sufficient ground and alternatives

All eight Associates vote to reverse the Ninth Circuit's recognition of the asserted right and resulting invalidation. This does not mean that all eight adopt a single general substantive-due-process methodology. The claim concerns willing physicians prescribing lethal medicine for competent terminally ill adults who seek to end their lives. It neither demands compelled service or public financing nor is limited to withdrawing unwanted bodily intervention. Describing it accurately preserves both its terminal-illness setting and the additional affirmative lethal assistance sought.

**Path A: no protected fundamental entitlement to the asserted assistance.** The preexisting bodily-integrity sources and actual Casey do not establish that entitlement. The longstanding legal distinction between refusal and assisting suicide, and public authority to prevent error, coercion and abusive intervention, weigh against recognition. At the presented scope the prohibition rationally serves preservation of life, protection of vulnerable people and maintenance of medical responsibilities. Terminal illness and an asserted present wish do not by themselves eliminate diagnostic error, pressure, impaired judgment or the difficulty of policing a legally authorized lethal practice. These are reasons for preventive regulation, not findings that any named claimant lacks capacity. The constitutional conclusion does not require proof that every risk materializes in every case.

The strongest counterargument is that tradition formulated around suicide may fail to address a competent dying person's liberty to govern an unavoidable death with a willing physician, and that preserving life abstractly can disregard the person. The response is specific: refusal protects against unwanted bodily intervention; the present claim adds permission for another's intended lethal act. Casey's supported reproductive choice remains controlling in its domain but does not supply an unrestricted autonomy rule. The State's protection of patients and legitimate medical boundaries remains concrete despite the limited class proposed. No compelled treatment or denial of lawful palliation is being approved.

**Path B: reasoned evaluation of a substantial liberty interest without making historical prohibition dispositive.** Souter, Stevens and Breyer insist that the constitutional interest must be evaluated in its real medical and personal setting; Ginsburg shares the refusal to foreclose all narrower cases. The strongest argument for invalidation is a demonstrated voluntary, informed and persistent decision in an extreme terminal condition where the asserted protective reasons do not justify the prohibition. That possible individual application differs from the general terminally-ill class entitlement adjudicated here; rejecting the latter rests on the explained protective grounds, without inferring that unreproduced patient evidence is absent from the Court's file. Actual Casey protects medical judgment within an established choice, not every proposed new procedure. On this presentation, serious protective risks and the uncertain effects of the requested rule justify rejecting the lower court's classwide constitutional entitlement. These Justices therefore reverse without endorsing a categorical rule that history forecloses every liberty claim or every application.

**Sustaining path — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** These four uphold §922(s)(2) and affirm the constitutional judgment in both dockets. Congress directly governs an identified commercial transaction and requires a limited information task reasonably connected to that rule. It does not require a State to legislate, adopt federal policy as State policy, take private property or liabilities, establish a general regulatory regime, arrest, prosecute, or send adverse notices. The command is compulsory, but its defined task, available-information basis, reasonable-effort limitation and retained policy/enforcement choices distinguish it from New York's transfer of regulatory or ownership responsibility. Garcia, Baker and the cooperative arrangements retained by New York rebut a categorical exemption for every task performed by State personnel. Those authorities do not independently decide this precise task; the conclusion is a confined application of commerce power and necessary implementing authority.

The added fiscal objections make it necessary to reject any premise that the assistance is free, State-authorized, or harmless merely because temporary. They do not make the task a compulsory appropriation of a specified amount or prove the claimed personal liability. Reasonable effort permits account to be taken of available resources; it is not read to authorize no effort whenever the State has omitted a budget line. Nor may Congress obtain unlimited administration by calling it a small burden. The affirmative ground remains the defined informational relationship to direct federal transaction regulation and the responsibilities left with the State. If the command is constitutionally valid, a contrary State prohibition cannot alone defeat it under the Supremacy Clause. The Court need not, and these commitments do not, construe the two States' particular laws or decide that an unidentified liability is preempted in every application. Section (7) supplies no blanket immunity for all expenditures. Neither payment nor a compensation remedy is made a condition of these four Justices' merits votes.

### T3. Conditional information duties must be separated

**T3B — §922(s)(6)(B).** The relevant officer is one to whom a purchaser statement is transmitted under §922(s)(1)(A)(i)(IV), a trigger distinct from the notice in §922(s)(2). Unless that officer determines that the transaction would violate Federal, State or local law, the officer must destroy the statement, any record containing information derived from it, and any record created as a result of the required notice within twenty business days after the date the purchaser made the statement. Under the same statutory exception, the information may be conveyed only to a person needing it to carry out the subsection and may be used only to carry out the subsection. The destruction clock does not begin upon an officer's election to participate. A nonparticipating officer could receive a dealer's required statement; neither receipt nor nonreceipt is established by the general nonparticipation posture. Affirmative record destruction and restrictions on use/disclosure are different duties even though contained in one subparagraph.

**T3C — §922(s)(6)(C).** Written reasons are due only if the officer determines that an individual is ineligible to receive a handgun and the individual requests the reasons. They are due within twenty business days after receipt of that request. Neither receipt of a statement nor objection to the program establishes either predicate. There is no unconditional duty to make an adverse determination, send an adverse notice, or furnish explanations to every purchaser.

## 2. United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder

**Identity and posture:** Review of the Ninth Circuit's judgment at 95 F.3d 787, lower appellate Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513, reversing separate indictment dismissals and returning both prosecutions. ### Components and sufficient proposed grounds

**M1 — Actual offense and common constitutional question.** Section 228(a), as enacted October 25, 1992, punishes willful failure to pay a past-due support obligation with respect to a child residing in another State. Section 228(d)(1) requires an amount fixed by a court order or State-law administrative process for support of a child, or of a child and the custodial parent, which has remained unpaid longer than one year **or** exceeds $5,000. Those are alternatives, although these indictments allege both. Willfulness and the different-state condition remain elements; the word debt, the amount, or the duration alone is not a constitutional substitute for the required interstate relation.

Both January 11, 1995 indictments are included. Mussari's concerns two Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning November 1, 1988; Schroeder's concerns four Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning February 1, 1992. The Court need not resolve the one-day discrepancy in the sources describing the initial District Court dismissal to review the identified appellate judgment. It finds no ability to pay, deliberate evasion, actual interstate travel, flight, guilt, specific arrears balance, or defect in a State support order. Constitutional review proceeds on the charged class and preserved question, leaving statutory proof and lawful defenses to each prosecution.

**M2 — Direct protection of these interstate payment obligations.** All eight Associates would sustain the statute against the presented Commerce Clause objection and affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of each constitutional dismissal. Their common sufficient ground is confined to the legally fixed private payment obligation for support of children residing across State lines. Section 228 addresses willful nonpayment of that cross-border economic obligation itself. It does not seek federal power through a chain from local family conflict to poverty, welfare expenditures, crime, or national productivity. Its different-state condition does actual work in delimiting the cross-border payment relation. A transfer required by an existing support obligation does not cease to be an economic payment because State family law created it rather than a commercial bargain.

Lopez, April 26, 1995, rejects a remote effects chain for stand-alone school-zone possession and forbids inserting a missing commerce element. It does not exclude every regulation touching family life or every payment lacking a negotiated exchange. Harris, April 27, 1995, and Bishop/Stokes, February 5, 1996, sustain their actual economic vehicle-taking class while requiring the enacted vehicle element; Hale, February 12, 1996, sustains possession integral to direct market regulation. They establish a need to identify the actual regulated object and relation, not a rule that every thing with interstate history or every debt is automatically commerce. Here the object is the due cross-border payment obligation, not a locally possessed object with remote travel or hypothetical downstream economic effects.

The common ground permits protection of the specified interstate payment obligation without finding an actual completed transfer. Withholding the required payment is the conduct Congress addresses; requiring that the money already have crossed State lines would not follow from this offense. The Court adds no flight, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire, particular payment-instrument, or motive-to-evade element. It neither constitutionalizes all unpaid family obligations nor establishes federal power to create or revise the underlying support duty. The different-state condition is necessary to this rationale; no sufficiency judgment is made about a statute lacking it or about an unrelated debt with an incidental geographical feature. The opinion should not adopt an unqualified formula that every obligation is a thing in interstate commerce merely because it is called a debt.

**M3 — Private regulation, State law, and domestic relations.** All eight reject a separate Tenth Amendment or categorical domestic-relations ground for these dismissals. The federal statute directs the private obligor to satisfy an existing obligation; it does not command a State to legislate, enforce the debt, investigate for federal officers, alter an order, or assume the obligor's liability. New York preserves direct federal regulation within enumerated power while forbidding the compulsory State responsibility it addressed. Printz's qualified executive-assistance rule is neither needed to authorize this private duty nor a reason to invent a State fiscal issue.

Ankenbrandt, June 15, 1992, confines the statutory diversity exception to issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees and rejects a general Article III domestic-relations exclusion. This federal criminal prosecution issues no such decree and treats the existing support obligation as a predicate. Its federal character must still rest on the sufficient commerce ground in M2; merely showing an absence of commandeering does not establish enumerated power. Conversely, the State-law origin of the obligation does not defeat direct federal enforcement within that power. The Court does not reopen custody, recalculate support, adjudicate a modification, prescribe future State enforcement machinery, or resolve a hypothetical interference with a different State proceeding. A lawful defense bearing on an actual statutory predicate remains available.

**M4 — Temporal limit, distinct prosecution proof, and restitution reservation.** Both alleged periods begin before enactment. Field's scope is retained. Subsection 523(a)(2)(A) excludes a statement respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition. **C2 — The nondischargeable amount is the valid liability for the fraudulent acquisition.** All eight would hold that when money or property is obtained by the fraud within §523(a)(2)(A), the qualifying debt includes the legally enforceable liability arising from that fraudulent acquisition, rather than only the net value the debtor received. The Code defines debt through liability on a claim. The phrase concerning the extent obtained by fraud identifies the fraudulent acquisition giving rise to the debt and separates it from lawful acquisitions; it does not impose a restitution-only ceiling on the consequences of that acquisition.

This reading gives work to both debt and obtained by. There must be a qualifying acquisition of money or property and a sufficient connection between that acquisition and the liability declared nondischargeable. It does not make every obligation of a dishonest debtor nondischargeable. Here the identified overcharged rents, not all lawful rent or every landlord obligation, supply that acquisition. An enforceable enhancement imposed for those fraudulent receipts is a liability for them even though the debtor did not separately obtain the dollars represented by the enhancement. State law determines the valid liability; federal law determines whether it falls within this discharge category. The Court creates neither a new State cause nor a new federal damages remedy.

Field deliberately left debt extent and the obtained-by relationship separate. It therefore supplies the fraud prerequisite, not an already settled result here. Noland, May 13, 1996, requires observance of the Code's actual categories rather than a judicial preference about punitive obligations; it concerns priority and equitable subordination and does not itself settle §523(a)(2)(A). Anderson's lower separate writing identifies the especially broad workplace authority and ineffective supervision as sufficient without deciding every supervisor's minimum authority; that reasoning illustrates this lawful application but supplies no Supreme Court precedent. Barkett's overlapping authority-aid ground is separable from her broader scope and notice theories.

### B. Focused vacatur and remand is lawful if the findings are held inadequate, but is not selected

A lawful competing disposition would vacate the employer judgment and return only the unresolved actual-material-aid determination under Andrade, with the supported historical findings reviewed under the ordinary standard and any truly necessary further proceedings determined below. It could not simply order a new trial or demand proof of an express threat. Its strongest support is Andrade's refusal automatically to restore a Rule 50 damages verdict when attribution, instructions, preserved grounds and sufficiency remained unresolved, together with Meritor's remand on insufficient findings.

The distinction here is that the relevant supervisory powers, their daily exercise, workplace structure, policy failure and agency determination emerged from a completed bench trial. Meritor had unresolved harassment and unwelcomeness questions; Andrade had a categorical notice bar and unresolved verdict/instruction issues. Neither establishes a general rule requiring factfinding again whenever an appellate court corrects its agency analysis. The ordinary restoration path is supported without curing the separate constructive-notice reasoning. No additional preserved independent employer-liability objection requiring a new proceeding is identified in the validated packet. An identified material omitted fact would require a focused source return, but a possible fact that might exist supplies no present reason for exploratory remand.

### C. Affirmance for the City is not selected

The City's strongest surviving case combines the personal nature of the acts, the absence of personnel consequences tied to advances, remoteness from City Hall, informal rather than designated complaints to Gordon, and prompt discipline after Ewanchew's letter. That account may defeat particular scope, notice or negligent-response theories. It does not defeat the independently supported actual-authority route. Treating either higher-management ignorance or prompt correction after eventual notice as conclusive would restore the categorical restriction Andrade rejected. The City remains entitled to the material-aid limitation; it is the proved workplace relationship, not a repudiation of that limitation, that defeats affirmance.

### D. Question 3 and the policy question

Three legally distinct options were examined. A complete care-and-nonuse defense could be considered in a case of proved effective prevention, a usable complaint route, reasonable correction and demonstrated unreasonable nonuse; Meritor's attention to effective procedures supports considering that possibility, but neither Meritor nor Andrade supplies its complete rule, burdens or exceptions. A narrower rule could leave established authority-aid liability intact and account for avoidable injury in the legally available remedy. Stevens's Meritor concurrence, joining Marshall, directly supports that distinction. A third option resolves the present policy contention and reserves whether a complete defense exists on materially different facts.

The common selected position is the third. A policy not communicated to the relevant workforce does not demonstrate an accessible preventive or complaint system, and failure to invoke that unknown route cannot by itself constitute unreasonable nonuse. Eventual correction after another employee's letter does not establish that the City had supplied effective prevention or an available route during the existing abuse. The City therefore cannot defeat this judgment by combining its paper policy, Faragher's failure to complain to distant higher management and its eventual response. No finding about the policy's unprovided exact text, an undisclosed promise, actual fear of retaliation or a particular employee's understanding is necessary.

**Strongest argument for deciding a complete defense now:** The grant expressly asks about it; a general answer could clarify the incentives and limits of supervisor attribution and avoid treating reasonable employers as insurers.

**Answer and operative limit:** The proper agency connection already limits attribution. This City's policy failure makes a complete defense based on effective precautions unavailable on the facts even if such a defense can exist elsewhere. A new burden-shifting formula or tangible-action exception would not perform necessary work here and would decide conditions the case does not present. Reservation is supported by that absence, not by a general preference for narrow opinions. Stevens additionally records the broader position below; the other seven do not adopt it as a general rule.

## 3. Shared issue analysis and competing lawful paths

### A. Actual authority, managerial rank and the instruction

**Adopted path.** Andrade, February 26, 1997, supplies a plaintiff-proved connection between actual delegated power and the actionable environment. Oncale, March 19, preserves attribution separately from sex causation and the Harris threshold. Faragher Part I, September 8, applies that rule while preserving its limits. Each Associate joined all three relevant propositions; Faragher's eight-Associate application demonstrates that daily operational power can matter without a completed personnel act. It does not establish what happened in Harrison.

Here Brown's work assignment power is not simply a title-based inference. The lower opinion, Appendix A at 3a–6a and 27a, reports evidence of orders taking Harrison from her station or buddy into isolated areas, successive work-related excursions followed by alleged sexual misconduct, disciplinary participation and a work-through-lunch order following a refusal. This evidence permits a finding of material aid and requires a legally accurate opportunity to decide that route. It remains evidence, not a new Supreme Court finding that every incident occurred or every element was proved.

The principal attribution charge, Appendix A at 24a–27a, demanded all of its listed agency, apparent-authority and reliance elements. The accompanying definition required managerial control amounting to alter-ego identity. The apparent-authority charge, 28a–31a, again conditioned liability on authority to commit the challenged conduct. Together they foreclosed the separate route even if the jury believed that actual assignment power materially aided the abuse. The phrase addressing authority otherwise than apparently did not supply a material-aid instruction or remove the cumulative requirements.

**Strongest contrary path: preserve the charge.** Its reference to control over employment conditions could encompass a shift foreman; its reference to authority beyond apparent authority might be read broadly; refusing the employee's proposed automatic-supervisor instructions was proper. Those are genuine points against treating every disputed word as error. They do not cure the entire charge: managerial control was expressly tied to alter-ego identity, apparent authority and reliance remained mandatory, and the instructions never asked whether actual delegated power materially aided the environment. The record distinguishes refusal of an inaccurate proposed sentence from refusal of any accurate instruction on the supported theory. Requested instruction 7 contained both a potentially valid aid theory and an invalid status-alone theory; instruction 24 raised aid, while instruction 25 proposed status-based responsibility. No commitment requires the court to give any proposal verbatim.

**Other broader path: impose automatic responsibility from supervisory status.** Rejected by Meritor and the three current-term decisions. Removing an alter-ego prerequisite does not turn every supervisor into the employer for every personal act. Nor does Brown's conceded employment establish the material-aid connection. This path cannot support either a rule or a directed employer judgment.

### B. Whole-charge prejudice and apparent authority

**Adopted path.** The error was prejudicial on this record. The principal instruction was given over objection; the employee requested the omitted route; the appellate court expressly considered the charge as a whole and found the route viable on the evidence, Appendix A at 24a–34a. Daily-control evidence coupled with lack of top-management status made the incorrect barrier consequential. The work-order evidence gave the omitted route a concrete basis, independent of belief that harassment was permitted. No supplied special verdict establishes that the jury necessarily rejected an untainted element independently of attribution.

**Strongest contrary path: error but harmlessness.** A jury could have rejected an actionable environment or discriminatory causation, and the separate verdict against Brown does not establish those statutory elements against Potash. Some proposed instructions were overbroad, while the employer had a policy and responded after notice. Those propositions prevent automatic liability but do not show that this jury's employer verdict rested on an independent, correctly decided ground. A possible lawful explanation for a general verdict cannot cure a charge that excluded a supported liability route. Policy knowledge after some incidents and the later response likewise do not establish that actual authority supplied no material aid. The commitment is a record-specific finding of prejudicial instructional error, not a rule that every instruction error requires reversal or that prejudice is presumed whenever a general verdict is returned.

**Narrower lawful option.** Affirm on the independently sufficient actual-authority omission without deciding whether a correctly instructed jury could find apparent authority. Each Associate accepts that limit. Restatement §27, reproduced at 30a, supports the modest clarification that the employee's reasonable belief must arise from the principal's words or conduct. Faragher did not decide apparent authority; its separate observations cannot supply a Court holding. There is no need to adopt the lower opinion's entire apparent-authority framework, its characterization of apparent permission to harass, or its suggestion that unknown policy by itself supplies sufficient apparent authority. Nor is there a need to extend Faragher's actual-delegation holding to every claimed apparent work power.

### C. Prevention, reporting and response

**Adopted path.** Preserve the distinction among actual authority, apparent authority, negligence and remedy. A policy can affect reasonable belief or employer care when the required facts are present. Prompt investigation and correction can bear on response and relief; they do not retroactively remove earlier actual aid. The account reports that Harrison learned of a posted policy after some incidents and that it was absent from her new-hire materials. It does not establish universal ignorance, no available official, unreasonable nonuse, a particular policy promise, or the legal inadequacy of every later step. The employer's belief that Brown perceived consent does not turn its finding of unwilling participation into a judicial finding that conduct was welcome.

**Strongest contrary path: complete defense from policy and response.** Faragher expressly left open a possible defense involving genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse. Harrison's eventual knowledge and Potash's remedial measures distinguish its facts from Faragher's undistributed policy. That difference permits attention to the actual evidence in appropriate inquiries; it does not establish a complete defense, its governing elements or its prerequisites here. The charge was not an accurate, separately justified instruction on a recognized defense. The reservation neither expands this grant and preservation nor supplies an appellate finding against Harrison. All eight decline to adopt a new complete defense or reject every possible such defense in deciding this instructional appeal.

### D. Independent statutory rights and the actual contract record

**Adopted path.** Gardner-Denver, 415 U.S. 36 (1974), preserves the distinction between contractual grievance rights and the independent Title VII right. Gilmer, 500 U.S. 20 (1991), enforced an actual agreement to arbitrate the statutory ADEA claim and distinguished the earlier contractual-arbitration cases. First Options, May 22, 1995, requires courts to determine the antecedent agreement and delegation rather than presume them from an assertion of arbitral power. Its scope is not expanded into a new rule about union representation.

The decisive record limitation is a litigation fact reported in Appendix A at 35a n.1 and 40a n.2: Potash supplied neither the CBA nor the district court's order on exhaustion in the appellate record, and its appellate brief did not identify an agreement to arbitrate statutory claims. That prevents the employer from establishing the statutory obligation it needs for reversal. It is not a finding that no contract ever existed, a fabricated waiver, or an inference from the operator's inability to retrieve a document. Membership in a union and the asserted existence of a grievance procedure do not establish statutory scope or delegation. No disputed contractual interpretation is necessary to decide whether sex-based conduct and delegated work power meet Title VII's elements.

**Strongest contrary path: enforce the contractual procedure.** Gilmer rejects a categorical objection to arbitration of statutory employment claims, and a proven agreement can create enforceable duties without extinguishing substantive rights. A clause could, in another case, present the question of an express collective statutory-arbitration obligation. This path fails as a basis for Potash's requested dismissal because no such clause or delegation is established in the actual appellate record. It cannot be supplied by inventing terms or presuming that a generic grievance process governs statutory claims.

**Broader alternative and limits.** A categorical ruling that no CBA may ever commit statutory claims to arbitration would exceed what is necessary here. A categorical FAA exclusion for every employment or collective agreement likewise does not follow from Gilmer, which reserved the general employment-exclusion question.
