## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate both judgments and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The three-judge court shall keep Section 5 effect separate from Section 2, shall not exclude evidence from the purpose inquiry merely because it also supports a dilution theory, and shall determine on the existing record and any lawful supplementation whether the Board adopted the plan with a purpose to deny or abridge the right to vote on account of race. It shall apply the rule that this purpose is not limited to an intent to worsen minority voting strength compared with the existing plan. The Court does not decide whether the Board acted with prohibited purpose and does not create a same-decision burden or defense.

### Issue 1: Relationship between Section 5 effect and Section 2

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal relationship between the statutes but not whether the plan violates Section 2.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 5 effect remains a comparison with the lawful operative baseline. A Section 2 violation does not independently establish prohibited Section 5 effect, and preclearance does not immunize a plan from a separate Section 2 action.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Section 2 violation does not itself establish prohibited Section 5 effect, which remains a comparison with the lawful operative baseline.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Sections 2 and 5 protect distinct statutory injuries through different benchmarks and procedures. Treating Section 2 liability as automatic Section 5 effect would erase that enacted distinction.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require proportional representation or maximization and does not decide a separate Section 2 action.

### Issue 2: Relevance of dilution evidence to discriminatory purpose

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the evidentiary error necessary to the disposition. The scope of the Section 5 purpose prong is decided separately in Issue 3, and any mixed-purpose causation or same-decision rule is reserved.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether evidence may be excluded from the Section 5 purpose inquiry solely because it also supports a Section 2 dilution theory.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Evidence of dilutive impact, rejected alternatives, sequence, procedural departures, and historical context may be relevant to why a jurisdiction selected a plan. It may not be categorically excluded merely because it also bears on Section 2. That conclusion does not establish prohibited purpose or alter the governing burdens and causation rules.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Evidence relevant to dilution may also be relevant to discriminatory purpose and may not be categorically excluded merely because it supports a Section 2 theory.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The court below treated the statutory labels as mutually exclusive evidentiary compartments. The same facts can bear on different legal questions without transferring the conclusion from one doctrine to another. Because the exclusion may have distorted the purpose inquiry, the preclearance judgment cannot stand.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not decide whether the plan was adopted with prohibited purpose or create an identical-plan same-decision defense or burden.

### Issue 3: The scope of the Section 5 purpose prong

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the scope of the purpose prong because it is within the question granted and because the remand cannot be given a legal standard without it. The three-judge court must know what kind of purpose it is looking for. Stone decides only that scope, not whether this Board acted with such a purpose.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Section 5's bar on a purpose to deny or abridge the right to vote on account of race is limited to a purpose to cause retrogression.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The text controls. Section 5 forbids preclearance of a change that has the purpose or will have the effect of denying or abridging the right to vote on account of race. The statute does not say a purpose to retrogress. Beer defines effect as retrogression against a benchmark. Purpose asks why the jurisdiction acted, and Stone reads the enacted prohibition as reaching a purpose to deny or abridge the vote on account of race even when that purpose does not include worsening the benchmark. The Court has treated discriminatory purpose as a requirement separate from retrogression. Miller preserves that distinction but does not itself decide the full scope of nonretrogressive-purpose liability. Stone's proposed construction prevents a jurisdiction from intentionally denying or abridging equal voting opportunity on account of race merely because it does not intend to make an existing arrangement worse. This construes the open statutory question and overrules nothing; it does not equate every consideration of race with prohibited purpose.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Section 5 bar on a purpose to deny or abridge the right to vote on account of race is not limited to a purpose to cause retrogression. A covered jurisdiction's adoption of a nonretrogressive plan with a discriminatory purpose cannot be precleared. Beer remains the rule for effect.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The Board adopted the Police Jury's recently precleared plan with no majority-Black district, and the parties stipulate that it is not retrogressive. Nonretrogressive effect does not rule out an unsuccessful intent to cause retrogression. Stone's broader construction also reaches a proved purpose to deny or abridge voting on account of race that does not include an intent to worsen the existing benchmark; stipulated nonretrogression therefore cannot resolve that separate purpose inquiry. On remand the three-judge court should weigh the evidence the lower court excluded, including the dilutive effect, the rejected alternative plan with two majority-Black districts, the sequence of decisions, departures from ordinary practice, and the history of discrimination in the parish. None of it is presumptive proof, and the court must find the Board's actual purpose. The existing allocation of the preclearance burden is not altered.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require maximization or proportional representation and does not treat a rejected feasible alternative as proof of purpose. It leaves Beer's rule for effect untouched and Section 2 independent. It does not decide a same-decision rule or any shift in the burden, and it does not decide whether this plan was adopted with a discriminatory purpose.

### Issue 1: Retrieval of stored pager data incident to arrest

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether activating the pager was a search and whether the search-incident exception justified the retrieval. The lawful arrest and physical seizure of the pager do not themselves resolve access to its stored content.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether pressing a control to reveal stored pager messages is a search distinct from seizure and whether immediate warrantless retrieval is justified by a concrete evidence-preservation need.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Activating the pager to expose numerical messages stored in its memory reveals concealed content and is a Fourth Amendment search distinct from the physical seizure. Search incident to arrest does not categorically open secured electronic memory. Its evidence-preservation rationale permits immediate retrieval limited to volatile data only when the known operation of the particular device and the circumstances establish a concrete, imminent risk that incoming transmissions will overwrite evidence before a warrant can reasonably be obtained and the information cannot be preserved without viewing it. General propositions about finite pager memory and what some pagers may do do not satisfy that showing.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Activating a pager to retrieve stored messages is a search distinct from seizure of the device. Incident to a lawful arrest, officers may make an immediate retrieval limited to volatile data when the particular device and circumstances establish a concrete overwrite risk before a warrant can reasonably be obtained and no nonsearch means will preserve the data. The general observations offered here do not satisfy that showing.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Seizure restrained Ortiz's possession and preserved the device; pressing the control disclosed information that was not exposed to observation. Robinson and Belton protect officer safety and prevent destruction of evidence, but their categorical physical-container treatment cannot be extended beyond those justifications to secured electronic memory. The record did not establish this pager's storage capacity or overwrite behavior, an imminent incoming page, or the inability to preserve the data by leaving the device secured while seeking a warrant. The Government therefore failed to establish the preservation necessity it invoked.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize exploratory review, delayed examination after an overwrite risk ends, access to unrelated or nonvolatile data, or a warrantless search based only on the generic possibility that electronic information can be altered. When officers can preserve the information without inspecting it, the warrant requirement governs.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved device-specific preservation exception and rejection of categorical memory access are proposed applications. They are not later digital-device holdings imported backward. His remand for preserved admission grounds and harmlessness respects Pinson and Wellons’s refusal to convert recognition of a search into automatic exclusion.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1 — preserved approved Ortiz answer.** Stone would retain the pager-search reversal and remand for the proper evidentiary consequences. General overwrite possibilities do not prove this device presented an imminent loss risk with no nonsearch preservation method; physical seizure and access to its stored content remain distinct.

**Questions 2–3 — new companion answers, proposed to the Court and user-approved.** Stone would affirm the Seventh Circuit’s vacatur of Hurtado’s sentence and its resentencing direction under §2J1.5 on this record. The enacted guideline chain calls for an actual best analogy, not an automatic obstruction/accessory classification whenever testimony is withheld. The limited testimony assisted identification, no purpose to aid acquittal was established, and aversion to informing does not itself establish accessory-after-the-fact conduct. The material-witness analogy therefore better captures the conduct found. Conduct, proved purpose and effect matter to selecting the analogy; actual obstruction, where established on different facts, may support a different guideline. He would leave lawful adjustments and the resulting sentence for resentencing, disturb no contempt conviction, and invent no fixed replacement term.

**Combined proposed disposition.** Reverse and remand as to Ortiz under his preserved approved search position; affirm the appellate vacatur/remand of Hurtado’s sentence under the expressly user-approved companion proposal. The two appeals remain distinct within one consolidated matter.

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The discriminatory residency condition may not be enforced against the camp. The Maine courts shall provide the exemption and any refund or prospective relief authorized by state remedial law. Maine may replace the scheme with a nondiscriminatory charitable policy.

### Issue 1: Residency-based charitable tax exemption

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial operation of the residency condition. The camp's national recruitment and predominantly out-of-state clientele are undisputed.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the existing nondiscrimination rule. Maine conditions a valuable tax exemption on serving residents and withdraws it when the same charitable service is sold principally to nonresidents. That classification disadvantages interstate customers and the provider that attracts them. The State may support charity, but it can do so through resident benefits, direct public programs, general-revenue grants, or neutral exemption criteria.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Maine's charitable property-tax exemption violates the dormant Commerce Clause because it denies exemption based on the nonresident identity of the institution's principal beneficiaries and thereby burdens interstate commerce in charitable and recreational services.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The camp markets a service nationally and brings children across state lines. The tax rule raises the cost of that service precisely because the users are nonresidents. Calling the exemption a subsidy does not change the discriminatory condition attached to the tax system. Maine is not purchasing a service as a market participant, and it has not shown that resident assistance requires penalizing a charity serving outsiders. Reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives are evident.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require a State to subsidize charitable work everywhere, invalidate direct resident public benefits, or prevent neutral exemption criteria based on nonprofit status, use, finances, or regulatory compliance. It forbids conditioning a tax exemption on the out-of-state identity of the persons served.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved result rejects enforcement of the residency condition. His instruction to provide the exemption should be understood as the selected proposed remedy on the presented state-law scheme, not a general federal rule that every discriminatory exemption must be extended. Any refund still requires the proper state-law and due-process analysis; no amount is invented.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The Secretary possessed statutory appointment authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory authority first because an unauthorized appointment would resolve the case without constitutional classification.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress authorized the Secretary of Transportation to appoint civilian judges of the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals and whether those judges are inferior rather than principal officers under the Appointments Clause.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret § 323(a) and the UCMJ together. This is ordinary statutory construction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 323(a) authorizes the Secretary of Transportation, as head of the department, to appoint civilian officers serving as judges on the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals, while the UCMJ separately governs their assignment and judicial duties.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Article 66 expressly permits civilian judges, and § 323(a) supplies the department head’s appointment authority in general terms broad enough to include officers serving within the Department. Assignment by the Judge Advocate General is not itself appointment, but the Secretary’s formal act cured that distinction. No text requires a separate office-specific appointment clause.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every general personnel statute authorizes appointment of officers or that an official other than a constitutionally recognized department head may make the appointment.

### Issue 2: The judges are inferior officers

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches constitutional status because the validity of appointment depends on whether Congress could vest appointment in the Secretary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress authorized the Secretary of Transportation to appoint civilian judges of the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals and whether those judges are inferior rather than principal officers under the Appointments Clause.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the Appointments Clause through the specific institutional structure. Supervision and constrained final authority, rather than the importance of the work alone, establish inferior status here.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Civilian judges of the Coast Guard Court of Criminal Appeals are inferior officers because the Judge Advocate General supervises their assignments and administration and the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces reviews their decisions; they cannot render final decisions on behalf of the United States free of superior executive control.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Clause protects accountability in appointment. These judges perform significant governmental duties and are officers, but they operate below principal officers who control the court’s administration, may remove them from assignments, prescribe rules, and review qualifying judgments. Their substantial adjudicatory discretion does not erase the hierarchical relationship. Simulated Ryder therefore remains fully intact but is not activated because the appointment route was lawful.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether appellate review alone is sufficient, whether Article III judges are inferior officers, or how to classify an officer possessing unreviewable final authority and no removable or supervisory superior.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's user-approved affirmance treats supervision, removal from assignments and higher executive review together as showing inferior status; that synthesis is his proposed answer to an open classification question. He cannot rely on Weiss's existing military commissions to validate civilian appointments. If either statutory authority or constitutional appointment fails on a timely direct challenge, Ryder's ordinary remedy here is new lawful Article 66 review of the affected petitioners, not acquittal or automatic reversal of their trial convictions.

Do not announce that expiration or loss of a habeas opportunity automatically makes an invalidity-dependent claim cognizable; Heck reserved those claimant categories and requires qualifying invalidation for the claim it decided.

### Issue 1: Section 1983 claims that necessarily invalidate prison discipline

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the cognizability of the deceit-and-bias claims and the proper disposition. He does not decide the merits, standing for future relief, or whether every alleged procedural defect necessarily affects the credits.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies simulated Heck. A procedural label does not avoid channeling when success on the specific defect would necessarily invalidate the discipline. Deceitful suppression of all exculpatory evidence and adjudication by a biased hearing officer would vitiate the good-time decision. A prospective rule governing future receipt of witness statements ordinarily does not invalidate the past judgment and may proceed if standing and equitable requirements are met.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Balisok's damages and declaratory claims based on hearing-officer deceit and bias are not presently cognizable under § 1983 because success would necessarily imply invalidity of the outstanding deprivation of good-time credits. A genuinely prospective or separable procedural claim remains cognizable.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The channel turns on legal consequence, not on whether the complaint asks directly for restoration. A declaration that the officer intentionally suppressed exculpatory evidence and decided the case with bias would undermine the disciplinary judgment itself. By contrast, an injunction requiring future date stamps can operate without altering past credits. The lower courts must classify each remaining allegation rather than use a categorical rule for all procedural claims.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Failure to provide a written statement or another defect that can be remedied without invalidating the result may be separable. The decision does not extend Heck to a claimant lacking a legally available habeas route through no fault of his own.

### Issue 2: Dismissal rather than stay

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the proper procedural disposition because the district court stayed the action pending state proceedings.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the registered consequence. A claim either proceeds under § 1983 or is temporarily unavailable because another lawful channel must first invalidate the judgment. The district court may not create an exhaustion stay that turns a noncognizable claim into a pending federal action.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The channeled claims must be dismissed without prejudice rather than stayed for exhaustion.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Dismissal preserves the habeas sequence and avoids an unauthorized federal hold over claims that cannot yet be adjudicated. It also leaves immediately cognizable prospective claims free to proceed.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A stay may remain available for an independently authorized reason, but not merely to impose state exhaustion on § 1983.

# 92. Clinton v. Jones

# SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

**Current approval status:** Approved by the user on October 13, 2026 (Version 2). It replaces Stone's previously approved October 11, 2026 position in full. It records Stone's position for the judicial simulation, not a Court decision, completed vote, or finding that the allegations are true.

## A. STATUS AND REACH

- **Historical decision baseline:** May 27, 1997. Use only the record and precedent available to the simulated Court at that time.
- **Proposed disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment, allowing the federal civil action to proceed under ordinary procedural law.
- **Primary approach:** Reject a constitutional shield based on presidential status, explain the official/unofficial distinction through an objective functional framework, and reject a term-long stay unsupported by a concrete showing of interference with Article II duties.
- **Conditional fallback:** If the Court will not adopt an articulated objective framework or broader language concerning unofficial acts, decide the two questions actually presented: no temporary constitutional immunity for the pleaded pre-presidential conduct in this federal civil case, and no justification on this record for the categorical trial stay. Preserve the same judgment.
- **Limits:** The judgment does not determine Clinton's liability, resolve the merits of Jones's federal or state-law claims, decide a separate immunity defense to the post-inauguration defamation allegations, or announce binding rules for criminal prosecution or state-court jurisdiction.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment. The district court's rejection of the claimed constitutional immunity stands, and its advance order staying trial until the end of the presidential term cannot stand on this record. Stone reaches the proper result without necessarily adopting the Eighth Circuit's characterization of a discretionary trial stay as the functional equivalent of constitutional immunity.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The action returns to the district court. Discovery and trial may be scheduled under ordinary procedural law. The court may use protective orders, sequencing, deposition accommodations, written or remote procedures where authorized, or tailored delay if the party requesting relief establishes an actual and material conflict with a particular presidential function. The need, length, consequences, and available alternatives must be assessed specifically. There is no automatic stay for the President's term, and nothing in this judgment requires him to attend a specified court session in person.
- **No merits decision:** The allegations are taken as pleaded solely to adjudicate this immunity question. The district court must independently decide jurisdictional, merits, evidentiary, and other defenses in the ordinary course.

### Issue 1: Presidential status does not immunize unofficial civil misconduct

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves whether Article II or separation of powers gives a sitting President temporary immunity, or a presumptive suspension of federal civil litigation, for events preceding the Presidency. The litigation concerns conduct alleged to have begun when Clinton was Governor of Arkansas, not an exercise of his presidential functions.
- **Stone's Primary Legal Position:** The Constitution protects the independence of the Presidency in discharging its assigned functions. It does not create a personal privilege to avoid judicial adjudication of private conduct merely because the defendant later assumes the office. The concern that damages liability could distort future official decisionmaking, which explains Nixon v. Fitzgerald, has no comparable force when the cause of action does not arise from a presidential decision. The court exercises Article III power over a private controversy; it does not take over a presidential responsibility by adjudicating it.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A sitting President has no constitutional temporary immunity from a properly brought federal civil action seeking damages for alleged unofficial conduct that occurred before taking office. The Presidency alone neither extinguishes the claim nor constitutionally requires it to be held until the term ends.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Jones alleges private misconduct, retaliation, and other injuries arising out of events in 1991. Her allegations also refer to Clinton's gubernatorial position and state personnel. For purposes of presidential immunity, none of those 1991 acts could have been performed in execution of the presidency Clinton did not yet hold. Whether some alleged conduct constituted action under color of state law, and whether other state-office or personal defenses exist, are different questions. The Court neither accepts the allegations as established facts nor determines that any claim will ultimately succeed.
- **Broad explanatory principle, beyond the necessary holding:** The basis for presidential official-act immunity is the function, not the officeholder's personal identity. Stone's view is that a President's purely private acts do not become presidential functions by occurring during a presidential term. The facts of this appeal, however, require a binding ruling only on the pre-presidential conduct properly presented.

### Issue 2: Objective functional identification of official and unofficial acts

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The distinction between presidential official and unofficial conduct is indispensable to rejecting immunity here. Because the principal challenged events predate Clinton's presidency, the Court need not decide a contested classification of a presidential act. Stone nonetheless proposes the following objective framework to explain, and constrain, the distinction for future cases. Its detailed formulation is proposed guidance, not an independently necessary holding about hypothetical presidential acts.
- **Stone's Proposed Framework:** First, identify the conduct with appropriate factual specificity and the presidential authority or responsibility claimed to support immunity. Second, ask whether, on objectively established circumstances, the conduct performs, directs, supervises, or is otherwise genuinely related to a function entrusted to the President by the Constitution or federal law, including the outer perimeter recognized in Fitzgerald. Third, as a check against a purely self-serving characterization, ask whether a reasonably informed holder of the Presidency, knowing the powers and circumstances of the office, would understand the conduct to be an exercise of presidential responsibility rather than a private act. The judge decides the legal classification on the relevant record; the actor's assertion or subjective good faith does not decide it.
- **Role of a Reasonable Person:** An ordinary reasonable observer's assessment of the visible circumstances may help expose a claimed official function that is only a label. It cannot itself supply, enlarge, or remove constitutional power. Stone therefore favors an objective reasonable-officeholder check within a functional legal test, not a public-opinion test or a free-standing jury verdict about whether conduct seems presidential.
- **What Does Not Decide Classification:** The President's title, location, access to aides or security, use of public resources, or declaration that an activity was official does not by itself establish presidential function. Nor does the allegation that a decision was unlawful, unconstitutional, retaliatory, or motivated by personal considerations automatically make an otherwise official exercise of presidential authority unofficial. An objectively connected presidential function may remain an official act even if judicial review finds the act invalid. Whether personal civil damages are available is a separate immunity question.
- **Illustrative Application:** Directing military operations or supervising a federal department ordinarily concerns presidential functions, even when the legality of a particular direction is challenged. A private interpersonal encounter does not become presidential government merely because it occurs at an official event or involves an official's staff. The Jones allegations concern events when Clinton held a different office; the Court does not convert any disputed state-official conduct into presidential conduct or decide its separate treatment under civil-rights law.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding, limited to this record:** Acts alleged to have occurred before Clinton assumed the Presidency are not official presidential acts, and the federal immunity recognized in Fitzgerald cannot be invoked solely because he subsequently became President.
- **Boundary:** This framework does not overrule Fitzgerald, create a qualified-immunity standard for Presidents, classify the post-inauguration defamation claim, or decide what relief is available for a proven unconstitutional official act. It distinguishes whether an act is official from whether it is lawful and whether a particular remedy is permitted.

### Issue 3: No automatic postponement; demonstrated interference warrants accommodations

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides whether the district court permissibly directed that trial await the end of the presidential term while permitting proceedings to move in other respects. The Court does not decide any specific compulsory attendance order or future request supported by new evidence.
- **Stone's Primary Legal Position:** Courts owe serious respect to the President's constitutional functions, not automatic deference to a status-based request for delay. Any claimed interference must identify a presidential function and a substantial, case-specific conflict. The party requesting a stay must substantiate the need; the judge should consider the claimant's interest in timely adjudication, loss of evidence, available accommodations, and the duration of relief.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Article II does not require a categorical stay of private federal civil litigation against a sitting President, and the district court abused its discretion by imposing an advance, presidential-term-long trial stay without a developed showing of need and without sufficiently accounting for Jones's interest in timely resolution.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Trial could not yet be assessed against a concrete presidential scheduling conflict, while a years-long delay risked loss of evidence and prejudice. The district court retains real docket-management authority under Landis v. North American Co. That authority is not immunity; a later, properly supported request for limited postponement remains available. The Eighth Circuit's judgment should therefore be affirmed even if Stone declines to adopt its reasoning that any trial stay is automatically equivalent to a grant of immunity.
- **Boundary:** A reasonable accommodation is not a personal exemption from law. Neither the President's office nor the plaintiff's demand creates an absolute right to a particular date, place, or method of testimony.

### Issue 4: Broader presidential accountability principles that Stone announces but does not decide

- **Stone's Express Constitutional Position:** Stone rejects the idea that holding Article II office, by itself, licenses private misconduct or forecloses ordinary legal accountability. Courts may assess the legality of official executive action even when Fitzgerald bars a particular personal-damages remedy. Personal civil immunity, review of official actions, and criminal prosecution are different questions.
- **Official misconduct and constitutional violations:** Stone would be prepared, in a case properly raising the issue, to examine whether Fitzgerald should be reconsidered or limited in cases involving proved treason or grave, deliberate constitutional wrongdoing. A charge that an act is unlawful is not, standing alone, an exception to Fitzgerald, and this record cannot support a binding exception.
- **Federal and state criminal law:** Stone's broader principle is that private criminal conduct is not transformed into constitutionally authorized executive conduct merely because a President commits it. But criminal liability requires an applicable offense, jurisdiction, proof, and an answer to any relevant immunity or supremacy question. This civil appeal cannot decide the timing of prosecution of a sitting President, the full scope of federal or state criminal jurisdiction, or whether immunity protects a specified criminally charged official act.
- **State courts:** Whether a comparable civil suit may proceed in a state tribunal raises federalism and federal-officer concerns different from the federal separation-of-powers argument decided here. Stone reserves that question.
- **Post-inauguration defamation:** The complaint includes statements attributed to presidential agents after Clinton took office. Whether Fitzgerald protects any claim concerning those statements is not presented for resolution and remains open.
- **Status of this issue:** These are declared Stone views and reserved questions. They do not become holdings through placement in the opinion, and Stone does not require agreement with them as the price of joining the judgment.

## C. CONDITIONAL FALLBACK FOR THE COURT

If a majority will not join Stone's broader objective framework or the propositions concerning private conduct during an incumbent President's term, Stone will accept the following narrower grounds without abandoning his separate position:

1. **Federal pre-presidential civil conduct:** Reject temporary immunity because the claimed injury did not arise out of an official presidential function and therefore is not governed by Fitzgerald.
2. **No categorical term-long stay:** Hold the actual stay premature and an abuse of discretion, preserving the district court's ordinary power to issue later, tailored accommodations on a demonstrated need.
3. **No new universal classification test:** State only that immunity depends upon official function rather than personal status; leave precise tests for mixed or disputed presidential acts to cases that present them.
4. **Express reservations:** Decide neither the post-inauguration defamation immunity defense nor liability, state-court jurisdiction, criminal prosecutions, or a proposed constitutional or treason exception to official-act civil damages immunity.

**Fallback judgment and remedy:** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment and remand for federal district-court proceedings. Stone may write separately to explain his broader doctrine if the Court will join only the fallback.

## D. PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

- Nixon v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 731 (1982), recognizes absolute personal civil damages immunity for presidential conduct within the outer perimeter of official responsibility. Allegedly improper motives or violations of law do not automatically put otherwise official conduct beyond that perimeter. Stone does not silently overrule that precedent.
- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219 (1988), supports evaluating immunity through the nature of the function rather than the actor's identity. Stone's particular reasonable-officeholder check is his proposed formulation, not a holding already made in Forrester.
- Landis v. North American Co., 299 U.S. 248 (1936), preserves district-court authority to manage its docket and grant justified stays; it does not authorize a status-based presumption that litigation involving the President must await the end of a term.
- Clinton v. Jones, 520 U.S. 681 (1997), presents the federal civil pre-presidential immunity and discretionary-stay questions. The actual controversy does not provide a vehicle for comprehensive civil and criminal presidential immunity doctrine.
- **No imported future law:** Later Supreme Court decisions on presidential criminal immunity are not part of Stone's 1997 simulated precedent baseline.

## E. FINAL PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm and remand. The federal civil action is not barred or automatically suspended because the defendant holds the Presidency. The district court shall proceed under ordinary law while giving concrete presidential functions tailored procedural protection when justified. Stone's primary functional account and his reservations about the full breadth of presidential immunity are stated explicitly; the fallback preserves the same disposition if the Court accepts only the minimum grounds required by this record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The agency’s no-development determination is final

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches ripeness because it is the sole basis of dismissal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Is a facially complete regulatory-takings claim unripe because the owner has not attempted to sell transferable development rights, when the agency has definitively determined that the parcel may not be developed and the transfer rights affect compensation or economic impact rather than the permitted use of the parcel?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received final-decision doctrine and distinguish land-use finality from proof of economic impact.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A regulatory-takings claim is final for federal adjudication when the responsible agency has definitively prohibited development of the parcel and no further land-use procedure can alter that decision. The owner need not first sell transferable development rights that do not change the parcel’s permitted use.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The agency has answered the institutional question assigned to it: this parcel cannot be developed. Marketing the transfer rights may show their worth, but the market cannot revise the land-use determination. Requiring a sale as a jurisdictional prerequisite would make a merits fact into a gate and could force the owner to dispose of a disputed asset before a court decides its legal significance.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone expresses no view on whether a taking occurred, how the rights affect economic impact or compensation, or what other remedial prerequisites may apply.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's proposed remand permits the takings claim to be heard; it supplies no Lucas total-loss finding and no damages award. The transfer rights remain relevant on the merits despite being unnecessary to obtain a final agency use determination.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Accepted pleas are not freely withdrawable because agreements remain pending

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Rule question because it controls the judgments and presents no threshold bar.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** When a district court accepts a defendant’s guilty plea but defers acceptance of the associated plea agreement, may the defendant withdraw the plea before sentencing as of right, or must the defendant show a “fair and just reason” under former Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 32(e)?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted procedural text without creating an equitable exception that collapses the plea and agreement into one event.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a district court accepts a guilty plea after the required Rule 11 inquiry but defers acceptance of the plea agreement, former Rule 32(e) requires the defendant to show a fair and just reason to withdraw the plea before sentencing.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The plea is the defendant’s admission and waiver; the agreement is the proposed disposition. Rule 11 separately authorizes deferral of the agreement and specifies the consequence if the court rejects it. Treating deferral as nonacceptance of the plea would make the separate provisions largely meaningless and would diminish the seriousness of the sworn colloquy. Fairness runs through the Rule’s express protections rather than an unwritten unilateral option.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether Hyde's preserved challenges establish a fair and just reason, whether any plea was involuntary, or whether the court must reject an agreement.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the First Circuit judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Invalidate Amendment 506 to the extent it excludes an applicable statutory recidivist enhancement from the maximum term authorized under § 994(h), and direct lawful resentencing.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The enhanced statutory maximum controls

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory issue because it is preserved and dispositive.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does 28 U.S.C. § 994(h) require career-offender guideline sentences at or near the maximum term authorized after applicable statutory recidivist enhancements, or may the Sentencing Commission define the maximum by ignoring those enhancements?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted words and reject the Commission’s contrary policy construction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** For an offender subject to a lawfully invoked statutory recidivist enhancement, the “maximum term authorized” in § 994(h) is the enhanced maximum. The Sentencing Commission may not define the term to exclude that legally operative penalty.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** “Authorized” refers to the sentence the governing statute permits for the offender, not a hypothetical maximum for a first offender. Congress knew how enhancements operate and gave the Commission no power to erase them. Concerns about charging leverage may inform legislation or prosecutorial policy, but they cannot make an applicable maximum disappear from the statute.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not require an enhancement to be sought, approve arbitrary charging, or decide constitutional challenges not preserved in these cases.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's proposed interpretation follows statutory authority before guideline policy, preserving applicable notice and statutory predicates for the enhancement rather than treating a hypothetical maximum as automatically applicable to every defendant.
