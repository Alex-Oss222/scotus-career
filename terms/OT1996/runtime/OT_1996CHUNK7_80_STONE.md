## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The record cannot support Jones Act seaman status

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches seaman status because it disposes of the Jones Act claim and makes the administrative-preclusion question unnecessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a maritime worker hired for one day of dockside painting could establish Jones Act seaman status by aggregating short-term assignments obtained through a union hiring hall on vessels lacking common ownership or control, and whether an administrative Longshore Act determination precluded the Jones Act claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Chandris without changing the test. The fleet alternative requires a real operational grouping through common ownership or control, not merely a shared labor source.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A worker cannot establish the substantial vessel connection required for Jones Act seaman status by aggregating short-term assignments on unrelated vessels that share only a union hiring hall. On this record, one-day dockside painting and unspecified prior engagements do not permit a reasonable finding of a substantial connection in duration and nature to a vessel or commonly controlled fleet.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The seaman category protects workers whose employment regularly exposes them to the perils of the sea. The common-ownership-or-control requirement makes coverage predictable and ties the fleet to an employment relationship rather than to every vessel that happened to hire from the same hall. Papai’s actual assignment involved no voyage, and his general testimony about other employers and unspecified Harbor Tug jobs cannot carry his summary-judgment burden. Stone applies the statutory category, not a policy preference between remedies.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every day worker is land based, require permanent employment, decide a brief assignment involving actual seagoing duties, or foreclose Longshore benefits and other maritime remedies. He does not decide the preclusive effect of the administrative ruling.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone remains bound by Chandris's qualified temporal guide although he did not join that part. His proposed common-control requirement is an articulated fleet application; the short dockside assignment must be analyzed as an actual assignment, not excluded by job title alone.
