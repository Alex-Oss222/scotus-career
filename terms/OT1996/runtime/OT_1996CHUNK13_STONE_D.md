## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 1 — Yes, where the supervisor materially used delegated control to accomplish the harassment.** Stone applies Title VII's agent language and Meritor's direction to distinguish the agency-aided route from scope of employment and negligence. On Harrison's account, a work order moved her from her assigned station into an isolated area where the misconduct occurred. If credited, that is a concrete use of employer-conferred power, not the ordinary opportunity for coworkers to encounter each other. The plaintiff still must prove unlawful sex-based conditions and the agency connection by the ordinary civil burden. Harris governs the former; it supplies no shortcut for the latter.

Stone's position accords with his approved Andrade answer and his proposed Faragher distinction, without treating either as a Court holding. It does not depend on an employer's supposed authorization of sexual misconduct. Such a requirement would make agency responsibility disappear precisely when delegated authority was abused.

**Question 2 — No mandatory hire-or-fire or alter-ego threshold.** The legally relevant inquiry is the power actually delegated and its demonstrated use. Authority over where, with whom and on what task a miner works can materially facilitate harassment even if hiring and discharge belong to someone else. Conversely, holding impressive managerial powers does not establish that those powers aided a particular act. Stone rejects both the instruction's categorical rank requirement and the employee's broader suggestion that significant supervisory authority alone always determines employer liability.

**Question 3 — Yes on this record, because the omission was material and prejudicial.** An instruction limited to authority to commit harassment and reliance on that authority leaves no route for misuse of actual work-assignment power. Those are different legal propositions. Harrison's work-order evidence directly supported the omitted route, so the defense verdict does not establish that a properly instructed jury rejected it. The state verdict against Brown does not identify the jury's resolution of all employer-attribution questions. Stone would retain ordinary review of the whole charge and prejudice, rather than label every agency-instruction error automatically reversible.

The new trial must separately state any genuinely supported apparent-authority or negligence theory. Apparent authority arises from employer manifestations and reasonable reliance; a victim's awareness that harassment is wrong does not nullify the supervisor's actual command power. The policy's dissemination and subsequent response bear on their proper grounds, not on a universal rule that posting a prohibition erases responsibility. No historical Faragher/Ellerth affirmative defense is inserted.

**Question 4 — No contractual-exhaustion bar to this independent Title VII action on the presented record.** Gardner-Denver remains binding; Gilmer addresses an actual agreement to arbitrate statutory rights, not an assumed one. The employer has not supplied the CBA or identified a commitment to arbitrate Title VII claims. Under simulated First Options, courts cannot infer the relevant agreement or delegation from the mere existence of a bargaining relationship. Stone's reservation about review after actual delegation does not enlarge his authority here; this case fails at the antecedent showing.

Livadas and Hawaiian Airlines reinforce the source distinction within their own holdings. This claim asks whether federal employment conditions were discriminatorily imposed, not what the bargaining agreement promises. An employer cannot turn possible contractual remedies into an additional jurisdictional prerequisite to that federal claim. Stone therefore rejects the defense without deciding that every labor contract is excluded from the FAA or that statutory arbitration is always invalid. He respects lawful collective bargaining and valid forum agreements while requiring the source and agreement that make a particular obligation enforceable.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Affirm the Tenth Circuit judgment on the questions presented: preserve its reversal of the employer's Title VII verdict and remand for a new trial on that claim under correct, nonautomatic agency instructions; affirm rejection of Potash's contractual-exhaustion cross-appeal on the actual record. Do not direct judgment for Harrison or transfer Brown's state damages to Potash. The ordinary proceedings may resolve the Title VII elements, agency connection, preserved defenses and authorized remedies; established state judgments remain undisturbed, with ordinary protection against duplicate recovery.

The retrial is necessary because the original jury received a prejudicially incomplete liability rule. No separate policy audit, new exhaustion proceeding or speculative CBA fact-development remand is warranted to rescue the employer's unsupported appellate assertion. This disposition follows Stone's source-based labor commitments and ordinary allocation of factual questions to the jury, without changing the claimant's proof burden or announcing a general anti-arbitration rule.
