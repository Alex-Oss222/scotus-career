## Reconciled common grounds and limits

### Constitutional route: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer

These five assume, without deciding, a concrete past informational injury fairly traceable to the Company's failure to provide timely reports. They do not reject informational injury as a category, attribute an absent-agency causation defect to CBE, or hold that all residents have standing. For this disposition, possible associational reliance on members' interests may also be assumed rather than independently resolved.

The sufficient rule is that none of the requested remedies is likely to repair the assumed past deprivation or prevent a supported ongoing or imminent personal injury on these allegations. Statutory authorization and satisfaction from enforcing public law cannot alone supply that connection. The reporting forms were current before the action commenced; the supplied complaint alleges no ongoing or imminent reporting violation. This is initial standing, not cessation during a suit that began with an adequately alleged continuing controversy.

### Statutory route: Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg

These three would hold that §11046(a)(1)(A)(iii)–(iv), read with the notice and enforcement structure, does not authorize a citizen action based solely on reporting failures fully corrected before commencement, with no alleged continuing or impending failure. The disposition leaves the constitutional standing dispute undecided as a ground of judgment.

The strongest contrary reading incorporates the mandatory filing deadlines through the references to §§11022(a) and 11023(a); unlike Gwaltney's Clean Water Act text, EPCRA does not say a defendant must be alleged presently to be in violation. Notice can end accumulating penalties, invite EPA enforcement, and encourage voluntary correction even if accrued citizen penalties remain available. Those arguments are substantial; the alternative is not dismissed as frivolous or absent from the enacted text.

The three nevertheless read completion and submission, the 60-day notice interval, and supplementary citizen enforcement together as making a citizen action unnecessary when the reporting obligation has already been performed before filing. Gwaltney's notice and supplementary-enforcement analysis supplies an analogy, not an automatic transplant of its different present-tense words. Section 11046(e)'s bar applies only when EPA **has commenced and is diligently pursuing** the specified administrative order or civil action to enforce the requirement or impose its penalty. Neither mere commencement without diligent pursuit nor agency inaction triggers that bar. Here no actual EPA enforcement bar is found; the provision's design bears on construction. The textual ambiguity also makes avoidance of an unnecessary constitutional ruling relevant.

This statutory conclusion does not erase the filing deadlines, declare belated reports timely, forgive any government-enforceable penalty, or decide liability, penalty amounts, coverage, notice compliance, or EPA discretion. Section 11045(c)(1) provides liability to the United States up to the stated maximum, and subsection (c)(3) treats each continuing day separately; neither makes the requested maximum an automatic award. The tier-II alternative incorporated in §11046(a)(1)(A)(iii) remains intact: §11022(a)(2)'s tier-I submission is unnecessary if tier-II information for the same year reaches the same recipients by the same deadline. The separate §11023(a) toxic-release requirement and its own qualifications remain distinct.

Meghrig requires respect for the enacted private cause and remedy, while its RCRA current-endangerment phrase decides no EPCRA issue by itself. Section 11046(g) preserves other existing rights without creating a new claim. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party litigation costs; it does not mandate them or convert this statutory action into compensatory damages.

### Sequencing and its distinct scopes

Scalia and Thomas support the general rejection of assumed disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested substantive claim. O'Connor and Kennedy can join that rule and its application **with their express reservation that the recognized exceptional settings do not form an exhaustive list**. Their acceptance of a qualified opinion is not a vote for an exceptionless ordering rule.

For these four, §11046(c), headed “Relief,” specifies enforcement and penalty powers in actions brought under subsection (a); it does not make every element of statutory liability a jurisdictional fact. Bell and Young preserve the difference between a substantial federal claim and its success. Whether subsection (a) reaches completed reporting violations is a substantial merits dispute. Gwaltney's statutory phrasing and lack of a decisive contested sequencing ruling do not require treating it otherwise.

Breyer agrees that the ordinary and appropriate course **in this case** is Article III first and joins the redressability ground. He does not join the separate general prohibition or a categorical §11046(c) classification used to announce it. He affirmatively allows judgment in exceptional circumstances where a genuinely difficult jurisdictional question may be reserved and the same party plainly prevails on an easily resolved merits issue. The present disputed EPCRA construction supplies no reason to invoke that exception.

Stevens and Souter regard §11046(c)'s express linkage to actions authorized by subsection (a) as a statutory threshold that may be resolved before the disputed constitutional question. Their statutory-first position also addresses the close relationship between the cause and the claimed remedies. They do not convert every unsuccessful federal claim into a jurisdictional defect, withdraw their Young joins, or endorse an unrestricted ability to decide new merits questions without Article III. Ginsburg adopts the narrower statutory-authorization and avoidance ground; she does not join their whole account of threshold ordering.

**Norton and Avrech retain their actual limits.** Norton involved the three-judge-court/appellate route and a contemporaneous decision that had already settled the merits, leaving the same practical outcome by either route. Avrech involved a court-martial-review issue and an intervening merits decision. Neither alone decides the contested EPCRA issue here. Stevens's Norton dissent established jurisdiction before reaching the merits; it is not evidence that he joined Norton's majority rationale. It also does not prove that he would reject a statutory jurisdictional ground here. No current Justice is assigned the later comparator as a date-eligible authority.

### John Paul Stevens

**Disposition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand directing dismissal on the statutory citizen-suit ground. Do not adopt Article III nonredressability as the sufficient ground.

**Ground and authority:** His actual Gwaltney joins support both the exclusion of wholly past citizen actions under that statute and close examination of the jurisdictional gateway; his Lujan separate position accepts concrete personal interests and resolves the statutory reach separately. His current Young join and Arizonans authorship distinguish a defeating substantive defense from absent jurisdiction and require lawful relief, but do not classify EPCRA's express gateway. He adopts the statutory construction and qualifications above.

**Strongest competing path:** The five Justices' application of the existing redressability element could avoid the contested statutory interpretation. He declines it because the direct informational deprivation and statutory sanction present a materially different question from an abstract demand for lawful government or an indirect third-party injury. Sanction and deterrence can benefit a person directly injured even when money goes to the sovereign. His meaningful-partial-relief approach does not require compensation to be the sole possible benefit. This objection is a reason not to reach the constitutional question, not an adjudicated entitlement to a penalty or injunction.

**Join scope and limits:** Supports the statutory-first analysis shared with Souter and the narrower statutory construction shared with Souter and Ginsburg. He may maintain a separate explanation criticizing the constitutional redressability rationale; that explanation has no other Associate join established here. It does not announce that every private dispute automatically satisfies Article III or that standing has affirmatively been adjudicated. He declines any general endorsement of hypothetical jurisdiction. The expanded fourth question removes the historical unpresented-issue criticism; no actual briefing fact is invented.

**Reconciliation:** Model D's constitutional route lacks a concrete changed premise sufficient to overcome the historical statutory commitment. The result is corrected in this handoff, not in the independent freeze.

### Statutory alternatives and legal grounds

**Affirm the statutory dismissal.** The funding provision obliges the recipient to provide a system in which reports lead promptly to investigation, but does not confer the asserted individual federal entitlement to an investigative response. The particular report trigger is important, yet a mandatory administrative response can remain a public institutional duty. **Strongest objection to affirmance:** Unlike aggregate compliance or staffing, the command identifies a report, its child beneficiary, and a concrete act that must begin promptly. A complete failure to act can be evaluated without defining every investigative technique. The affirming ground answers that judicial manageability and intended benefit are necessary but not sufficient to turn an institutional reporting-and-investigation obligation into a private entitlement; both the text's recipient focus and Suter's analogous reporting limit remain material.

**Reverse the statutory dismissal and remand.** The provision can instead be read to confer the limited right of a child identified in a received abuse or neglect report to prompt initiation of an appropriate investigation directed to that report's accuracy. It does not guarantee a particular outcome, removal, universal numerical deadline, or freedom from all private violence. The duty to start a concrete investigation is different from a claim to general agency adequacy.

**Strongest objection to reversal:** A mandatory governmental duty is not necessarily an individual right, and Suter rejected a similarly child-specific reporting claim. The reversing ground distinguishes a limited claim to the service Congress expressly commands for the reported child from the broad caseworker relief supported by Suter's reporting theory. “Promptly” permits application to concrete alleged complete inaction, while choices about how to investigate remain discretionary. That is a proposed provision-specific distinction, not a holding that every beneficiary may enforce every grant condition.

No damages, injunction, staff complement, removal decision, or fixed deadline follows. On either path, ordinary federal oversight and local statutory duties remain.

## 111. Johnson v. Fankell

**Identity and reach:** No. 96-292; argued February 26, 1997; decision June 9, 1997. Fankell, the employee/respondent, filed the state §1983 action. Affirmance neither treats qualified immunity as a mere liability defense nor licenses state courts to dilute its federal content. The Court decides no entitlement to immunity, factual dispute, underlying §1983 violation, or damages.

**Remedy:** Affirm Idaho's dismissal of the attempted interlocutory appeal. ## 117. Mazurek v. Armstrong — June 16, 1997

### Reconciled components and exact governing standard

**M1 — Corrective summary disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support summary review, vacatur of the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand under actual Casey/Fargo and ordinary preliminary-relief standards. The District Court's suggestion that purpose is insulated whenever some approving legislator could have a health motive is wrong. The Ninth Circuit properly recognized the need for objective purpose analysis, but its purpose/substantial-obstacle formulation omits the controlling residual-interference branches. Neither court has supplied a complete assessment.

The remand must apply all six steps with their qualifications:

1. Identify the actual enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, sanctions and clinical circumstances. Before viability the patient need not prove medical necessity or obtain official approval of her reasons. Prohibition, effective third-party control and denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violate the baseline; care cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration.
2. Identify actual function and permissible objective. Obstructive purpose objectively established from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty. Opposition to abortion or sponsorship by opponents is not itself that showing. Truthful persuasion and legitimate medical regulation remain possible; medical claims require medical support.
3. Challengers establish concrete, nontrivial attributable additional interference, a stable population defined by the duty before injury is determined, and causal comparison with actual conditions absent the challenged duty. Consider supported subgroups and cumulative effects without double counting. Capacity, cost, delay, travel and alternatives matter only in their actual, usable operation. Reliable prospective evidence can suffice; there is no universal monetary, mileage or hourly threshold.
4. Baseline violation, prohibited purpose or a substantial obstacle is independently invalid and cannot be saved by benefits. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs timely, clinically appropriate care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible justification and fit, including an answer to an identified concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. The State may show inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences. It need not select the cheapest imaginable policy, disprove hypothetical alternatives, produce a new study in every case, prove scientific certainty or calculate numerical benefit. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends, while purpose, other constitutional protections and ordinary legality remain.
5. Assess medical justification using competent evidence, professional standards, patient circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Professional agreement deserves serious weight without a veto; neither bare professional assertions nor unsupported official preferences are conclusive. Necessary care and clinical judgment remain protected, with no unnecessary prior-permission barrier. Actual fault requirements remain distinct; later reasonable medical disagreement alone establishes no offense. No compelled-communication claim is presented here.
6. Identify the failing duty or application and match preliminary or final relief to the proved population, violation and equitable prerequisites. Preserve severable lawful duties and address actual enforcement dependencies. Other patients' compliance does not defeat individual claims; broader relief requires corresponding proof. An indivisible mandatory condition may fail when ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference without sufficient justification; otherwise relief remains confined to supported applications or groups. No independent numerical facial threshold or automatic all-applications bar applies.

Cahill's previously approved, supervised practice is a concrete alternative to examine at the applicable tier. It is not itself proof of patient burden, a constitutionally sufficient alternative, a lack of all medical justification, or an entitlement to a professional license. ## 118. Crawford-El v. Britton — June 16, 1997

### Reconciled components and grounds

**C1 — Proof quantum:** All eight reject the special clear-and-convincing burden for unconstitutional motive. The ordinary preponderance burden governs this retaliation claim, with legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence. Neither §1983, the underlying First Amendment rule, Harlow's objective qualified-immunity defense, nor the governing Federal Rules supplies the special burden. Kimberlin already separated the motive element from the defense and preserved ordinary procedural burdens, but expressly adopted no clear-and-convincing standard. The reserved quantum question is decided here on the additional lack-of-authority ground; it was not already adjudicated in Kimberlin.

**C2 — Discovery and immunity:** All eight reject a categorical requirement that proof meet the new heightened quantum before necessary, appropriately limited discovery may occur. Harlow permits early disposition of the legal defense on the actual conduct-date law; a motive allegation alone does not defeat it. Courts may require specific, nonconclusory factual support identifying the challenged conduct and cognizable injury, use rule-authorized clarification or replies, sequence or restrict discovery, issue protective orders and apply ordinary summary-judgment duties. A properly supported summary-judgment motion requires affirmative responsive evidence, not a general credibility attack. Necessary factual inquiry may be limited without deciding disputed credibility or demanding conclusive proof available only through discovery.

The lower court's Williams proof-before-discovery proposal was not a majority holding. D. Ginsburg allowed tailored discovery on a sufficiently specific forecast tied to the heightened burden; Edwards and four colleagues rejected that burden under ordinary Rules; Silberman proposed a distinct lawful-purpose immunity rule. None is attributed to all lower judges, and D. Ginsburg's writing is not Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg's position.

**C3 — Corrected remand:** Vacate the lower judgment insofar as it imposes the heightened quantum and directions dependent on it; remand with the existing retaliation/conversion remand otherwise preserved. No final finding establishes protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury, notice, immunity, liability or damages. The prior access-to-courts and due-process dispositions remain closed. The District's municipal issue retains its separate panel posture, and the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits.

**C4 — Rejection of the alternative categorical immunity:** A merely possible lawful reason is not itself a proved Mt. Healthy same-decision defense. Claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; defendant may prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. Neither some improper motive nor mixed motives alone establishes recovery. Kimberlin's objective legal defense remains available on the actual conduct-date notice, but cannot delete a motive element from the wrong. Settle supplies the current actual-causation distinction, not a school-specific doctrine automatically transplanted wholesale to prisons.

The strongest contrary arguments are real: officials should not face costly investigations merely because motive is easy to allege; Britton offers a facially ordinary safekeeping explanation; and the time interval, other prisoners' treatment and asserted injuries may leave this claim factually insufficient. None supplies the missing authority for a new burden or an established independent merits ground permitting this Court to select contested inferences. Ordinary early immunity, factual specificity, limited discovery and actual causation requirements answer those concerns at the supported scope.
