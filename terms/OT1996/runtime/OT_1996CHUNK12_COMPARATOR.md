# 134. Plyler v. Moore

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR

The historical Supreme Court denied certiorari in No. 96-8596 on June 2, 1997, at 520 U.S. 1277. That order supplies no merits holding and no vote on the PLRA's constitutionality.

## B. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT RESULT

The Fourth Circuit affirmed termination. It held that §3626(b)(2) permissibly changed the law governing prospective relief, did not reopen the kind of final judgment protected by Plaut or dictate a forbidden result under Klein, and did not violate due process or equal protection. It rejected the contention that all decree-created protections beyond federal law were themselves federal rights preventing termination. Its use of the historical Lewis opinion must be kept separate from this simulation's materially different controlling Lewis record. [Appellate opinion](https://app.midpage.ai/document/plyler-v-moore-2966517); [official U.S. Reports volume 520](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/520bv.pdf).

# 142. Akins v. FEC

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT COMPARATOR

The D.C. Circuit en banc recognized the voters' informational standing, rejected the Commission's broad major-purpose limitation for the asserted candidate-related financing, reversed the district court, and remanded to the FEC. Its amended opinion preserved a dispute over standing and the significance of agency discretion; it did not adjudicate all underlying transactions.

This records the historical lower-court disposition only. It does not establish a completed simulated Supreme Court outcome or supply governing post-divergence Supreme Court law.

[Primary opinion](https://openjurist.org/101/f3d/731/akins-v-federal-election-commission)

## B. LATER HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR — OUTSIDE GOVERNING SIMULATION LAW

On June 1, 1998, the historical Supreme Court recognized the voters' informational standing but vacated and remanded in FEC v. Akins, 524 U.S. 11, No. 96-1590. Shared informational injury was not automatically a generalized grievance, and lawful agency discretion did not defeat review of a legal premise. The Court did not finally resolve the major-purpose issue: it directed attention to the membership-communications exclusion and the Commission's developing membership rules. Those later regulatory developments are not imported into the OT1996 entering-law packet.

[Historical Supreme Court opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/524/11/). This subsequent historical result supplies no simulated holding, vote, or effective precedent for this packet.
