# OT1996 chunk 13 medical cases — form fixed before assignment

The June 26, 1997 decisions in Vacco v. Quill and Washington v. Glucksberg issue as separate signed opinions after plenary review. Their distinct equality and substantive-liberty questions warrant separate controlling explanations; neither supplies entering law for the other. Form and propositions were fixed before the assignment census.

Vacco: one judgment reversing and remanding for rejection of the presented equal-protection challenge. A five-Justice Court opinion states the ordinary rationality of the treatment-refusal/intentional-assistance distinction, with actual-disadvantage analysis and consent, causation, intent and professional-role grounds; all material refusal, palliation and particularized-claim reservations remain. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support every governing proposition. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the judgment on the reconciled separate grounds. O'Connor also concurs. Ginsburg adopts substantially O'Connor's separate reasons; Breyer joins that separate analysis except its joins to the principal opinions and adds his own reasons.

Glucksberg: one judgment reversing and remanding for rejection of the asserted class-wide fundamental assistance entitlement and entry of judgment for Washington on that claim. A five-Justice Court opinion identifies the competent terminally ill adult's request for willing physician assistance, explains why text, structure, precedent and relevant tradition do not establish it as fundamental, and applies rational review to Washington's concrete protective grounds. It supplies no universal history-only fundamental-rights methodology. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support every governing proposition. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the judgment on their distinct reconciled reasons. The same precise O'Connor/Ginsburg/Breyer adoption relationships apply. Existing refusal and reproductive law remain intact; a materially different individual claim, forced treatment and legally denied palliation are not decided.

No full principal join is inferred from a judgment vote, and no new post-reconciliation commitment is proposed. Each principal opinion has direct five-Justice support; no Marks synthesis is necessary. Separate writings are independently grounded in each case even where their reasoning overlaps.
