## Cohen

Taxman is the relevant new comparison, but it does not displace Kelley or alter the Cohen commitments. Its two controlling Title VII grounds concern a race-selected discharge to preserve departmental diversity **without** the recognized remedial premise, and the concentrated burden of this nonremedial incumbent-job loss despite an available nonracial tie procedure. Cohen instead presents Title IX's independently recognized alternative athletic-accommodation routes, actual competitively viable unmet interests, lack of responsive expansion, and prospective correction of an established program violation. Taxman neither makes statistical imbalance alone actionable nor abolishes supported remediation; the original Cohen model likewise rejects imbalance-only liability and a compulsory proportionality quota.

All eight Associates join Taxman's separation of statutory from constitutional permission. That reinforces Cohen's separate Title IX construction and review of governmental sex classification; it supplies no reason to collapse them or to make private Brown a state actor. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's Taxman majority positions and Scalia/Thomas's textual reservations do not retract their actual Kelley joins. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's broader proposed Title VII educational-integration allowance supplies no new necessary ground for Cohen, which rests on demonstrated unmet opportunity under its own governing statute.

Taxman creates no general ban on every reduction of opportunities during remedial restructuring. The Cohen model preserves institutional choice among **lawful** plans and does not preapprove a particular reduction, announce immunity from other legal duties, or revive the vacated four-team final order. Its distinction between continuing preliminary protection and selection of a final compliance plan therefore stands. No other new event changes the athletic framework, the actual unmet-demand premises, independent constitutional scrutiny or equitable remedial scope.

## Coeur d'Alene

Alaska adds controlling submerged-title law: clear inclusion of the estate and clear intent to defeat future State title are separate demanding inquiries, with federal retention established there through the particular prestatehood instruments and congressional provisions. It is an original action by the United States against Alaska, not a private tribal officer action asserting an exception to State immunity. It decides no Ex parte Young question and supplies no finding about the 1873 reservation order, 1891 statute or alternative aboriginal-title theory pleaded in Coeur.

The original Coeur model already reserves federal title and distinguishes the State/agency and direct quiet-title dismissals from the officer-relief question. Alaska therefore belongs to the law available if the title merits are lawfully reached; it neither establishes tribal ownership nor creates a jurisdictional exception or bar at the pleading stage. O'Connor and Kennedy's Alaska federal-title joins are compatible with their view that this particular comprehensive private officer prayer operates as a barred sovereign-title remedy. They do not regard State ownership as categorically indefeasible. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Alaska joins likewise do not enlarge their limited officer-route commitments into a decree binding Idaho's title. Scalia and Thomas's express-retention and pending-application objections concern Alaska's instruments and statutory setting; their separate opinion expressly does not extend its post-1953-State statutory analysis to earlier-admitted States. No contrary Coeur title finding or revised immunity vote follows.

Del Monte Dunes preserves municipal damages liability while expressly leaving sovereign immunity separate. Fletcher concerns personal prosecutorial functions and defenses, not State sovereign immunity or prospective official-capacity relief. Salazar-Haro withdraws a particular statutory review channel while reserving distinct collateral questions; it establishes neither a Young exception nor the existence of a complete substitute forum for the Tribe. None changes Seminole's actual officer holding or the four-versus-four independent assessment of Coeur's comprehensive prayer.
