## Matters

### Ayeni v. Mottola

The pleading-stage Fourth Amendment claim is sufficiently alleged; the media attendance and recording were not shown to assist execution of the warrant. The qualified-immunity question remains a date-specific notice inquiry. Commitments: affirm denial of dismissal, 6–3 after Stone enters; non-Stone support Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, with Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas opposing.

### CompuServe, Inc. v. Patterson

Affirm the Sixth Circuit on specific jurisdiction. The continuing Ohio-centered commercial relationship and claim-linked contacts support a prima facie showing without a physical-presence rule. Commitment: affirm 8–1, with Scalia opposing; O'Connor eligible to author.

### Hincapie-Nieto v. INS

Affirm dismissal of the direct petition under §440(a), while preserving qualifying §2241 review of substantial legal and constitutional challenges; do not adjudicate an unfiled habeas case. Commitment: affirm 6–3; Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose. Souter eligible to author.

### Kinman v. Omaha Public School District

Mixed judgment. The Title IX remand remains available but must use recipient-specific responsibility rather than automatic vicarious liability. The §1983 supervisory judgments require individualized notice and response analysis; the municipal dismissal remains affirmed on this record. Commitments: Title IX vacate and remand 6–3; supervisory §1983 judgments vacate and remand 5–4; municipal dismissal affirm 9–0. Thomas eligible to author the mixed opinion.

### Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.

Affirm the Federal Circuit’s reversal and remand for claim construction using the intrinsic record as primary, while permitting technical material to illuminate genuine terminology disputes. No infringement, validity, damages, or equivalents ruling. Commitment: affirm 7–2; Scalia and Thomas oppose. Stone-Zsela is eligible to author if the final coalition accepts the stated form.

### Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas

Reverse and remand with the federal action barred by the Tax Injunction Act because the privately owned associations are not the United States and the state remedy is adequate. Commitment: 9–0; O'Connor eligible to author.

### Boggs v. Boggs

Affirm the Fifth Circuit’s rejection of ERISA preemption on the supplied testamentary community-property record, without deciding post-distribution assets or independent obligations. Commitment: affirm 5–4; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer support; Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas oppose.

### De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund

Reverse and remand. A generally applicable health-facility assessment does not become a plan command merely because the plan owns the facility or bears the cost directly. Commitment: reverse 8–1, with Scalia opposing; Souter eligible to author.

### Lords Landing Village Condominium Council of Unit Owners v. Continental Insurance Co.

Grant, vacate, and remand for consideration of the authoritative Maryland decision. No insurance merits holding. Commitment: GVR 9–0; per curiam form is available and no signed lead opinion is counted.

### McMillian v. Monroe County

Reverse and remand. Alabama law, assessed function by function, makes the sheriff the county policymaker for the challenged law-enforcement activity on this record. Commitment: reverse 5–4; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer support; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose. Stone-Zsela is eligible to author.

### Saratoga Fishing Co. v. J.M. Martinac & Co.

Reverse and remand. User-added equipment is other property under East River when the defective vessel later destroys it; defect, causation, and damages remain for further proceedings. Commitment: reverse 7–2; Scalia and Thomas oppose. Kennedy eligible to author.
