## 127. Agostini v. Felton; consolidated Nos. 96-552 and 96-553

### Constitutional rule, Rule 60 channel and limits

Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test. Its purpose, effects and entanglement inquiries supply evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives attribution-and-function analysis under ordinary procedure. These are routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test.

Religious-school location, presumed indoctrination from location, incidental institutional benefit and ordinary secular monitoring do not alone establish prohibited sponsorship, financing or institutional control. Public control, independently secular work and enforceable protection against devotional use and diversion remain material. The rule overrules Aguilar's categorical treatment of this public Title I service and displaces Ball's corresponding onsite rationale to that extent, without reopening Ball's judgment or approving its separate Community Education program. Specific prohibitions, distinct doctrines and the actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain intact. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding.

Rufo requires a significant change in fact or law making continued prospective enforcement inequitable and relief suitably tailored to that change. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a relevant risk can impose a heavy burden; foreseeability alone is no absolute bar. Costs, a cheaper alternative or anticipated judicial disagreement alone do not suffice. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply Aguilar. The Court's decision supplies the present legal change; no second motion is required solely to invoke it. Rule 60(b)(5) does not generally reopen completed judgments or extinguish independently supported statutory, contractual or decree obligations.

### Remedy and source limits

The four Associates joining the Court support mandatory removal of the onsite prohibition insofar as it rests on religious location, presumed indoctrination from that location or ordinary secular monitoring. If no independent basis supports that prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any unresolved issue must identify a supported prohibition material under the updated Lemon test; ordinary procedure applies, without an impossible-misconduct guarantee or presumed inseparability. Existing lawful services may continue during prompt implementation. Administrative delay cannot perpetuate the categorical rule indefinitely, and the City cannot disregard a still-operative injunction before the district court acts under the mandate. The four dissenters would preserve the injunction by affirming denial of relief. The consolidated dockets have the same disposition.

The Rule 60 motions were filed in October and December 1995; the record does not allocate each month to a particular filer or establish precise filing days. No factual transformation, unproved diversion, new damages entitlement, compulsory onsite funding, fixed implementation period or extinguishment of an independent obligation supplies the decision.

### Two lawful paths and their limits

**Affirmance path.** Section 1681(b) does not require preferential treatment simply to eliminate numerical imbalance. The regulation and 1979 interpretation nonetheless may use relevant participation evidence and three independent means of showing effective accommodation. Substantial proportionality is one sufficient route; continuing responsive expansion and full effective accommodation are others. Brown failed the first two on this record, and the supported findings of competitively viable interest and ability in the women's gymnastics, fencing and ski teams and water-polo club supply the third-route failure. This is not liability from percentages or from the loss of one team by itself.

Brown's strongest objection is that requiring full accommodation of the underrepresented sex while allowing unmet interest among the overrepresented sex makes proportionality coercive in practice. That objection would have force if the latter routes were illusory, comparative interest were excluded from every relevant factual inquiry, or viable interest were simply presumed from enrollment. The current Kelley rule, joined by all eight, makes the routes genuinely independent. Actual unmet demand, not a compelled assumption about identical preferences, defeats the defense here. Evidence measuring real interest and ability remains relevant within prong three; a comparison with men's aggregate preferences does not authorize leaving proved women's opportunities unserved merely because a larger share of men might participate. No new fixed participant-count formula, survey method, roster-number threshold or presumption of equal interests is adopted.

**Brown's narrowing/reversal path.** A court could require reconsideration if the liability findings rested on a quota, an unsupported demand estimate, an invalid counting rule material to the result, or an interpretation contradicting §1681(b). That route is legally real, and each Justice rejects a contrary administrative command. The supplied lower record instead contains actual competitively viable unmet interests and failure of the independent expansion route. Brown's objections remain arguments; the packet supplies no adopted correction of those findings that would make remand necessary. The shared rule does not authorize this Court to invent a contrary finding, later survey or later compliance event.

**Independent constitutional issue.** Brown's federal funding does not make it a public university. The identified governmental conduct is federal implementation and judicial enforcement of the sex-conscious accommodation rule. The objective is effective athletic opportunity in the face of demonstrated inequality; the alternative routes and the actual unmet-demand findings substantially connect the application to that objective. The principle works symmetrically for the actually underrepresented sex and neither declares women inherently less interested nor treats all numerical disparities as constitutional injuries. Kelley requires that analysis independently of statutory compliance. The Associates need not decide every imaginable application or reopen Kelley's result.

**Remedial alternative.** The original preliminary relief restoring gymnastics and volleyball is distinct from the later final direction to fund gymnastics, fencing, skiing and water polo. The First Circuit preserved the former while vacating the latter and returning meaningful choice of means, including lawful reductions. Effective correction may ultimately require specific relief if a permissible institutional plan fails or narrower alternatives cannot cure the violation. But liability alone does not prove that the four-team choice is necessary now. The planned remand preserves that distinction, permits no indefinite postponement, fixes no budget or roster, and supplies no damages or new funding sanction. Kinman's recipient-damages threshold for harassment is not imported into this prospective athletic-accommodation action.

### Adversarial comparison

**Officer-route path.** Young permits prospective relief from an ongoing violation of federal law by officials who actually exercise the challenged regulatory authority. The federal title question may be a premise of their lawful conduct without becoming a judgment binding the immune State's title. Tindal and the officer-property reasoning in Treasure Salvors supply the relevant distinction; Edelman and Green preserve the boundary against retrospective compensation or declarations about completed violations. Pennhurst excludes compelled compliance with state law, not this pleaded federal-law claim. Current Seminole reinforces the availability of bounded officer relief without creating a general rule for all statutes or deciding the land question.

The strongest objection is practical: an order giving the Tribe exclusive use and preventing the State's officers from governing the beds, banks and waters would transfer the operative control even if the decree declares that the State is not formally bound. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would enforce the Ninth Circuit's express limits and require a proved federal violation and an appropriate final decree. They would not assume at Rule 12 that title is already established, or let the State's assertion of an unusually important interest decide the federal merits indirectly. The width of the prayer supports careful remedial review, not dismissal of otherwise proper officer claims before those questions can be resolved.

**Functional-sovereign-relief path.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would treat the requested exclusive occupation and sweeping exclusion of State regulation as effectively the direct sovereign-property decree that the lower court has already barred. The comprehensive federal-title premise and displacement of sovereign control distinguish this prayer from an ordinary order stopping a particular enforcement act. Their rule rests on the actual scope and effect of relief; it is not a general assertion that every suit involving State land or every important State interest defeats Young.

Its strongest objection is that Young commonly restrains important official conduct and often requires deciding whether asserted State authority conflicts with federal rights. If the State can prevail by describing the affected interest as sovereign, the exception would swallow federal supremacy. These four retain ordinary Young suits and demand the unusual equivalence between this prayer and the barred title/control remedy. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas will not join an independent generalized balance of federal need, State importance and alternative forums. Kennedy may explain why the waters' regulatory sovereignty matters to that equivalence, but no additional balancing proposition is necessary to the shared disposition.

**Narrower remedial possibility and limits.** A demonstrably narrower claim against a connected officer could present a different case; no amended or severed complaint is found here. Conversely, the affirmance votes do not approve the prayer in every requested respect. Both camps reject retrospective treasury relief, a binding quiet-title decree against Idaho, automatic immunity waiver and a merits ruling on the separate United States action. That action covers only part of the geography, so its existence supplies neither complete substitute relief nor a basis to presume the Tribe has no practical need for this proceeding. The four reversal votes may not be enlarged into the broad Article I immunity rule they proposed in Seminole; current law supplies no such controlling rationale.

## 130. Kansas v. Hendricks; Hendricks v. Kansas, Nos. 95-1649 and 95-9075

### Component separation and record discipline

The initial commitment's substantive predicate, civil/criminal classification, successive-punishment question and retrospective-punishment question remain separate. A civil label and an unusually protective proof burden answer neither substantive sufficiency nor punitive operation by themselves. The initial October 1994 record is not merged with the unadmitted August 1995 habeas record or later advocacy about weekly treatment hours.

The defined condition affects emotional or volitional capacity and must cause the specified likelihood of predatory sexual violence. The present abnormality and impaired-control evidence, not the bare earlier conviction, do the substantive work. Hendricks's own account under stress, his pedophilia and lack of cure, and the statutory finding support a bounded conclusion about this commitment; no universal diagnosis-by-name rule or prediction from a criminal record alone is proposed. A disagreement over the descriptive term mental illness does not settle the federal issue.

### Substantive-due-process paths

**Sufficient-condition path.** Foucha rejects keeping a no-longer-ill insanity acquittee solely because he cannot prove safety; it expressly leaves materially narrower preventive regimes open. Sahhar allows otherwise lawful civil custody beyond a hypothetical criminal maximum but requires current qualifying grounds and meaningful review/release. Kansas has provided a new proceeding and a specifically defined condition causally connected to predatory violence. The record includes impaired-control evidence rather than danger alone. These features permit rejection of a constitutional rule making the State psychologist's choice of conventional illness terminology conclusive. The statutory reasonable-doubt burden is substantial protection but not a substitute for those substantive conditions.

The strongest contrary path says the abnormality definition merely restates a sexual propensity and turns every sufficiently dangerous repeat offender into a mental patient. The Associates' acceptance is therefore bounded: the causal condition must actually do work beyond past conduct and a forecast, and the supplied evidence of serious impaired control is material to this application. This does not declare the statutory verdict dispositive of federal constitutionality, require proof of an absolute inability under every circumstance, prescribe a new jury instruction, or decide every possible application of personality disorder. The State's own diagnostic distinction merits examination but does not establish that a present qualifying condition is absent in the sense conceded in Foucha.

**Resulting join boundary.** The eight Associates support rejecting the categorical terminology ground on this record. They do not thereby agree on a free-standing preventive detention power, a constitutional guarantee of successful cure, or punishment classification. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's alternative ground would preserve the lower judgment rather than restore the commitment. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse because they also reject the punishment challenges.

### Punishment paths, counterarguments and source limits

**Civil path.** The Act expresses a care, control and treatment purpose; custody is assigned to social-services authority; a present causally connected condition and risk are required; and continued commitment depends on continuing statutory grounds rather than a new fixed penalty for the earlier offense. Annual examination and judicial review and the separate release routes constrain lawful retention. The original lack of a fully operating treatment program is serious but does not, without more, negate the preventive purpose or make the legal scheme a criminal sentence. Difficulty of treatment does not require release of a person otherwise civilly committable, and the statutory care duty remains enforceable on its own terms. Criminal conduct may supply evidence and eligibility without becoming the object punished anew.

The strongest objection is that the practical effect was another incarceration following the completed sentence, selected by past sex offenses and supported by a statutory diagnosis the State distinguished from conventional illness, while the original record identified no dedicated professional hires or signed treatment contract at the stated point, and the October court expressly accepted possible future treatment availability. A statute's therapeutic promise cannot alone defeat that evidence. O'Connor's Allen join supports examining conditions, but she would not infer a punitive regimen from the procurement and staffing deficiencies while disregarding the original testimony about available hospital professionals and intended individualized care. Kennedy's Ursery concurrence calls for objective examination; he finds present condition, social-services authority and release duties enough to resist that inference here, while preserving a different challenge to punitive operation supported by a fuller actual showing. Scalia and Thomas additionally reject conversion of every civil consequence into criminal jeopardy, without overriding binding contrary law. None relies on the later claim of 31.5 treatment hours to repair the original record.

**Punitive original-operation path.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer draw a punitive inference from the combined features of this new, potentially indefinite personal confinement after a completed sentence, its offense-linked selection, the restrictive custodial setting, and the October court's decision to leave commitment standing on the possibility of a future treatment program. No specialist contract, dedicated professional program staff or clinical director had been secured. Those established deficiencies and the lower court's prospective treatment rationale matter together; neither statutory care language nor criminal-style procedural protection resolves the classification.

This is their constitutional assessment of the supported circumstances, not a neutral factual finding of total treatment absence, deliberate withholding, a false promise or an incurable condition. They acknowledge Davis's procurement steps, House's account of available interim clinicians and individualized plans, and the earlier qualified prison-treatment offer. They nevertheless regard anticipated generic clinical availability and a possible future specialized program as insufficient to overcome the punitive significance they attach to the actual additional restraint and the way it was sustained. The clinical content of a treatment plan was not supplied by House's lay testimony; his ignorance does not prove that no plan existed elsewhere. These four do not treat either unproved proposition as established.

Its strongest objection is that protection from a presently dangerous, control-impairing condition is independently nonpunitive and that clearest proof cannot be replaced by a demand for ideal care or a completed contract before the first patient arrives. The four retain the demanding standard and reject a rule that missing job titles, procurement problems, an uncured disorder or a first admission alone makes commitment criminal. Their inference concerns the cumulative scheme and original commitment, including the judicial acceptance of treatment's possible future availability. They do not require an admission of bad motive. They also do not infer that a feasible therapy was deliberately withheld during the prison term: the qualified offered-program testimony precludes that shortcut.

Stevens's Allen dissent and Ursery punitive-operation position give him the most direct affirmative basis for examining a criminally connected personal restraint despite its benign stated purpose. Souter distinguishes his Ursery join through the nature of this deprivation and his Foucha and Sahhar insistence that actual present grounds, the character of custody and meaningful release remain connected. Ginsburg's Ursery and Sahhar joins likewise require evaluating the asserted civil function in its actual setting; she treats the future-availability ruling as material, while recognizing that statute, testimony and delivered care are different propositions. Breyer's Ursery and Sahhar joins support a practical examination of the combined design and administration; he gives weight to the procurement effort but finds that the original confinement was sustained before the promised specialized therapeutic capacity was established. These are Justice-specific legal applications of the supplied original evidence, not a shared factual finding that the State had no clinicians or had refused effective treatment.
