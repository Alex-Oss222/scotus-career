## 90. United States v. Thomas
A signed lead in four parts addresses the distinct statutory, First Amendment, venue and scope questions. Stevens joins I and III and dissents on II and the judgment; the eight judgment supporters join the whole lead. One case and one lead opinion; no part division among authors is needed.

Eligible full-opinion authors: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## 91. Church on the Rock v. City of Albuquerque
One signed lead: I viewpoint, II concrete establishment result, III complete contextual explanation, IV funding and relief. Six complete joiners; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join I, II and IV and issue one private-access concurrence. Stevens' retained scope is explained in Part I rather than generating a redundant separate opinion. No split authorship is needed because six support every part.

Eligible full-opinion authors: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## 93. Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency
One signed narrow finality opinion, unanimously joined; no alternative discussion making possible saleability, agency award or valuation decisive to finality. Scalia, O'Connor and Thomas explain their preserved compensation-only position in a concurrence. The choice rests on the common legal ground and leaves the competing merits issue open; it predates any count or author selection.

Eligible full-opinion authors: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## 94. United States v. Hyde
One signed unanimous lead states the withdrawal rule with the actual Rule 11 agreement types, rejection protections and limited remand. No separate writing or division of the opinion is needed because every Justice joins the complete governing rule and qualifications.

Eligible full-opinion authors: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## 95. United States v. LaBonte
One signed six-Justice Opinion of the Court with the benchmark rule, distinct proximity qualification and three-respondent remedial application. One common three-Justice dissent supports the opposite statutory construction. The related respondents issue in one opinion because they present the same grant; no count affects that form and no separate opinion part requires a different author.

Eligible full-opinion authors: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas.
