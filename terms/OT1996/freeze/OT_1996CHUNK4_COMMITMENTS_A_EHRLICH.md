# Independent non-Stone commitments — Ehrlich v. City of Culver City

Independent modeling handoff, prior to historical reconciliation or Stone's current position. Cutoff: immediately before February 18, 1997. The authorized grant and event calendar are accepted; neither supplies statutory finality. All eight Associates participate. No opinion assignment is made.

## Result

All eight Associates support **dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted**. They do not support a merits disposition of the recreation charge on this unfinished state judgment. They agree that the art issue presents a materially stronger case for separate review under Cox's second category, but decline to retain this grant to decide that distinct issue on the particular record supplied. They need not decide that the art judgment is beyond §1257 jurisdiction, and do not join a categorical rule that another remaining federal question always defeats Cox review. The terminal action leaves the California Supreme Court's judgment and recreation remand undisturbed. It creates no Supreme Court holding about monetary exactions, legislative conditions, the Mitigation Fee Act or the art ordinance.

This is a vehicle disposition, not a determination that the constitutional objection lacks merit, an unexplained conversion to certiorari denial, or an open request for the parties to reconstruct the research record.

## Threshold comparison

### Recreation component

The state judgment requires the City to decide whether any recreation charge is justified and, if so, its extent. It is not a fixed liability award awaiting a ministerial calculation. The City's remaining inquiry includes permissible cost categories, net effects, benefits and specific findings. A lawful answer could be no charge; an accepted charge could end the controversy; a disputed new charge could present its own federal nexus or proportionality question. The presently rejected contention that ending a private benefit can never justify such a condition is a completed legal ruling, but that alone does not complete the case.

Cox's first category does not follow from the finality of that proposition: the substantive remand is neither shown preordained nor practically concluded. The second does not require immediate decision of the recreation issue because the remand could make the federal challenge unnecessary. The third has no established predicate: no rule prevents eventual federal review after an adverse final judgment. The fourth requires both a litigation-ending federal decision and serious erosion of federal policy from postponement. Even assuming a decision forbidding this recreation-cost theory would end the corresponding controversy, the latter requirement is not satisfied merely by having to litigate a takings claim. The project received its 30 occupancy certificates while the protest preserved the lawsuit. The record shows neither a continuing permit embargo nor the special interference with speech, exclusive federal adjudication or federal operations found in the cited Cox applications. Ordinary litigation burden alone cannot replace the separate policy showing.

The strongest contrary position emphasizes that the owner's no-fee theory is final and implicates the constitutional protection against conditioning development on surrender of property. Scalia's Nollan and the canonical Dolan majority supply serious support for that substantive concern. They do not eliminate §1257's finality requirement, and Dolan's own limited remedy shows that a demand for lawful further justification is not invariably itself a completed taking. No exemption from finality for land-use litigation is adopted.

### Art component and the retained-grant alternative

The art obligation was sustained, compliance occurred under an express reservation, and recreation proceedings cannot simply eliminate that obligation. This is the strongest route to component-specific review under Cox II; its survival must be acknowledged rather than dismissed by calling the entire judgment interlocutory. Cox describes dissociated federal issues and also emphasizes avoiding multiple federal appeals; its Radio Station WOW example involved a remaining accounting that could not itself present another federal issue. Here the unfinished recreation dispute concerns related development conditions and could produce a later constitutional appeal. That distinction is relevant to retaining this grant, but these commitments do not resolve whether it necessarily defeats art jurisdiction.

Assuming the art component could be separately reviewed, the granted controversy would change materially. The individualized recreation charge presents the monetary-exaction question; the art program instead imposes a valuation-based requirement across defined classes with several methods of compliance. The successor used onsite art, retained title, and remained subject to maintenance, insurance and public-accessibility terms. The ordinance permits either at least ten hours of free public availability or equivalent public accessibility based on the work's characteristics or placement. No particular compelled-entry easement or mandatory physical occupation of the site is established. The legal claim that title or public access was appropriated cannot be decided by silently substituting the cash or donation alternatives for the option actually selected, nor by presuming the equivalent-access route satisfied or defeated the claim.

A court could decide a suitably bounded general challenge without the precise site arrangement; its absence is not a litigant default or a jurisdictional fact found against Ehrlich. But partial review would leave the grant's central individualized-charge question unresolved while inviting a broad rule about general aesthetic regulations or a physical-occupation holding on unestablished site facts. The Associates independently elect to dismiss this grant, leaving later review on a final, suitable judgment available. They do not order a new evidentiary record or a factual remand on the art issue.

### State-law ground and preservation

No adequate independent state-law basis is used to dismiss. Only three state Justices expressly join the lead opinion's Mitigation Fee Act construction; that reasoning expressly integrates perceived federal requirements. Mosk's specified joins, Werdegar's refusal to reach Part II and Kennard/Baxter's different recreation rationale do not create a fictional majority independent ground. Long's inquiry concerns the basis actually supporting the judgment.

Nor is the art theory treated as automatically forfeited. Compliance and acceptance are distinct from preservation of a physical-invasion argument. The supported pleading and later appellate quotations give the owner a substantial Dewey argument that this is a reason within the litigated takings question; the City raises Cardinale's concern about new questions and missing material facts. Dismissal of this grant decides neither a new forfeiture rule nor the truth of the asserted physical occupation.

## No conditional merits votes converted into law

The enhanced comparison identifies competing merits paths only to explain why the threshold and vehicle issues matter. The Associates cast no operative vote on extending rough proportionality to money, rejecting every recreation-replacement theory, sustaining the art program, assigning an evidentiary burden, recognizing an actual easement, or determining a refund. Nollan, Dolan, Penn Central, Sperry, Connolly and Webb's retain their existing scopes. The state judgment remains operative without Supreme Court endorsement of its federal reasoning.

No public explanation is mandated by these commitments. If assembly chooses a reasoned explanatory writing within the compatible final topology, it may explain the vehicle limitations but must not portray unadopted statutory jurisdiction or merits conclusions as holdings. No private poll belongs in the public action. The unchallenged $30,000 park fee is untouched. No in-world production demand, continued hold or later return condition is imposed.

**Open modeling blocker:** None for the terminal DIG position. A merits resolution of the art or recreation component is outside this frozen position and cannot be substituted merely because another Justice prefers it. Reconciliation may conduct its own permitted review before freezing final non-Stone positions.
