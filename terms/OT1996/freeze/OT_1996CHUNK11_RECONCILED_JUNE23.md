### Paths, history and remedial limits

The reversal path requires the unusual combination of a demand effectively transferring all practical ownership/control and eliminating the State's sovereign regulation of navigable waters. Ordinary prospective relief involving land, substantial compliance costs, or an important governmental interest is not enough. Tindal's ordinary possession/title distinction and Treasure Salvors's officer-property reasoning do not, for these four, establish that the State can be stripped of this regulatory jurisdiction without an effective sovereign-title decree. Young remains valid, Pennhurst's state-law limit remains separate, and the narrow rule supplies no title merits holding.

The strongest objection is that every Young injunction displaces asserted official authority, sometimes very important authority, and the State cannot defeat a federal-right allegation by claiming property ownership. The affirmance four accept that objection: federal title may be a necessary premise of an officer judgment without binding the immune State. The Rule 12 posture and Ninth Circuit's limits warrant adjudication before relief is fixed.
