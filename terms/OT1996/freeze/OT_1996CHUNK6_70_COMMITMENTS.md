## Judgment, reachability and scope

All eight Associate Justices participate and commit to vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand for provision-specific adjudication. Its undifferentiated right to systemwide substantial compliance cannot stand; the district court's blanket foreclosure ground likewise cannot justify dismissal of every possible concrete right. This disposition establishes neither a specific Title IV-D right nor a violation, monetary entitlement or approved statewide injunction.

The State's later requests concerning the Eleventh Amendment and overruling Maine v. Thiboutot were neither raised or decided below nor presented in the certiorari petition. All eight decline to decide those requests in this review of the stated statutory-right and foreclosure questions. That scope choice is grounded in preservation and the grant, not treated as a predetermined result. It does not adjudicate every defendant-specific or remedial issue that may arise on the actual preserved claims below.

## Exact common propositions available for joinder

**B1 — Identify the right, not just a violated law.** A claimant using §1983 to enforce a federal statute must identify a provision conferring the asserted individual federal right. The provision must be intended to benefit the claimant, impose a binding rather than merely precatory obligation, and define the claimed right with sufficient precision for judicial enforcement rather than leaving it vague or amorphous. Statutory benefit, state administrative duty and enforceable individual right are distinct. The inquiry is provision-specific and reads the text in its statutory setting; it does not treat an entire funding program as an indivisible promise.

**B2 — Programwide substantial compliance is not the claimed individual right.** Title IV-D's systemwide substantial-compliance measure serves federal evaluation and administration of the State program. The measure does not itself give each beneficiary an individually enforceable entitlement to compel every operation of the entire program to achieve that administrative benchmark. An aggregate level of compliance can coexist with failures affecting particular people; conversely, an individual right, if one exists in a particular provision, cannot be extinguished merely because the State's aggregate performance is satisfactory. The mandatory character of many state-plan duties therefore does not establish the broad right recognized below. This holding does not decide that every staffing, service, distribution or other particular provision fails B1.

**B3 — Foreclosure is a separate inquiry.** When an identified provision creates an individual right, §1983 supplies its statutory enforcement channel unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or establishes a sufficiently comprehensive remedial arrangement incompatible with it. Title IV-D's federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions do not by themselves establish blanket displacement of §1983 for every otherwise existing individual right. Administrative detail and funding withdrawal are not equivalent to an incompatible remedy for the person whose identified right is violated. A specific provision's remedial design must still be assessed on the actual claim; this conclusion does not adjudicate a hypothetical undisclosed remedy or immunize every requested injunction from ordinary limits.

**B4 — State-plan placement creates no categorical bar or entitlement.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 forbid deeming a Social Security Act provision unenforceable merely because it appears in a section requiring a state plan or prescribing plan contents. Their enacted pending-action rules apply here. Their savings language preserves the specified §671(a)(15) nonenforceability result, and their text does not otherwise create every asserted individual right or remedy. The actual Suter holdings already require provision-specific treatment and distinguish §1983 from a separately implied action; no historical Suter rationale or unrecorded state-plan rule is imported through the statutory reference.

**B5 — Concrete allegations and temporal law require examination on remand.** The complaint's broad prayer does not erase Madrid's allegation that collected support was not distributed under former §657(b)(1). It must be considered as an allegation implicating a particular provision, not a proved violation or an adjudicated right to a sum. Under the official 1994 text, subsection (b) concerns support collected under an approved plan in the stated post-1976 fiscal years and is subject to subsection (d)'s distinct foster-care distribution rules. Paragraph (b)(1) directs payment to the family of the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, and the first $50 for each prior month received in that month when the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It is not a new $50 entitlement for every separate installment regardless of month or payment timing. No finding establishes which dates, amounts, family status or exceptions apply to Madrid.

The lower court must identify each actual claimed provision, the conduct and relief at issue, and the governing version and effective date before deciding right, violation and remedy. Pub. L. 104-193 did not make every 1996 change effective on one date: the supplied §654 notes preserve the general October 1, 1996 state-plan implementation rule, the enactment-date residual rule, state legislative and constitutional grace periods and separately deferred amendments. No Arizona legislative implementation event is established. Neither a superseded obligation nor a not-yet-effective one may silently support a current statewide injunction. The remand does not resolve specific application of those transitions without the actual claim and dates.

**B6 — Remedy follows the identified right and violation.** Further proceedings may clarify the particular claims and assess them under B1–B5. Rejection of the broad compliance theory supplies no automatic order to redesign staffing, accounting, paternity or collection systems. Any relief must redress an established right, fit the demonstrated violation and satisfy its own source, defendant and temporal requirements. No particular payment, damages award, statewide injunction or blanket dismissal is ordered. No implied action directly under Title IV-D is decided merely by resolving the §1983 inquiry.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Affirm a broad individually enforceable right to substantial compliance.** The strongest case is that Congress intended assistance to actual families, used mandatory state-plan language and specified many service duties; Wright and Wilder recognize individual rights within spending programs, and the 1994 statutes reject state-plan location as a bar. The counterargument is decisive at this claimed level: an administrative programwide compliance benchmark does not identify what any one claimant is entitled to demand as an individual right. All eight reject the broad right while preserving the distinction between it and particular service or distribution provisions. Complexity alone is not the reason for rejection.

**Reverse for dismissal of all Title IV-D claims because federal oversight is exclusive or plan provisions never create rights.** The State's strongest argument is the detailed audit, correction and funding design, coupled with Pennhurst's need for a clearly defined funding obligation. The actual Suter and Livadas holdings, together with §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10, defeat that categorical shortcut. Congress's administrative oversight can coexist with §1983 enforcement of an independently established right; a specific incompatible remedial arrangement must be identified. All eight reject blanket dismissal without adjudicating a particular claim's ultimate success.

**Decide now that Madrid has an enforceable $50 entitlement and award payment.** The specific mandatory distribution language is far closer to Wright's defined entitlement than an aggregate performance benchmark. Its presence requires preservation and analysis, not immediate judgment: the actual claim, statutory period, eligibility, payment timing, exception, proof, proper defendant and lawful requested remedy have not been adjudicated below. All eight decline to decide that distinct right or award. This avoids both inventing liability and pretending the complaint contains no concrete allegation.

**Selected path: vacate and remand under B1–B6.** This resolves both mistaken categorical approaches while preserving the actual pleading and statute. It does not require lower courts to assume all Title IV-D rights exist or that none can exist. The strongest objection is additional litigation over an already elaborate program; that cost cannot replace Congress's right and remedial design. Remand must remain tied to identified provisions and claims rather than becoming a general commission to supervise the State.

## Precedent treatment

- Suter retains its actual §671(a)(15) and (9) holdings and separate implied-action rulings; its unanimous preservation of §671(a)(16) supports the provision-specific remand, without deciding that provision or importing a historical rationale.
- Livadas supplies the separation of individual federal right, §1983 channel, congressional foreclosure, defendant and relief; general preemption or statutory duty alone is insufficient.
- Wright preserves enforcement of its defined rent/utility entitlement and its separate foreclosure inquiry; it does not create a programwide Title IV-D right.
- Wilder preserves its identified reimbursement obligation and rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal; its actual entitlement analysis does not establish these unidentified claims.
- Pennhurst supplies clear notice of the asserted funding obligation, not categorical immunity for state-plan provisions.
- Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson preserve displacement by a comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme; the listed federal administrative oversight alone does not establish that incompatibility here.
- Thiboutot remains undisturbed; its proposed overruling is outside this preserved review.
