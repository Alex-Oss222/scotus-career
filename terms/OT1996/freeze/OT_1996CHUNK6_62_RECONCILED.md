## Comparator and the material joinder distinction

Historically the Court reversed by per curiam opinion; Stevens concurred only in the judgment, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer. The independent P1–P3 judgment matches, but its eight full rationale joins require an explicit explanation rather than importing unanimity from agreement on reversal.

The historical concurrence accepted reversal on the understanding that abortion serving the minor's best interests was sufficient to authorize the procedure, but withheld assent insofar as the per curiam's equivalence wording might require proof both that abortion was beneficial and that notification was not. It separately emphasized that the enacted text makes a showing that notice is not in the minor's best interests sufficient by itself. Thus the concrete join barrier is an added cumulative proof burden, not opposition to reversal or an insistence on the Ninth Circuit's wording rule.

The frozen P1–P3 avoid that historical ambiguity. They decide only the facial objection to the notice-focused wording, preserve the independent enacted grounds, and expressly do not impose a new proof burden. For final common-opinion compatibility, make the existing statutory consequence explicit: **a qualifying finding that notification is not in the petitioner's best interests itself requires authorization without notice; no separate finding that abortion is in her best interests is an additional prerequisite to that route.** Maturity and abuse remain independent alternative grounds. This is the statute and the already frozen no-new-proof-burden limit, not a new construction derived from unrecovered party assurances.

The majority's historical reliance on the exact Akron language supports P1. The historical opinion's broad-sounding facial sufficiency and assumption about no state construction cannot displace actual Casey/Fargo/Miller or turn absence of a recovered adverse state decision into proof none exists. No new fact from the comparator is needed. The actual substantive framework is a changed legal premise from historical doctrine, but the direct reason the three former judgment-only Justices may join is the common formulation's removal of the cumulative-proof implication they expressly objected to.
