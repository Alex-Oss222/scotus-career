## Objective lower-opinion distinctions already supported

- The accident occurred on a state highway within the Fort Berthold Reservation. The plaintiff Fredericks and defendants Stockert/A-1 were nonmembers; Fredericks's marriage to a deceased member and her children's membership do not make her a member. The lower writings discuss her reservation residence differently; do not resolve disputed residence by assumption.
- A-1 had a subcontract with the tribally owned LCM construction enterprise. Whether the truck was performing that contract at the time of the accident was alleged and debated, not established as a fact by the majority. The Utah choice-of-law/forum clause was described as outside the record and did not receive controlling weight. A generic contractual relationship and a claim arising from that relationship are distinct propositions.
- The adult children's consortium claims were expressly outside the federal appeal because the relevant lower tribunals had not decided their jurisdiction. Do not enlarge the judgment to decide their claims. An insurer had been dismissed by stipulation. The tribal defendants' limited consent to federal injunctive claims should not be rewritten as a universal waiver of tribal immunity.
- This is a federal jurisdictional challenge after tribal proceedings/exhaustion, with federal-question jurisdiction and review of tribal adjudicatory authority at issue. Exhaustion, adjudicatory authority, substantive regulatory authority, state-court availability, and removal are not interchangeable.
- The en banc majority applies Montana's limits to nonmember conduct and rejects both asserted exceptions on its understanding of this dispute. It treats Iowa Mutual principally as an exhaustion decision and rejects an unqualified distinction between adjudicating a nonmember tort and regulating nonmember conduct. These are the lower court's grounds, not already controlling Supreme Court propositions for this matter.
- The three dissent writings share four judges but offer distinct routes. Beam's writing stresses reservation geography and the tribal interest in a forum; Floyd Gibson's disputes extending Montana beyond fee land and alternatively invokes the exceptions; McMillian's emphasizes tribal territorial adjudicatory power, trust/right-of-way land and the absence of express divestiture. Concurrent state jurisdiction is not itself proof against tribal jurisdiction, and uncertainty about a state remedy is not itself a grant of tribal power.
- There is no need to select an exact damages figure: the lower text and the objective historical recitation use different figures. Preserve attribution if a figure becomes material.
