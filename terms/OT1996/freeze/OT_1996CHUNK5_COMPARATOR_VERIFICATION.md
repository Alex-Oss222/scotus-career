### 51 Arizonans

Sponsor standing remains expressly unresolved; the Court assumes it only for its antecedent jurisdictional disposition, never to adjudicate merits. It finds the individual prospective controversy moot after departure from state work, with no continuing class, replacement plaintiff, or surviving personal-capacity damages route. A State's section1983 nonpersonhood is distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity. The Ninth Circuit had excluded the State from party status and never revised that designation, despite allowing constitutional argument under section2403(b); no invented restoration of party status supports the judgment. Historical vacatur rests on the prevailing plaintiff's voluntary mooting act, timely attempts to obtain appellate review, and exceptional procedural/equitable circumstances. Certification is distinguished from Pullman abstention; neither unique circumstances nor a concession of constitutional invalidity on the opponent's reading is a prerequisite. No constitutional merits or authoritative state construction is decided. Sources include the complete statutory appendix and all 31 notes.

### 52 Gonzales

The formal command concerns the later federal sentencing court and any existing state or federal imprisonment; other federal terms retain their lawful section3584 arrangement. It does not require the firearm sentence to be served first and leaves the later-state-sentencing scenario unresolved. Stevens, joined Breyer, objects that a federal-only construction better fits context, amendment history, uniformity, and avoidance of prosecution-order anomalies. Breyer, joined Stevens, adds the practical same-conduct state-firearm-enhancement problem and would preserve sentencing discretion over the existing state term rather than require concurrency. His dissent does not demand concurrent terms in every case. The majority replies through text and the 1984 amendment's rejection of Simpson/Busic limits. Its historical Witte references require entering-law comparison before use in-world.

### 53 Warner-Jenkinson

The unanimous writing preserves equivalents under the 1952 Act, requires element-by-element comparison, forbids vitiating a claim element, and preserves prosecution-history estoppel. For an unexplained required narrowing amendment, the patentee must establish a reason sufficient to avoid estoppel; absent explanation, a patentability-related reason is presumed. The court may investigate why the examiner objected but may not relitigate the correctness of the patentability objection in infringement proceedings (note7). The majority rejects intent as an element of equivalents, measures interchangeability at infringement rather than issuance, and declines to finally settle the entire judge/jury allocation. Note8 separately identifies judicial resolution of legal limits and ordinary summary-judgment/JMOL duties, while offering special verdicts/interrogatories as guidance, not commands. Ginsburg's concurrence, joined Kennedy, cautions against wooden application of the new presumption to already-completed prosecutions whose files were prepared without that evidentiary incentive; remand should consider reasons previously offered or capable of being established now. The supplied comparator's abbreviated holding should not erase that qualification.

### 57 Hubert - material missing topology in supplied comparator

Kennedy's four-Justice rationale uses date-of-death valuation/present-value reasoning, distinguishes qualification from valuation, and treats only material limitations on income as reducing the value of the qualifying interest. O'Connor/Souter/Thomas concur in the result but expressly refuse to adopt that generalized valuation theory: its applicability is unclear and some regulatory treatments conflict with it. They rely on the existing regulation/revenue-ruling combination and quantitative materiality, with no definitive universal measurement test. They emphasize the case-specific Rule142(a) burden because the challenged expense adjustment was omitted from the deficiency notice, the Commissioner's categorical litigation strategy, and absence of an alternative quantitative showing. Their agreement in judgment does not adopt the plurality's date-of-death-only rule.

Scalia/Breyer would reverse because the obligation to pay administration expenses from qualifying income affects the net interest passing and the regulation's materiality term means relevance/consequence to value, not an unspecified substantiality threshold. They reject treating the interest-on-tax Revenue Ruling93-48 as a reversal of the Commissioner's separate administration-expense position and reject treating qualification rules as valuation rules. BOTH leave the precise amount/calculation for remand because the dollar-for-dollar versus discounted-value dispute was not adequately briefed or passed on below. Breyer's separate dissent stresses economic equivalence of paying from income/principal, the net-value purpose, and possible reasonable administrative simplification, but does not finally approve dollar-for-dollar calculation. Do not label either historical dissenter as finally adopting that unqualified methodology.

The marital/charitable questions received the same disposition by the parties' agreement for this case; distinct statutory/regulatory text is not thereby collapsed. Principal-funded expenses remain distinct. There is no majority adoption of a universal valuation-date rule, materiality percentage, calculation formula, or categorical immunity for income-funded expenses. Any in-world Marks determination requires comparing actual reconciled rationales rather than importing historical headcounts or collapsing the two affirming rationales.
