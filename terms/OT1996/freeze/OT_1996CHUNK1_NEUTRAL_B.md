## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether an individual who has regularly observed or studied a particular endangered animal suffers an injury in fact under Article III when government action facilitates the animal's removal from the location where the individual observes it.

2. Whether a plaintiff establishes causation and redressability when the challenged federal authorization permits, but does not independently compel, a private party's conduct allegedly causing the plaintiff's injury.

3. Whether a claimed deprivation of procedures required by the Endangered Species Act can confer standing without a separately established concrete interest threatened by the alleged procedural violation.

## B. ACTUAL RECORD, POSTURE AND REQUESTED RELIEF

The Humane Society seeks review of the D.C. Circuit judgment vacating the district court’s merits summary judgment and directing jurisdictional dismissal. The Secretary of the Interior, Hawthorn Corporation and the named Ringling Brothers interests are respondents in the supplied lower-court caption. This is appellate review, not an original animal-custody proceeding or a Circuit Justice application.

Lota, an Asian elephant at the Milwaukee Zoo since 1954, was donated to Hawthorn in Illinois after attempts to control aggression toward other elephants failed. The zoo decided on the donation in November 1990. The Fish and Wildlife Service issued a CITES pre-Convention certificate in July 1991 and amended it in August to exempt Lota as a pre-Act animal from specified ESA transportation restrictions. The challenged action is that later ESA amendment. The Society did not contest the CITES certificate and waived appeal of its separate claim challenging the November 1990 transfer. A favorable ruling cannot revive that abandoned claim.

The Society requested suspension of Hawthorn’s certificate and a declaration that the Service’s interpretation of commercial activity was unlawful. That interpretation excluded transportation for exhibition without a change in ownership or control from the relevant restriction. The district court denied interim relief and sustained the interpretation on summary judgment. Its statutory ruling provides context; the current questions do not add a final determination of that merits issue.

The standing materials included investigator Michael Winikoff’s declaration and member letters, and Milwaukee resident Kay Mannes’s affidavit. Mannes described several visits between 1968 and 1992 and educational and aesthetic enjoyment from observing Lota, but framed her loss chiefly as inability to study Asian elephants generally. Three other Asian elephants remained; she gave no concrete return plan or continuing observational project. Winikoff summarized 250 letters and 200 calls and attached 83 letters. Only three attached writers mentioned visiting the zoo, and none connected personal observation of Lota with impaired future activity. The appellate court considered the letters because an objection to their unsworn form was not timely preserved below; consideration does not add facts they omit. The Owens letter referred to the Society’s access to Lota, not the personal access counsel attributed to its authors.

Concern about mistreatment, opposition to commercial exhibitions, and impairment of a member’s own observation are distinct interests. No finding establishes that certificate invalidation would return Lota to Milwaukee. Hawthorn could retain her in Illinois, and the zoo’s animal-management reasons for transfer persisted. No compulsory return order against the zoo is within the requested relief.
