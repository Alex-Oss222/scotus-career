Factual and posture checks from the lower opinion:

- After a fourteen-day trial, the district court found a defective hydraulic design, legal causation, and manufacturer/designer liability. It also found Saratoga's poor maintenance, alterations and continued operation despite a leaking hose contributed to the casualty and reduced recovery by two-thirds. The appellate court affirmed those determinations; its ruling on user-added equipment is one part of a mixed disposition. The neutral packet's statement that the fire was merely allegedly caused by a defect omits the adjudicated trial posture.
- Martinac built the vessel for Madruga and contracted with Marco for the hydraulic-system design. Martinac installed the system; Marco inspected it. The vessel was launched in 1972, outfitted with fishing equipment, used, and sold in 1974 to the corporation later called Saratoga Fishing. Equipment was present at resale. The casualty occurred January 16-17, 1986. The respondent calls the original delivered vessel an incomplete fishing machine requiring additional outfitting; that is its characterization of the bargain, not a reason to erase the earlier sale and use.
- The district court awarded $452,326, one-third of the combined value of listed catch, net, skiff, fuel, equipment, crew losses, rescue costs and cash. It denied hull and replacement-part recovery. The circuit excluded equipment transferred with the vessel at resale, retained tuna-catch recovery, and rejected the separate replacement-component and hull-against-designer theories. The current classification question does not by itself reopen every affirmed liability, comparative-fault, interest or damage ruling.

The underlying dispute includes who supplied each category and whether it was user-added equipment or a replacement component, not simply whether something was physically aboard at the casualty.
