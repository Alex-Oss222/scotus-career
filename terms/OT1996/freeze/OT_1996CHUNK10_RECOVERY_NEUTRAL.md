### Governing law and statutory qualifications

The operative standing register is “Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing,” present operation effective Lujan, June 12, 1992. Its controlling injury, traceability, redressability and litigation-stage requirements apply. Lujan's absent-agency redressability application had three joins and cannot be treated as the Court's rule. At this pleading stage, supported allegations and favorable reasonable inferences are distinct from the evidence later required at summary judgment.

Church of Scientology permits meaningful partial relief when something effectual remains; it does not make an abstract declaration sufficient. Brown Group permits Congress's specific displacement of Hunt's prudential participation condition while retaining member injury and likely redress. Its WARN authorization and employee monetary remedy are not EPCRA remedies. Arizonans requires an actual controversy and legally available relief and recognizes limited authority to correct a lower court's jurisdictional error. Bennett accepts pleaded concrete economic injury and a sufficiently supported remedial link, while preserving constitutional standing independently of citizen-suit permission. Young separates defeat of a substantial federal claim on its merits from absence of statutory jurisdiction; it does not expressly authorize assuming Article III.

The official [1994 Code, title 42](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title42/html/USCODE-1994-title42.htm) was retrieved and the complete relevant sections mechanically preserved. Section 11046(a)(1)(A)(iii) addresses failure to complete and submit the inventory form required by §11022(a), subject to that subsection's tier-II alternative; (iv) addresses the toxic-release form under §11023(a). Section 11022(a)(2)'s March 1 deadline does not require a separate tier-I form when the operator supplies tier-II information to the same recipients by the same deadline for the same calendar year. Section 11023(a)'s required form is due July 1 for the preceding calendar year. The full sections retain their coverage, threshold and information qualifications; coverage is not newly adjudicated here.

Section 11046(c) grants jurisdiction to enforce the requirement concerned and impose the associated civil penalty. The meaning and classification of that language are part of the sequencing/statutory questions, not an assumed resolution of them. Section 11046(d)(1) forbids commencement before 60 days after prescribed notice to the Administrator, State and alleged violator. Section 11046(e) separately bars commencement when the Administrator has commenced and is diligently pursuing an administrative order or civil action enforcing the requirement or imposing its penalty. The lower opinion's simplified description of agency enforcement must not replace the statutory diligent-pursuit condition.

Section 11045(c)(1) makes a nongovernmental violator of §11022 or §11023 liable to the United States for up to $25,000 for each violation; subsection (c)(3) treats each continuing day as a separate violation. This is not compensation payable to CBE. Section 11046(f) permits, rather than mandates, an appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party award of litigation costs, including reasonable attorney and expert fees, when issuing a final order. It does not expressly provide standalone damages for delayed information. Section 11046(g) preserves without expanding or restricting other existing statutory or common-law rights; it does not establish an unpleaded alternative claim.

Meghrig supplies independent attention to the enacted environmental cause and remedy. Its RCRA current-danger language and restrictions on retrospective cleanup reimbursement do not themselves answer EPCRA's distinct text. Gwaltney's Clean Water Act language addresses an alleged present or intermittent violation; its statutory result cannot simply be transplanted. Its text, purpose and notice analysis remain relevant competing arguments.

### Material lawful alternatives for independent assessment

1. **Jurisdictional disposition.** Apply Article III before adjudicating the contested statutory cause. Even assuming a concrete past informational injury, determine whether any pleaded and legally available remedy is likely to benefit these plaintiffs. The strongest objection is that Congress protected timely information and supplied penalties to deter recurrence, that the information remains relevant annually, and that favorable pleading inferences may connect enforcement to the members' continuing use. The response requires analysis of the actual pleaded threat and each remedy, not a categorical assertion that every Treasury penalty is constitutionally useless.
2. **Sustain standing, then decide statutory scope.** Concrete loss of timely information may be distinct from a generalized grievance; relief through compliance, deterrence or other concrete benefit must still be established. A statutory merits ruling could either preserve enforcement of missed deadlines incorporated by “under” §§11022 and 11023, or treat completion before suit and the notice/enforcement structure as defeating this citizen cause. The Seventh Circuit's strongest points are the absence of the Clean Water Act's present-tense formulation, incorporation of mandatory deadlines, and notice's continuing purposes. The Company's strongest points are the completed reporting act, the supplemental nature of citizen enforcement and the separate government penalty power. The statute's purpose cannot supply missing Article III redress.
3. **Statutory-first disposition.** The fourth granted question permits assessment of whether an applicable threshold doctrine or narrow pre-divergence sequencing precedent lawfully permits resolving a statutory defect before contested Article III standing. Do not label a merits construction “jurisdictional” merely to bypass that inquiry. A modeler choosing or rejecting this route must verify the actual date-eligible sequencing authorities and distinguish prudential, statutory and constitutional prerequisites. Neither the supplied packet nor this preflight decides that issue.
4. **Bounded remand.** Remand requires an identified unresolved allegation, legal classification or procedurally available amendment question. It cannot rest on an invented future violation, a free-standing entitlement to discovery or a research gap. Prefiling compliance is not cessation after the filing of a live case; postfiling voluntary-cessation rules cannot be applied without addressing that distinction.

The merits of EPCRA liability, the injury question and the sequencing question need not all receive holdings if a sufficient threshold ground resolves review. Conversely, agreement on a dismissal does not merge distinct jurisdictional and statutory rationales.

## 114. Chalmers v. Tulon Co. of Richmond

### Event and scope

The approved neutral source establishes simulated merits review on June 12, 1997 of the Fourth Circuit's December 4, 1996 decision, 101 F.3d 1012, No. 95-2594. No separate Supreme Court docket is supplied. Review concerns Title VII accommodation for the religious letters, adequacy and timing of employer notice, and whether the conduct and third-party effects permit accommodation without undue hardship.

The Court reviews summary judgment for Tulon. Chalmers seeks reversal and trial; Tulon seeks affirmance. The separate disparate-treatment theory was not pursued on appeal. A legitimate nondiscriminatory reason therefore does not, without more, resolve the surviving statutory accommodation duty. No free-exercise, compelled-speech, tort, privacy, constitutional or mixed-motive claim is added.

### Source validation and material qualifications

The [primary lower opinion](https://openjurist.org/101/f3d/1012/chalmers-v-tulon-company-of-richmond), including Motz's majority, Niemeyer's dissent and every footnote, was read in full. The opinion reproduces both letters and provides the actual competing record accounts. Original briefs and joint appendix were not retrieved or represented as read; published recitations suffice for the presently identified paths.

Chalmers was the sole regularly present management-level employee in a small Richmond service center, reported to LaMantia and supervised Combs. She acknowledged responsibility to promote workplace harmony. Her evangelical belief and religious reason for writing were unchallenged; the letters caused her discharge. Known prior religious conversations, the lack of prior objection to them, her professional performance, the delivery of both letters from home on September 6, 1993, the September 21 discharge, and the absence of a written letter prohibition are supported. Absence of a written rule does not itself establish absence of a legitimate workplace requirement.

The LaMantia letter urged repentance and referred generally to conduct displeasing to God; it did not accuse him expressly of adultery. Chalmers testified that her concern was misleading customers about turnaround time. His wife interpreted it as adultery, became distraught, interrupted his business presentation, and discussed his prior infidelity with Chalmers. Chalmers explained her intended reference and later apologized for offense. LaMantia reported personal anguish and marital strain, said he could no longer work with Chalmers, and requested termination. Those reported reactions cannot be omitted merely because Chalmers disputed their employment consequences.

The Combs letter expressly criticized adultery, connected sin with possible illness, and also disclaimed asserting that this explained Combs's illness. Her subordinate status and convalescence are material. Faber reported that Combs was upset and described the letter as cruel. Combs later stated she was not offended and her working relationship was unchanged. **That later statement was unsworn.** The majority's footnote 2 identifies another unsworn Faber statement attached to his affidavit, reporting that Combs felt awkward but had to work through it because Chalmers was her boss; the majority expressly did not rely on that material in affirming. The admissibility and proper use of either statement must not be silently resolved in a party's favor.

The dissent also reports Chalmers's account of no changed conduct toward her and no adverse Richmond workplace change before discharge. Those accounts differ from the Company's characterizations and permit a real dispute about inferences, while the underlying distress and marriage conflict are not erased. The majority describes the material facts as undisputed; the dissent challenges the treatment and inferences drawn from those facts. The Supreme Court must identify genuine material disputes under Rule 56, not simply recite either judge's label.

The dissent's footnote 1 says counsel referred below to testimony that LaMantia asked about starting Bible study, but that portion was **not formally attached to the response to summary judgment**. No favorable commitment may treat that alleged invitation as unquestionably admitted Rule 56 evidence. Ordinary prior religious conversations and their lack of discouragement are independently supported.

The majority's notice ruling is not as broad as the initial packet sometimes suggests. Footnote 3 expressly disclaims holding that general knowledge of sincere beliefs can never provide notice of some religious conduct, with ashes and a yarmulke given as examples. Its ruling concerns this employer's knowledge of these personal letters sent to homes. The majority also rejects post-act notice and lesser punishment as sufficient on this record and supplies an independent inability-to-accommodate ground. Both grounds require assessment.

### Corrections to the source packet's framing

The source packet's statement that the present record “does not permit” a final hardship finding, and its Section D presenting only reversal as the available path, prejudge a disputed issue. They are not carried into this validated packet as law or fact. Its reference to a “feasible” warning likewise states a possibility to assess, not a proven accommodation.

“Actual hardship” does not mean an accident, realized disruption, complaint, or monetized loss must already have occurred. The governing Beadle, Brown and Opuku-Boateng holdings expressly permit concrete noncash burdens and reasonable evidence-supported predictions. Conversely, a judicial label of intrusive or unaccommodable conduct does not dispense with statutory burdens or Rule 56. The statutory phrase is undue hardship on the conduct of the employer's business; interpersonal harm matters through a legally sufficient connection to that inquiry.

### Entering law

The register “Religious accommodation and demonstrated operational hardship,” present operation effective Beadle, February 5, 1996, preserves Hardison's more-than-de-minimis threshold. Ansonia allows an employer to choose an effective reasonable accommodation without providing the employee's preferred option. If none resolves the conflict, the defense must address supported materially distinct proposals and actual operations. No independent negotiation, experiment, accident or every-alternative requirement applies.

Brown, March 17, 1997, was directly joined by all eight Associates on its statutory accommodation part. It retains sincere practice, employment conflict, employer knowledge and adverse treatment, and requires practice-specific accommodation or a supported hardship showing. Supervisor status, coercion, noncash burdens and reasonable forecasts matter; supervisor status and offense alone are not automatically dispositive. Brown's constitutional targeting and completed-trial mixed-motive holdings do not transfer to this private-employer summary-judgment claim.

Opuku-Boateng, April 7, 1997, is unanimous on the same accommodation rule, supported operational prediction, independent evidentiary limitations and the distinction between discussion and effective accommodation. Its concluded trial liability is not a basis to declare Tulon's liability proved. This case presents summary judgment, different practices, different burdens and different evidence.

Section 2000e(j)'s official text includes religious observance, practice and belief unless the employer demonstrates inability reasonably to accommodate without undue hardship on its business. Section 2000e(m) defines “demonstrates” as meeting production and persuasion burdens. Section 2000e-2(a)(1) forbids religion-based discharge and other specified employment discrimination. The statutory accommodation inquiry remains distinct from the abandoned disparate-treatment claim. The source event is September 1993; no pre-1991 conduct assumption or automatic import of Brown's historical remedial restriction is warranted.

### Material lawful alternatives for independent assessment

1. **Affirm on notice.** General familiarity with evangelism and friendly conversations may not sufficiently notify an employer of individually directed letters criticizing a supervisor's and subordinate's private lives at home. Advance knowledge can matter when accommodation must prevent completed third-party injury. The strongest contrary argument is that Tulon understood the conduct's religious character before discharging Chalmers, no express letter rule had been communicated, and the statute does not expressly require forecasting each particular manifestation. The modeler must decide what notice the statutory duty requires and when; the majority's narrow footnote and the difference between harmless visible observance and a completed intrusion must both be preserved.
2. **Affirm on sufficient hardship even if notice suffices.** The supervisory relationship, the actual distress, interrupted business presentation and LaMantia's reported inability to work with Chalmers may support a reasonable inference of more-than-de-minimis business hardship. Supported prevention of religious pressure can count without forcing a coworker to undergo more incidents or litigate harassment. The strongest contrary argument is that the disputed meaning and effects were resolved against the nonmovant, the employer did not show why a future boundary would fail, and a coworker's offense or demand for removal does not automatically establish statutory hardship. A modeler must explain whether the record legally establishes the defense, not presume it from a legitimate disciplinary reason.
3. **Vacate or reverse and remand for correct accommodation analysis.** Employer awareness before discharge, the relation of previous conversations to the letters, competing accounts of workplace effects and the possibility of prospective limits may prevent summary judgment under the correct notice and hardship rules. This path must preserve lawful employer limits on coercion, harassment, repeated targeted intrusion, privacy effects and supported operational burdens; it establishes neither automatic liability nor a right to retain employment after every religiously motivated act. The strongest objection is that a warning cannot undo already completed harm and that an employer need not experiment or disprove hypothetical alternatives. Whether the record supports a materially distinct accommodation, rather than only speculative leniency, is central.
4. **Narrower ground or part-specific holding.** A Justice may reject a universal advance-notice rule while sustaining this particular notice result, or find notice sufficient while treating hardship as unresolved. Such positions are not interchangeable. Distinguish a duty to offer accommodation from an obligation to select lesser discipline, and a question reserved for remand from a rule commanding trial or a particular warning.

The two letters concern different recipients and relationships; their individual effects must be retained even though the judgment concerns one discharge. Nothing permits reconstruction of new testimony, a concession that every warning would work, a finding that Chalmers would refuse all limits, or a categorical immunity for off-premises conduct.
