## 131. Lindh v. Murphy

**Validated intake:** No. 96-6298; review of the Seventh Circuit's en banc judgment, 96 F.3d 856 (September 10, 1996). Argument April 14, 1997 and consideration June 23 are supplied neutral chronology. The application filed July 9, 1992 was pending on AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment. The issue is temporal application of chapter 153 to this noncapital application; it is not disposition of the underlying cross-examination claim.

**Read:** Landgraf and Felker in the focused copy; Hughes Aircraft in the current supplement. The saved [AEDPA text](../sources/chunk9-neutral-a/AEDPA.txt), §§101–107, provides the enacted comparison: §104 contains the §2254 changes; §107(c) expressly addresses chapter 154's pending-case application. Felker expressly reserved other Title I temporal questions. Bracy and Lambrix in the current supplement are bounded habeas context, not holdings resolving Lindh's amendment question.

**Supported alternative paths:** The Court can infer from the adjacent statutory structure that Congress confined the chapter 153 changes to newly filed applications; that path must identify the inference's actual scope and leave provisions with independent temporal commands intact. Alternatively, it can find the chapter 154 clause insufficient to prescribe chapter 153's reach and apply the governing provision-specific temporal inquiry, including whether changed review standards regulate present adjudication or impose a new consequence on a completed event. The strongest objection to the first route is that the capital chapter addresses a distinct State opt-in system and may not establish a negative implication for every ordinary-habeas amendment. The strongest objection to the second is that it may deprive Congress's deliberate chapter contrast of practical meaning and treat a label as a substitute for statutory scope. These are analytical alternatives, not attributed additional filings.

**Justice-specific authorities to use:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg joined Landgraf's sequential framework; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopted the separate relevant-activity approach. Breyer joined O'Connor's six-Justice temporal explanation in Felker and authored the controlling completed-event application in Salazar-Haro. All eight Associates joined Kennedy's substantive Hughes Aircraft application. Preserve those distinctions instead of assuming that Hughes erased the prior methodological disagreement. No current Justice's Lindh position follows automatically from those authorities.

**Limits/readiness:** Ready for independent modeling on the temporal question. The neutral packet establishes the uncontested filing and procedural dates; this preflight did not reread the petition, merits briefs or appendix and makes no claim that they are unavailable. A result requiring resolution of the confrontation merits, a State opt-in finding or another amendment's independent temporal operation needs the relevant scoped source first.

## 132. Metro-North Commuter Railroad Co. v. Buckley

**Validated intake and correction of posture:** No. 96-320; argument February 18, 1997; June 23 consideration. The [Second Circuit opinion, 79 F.3d 1337](https://openjurist.org/79/f3d/1337), establishes an April 1, 1996 decision vacating and remanding a Rule 50 judgment entered at the close of Buckley's case, before Metro-North presented its case. Thus the neutral shorthand describing appellate reversal is not the precise lower mandate. Negligence was conceded; injury, causation and legally available relief were not. The source's account supports extensive exposure, absence of manifested asbestos disease, expert increased-risk evidence and medical-monitoring recommendations. His testimony and the corroboration discussed below are evidence viewed favorably to the nonmovant, not findings by this Court.

**Read:** Gottshall and the exact FELA Standard in the focused copy; 45 U.S.C. §51, with Buell, Urie, Monessen, Ellis and Rogers at their scopes specified by Gottshall. No selected earlier current-term decision resolves asymptomatic monitoring. The packet's suggestion that categorical rejection necessarily misclassifies a present cost is an argument for one side, not established law.

**Emotional-distress alternatives:** A legally bounded physical-impact interpretation may exclude symptomless toxic contact and distinguish later disease, allowing disposition without setting a universal severity rule. The competing construction treats the extensive bodily contact as impact and then separately tests genuine compensable distress under Rule 50. A record-specific insufficiency route is also available only after applying the correct evidentiary standard; lack of psychiatric treatment is not itself an established universal bar. The lower court identified a coworker's testimony and a governmental investigation report as possible corroboration of worker complaints. That defeats a premise that literally no evidence other than Buckley's words exists, while leaving the legal sufficiency of that evidence open. Neither the broad remedial purpose nor serious fear alone eliminates Gottshall's independent gate.

**Monitoring alternatives:** One path treats reasonably necessary additional medical surveillance as a current economic injury caused by negligence, with proof of necessity, increased risk, incremental cost and an appropriately limited remedy still required. Another finds that FELA's injury requirement and relevant common-law background do not authorize a stand-alone lump-sum recovery for this asymptomatic exposure record. The principal challenge to recognizing recovery is whether the judicially selected remedy creates compensation beyond the statute's existing injury category and permits speculative or duplicative recovery. The principal challenge to denying it is whether actual surveillance need is wrongly treated as the feared future disease itself. Denying the demanded damages form need not decide every employer-provided program, reimbursement arrangement, monitoring fund, later manifested illness or materially different exposure. No monitoring schedule, cost, offset or system is established here.

**Justice-specific authorities to use:** Thomas authored Gottshall; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter joined its controlling physical-danger boundary and reservations. Stevens and Ginsburg's public prior-term positions, if used to support a broader application, must be read at their actual scope rather than reconstructed from their judgment votes. Breyer was not on the Gottshall Court and has no inherited join there; his position must be supported by the statute, the preserved authorities and any genuinely relevant date-eligible writing, not assigned a predecessor's view. The lower court's quotations from historical Gottshall cannot override the simulated controlling holding.

**Limits/readiness:** Ready for independent modeling of the two distinct components. Targeted source checks examined the lower opinion's record, posture, impact and distress discussions. No unrecovered trial transcript is treated as proof of litigant default. A particular remedy amount or assertion that monitoring is already fully funded would require further record support.

## 133. Richardson v. McKnight

**Validated intake:** No. 96-318; Sixth Circuit judgment June 7, 1996; argument March 19, 1997; June 23 consideration. The supplied neutral record identifies a legal collateral-order appeal concerning guards employed by a for-profit prison contractor. State action is assumed for this appeal; excessive restraints remain allegations. The route does not authorize a present decision on corporate liability, actual constitutional violation or damages.

**Read:** Wyatt, Elder and Behrens and the immunity Standards in the focused copy; Ayeni, Johnson v. Fankell, Crawford-El and Fletcher in the current supplement. The unanimous Crawford-El holdings retained existing qualified immunity while rejecting unsupported proof burdens. Fletcher applies a historically supported functional classification, preserving qualified immunity for an eligible prosecutor's unprotected evidentiary act. Neither converts all persons exercising governmental functions into eligible officers or abolishes the defense.

**Supported alternative paths:** Deny immunity where the claimed 1871 analogue and the modern operational setting do not establish a historically or functionally justified immunity from suit; private market incentives, insurance and employment arrangements may distinguish public-official concerns. The competing path extends immunity to delegated coercive custody on the ground that the immediate task and potential deterrence of lawful discretion remain materially the same despite the employer's corporate form. Its strongest difficulty is Wyatt's refusal to infer immunity from state action alone and the need to establish a real historical and functional basis. The strongest difficulty for denial is distinguishing full-time delegated confinement from a private litigant's self-interested resort to attachment. A narrow decision can reserve brief private assistance, close official supervision, public integration and other materially different arrangements. Any distinct good-faith defense remains a separate, unadjudicated matter.

**Justice-specific authorities to use:** O'Connor authored Wyatt; Stevens, Scalia and Kennedy joined its full private-process rule; Souter and Thomas dissented. The existence of their dissent is verified in Holdings, but its precise ground is not supplied by that entry: do not invent a full dissent rationale. Ginsburg and Breyer were not on that Court. All eight Associates' joins in Fletcher's functional analysis, and all nine joins in Crawford-El's preservation of existing immunity and ordinary proof, are available current evidence. The legal appellate boundaries do not predetermine immunity eligibility.

**Limits/readiness:** Ready on the supplied eligibility question, with enhanced comparison appropriate. Direct retrieval of the lower opinion was unsuccessful; no contrary fact was found, and the detailed approved neutral record remains the support used. Any proposed disposition dependent on a disputed contractual provision, historical factual assertion about private-jailer immunity, or particular insurance term needs that source rather than a generalized market assumption. This is a limit on that proposed rationale, not a reason to presume liability or halt all modeling.

## 134. Plyler v. Moore

**Validated intake:** Fourth Circuit No. 96-6884, 100 F.3d 365, November 14, 1996; granted lower-court addition under the stated simulation questions. June 23 is the supplied merits-hearing/consideration slot. The timely filing and grant chronology are expressly adopted procedural stipulations, not recovered historical Supreme Court actions. The decree dates to March 1986; the actual termination motion uses §3626(b)(2), not the two-year route. The United States intervened to defend the enactment.

**Read:** Rufo, Lewis, Robertson, Morgan Stanley and Plaut in the focused copy and the three exact decree/PLRA Standards. Joseph A., April 21, 1997, in the current supplement distinguishes proof that agreed release conditions have been performed from a request to modify duties; it expressly found no prison-conditions predicate and therefore does not displace the PLRA route here. Do not use same-day Agostini as intervening law.

**Supported alternative paths:** Affirm the legal authority to terminate ongoing relief under a new statutory standard while preserving judicial application of its conditions; determine that the decree's additional negotiated terms are not themselves the underlying federal right that makes every term immune from termination. The competing Article III/due-process path treats the operation as an impermissible displacement of adjudicated rights, but must explain why ongoing equitable enforcement, retained judicial findings and the statutory preservation clause do not distinguish completed final merits adjudication. A narrower vacatur is possible for a preserved, material failure to apply §3626(b)(3), but cannot rest solely on an imagined ongoing violation or lack of ceremonial wording.

**Required qualifications:** The original exception preserves relief upon written record-based findings of necessity to correct a current OR ongoing federal violation, narrowness, need and least intrusion. The later conjunctive wording does not apply. Lack of formulaic original findings does not prove that adequate substantive findings were absent. Conversely, the source's description of relief exceeding constitutional minima does not establish present constitutional compliance throughout the institutions. The statute's termination command, the court's predicates and independent federal rights are distinct. Section 3626(e)'s automatic stay is not the appealed termination judgment. No result here decides accrued damages, a separate private contract, state remedies or every PLRA provision.

**Justice-specific authorities to use:** All eight Associates joined Kennedy's controlling Lewis Part IV application, which left constitutionality open. Scalia, Kennedy and Souter joined Rufo's controlling rationale; O'Connor's remand vote was narrower. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the controlling Plaut reinstatement rationale; Scalia, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas dissented. Those prior finality disagreements do not establish positions on mutable prospective relief. All nine joined Joseph A.'s separation of performance and modification. Robertson preserves real change in governing law and independent adjudication.

**Limits/readiness:** Ready for the legal questions on the approved record. The attempted lower-opinion retrieval did not succeed. No new factual compliance finding or unrecorded hearing is inferred. A ruling requiring an additional evidentiary remand must identify a preserved defect from an actual source, rather than infer one from the operator's lack of the complete decree file.

## 135. Princeton University Press v. Michigan Document Services

**Validated intake:** Sixth Circuit No. 94-1778, en banc judgment November 8, 1996; June 23 granted merits slot under the supplied simulation stipulations. No separate Supreme Court docket is invented. The [primary lower opinion, 99 F.3d 1381](https://openjurist.org/99/f3d/1381/princeton-university-press-v-michigan-document-services-inc-m), independently confirms that the en banc judgment affirmed fair-use summary judgment, vacated damages and remanded for reconsideration of damages and a separately entered, more precise injunction. The Supreme Court therefore cannot describe an intact $5,000-per-work award as the appellate mandate it simply affirms.

**Material source clarification:** The six excerpts ranged from 17 to 95 pages and 5% to 30% of their books. The lower opinion reports an existing permissions market and rejects an automatic implication that failure to buy a whole book removes all possible excerpt-license substitution. It also reports that publishers did not contest Smith's actual good faith, challenging reasonableness instead. The en banc court did not sustain the willfulness finding. Its remedy discussion identified possible lawful fair-use copying and Rule 58 specificity, while distinguishing the claim about coverage of future copyrighted works. Those are separate subjects. These are lower-court recitations and dispositions, not this Court's factual reweighing or merits rulings.

**Read:** Campbell and the exact fair-use Standard in the focused copy. ProCD, January 27, 1997, preserves the separate inquiries for actual contractual promises and copyright; no promise by this copy shop is established here, and ProCD expressly leaves fair-use questions open. Surviving Sony and Harper & Row remain available only at their scopes preserved in Campbell.

**Supported alternative paths:** Affirm infringement through an integrated four-factor analysis of commercial verbatim reproduction, actual educational selection, protected expression, substantiality and supported substitute licensing. The competing route gives decisive weight on this record to classroom purpose, noncommercial end use, pedagogical selection, factual scholarship and the risk of circularly assuming every demanded license is cognizable harm. The strongest objection to affirmance is treating a revenue demand, commercial label or classroom-guideline excess as dispositive. The strongest objection to reversal is failing to explain why reproduction performing the same assigned-reading function does not substitute in a demonstrated ordinary licensing market. A fact-dependent remand remains possible if material factors cannot be resolved under the proper summary-judgment standard. No fixed percentage, indispensable-transformation rule or categorical commercial presumption follows.

**Remedy alternatives and limits:** Liability and knowing/reckless infringement are separate. Intentional copying alone does not establish willfulness, while sincerity alone need not make an objectively reckless view reasonable. The source's uncontested subjective good faith must not be recast as disputed. Any affirmance of the existing damages remand must say what remains for determination. The injunction cannot silently prohibit all future fair use; the present grant does not establish a historical publisher cross-petition. Classroom-copying guidelines are nonstatutory minimum guidance, not the maximum of §107 or an independent rule deciding infringement. No ruling determines all reserve-room copying, individual student copying, nonprofit copying or digital distribution.

**Justice-specific authorities to use:** Souter authored Campbell; every other then-seated Associate, including Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg, joined its full integrated rule. Kennedy's separate caution did not withdraw his join. Breyer was not seated for Campbell; he joined the later ProCD decision, which is relevant to refusing categorical private control of public-domain material but does not determine §107 here. For any broader Breyer-specific copyright proposition, obtain a date-eligible writing rather than attribute Campbell authorship or a predecessor's position to him.

**Limits/readiness:** Ready for separate modeling of infringement, willfulness/damages and injunctive scope. The targeted lower-source review covered majority record, factors and remedies; it did not purport to recover all underlying affidavits, briefs or every dissent in full. No particular disputed classroom fact beyond these recitations is supplied by this handoff.

## 136. Kolster v. INS

**Validated intake:** First Circuit No. 96-1194, 101 F.3d 785, December 4, 1996; June 23 granted merits slot with express timely-filing/grant stipulations. The [primary lower opinion](https://openjurist.org/101/f3d/785) confirms the BIA's January 30, 1996 final order, February 28 petition, documentary conviction basis, absence of an asserted colorable defense to deportability, September 2, 1992 plea and pretermission of a legal eligibility claim. This is not an adverse discretionary balancing decision. The source's reasoning that reliance was unlikely is a legal inference from those facts, not proof of Kolster's unexpressed subjective motives.

**Read:** Landgraf, Felker and Flores in the focused copy; Reyes-Hernandez (May 12), Hincapie-Nieto (May 27), Salazar-Haro and Hughes Aircraft (June 16) in the current supplement. Read the saved [IIRIRA §306](sources/IIRIRA_306_text.txt), [§309](sources/IIRIRA_309_text.txt) and [October 11 correction](sources/PLAW_104_302_text.txt), together with [AEDPA §440](../sources/chunk9-neutral-a/AEDPA.txt). The correction has September 30 effect. The ordinary new-scheme commencement is April 1, 1997; the old January 1996 final order does not satisfy §309(c)(4)'s later-order trigger, and no qualifying election is established. The saved-proceeding rule does not erase AEDPA's separate bar.

**What earlier current law settles and reserves:** Reyes-Hernandez preserves ordinary review where surrender of a colorable defense acquired the specified new consequence; Hincapie-Nieto and Salazar-Haro reject exemption based only on filing or expectation of favorable discretionary review. Those decisions do not turn surrender of a defense into the exclusive imaginable completed event. Salazar-Haro expressly reserves the actual source, scope and predicates of §2241 review because it presented discretionary reweighing rather than this kind of developed legal eligibility objection. Felker requires attention to an actual retained channel; §1651 alone creates none. Thus Kolster's legal issue cannot be disposed of merely by repeating Salazar-Haro's absence-of-a-legal-defect premise.

**Transition qualification:** The [authoritative sanitized Salazar public handoff](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK12_SALAZAR_PUBLIC.md) and regenerated current supplement preserve the complete §306(c)(2) holding. The protection requires an actual judicial judgment or order under former INA §106, application of that provision as amended by AEDPA §440, and an asserted §306(c)(1) operation that would invalidate the judgment or require reconsideration. It protects against that transition-rule disruption; it neither supplies jurisdiction, reopens merits review nor blocks ordinary appellate correctness review. Public Law 104-302 left paragraph (2) unchanged. Accordingly, no unexplained reference to saved orders may be used as a reason that the present Court cannot review Kolster's lower dismissal on its actual grounds.

**Supported alternative paths:** Apply §440(a) to pending ordinary review on this record, while construing a genuine retained collateral route capable of considering the identified statutory legal objection and granting effective relief if its own predicates are established. Alternatively preserve only the concrete minimum necessary constitutional review or vacate the lower constitutional conclusion where its supposed alternative is insufficiently identified. A temporal exception remains analytically possible only on a supported completed-event consequence; it cannot assume an actual surrendered defense absent here or that four years of claimed domicile made the seven-year benefit available at the plea. The strongest objection to an affirmative retained-habeas construction is deciding a collateral case and its predicates when no such petition is pending. The strongest objection to relying on an unspecified possible remedy is that the actual legal eligibility claim could be left unreviewable while the constitutional ruling assumes otherwise. A channel decision does not resolve the seven-year counting merits, award a waiver or make the sentencing recommendation binding.

**Justice-specific authorities to use:** Breyer's six-Justice Salazar-Haro temporal explanation includes Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retained the separate relevant-activity approach. All eight Associates joined its collateral reservation, tied to the absence of a presented legal defect. They must now confront the changed claim type independently. The Ginsburg-authored Hincapie-Nieto opinion and the published Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas positions, copied in the current supplement, further identify that distinction without forecasting this case's answer. All eight Associates' actual Felker independent-review joins are available; no published join establishes an already adopted affirmative immigration §2241 rule.

**Limits/readiness:** Ready for enhanced independent modeling of temporal reach, meaningful retained legal review and the bounded mandate as separate components. No custody finding, collateral filing, exhaustion ruling, respondent, transfer, stay or new administrative event is invented. An affirmative collateral construction must state its conditional predicates; it cannot declare a nonexistent habeas petition adjudicated. Section 440(d)'s eligibility change remains distinct from the §440(a) channel question.
