### Issue 1: Findings and standard for terminating the foster-care decree

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Rufo (1992) is completed simulated law, not merely a surviving historical authority: five rationale joins require a significant factual or legal change making continued enforcement inequitable for a requested modification, followed by suitably tailored relief preserving lawful remaining purposes. It does not permit rewriting a voluntarily assumed obligation to the constitutional minimum. Missouri v. Jenkins (1995) applies the complete Freeman partial-release inquiry and affirms continued relief where program installation did not prove full compliance, practicable elimination, whole-decree good faith, and the needed causal/remedial connections. No consensus, reviewer endorsement, or Rufo changed-circumstances prerequisite is added to ordinary compliance-based release.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Violation-remedy nexus and incremental withdrawal of control require identification of the obligation addressed, present compliance, and continued necessity. They do not replace the decree's own termination clause.
- **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 52(a); the 1983 consent decree; federal equitable authority over judgments; Rule 53 as applicable to a Special Master's findings.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Application to the decree’s own termination clause

The Department invokes twelve consecutive months of substantial and continuous compliance, so it must establish that contractual termination condition through reviewable findings. It need not additionally prove a Rufo change merely to invoke the decree’s own release clause. If instead it seeks to reduce the still-unmet duties because law or conditions changed, Rufo supplies that different threshold and fit inquiry. Jenkins prevents substituting spending or implementation alone for the necessary remedial findings. Low compliance measures and adverse case studies must be addressed in relation to the actual essential obligations; a master’s unreasoned conclusion does not itself establish discharge.
