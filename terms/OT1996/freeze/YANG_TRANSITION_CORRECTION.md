## Required correction to the transition account

The earlier assertion that the discretionary-review restriction was simply not yet effective omits the provision-specific structure and should be replaced.

1. IIRIRA §309(a) generally makes Title III-A and its amendments effective on the first day of the first month beginning more than 180 days after September 30, 1996: April 1, 1997. It expressly excepts §309 itself and §§303(b)(2), 306(c), 308(d)(2)(D) and 308(d)(5). It is not a blanket statement that every review-related provision is delayed.
2. Public Law 104-302 §2 is expressly effective September 30, 1996. It replaces the original §306(c)(1) applicability language for the amendments made by §306(a) and (b) with application as provided in §309, subject to the separate §242(g) clause. It also changes the §309(c)(1) description from proceedings as of the general effective date to proceedings before that date, and removes §309(c)(4)'s reference limiting it to the case described in paragraph (1).
3. Corrected §309(c)(1), subject to its following provisions, preserves the old proceedings and judicial review for an alien in exclusion or deportation proceedings before the general effective date. Corrected §309(c)(4) separately concerns a final exclusion or deportation order entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996. Its paragraph (E) enumerates discretionary decisions under INA §§212(c), 212(h), 212(i), 244 and 245. It does not name former §241(a)(1)(H). The supplied Yang administrative final order and review already existed in 1995; no new post-October-1996 final order is established.
4. New §242(a)(2)(B), added by §306(a), distinguishes listed forms of discretionary relief in clause (i) from clause (ii)'s other decisions or actions whose authority the INA specifies to be discretionary, with its separate §208(a) exception. Do not conflate that new permanent provision with §309(c)(4)(E)'s transitional list.
5. Section 306(c)(1)'s retained separate clause says new §242(g) applies without limitation to claims arising from past, pending or future exclusion, deportation or removal proceedings. Section 242(g) concerns claims arising from the Attorney General's decision or action to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases or execute removal orders, with the statute's exception for review provided in §242. The existence of this retained exception prevents source-only certification that every potentially relevant restriction was delayed. Its operative commencement, relation to the corrected §309 saving provisions, and application to this particular statutory scope-of-discretion question require legal construction. No later Supreme Court interpretation is used here.
6. Section 306(c)(2) separately preserves judgments or orders under former §106 as amended by AEDPA §440 against invalidation or required reconsideration by paragraph (1). Section 306(d) makes its technical criminal-offense amendment effective as if included in AEDPA. The handoff supplies no covered Yang criminal conviction or other factual predicate for the criminal-alien review restriction; fraud described in an administrative record is not itself such a conviction.

The general effective date and the past/pending/future reach of a particular provision are different questions. An authoritative enacted-text correction establishes the words and their effective-date commands; it does not select an interpretation of the disputed jurisdictional overlap. The neutral replacement should present both the strongest preserved-review route and any textually supportable jurisdictional objection, then allow the fresh modeler to decide them. The Court's review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), the 1995 lower judgment and conceded waiver eligibility remain the supplied procedural facts; no filing default, new final order, agency election or jurisdictional fact is invented.

## Exact eligibility qualifications to retain

Former INA §241(a)(1)(H), 8 U.S.C. §1251(a)(1)(H), permits discretionary waiver of the paragraph's deportation grounds based on entry excludability for the fraud or misrepresentation described in §1182(a)(6)(C)(i), whether willful or innocent. The applicant must be a spouse, parent, son or daughter of a United States citizen or lawful permanent resident; must have possessed an immigrant visa or equivalent document; and must otherwise have been admissible at entry. The admissibility exceptions are the grounds in §1182(a)(5)(A), concerning labor certification, and §1182(a)(7)(A), concerning immigrant documentation, **only when those grounds were a direct result of the fraud or misrepresentation**. This is not a waiver of every admissibility ground or every documentary defect.

The separate exclusion of an alien described in §1251(a)(4)(D), concerning assistance in Nazi persecution or participation in genocide, is not the labor-certification/documentation exception. A waiver actually granted under §241(a)(1)(H) also waives deportation based on grounds of inadmissibility at entry directly resulting from the fraud or misrepresentation. This consequential operation is distinct from the initial eligibility criteria and does not itself enumerate permissible discretionary balancing factors. Eligibility is accepted in Yang's supplied record; no unpresented eligibility dispute is created by completing these qualifications.
