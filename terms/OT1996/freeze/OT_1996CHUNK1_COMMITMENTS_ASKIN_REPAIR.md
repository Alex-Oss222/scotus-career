## Component comparison and provisional conclusions

### 1. Reachability and surviving relief

All eight reach the granted merits. The live injury is the extant contempt adjudication with identified bar and criminal-contempt consequences, not an assumption that civil confinement continues. Vacating that adjudication can provide meaningful relief even after the underlying trial. The strongest mootness objection is that completed coercive confinement alone cannot support prospective relief and that generic reputational concern is insufficient. The concrete outstanding adjudication and identified proceedings distinguish a controversy resting only on abstract disapproval. No outcome in the separate criminal case or automatic bar restoration is adjudicated. No unverified later event is invented to preserve or defeat jurisdiction.

### 2. Former statutory categories

All eight reject the argument that Askin's landline/cellular endpoint alone makes the intercepted cordless-radio portion a **wire communication** or **electronic communication** under the former definitions. The specific handset/base exclusion applies to both directions of that acquired radio segment. The general 'whole or in part' language cannot erase the particular exclusion. This concerns the acquired portion; it does not declare every segment of the complete call unprotected.

The **oral-communication** inquiry is different. Former §2510(2) requires an exhibited expectation against interception under circumstances justifying it and excludes electronic communication. The last qualification predates the 1994 amendment. Former §2510(12)(A), however, expressly excludes this cordless-radio portion from the statutory electronic category. Calling every technologically transmitted sound electronic under ordinary usage would erase that statutory exclusion and cannot resolve §2510(2). Conversely, falling outside the electronic definition does not automatically satisfy either expectation requirement for oral communication.

All eight commit to rejecting categorical oral noncoverage based solely on the medium and the two other exclusions. The lower court's assertion that Congress addressed cordless calls only as wire/electronic communications cannot substitute for the complete oral definition. Its oral-coverage discussion supplies no independent determination of this speaker's exhibited and justified expectation under a correct personal inquiry. A bounded remand is required to decide that issue on the existing claim and actual record.

**Strongest contrary statutory path:** Reclassifying the radio segment as oral can seem to undo Congress's express exclusion and the 1994 deletion's evident function. This is a substantial contextual objection. It loses at the categorical level because the two former exclusions remove automatic category-based protection, whereas the oral definition has additional person-specific requirements and its own electronic exclusion. Conditional oral coverage does not make all cordless calls protected or make the deletion superfluous. The deletion affords category protection without requiring oral-communication findings. The alternative protected-endpoint wire theory fails for a different reason: it would rewrite the express radio-portion exception itself.

The commitments do not find that Askin actually lacked or possessed the necessary expectation. Endpoint knowledge is not established either way. His use of an ordinary/cellular telephone and a private interpersonal call is relevant to his claim, while the actual signal's accessibility and any established knowledge are relevant to the opposing claim. The Court should not create a new scored or mandatory multifactor test or make the caller's ignorance an assumed fact. The remand corrects a lower legal shortcut; it does not penalize a litigant for incomplete modern research.

### 3. The 1994 amendment's temporal effect

All eight apply the former categories to the completed acquisition. Title II §202 has no four-year delay; Title I §111(b) concerns §§103 and 105, and §111(a) speaks to its own title. Nevertheless, immediate enactment does not mean retrospective reclassification. The amendment contains no direction giving new interception prohibitions to completed preenactment acquisition. Applying its expanded category coverage to declare this earlier acquisition unlawful would attach a new legal consequence to that completed conduct. Corrective purpose and a still-pending appeal do not supply the missing direction.

**Alternative later-conduct path:** Disclosure, use, evidentiary reception and refusal are distinct acts; a new provision could govern a genuinely later act without governing the earlier acquisition. The source supplies no exact post-amendment use or refusal date. More fundamentally, §2511(1)(c)/(d)'s ordinary disclosure/use prohibitions require the relevant knowledge concerning an interception in violation of the subsection; §2515 also contains its own chapter-violation condition. Later use cannot simply be assumed to make an acquisition unlawful that was outside coverage when made. A preserved contention identifying some independently applicable later-act prohibition must be assessed on its actual date and terms. Neither the Supreme Court's 1996 review nor the April 1994 subpoena supplies that predicate. No such later-act finding is made or required merely because review is pending.

The former oral category remains available on its own preexisting terms; rejecting retrospective use of the amendment does not extinguish that separate claim. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept this case-specific result through the activity the amended prohibition regulates, not by joining every part of the earlier *Landgraf* framework. Breyer applies the current register, with no invented *Landgraf* authorship or join.

### 4. Askin's personal Fourth Amendment interest

All eight reject two categorical grounds: statutory exclusion is not affirmative constitutional authorization, and another participant's vulnerable endpoint does not by itself establish that this speaker surrendered every legitimate expectation in his own words. An actual privacy determination must address Askin's own interest in the conversation under *Katz*, together with the actual exposure. The lower court need not declare a statutory exception unconstitutional merely to hold that a particular government acquisition violates the Constitution; a statute's failure to forbid all constitutionally prohibited conduct is not itself authorization of that conduct.

**Exposure/White path tested:** Both sides of the call were radiated over an easily intercepted link. *White* warns a speaker about reliance on an unreliable recipient; knowingly using public radio can support nonprivacy. The strongest objection to Askin's position is that protecting one speaker would constitutionally restrict interception of the same open signal that the other participant voluntarily employed. But the record establishes neither willing disclosure to the Government nor Askin's knowledge and voluntary public exposure. *White*'s participant cooperation cannot be supplied by analogy as a fact. The content obtained here is the conversation itself, not an inference drawn from exterior warmth. That distinction matters even to Kennedy and Breyer under their precise *Pinson/Myers* dissents.

**Protected-conversation path tested:** Actual words communicated privately are close to *Katz*, and *Wellons* rejects deciding the claimant's privacy solely by another person's formal or practical relation to the medium. Its strongest obstacle is that objective justification cannot be established simply from the caller's chosen endpoint or an assumed ignorance of the radio link. *Pinson* supplies no universal rule protecting every external emission, and no factual premise about technological secrecy may be invented. These limits support correcting the categorical analysis and remanding, rather than declaring this acquisition a constitutional search as a final factual/legal application.

No Associate votes here for a final finding of a search, a final justification ruling, constitutional invalidation of the former statute, or automatic suppression. If the preserved constitutional question must be reached after the statutory inquiry, the lower courts must assess the personal interest and any legally available justification on the actual record. No warrant is established; ordinary exceptions cannot be presumed or deemed forfeited solely because their papers are not in the research materials.

### 5. Prohibition, personal remedy, derivation and just cause

All eight recognize the following statutory route without finding its predicates satisfied: if Askin establishes his own covered wire/oral communication, an interception violating Title III after considering actually applicable authorization or exceptions, and compelled testimony whose reception §2515 prohibits as contents or derived evidence, *Gelbard* permits that statutory prohibition to constitute just cause under §1826(a). Here the surviving potential category is oral; electronic coverage alone would not automatically obtain §2515's wire-or-oral remedy. A grant of use immunity does not repeal that statutory evidentiary protection. The Government's occurrence response under §3504 is satisfied by admitted monitoring; it is not an admission of illegality or derivation.

The five refused inquiries must be addressed to their actual relationship to the interception. A question's subject appearing in the calls does not by itself establish exclusive derivation, an independent source, or attenuation. No blanket entitlement to refuse every question follows. Conversely, naming a remedial exception does not establish its applicability or facts. The remand permits the ordinary disposition of the preserved claim, not discovery or a hearing automatically required by a research gap.

**Constitutional remedy remains a separate, reserved question.** *Calandra*'s grand-jury no-exclusion result is not dispositive at a criminal trial. Its explanation that derivative questioning is not itself a new search, and that exclusion is a remedy ordinarily tied to use against the search victim, does expose a real objection to a nonparty immunized witness's claim. The opposing path stresses direct governmental exploitation of this speaker's own private communications in a criminal trial and the deterrence of deliberately obtaining trial evidence that way. *Gelbard* settles the statutory route, not this constitutional extension. None of the eight needs to settle that constitutional exclusion/just-cause question before the potentially dispositive statutory inquiry and still-unmade derivation and personal-interest determinations. Their reservation is not a vote that the Constitution either always or never allows the refusal.

*Leon* and *Krull* supply no automatic good-faith disposition. There was no warrant for the acquisition, and the former coverage exclusions are not themselves a statute directing a constitutionally valid search. Whether a properly presented reliance theory fits an existing exception, and whether its objective predicates are supported, must be decided rather than presumed. Independent source and attenuation likewise require their own supported grounds. No new police-technology exception is created, and no requirement to prove a subjective bad motive is inserted.

### 6. Formal disposition and limits

**All eight provisionally vote to vacate the Fourth Circuit's affirmance of the civil-contempt adjudication and remand for lawful disposition of the preserved claim under the corrected statutory and personal-interest analysis.** The former wire/electronic exclusions and the nonretroactive acquisition ruling remain binding legal conclusions. The oral category, personal constitutional interest if necessary, statutory prohibition and applicable exceptions, personal remedial entitlement, derivation, and resulting just cause require the source-specific sequence described above. The remand may result in lawful reinstatement or setting aside of the civil-contempt adjudication; it does not itself establish Askin's ultimate entitlement to refuse.

No automatic suppression, blanket testimonial privilege, dismissal of the separate criminal-contempt indictment, bar-status order, damages, new confinement, release from an unestablished present confinement, new hearing, or fixed follow-up date is ordered. The separate attorney-client and Sixth Amendment theories mentioned in the lower history are not added to the grant. The substantive unknowns remain identified and are not adjudicative defaults.
