## Common rule, application limits and lawful bases

**Announcement and this category.** Each Justice applies his or her actual join in Thomas's Wilson v. Arkansas opinion. Announcement of identity and authority contributes to reasonable dwelling entry, with supported excuses for danger, futility, escape or threatened destruction. A warrant's authorization to search does not settle the reasonable manner of entry. Wisconsin's felony-distribution category assumes its conclusion in every included case, although the actual occupant, sought evidence and execution setting can differ. General associations between drugs, weapons and disposal do not establish that every search in that category requires surprise. The rule would remove most of Wilson's work in a large class without examining circumstances.

This conclusion does not reject every categorical Fourth Amendment rule. Buie's permission to check immediately adjoining attack spaces and Maryland v. Wilson's passenger-exit direction rest on specific relationships between the intrusion and a recurring safety need. Buie's rejection of an automatic violent-offense rule for a broader sweep is the closer analogy here. An unannounced forcible dwelling entry affects occupant safety, the opportunity for voluntary admission, property and privacy; it is not merely a limited relocation during a lawful traffic stop. Chandler confirms attention to the actual governmental justification in its distinct special-needs setting, but supplies no universal prohibition of categories.

**Operational threshold.** The State must identify specific circumstances and rational inferences supporting reasonable suspicion that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile, or would inhibit effective investigation, including by allowing destruction of evidence. For the evidence-destruction ground applied here, the suspicion must connect the normal announcement and opportunity to admit officers to a threat to preservation of the evidence. It need not establish probable cause of imminent destruction or await the sound of a toilet or actual destruction. Buie's use of articulable suspicion for a limited protective step under an existing lawful arrest supports the practicable threshold by analogy. The present step regulates the execution of an independently authorized search; it does not authorize entry or a search unsupported by a warrant or another lawful basis. The analogy does not make a protective sweep and a surprise entry identical, or transfer every Buie rule to evidence searches.

The threshold is modest but reviewable. A drug label, unsupported general firearms claim or officer hunch is inadequate. The relevant facts must be known when the challenged force is initiated; later flight or successful discovery cannot retroactively supply justification. The general quantum applies to the identified execution exceptions; the decision neither fixes a universal waiting period nor resolves every factual predicate of those exceptions. In particular, it does not equate suspicion of police identity with knowledge of their purpose or hold that seeing a uniform automatically makes announcement futile. The distinct useless-gesture contention remains unneeded on these facts; no federal statutory formulation is silently displaced.

**Eight-Justice application.** The credited sequence supports a particular inference that the ruse had exposed the police and that further delay risked destruction of the cocaine sought. Pharo observed the occupant look toward the uniformed officer three or four feet away and react with surprise; he then started to move in, the door was rapidly shut, and the lock was engaged while immediate reopening was attempted. The trial judge rejected the material part of Richards's conflicting denial and inferred awareness of the uniformed officer. Recognition remains an inference on disputed evidence, not a concession of counsel. The inferences concerning surprise, exposure of the police and evasive closure, combined with the specifically sought readily disposable cocaine, furnish more than the offense category alone.

For all eight, the observation preceding Pharo's first attempt to move in already supplied a reasonable suspicion that the surprise had been lost; the rapid closure and locking strengthened it for the subsequent force. This is a legal assessment of the credited contemporaneous observations, not a new finding that the occupant intended to destroy drugs. Actual certainty of recognition, knowledge of the warrant or proven destruction is not required for this particular exception. The ordinary possibility that a person would close the door against a late-night supposed maintenance worker is the strongest contrary inference, but does not eliminate reasonable suspicion arising from the credited look toward the actual uniform, expression and conduct in this warranted cocaine search.

The grounds do not depend on saying identity preceded all force. Identity was shouted two or three seconds after closure, while an opening attempt may have been underway; kicking followed the shout. Purpose was recalled as announced with the bang of the successful opening. Neither a belated purpose announcement nor identification before threshold crossing retroactively cures an otherwise unlawful first use of force. All eight conclude that an exception already supported the immediate execution, so no such cure is needed. They do not rely on window flight, later discovery above ceiling tiles, nonexistent destruction noises or a finding of a weapon. They need not resolve the objection to supplemental testimony, because the ground rests on the original hearing's observations and findings, not the later purpose testimony.

The judge's advance refusal of requested no-knock authorization matters but does not forbid action responsive to materially different circumstances arising during execution. The officers could not simply disregard that refusal based on the same generalized drug premise. The credited exposure of police identity and rapid response occurred at the doorway and supplied a new particularized execution basis. No general doctrine governing every police-created exigency is decided, and the maintenance ruse is not treated as the occupant's consent to a forcible search.

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer use Ornelas's functional distinction by analogy: preserve historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual inferences, give grounded experience real weight, and independently assess whether those facts meet the constitutional threshold here. They do not assert that Ornelas already held that allocation for every warrant-execution question. Scalia supports the substantive rule and application while withholding assent to importing Ornelas's disputed appellate allocation; the supplied facts support affirmance for him without resolving that methodological disagreement anew.

**Stevens's reconciled application.** His Buie concurrence insists that an asserted protective justification actually explain the particular intrusion and recognizes a remand when the record and state appellate posture leave that connection unresolved. It does not make remand obligatory whenever the reviewing court rejects the lower court's general rule. His actual Robinette dissent likewise shows willingness to decide an application on an adequate record; his Maryland v. Wilson dissent join objects to categorical added restraint lacking a particular justification. Here he accepts the already made recognition finding and the pre-force observations supporting it. His own historical Richards opinion expressly relied on that finding, rather than making the result depend on an undisputed concession, proof of intended destruction, or knowledge of warrant purpose. The original hearing provides the operative foundation despite the trial court's additional, unnecessary references to later flight and threshold crossing. He therefore joins the foregoing application and affirmance. His insistence on a real connection remains satisfied by apparent exposure of police identity in this particular cocaine search, rather than by the offense category alone.

## Useless gesture, exclusion and final remedy limits

Wisconsin expressly presented both useless gesture and nonexclusion, and Richards expressly disputed their present resolution and requested remand. Neither argument is missing merely because the state majority did not reach it. Nor does either side's assertion of preservation settle every procedural issue. A respondent's defense of its judgment differs from a request to enlarge it, but that distinction alone need not resolve the scope of this grant here.

All eight need not choose an independent futility or exclusion rationale. They neither decide the cross-petition objection nor announce a new remedial rule. The valid warrant and independent-source/causation contentions must be examined if remedy becomes necessary; unlawful execution cannot automatically be equated with exclusion, and an independently supported warrant cannot automatically be equated with an independent evidentiary source or inevitable lawful discovery. Wilson left this precise constitutional remedy question open. This freeze selects no new exclusion rationale and consequently does not claim that counsel's accounts of Harris, Crews, Segura, Murray, Nix, Miller or Sabbath alone establish such a holding.

All eight affirm the existing judgment on lawful execution, rejecting its categorical reasoning. No Associate Justice supports a merits reversal, vacatur, remand for a new application, suppression order, or plea vacatur. No Justice assigns an author, supplies Stone's vote, imposes a new sentence, orders a hearing, or treats a source limitation as litigant default.

## Historical reconciliation by component and Justice

**Strongest historical evidence.** The sole historical opinion was unanimous; Stevens wrote it. Part II, 520 U.S. 391–395, rejects the drug category, protects the opportunity for voluntary admission, property and personal privacy despite a valid warrant, and adopts a modest but reviewable reasonable-suspicion threshold. Part III, 395–396, identifies the trial judge's finding that officers reasonably believed Richards had recognized them, together with the disposable cocaine, as the actual justification for immediate entry. Footnote 7 preserves execution-time judgment despite refusal of advance authorization. Footnote 3 treats Wilson's remand as evidence against a blanket drug exception, not as an obligatory remedy in every later case. There is no separate historical writing to reconcile. This evidence supports each Justice's individual rule and application commitment, not an imported simulated authorship or vote.

**Record corrections do not establish a changed operative premise.** The historical narrative at 388 inaccurately characterizes recognition as acknowledged and compresses the force sequence. The clean original-source packet controls: Richards disputed seeing the uniform; the trial judge credited the contrary inference; the first move or arm force cannot be placed after every announcement; identity and purpose were announced at different stages. Those corrections are retained throughout this handoff. They do not remove the pre-first-force look and surprised reaction or the trial finding on which the historical application expressly relied. The judgment need not be defended by the inaccurate recital. Conversely, immediate flight during completed entry and later-discovered drugs cannot justify earlier force. No new source fact is introduced by this reconciliation.

**Actual antecedents tested.** Wilson's actual unanimous announcement rule preserves individualized exceptions and leaves their quantum open, as the historical opinion's starting point did. Actual Ornelas controls the particular appellate issues it decided and supports a functional analogy, subject to Scalia's existing disagreement; it does not require another factual hearing here. Actual Robinette separates an adequate record from an unresolved application; its corrected-rule remand creates no categorical remand obligation. Actual Maryland v. Wilson leaves Kennedy and Stevens concerned with the justification for an added intrusion, but the particular execution observations here were argued and supported, unlike the unpreserved factual alternative in that case. Chandler's bodily-testing holding does not alter a warranted dwelling-entry exception. None supplies a changed legal predicate requiring a different result for any Associate.
