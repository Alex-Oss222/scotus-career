## Threshold and remedy components

**Every Associate would reach the old prohibition's constitutionality.** Chapter 419 expressly revives prior law if the McKenna judgment is reversed. The officials actively seek that reversal against the same party's asserted nomination right. This concrete legal contingency defeats the argument that replacement legislation has eliminated the dispute. City of Mesquite, 455 U.S. 283, 288–289 and notes 9–11, separates voluntary cessation from mootness and the need for injunctive relief. Stevens authored that holding and O'Connor joined; for the remaining Associates it is controlling pre-divergence law, applied rather than attributed to a nonexistent earlier personal vote. Norman's election-mootness rule also prevents the completed 1994 election alone from disposing of this continuing restriction.

The strongest alternative is mootness with appropriate treatment of the appellate judgment because the new law permits some simultaneous nominations. It fails here because the enacted restoration condition makes relief from the old judgment consequential, rather than an advisory evaluation of a permanently superseded restriction. The new written-chair-consent, ballot-position, single-name and party-status requirements are not all independently before the Court. No Justice treats compliance with them as established by Dawkins's earlier consent or the DFL's absence of objection.

**All eight preserve the statutory contingency accurately.** There is no finding that a stay or mandate recall existed, no invented date of suspension, no adjudication of an intervening election or filing, and no declaration of continuous operation of either scheme. A reversal of McKenna engages chapter 419's revival condition according to its terms; an affirmance leaves the old ban unenforceable at the adjudicated scope and the conditional replacement scheme subject to its own terms. The Court need not decide an unverified intermediate stay to resolve the live merits. If assembly proposes relief allocating rights in a particular intervening filing or election, actual stay/recall history and that filing's facts become necessary. None of the present commitments authorizes that additional adjudication.

## Shared governing law and genuinely competing merits paths

All eight apply Anderson as carried by Burdick: examine the actual burden, precise interests and their connection. Severe restrictions demand a narrowly drawn compelling justification; reasonable nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily may rest on important regulatory interests. Neither a severe-burden label nor a least-restrictive-means obligation follows from the mere existence of a party-association claim.

**Path A — reverse and sustain the old prohibition in this application.** The restriction limits an official ballot nomination, while leaving this party able to endorse and campaign for Dawkins, voters able to vote for him, and the party able to nominate another eligible candidate. On the complete system supplied, the burden is significant but not severe. The State can preserve coherent nomination channels, distinct ballot choices and stable party identities through a general prohibition without proving that this particular consensual nomination already caused fraud. Burdick, Storer and Munro support preventive administration; Colorado Republican regulates expenditure restrictions on the party's own expression and does not convert the official ballot into a general expressive forum. The best objection is that an actual qualifying nominee is being excluded from the party's line even though there is no added candidate, consent is present and signature rules already screen weak nominations. This path answers that objection only by identifying the limited ballot burden and proportionate general nomination-system interests; it cannot rest on an interest in protecting incumbents from competition or call this nomination a fictional protest vote.

**Path B — affirm the as-applied constitutional judgment.** Choosing a willing, otherwise eligible and sufficiently supported standard bearer is a central party function. Another candidate or an endorsement without the party nomination does not furnish the same associational choice. Even if the burden is not assigned the severe label, the asserted interests do not adequately fit this consensual combination: no sore loser is being restored, the major party does not object, existing support requirements apply, and the additional designation does not add another individual to the race. Norman, Tashjian and Eu make actual association and precise fit matter. The best objection is that reasonable election rules may use general lines and anticipatory judgments without case-specific proof, and there is no entitlement to maximize a party's influence. This path accepts that premise but finds the complete bar insufficiently connected to the actual dangers asserted here. It does not require separate party-line vote totals, duplicate votes or a court-designed ballot.

| Stevens | **Affirm the old-law as-applied invalidation; remand only for prospective relief consistent with the statutory development.** Adopt Path B. | Scalia | **Reverse on Path A; same bounded remand.** The official ballot may require each eligible candidate to enter through a defined nomination channel; the Constitution protects advocacy without requiring recognition of every party affiliation on that ballot. | Joined Burdick's Court opinion, including complete-access and arbitrary-message holdings; joined Kennedy's Colorado Republican concurrence protecting the party's own campaign expression even when coordinated. ## Frozen result, joins and limits

On the merits, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer support reversal on the shared contextual lesser-burden ground; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support affirmance on the shared as-applied inadequate-fit ground. The latter three can join that ground without a uniform severe-burden declaration. All eight support the live-controversy and conditional-enactment limitations above. These are non-Stone counts only and establish no Court judgment or authorship.

No new election, retrospective filing adjudication, duplicate-vote right, compulsory ballot design, invalidation of the two-major-party prohibition, or independent approval of chapter 419's other conditions is supported. Any separate writing should perform the substantive functions recorded in the table: explain the inadequacy of the fit, or preserve the distinction between protected party campaign expression and official nomination machinery. A separate writing is not required merely because those different emphases exist.
