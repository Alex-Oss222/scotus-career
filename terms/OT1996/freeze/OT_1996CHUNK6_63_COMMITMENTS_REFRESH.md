## Concrete source premises and evidentiary qualifications

**Individual contact.** The opposition's p.13 account cites Feldman's trial testimony at Tr.22–23 for seeing a Welcome to Edmond display in 1988 and Tr.27–28 for calling the City Manager to object to the Christian symbol. This is a precise filed description of trial testimony, not a reproduced complete transcript. The district's historical account that no complaint arose before 1992 conflicts with this account; the Court need not resolve the date of the first complaint to find a present personal connection.

Opposition p.20 specifically represents that Feldman personally received the cross-bearing seal on his monthly City water bill and experienced exclusion. It is counsel's individualized account, not a separately reproduced bill, sworn affidavit or quoted district finding. The appellate finding that every City utility bill carries the seal corroborates the described mechanism, but does not alone prove who receives a bill. The district's actual posttrial memorandum, Appendix 20a–21a, finds the four merits plaintiffs were taxpayers whose taxes ultimately paid for the seal and that at least some plaintiffs' behavior was affected. Its behavioral finding names neither the persons nor the conduct. This Court does not assign that unnamed conduct to Feldman or anyone else by inference.

The jurisdictional application accepts the specific, uncontradicted individualized monthly-delivery account as an accurate published recitation of the litigated record, considered with the cited personal-encounter testimony, actual posttrial findings and established continuing municipal use. This is a material source premise under Engine §14, not a rule that a lawyer's unsupported assertion alone satisfies the proof required after trial. The complete trial transcript was not recovered in these papers; that is not evidence that the litigants failed to prove contact. If actual source review contradicts Feldman's personal recurring receipt or shows it was not a record fact, R1-A requires renewed assessment. No new affidavit, production demand or litigant default is invented.

Robinson's, Battles's and Miller's identities, beliefs and objections are supported, but the recovered material does not identify a separate recurring encounter, avoidance route, extra expense or disrupted civic transaction for each. None is manufactured from the seal's prevalence. No jurisdictional holding depends on the district's taxpayer theory, the City's failure to contest standing, the opposition's jurisdictional characterization, or a newly invented avoidance requirement.

**Completed trial and disputed purpose.** Eight witnesses testified at the May 24–25, 1994 bench trial. The district found a secular historical/identification purpose, no impermissible effect and no entanglement. The Tenth Circuit reversed on effect without deciding purpose or entanglement. The purpose finding is not an undisputed agreement: respondents cite contest materials, designer testimony with historical and contemporary meanings, councilmember testimony and Feldman's earlier complaint against the City's account. The present merits grounds assume the district's historical-purpose finding for decisional purposes; they neither silently reverse it nor find covert evangelizing intent.

The 1965 council minutes document official authorization, public prize funding and selection of judges, without an exclusively historical-subject requirement. Newspaper materials mention several civic subjects and church-related characteristics and explain the official-stationery/flag purpose. The designer's religious affiliation does not establish church control. The district found no evidence that a church participated in adoption or retention; these commitments accept that limit.

**Governmental adoption and design.** The circular four-quadrant design depicts the steam engine/oil derrick, Old North Tower, covered wagon with 1889, and a Latin Christian cross. Documentary examples include a property sticker, City-funded lapel pin, police patch and welcome sign. The March 26, 1993 trademark certificate identifies the City as registrant for municipal services. Together with the published findings of official use on signs, flags, uniforms, vehicles, property, utility bills, stationery, newsletters and council chambers, those facts establish the City's adoption and continuing authentication function. They do not establish that every plaintiff encountered each example.

Comparative visibility, graphical prominence and the ultimate message remain contested merits matters. These commitments rely on the discernible cross occupying its own identifying quadrant and the accepted official functions, not an independent graphic measurement, a finding that it dominates the other three quadrants, or a neutral validator's supposed determination of constitutional effect. No additional motto, rays, compulsory profession, threat, excluded civic benefit or religious qualification is found.

## Exact propositions and joins

**R1-A — Concrete contact and a live prospective claim.** A plaintiff personally subjected to an ongoing governmental communication alleged to identify civic authority with a particular faith may challenge that practice when the record supports his actual or imminent recurring contact, a personal objection to that direct contact, and relief capable of ending the identified use. Mere residence, awareness that government uses a religious symbol, abstract disagreement, taxpayer status or jurisdictional noncontest does not suffice. Feldman's specific personal-display testimony account and individualized monthly-bill recitation, considered with the actual posttrial findings and established continuing municipal communication, support this challenge on the stated source premise. The City supplies the communication, and an injunction against the offending official use redresses his recurring exposure. No proof of a purchased alternative route, monetary avoidance expense or denial of a government service is categorically required. The threshold does not establish that the symbol is unconstitutional. **All eight join.**

R1-A does not treat the 1988 encounter alone as sufficient prospective injury or turn subjective offense at someone else's private belief into standing. It rests on the direct, continuing personal municipal communication, with the concrete historical encounter supporting the claimed relationship. It neither adopts general municipal-taxpayer standing nor decides the other three merits plaintiffs' separate standing where unnecessary to the same prospective relief. Orza receives no merits standing determination. Lujan governs the personal-interest and proof requirements; Ingebretsen confirms that supported impending exposure can be cognizable without completed coercion, but its school setting is not a municipal-seal holding.

**R1-B — Actual governmental attribution.** An emblem officially selected and adopted to authenticate the City's own ordinary acts is governmental expression even though a private resident initially designed it. The contest authorization, City uses and City trademark establish that relationship here. Neither private design nor a generally civic purpose turns the City's adopted seal into independently sponsored private speech. Pinette, Chabad and Rosenberger therefore provide no private-access immunity. **All eight join.**

**R2 — Contextual effects.** Under Lemon's effects inquiry and Allegheny's contextual affiliation analysis, the City may not communicate official preference for Christianity through this discernible Christian cross used as an identifying part of its continuing municipal seal. The informed observer considers the composite design, history, actual official uses and absence of church control together. Even assuming the district's historical-purpose finding, the three secular quadrants and benign witnesses' reactions do not remove the faith-specific identification in this setting. No independent coercion showing is required; neither religious imagery in isolation nor a tally of offended or comfortable observers is the test. **Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the rule, explanation and application. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join R2's methodology.**

**R3 — Independently sufficient official-sectarian-identification ground.** Government may acknowledge religion's historical role, but Edmond's deliberate use of the visible Christian cross as an identifying quadrant of its own continuing badge of civil authority, pervasively employed in the ordinary governmental functions described here, exceeds incidental historical acknowledgment. The constitutional defect lies in making the present civic institution itself bear that sectarian identification. It does not depend on an adjudicated evangelical purpose, church control, a proselytizing inscription, a compulsory profession, legal exclusion from civic benefits, or graphic predominance over every other quadrant. **All eight expressly join this bounded rule, explanation and application.**

R3 does not prohibit every historical religious reference, place name, depiction of a particular historic building, inherited coat of arms, holiday display or privately sponsored religious symbol on public property. Its combined predicates are this recognizable sectarian symbol, actual governmental adoption as an institutional identifier, and permanent, pervasive authentication of present municipal business. Ownership alone or an observer's mistake is insufficient. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept this specific official-identification limit without R2's observer test, a school-pressure analogy to adults, or a ban on all noncoercive governmental acknowledgments. The other five adopt R3 directly as an independent sufficient ground; no Marks aggregation or presumed broader-to-narrower join supplies their votes.

**R4 — Review and fitted relief.** Affirm the granted Establishment Clause merits judgment and permit appropriate declaratory and prospective relief against this continuing official seal use, with lawful implementation below. No replacement artwork, fixed redesign deadline, destruction of historic materials, removal of private religious speech, individual damages, immunity result or independent fee award is prescribed. The district's historical-purpose finding can be accepted without sustaining this effects/identification result. The Court need not resolve a universal standard for reviewing all Establishment Clause constitutional facts; no credibility finding or contested exact dimension supplies the reversal of the district court's legal conclusion here. The holding creates no broad Rule 52 exception. Purpose and entanglement remain unnecessary independent grounds, and Lemon is not replaced. **All eight join.**

## Adversarial tests and reasons the merits commitments remain unchanged

**Jurisdictional dismissal or a new proof remand.** Its strongest legal case is the distinction between personal concrete injury and offense at government conduct, coupled with Lujan's litigation-stage proof requirement. A single encounter years before judgment, taxpayer status, undefined effects on some plaintiffs and the City's silence would not establish this prospective case. The new record supplies what the original snapshot described too generally: a specific cited encounter and an individualized recurring-bill account consistent with the proved continuing municipal channel and the posttrial setting. All eight accept that account as a source-supported description of the record, not a replacement for proof. No fresh procedural remand is justified solely because modern retrieval lacks the full transcript. The analysis neither calls a jurisdictional concession binding nor attributes unspecific behavioral findings to Feldman. If the individualized account were materially contradicted, the threshold would have to be revisited.

**Reach every plaintiff on residence, noncontest or taxes.** All eight reject this broader route. Feldman supplies the necessary concrete connection for the shared prospective challenge, so the Court need not decide whether each of the other three independently proved equivalent contact or whether the district's taxpayer reasoning furnishes another sufficient route. No need to decide those questions licenses an award on any claimant's separate personal remedy. Orza remains outside the merits.

**Sustain the seal as historic civic acknowledgment with trial deference.** The newly recovered material makes this argument more concrete: the district actually heard witnesses and found historical purpose, and the City presented a composite civic design with no church control. It is not enough merely to cite government ownership. Each Justice's contrary analysis accepts the purpose finding for decision, considers all four quadrants and distinguishes a permissible acknowledgment from making a present civic institution bear a visible sectarian identifier throughout routine governmental activity. The City's documentary selection, trademark and continuing authentication use reinforce a predicate already essential to the original merits commitment. The new records therefore do not change the judgment or merits joins. They remove the original shorthand suggesting all relevant perceptions or historical accounts were undisputed.

**Invalidate on illicit purpose, overpowering graphic prominence, church control or absence of all earlier complaints.** None is an adopted ground. Purpose is genuinely disputed but unnecessary; the district's no-church-participation finding is retained; exact comparative visibility was not neutrally measured; and the first-complaint chronology has competing record support. The cross's accepted presence as its own recognizable identifying quadrant is enough for the stated application without deciding that it visually dominates the seal. If a later proposed opinion requires more specific dimensions, subjective intent, historical exclusivity or individual encounters with particular exhibits, its proponents need exact supporting sources and affected refreshed joins.

**Treat actual Ingebretsen as an automatic merits or standing answer.** All eight refuse. Its standing holding supports a concrete personal-exposure inquiry and distinguishes standing from illegality. Its official-school-worship and school-pressure grounds have different coalitions and school-specific predicates. The five R2 Justices rely on existing display law and contextual affiliation as well as their actual attribution commitments; the other three rely on the independently retained official-sectarian-identification boundary. None turns the school-pressure holding into coercion at an adult water-bill transaction, and none infers invalidity merely from standing.
