### 115. Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash, No. 96-454

The $31,875 figure is the debtors' wholesale estimate, calculated as 75 percent of a $42,500 retail estimate, with testimony about dealer reconditioning and commissions. It is not a proved foreclosure recovery after separately quantified disposition expenses. The lower majority expressly records the absence of evidence of disposition costs. Associates proposed $41,000; the debt was $41,171.01. No Supreme Court dollar finding follows.

The preserved question compares the first sentence's creditor-interest language with the second sentence's valuation-purpose and proposed-use command, in the retained-truck option under §1325(a)(5)(B). A creditor-specific liquidation market and a comparable-property acquisition market must each be evaluated on their own statutory case. Timbers' bar on compensation for delayed foreclosure does not itself decide the confirmation market. Dewsnup, Nobelman, and BFP govern different provisions and settings.

The lower determination that Associates lacked a perfected interest in the lease earnings was not appealed. No business-income capitalization method was argued. Neither is a new issue here. Reversal requires valuation and consequential plan reconsideration, not an ordered liquidation, automatic confirmation, or a new interest-rate ruling.

### 117. Mazurek v. Armstrong, No. 96-1104

The bounded primary opinion gives August 27, 1996, rather than the packet's September 11. It vacated denial of preliminary relief and remanded; it did not issue a final invalidation. The ruling addresses the physician-only restriction enacted in Chapter 321, 1995 Montana Session Laws, HB 442, as applied to Cahill's previously approved supervised work. Other challenged provisions and the separate bill-of-attainder theory below are outside this granted disposition and remain undecided.

The District Court's suggestion that purpose cannot be questioned if any approving legislator might have a health motive is incorrect under the governing objective inquiry. The Ninth Circuit's purpose/substantial-obstacle formulation also omits material branches of actual Casey. Full assessment includes the protected baseline, objective purpose, attributable burden and stable affected population, severe and meaningful interference tiers, competent clinical evidence and safeguards, and matched relief. Absence of a substantial obstacle is not conclusive. Opposition to abortion, professional targeting, or lack of a new study is not conclusive the other way.

The record permits review of the legal framework without a final finding about purpose, the relevant population, travel, capacity, cost, medical justification or an adequate less burdensome alternative. Actual supervised practice is a concrete comparison for examination, not a proved constitutional entitlement to an individual license. The pending interim order's precise duration is not established. A remand leaves ordinary authority over existing orders and ordinary equitable prerequisites intact; it neither automatically dissolves nor indefinitely continues an unknown order.
