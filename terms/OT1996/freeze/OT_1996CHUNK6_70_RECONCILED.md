## Reconciled disposition and proposition-level joins

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each **vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand for further district-court proceedings identifying and assessing the actual specific claims**. All eight directly join B1–B6 with the scope clarification and limits below. Their agreement neither establishes a particular Title IV-D right nor orders blanket dismissal, a specified payment, damages, a statewide staffing or computer redesign, or an approved structural injunction.

**B1 — Individual right, not statutory noncompliance alone.** A claimant must identify the particular provision conferring the asserted individual federal right: it must be intended to benefit the claimant, impose a binding obligation, and define the asserted interest sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than leave it vague or amorphous. Text, statutory setting and the actual claimed entitlement matter. A beneficial purpose, mandatory administrative direction or funding condition alone is not the right.

**B2 — Aggregate and general administrative entitlements fail.** Title IV-D's substantial-compliance measure evaluates aggregate state performance and triggers federal oversight and funding consequences; it does not entitle every beneficiary to compel the whole program to reach that administrative benchmark. A State can meet an aggregate benchmark while failing a particular person's needs, and an independently existing individual right is not extinguished by aggregate compliance. The same asserted general entitlement cannot be revived by calling it a right to sufficient agency staffing or satisfactory computerized administration as such.

Section 654(3)'s single-unit/staffing organization direction does not identify a beneficiary's personal entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload or staffing-level remedy. Former and amended §654(16), read with their associated provisions and §654a's management/performance requirements, establish the administrative system; their detail does not make general computer administration an individual right. These conclusions retain the substance of the historical Court's rejection of generalized staffing and computer-services rights. They are not mere reservations leaving those generalized claims open on remand.

The complaint's shortages, backlogs and recordkeeping allegations describe asserted causes of failed collections. The available source does not establish a separately pleaded claim to every standardized data requirement, a confidentiality right, one exact staff formula or a missed individual transmission deadline. A genuinely distinct provision-specific protection, if actually asserted, must be examined under B1 at the applicable time; it is neither preapproved nor foreclosed merely by the name of the administrative section containing it. The statutory ground suffices to reject the general theory. No independent disposition of every clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10, no universal prohibition on regulatory elaboration of an established statutory right, and no rule that every qualitative standard defeats judicial competence is adopted without the necessary text and actual claim.

**B3 — Foreclosure remains a separate inquiry.** An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive, incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D's federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions are not alone such an exclusive scheme. Their administrative complexity does not substitute for an incompatible personal remedy; Sea Clammers and Smith involved materially different detailed remedial arrangements. This rejects chapterwide foreclosure without deciding a hypothetical provision-specific remedy or dispensing with defendant and relief requirements.

**B4 — Plan placement is not the answer.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 forbid deeming a provision unenforceable merely because it appears among state-plan requirements. Their applicable pending-action provisions and their express savings of the specified §671(a)(15) nonenforceability result remain. They do not create every asserted right. Actual Suter's provision-specific rights and implied-action rulings, including its separate case-plan remand, govern at their real scopes; no broader historical state-plan theory is imported.

**B5 — Concrete claims and operative statutory versions remain for adjudication.** Madrid's allegation that collected support was not distributed implicates former §657(b)(1) and cannot be erased by the broad prayer. It is not a finding of an enforceable right, violation or amount. The authentic former text concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then when the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not create a new $50 entitlement for every installment regardless of timing. Subsection (b)'s stated fiscal-year scope and subsection (d)'s separate foster-care distribution rules remain; the present record resolves none of Madrid's relevant payment, eligibility or exception facts.

The lower court must identify each claimed provision, conduct and remedy, its version and effective date. Enactment, state-plan effectiveness, legislative or constitutional grace periods, and implementation deadlines are distinct. The 1996 general state-plan rule, residual enactment rule and separate grace periods may not be compressed into one universal date. The old computer implementation deadline was amended; the 1996 compilation has distinct October 1, 1997 and October 1, 2000 requirements, with the specified extension for delayed federal rulemaking. Section 654a(g)'s separate October 1, 1998 effective rule and limited local-court-processing exception are not present April 1997 mandates by virtue of appearing in the compilation. The supplemental model's precise date analysis is preserved; no Arizona implementation fact, current deadline default or premature application is inferred.

**B6 — Remedy follows the proved right.** The district court must identify the actual claims and determine any right, violation, proper defendant and lawful relief. General administrative failures may bear on proof or a fitted remedy for an established individual right but do not themselves authorize structural supervision. No implied action directly under Title IV-D is decided through the §1983 inquiry. The unpreserved Eleventh Amendment and request to overrule Thiboutot remain outside the stated review; their exclusion is not a universal decision on every possible future defendant-specific issue.
