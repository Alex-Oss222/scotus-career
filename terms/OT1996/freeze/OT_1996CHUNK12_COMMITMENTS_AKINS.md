## Defined components and bounded alternatives

### K1. Article III reach: injury, causation and redress

**Affirm-standing path.** These politically active voters seek particular campaign-financing information for their own electoral participation, rather than a declaration that the government must generally obey the law. The asserted denial is continuing: no source establishes that the sought information has already been disclosed. Public Citizen permits a concrete informational-access injury even when many other people could make the same request. Lujan requires a personal concrete interest and support appropriate to the stage, not an injury unique to one citizen. The supported connection to the voters' electoral activity is sufficient here; the precise transactions may be unknown because of the alleged nondisclosure. This conclusion neither makes filing an administrative complaint an injury nor presumes the ultimate statutory entitlement correct.

The FEC's legal interpretation is an actual ground of the political-committee dismissal. Correcting it can restore the statutory process for obtaining information still withheld. The Commission's retained authority to make lawful findings and enforcement choices does not make that remedial link categorically speculative. Bennett requires examination of the actual link; its announced agency-compliance fact is not imported here. Lujan's procedural-right rule permits review tied to a concrete interest without certainty of a changed ultimate outcome. Steel Co. is different because the requested reports there were already current and no remedy repaired the completed injury. Here useful information remains the object of relief.

**Jurisdictional-dismissal path.** The competing view treats the supported interest in understanding and opposing AIPAC's influence as the public's generalized interest in informed electoral oversight, insufficiently particular to these litigants. The injury is not established merely by alleging that a regulator should classify and regulate a third party differently. On this view, Public Citizen's denial of specifically requested records and access does not remove the requirement to establish these voters' own concrete use of the information the different FECA process may produce. Congressional permission to challenge dismissal does not supply that connection. This is a legal assessment of the supported personal stake, not a new finding about undisclosed records, absence of an affidavit or failure to meet an invented document requirement.

**Strongest objections and limits.** The strongest objection to affirming standing is that almost any voter could convert an interest in enforcing election law into an information claim. The answer is the particular legally protected information sought for actual electoral activity, not complaint status alone. The strongest objection to dismissing is that numerical breadth and nondisclosure-caused inability to identify each affected candidate do not make a real access injury abstract; Public Citizen and Lujan's preserved congressional power require a substantive distinction. The dismissal proponents maintain that the present connection remains an undifferentiated electoral interest, rather than declaring every widely shared informational injury nonjusticiable. No row uses Findley's past candidacy or an agency failure to challenge it as a substitute for Article III.

The strongest redressability objection is the sequence of future Commission action, third-party compliance and useful disclosure. An affirm-standing vote accepts likely redress through the actual statutory channel, not a guarantee that the Commission must prosecute or that any possible settlement without the desired information is unlawful. The separate corporate-prohibition nonenforcement ground cannot be silently reassigned as the actual basis of the political-committee dismissal.

### K2. Express review and available remedy

Section 437g(a)(8) permits an aggrieved administrative complainant to challenge a dismissal within sixty days. Its separate 120-day inaction trigger is not the route invoked here. A court may declare the dismissal contrary to law and may direct the Commission to conform within thirty days; failure to conform may permit the complainant's own action addressing the original violation. These permissions do not automatically command prosecution, a penalty, a settlement or wholesale disclosure.

This express channel allows correction of the legal premise actually used. Ordinary nonenforcement discretion does not insulate that premise from all judicial review. Conversely, statutory review does not erase lawful agency factfinding and discretion or authorize courts to substitute an unexercised discretionary rationale. Brown Group distinguishes enacted enforcement authority from the separate constitutional minimum. Bennett requires the claim to fit its actual statutory channel. Thomas's Legi-Tech opinion preserves actual authorized Commission decisionmaking and statutory prerequisites; it supplies neither an automatic enforcement remedy nor a generic bar to review.

The competing path sustaining a lawful discretionary dismissal would require a legally permissible ground actually supporting this dismissal. The separate close membership question for the §441b corporate prohibition is not, without more, that ground for the political-committee decision. The present commitment does not invalidate the separate corporate enforcement choice.

### K3. Major purpose and genuinely covered financing

**Corrected-construction path.** Section 431(4)(A) identifies a group receiving contributions exceeding $1,000 during a calendar year or making expenditures exceeding $1,000 during a calendar year. A group's payment to a candidate must be analyzed through the relevant definitions; the definition does not literally say that every group making any kind of contribution above $1,000 is automatically a political committee. Covered candidate-connected payments can constitute expenditures under §431(9), subject to each applicable exclusion. Threshold amount, calendar-year aggregation, candidate connection and exclusions remain independent predicates.

Buckley's major-purpose discussion addresses the danger of sweeping issue discussion and independent expression into election regulation. MCFL likewise concerns independent spending, while recognizing the distinct treatment of actual contributions. Those decisions do not establish an organization-wide exemption for otherwise covered candidate financing merely because lobbying occupies most of the organization's budget. The Commission cannot use that categorical premise to terminate the inquiry. Colorado Republican requires actual cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion where the asserted statutory contribution theory requires it; lobbying, shared policy views or political sympathy do not supply that connection.

**Strongest competing construction.** Political-committee status entails organizational and associational burdens beyond disclosure of one transaction. Buckley's language and MCFL's concern with the full regulatory structure can therefore be read to require a candidate-controlled or primarily electoral organization even when some transactions are candidate-connected. This is a serious constitutional-avoidance argument, not merely a policy preference. The corrected-construction path answers that actual candidate resources have the constitutionally material connection missing from independent issue speech, while preserving genuine as-applied objections to particular obligations and preserving exclusions. It does not uphold every organizational burden whenever $1,001 is alleged or require disclosure of every contributor regardless of the statute.

The extent of a judicial constitutional narrowing is for the court to determine. Deference to agency administration does not make the agency's reading of Buckley or MCFL conclusive. This commitment establishes no universal denial of Chevron deference to the FEC on genuinely delegated statutory questions.

### K4. Membership, other exclusions and remand

The Commission must apply the effective provisions separately before a final threshold or organizational determination. Section 431(9)(B)(iii) excludes qualifying communications to members, stockholders or executive/administrative personnel by an organization not primarily organized to influence federal nomination or election. It nonetheless requires reporting of costs directly attributable to express advocacy of a clearly identified candidate when those costs exceed $2,000 for an election, except a communication primarily devoted to subjects other than that express advocacy; the specified §434 reporting schedules govern. Exclusion from expenditure status therefore does not necessarily erase this distinct reporting proviso.

Section 441b(b)(2) separately excludes the specified corporate communications to stockholders, executive/administrative personnel and their families, labor communications to members and families, the specified nonpartisan registration/get-out-the-vote activity, and establishment, administration and solicitation of a separate segregated fund by the named types of organizations. Its ordinary-course bank-loan qualification remains distinct. Section 431(8)(B)(vi) and (9)(B)(v) separately cross-reference payments that would not constitute expenditures under §441b(b). The §431(9)(B)(iii) $2,000 proviso is not transplanted into those other exclusions.

No final AIPAC membership finding, actual coordination finding, amount finding after exclusions, comprehensive classification, compulsory donor disclosure or penalty is supplied. No later proposed membership rule is operative law. Correcting the major-purpose premise permits a remand under the actual statutory provisions; it does not decide that any original violation is proved. A narrower remand withholding a general major-purpose ruling remains available where a Justice finds the organizational or transaction distinction material.

## Justice-specific commitments and adversarial comparisons

### Stevens

**Judgment:** Affirm the en banc judgment recognizing standing and rejecting the categorical major-purpose ground, with further Commission proceedings confined by K2–K4. **Grounds joined:** K1's affirm-standing path, K2, K3's corrected construction and K4. No final violation or penalty.

His Public Citizen majority join supports a real informational interest without requiring numerical exclusivity. His Brown Group and Bennett joins preserve constitutional injury while respecting the actual enforcement channel. His Steel Co. statutory-threshold concurrence does not require avoiding standing here: jurisdiction can and should be established before these contested merits under current Court law. His Colorado Republican dissent accepted realistic contribution-channel regulation, and his MCFL dissent supported the statutory corporate safeguards; neither makes every lobbying payment a contribution. The strongest opposing construction is the risk of regulating an entire issue organization because of isolated spending. He answers it by requiring actual covered financing and applying exclusions rather than giving larger lobbying budgets a categorical exemption. His join excludes status based on policy sympathy, a blanket donor-disclosure order or compulsory enforcement on unproved facts.

### O'Connor

**Judgment:** Affirm standing and reviewability; affirm rejection of a categorical major-purpose exemption for genuinely covered candidate financing; remand under K4. **Grounds joined:** K1's affirm-standing path and K2–K4 at the stated limited level. She does not adjudicate the constitutionality of every obligation that might follow a final political-committee classification.

Her Steel Co. concurrence expressly distinguishes a continuing reporting deprivation, and her Bennett join rejects automatic defeat by retained agency choice. Her own Colorado Republican Part I insists on an actual candidate connection. Her MCFL separate opinion distinguishes reasonable campaign disclosure from the additional organizational restraints, making the broader organizational-burden objection substantive. K3 is acceptable because it rejects an across-the-board major-purpose barrier without deciding that every associated restraint is valid in every application. The narrower alternative is to defer the entire construction until membership is resolved. She finds correction of the premise that actually ended the inquiry appropriate now, while retaining membership and concrete constitutional applications for lawful reconsideration. She rejects blanket attribution of all lobbying costs and any conversion of the membership reporting proviso into political-committee status by itself.

### Scalia

**Judgment:** Vacate the en banc merits judgment and remand for jurisdictional dismissal. **Sufficient ground:** K1's jurisdictional-dismissal path. No operative vote on K2–K4's statutory merits, and no affirmance of the FEC's construction on the merits.

His Lujan Parts II, III-A and IV require the plaintiffs' own concrete stake and stage-appropriate support; Congress's generalized enforcement permission cannot supply it. His Steel Co. joins require deciding that threshold before this contested statutory interpretation. He regards the supported interest in learning and opposing AIPAC's electoral influence as insufficiently particular to these voters, rather than treating the filing of a regulatory complaint as an access injury. Public Citizen is the strongest contrary authority: a request for particular information can be concrete even if widely shared. He distinguishes its denied direct access from the present effort to require regulatory classification and disclosure by a third party; the voters' generalized electoral use does not, in his view, bridge that distinction. Bennett prevents treating agency discretion alone as fatal, so he does not rely on such a categorical redressability rule. His MCFL and Colorado Republican speech positions would make organizational burdens a serious merits issue, but they are not a merits holding without jurisdiction.

### Kennedy

**Judgment:** Affirm the standing and legal-review rulings; affirm rejection of a major-purpose barrier for actual candidate transfers and genuine contribution conduits; vacate any broader instruction necessarily treating all coordinated organizational expression alike, and remand under K4. **Grounds joined:** K1's affirm-standing path, K2, K4, and the narrower part of K3 just stated. This is not a vote sustaining the Commission's blanket construction.

His Lujan joins retain Congress's ability to protect concrete interests, and his Bennett/Steel Co. positions permit review of an ongoing informational denial with a useful statutory route. His Public Citizen separate writing concerned statutory construction and presidential nomination, not an announced rejection of informational standing. His actual Colorado Republican concurrence distinguishes an association's own campaign expression from candidate cash and genuine conduits even when cooperation occurs; that published distinction prevents a blanket join in K3's treatment of every coordinated expenditure. The strongest broader path is Buckley's recognized candidate connection, sufficient for actual transfers and conduits. The strongest exemption argument is the burden on an issue association's own speech. He requires the Commission to identify what these payments actually are after membership exclusions before that latter constitutional question is answered. No independence is inferred merely from AIPAC's lobbying purpose, and no political-party-specific holding is automatically extended to every nonprofit.

### Souter

**Judgment:** Affirm the en banc standing and corrected-construction judgment, with bounded remand under K2–K4. **Grounds joined:** K1's affirm-standing path and K2–K4; no automatic committee classification or enforcement order.

His Lujan joins distinguish the plaintiffs' concrete interests from abstract supervision; the specific information sought for electoral participation satisfies that distinction here. His Brown Group opinion insists on separating actual statutory authorization from Article III, and his Bennett join permits a meaningful remedy despite a later lawful choice. His Steel Co. join in Stevens's statutory discussion supplies no need to bypass the reachable standing issue. The O'Connor framework he joined in Colorado Republican preserves inquiry into actual candidate resources while respecting independent expression. The strongest contrary major-purpose reading invokes organizational burdens beyond isolated spending. He rejects a universal exemption but preserves each statutory predicate and an actual constitutional challenge to particular burdens. Mere candidate sympathy cannot replace coordination, and the three different membership provisions cannot be merged.

### Thomas

**Judgment:** Vacate the en banc merits judgment and remand for jurisdictional dismissal. **Sufficient ground:** K1's jurisdictional-dismissal path. He makes no merits determination approving the major-purpose rule or rejecting the express statutory review channel in a case with standing.

His controlling Lujan joins and Steel Co. joins require a demonstrated personal constitutional stake before statutory merits. The supported account supplies active participation in public political debate and a desire for generally useful election information, but he does not regard those interests as sufficiently particular to these plaintiffs to authorize supervision of the Commission's regulation of AIPAC. The strongest contrary path is Public Citizen's informational-access rule; he distinguishes the requested regulatory classification and prospective third-party reports from denied direct access, while accepting that shared information injuries are not invariably abstract. His unanimous Bennett join forecloses a general rule that third-party choice defeats redressability; that issue is not the sufficient dismissal ground. His Legi-Tech opinion preserves actual agency authority and statutory prerequisites, but express complaint review does not dispense with the constitutional injury requirement. His Colorado Republican expression position remains a potential merits concern, not an additional merits vote.

### Ginsburg

**Judgment:** Affirm the en banc standing and corrected-construction judgment; remand for the separate transaction and membership predicates. **Grounds joined:** K1's affirm-standing path and K2–K4.

Her Brown Group and Bennett joins require an authorized route and a personal stake, both supplied by the particular election information sought and this dismissal-review channel. Her Steel Co. concurrence favors narrow authorization-and-avoidance analysis but does not establish a categorical jurisdiction bypass; she can resolve standing here without treating the statutory claim as already won. Her Colorado Republican dissent permits regulation of actual contribution channels while its institutional party reasoning cannot itself classify a lobbying organization. The major-purpose construction's strongest objection is intrusive regulation of issue association; K3 answers only the categorical exemption and leaves actual obligations, exclusions and supported as-applied claims distinct. She will not join wholesale donor disclosure or treat policy advocacy as proof of coordination. The separate §441b discretion decision remains separate from the reviewed legal premise.

### Breyer

**Judgment:** Affirm the en banc standing and corrected-construction judgment, subject to K2–K4's bounded remand. **Grounds joined:** K1's affirm-standing path and K2–K4.

His Brown Group and Bennett joins support a concrete informational interest and a real remedial opportunity without certainty of ultimate agency action. His Steel Co. concurrence's exceptional merits-first conditions do not apply: the statutory issue is contested and not an inevitable same-winner disposition. Standing is therefore addressed first. His Colorado Republican join and the O'Connor framework he joined preserve independent advocacy while distinguishing actual resources supplied to candidates. The strongest agency argument is that major-purpose protection avoids costly organizational restrictions on predominantly issue-oriented groups. He accepts the burden concern but not a categorical rule allowing covered candidate financing to disappear within a larger lobbying budget. The more restrained remedy is actual reconsideration after exclusions, not judicial classification, a compelled penalty or a finding that all reported contacts were coordinated expenditures.

## Exact disposition and rationale boundaries

The two jurisdictional-dismissal commitments end merits adjudication for those Justices; they are not votes affirming the Commission's major-purpose interpretation. The six standing commitments establish neither a statutory violation nor an unconditional right to every requested record. All six support review of the legal premise actually used while preserving lawful enforcement discretion.

Kennedy's merits commitment is narrower than the other five reaching the statutory construction: he commits to the actual-transfer/genuine-conduit proposition and requires separate treatment before extending that conclusion to an association's own coordinated expression. It cannot be counted as a complete join in an opinion applying one rule to every coordinated transaction. None of the six approves a final committee classification on the supplied record.

The §431(9)(B)(iii) reporting proviso remains distinct even when a communication is excluded from expenditure classification. The distinct §441b exclusions and §431 cross-references retain their own text. The Court may correct the dismissal's legal basis and return the remaining questions through the court of appeals for lawful Commission proceedings; it may not choose an enforcement settlement, impose a penalty or assume that a later proposed membership rule has become law.
