At the Government’s request, the trial judge treated materiality as a judicial issue and instructed the jury on falsity using “material fact” language. The jury convicted. While the appeal was pending, Gaudin held that when materiality is an element, the jury must decide it.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Government presented the no-materiality argument in supplemental appellate briefing, the Eighth Circuit passed on it, and the certiorari petition presented it. Separate claims that the indictment allegation or jury instructions independently require relief were not resolved below and may be considered on remand if properly preserved.

### Issue 1: Preservation

* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Reviewability of the statutory issue does not erase any independent prejudice from the actual indictment or instructions.
### Issue 2: Materiality

* **Reasoning Below:** The Eighth Circuit treated materiality as an element and applied Gaudin.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The text covers “any” knowing false statement made for the purpose of influencing a bank and omits materiality despite express use elsewhere.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** Historical false-statement law and the severity of punishment imply a materiality limit; otherwise trivial falsehoods become felonies.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** Purpose to influence, knowledge, falsity, prosecutorial discretion, and sentencing may constrain marginal cases without adding an element.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Removing materiality does not remove the jury’s duty to find knowledge, falsity, and purpose beyond a reasonable doubt.
