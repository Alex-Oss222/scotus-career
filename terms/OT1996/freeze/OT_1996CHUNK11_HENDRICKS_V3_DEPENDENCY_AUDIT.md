The controlling rule extends the civil-intent and clearest-proof method to this commitment's punitive classification. The cumulative original operation—post-sentence confinement, reliance on prior offenses, prisonlike restraint and initial treatment deficiency sustained on the prospect of future specialized treatment despite possible treatment—establishes punishment in this application. The Record preserves the contrary original evidence and distinguishes a cumulative legal inference from an invented finding that all treatment was absent. It imposes no mandatory alternatives inquiry, successful-cure requirement, treatment protocol, general release procedure or burden shift. It does not presume rejected adequate alternatives or sham release administration.

Hendricks may not remain confined under the invalid application. The decision sets no new release date, stay, hearing schedule or factfinding remand and authorizes no alternative custody. It leaves other independently lawful routes and the validity of genuinely civil prospective applications undecided. Double Jeopardy is not reached. Part I's eight-Justice diagnostic-vocabulary discussion is expressly non-dispositive and noncontrolling; Ginsburg reserves it. Foucha, Sahhar, Addington, Heller and the bounded parole rules are not displaced by that discussion. The Record changes no roster, circuit allotment or standing practice and imports no later event.
