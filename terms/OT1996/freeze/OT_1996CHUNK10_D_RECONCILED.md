## Reconciled common grounds and limits

### Constitutional route: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Breyer

These five assume, without deciding, a concrete past informational injury fairly traceable to the Company's failure to provide timely reports. They do not reject informational injury as a category, attribute an absent-agency causation defect to CBE, or hold that all residents have standing. For this disposition, possible associational reliance on members' interests may also be assumed rather than independently resolved.

The sufficient rule is that none of the requested remedies is likely to repair the assumed past deprivation or prevent a supported ongoing or imminent personal injury on these allegations. Statutory authorization and satisfaction from enforcing public law cannot alone supply that connection. The reporting forms were current before the action commenced; the supplied complaint alleges no ongoing or imminent reporting violation. This is initial standing, not cessation during a suit that began with an adequately alleged continuing controversy.

### Statutory route: Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg

These three would hold that §11046(a)(1)(A)(iii)–(iv), read with the notice and enforcement structure, does not authorize a citizen action based solely on reporting failures fully corrected before commencement, with no alleged continuing or impending failure. The disposition leaves the constitutional standing dispute undecided as a ground of judgment.

The strongest contrary reading incorporates the mandatory filing deadlines through the references to §§11022(a) and 11023(a); unlike Gwaltney's Clean Water Act text, EPCRA does not say a defendant must be alleged presently to be in violation. Notice can end accumulating penalties, invite EPA enforcement, and encourage voluntary correction even if accrued citizen penalties remain available. Those arguments are substantial; the alternative is not dismissed as frivolous or absent from the enacted text.

The three nevertheless read completion and submission, the 60-day notice interval, and supplementary citizen enforcement together as making a citizen action unnecessary when the reporting obligation has already been performed before filing. Gwaltney's notice and supplementary-enforcement analysis supplies an analogy, not an automatic transplant of its different present-tense words. Section 11046(e)'s bar applies only when EPA **has commenced and is diligently pursuing** the specified administrative order or civil action to enforce the requirement or impose its penalty. Neither mere commencement without diligent pursuit nor agency inaction triggers that bar. Here no actual EPA enforcement bar is found; the provision's design bears on construction. The textual ambiguity also makes avoidance of an unnecessary constitutional ruling relevant.

This statutory conclusion does not erase the filing deadlines, declare belated reports timely, forgive any government-enforceable penalty, or decide liability, penalty amounts, coverage, notice compliance, or EPA discretion. Section 11045(c)(1) provides liability to the United States up to the stated maximum, and subsection (c)(3) treats each continuing day separately; neither makes the requested maximum an automatic award. The tier-II alternative incorporated in §11046(a)(1)(A)(iii) remains intact: §11022(a)(2)'s tier-I submission is unnecessary if tier-II information for the same year reaches the same recipients by the same deadline. The separate §11023(a) toxic-release requirement and its own qualifications remain distinct.

Meghrig requires respect for the enacted private cause and remedy, while its RCRA current-endangerment phrase decides no EPCRA issue by itself. Section 11046(g) preserves other existing rights without creating a new claim. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party litigation costs; it does not mandate them or convert this statutory action into compensatory damages.

### Sequencing and its distinct scopes

Scalia and Thomas support the general rejection of assumed disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested substantive claim. O'Connor and Kennedy can join that rule and its application **with their express reservation that the recognized exceptional settings do not form an exhaustive list**. Their acceptance of a qualified opinion is not a vote for an exceptionless ordering rule.

For these four, §11046(c), headed “Relief,” specifies enforcement and penalty powers in actions brought under subsection (a); it does not make every element of statutory liability a jurisdictional fact. Bell and Young preserve the difference between a substantial federal claim and its success. Whether subsection (a) reaches completed reporting violations is a substantial merits dispute. Gwaltney's statutory phrasing and lack of a decisive contested sequencing ruling do not require treating it otherwise.

Breyer agrees that the ordinary and appropriate course **in this case** is Article III first and joins the redressability ground. He does not join the separate general prohibition or a categorical §11046(c) classification used to announce it. He affirmatively allows judgment in exceptional circumstances where a genuinely difficult jurisdictional question may be reserved and the same party plainly prevails on an easily resolved merits issue. The present disputed EPCRA construction supplies no reason to invoke that exception.

Stevens and Souter regard §11046(c)'s express linkage to actions authorized by subsection (a) as a statutory threshold that may be resolved before the disputed constitutional question. Their statutory-first position also addresses the close relationship between the cause and the claimed remedies. They do not convert every unsuccessful federal claim into a jurisdictional defect, withdraw their Young joins, or endorse an unrestricted ability to decide new merits questions without Article III. Ginsburg adopts the narrower statutory-authorization and avoidance ground; she does not join their whole account of threshold ordering.

**Norton and Avrech retain their actual limits.** Norton involved the three-judge-court/appellate route and a contemporaneous decision that had already settled the merits, leaving the same practical outcome by either route. Avrech involved a court-martial-review issue and an intervening merits decision. Neither alone decides the contested EPCRA issue here. Stevens's Norton dissent established jurisdiction before reaching the merits; it is not evidence that he joined Norton's majority rationale. It also does not prove that he would reject a statutory jurisdictional ground here. No current Justice is assigned the later comparator as a date-eligible authority.

## Final Justice-specific commitments

The common analyses above apply only at the scopes stated below. Each row records a supported conclusion and its alternatives, not private dialogue or a fictional circulation history.

### John Paul Stevens

**Disposition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand directing dismissal on the statutory citizen-suit ground. Do not adopt Article III nonredressability as the sufficient ground.

**Ground and authority:** His actual Gwaltney joins support both the exclusion of wholly past citizen actions under that statute and close examination of the jurisdictional gateway; his Lujan separate position accepts concrete personal interests and resolves the statutory reach separately. His current Young join and Arizonans authorship distinguish a defeating substantive defense from absent jurisdiction and require lawful relief, but do not classify EPCRA's express gateway. He adopts the statutory construction and qualifications above.

**Strongest competing path:** The five Justices' application of the existing redressability element could avoid the contested statutory interpretation. He declines it because the direct informational deprivation and statutory sanction present a materially different question from an abstract demand for lawful government or an indirect third-party injury. Sanction and deterrence can benefit a person directly injured even when money goes to the sovereign. His meaningful-partial-relief approach does not require compensation to be the sole possible benefit. This objection is a reason not to reach the constitutional question, not an adjudicated entitlement to a penalty or injunction.

**Join scope and limits:** Supports the statutory-first analysis shared with Souter and the narrower statutory construction shared with Souter and Ginsburg. He may maintain a separate explanation criticizing the constitutional redressability rationale; that explanation has no other Associate join established here. It does not announce that every private dispute automatically satisfies Article III or that standing has affirmatively been adjudicated. He declines any general endorsement of hypothetical jurisdiction. The expanded fourth question removes the historical unpresented-issue criticism; no actual briefing fact is invented.

**Reconciliation:** Model D's constitutional route lacks a concrete changed premise sufficient to overcome the historical statutory commitment. The result is corrected in this handoff, not in the independent freeze.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Disposition and ground:** Vacate and remand directing dismissal for want of Article III standing, on the complete remedy analysis above; assume past injury and causation and leave statutory temporal reach undecided.

**Personal basis and alternative:** Her actual Lujan dissent join protects favorable reasonable inferences and concrete use interests; it does not eliminate the need for a supported threatened injury. Her Church of Scientology, Young, Brown Group, Arizonans, and Bennett joins distinguish partial practical relief, statutory authorization, and constitutional requirements. The strongest opposing inference is that repeated nonfiling, continuing annual duties, and proximity make deterrence personally useful. She accepts that theory's legal possibility but not the unpleaded ongoing or imminent violation needed on this record.

**Join scope:** Joins the constitutional remedy analysis and the qualified jurisdiction-first/classification explanation. She requires the nonexhaustive reservation above and refuses an exceptionless ordering rule. That reservation can accompany a full join; it need not be treated as judgment-only agreement. No new factfinding, amendment, or proof-document requirement is imposed.

**Reconciliation:** Substantive result matches the historical position. The provisional scope is clarified to preserve her actual full join plus reservation, rather than characterize her as necessarily withholding every general sequencing proposition.

### Antonin Scalia

**Disposition and ground:** Vacate and remand directing Article III dismissal. Adopt the complete redressability analysis and the classification of this statutory controversy as a merits issue; no EPCRA temporal-scope holding.

**Personal basis and alternative:** His controlling Lujan Parts II and IV require concrete injury and likely redress independently of citizen-suit permission; his Young, Brown Group, Arizonans, and Bennett joins preserve those boundaries. He will not adopt the statutory construction after finding Article III absent or treat citizen enforcement and litigation expenses alone as personal redress.

**Reconciliation:** No material historical departure is needed. Lujan's absent-agency application remains noncontrolling and unnecessary.

**Disposition and ground:** Vacate and remand directing dismissal for lack of Article III redressability; assume the past injury and causation. Adopt the remedy-specific constitutional analysis and reserve EPCRA temporal scope.
