## Common rule, application limits and lawful bases

**Announcement and this category.** Each Justice applies his or her actual join in Thomas's Wilson v. Arkansas opinion. Announcement of identity and authority contributes to reasonable dwelling entry, with supported excuses for danger, futility, escape or threatened destruction. A warrant's authorization to search does not settle the reasonable manner of entry. Wisconsin's felony-distribution category assumes its conclusion in every included case, although the actual occupant, sought evidence and execution setting can differ. General associations between drugs, weapons and disposal do not establish that every search in that category requires surprise. The rule would remove most of Wilson's work in a large class without examining circumstances.

This conclusion does not reject every categorical Fourth Amendment rule. Buie's permission to check immediately adjoining attack spaces and Maryland v. Wilson's passenger-exit direction rest on specific relationships between the intrusion and a recurring safety need. Buie's rejection of an automatic violent-offense rule for a broader sweep is the closer analogy here. An unannounced forcible dwelling entry affects occupant safety, the opportunity for voluntary admission, property and privacy; it is not merely a limited relocation during a lawful traffic stop. Chandler confirms attention to the actual governmental justification in its distinct special-needs setting, but supplies no universal prohibition of categories.

**Operational threshold.** For the evidence-destruction ground at issue, the State must identify specific circumstances and rational inferences supporting a reasonable suspicion that the normal announcement and opportunity to admit officers would threaten preservation of the evidence. It need not establish probable cause of imminent destruction or await the sound of a toilet or actual destruction. Buie's use of articulable suspicion for a limited protective step under an existing lawful arrest supports the practicable threshold by analogy. The present step regulates the execution of an independently authorized search; it does not authorize entry or a search unsupported by a warrant or another lawful basis. The analogy does not make a protective sweep and a surprise entry identical, or transfer every Buie rule to evidence searches.

The threshold is modest but reviewable. A drug label, unsupported general firearms claim or officer hunch is inadequate. The relevant facts must be known when the challenged force is initiated; later flight or successful discovery cannot retroactively supply justification. The decision does not set the exact threshold for each other Wilson exception, define a universal waiting period, or replace the distinct knowledge-of-purpose inquiry in a claim of useless gesture with a suspicion of police identity.

**Seven-Justice application.** The credited sequence supports a particular inference that the ruse had exposed the police and that further delay risked destruction of the cocaine sought. Pharo observed the occupant look toward the uniformed officer three or four feet away and react with surprise; he then started to move in, the door was rapidly shut, and the lock was engaged while immediate reopening was attempted. The trial judge rejected the material part of Richards's conflicting denial and inferred awareness of the uniformed officer. Recognition remains an inference on disputed evidence, not a concession of counsel. The inferences concerning surprise, exposure of the police and evasive closure, combined with the specifically sought readily disposable cocaine, furnish more than the offense category alone.

For these seven, the observation preceding Pharo's first attempt to move in already supplied a reasonable suspicion that the surprise had been lost; the rapid closure and locking strengthened it for the subsequent force. This is a legal assessment of the credited contemporaneous observations, not a new finding that the occupant intended to destroy drugs. Actual certainty of recognition, knowledge of the warrant or proven destruction is not required for this particular exception. The ordinary possibility that a person would close the door against a late-night supposed maintenance worker is the strongest contrary inference, but does not eliminate reasonable suspicion arising from the credited look toward the actual uniform, expression and conduct in this warranted cocaine search.

The grounds do not depend on saying identity preceded all force. Identity was shouted two or three seconds after closure, while an opening attempt may have been underway; kicking followed the shout. Purpose was recalled as announced with the bang of the successful opening. Neither a belated purpose announcement nor identification before threshold crossing retroactively cures an otherwise unlawful first use of force. The seven conclude that an exception already supported the immediate execution, so no such cure is needed. They do not rely on window flight, later discovery above ceiling tiles, nonexistent destruction noises or a finding of a weapon. They need not resolve the objection to supplemental testimony, because the ground rests on the original hearing's observations and findings, not the later purpose testimony.

The judge's advance refusal of requested no-knock authorization matters but does not forbid action responsive to materially different circumstances arising during execution. The officers could not simply disregard that refusal based on the same generalized drug premise. The credited exposure of police identity and rapid response occurred at the doorway and supplied a new particularized execution basis. No general doctrine governing every police-created exigency is decided, and the maintenance ruse is not treated as the occupant's consent to a forcible search.

O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer use Ornelas's functional distinction by analogy: preserve historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual inferences, give grounded experience real weight, and independently assess whether those facts meet the constitutional threshold here. They do not assert that Ornelas already held that allocation for every warrant-execution question. Scalia supports the substantive rule and application while withholding assent to importing Ornelas's disputed appellate allocation; the supplied facts support affirmance for him without resolving that methodological disagreement anew.

**Stevens's application and remand.** Stevens joins rejection of the category and the articulable-suspicion evidence-risk standard. His Buie concurrence separates a defensible general threshold from an inadequately justified application and gives the state courts their proper record-review role. His Robinette dissent and join to Kennedy's Maryland v. Wilson dissent likewise require an actual connection between the added intrusion and its asserted justification.

He credits the trial court's resolution of the conflicting recognition testimony rather than treating it as nonexistent. Nevertheless, recognition of police, surprise at a late-night ruse and closure while a purported maintenance worker begins to force entry do not themselves settle whether dispensing with announcement and an opportunity for admission would preserve evidence. The initial move to enter preceded the closure on which much of the State's argument relies. The original ruling compared identity before crossing the threshold to compliance, relied on general disposability and used later flight in its explanation. The state appellate majority's category then made the actual timing and justification unnecessary to its decision. Stevens would not convert those premises into a definitive appellate conclusion that force at every stage was justified.

His preferred disposition is reversal of the categorical federal ruling and remand to the Wisconsin Supreme Court for the actual execution inquiry on the supported record. The reviewing court must separate pre-force observations from subsequent events and identity from purpose, respect the existing credibility ruling and address properly available alternatives. He does not direct a new evidentiary hearing, hold the State forfeited its alternative grounds, establish a final entry violation, order suppression, vacate the pleas or finally set aside the conviction. If the entry is held unlawful, the separately presented exclusion question and its lawful procedural scope remain to be adjudicated.

## Useless gesture, exclusion and final remedy limits

Wisconsin expressly presented both useless gesture and nonexclusion, and Richards expressly disputed their present resolution and requested remand. Neither argument is missing merely because the state majority did not reach it. Nor does either side's assertion of preservation settle every procedural issue. A respondent's defense of its judgment differs from a request to enlarge it, but that distinction alone need not resolve the scope of this grant here.

The seven affirming Justices need not choose an independent futility or exclusion rationale. Stevens's remand preserves their appropriate consideration without deciding the cross-petition objection or announcing a new remedial rule. The valid warrant and independent-source/causation contentions must be examined if remedy becomes necessary; unlawful execution cannot automatically be equated with exclusion, and an independently supported warrant cannot automatically be equated with an independent evidentiary source or inevitable lawful discovery. Wilson left this precise constitutional remedy question open. This freeze selects no new exclusion rationale and consequently does not claim that counsel's accounts of Harris, Crews, Segura, Murray, Nix, Miller or Sabbath alone establish such a holding.

Seven affirm the existing judgment on lawful execution, rejecting its categorical reasoning; Stevens dissents from that formal affirmance and supports the bounded reversal/remand described above. No Justice assigns an author, supplies Stone's vote, imposes a new sentence, orders a hearing, or treats a source limitation as litigant default.
