## Shared component commitments

**1. Error and forfeiture.** Gaudin and Griffith govern the pending direct appeal. A genuine §1623 materiality element must be submitted to the jury beyond reasonable doubt; the judge's correct statement of law cannot replace the jury's application. Giving the element to the judge was error. Johnson's failure to object, and her earlier reliance on the circuit's then-settled allocation rule when opposing materiality evidence, establish no valid intentional relinquishment of a known jury right on this packet. Treat the case as forfeiture, not waiver or invited error extinguishing the claim.

**2. Plainness.** When trial law settled the issue against the defendant but controlling law changed before completion of direct review, this error is plain at the time of appellate consideration. A futile trial objection is not made the price of having the now-obvious error considered under Rule 52(b). This resolves the reserved intervening-law situation at that scope; it does not erase the separate substantial-rights and discretion requirements or decide every initially unsettled-law case.

**3. Substantial rights and classification.** Assume in Johnson's favor that the omission affects substantial rights, including any properly asserted presumed prejudice, and decide the independent fourth requirement. Do not adopt the Eleventh Circuit's third-step reasoning as a new categorical rule. Gaudin reserved classification; Roy rejects automatic structural treatment of every omitted element and Sullivan concerns destruction of the reasonable-doubt standard throughout the charge. No decision of this particular omission's structural classification or of a universal Rule 52(b) exception is necessary. The assumption is not a holding that every omitted element either does or does not require a prejudice showing.

**4. Discretionary correction.** Even with the first three conditions satisfied, Rule 52(b) permits correction only where this forfeited error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation. Here the evidence's supported connection to the grand jury's inquiry makes the materiality issue substantively clear; the judge followed then-settled circuit law, and the record supplies no separate circumstance showing a serious institutional injury that requires a new trial despite this confined setting. The clarity of the link is relevant to remedial discretion, not a judicial verdict or a finding of harmlessness under a preserved-error standard. The Court may leave this conviction in place without denying the violation or declaring that proof of likely guilt always defeats correction. Actual innocence is not a prerequisite, nor is lack of proven innocence a sufficient reason to deny relief.

## Adversarial path comparison

**Affirmance at step three** would place the ordinary defendant-borne outcome burden on Johnson and find it unmet through the overwhelming materiality evidence. The strongest objection is that the withheld element was never found, and general sufficiency does not reliably describe the effect on the actual verdict. Roy's qualified whole-record method and Scalia/Ginsburg's stricter position are relevant, although Roy involved preserved personal-intent error and a State-borne burden. Reserving step three avoids deciding that classification or importing Roy's burden into Rule 52(b). No Associate must endorse third-step affirmance to support the present judgment.

**New trial through automatic correction** would treat the failure to obtain a materiality verdict as necessarily affecting both substantial rights and institutional integrity. Its strongest basis is Gaudin's guarantee of a real jury determination and Sullivan's prohibition on appellate substitute verdicts. It fails as an automatic theory because those cases do not make every forfeited omission exempt from Olano's separate discretion, and Roy expressly rejects a universal omitted-element structural premise. A case-specific new-trial argument remains lawful, but must explain why this conceded-in-judicial-treatment legal allocation under then-settled law and the clear source-of-funds relationship seriously impairs the proceeding beyond the assumed substantial-rights injury. The supported record does not supply that additional basis. No actual party concession on materiality is inferred by the reference to settled judicial treatment.

**Affirmance at step four** gives the constitutional violation full recognition and assumes prejudice while applying the independently controlling remedial condition. Its strongest objection is that an overwhelming-evidence formula could merely rename appellate factfinding and empty the jury right of force. The confined formulation therefore avoids a universal formula: it combines the specific investigation/account connection, the intervening change from settled contrary law, forfeiture rather than waiver, and the absence of another established integrity defect. It does not deem the materiality element found, adopt sufficiency alone as a cure, or relieve trial courts of Gaudin after that rule governs. No remand is warranted merely to obtain an operator's missing source or repeat an adequately supported contextual determination.
