## 108. Doe v. District of Columbia

**Identity and reach:** D.C. Circuit No. 95-7077, 93 F.3d 861; assigned merits decision June 2, 1997. Reach the granted federal statutory and investigative-procedure questions under the supplied §1254 route. ### Entering law and distinct components

Blessing, April 21, 1997, is controlling current-term law. Its unanimously joined individual-right inquiry requires a particular intended beneficiary, a binding obligation, and an entitlement sufficiently determinate for judicial enforcement; mandatory administrative duties alone do not suffice. A proved right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress forecloses it. General funding oversight does not alone foreclose enforcement.

Suter's March 25, 1992 holdings independently reject the asserted §671(a)(15) reasonable-efforts and §671(a)(9) reporting rights and direct implied actions. Its individual-right and remedy limits survive; it supplies no categorical state-plan rule. Livadas, June 13, 1994, keeps right, §1983 channel, foreclosure, defendant, and relief separate. Wright and Wilder remain operative. The Social Security Act's state-plan-location amendments do not directly apply to CAPTA.

The operative §5106a(b)(2) imposes two different commands: initiation of an investigation promptly after receipt of a report of known or suspected abuse or neglect, to substantiate the report's accuracy; and immediate protective steps after a finding of abuse or neglect, extending to other children under the same care who may be endangered. The asserted claim reviewed here concerns the first command. No preinjury abuse finding activates the second on this record. The limited statutory waiver machinery is retained, but no District waiver is established. The later four-year-plan requirement cannot be imposed on 1989 conduct. An implied CAPTA action is not asserted.

Sandin's June 19, 1995 substantive-constraint rule, Pinder's May 21, 1996 distinction between omissions and an official's own affirmative deprivation, and Uhlrig's February 18, 1997 conduct-time immunity holding do not create an investigative liberty interest. DeShaney denies a general affirmative protection duty outside custody while leaving independently created entitlements analytically distinct.

### Statutory alternatives and legal grounds

**Affirm the statutory dismissal.** The funding provision obliges the recipient to provide a system in which reports lead promptly to investigation, but does not confer the asserted individual federal entitlement to an investigative response. The particular report trigger is important, yet a mandatory administrative response can remain a public institutional duty. Suter's separate reporting holding is the closest controlling analogy: an identified child's predicament and a specific required official response do not alone establish an individual right. The conclusion cannot rest merely on funding, plan placement, a supposed absence of all mandatory duties, or an unasserted implied-action theory.

**Strongest objection to affirmance:** Unlike aggregate compliance or staffing, the command identifies a report, its child beneficiary, and a concrete act that must begin promptly. A complete failure to act can be evaluated without defining every investigative technique. The affirming ground answers that judicial manageability and intended benefit are necessary but not sufficient to turn an institutional reporting-and-investigation obligation into a private entitlement; both the text's recipient focus and Suter's analogous reporting limit remain material.

**Reverse the statutory dismissal and remand.** The provision can instead be read to confer the limited right of a child identified in a received abuse or neglect report to prompt initiation of an appropriate investigation directed to that report's accuracy. It does not guarantee a particular outcome, removal, universal numerical deadline, or freedom from all private violence. The duty to start a concrete investigation is different from a claim to general agency adequacy.

**Strongest objection to reversal:** A mandatory governmental duty is not necessarily an individual right, and Suter rejected a similarly child-specific reporting claim. The reversing ground distinguishes a limited claim to the service Congress expressly commands for the reported child from the broad caseworker relief supported by Suter's reporting theory. “Promptly” permits application to concrete alleged complete inaction, while choices about how to investigate remain discretionary. That is a proposed provision-specific distinction, not a holding that every beneficiary may enforce every grant condition.

**Remedy on reversal:** Only the CAPTA dismissal would be reversed. The lower court would address the conduct-time duty, actual reports and timing, breach, personal responsibility or lawful municipal attribution, causation, applicable immunity, and relief. No damages, injunction, staff complement, removal decision, or fixed deadline follows. On either path, ordinary federal oversight and local statutory duties remain.

### Investigative-procedure due process and the reserved local-remedy issue

**Common sufficient ground for the investigative theory:** Mandatory directions about how officials investigate reports do not, by themselves, create a protected substantive liberty or property entitlement to the investigation. The cited D.C. provisions authorize services and condition emergency removal on further findings; the favorable allegations do not establish a preinjury finding requiring a particular substantive protective disposition. DeShaney's no-general-duty rule remains pertinent, but noncustodial status is not a universal bar to independently created entitlements. No affirmative obstruction of private protection or governmental placement is established by the alleged failure to investigate. Custody acquired after injury cannot supply an earlier custodial duty.

**Alternative tested:** Remit the investigative theory for identification of a substantive local entitlement. Its strongest support is the comprehensive local scheme, the individualized trigger, and Sandin's rejection of a rigid mandatory-word analysis. Its difficulty is that the presented theory identifies failure to perform investigation rather than deprivation, without adequate procedure, of a separately vested substantive benefit. Turner’s local special-duty theory can sustain local tort analysis without turning each investigative instruction into a constitutional procedural entitlement. The narrower affirmance leaves independently vested benefits and the separate loss-of-remedy theory open.

## 110. Gilbert v. Homar

**Confirmed scope:** Every reference to a protected wage or employment interest in this section rests on the assumed premise for this case; no general property-interest holding is adopted. The unexposed neutral/model receipt confirms all eight components and sufficient grounds on that premise.

**Identity and reach:** No. 96-651; Third Circuit decision July 18, 1996; argued March 24, 1997; decision June 9, 1997. Reach the requirement of process before this initial unpaid suspension and the need to assess prompt, meaningful process during its continuation. Do not decide final demotion, substantive due process, stigma, individual immunity, or damages.

### Governing propositions and alternatives

Mathews requires attention to the private interest, error risk and the value of additional safeguards, and the government's interests. Loudermill ordinarily requires an initial opportunity to respond to a proposed significant employment deprivation; its identification of paid suspension as an available option is not an unconditional command that every suspension without advance process preserve pay. Mallen permits urgent provisional action when a substantial governmental interest and independent assurance against baseless deprivation justify postponement, with the subsequent delay separately justified. Barry requires useful, prompt post-suspension review. Young preserves the temporary/final distinction without supplying employment-specific hearing procedures.

**Path supporting reversal of the initial ruling:** On the undisputed felony-arrest premise, the University could suspend this officer immediately without pay pending investigation and prompt further process. Sensitive police duties and public confidence support prompt removal. The suspension/pay interest is assumed protected for this case, without deciding that preliminary issue, and remains part of the balance: the immediate provisional loss is limited in expected duration, the government has an interest in withholding pay from an officer it has substantial grounds temporarily to remove, and the independent criminal accusation reduces the risk that an initial suspension rests solely on employer mistake. A preliminary response offers limited additional assurance about the fact of that arrest. The premise supports a short provisional measure, not indefinite punishment or a finding of guilt.

**Strongest opposing path:** Sustain a prehearing-pay requirement on this record while permitting immediate removal from duty. Continued pay would protect the University's safety interests, salary is livelihood, and an arrest does not decide workplace wrongdoing. This is a serious Mathews consideration, but the existence of a less burdensome option does not make that option constitutionally compulsory in every urgent case. Mallen's general principle and the independent arrest permit an initial unpaid measure here without demanding proof of a separate fiscal emergency.

Dismissal of charges on September 1 changed the premise; officials' later receipt of a report attributing admissions creates a separate possible employment basis whose allegations are disputed. Review must examine the justification for continued deprivation and delay, the reliability of the remaining basis, and whether the employee received enough information to respond meaningfully. September 18 cannot be deemed adequate merely because a meeting occurred when the report's contents were withheld. No fixed permissible number of days is adopted. Dismissal of the criminal case does not itself decide the employment merits, require automatic reinstatement, or forbid investigation on independent grounds.

**Backpay and remedy:** Later backpay does not retroactively supply process. It may affect relief if a violation is established. Reverse the reviewed categorical pre-suspension-hearing ruling and remand for consideration of the promptness and adequacy of post-suspension process. Do not restore blanket summary judgment for officials, decide that the entire suspension was lawful, award money, resolve qualified immunity, or disturb other lower-court components outside the grant.

## 111. Johnson v. Fankell

**Identity and reach:** No. 96-292; argued February 26, 1997; decision June 9, 1997. Fankell, the employee/respondent, filed the state §1983 action. Johnson and the other official petitioners seek immediate appellate review of denial of their qualified-immunity defense. Reach the federal challenge to Idaho's conclusive refusal to entertain that interlocutory appeal under the supplied review posture; the underlying liability action remains pending.

### Sufficient ground, alternatives, and remedy

**Affirmance ground:** Section 1983 and federal qualified immunity require the competent state court to apply the federal substantive defense. They do not incorporate §1291's collateral-order appeal into the State's appellate jurisdiction. A neutral final-judgment rule may postpone review while the trial court applies the immunity and ordinary final appellate review remains available. Howlett and Testa forbid discriminatory avoidance of federal law; Felder invalidates a state condition incompatible with the federal right. Idaho's supplied rule is not shown to do either.

**Strongest reversal ground:** Qualified immunity protects against trial and its burdens, not merely a damages judgment; an erroneous denial cannot be fully repaired after trial. Immediate state review could therefore be considered indispensable to the federal protection. That cost is real, but Mitchell's federal appellate holding remains a construction of §1291's final-decision rule. Johnson v. Jones, Swint, and Behrens preserve bounded appellate categories even when later review leaves some immunity-related litigation costs unrecovered. The substantive defense does not create a freestanding federal command that every competent court system offer identical intermediate appeals.

**Limits:** No holding makes neutral procedure immune from all preemption. A discriminatory rule, deliberate evasion, or procedure that nullifies the substantive defense would require its own analysis. None is established here. Affirmance neither treats qualified immunity as a mere liability defense nor licenses state courts to dilute its federal content. The Court decides no entitlement to immunity, factual dispute, underlying §1983 violation, or damages.

**Remedy:** Affirm Idaho's dismissal of the attempted interlocutory appeal. The state action proceeds subject to substantive qualified immunity and the State's ordinary procedures, including final review. No Supreme Court stay, newly compelled appeal, liability ruling, or order granting immunity follows.
