### R. A confined review channel

The denial conclusively rejects an asserted immunity from undergoing present litigation; final review cannot restore the protection if it exists. Fitzgerald's immediate-review analysis, 457 U.S. 741–743, and the immunity distinction preserved in current Swint support the immunity appeal. The Court has §1254(1) review of the case in the court of appeals.

The particular trial-stay ruling is reviewable with that immunity decision. The District Court resolved the same asserted present protection by denying dismissal, permitting discovery, and categorically withholding trial for the Presidency. Those are interdependent determinations of the scope and remedy of one asserted immunity; appellate review may resolve that integrated protection order. The statutory finality rule is not displaced merely because issues share facts, parties or convenience. Current Swint left genuine necessary or inextricable review open; this case adopts it only for this integrated immunity-and-postponement ruling. The District Court's alternative reference to docket control does not insulate its existing categorical postponement from that review: the challenged relief remains the same temporal protection, and its categorical justification and duration can be tested without deciding any claim's merits or any future scheduling request.

Ferguson's separate liability or personal defenses are not independently reviewable through Clinton's appeal. Because his present trial protection was expressly derivative of the very presidential protection under review, removal of that derivative stay is a remedial consequence of resolving the integrated order, not a decision on an independent interlocutory defense. Any independently justified future Ferguson accommodation needs its own ground. The undeveloped press-secretary defense is not necessary to review and is not decided. The already moot dispute about discovery pending the lower appeal supplies no live merits question. No §1292(b) certification, general pendent-party doctrine or new interlocutory route for later scheduling orders is inferred.

### T. No temporary immunity on the presented private-conduct claim

Holding presidential office does not constitutionally entitle the incumbent to dismissal without prejudice or suspension until leaving office of this federal civil action concerning alleged unofficial conduct before office. The structural protection recognized in Fitzgerald concerns presidential official responsibility; the present private conduct was no exercise of that responsibility. Protecting against an actual interference with present duties is a different inquiry from granting immunity from the entire suit. This conclusion reaches neither presidential criminal liability nor a state-court action, and does not decide compulsory attendance at a specified time or place.

United States v. Nixon, 418 U.S. 683, 703–716, defeats a general premise that Article III process cannot reach a President, but its criminal-evidence setting and express civil-evidence reservation prevent treating it as the answer to every demand in this action. Fitzgerald's concern with presidential distraction is substantial; its rule nevertheless links damages immunity to the official function whose independent exercise liability might inhibit. The new temporary exemption lacks that link. Neither the asserted political motives of the suit nor an assessment that this plaintiff's claim is unimportant supplies an immunity test.

### F. Preserve the official-act rule and the unadjudicated defense

Fitzgerald's outer-perimeter protection for presidential official responsibility remains in force. Function governs; present title alone does not convert earlier private conduct into an official presidential act. Conversely, an allegation of unlawful purpose alone does not remove an act that otherwise lies within that perimeter. Forrester, 484 U.S. 223–229, supplies the functional distinction while expressly recognizing the President's unique official-function immunity; Antoine supplies an analogous insistence on historical and functional justification, not a presidential result.

The press-secretary statements allegedly occurred during the Presidency. Whether they were within presidential official responsibility, whether Clinton bears legal responsibility for them, and whether the defamation elements are met remain for the District Court on a fuller record. The denial of temporary immunity neither rejects nor establishes that distinct defense. Nor does it determine immunity under state law, any separate qualified-immunity issue, the truth of the pleaded allegations, or liability under §§1983 or 1985.

### S. Reject this categorical trial stay; preserve tailored relief

Affirm the judgment below insofar as it denies the asserted temporary immunity and removes the trial stays resting on that assertion, leaving the federal litigation to proceed under proper case management. The existing order postpones trial for the Presidency without a demonstrated conflict between a particular litigation demand and identified presidential duties. Presidential status and generalized forecasts do not satisfy the justification for that categorical duration. Allowing discovery does not make the trial delay harmless: delay can prejudice access to witnesses and evidence, and no source establishes that preservation procedures eliminate those risks. It is unnecessary to choose Arkansas survival law or find that any evidence actually has been lost.

Landis, 299 U.S. 248, 254–258, supplies the ordinary docket-control principles. Where even a fair possibility of harm to the other party exists, the stay applicant must clearly show hardship or inequity in proceeding. An extraordinary public interest may justify a moderate, nonoppressive delay; duration must be bounded when the stay is entered, and a later opportunity for revision does not cure an initially immoderate stay. Those principles inform this private action; Landis did not itself announce a special presidential test. A calendar endpoint tied to leaving office does not establish that this extended postponement is moderate or justified by a concrete need. The categorical order fails both as a constitutional entitlement and as the exercise of discretion presented here.

Ferguson has no independent presidential immunity; his derivative trial stay falls with the stated basis for Clinton's. Discovery may continue against both subject to lawful privilege and case management. This does not compel any particular discovery, set a trial date, prohibit all later stays, or grant Jones a judgment on any claim. The distinct temporary discovery stay pending the completed lower appeal remains moot. Proceedings return for ordinary adjudication, including initial determination of the reserved official-function defense.

### A. Constitutionally respectful management of concrete demands

The District Court must give substantial respect to the President's constitutional duties when managing this action. A litigant requesting accommodation must identify the demand and the conflict or hardship; office alone does not establish an entitlement to a Presidency-long suspension. The court may adjust timing, sequence, manner or duration, and may grant a properly supported, bounded stay, while accounting for the plaintiff's legitimate interest in timely adjudication and preserving evidence. A demonstrated interference with performance of constitutional duties cannot be dismissed as an ordinary inconvenience. Any protection should respond to the actual conflict and extend no further than its demonstrated justification; it remains subject to reconsideration as circumstances change. Landis supplies its stated clear-showing burden when a requested stay presents even a fair possibility of harm to the other party.

No named multistep procedure, universal evidentiary hearing, fixed discovery cap, general presumption against the plaintiff, or new automatic interlocutory appeal is adopted. Existing avenues of appellate relief retain their own requirements. The adequacy of a particular accommodation, compulsion order, sanction, privilege claim or remedy for a future conflict remains open because none is presented. The holding supplies neither a finding that litigation will impose no serious burden nor an entitlement to unlimited judicial control of presidential time.

Breyer's additional qualification is that protection against a demonstrated serious impairment of Article II performance is a constitutional obligation, not merely a favor within unbounded trial-court discretion. If ordinary timing and manner adjustments cannot avert such an impairment, the court must provide an effective accommodation, including a limited stay when that is the necessary remedy. He does not presume such a conflict, reverse the applicant's obligation to identify it, prescribe a new standard of proof, or decide the effect of an actual irreconcilable demand now. His separate-writing function exists only if the eventual Court opinion leaves that constitutional obligation uncertain or describes accommodation as entirely optional despite proven serious impairment.
