## O'Dell ? framing and record

The legal question remains whether the particular parole-information protection was dictated at October3,1988 finality, not whether one can cite a general fairness proposition or the date1994 alone. Graham and current Lambrix require a substantive comparison of the old protection's function and the proposed application. Gilmore and Caspari preserve the possibility of a new rule when that comparison reveals a genuine extension. Gardner's right to answer adverse sentencing information and Skipper's future-danger rebuttal protection support an old-rule argument. The competing new-rule argument distinguishes legal information about the State's sentencing alternatives from excluded factual evidence, invokes Ramos's treatment of sentencing-information choices, and asks whether the earlier holdings compelled this precise protection. Both paths require the strongest response on the legal/factual distinction; the packet supplies no conclusion that one already wins. If a new rule is found, both Teague exceptions as they existed in1997 remain available for actual assessment, including the demanding watershed exception. No later abolition of that category enters.

The source extracts support a requested parole-ineligibility instruction and an attempted question whether life meant life without parole, both refused. They also establish the materially different statement the jury did hear: O'Dell described sixteen additional years on an earlier parole violation, said this was effectively life at his age, and said he was never going to get out. That statement must not be silently equated with an accurate explanation that the only noncapital sentence for this conviction legally excluded ordinary parole; neither may it be omitted when deciding effective opportunity, prejudice or scope.

The prosecutor argued that prior punishments had not stopped violent conduct and that death would do so. Petitioner's source account characterizes the presentation as an implication of future parole; respondent disputes that characterization and stresses the earlier prison homicide. Those are competing interpretations of the quoted record, not findings of an express false statement that this life sentence carried parole. Simmons permits the State to prove danger to inmates or guards; it does not create a general prison-danger exception to the legally responsive parole explanation. Allridge's rejection of favorable release predictions where ordinary parole remains possible is distinct from actual legal ineligibility.

The jury's written verdict found both future dangerousness and vileness. The source explains that a direct-appeal reference to only dangerousness does not accurately describe the verdict. Neither second aggravation nor substantial evidence of prison danger establishes harmlessness automatically. Likewise a refused requested instruction is not itself structural error or an automatic warrant for a new penalty proceeding under current Simmons. The District Court's actual relief remains conditioned on proof that O'Dell was legally parole-ineligible; the parties' assertions do not convert that predicate into an unconditional adjudicated fact. The quoted state provision is only a pertinent-part extract, not a certification of its complete eligibility rules.

Lambrix (May12) governs nonjurisdictional sequence and disciplined old-rule comparison, but does not dictate this distinct parole-information result or erase independent default/statutory conditions. Simmons's Chapman application is a direct-review holding. The historical briefing's Brecht formulation cannot be imported as current local law: the local Brecht holding differs from history. The supplemental current-law slice supplies Brecht, O'Neal, Gray and Simmons: Brecht and O'Neal apply Chapman to their identified preserved claims without a universal all-claims rule; Gray finds harm under either competing approach and likewise leaves the universal question open. Gray also requires resolution of genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory conditions before an operative writ. If modeling reaches harmlessness or an operative writ here, it must use those actual scopes rather than an automatic imported historical standard, or preserve that component for lawful review. ## Coeur d'Alene ? framing and record

The lower case was dismissed at the pleading stage. The Ninth Circuit affirmed the dismissal of the State/agencies and direct quiet-title claim, permitted the limited prospective officer claims, and held the pleaded ownership theories sufficient to survive the Rule12 dismissal. That procedural sufficiency is not a finding of tribal title, navigability, prestatehood divestment or final entitlement to an injunction. The direct-State and quiet-title dismissals remain distinct from the granted officer question.

The actual prayer is broad: it concerns beds, banks and waters within the1873 reservation boundary, seeks exclusive use/occupancy/quiet enjoyment, challenges state regulation affecting them and seeks to prevent officers from regulating, permitting or taking actions in violation of those claimed rights. It cannot be reduced to a hypothetical narrow permit dispute. Conversely, the lower appellate remedy expressly would not quiet title against Idaho and its agencies or foreclose their claims in a later proceeding. It contemplated a declaration against claimants other than the immune State/agencies and prospective officer compliance. Whether that limitation preserves a genuine officer remedy or nonetheless accomplishes an immunity-barred transfer of state control is the legal dispute.

The supplied complaint-related sources allege that the officials exercise the very regulatory control to be restrained; the named roles include Land Board officials and the water-resources director. That allegation supplies the pleaded connection to be tested and is not merely a general duty to obey federal law. It does not establish a final factual finding about each incumbent's authority or authorize relief against an officer unconnected to a proven violation. No replacement officeholder, personal liability or retrospective treasury remedy is invented.

One supported path treats the alleged ongoing federal wrong and connected officers under Young, distinguishing Tindal and Treasure Salvors's officer relief from a judgment binding the sovereign's title, with final scope limited to established federal rights. Its strongest objection is the practical displacement of Idaho's regulatory sovereignty over navigable waters notwithstanding labels. The competing path treats the comprehensive declaration/injunction as functionally equivalent to the barred sovereign-title remedy, with its strongest objection that ordinary prospective federal supremacy would otherwise depend on the State's assertion of property ownership before the federal right could be heard. A narrower alternative scrutinizes or limits particular remedial demands without resolving title or announcing a categorical exception. These are analytical possibilities, not findings that the existing prayer has already been amended or severed.

Seminole's five-Justice officer holding permits an ongoing federal-duty claim despite state-directed text and statutory remedial detail in IGRA. It supplies neither a controlling rationale on every direct-State abrogation question nor an automatic result for submerged lands. Its complete statutory sequence is not transplanted to this different federal property claim. No current holding supplies the proposed categorical sovereign-lands exception. Young, Edelman, Green, Pennhurst, Tindal, Larson and Treasure Salvors require attention to the claimed federal source, actual defendant and relief; a label such as prospective or ownership does not alone answer all three.

The separate United States case concerns a smaller area, and the Tribe's intervention is limited to that area according to the supplied source account. That is neither consolidation nor complete substitute relief; the source does not establish an entered title judgment. The1873 order,1891 statute and alternative aboriginal-title theory remain distinct pleaded bases. OCR's isolated1981 parenthetical for the1891 statute is a source transcription error, not another enactment. No new merits title premise or geographic measurement is adopted.
