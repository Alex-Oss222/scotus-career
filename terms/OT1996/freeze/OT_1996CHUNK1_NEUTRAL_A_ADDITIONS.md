## 1. Department of State v. Legal Assistance for Vietnamese Asylum Seekers

It characterized those claims as alternative grounds for affirmance, rejected by the district court and not reached by the D.C. Circuit. Respondents likewise maintained those claims. No dismissal for mootness may rest merely on §633's enactment.

The Government requested vacatur and remand in light of the new statute as its principal disposition, with reversal alternatively. It argued that the amendment governs the prospective injunctive request, removes the statutory nondiscrimination basis for the decision, and strengthens its arguments concerning administrative discretion and the constitutional claim. Its legislative-history contention that the authority is unreviewable is an argument, not the statute's enacted text or an established jurisdictional bar.

Respondents requested merits affirmance, or alternatively dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted. They distinguished a preventive injunction regulating future processing from a reparative injunction supplying processing allegedly unlawfully withheld before enactment. ## 2. California v. Roy

### Corrected component and lower-court account

This review concerns Roy's robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions involving Mannix. The separate second-degree murder conviction involving Clark is not challenged in the federal petition and must not be vacated or made subject to a new retrial direction. The robbery-felony-murder special-circumstance finding had already been reversed on direct state review; its existence and instructions may bear on harmlessness, but it is not an operative conviction or new punishment to restore.

The State's petition and *Roy v. Gomez*, 81 F.3d 863 (9th Cir. April 15, 1996), agree that the omission is subject to some harmless-error review. The en banc court did not hold that every omission is automatically structural. Its distinctive reasoning required necessary jury findings of the missing purpose element and treated their absence as producing grave doubt under the historical collateral-error cases. Its majority and dissent agreed on trial-error classification and disagreed about the inquiry and application. Replace any suggestion that the judgment below rested on an unqualified structural-error rule.

The State relied on the instructions as a whole, the surviving verdicts, the special-circumstance language, physical evidence and testimony about Roy's statements. It argued that the jury's findings and record made purposeful assistance unavoidable. The en banc majority emphasized that knowledge of McHargue's robbery purpose and aid with that effect were not necessarily a finding of Roy's purpose to assist robbery; it identified a possible assistance-to-a-companion inference. These are competing applications, not fresh fact findings by this Court. The State's own petition confirms repeated presentation of the personal-intent objection in the state and federal proceedings; no new default is inferred.

### Entering law and alternatives

The Standards provision **Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review**, present operation effective *O'Neal*, February 21, 1995, places the *Chapman* burden on the State for the preserved personal-intent category. O'Connor authored the controlling portions; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined among current Associates. Stevens and Kennedy supported further proceedings on a different harmlessness rationale; their judgment votes do not enlarge the *Chapman* coalition. The authentic State petition's historical *Brecht/O'Neal* substantial-influence formulation is advocacy founded on a displaced legal premise and cannot be treated as governing law.

The exact *O'Neal* holding involved combined instructions and prosecutorial argument allowing conviction without the defendant's required intent. Roy's omitted purpose instruction presents the same identified personal-intent concern through an instructional omission. Application of that category must be explained; neither a universal *Chapman* rule for all habeas errors nor a universal exception for omissions is inherited.

**A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error**, effective *Sullivan*, June 1, 1993, concerns the absence of a constitutionally valid reasonable-doubt verdict altogether. **Jury determination of criminal materiality**, effective *Gaudin*, June 19, 1995, places an offense element with the jury but expressly leaves a universal structural-error rule undecided. Scalia authored both relevant opinions; all then-participating Justices joined *Sullivan* and all nine joined *Gaudin*. Scalia's pre-divergence *Carella* concurrence, 491 U.S. 263, 267–273 (1989), is a material own-writing source for the necessary-findings argument; its concurrence status must be retained. The petition's stray citation to 491 U.S. 376 is not the correct reporter page for *Carella*.

A supported narrow disposition classifies this omission as assessable trial error, explains the controlling personal-intent *Chapman* inquiry, and returns the affected convictions for whole-record review without deciding harmlessness or ordering immediate release. The strongest contrary path maintains that the actual jury findings cannot supply the missing purposeful-assistance determination and that the lower result can survive under *Chapman*. Its proponent must address the State's instructions-and-evidence argument without substituting hypothetical findings for actual adjudication. A structural path requires more than the uncontroverted element-allocation violation; the difference from *Sullivan* must be established. A Kotteakos path requires an express, supported distinction or reconsideration of current *O'Neal*, rather than citation to historical doctrine.

**Readiness:** Ready for a legal-standard disposition and bounded remand. A final harmlessness finding, immediate conditional writ or reinstatement of the habeas denial requires full treatment of the complete charge, verdicts and trial evidence. The archive contains a substantial separately titled Appendix; it has not been read here, and no claim that relevant evidence is absent is made. Existing independent habeas gates remain distinct; no new AEDPA temporal ruling follows merely from this November decision date.

## 4. INS v. Yueh-Shaio Yang

### Statute, agency reasons and scope

The operative provision is discretionary, not the earlier mandatory version construed in *INS v. Errico*, 385 U.S. 214 (1966). The petition reproduces former §1251(a)(1)(H): deportability based on covered entry fraud, willful or innocent, may be waived in the Attorney General's discretion for a qualifying spouse, parent, son or daughter of a citizen or permanent resident who possessed an immigrant visa or equivalent document and was otherwise admissible, subject to the specified fraud-related labor-certification and documentation exceptions. The Nazi-persecution/genocide exclusion remains distinct. A granted waiver also reaches grounds of inadmissibility at entry directly resulting from the fraud. These eligibility and consequential-waiver clauses do not themselves enumerate the permissible discretionary factors.

The BIA accepted Yang's statutory eligibility, explicitly said the initial entry fraud was disregarded, and relied on a pattern: assisting the wife's assumed-identity scheme through divorce/remarriage, his own entry, the later naturalization application perpetuating the false identity and citizenship representation, another divorce to rectify the wife's identity, and lack of candor at the hearing. It also considered positive residence, property/business and family ties, his health, connections to Taiwan and the availability of treatment there. Those are agency findings and assessments, not new Supreme Court findings. The BIA's written balancing prevents the packet from implying a categorical refusal based solely on the minimum fraud necessary for eligibility.

The Ninth Circuit's remand allowed consideration of alleged dishonest testimony, including assertions about involvement in the fraud and economic circumstances, while excluding collaboration with the wife and the naturalization fraud as inextricably connected to entry. Thus even respondents' successful ground below did not erase all misconduct or demand the waiver. The State's position cannot be answered by assuming that the agency ignored every favorable factor; the actual written reasons must be evaluated.

### Entering law and alternatives

**Unauthorized-employment conditions on immigration release**, effective *National Center for Immigrants' Rights*, December 16, 1991, supports lawful structuring of discretion within statutory boundaries and preserved individual determinations. It is an analogy, not a holding about this waiver. Stevens authored it; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas joined among current Associates. No controlling reopening rule may be imported from the fractured simulated *Doherty*.

Add the administrative-change components of **Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change**, present operation effective *Smiley*, June 3, 1996, with the earlier *Good Samaritan* rule preserved. All eight current Associates joined *Smiley*'s explained-change portion. A changed interpretation is not automatically disqualified, but unexplained reversal, disregard of legitimate reliance and substitute litigation explanations retain their legal importance. This does not prove a policy change occurred in Yang or authorize retroactive legislative rulemaking.

*Jay v. Boyd*, 351 U.S. 345 (1956), supports the difference between eligibility and discretionary selection. *Errico*'s mandatory predecessor statute and family-preservation purpose supply the strongest objection to allowing an eligible applicant's unavoidable fraud to defeat the very waiver Congress created, but the change to discretionary statutory wording must be confronted. The settled-policy issue is separate: a legal power to weigh fraud does not excuse arbitrary departure from a binding policy.

A broad statutory path rejects the Ninth Circuit's categorical exclusion while retaining ordinary review of reasons and policy consistency. A narrower path distinguishes the initial qualifying fraud from independently relevant continuing conduct and resolves the record under that distinction. The opposing path would require the agency to disregard the fraud necessarily incidental to the qualifying entry and reweigh the remaining factors. Its strongest objection is that the statutory text conditions eligibility without erasing conduct from discretionary consideration, while the BIA actually identified distinct acts and considered favorable factors. None of these paths requires this Court to grant the waiver or reweigh equities itself.

**Readiness:** Ready for modeling statutory scope and a disposition restoring or remanding agency discretion. A universal rule requiring disregard of every entry-related act, or a finding that a settled agency policy was changed, requires a supported policy construction. The BIA's reasons are recovered; they are not missing. Any event-date jurisdictional effect of the 1996 amendments must be checked from the official enacted-law source, not from a current Code webpage. Eligibility is conceded here, so no unpresented amended-eligibility determination is supplied.

## 5. Ohio v. Robinette

### Full judgment below and evidentiary distinctions

The Ohio Supreme Court adopted two grounds: it held detention became unlawful when the deputy ordered Robinette out of the car for unrelated inquiry after resolving the traffic investigation, and it separately announced a clear-release-warning rule. It found no temporal break or intervening circumstance to attenuate the consent from the asserted unlawful detention. The warning rule is therefore not the entire judgment below.

The trial court, which reviewed a stipulated videotape, found that the deputy made clear that the traffic matter was concluded and that the manner, wording, position of the parties, and Robinette's education showed voluntary consent without overbearing conduct. The intermediate appellate court and state supreme court disagreed with the legal classification. The actual film has not been viewed in this preflight; the trial court's findings and appellate descriptions are the available evidence, not a reconstructed video.

The transcript establishes that the deputy returned the license before the contraband and search requests (JA11–13). Robinette testified that he thought he was free to leave and began to do so, but was then shocked, answered the search request automatically, and felt unable to refuse (JA26–27,29,31). He described the deputy as nice, denied a drawn weapon or fear for safety, and had a college degree (JA28,33). His subjective understanding is evidence relevant to voluntariness; it is not a replacement for the objective seizure test. The deputy's uncommunicated intent to issue only a warning and his uncommunicated willingness to let drivers refuse likewise cannot decide the objective seizure question.

The state supreme court quotes the transition as: “One question before you get gone” followed by a contraband inquiry (Pet.App2,8). Its opinion says he was never told he was free to go. A compound question at JA26 and the original suppression memorandum (Pet.App28) describe a free-to-go indication, but the answer does not establish a specific spoken release formula against the appellate account. Preserve that distinction; neither categorical no-release language nor the isolated compound answer substitutes for the source-supported findings.

The Ohio opinion expressly invokes both the Fourth Amendment and Article I, §14 of the Ohio Constitution, with federal authorities interwoven in its explanation. Under *Michigan v. Long*, 463 U.S. 1032 (1983), determine whether a clear independent state ground supports the judgment; do not simply erase the Ohio provision. A federal ruling does not forbid Ohio from adopting an independent state-law rule on remand.

### Entering law and alternatives

**Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop**, effective *Whren*, June 10, 1996, is Thomas's unanimous opinion. It removes a subjective-purpose/customary-enforcement prerequisite for a lawful initial stop but preserves independent constraints on manner, scope and duration. It neither validates every later detention nor supplies suspicion of drugs. *Pennsylvania v. Mimms*, 434 U.S. 106 (1977), is material to whether ordering a driver out during a lawful stop requires additional suspicion; it does not itself authorize extending a completed stop indefinitely. The relation between those objective authorities and the Ohio court's motive-based reasoning must be addressed expressly.

**Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause**, effective *Ornelas*, May 28, 1996, distinguishes historical facts/credibility from ultimate constitutional sufficiency and requires genuine weight for supported contextual inferences. O'Connor authored the controlling portion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer among current Associates. Its scope cannot be converted into a holding that every mixed question, including every voluntariness question, receives identical review.

*Schneckloth v. Bustamonte*, 412 U.S. 218 (1973), supplies total-circumstances voluntariness and makes knowledge of a right to refuse relevant without an indispensable warning prerequisite. *Florida v. Bostick*, 501 U.S. 429 (1991), authored by O'Connor and joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Souter among current Associates, rejects an automatic seizure rule in favor of whether the person could decline requests or terminate the encounter. Stevens joined its dissent; his own-date contrary writing is relevant to the practical force of police authority. *Florida v. Royer*, 460 U.S. 491 (1983), and *Brown v. Illinois*, 422 U.S. 590 (1975), keep voluntariness distinct from whether consent purges a prior unlawful detention. No automatic rule that any consent obtained during illegality is necessarily incurable follows.

A supported narrow path rejects a universal federal release script and returns the case for consideration of whether, objectively, a detention continued or was extended without independent grounds, whether consent was voluntary, and, if necessary, whether it was sufficiently independent of any illegality. This path must address both grounds below and the trial findings. The strongest opposing path affirms suppression on the actual continuity of authority and lack of attenuation, without defending an across-the-board warning command. Its proponent must confront the returned license, Robinette's stated understanding, trial findings and *Mimms/Whren* objective limits. A path upholding the search must explain why the actual encounter was consensual or the detention lawful and why consent was voluntary; rejection of the warning rule alone cannot perform those jobs.

**Readiness:** Ready for modeling a federal-rule decision and bounded remand addressing both grounds. Final record-specific validation or suppression is more demanding: it must resolve the actual findings, review allocation, duration, consent and any attenuation question. No assertion is made that the videotape or evidence is absent from the Court's file. Do not automatically reinstate the conviction merely because the per se federal rule fails.
