## 85. United States v. Ortiz; United States v. Hurtado

The authorized simulated merits grant covers two defendants and separate judgments. Ortiz's pager retrieval occurred at the parking lot after a lawful arrest and physical seizure. His wristwatch retrieval, automobile search and firearm seizure are outside the grant; the appellate opinion's discussion of them supplies context only. Hurtado challenges only the 37-month consecutive contempt sentence; the contempt conviction is not challenged.

Ortiz presents a real Robinson/Belton path: an immediately associated object searched contemporaneously with lawful custodial arrest can fall within the established categorical incident authority, without case-specific proof that this arrestee could destroy evidence. Its strongest objection is that secured concealed memory presents an informational search whose justification must be shown, and the packet establishes no particular overwrite risk. The competing Chimel/Chadwick path distinguishes seizure from additional examination and requires a supported exception once the object is secured; its strongest objection is that Chadwick expressly addressed property not immediately associated with the person and a search remote from arrest, whereas this pager was taken from Ortiz and read immediately. Pinson and Wellons establish protected-information/container principles but do not themselves resolve which search-incident rule governs a pager. Richards rejects an unsupported offense category in warrant execution, while expressly preserving categorical rules supported in other settings; Chandler concerns a different special-needs justification. Neither can mechanically repeal Robinson.

The primary Seventh Circuit opinion, read in full, corroborates the packet. Paragraphs 45–50 rely on both immediate search-incident authority and generalized pager-memory destruction; neither the specific capacity nor a specific incoming overwrite event is found there. A categorical rejection may permit remand for preserved alternative grounds and harmlessness, without automatically suppressing every item or vacating unrelated rulings. A categorical affirmance does not establish a general rule for all electronic devices or delayed searches.

For Hurtado, §§2J1.1 and 2X5.1 require the most analogous guideline. The §2J1.1 commentary points to the nature and circumstances of contempt, effect on administration of justice and need to vindicate authority, while saying obstruction is analogous in some cases. The appellate opinion sets out the alternatives, underlying drug level 26, accessory reduction to level 20, refusal after immunity, limited helpful testimony, and absence of evidence of an objective to secure Ortiz's acquittal. Obstruction need not succeed to be serious; acquittal is not a necessary condition of its analogy. Conversely every refusal cannot automatically acquire the accessory cross-reference merely because trial testimony was withheld. Stinson requires fidelity to the text and consistent commentary; Koon separates legal errors, supported findings and discretionary judgments. The lower court's comparison with sentences for other crimes is not a freestanding power to select whichever range seems gentler. Model the permitted analogy on conduct, supported purpose and effect; keep a definite §2J1.5 remand distinct from a remand requiring a properly supported analogy determination. No resentencing number is predetermined.
