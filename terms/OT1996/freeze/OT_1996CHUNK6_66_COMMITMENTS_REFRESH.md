## Refreshed factual assessment

The supported published recitation establishes forty-one days of registration use, uptake by about one-third of the State's registration officials, no election before abandonment and no election then imminent. It attributes abandonment to the point when officials recognized that the legislature would not enact the amendments they expected and considered necessary. These are material facts and attributed background explanations, not an independent legal determination that each registration was invalid under state law.

These additions strengthen the State's account of a brief, incompletely implemented arrangement awaiting anticipated legislation. They weaken an inference that the Plan had become the settled statewide system through extensive electoral use. That is a real change in the evidentiary balance on classification, even though the judgment below still cannot stand.

The qualifications matter equally. One-third describes registration officials, not voters, counties, completed registrations or electorate coverage. The absence of an election does not mean that registration did not occur or that no individual relied on it. Forty-one days is not a statutory maximum or a universal test for temporary practice. Attributed expectation of state-law amendments is not a supplied holding of state-law invalidity. No individual registration's legal effect, exact implementation chronology or voter count is reconstructed.

## Reachability and judgment

All eight Associate Justices participate and commit to reverse summary judgment for Mississippi and remand. The direct appeal presents §5 coverage and the claimed reach of earlier submission, not substantive discriminatory purpose or effect. The State's preferred Old System supplies a sufficient comparison to reject its assertion that all New System features are merely restoration. The Court need not finally choose the Provisional Plan as the binding baseline to decide this appeal.

The refreshed commitments expressly decline both a holding that this Provisional Plan must be the benchmark and a holding that temporary or state-law-defective practices categorically cannot be benchmarks. The additional facts make the former broader holding less secure; they do not decide the independent changed-features ground. No Justice conditions reversal on selecting the Plan as the benchmark, and no Justice treats registration use as fictitious because no election occurred.

## Exact propositions and common joins

**Y1 — Discretionary implementation remains covered.** When a covered State changes registration practices directly affecting voting, the federal occasion for the change does not exempt the State's discretionary implementation choices from §5. The NVRA's valid federal-registration obligations and non-displacement of the Voting Rights Act leave both statutes operative. A feature compelled in every detail by federal law, leaving no state choice, remains outside the necessary decision.

**Y2 — New features defeat the complete-restoration defense even on the State's preferred baseline.** Assuming the Old System is the correct comparison rather than the forty-one-day Provisional Plan, the post-February 10 New System still contains registration machinery and voter communications absent from the Old System. Retention of the old state-registration rules does not establish that new manuals, instructions, reporting requirements, agency choices and administration of different federal/state registration status are unchanged. The State therefore cannot obtain summary judgment on the ground that the entire New System simply restores the Old System. The holding uses this sufficient comparison without establishing a general duration, uptake, election-use or state-law-validity rule for selecting baselines.

**Y3 — Submission is change-specific.** Submission of an earlier NVRA implementation plan does not preclear a later discretionary change that the submission did not identify. The supplied record establishes that the later dual system was not submitted. Earlier approval is not blanket approval of its new features. The actual terms control any genuinely disputed question about the reach of a particular submission on remand; no missing DOJ correspondence is reconstructed or deemed adverse to a litigant.

**Y4 — The remedy enforces the statutory sequence within lawful bounds.** Covered unprecleared changes may not be administered as the ordinary registration system without §5 approval. The enforcement court identifies the covered changes and actual submission status and fashions lawful relief; the Attorney General or D.D.C. decides substantive preclearance. Relief must preserve the NVRA's valid federal-registration duties and fit the actual practices and timing. No identical-roll command, chosen interim plan, discrimination finding, damages, completed-election consequence or cancellation of individual registrations is adjudged.

All eight expressly join Y1–Y4, including their reservations. Their votes do not include a final Provisional Plan baseline holding. No separate-writing function is needed to communicate these commitments.

## Enhanced competing paths

**Path A — Affirm as restoration of the Old System or unavoidable federal compliance.** The new facts materially strengthen the premise that officials tried a provisional, partially implemented arrangement while anticipating legislation and promptly abandoned it without an intervening or imminent election. The State need not accept a rule permanently expanding state registration merely because that attempt occurred. Voting Rights Coalition also permits separate federal and state qualification administration. The decisive objection is independent: even accepting the State's preferred Old System baseline, the New System introduced its own discretionary machinery and communications. Neither the provisional facts nor the federal duty establishes that those choices existed before or were specifically submitted. All eight reject affirmance on this record without deciding that every detail of federal implementation is discretionary.

**Path B — Reverse because actual registration necessarily makes the Provisional Plan the operative benchmark.** The strongest argument is §5's attention to administered practices and the undisputed fact that actual registration occurred. Allen and Perkins protect changes in practical access; Lopez does not allow labels or statutory origin to erase administration. The strongest objection is now fuller: forty-one days, limited official uptake, no election or imminent one, and abandonment once anticipated legislation would not occur. Those facts caution against converting this interval into a conclusively settled statewide benchmark. Conversely, neither no election nor anticipated state-law difficulty proves that use and reliance never occurred. All eight decline this unnecessary categorical route. The data support competing classification arguments, not a litigant default or an invented statutory duration rule.

**Path C — Reverse for new practices even against the Old System and remand for lawful enforcement.** This remains the selected path for every Associate. It gives the State the benefit of its baseline argument for purposes of the dispositive comparison and still rejects both summary-judgment grounds. Its strongest objection is that a court must ordinarily identify a baseline before deciding whether a change exists. Here a sufficient comparison is identified: the very Old System the State says controls. The Court need not settle an alternative benchmark where the State loses its complete-restoration defense even on its own premise. Component-specific comparison and actual submission can then guide lawful relief; this is not an assertion that every New System feature necessarily differs from every conceivable baseline.

The remaining practical objection is the burden of operating federally required registration while awaiting approval. Actual Lopez treats difficulty as relevant to fitted lawful relief, not as approval of the governmental plan. A genuinely judicial temporary arrangement would require independent lawful authority and the complete Lopez qualifications; a judicial label cannot exempt the State's own policy choices. No extraordinary circumstance or completed-election remedy is supplied by the new fact that no election occurred during the earlier provisional interval.
