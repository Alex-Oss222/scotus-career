## 21. Atherton v. FDIC — No. 95-928

### Reconciled result and component rules

All eight Associates support **vacating the Third Circuit judgment and remanding**, replacing the provisional A4 label “reverse/remand.” The A1–A3 substantive commitments remain, with the following complete scope. The case was argued November 4, 1996 and is scheduled for January 14, 1997; the review is of the interlocutory §1292(b) judgment through §1254(1), not a liability judgment.

**A1 — Reach the source question.** Whether an asserted federal-common-law duty exists is an included legal argument concerning the same restored federal claims and the petition's question about §1821(k)'s displacement or preservation of those claims. The Court may decide it on this record, where both sides have addressed it and no new claim, factual findings or requested relief is introduced. This is a bounded exercise of review, not a universal exception for antecedent questions. Yee distinguishes an argument from a separate claim while preserving the independent question-presented limit; United States National Bank permits decision of necessary governing law after a fair opportunity to address it; Lebron permits discretionary consideration of an included argument on an established record. Izumi is different because a separate adverse intervention judgment had to be displaced before the question presented could be reached. The state-claim withdrawal remains a different procedural matter.

**A2 — No general federal corporate-duty rule for these claims.** State law supplies the ordinary duty rules for these acquired corporate-governance claims unless enacted federal law or a specifically demonstrated significant conflict with an identified federal policy requires displacement. Federal chartering, federal insurance, the receiver's desire to recover and general administrative uniformity do not establish such a conflict on the supplied record. O'Melveny's independently sufficient pre-FIRREA ground applies the Erie/Wallis conflict requirement; its separate conditional FIRREA construction is not a retroactivity decision. Kimbell Foods distinguishes federal program rights and rejects automatic national uniformity even there. No identified charter command or operational obligation requires a different result here. This rule neither abolishes exceptional federal common law nor holds that a federal charter can never be material.

**A3 — Section 1821(k)'s authorization and savings sentence.** The provision covers insured depository institutions whether federally or state chartered. In a civil action by, on behalf of, or at the request or direction of the Corporation, prosecuted wholly or partly for its benefit, its authorization applies when the Corporation acts as receiver or conservator; acts on a claim purchased from, assigned by, or otherwise conveyed by such receiver or conservator; or acts on a claim purchased from, assigned by, or otherwise conveyed wholly or partly by an insured institution or its affiliate in connection with §1823 assistance. It permits personal monetary liability for gross negligence, similar conduct, or conduct demonstrating a greater disregard of duty, including intentional tortious conduct, with the culpability terms defined under applicable state law. When applicable it prevents a more protective state liability shield from defeating that authorization. It does not itself find breach, causation, absence of defenses or an amount due.

The final sentence preserves rights actually available under other applicable law, including a state-law duty allowing recovery for less culpable conduct, such as ordinary negligence. Gross negligence is therefore a federal floor, not a ceiling excluding stricter state standards. The savings language is not restricted to other provisions of the same federal enactment. It preserves rather than creates other rights; it neither manufactures a federal common-law cause nor reinstates withdrawn pleadings. Both the coverage clause and savings clause operate by their enacted terms. The proposed common explanation relies on this text and the existing source-of-law rule, not legislative history.

**A4 — Vacatur and bounded remand.** Vacate the reviewed appellate judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with the rejection of its federal-common-law premise and its exclusion of federally chartered institutions from §1821(k). The Third Circuit reserved the content of the proposed federal standard, including ordinary versus gross negligence; do not describe it as a completed holding of ordinary-negligence liability. The statutory pleading remains subject to ordinary adjudication. State claims were treated as withdrawn below, and this disposition does not revive them. The effect of that treatment, permissible amendment and any properly available state theory remain for the lower courts. The precise New Jersey standard, conduct, liability, damages and FIRREA's temporal reach are not decided. Rejecting the asserted federal-common-law rule is independently sufficient without applying FIRREA to earlier conduct; the statutory construction states the section's operation when applicable.

### Definitive component propositions and remedies

**B1 — Forum and action are separate.** Section 1350 confers original jurisdiction over an alien's civil tort action alleging a violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty. These supported official-torture allegations are within that category and not insubstantial. Jurisdiction does not by itself supply a domestic damages action, establish liability, waive sovereign immunity or authorize every asserted international claim. All eight Associates adopt this separation and the present forum conclusion.

**B2 — Domestic action for this established personal wrong, six affirmative commitments.** The statute's historical reception of judicial adjudication of law-of-nations torts, together with the received common-law treatment of the particular prohibition, supports a domestic action against the individual for his own deliberate participation in or personal supervision of official torture. The operative norm forbids severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation. International law identifies this wrong; the confined domestic common-law action permits its enforcement in the congressionally supplied forum. Neither the jurisdictional label alone nor universal condemnation alone supplies the action.

This specific reception differs from Suter's administrative-plan provisions and the new receiver preference rejected in O'Melveny. Central Bank and Musick, Peeler support bounded development of a received action only within their actual holdings; they do not enact an ATS cause or make professional status sufficient. Filartiga, including the 1984 remedial decision, is persuasive evidence of the particular tort's established judicial treatment. The Court need not adopt the lower court's general Lincoln Mills analogy or legislative-history premise to sustain this confined action. No rule for every customary norm, private nonstate violence, corporate liability, official title alone or an absent superior follows.

**B2 contrary position — Scalia and Thomas.** The jurisdictional sentence and the established substantive prohibition do not demonstrate congressional authorization to create this asserted domestic federal damages action. Historical availability of a forum for independently actionable international torts does not necessarily authorize judicial recognition of every later norm. The lower-court practice and the statute's common-law setting are the strongest affirmative grounds, but these two Justices find them insufficient for this asserted action. Suter's source inquiry, O'Melveny's restraint and Meyer's distinction between federal jurisdiction/consent and cause support that conclusion. The binding Central Bank holding is acknowledged at its confined received-securities scope; its settled Supreme Court premise is absent here. This is disagreement over domestic action authority, not approval of the conduct.

**B3 — Compensation, six affirmative commitments.** Once B2's confined action is recognized, compensation may redress proved injury caused by the individual's qualifying conduct. Traditional tort reparation supports this remedy, while Franklin preserves the distinction between an existing action and appropriate relief. The trial findings, not allegations alone or Negewo's title, sustain the categorical authority of the compensatory judgment. Scalia and Thomas reject this relief on this asserted action because they reject B2, not because a valid intentional-tort action can never compensate injury.

**B4 — Punitive authority separately justified, six affirmative commitments.** The domestic common-law action permits punitive damages for malicious, willful or equivalently reckless aggravated torture; liability does not require punishment in every case. Arms supplies the traditional distinction between compensation and punishment for aggravated personal wrongdoing, and the 1984 Filartiga decision supplies a persuasive application to official torture. Franklin did not decide punitive authority. The action's common-law remedial character and independently justified aggravated-tort remedy, not Franklin's silence or international condemnation alone, supply this conclusion. The proved intentional personal torture supports availability here. No sovereign or population is punished and no criminal conviction is entered.

The strongest separate objection is that recognizing compensation need not authorize penal relief with international consequences. The six answer it through this confined domestic tort and traditional punitive remedy, while requiring the availability, culpability and amount questions to remain distinct. Filartiga's default, its conclusion that Paraguay would not prosecute, its choice-of-law findings and its dollar amounts are not facts or necessary conditions imported into this case. TXO and BMW retain their actual procedural and substantive authority and disagreements. The granted categorical authority question does not establish an independent amount challenge, prove wealth, supply an assessment method, require a ratio or certify every possible calculation. Scalia and Thomas express no separate categorical rule against punitive damages in an otherwise authorized action; their negative B4 disposition follows the rejected B2 source.

**B5 — No categorical political-question bar, all eight commitments.** The present claim requires adjudicating personal conduct under the established prohibition and applying ordinary remedial law. No supported issue requires judging the legitimacy of the Ethiopian government, directing American diplomacy or resolving a constitutionally committed war-powers dispute. Baker's issue-specific inquiry, retained in Montana and Nixon, requires the actual commitment or manageability obstacle; a foreign setting alone is insufficient. Sanchez-Espinoza concerned United States foreign-policy operations and domestic sovereign immunity, and its footnote 5 expressly distinguished suits against foreign officials. It therefore supplies neither an immunity holding nor a categorical political-question bar for Negewo. This ground does not itself create the action or dispose of unpresented defenses.

**B6 — Exact judgment choices.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the judgment against the three granted categorical objections. Each plaintiff's $200,000 compensation and $300,000 punitive award remains distinct; the aggregate is $1,500,000. The supported findings are Negewo's direct participation and personal supervision concerning Abebe-Jira and Taye, and his personal supervision of some torture concerning Demissie. The rule does not attribute every blow or every disappearance to him or substitute office for participation. Affirmance rests on the proved torture ground and does not announce an independent rule for every cruel, inhuman or degrading act.

Scalia and Thomas reverse the §1350 damages judgment and remand consistently with the absence of this asserted action. Their disposition reaches both compensation and punishment through that sufficient source ground. Neither path adds a TVPA theory, supplies retrospective amendment, adjudicates the ungranted limitations or counsel questions, decides a new immunity defense, reallocates trial damages or finds additional facts.
