## Reachability and proposed judgment

All eight Associate Justices participate. The statutory direct appeal properly presents the coverage and prior-submission rulings, not substantive discriminatory purpose or effect. Each commits to reverse the summary judgment for Mississippi and remand for enforcement of §5 with identification of the covered changes, their actual submission status and lawful relief. None orders the State to adopt a particular interim system, merge the federal and state rolls, or suspend the NVRA's valid federal-registration duties.

## Exact common propositions available for joinder

**Y1 — Discretionary implementation remains covered.** When a covered State changes a registration practice directly affecting voting, the federal origin of the occasion for change does not itself excuse §5 preclearance of the State's discretionary implementation choices. The NVRA's federal-registration obligations and non-displacement of the Voting Rights Act leave the two statutes operative together. This case need not determine how §5 applies to a particular feature compelled in every detail by federal law, leaving no state choice.

**Y2 — Restoration does not describe all of the New System.** Even assuming Mississippi's old system is the proper comparison baseline rather than the forty-one-day Provisional Plan, the post-February 10 arrangement contains new registration procedures and voter communications. Restoring old state-registration qualifications does not establish that manuals, instructions, agency choices, reporting and the administration of different federal/state registration status are unchanged. The State therefore is not entitled to judgment on the premise that the New System merely restores the old one. This sufficient ground does not decide whether every short-lived or state-law-defective practice is disqualified as a §5 baseline. The temporary plan's limited duration and lack of enacted state authorization do not license that broad rule.

**Y3 — Submission is change-specific.** Prior submission of an NVRA implementation plan does not preclear a later discretionary change not identified in that submission. On the supplied record the later dual system was not submitted; the district court cannot treat earlier approval as blanket approval of its unsubmitted features. If the reach of a particular submission becomes genuinely disputed on remand, its actual terms govern. The holding does not invent DOJ correspondence or construe an unrecovered submission.

**Y4 — Sequence and relief.** Covered unprecleared changes may not be administered as an ordinary registration system without §5 approval. The enforcement court identifies coverage and submission and fashions lawful relief; the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia decides substantive preclearance. The remand must preserve the valid federal-registration duties and consider the actual practices and timing. No discriminatory purpose, retrogression, damages, completed-election consequence, particular interim plan, or identical-roll requirement is adjudged.

## Adversarial comparison and join boundaries

**Path A: affirm as restoration or federal necessity.** The State's strongest position is that a plan never validly established under state law cannot force permanent expansion of state registration, and NVRA compliance is mandatory. That position preserves the State's objection to using the Provisional Plan as the baseline and correctly rejects an automatic identical-roll rule. It does not establish that the New System's independently chosen registration machinery existed under the Old System or was specifically submitted. All eight reject affirmance on the supplied record for that reason; none treats federal necessity as a finding that every implementation detail is compelled.

**Path B: reverse because the Provisional Plan must be the baseline and every retreat from it must be submitted.** The strongest argument is §5's attention to operative voting practices rather than labels. The strongest objection is the plan's brief provisional operation, abandonment and missing state authorization. The present judgment need not resolve that contest: the New System includes changes even from the State's preferred Old System. All eight select the common narrower sufficient ground Y2. A later request for a definitive Provisional Plan baseline holding requires supported source review and fresh affected commitments; it is not supplied by these rows.

**Path C: reverse for actual new discretionary practices, reserve ultimate preclearance and remand for lawful relief.** This path gives effect to both federal statutes, answers both stated summary-judgment grounds, and follows the actual Lopez joins without automatically extending that case's state-compulsion holding to every federal mandate. Its strongest objection is the possible practical difficulty of separate registration systems and submission. Under Lopez, implementation difficulty affects fitted lawful relief, not whether an identified discretionary voting change was submitted. All eight adopt Y1–Y4 at their stated level of generality.
