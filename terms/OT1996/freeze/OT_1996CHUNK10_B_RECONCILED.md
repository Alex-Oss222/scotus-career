## 115. Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash — June 16, 1997

### Reconciled components and grounds

**R1 — Replacement value and remand:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse and remand. Section 506(a)'s first sentence identifies the creditor's interest in the estate's collateral and separates secured from unsecured portions; it does not choose the valuation market. The second directs attention to the valuation's purpose and the proposed disposition or use. For the debtor's §1325(a)(5)(B) retention and use, the appropriate benchmark is the price a willing buyer in the debtor's trade, business or situation would pay a willing seller for comparable property of like age and condition. An averted foreclosure is not the proposed use.

The standard does not require new-property cost, a universal retail price, debtor-specific earning value, a midpoint between incompatible methods, or unrestricted case-by-case choice of a different governing standard. The bankruptcy court determines the appropriate evidentiary measure of replacement value for this debtor and property. If a retail measure separately includes benefits the debtor does not receive by retaining the truck—such as warranties, inventory storage or reconditioning—appropriate adjustment excludes them. Accessories or modifications outside the creditor's lien cannot enlarge its secured value. That is not a mechanical deduction of all dealer profit or overhead from a demonstrated comparable-market price.

**R2 — Stevens's creditor-interest alternative:** Affirm the existing valuation judgment. Section 506(a)'s creditor-interest language and §1325(a)(5)'s present-value purpose favor what the lienholder could realize with the collateral in its hands. The proposed-use words retain functions elsewhere in the Code without requiring different retention and surrender valuations here. Time-value payments and adequate protection answer the asserted risk and depreciation concerns; granting more shifts value from unsecured creditors without sufficient text.

## 117. Mazurek v. Armstrong — June 16, 1997

### Reconciled components and exact governing standard

**M1 — Corrective summary disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support summary review, vacatur of the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand under actual Casey/Fargo and ordinary preliminary-relief standards. The District Court's suggestion that purpose is insulated whenever some approving legislator could have a health motive is wrong. The Ninth Circuit properly recognized the need for objective purpose analysis, but its purpose/substantial-obstacle formulation omits the controlling residual-interference branches. Neither court has supplied a complete assessment.

The remand must apply all six steps with their qualifications:

1. Identify the actual enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, sanctions and clinical circumstances. Before viability the patient need not prove medical necessity or obtain official approval of her reasons. Prohibition, effective third-party control and denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violate the baseline; care cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration.
2. Identify actual function and permissible objective. Obstructive purpose objectively established from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty. Opposition to abortion or sponsorship by opponents is not itself that showing. Truthful persuasion and legitimate medical regulation remain possible; medical claims require medical support.
3. Challengers establish concrete, nontrivial attributable additional interference, a stable population defined by the duty before injury is determined, and causal comparison with actual conditions absent the challenged duty. Consider supported subgroups and cumulative effects without double counting. Capacity, cost, delay, travel and alternatives matter only in their actual, usable operation. Reliable prospective evidence can suffice; there is no universal monetary, mileage or hourly threshold.
4. Baseline violation, prohibited purpose or a substantial obstacle is independently invalid and cannot be saved by benefits. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs timely, clinically appropriate care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible justification and fit, including an answer to an identified concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective. The State may show inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences. It need not select the cheapest imaginable policy, disprove hypothetical alternatives, produce a new study in every case, prove scientific certainty or calculate numerical benefit. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends, while purpose, other constitutional protections and ordinary legality remain.
5. Assess medical justification using competent evidence, professional standards, patient circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Professional agreement deserves serious weight without a veto; neither bare professional assertions nor unsupported official preferences are conclusive. Necessary care and clinical judgment remain protected, with no unnecessary prior-permission barrier. Actual fault requirements remain distinct; later reasonable medical disagreement alone establishes no offense. No compelled-communication claim is presented here.
6. Identify the failing duty or application and match preliminary or final relief to the proved population, violation and equitable prerequisites. Preserve severable lawful duties and address actual enforcement dependencies. Other patients' compliance does not defeat individual claims; broader relief requires corresponding proof. An indivisible mandatory condition may fail when ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference without sufficient justification; otherwise relief remains confined to supported applications or groups. No independent numerical facial threshold or automatic all-applications bar applies.

Cahill's previously approved, supervised practice is a concrete alternative to examine at the applicable tier. It is not itself proof of patient burden, a constitutionally sufficient alternative, a lack of all medical justification, or an entitlement to a professional license. The absence of a substantial obstacle does not establish the absence of meaningful or severe interference. Conversely, targeting one practitioner and lack of a new study do not establish unconstitutional purpose or invalidity.

**M2 — Competing disposition:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the appellate judgment and reinstate the denial of preliminary relief on the present showing. Kennedy retains protected choice and necessary care but follows his published reasonable-regulation approach. Scalia and Thomas reject the constitutional foundation and heightened framework. Their common judgment does not make those rationales identical or authorize lower courts to disregard Casey.

## 118. Crawford-El v. Britton — June 16, 1997

### Reconciled components and grounds

**C1 — Proof quantum:** All eight reject the special clear-and-convincing burden for unconstitutional motive. The ordinary preponderance burden governs this retaliation claim, with legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence. Neither §1983, the underlying First Amendment rule, Harlow's objective qualified-immunity defense, nor the governing Federal Rules supplies the special burden. Kimberlin already separated the motive element from the defense and preserved ordinary procedural burdens, but expressly adopted no clear-and-convincing standard. The reserved quantum question is decided here on the additional lack-of-authority ground; it was not already adjudicated in Kimberlin.

**C2 — Discovery and immunity:** All eight reject a categorical requirement that proof meet the new heightened quantum before necessary, appropriately limited discovery may occur. Harlow permits early disposition of the legal defense on the actual conduct-date law; a motive allegation alone does not defeat it. Courts may require specific, nonconclusory factual support identifying the challenged conduct and cognizable injury, use rule-authorized clarification or replies, sequence or restrict discovery, issue protective orders and apply ordinary summary-judgment duties. A properly supported summary-judgment motion requires affirmative responsive evidence, not a general credibility attack. Necessary factual inquiry may be limited without deciding disputed credibility or demanding conclusive proof available only through discovery.

The lower court's Williams proof-before-discovery proposal was not a majority holding. D. Ginsburg allowed tailored discovery on a sufficiently specific forecast tied to the heightened burden; Edwards and four colleagues rejected that burden under ordinary Rules; Silberman proposed a distinct lawful-purpose immunity rule. None is attributed to all lower judges, and D. Ginsburg's writing is not Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg's position.

**C3 — Corrected remand:** Vacate the lower judgment insofar as it imposes the heightened quantum and directions dependent on it; remand with the existing retaliation/conversion remand otherwise preserved. The prior access-to-courts and due-process dispositions remain closed. The District's municipal issue retains its separate panel posture, and the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits.

**C4 — Rejection of the alternative categorical immunity:** A merely possible lawful reason is not itself a proved Mt. Healthy same-decision defense. Claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; defendant may prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. Neither some improper motive nor mixed motives alone establishes recovery. Kimberlin's objective legal defense remains available on the actual conduct-date notice, but cannot delete a motive element from the wrong. Settle supplies the current actual-causation distinction, not a school-specific doctrine automatically transplanted wholesale to prisons.
