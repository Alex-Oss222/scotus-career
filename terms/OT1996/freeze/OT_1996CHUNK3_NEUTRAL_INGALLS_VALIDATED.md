## Record and remedial limits

Jefferson Yates worked periodically for Ingalls from 1953 to 1967, later retired from unrelated work, and filed a disability claim in 1981. Ingalls admitted compensability in 1982 and entered an approved section 8(i) settlement in 1983. Between May 1981 and January 1984 he settled eight third-party actions without employer approval. Maggie Yates was not a named plaintiff, but signed releases of consortium and, in some settlements, future wrongful-death claims. He died January 28, 1986; the parties stipulated that asbestosis contributed to death. She filed her own section 9 claim in April 1986. These are distinct rights at distinct dates.

Three later wrongful-death settlements in 1987 and 1989 had Ingalls's written approval. The six nondependent children sought no LHWCA death benefits. The ALJ apportioned the recovery and initially allowed Ingalls a larger contractual credit; the Board reversed that credit and the Fifth Circuit upheld the widow's one-seventh apportionment, interpreting the settlement language as insufficient to surrender more. Ingalls did not claim a predeath-settlement offset under section 33(f). Do not issue a new allocation, adjudicate a benefit amount, or decide an ungranted contract issue.

The Director appeared as respondent in an employer-initiated appeal with adverse private parties. The Fifth Circuit denied the employer's motion to strike his brief under its White precedent and the distinct reservation in Newport News. The packet's phrase employee-initiated review is clerically incorrect for this case. The reviewing petition was Ingalls's; neither the Director nor Mrs. Yates initiated that Fifth Circuit review.

## Entering law and supported competing paths

Cowart (June 22, 1992) controls the timing of an existing substantive entitlement, with six direct joins: Kennedy, Stone, White, Scalia, Souter and Thomas. Receipt of payment, acknowledgment or formal award is not the trigger. It does not establish that every injury or possible future beneficiary already has a vested right. Its register is Vested LHWCA entitlement and third-party settlement approval, present operation June 22, 1992. For a covered settlement below compensation entitlement, section 33(g)(1) requires written employer-and-carrier approval before execution and filing within thirty days. Section 33(g)(2) gives notification an independent function for judgments and sufficiently large settlements. Do not announce that every third-party recovery requires prior approval or erase the notification branch.

Under section 9, the death benefit depends on death caused by the covered injury and a qualifying survivor. The 1984 section 9 amendment applies to deaths after September 28, 1984; Yates died in 1986. Before death Mrs. Yates might have died, divorced, or lacked a qualifying work-related death predicate. One supported path therefore applies Cowart's vesting rule to distinguish her contingent future claim and rejects section 33(g) forfeiture on these predeath settlements. The strongest contrary path reads entitlement prospectively to protect employer subrogation when a spouse releases claims that could later affect death-benefit recovery. That path must explain why the statutory entitlement threshold attaches before the substantive conditions occur; approval's protective purpose cannot simply delete the threshold. Existing widow-entitlement, statutory amounts and the ungranted section 33(f) credit remain distinct.

Newport News (March 21, 1995), under Administrative interest and LHWCA appellate aggrievement, holds that general administrative interest alone does not make the Director adversely affected or aggrieved for independent section 921(c) review of an unappealed employee award. Eight Justices joined O'Connor's rationale; Ginsburg agreed only with the statutory judgment. Respondent status was expressly reserved; no categorical governmental incapacity or new Article III rule controls here.

For respondent status, separately consider the then-effective Rule 15(a) naming requirement, the agency's institutional structure, section 921a representation, and the Department's designation of the Director. The affirmative path treats the Director as the Department's proper litigating representative while the Benefits Review Board is its adjudicator. The contrary path would confine the named agency to the Board or require a separate respondent interest. Neither path may convert a party-label dispute into Article III absence while the employer and widow supply a live controversy. The Director's ability to initiate or maintain litigation when every private party withdraws is outside this record. Deference to the Director on the merits is a separate issue and need not be resolved if enacted meaning suffices.
