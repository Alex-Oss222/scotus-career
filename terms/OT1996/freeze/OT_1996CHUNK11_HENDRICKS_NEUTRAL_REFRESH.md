## What the recovered original record supports

**Procurement and planned transition.** Davis testified that SRS published a clinical-treatment request for proposals, received two proposals, reviewed best and final offers, and was awaiting the Department of Administration's contract process. He believed both proposals met the requested qualifications, but could not certify that a proposal had yet been accepted or the contract finalized. The admitted exhibit was the request for proposals; the actual two responses were not included in that exhibit. The court overruled the exhibit objection. That establishes admission of the requirements document, not a judicial finding that a completed contract or fully staffed program existed.

Davis estimated that the contracted clinicians would begin approximately sixty days after signing. He described arrangements for existing Larned professionals to undertake preliminary program work during the interval. His source text has an apparent OCR anomaly in the verb describing those preliminary phases; this supplement does not treat that verb as a verified clinical description. In the adjacent testimony he assigned program operation and internal security to SRS and perimeter security to Corrections, and said SRS would provide its own program director rather than have the clinical contractor provide one. These are administrative arrangements and expectations, not proof of the content, quantity or effectiveness of treatment actually delivered to Hendricks.

**The prison-treatment offer and Hendricks's qualification.** Hendricks acknowledged that he was invited to participate in a prison sexual-offender program, that enrollment had no charge, that participation paid less than prison work, and that he did not attend. He said he did not know the program's contents and denied that his reason was a belief that he did not need treatment. Although he acknowledged choosing the higher-paid work, he rejected counsel's unqualified characterization as choosing money over treatment and said a named parole-board member made the choice for him. The excerpt does not explain that asserted involvement. It would be inaccurate either to erase the offered program and pay choice or to convert the qualification into an established coercive order. The testimony does not establish the offered program's therapeutic efficacy, suitability for his condition, duration, place in release decisions or equivalence to the later commitment program. His acknowledgment of no treatment during the imprisonment does not itself prove the State withheld all available treatment.

**October 20 staffing and procurement.** House, an SRS attorney called by Hendricks, testified that neither proposed contract had been signed. No dedicated professional program staff, clinical director, psychiatrist or psychologist had yet been hired specifically for the predator program. He distinguished already hired direct-care personnel from those professional positions. He also testified that existing Larned physicians, nurses, psychologists, social workers and therapists were available for interim services and that some staff had experience treating sexual dysfunctions, including pedophilia. Thus the absence of dedicated hires is not testimony that no professional personnel were available at all.

House could not explain why the bids were not accepted. An objection to the suggested explanation that contractors could not supply the staff was sustained for lack of foundation; the later compound summary question also drew a sustained objection. Neither counsel's suggestion nor the sustained questions supplies the missing reason. House expressly disclaimed clinical expertise and knowledge of the precise treatment modalities. His assertion that interim staff could provide substantial services is relevant testimony, but it is not a clinician's demonstrated treatment plan or proof of meaningful services already delivered.

**Cross-examination and redirect complete that account.** House said there was not yet a designated predator at Larned, that Hendricks would be the first, and that planning and staffing were proceeding in anticipation of a patient. He described individualized evaluation and treatment planning upon arrival and expected existing personnel to begin that process. Those statements concern actual availability and projected administration; they do not undo the legal commitment order or establish a particular transfer date. He knew that Dr. Befort had performed an evaluation but was not familiar with Befort's professional background. On redirect he again did not know the modalities, any planned change in them, or the content of group treatment. A promise to individualize care is not proof of delivered therapy; lack of his personal knowledge is not proof that no clinical plan existed elsewhere in the record.

**Lower-court rulings must be retained at their actual scope.** The original trial court found the proceedings civil, relying in part on distinct SRS custody, patient rights and Corrections' security role despite a prison setting. That was its legal classification and rationale, not a conclusive finding of effective treatment. More specifically, the October 20 court rejected the new-trial/dismissal contention because it believed SRS had established a treatment program that might be available in the future, and said a later response remained possible if it proved nonviable. It left the commitment in place and directed transport to begin treatment. This ruling confirms that the court did not rest on a finding that the complete promised specialist program was already operating for Hendricks. It also did not find deliberate withholding, contractual impossibility or a particular amount of actual therapy. The lower court's willingness to rely on planned treatment is itself part of the legal dispute, not a present decision that such reliance was sufficient.

## Statutory commands and implementation are different propositions

The existing final packet supplies the Act's care-and-treatment requirement, current qualifying-condition and danger predicates, review provisions and release mechanisms. Those commands remain legal text; procurement efforts, staffing, offered prison treatment and the courts' characterizations are evidence about implementation. The State's written or oral undertaking to supply care cannot prove compliance merely because the statute commands it. Conversely, a procurement failure or absence of a dedicated job title cannot by itself prove that the statute commanded punishment or that no interim clinical work was possible. Whether the defined condition justifies confinement and whether the scheme is punitive retain their separate constitutional inquiries under the current-law slice.

## Competing supported readings, without a selected conclusion

**A developing civil program.** The State can point to the RFP, two qualifying proposals, procurement steps, SRS administration, hired direct-care staff, available hospital professionals and intended individualized evaluation. The earlier prison program was offered rather than categorically withheld. These facts support a contention that the initial deficiencies reflect program development and procurement problems rather than an established policy deliberately to postpone all feasible treatment until punishment ended. The strongest contrary point is concrete: no promised specialist contract or dedicated professional program was in place at the October hearing, despite the earlier expectation, and the trial court accepted possible future availability. Planning and generic hospital resources do not establish meaningful treatment for the defined population.

**Confinement ahead of the promised treatment capacity.** Hendricks can point to the interval between the procurement assurances and the absence of a contract, the unfilled professional posts, the post-signature sixty-day estimate, House's inability to identify actual modalities, and the trial court's future-availability language. Those facts support questioning whether confinement began on a sufficiently developed therapeutic basis and how much weight the stated treatment purpose deserves. The strongest contrary point is that the same original record describes procurement efforts, interim professional resources, individualized planning and a prior treatment offer; it does not establish why contracting failed or show that an identified effective therapy was available and deliberately withheld. The evidence must be assessed as a whole, not reduced to either the presence of a statutory label or the absence of a specialist title.

The supported distinction is therefore between demonstrated initial deficiencies and a further inference about their cause, purpose and constitutional significance. No witness admission of deliberate delay is required before a court may evaluate circumstantial evidence under the correct legal rule; nor may this neutral handoff manufacture that factual inference. This supplement chooses neither startup failure nor deliberate deferral as an established account and supplies no new treatment adequacy or punishment holding.

## Specific remaining limits and readiness

The recovered excerpts do not establish why the two bids failed, the complete offered prison program, the meaning of the parole-board qualification, a particular feasible therapy intentionally deferred, the exact interim clinical plan, or the services Hendricks personally received after transfer. A conclusion requiring one of those facts needs an identified supported inference or targeted further source verification; an operator's inability to supply it is not adverse proof or a litigant default. No separate 1995 finding or later representation is admitted through this supplement.

The factual framing is ready for resumed independent modeling on the supported original record and current law. The model may assess these competing readings and their legal significance without assuming the unresolved facts. This refresh makes no change to the original grant, hearing schedule, participation, statutory text, current precedent, prior final packet or any completed Court action. Only an affected original-treatment premise needs reconsideration; no unaffected matter is reopened.
