## Material factual premises

The City adopted the circular seal in 1965 after a contest sponsored by the City Council and a newspaper. Its four quadrants show a steam engine and oil derrick, the Old North Tower associated with territorial higher education, a covered wagon and 1889 settlement date, and a recognizable Latin Christian cross. The cross is a prominent, discernible element, rather than an obscure shape in a reproduced historical document. No additional missionary inscription, rays, compulsory profession, threat, benefit exclusion, or religious office qualification is found.

The City itself selected and adopted the emblem, registered it as a trademark, and uses it to identify its own government on signs, flags, police and fire uniforms, vehicles, property stickers, utility bills, stationery, newsletters and its council chamber. Private design and contest origin do not preserve private ownership of the resulting governmental message. The cross remains one of four selected identifying subjects; the accompanying images are relevant context and are not ignored.

These are published record recitations and the supplied neutral facts. No new survey, historical motive, exact graphic dimensions, registration record, personal encounter detail or administrative practice is reconstructed. Plaintiffs' continuing personal exposure as residents or workers is accepted at the supplied, undisputed standing posture; citizenship or objection alone would not suffice.

## Exact propositions and joins available for assembly

**R1 — Jurisdiction and actual governmental attribution.** Repeated personal exposure to the City's continuing official practice, with prospective relief capable of ending that exposure, supplies the concrete connection for this challenge. This does not establish generalized-objector or taxpayer standing. An emblem selected and adopted by the City to authenticate its own ordinary acts is governmental expression even though a private resident initially designed it. Genuine private access under Pinette, Chabad and Rosenberger is therefore not this case. **All eight join.**

**R2 — Contextual effects ground.** Under Lemon's effects inquiry and Allegheny's contextual treatment of religious affiliation, the government may not communicate official preference for Christianity through this recognizable cross used pervasively as its continuing municipal identifier. An informed observer considers the composite design, actual history and official uses together; the City's secular historical purpose, three secular quadrants and witnesses' lack of offense do not neutralize the religious identification on this record. Neither religious imagery alone nor a tally of offended or comfortable residents supplies the test. No separate coercion showing is necessary for this governmental affiliation. **Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the rule, explanation and application. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this methodology.**

**R3 — Independently sufficient official-sectarian-identification ground.** The City may acknowledge religion's historical role, but it may not make a prominent Christian cross its own continuing badge of governmental authority throughout the ordinary operations identified here. This permanent, pervasive adoption identifies the civic institution with a particular faith; it exceeds incidental historical acknowledgment even when no resident is legally compelled to worship and even when the cross shares space with secular symbols. On this record the absence of a proselytizing inscription, a compelled profession or measurable religious exclusion does not remove that official identification. **All eight expressly join this bounded rule and application.**

R3 is not a rule against every religious reference in civic history, every religious place name, every depiction of an actual historic building, every inherited coat of arms, every holiday display or every privately sponsored cross on public property. The Court need not decide those configurations. Its trigger combines the recognizable sectarian emblem, the City's deliberate adoption as its own identifier, and the pervasive continuing authentication of present governmental business. The holding requires no finding about officials' private evangelizing motives and no presumption that government ownership alone establishes religion. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this specific ground without accepting R2's observer-based explanation or a universal ban on noncoercive religious acknowledgment. The five R2 Justices also adopt R3 directly; their support is not inferred mechanically from a broader position under Marks.

**R4 — Fitted relief and retained law.** Affirmance permits appropriate declaratory and prospective injunctive relief against continuing official use of this offending seal, with implementation settled below. It does not prescribe replacement artwork, a redesign deadline, destruction of historic materials, removal of private religious expression, or an independently adjudicated damages or fee award. Purpose and entanglement need not be independently resolved because the stated effects and identification grounds suffice. Lemon remains governing, and no comprehensive replacement framework is adopted. **All eight join R4.**

## Enhanced comparison and source-sensitive boundaries

**Sustain the seal as historical civic acknowledgment.** The City's strongest argument is the whole four-quadrant composition, its contest origin, its history since 1965 and the absence of compelled devotion or legal disability. Lynch and Kennedy's Allegheny writing preclude treating every public religious reference as an establishment, and actual Pinette/Chabad protect private religious speech. This path would be stronger for a reproduced historical artifact or an independently explained inherited family coat of arms. Those are not findings about Edmond's seal. The direct adoption and recurring governmental-authentication function defeat this path for all eight at R3's limited level. R2's five Justices independently find impermissible contextual endorsement. No one relies on a finding that secular quadrants are legally irrelevant.

**Invalidate any government emblem containing a recognizable cross.** That broader path exceeds the necessary decision. The complete lower opinion acknowledges context, distinguishes the Austin historical coat-of-arms example and notes the visibility qualification discussed in Friedman. Allegheny's separate writings disagree about method but do not establish a universal no-cross rule. All eight refuse a categorical prohibition independent of composition, function, attribution and history. R2 is contextual, and R3 concerns this continuing institutional identification.

**Apply only the school-pressure cases.** Actual Ingebretsen strengthens the distinction between governmental adoption and independently attributable private delivery, while showing exactly where Kennedy and the other two narrower Justices withheld joins. It does not automatically extend school-pressure reasoning to adults seeing municipal documents. The R2 five use their actual sponsorship and attribution commitments together with existing display law; the R3 three rely on actual sectarian adoption and the independently retained display boundary. Neither the Ingebretsen dissent nor its majority supplies an imagined finding about this City's conduct.

**Require additional factfinding or restore trial judgment because local witnesses viewed the emblem as history.** The actual municipal uses and sectarian character are not unresolved factual issues in the supplied record. A majority's comfort is not the constitutional rule, and no witness-credibility finding is displaced. Affirmance is supportable on the accepted facts without resolving all constitutional-fact review questions. An unsupported exact-size claim, alleged new motto, covert evangelical motive or supposed denial of civic benefits would require its own source and does not enter these commitments.
