## Greene v. Georgia - No. 96-5369

**Scheduled petition-stage date:** December 16, 1996.

**Stone's final certiorari position:** Grant certiorari, limited to the source and required scope of the state appellate review of the five challenged capital-juror exclusions.

**Stone's interim petition-management position:** None. This is not a relist, hold, or CVSG, and no earlier interim poll is invented.

**Exact question scope:** Whether Wainwright v. Witt's federal-habeas deference requires a state appellate court, on direct review of a capital judgment, to apply that deference to challenged for-cause juror exclusions, or whether the state court must identify the review permitted or required by its own appellate law subject to the governing federal constitutional rules. The question includes whether the Georgia judgment actually rests on federal compulsion or a sufficient independently selected state standard. It does not ask this Court to determine any of the five jurors' qualification, prescribe de novo review of all demeanor findings, or order a new guilt or sentencing trial.

**Case-specific institutional grounds:** The state supreme court affirmed a capital judgment while describing Witt as controlling both death qualification and appellate deference. That presents a focused question about what this Court's own precedent requires in a materially different procedural setting. Its resolution matters beyond one disputed voir-dire answer: the asserted source of deference governs how the appellate court evaluates all five preserved exclusions. Capital-jury impartiality and the accurate separation of federal collateral review from state direct review justify review even without a demonstrated interstate conflict.

The strongest reason to deny is that Georgia may permissibly choose substantial deference under state law and may reach the same result. Review might therefore yield only further appellate consideration. That possibility limits the question and potential relief but does not remove the institutional reason to determine whether the judgment treated federal law as obligatory when selecting its review method. The objection is to the asserted source of the method, not merely a demand to reweigh an individual juror's answers. The neutral record supports examining that source without resolving the full voir dire.

**Vehicle, preservation, and finality:** The supplied posture is direct review of the state supreme court's affirmance of the capital judgment. The five exclusions were objected to; the packet identifies the review-source question as preserved. The apparent reliance on federal law permits consideration of the federal basis under entering Long/Evans and current-term Robinette, while preserving any genuinely independent sufficient state ground. No unresolved lower proceeding or independent state ground is established that presently blocks the limited petition. This assessment does not decide whether Georgia's actual review was legally defective or whether any defect was material.

**Split, recurrence, percolation, and timing:** No interstate split or related pending matter is established by the supplied neutral materials; neither is invented. Recurrence is an institutional inference from the general capital-juror review method, not a finding about the number of affected cases. Further lower-court development is less valuable for this confined source question than for a new universal review standard. No material intervening authority or scheduled related event supplies a reason to hold. No Solicitor General participation is established, and the State's direct-review question supplies no identified need for a CVSG.

**Entering-law significance and limits:** Morgan preserves Witt's substantive substantial-impairment standard. Thompson distinguishes its former-habeas legal and factual review questions without prescribing a universal juror-review rule. Evans and Robinette preserve both federal review of interwoven grounds and independent state protection. These authorities show why the source question is intelligible and bounded; Stone's prior agreement or disagreement on their merits is not treated as a reason to grant or as a prediction of his answer here. AEDPA's federal-habeas amendments do not make this state direct appeal a collateral proceeding.

## United States v. Watts; United States v. Putra - No. 95-1906

**Scheduled petition-stage date:** January 6, 1997. One Rule 12.4 petition covers two separate Ninth Circuit judgments; no prior grant is inferred from the packet's superseded "Question Granted" wording or reporter citation.

**Stone's final certiorari position:** Grant certiorari on the categorical acquitted-conduct question in both judgments. Stone favors plenary consideration of that question.

**Stone's interim petition-management position:** None. This is not a relist, hold, or CVSG, and no earlier interim poll is invented.

**Exact question scope:** Whether, under 18 U.S.C. section 3661, the then-mandatory Sentencing Guidelines, and the Double Jeopardy Clause, Due Process Clause, and jury-trial guarantee, a federal sentencing court may increase the binding Guidelines range for an offense of conviction by finding by a preponderance conduct underlying a general acquittal; and what legal effect that acquittal has on the sentencing finding. The question reaches Watts's firearm adjustment and Putra's additional drug-transaction aggregation separately. It does not request an exact sentence, a factual finding of disproportionate sentence magnitude, or treatment of a special verdict absent from the supplied record.

**Case-specific institutional grounds:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical prohibition governs a recurring federal sentencing practice and the operative effect of an acquittal. The two judgments present distinct common applications of the same rule: a firearm adjustment and aggregation of drug quantity. Together they permit the Court to examine the common legal question without making one offense's wording stand for all sentencing facts. The Government directly seeks review, and uniform administration of the mandatory federal Guidelines gives the question national importance even though the supplied neutral materials do not establish an intercircuit split.

Witte expressly leaves acquitted conduct and a complete sentencing-jury test open. Gaudin protects offense elements without deciding that every sentencing fact is an element. Current-term Roy likewise supplies no sentencing classification. Thus review would address an unresolved boundary of existing law with actual consequences for both judgments, rather than resolve an abstract disagreement. These reservations support taking the case; they do not dictate which side should win.

The strongest argument to defer is that fuller lower-court development could distinguish genuinely different enhancement predicates or exceptional sentence effects. Exact ranges and final sentence figures also are absent. Those limits counsel against summary resolution of a broad constitutional framework and against any exact sentencing command; they do not defeat review of the preserved categorical rule. Stone's preference for plenary consideration rests on the independent statutory, double-jeopardy, due-process, and jury questions and their possible interaction, not on an anticipated merits result.

**Vehicle, preservation, and finality:** Both sentencing objections are preserved. Each Ninth Circuit judgment vacated a sentence on the categorical rule and requires its own ultimate mandate. Further resentencing proceedings remain, so no completed final resentencing judgment is invented. The supplied federal appellate petition route nevertheless presents a conclusive legal rule that controls those proceedings, and no jurisdictional impediment to the petition is identified. The general acquittals, relevant district-court preponderance findings, and mandatory range effects supply the premises for categorical review. Missing presentence reports, exact ranges, or a special verdict create a possible vehicle barrier only for a theory requiring those additional facts, which this grant excludes.

**Split, recurrence, percolation, and timing:** No specific contrary circuit holding is established and no circuit conflict is asserted as fact. Putra's lower-court dissent shows a developed disagreement within that judgment; it is not itself an intercircuit split. The two applications and the nationwide mandatory Guidelines give the recurring question sufficient institutional weight without an invented numerical recurrence estimate. No related pending matter or identified imminent change makes a hold useful. The United States is the petitioner; no separate Solicitor General filing or additional position is inferred, and a CVSG offers no identified information need on the present neutral record. Further record development might matter to a later fact-dependent claim, but is not necessary before granting this categorical question.

**Separate merits treatment:** Preference for plenary review is a petition-management choice. It supplies no vote on the validity of either sentence, no summary reversal or affirmance vote, no constitutional holding, and no instruction automatically to restore or recalculate either sentence. Any Court decision to proceed summarily still requires an independently authorized Stone merits position; this handoff cannot fill it.
