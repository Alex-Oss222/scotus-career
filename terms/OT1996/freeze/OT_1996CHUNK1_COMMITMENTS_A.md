## 1. Department of State v. Legal Assistance for Vietnamese Asylum Seekers, No. 95-1521

**Event:** Argued post-grant disposition, October 21, 1996; argument October 15. Review of the D.C. Circuit statutory judgment, with the newly enacted visa-processing provision and its remedial consequences within the supplied scope. Cutoff: October 21, 1996.

**Entering law:** The Standards rule *Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct* operates through *Rivers*, April 26, 1994; *Landgraf* supplies the provision-specific sequence, and a prospective-remedy label alone does not resolve the status of reparative relief. *Grant, vacatur and remand after a material development*, effective *Lawrence*, January 8, 1996, requires reasonable probability of material reconsideration and independent equitable justification. Section 633 removes a limitation in §1152(a)(1) concerning visa-processing procedures and locations; its enacted text does not itself make this entire case moot, declare all discretion unreviewable or dispose of the APA and constitutional claims.

### Decisional alternatives and common limits

**Limited vacatur and remand:** The new provision addresses the precise processing-location premise supporting the statutory judgment. There is a reasonable probability that the D.C. Circuit will reconsider an outcome-material part of that judgment or its remedy. Vacatur need not pronounce the previous interpretation wrong or decide retrospective reach. The strongest objection is that it disturbs a judgment protecting matured claims and may produce harmful delay, including respondents' specifically asserted forcible-repatriation risk. A permissible remand must preserve consideration of reparative relief and the independent claims, permit prompt application for relief within the receiving courts' authority, and avoid reopening independently closed matters. It cannot promise that Hong Kong will continue protection or that any United States court can bind it. Those limits reduce the legal prejudice of reconsideration; they do not establish that practical danger is absent.

**Dismissal as improvidently granted:** The prospective issue that warranted review has materially changed while respondents assert a closed set of pre-enactment claims. Dismissal leaves the adjudicated judgment in place and allows the enactment's actual effect on continuing relief to be considered through lawful lower-court proceedings. The strongest objection is that the judgment retains a statutory premise Congress specifically addressed. The answer for a DIG proponent is that the new text supplies no finding that pre-enactment liability or reparative relief is eliminated and that the Court can decline discretionary review without freezing the law governing future relief. DIG does not affirm the merits or exempt the lower courts from §633.

A merits construction of §633 is legally available but does not alone dispose of all claims. No Associate selects whole-case mootness or an immediate final ruling on the constitutional or APA claims. Neither path below makes the asserted Hong Kong undertaking, its precise expiration, actual repatriation or the October 11 declaration's contents an adjudicated fact.

## 2. California v. Roy, No. 95-2025

**Event:** Non-argued review of a federal habeas judgment, November 4, 1996. Only the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions are at issue. The Clark second-degree conviction is outside this petition; the reversed special-circumstance finding is evidence relevant to the charge, not a punishment to restore. Cutoff: November 4, 1996.

**Entering law:** *Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review*, present operation *O'Neal*, February 21, 1995, supplies State-borne *Chapman* review for the specified preserved personal-intent category. Roy's omitted purpose-to-facilitate instruction presents that personal-intent concern by omission rather than through the combined charge and argument in *O'Neal*. That difference does not, by itself, justify replacing current law with historical *Brecht*. *A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error*, effective *Sullivan*, June 1, 1993, concerns invalidity of the governing standard for the entire verdict. *Jury determination of criminal materiality*, effective *Gaudin*, June 19, 1995, requires the jury to decide an element but reserves universal structural treatment.

### Component comparison

**Classification:** All eight treat this omission as susceptible to an appropriately constrained harmless-error inquiry. The strongest automatic-reversal argument is that a judge may not supply a missing jury finding. *Carella*, *Sullivan* and *Gaudin* establish the importance of that objection, but *Carella* permits harmlessness where the actual findings necessarily encompass the missing element, and *Sullivan* addresses a defect that vitiates every reasonable-doubt finding. A categorical structural holding about all omitted elements is neither compelled nor adopted.

**Standard and inquiry:** The governing Court rule is the State's burden to show beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the error did not contribute to the verdict. The Ninth Circuit did not announce automatic structural error: it used an actual-necessary-findings restriction to generate grave doubt under historical collateral doctrine. The strongest objection to remand is that failure under that less protective historical test should necessarily establish failure under *Chapman*. That inference fails if the lower court's grave doubt came from an unduly exclusive legal method rather than a full application of the controlling actual-verdict inquiry. Conversely, strong evidence of guilt alone is not the required noncontribution finding; a hypothetical correct jury cannot simply replace this jury. The degree to which actual necessarily found facts constrain review remains Scalia's particular join barrier below.

**Remedy:** A legal-standard remand can correct the inquiry without deciding the complete-record question. If a properly available violation is established and the State fails the controlling *Chapman* showing, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected Mannix convictions. A proper harmlessness determination permits habeas denial. This handoff finds neither harmlessness nor entitlement to an immediate writ; it supplies no AEDPA temporal holding or new independent habeas gate.

## 3. Lopez v. Monterey County, No. 95-1201

**Event:** Direct appeal from the three-judge district court; argued October 8, decision November 6, 1996. The supplied questions include state compulsion, electorate changes, designation, prospective laches and interim authority; they are not falsely attributed to the narrower printed question in appellants' brief. Cutoff: November 6, 1996.

**Entering law:** *Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority*, effective *Presley*, January 27, 1992, distinguishes changes in who votes or how officials are selected from a transfer of governing duties. *Voting Rights Act preclearance of primary-like party participation fees*, effective *Morse*, March 27, 1996, preserves the coverage/substantive-preclearance distinction. *The designated channel for preclearance review*, effective *Shaw*, June 28, 1993, reserves the proper federal channels. *Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury*, clarified by *Johnson v. Miller*, June 29, 1995, rejects a constitutional violation based only on racial predominance in a shared territorial electorate. Section 5's enact-or-administer language and §4(b)'s review restriction remain separate from §4(a)'s D.D.C. bailout route.

### Components and competing paths

**1. Covered administration despite a state mandate:** The County administers the changed judicial elections. An asserted lack of local choice does not make the administered change cease to be a change within a covered subdivision. The strongest opposing construction treats the noncovered State as the sole relevant decisionmaker and reads seeking to administer as requiring voluntary local policy. That would allow the origin of a mandate to remove a covered County's actual election administration from the statute. The text separately reaches enactment and administration; requiring nonuse pending lawful preclearance does not generally subject California outside its covered subdivisions to §5 or confer power to redesign its judiciary.

**2. Direct voting change, submission and defenses:** Replacing nine district electorates with a countywide electorate directly changes the voters selecting the judges. *Presley*'s internal-governance rule does not exempt it. The supplied absence of submission of earlier ordinances is not cured merely by a later related state submission; *Clark* and *McCain* require identification of each change and resolve ambiguity against the submitter. The exact reach of the 1983 submission concerning the final ordinance remains for determination on its actual terms. The §4(b) determination cannot be retried in this enforcement action; §4(a)'s separate statutory route is not a license for collateral invalidation. Laches does not make an uncleared change available for prospective use simply because the consolidation is old. Reliance and the timing of enforcement remain relevant to fitted relief; no general ruling on every retrospective equitable claim is made.

**3. Court order and interim remedy:** A governmental plan does not become wholly judicial merely because the district court orders it used. *Connor* and *McDaniel* preserve a genuine court-developed-plan distinction; *Clark* rejects substituting ordinary equitable convenience for preclearance. The strongest contrary argument is the harm of prolonged failure to elect judges and interference with state judicial structure. The already used temporary four-division arrangement, precleared March 6, 1995, is a material possible remedy, but its preclearance does not decide state-law linkage, minimum population, municipality or residence objections. Its terms reportedly end in January 1997. Nor can racial predominance alone, the concern identified in the district court's account, establish its constitutional invalidity under current *Johnson*. No Associate selects or permanently validates that plan here.

An extreme circumstance of the sort reserved in *Clark* requires its own supported urgency and equitable basis; prolonged awareness of the problem and authorization of ordinary six-year terms do not establish it. The lawful selected remedy is to reverse the permission to administer the uncleared governmental system and remand for enforcement and a lawfully supported interim arrangement. No actual March election, cancellation, vacancy, holdover, removal of a judge, or disposition of completed judicial acts is presumed.

## 5. Ohio v. Robinette, No. 95-891

**Event:** Argued review of the Ohio Supreme Court suppression judgment, November 18, 1996. Cutoff: November 18, 1996. The judgment has both a continued-detention ground and a release-warning rule and invokes both the Fourth Amendment and Article I, §14 of the Ohio Constitution.

**Entering law:** *Evans*, March 1, 1995, retains *Long*'s federal/interwoven-ground presumption absent a clear independently sufficient state ground. *Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop*, effective *Whren*, June 10, 1996, validates the ordinary objectively justified stop without inquiry into uncommunicated motive, while retaining independent execution and duration limits. *Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause*, effective *Ornelas*, May 28, 1996, separates historical facts and credibility from ultimate sufficiency without deciding the review allocation for every consent question. *Mimms* addresses exit orders during lawful stops; *Schneckloth* requires voluntary consent under all circumstances without indispensable advice of a right to refuse. *Bostick* distinguishes freedom to decline or terminate from automatic seizure rules. *Royer* and *Brown* require a separate inquiry into whether consent is independent of prior illegality.

### Components and alternatives

**Jurisdiction:** Under current *Long/Evans*, Ohio's federal explanation is intertwined with its state reference and does not clearly announce an independently sufficient state ground. The contrary position, maintained by Stevens and Ginsburg in their own published *Evans* writings, would require affirmative assurance of federal authority and decline review when a sufficient state basis may support the result. This disagreement is not converted into a finding that Ohio made an express independent-ground statement. State authority to provide greater protection remains under either approach.

**Federal warning and detention:** A universal federal requirement of a release script cannot replace the objective seizure and total-circumstances consent inquiries. The strongest opposing consideration is that an uninterrupted exercise of roadside authority can make a nominal request function as a command. That is material to the actual encounter, not sufficient to make a fixed spoken formula constitutionally indispensable in every case. Rejection of the script alone cannot reverse the result on the second lower ground.

The detention holding's reliance on the officer's private decision not to issue a ticket and his unrelated motive is inconsistent with *Whren/Mimms* insofar as the lawful traffic business was still objectively being completed. The supplied facts nevertheless require a separate inquiry after completion of that business. Returning the license and issuing the warning are strong evidence of transition, but the actual follow-up phrase and continuity of authority also matter. Neither secret willingness to permit refusal nor Robinette's subjective account alone establishes the objective legal result.

**Consent, attenuation and remedy:** Trial findings of clear completion, a nonoverbearing manner, wording, positioning and education must be respected at their factual level and evaluated under the correct constitutional rule. Robinette's account includes both an initial sense of freedom and a later feeling that he could not refuse. No universal factual conclusion is extracted from either fragment. If an unsupported detention remained, voluntary consent and its independence from the detention are distinct inquiries; suppression is not automatic solely because illegality occurred. The six review-and-remand positions below reject the defective federal rules but require disposition of both lower grounds, voluntary consent and, if necessary, attenuation. They do not reinstate the conviction or order evidence admitted.
