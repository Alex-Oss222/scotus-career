## 28. Babbitt v. Youpee, No. 95-1595

Argued December 2, 1996; scheduled decision January 21, 1997. Review of the Ninth Circuit's September 5, 1995 judgment, 67 F.3d 194, affirming declaratory and injunctive relief against uncompensated escheat of the disputed interests.

### Common threshold and controlling distinctions

All eight Associates would reach the granted Fifth Amendment question. The descendants suffer concrete loss of the interests devised to them. Irving's representative-standing ground addresses the conflict inherent in asking the Secretary, who administers the challenged escheat, to vindicate the decedent's surviving property claim. The Government conceded standing below. The administrative board's inability to decide the constitutional question supplies no merits adjudication or exhaustion defect requiring invention of further proceedings. No supplied material establishes a jurisdictional or preservation obstacle to review of the final federal judgment.

The statutory inquiry uses the 1984 reforms, with the death-date temporal qualification in the neutral receipt. The amended trigger concerns an interest of 2 percent or less incapable of earning $100 in any one of the five years from death. Earnings below $100 in any one of the preceding five years create a rebuttable presumption of that prospective incapacity; they are not a conclusive backward-looking substitute. Subsection (b) permits devise to another owner of an undivided fractional interest in the same parcel. Subsection (c) separately permits a Secretary-approved tribal disposition code taking precedence over subsection (a), subject to the prohibition on approving a code that fails the purpose of preventing further descent or fractionation of escheatable interests. The code option is not an operative code for this estate. The qualified reported account that codes apparently had not been developed is not converted into a finding about every tribe.

The reported $1,239 aggregate value concerns these disputed interests, not their annual income or a new judicial appraisal. Each existing fraction was devised to one descendant; none of the recipients held an existing interest in the relevant allotment. These devises preserve existing fractions without consolidating full parcels. The administrative record reports no rebuttal of the presumption. That establishes the recorded probate posture, not a general failure to prove the constitutional claim.

The legal baseline is Hodel v. Irving, 481 U.S. 704, 712–718 (1987), read with Penn Central's impact, expectations, and character inquiry. Irving reserved the amended statute. Its succession holding does not depend on extinction of every lifetime use, but it also does not make every restriction of testamentary choice a taking. The simulated Lucas holding, June 29, 1992, addresses proved complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use and preserves contextual analysis for partial burdens. Dolan, June 24, 1994, addresses individualized permit dedications. Neither supplies an automatic alternative disposition here. The simulated Bennis decision, March 4, 1996, preserves the distinction between an independently lawful forfeiture and an asserted compensation exemption; this estate presents neither proved misuse nor the forfeiture setting decided there.

## 29. Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth

Final Massachusetts judgment: 420 Mass. 749, 652 N.E.2d 580, July 17, 1995. The authorized simulated merits grant, November 4, 1996 argument, and January 21, 1997 consideration are procedural stipulations, not a historical petition disposition. The three granted federal constitutional questions concern parental liberty, free exercise, and compulsory parental exclusion/notification provisions.

### Common threshold, record, and separable grounds

All eight Associates would exercise review of the final federal-claim judgment and affirm summary judgment for the defendants on the granted questions. The historical petition identifier supplies no outcome. They do not decide the state statutory theories expressly waived below, the unpresented state constitutional claims, or a freestanding medical-consent question. The lower opinion's mention of RFRA is retained as a source fact; an independent statutory RFRA disposition and RFRA's constitutionality are outside the supplied grant.

Program entry is optional. For junior-high students who request condoms from the nurse, the quoted memorandum requires counseling before receipt and directs provision of pamphlets. High-school students can receive condoms from the nurse or buy them for 75 cents from restroom machines; counseling is available on request. The program contains no parental exclusion or notice mechanism. Students need not request condoms or join the program, and abstention produces no grade or disciplinary consequence. The lower court's general characterization of voluntariness does not erase the junior-high receipt condition. The memorandum's abstinence emphasis and instruction that staff withhold personal opinions are the supported program terms; the content of an unreported individual counseling exchange is not invented.

**C1 — Parental liberty and familial privacy.** Meyer, Pierce, Prince, and Yoder protect parents' direction of upbringing, education, and religious formation while recognizing that parental authority does not exclude every public role concerning children. Providing the described optional preventive resource does not prohibit parental teaching, compel a student to accept it, transfer custody, or legally displace the parent's authority to advise and direct the child. Direct provision to the child is more than mere exposure to an idea, but the additional act of providing this item still does not establish the asserted constitutional right to compel the school to enforce a parent's refusal. No general requirement of physical compulsion or express punishment for every conceivable parental-liberty injury is adopted.

**C2 — Free exercise.** The parents may teach their beliefs and direct abstention; neither they nor their children must use the program, affirm a contrary belief, or engage in sexual conduct to receive ordinary education. No religious targeting, belief-based penalty, unequal access, or conditioning of ordinary school benefits on religious surrender is established by this program's stated terms. The claimed conflict with parental instruction and generalized peer pressure do not establish the asserted constitutional burden. Lyng and Bowen v. Roy support the distinction between an incidental conflict with beliefs and governmental interference requiring religious conformity or sacrificing exercise. They do not turn direct services to children into government action immune from all constitutional review. This threshold resolves the claim without a new holding about every neutral law under Smith. The combination of two asserted rights does not itself supply the missing interference or trigger strict scrutiny.

**C3 — Exclusion, notice, and judgment.** Because neither granted constitutional predicate is established on the program as presented, the Constitution does not require the requested parental exclusion or notification provisions here. The sustained parental-consent provisions in simulated Casey permit that qualified statutory arrangement for abortion, including an independent judicial bypass; they do not command it for every preventive item. The Court need not decide a child's independent constitutional entitlement to receive a condom, validate every parental-involvement restriction, or forbid voluntary accommodation. Summary judgment may be affirmed without finding the program wise, medically necessary, successful, harmless, or the least restrictive policy. Plaintiffs' expert affidavits concerning possible adverse consequences are not erased; those policy disputes do not become material under the adopted threshold ground. No injunction, evidentiary hearing, or newly prescribed procedure follows.

### Enhanced competing paths, remedies, and reservations

**Threshold affirmance.** The State supplies items directly and without a parent-controlled exclusion mechanism. That fact must be faced, but the particular act is still optional access rather than forced treatment, compelled conduct or affirmation, educational penalty, legal alteration of custody, or prohibition on family teaching. C1 rests on the scope of the claimed parental entitlement; C2 independently rests on the lack of the claimed religious burden under these program terms. Neither ground depends on the truth of the school's health predictions. The eight Associates provisionally join both grounds and C3.

**Infringement and justification path.** A court could hold that direct state facilitation of a minor's conduct without parental participation sufficiently intrudes on upbringing to require justification even though the child may decline. Its strongest support is the protected parental role recognized by Meyer, Pierce, and Yoder, with the distinction between providing an item and merely discussing information. Its decisive difficulty for these eight commitments is the absence of authority carrying that right to compulsory parental control of this voluntary preventive resource. The custody and termination cases involve a different state-imposed loss. Casey's permission for a qualified abortion-consent statute cannot supply a constitutional command here. Smith's reference to combined claims cannot bootstrap a parental intrusion the actual facts do not establish. This rejection is a confined boundary, not a holding that only a criminal penalty can burden parents.

If the contrary parental-infringement ground were adopted, the existing judgment could not be sustained merely by declaring the affidavits immaterial. Government justification, alternatives, and any properly material facts would require assessment; vacatur and remand would be the supported next path, rather than a Supreme Court finding that the program necessarily fails or that a single mandatory opt-out cures every injury.
