## Disposition and common grounds

All eight Associates support affirming the final summary judgment on individual qualified immunity, without adopting the Tenth Circuit's five-element substantive-due-process test or deciding the constitutional merits of the affirmative-danger claim. This final-judgment appeal is not subject to a newly invented interlocutory factual-review bar. The Court can decide notice on the estate's supported favorable account without choosing between conflicting district/appellate factual characterizations.

The sufficient rule is particularized objective notice: even accepting the supported inference that administrators knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a definable staff group, preconduct law did not sufficiently establish that these administrative decisions, with placement left to clinical personnel and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive constitutional deprivation. That conclusion concerns constitutional attribution in this institutional setting, not a finding of reasonable care, ignorance, no causation, or mere negligence. Neither a precise factual twin nor unanimity among lower courts is required.

Notice is fixed at the particular 1991 acts, no later than the February 23, 1992 injury. Collins was decided three days after the injury. Collins, later Pinder and Elder govern the present court's method but cannot supply earlier notice. DeShaney, Daniels, Anderson and Harlow remain preconduct law. Bowers, Nishiyama, Archie, Wood, Cornelius, Ross and Archuleta are relevant notice evidence at their respective actual scope, not excluded simply because they are circuit decisions or because some denied relief.

## Enhanced comparison of lawful paths

**Constitutional rejection.** The record can be viewed as allocation of psychiatric-hospital resources, professionally mediated placement and workplace risk, without the arbitrary affirmative deprivation necessary under Collins. The strongest objection is that an affirmative closure over explicit high-risk warnings differs from simply declining to provide a safer workplace; the appellate court itself preserved a genuine awareness issue. None of these eight commitments needs an additional finding that the estate's constitutional account fails. No author may silently turn judgment agreement into assent to that independent merits ground.

**Affirmance on notice.** The qualifying distinction is between the official's direct culpable creation of a lethal encounter or displacement of an available rescue, and the challenged hospital restructuring with intervening clinical placement and disclosed dangers. Cornelius is the strongest contrary notice authority because it involved workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners still in state custody. Its affirmative assignment, supervision and assurances support a serious argument for liability here; those features do not, however, clearly resolve the attributable wrong of each administrator in the materially different clinical and institutional setting. Nishiyama's provision of apparent police authority, Wood's officer-created roadside vulnerability and Ross's obstruction of rescue likewise establish more than simple public nonprotection, but do not erase the present attribution question. No rule that warnings categorically defeat liability, or that deceit is indispensable, is adopted.

**Vacatur/remand.** A Justice could conclude that the affirmative-danger principle was sufficiently apparent and require individualized reconsideration, preserving disputed knowledge and causal inferences. The strongest objection is that notice must concern the constitutional character of each official's conduct, not simply the foreseeable seriousness of the danger. An individualized factual remand cannot retroactively establish legal notice. The immunity ground here accepts the strongest supported account rather than using the factual differences to reject it.
