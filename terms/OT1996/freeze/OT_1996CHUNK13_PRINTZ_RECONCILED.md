### T1 — Jurisdiction, grant scope and actual injury

All eight Associates reach the direct challenge to the compulsory inquiry imposed on the sheriffs through their offices. Raines concerns unauthorized legislators alleging abstract institutional dilution after their votes had legal effect. The actual duties alleged here present a different injury and relief relationship; official status does not categorically exclude it. Raines supplies no general standing to challenge duties borne only by dealers or purchasers, and no new criminal prosecution need be invented to obtain review of a compulsory civil command.

The grant covers the interim executive commands and their statutory consequences. ### T2 — Mandatory inquiry and its implied acceptance requirement

Section 922(s)(2) addresses the chief officer to whom the dealer has provided the notice specified by (s)(1)(A)(i)(III). It commands reasonable effort within five business days to ascertain legal eligibility, including research in available State and local recordkeeping systems and the designated national system. Reasonableness concerns the effort required; it does not make the office's participation optional. The officer need not arrest, prosecute, prevent a transfer or give adverse notice to the dealer. The statutory designee option does not allow the office itself to refuse the assigned federal responsibility.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Invalidate that command and the duty to accept notice and the purchaser's statement implied by the mandatory inquiry. New York's structural prohibition cannot be avoided by addressing the State's executive officers instead of the State. Federal regulation of private transactions remains available; commandeering unwilling state officers to administer that regulation is a distinct means that is not proper merely because the commercial object is within federal power. No burden-benefit balancing or exception for temporary, inexpensive or nonpolicymaking administration supplies the answer.

The source-specific duties in Voting Rights Coalition, state adjudication under Testa, FERC's adjudicative/cooperative setting in a federally preemptible field, and Garcia/Baker regulation of State activity remain distinct. The rule does not revive immunity for every traditional government function. Officers performing adjudicative work traditionally performed by judges remain distinguishable by the function, not merely the formal title of the office. Voluntary cooperation, lawful inducements and direct federal regulation remain available. Constitutional structure, State accountability and New York supply the sufficient ground; historical practice is supporting evidence, not a freestanding power or independently conclusive proof.

The budget and state-law assertions illustrate the problem of assigning federal responsibility without State choice, but the result does not depend on proving a dollar cost, a State-law prohibition or personal liability. State law cannot veto a constitutionally valid federal law; constitutional validity of this means must first be established. An assumed promise of reimbursement cannot cure the structural objection, and no payment mechanism is enacted by this decision.

**Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Sustain the defined interim assistance and affirm the contrary constitutional ruling. Congress regulates the commercial transaction itself and may require this temporary, resource-sensitive information work as a necessary means of that regulation. It has not required the State to enact a regulatory policy, take private property and liabilities, create a general enforcement regime, arrest or prosecute. New York remains binding in its own compelled-legislation/ownership setting. Garcia, Baker, FERC and the Constitution's supremacy and oath obligations support federal cooperation within delegated authority, while the election decisions retain their distinct constitutional sources. The view does not authorize every conceivable command to State institutions or decide the validity of a permanent assignment.

They reject treating the task as costless or State-authorized. Its actual limits and the responsibilities left with the State, not smallness alone, support their answer. Reasonable effort may take actual resources and time into account without becoming permission to disregard the command whenever a budget line is absent. The fiscal assertions do not themselves prove a taking, a spending threshold or a legal entitlement to payment. Their affirmation does not adjudicate State liability or every possible fiscal claim.

**Souter:** Reject the asserted categorical prohibition on requiring appropriate State-executive assistance under an otherwise legitimate federal power, but preserve his separate fiscal/remedial limit. His New York join remains strong evidence against directing a State legislature's policy choice; an executive's function of executing law is different. The straightforward reading of Federalist Nos. 27 and 44, read with Nos. 36 and 45, supplies his affirmative constitutional understanding. Early administrative examples alone would not persuade him. The duty still requires a congressional textual direction; the oath alone does not automatically create every affirmative task. A command inconsistent with the officer's proper branch function remains beyond his ground.

Those sources do not, in his view, justify compulsory administrative support without an obligation to pay fair value. He therefore would remand for development and consideration of the petitioners' asserted lack of budget provision and exposure to unauthorized-expenditure liability. That inquiry is part of deciding whether the existing compelled-assistance arrangement may be sustained on his constitutional theory. It is not an award of damages, a finding that a compensation cause of action was pleaded, a determination of fair value, or an order that Congress appropriate a particular sum. He cannot join unconditional affirmance based on the absence of a separately identified money claim.

### T3 — Conditional destruction, information restrictions and requested reasons

**The exact terms remain distinct.** Section (6)(B) concerns an officer to whom the purchaser statement is transmitted under (1)(A)(i)(IV). Unless that officer determines that the transaction would violate Federal, State or local law, destruction of the statement, derived-information records and records created from the notice is due within twenty business days after the purchaser made the statement. The same exception qualifies the restrictions on conveyance except to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection, and use only to carry out the subsection. Affirmative destruction and restrictions on use/disclosure perform different work. The twenty-day clock does not begin with an assumed decision to volunteer.

Section (6)(C) requires both an ineligibility determination and the individual's request. Written reasons are then due within twenty business days after receipt of that request. The Act does not require an officer to make an adverse determination or send a prohibitory notice. Neither receipt of a statement nor general opposition to the scheme establishes the two required predicates.

**The invalidating four:** These provisions are not declared unconstitutional. With mandatory checking and its implicit required acceptance removed, the officers may decline those program tasks; the challenged ancillary clauses do not require them to commence the very participation from which the constitutional disposition relieves them. On that prospective nonparticipating posture, the destruction and requested-reasons requirements supply no additional compulsory implementation adjudicated in these cases. Their operation following genuinely voluntary participation is not decided. The statutory validity or treatment of a separately established passive transmission or actual retained information is likewise not decided by labeling its recipient a volunteer.

This is a limit on the action and relief decided, **not** a transaction-specific finding of nonreceipt, absence of records or absence of an earlier determination. The fresh neutral restriction against inventing those facts remains binding. No officer receives a general license to retain, use or disclose information free of otherwise applicable law. The Court does not order future receipt, investigation, destruction of unspecified records or explanations on an unestablished request. It does not reopen every theoretical ancillary application merely because such an application is conceivable.

The four therefore reverse the appellate constitutional judgment without the replacement model's affirmative instruction to revive and fact-develop every residual claim. The remand of the old severability appeals is not a necessary consequence of rejecting mandatory checking when the Court itself supplies the bounded statutory/remedial disposition and reserves independent questions. Ordinary implementation of the Court's judgment remains distinct from an express order opening further constitutional adjudication. If a genuinely distinct later application is properly presented, it requires its own actual statutory predicates and lawful review; this judgment supplies neither satisfaction nor failure of them.

**Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Uphold the conditional clauses as safeguards connected with the interim program they regard as valid, preserving their exact exceptions and clocks and without finding their triggers satisfied for any transaction. Their affirmation requires no severance and creates no general power to impose unlimited record work. **Souter** accepts that these bounded protections can accompany constitutionally proper administrative assistance, but his required consideration of fiscal/legal objections prevents an unconditional affirmation of every imposed administrative aspect. His remand does not find any particular destruction or reasons duty triggered or require a new money claim.

## 3. Separate positions that must remain substantively distinct

This section supplies the full substance of the supported separate positions for any resulting publication. It does not select the principal author or import historical authorship as an assignment. **O'Connor's qualification.** Her full structural join is compatible with a concurrence emphasizing voluntary State participation and federally funded contractual cooperation. She reserves purely ministerial reporting requirements, which differ from compelled investigation and administration; an information-only statute is not decided merely because the present background-check command fails. The reservation is neither a partial refusal to join the structural opinion nor an exemption for any task called ministerial.

**The Stevens/Ginsburg/Breyer sustaining position.** Commerce authority and necessary implementation permit these temporary, defined tasks. The supremacy and oath obligations, Testa/FERC cooperative relationships and retained direct regulation support federal cooperation; New York's compelled legislation and broad State policymaking remain distinct. National accountability and political safeguards bear on why the federal scheme need not require a new federal bureaucracy simply to avoid local participation. The argument addresses the fiscal assertions without calling them proved or treating the Act as costless. It does not establish that every permanent assignment is valid or that federal convenience alone defeats structural objections. They would affirm, not award compensation or open a new proceeding.

**Souter's qualified common join and distinct remedy.** The early statutory examples alone do not establish his vote. His reading of Federalist No. 27's auxiliary role, reinforced by No. 44's oath explanation and Nos. 36/45's execution examples, supports appropriately directed executive assistance under legitimate federal power. The duty requires an enacted direction and must fit the official's proper branch function. Legislative choice remains protected by New York. He also retains the fair-value qualification and would remand to consider the existing budget/expenditure objections. He may join the common rejection of categorical invalidation and its limited-assistance ground only subject to those qualifications; a shared dissent cannot label him an unconditional affirmance vote. His remand creates no finding of liability, fixed payment duty, damages award or particular compensation cause of action.

**Breyer's additional functional position.** A division of execution between federal and State officials can preserve State autonomy and limit central administrative expansion. The contrary assumption—that separate federal implementation necessarily protects liberty better—does not follow from federalism alone. Comparative federal arrangements may illustrate this institutional possibility but are not American constitutional authority or proof that their arrangements transfer unchanged here. His sufficient domestic ground remains commerce power, necessary means, the statute's reasonable-effort limitation and retained State responsibilities. This supplements the confined sustaining position; no Justice's join to it is presumed merely from a common judgment.

**Thomas's additional historical observations.** The verified historical concurrence joined the structural opinion in full and separately questioned broad commerce doctrine and noted an unraised possible individual Second Amendment question.
