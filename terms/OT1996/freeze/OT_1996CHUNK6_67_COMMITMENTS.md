## Reachability and judgment

The supplied merits grant presents statutory accommodation and applicant coverage. All eight Associate Justices participate. Each commits to affirm the Ninth Circuit's judgment reversing the employer's trial judgment and remanding for appropriate relief, on the grounds below. Liability is resolved on this completed record; the Court does not remand the already-tried hardship defense for another opportunity to supply missing proof. The Court expressly declines to adopt the appellate language insofar as it requires an experimental accommodation, treats good-faith negotiation as a freestanding prerequisite to a lawful hardship defense, or requires realized disruption.

## Common propositions and exact scope

**O1 — Applicant coverage.** Sections 2000e-2(a)(1) and 2000e(j) protect applicants as well as existing employees from refusal to hire because of a known sincere religious practice that conflicts with work. The conceded prima facie conflict and refusal to complete this appointment trigger the same reasonable-accommodation/undue-hardship inquiry. Prior temporary employment is not a prerequisite to that protection.

**O2 — Governing hardship rule.** An employer must provide effective reasonable accommodation or demonstrate inability to accommodate without more than a de minimis hardship under Hardison's then-governing rule. Concrete noncash costs, legitimate transferred burdens, protected seniority, coverage, efficiency, security and supported professional predictions count; no experiment, realized accident or complaint, preferred accommodation, or exhaustion of every conceivable option is required. When no effective accommodation resolves the conflict, the employer's evidence must address the supported materially distinct proposals and actual operations. Brown and Beadle supply that rule; Ansonia ends the inquiry when an effective reasonable accommodation already exists.

**O3 — Insufficient proof on this record.** California's good-faith discussions did not produce an effective accommodation, and its completed proof did not establish the requisite hardship from supported combinations of alternative undesirable shifts and voluntary trades. Neutral rotation alone does not show hardship. This station had no established collective-bargaining or contractual seniority restriction; that distinguishes Hardison's protected rights without making contractual protection the only cognizable third-party burden. Equal annual numbers of undesirable shifts do not prove universal equivalence of every shift for every worker. Here, however, the State did not connect a particular material transferred burden to the offered exchanges and combinations.

The poll's occurrence was admitted, but its reported results were expressly not admitted for their truth. A trial finding cannot use those results to prove collective unwillingness. Lozano's response concerned an erroneously understood three-day absence; Whitacre could not recall the question or number surveyed. Those facts do not prove every employee misunderstood, and the Court adopts no such appellate inference. They do establish that the cited evidence does not prove no adequate combination of voluntary trades was available. Likewise, testimony about varied schedules to prevent collusion did not connect this limited Sabbath absence, with variation on other days, to a concrete security loss. General morale concerns and hypothetical future requests did not supply the missing operational showing. Existing scheduling flexibility for courses and a minister's Sunday mornings supports the need for a specific comparison; it does not establish an unlimited exemption right.

**O4 — Review and disposition.** Rule 52 protects permissible trial-level factual choices and reasonable operational predictions; it does not insulate reliance on evidence expressly excluded for its truth, a mistaken description of the request, or a legal equation of neutral rotation with adequate accommodation. The reversal is sustained because these defects leave the existing hardship defense unsupported under the correct predictive rule. The absence of an implemented trial schedule is not independently dispositive, and no categorical finding that all proposed accommodations were feasible is necessary. The State had its trial opportunity under the existing burden; no newly announced burden or material exclusion preventing its hardship proof is established.

**O5 — Relief and temporal limits.** Remand determines appropriate source-authorized employment relief, backpay and mitigation, equitable conditions, any continuing basis for prospective relief, and fees/costs under their ordinary predicates. Neither appointment, a particular schedule, a transfer vacancy nor a fixed award is guaranteed. Current staffing circumstances can bear on prospective relief without reopening 1982 liability. The 1991 Act's new compensatory/punitive damages and dependent jury entitlement are not applied retrospectively; Brown preserves the distinction. No constitutional claim or mixed-motive theory is decided.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Restore the district judgment.** Its strongest ground is clear-error deference coupled with Hardison's low cost threshold and the common-sense burden of a permanent exception at a continuous inspection station. That ground would succeed on an evidence-supported prediction of material coverage, security, scheduling or transferred burdens; neither Brown nor Beadle requires actual injury. Its failure here is specific: the collective unwillingness finding uses evidence excluded for that purpose, Lozano describes a materially different request, and the cited security/policy evidence does not connect the supported alternatives to the asserted burden. All eight reject restoration without treating operational expertise as valueless.

**Vacate for fresh findings under the correct standard.** Beadle supplies the strongest model for remand when the trial court failed to assess distinct supported proposals. That alternative respects the factfinder and avoids declaring every proposal feasible. Brown distinguishes it when a completed trial record cannot carry an existing defense. Here the appellate record identifies the actual proof, the evidentiary restriction and the material scheduling testimony; correction does not leave a supported alternative factual choice sufficient to establish hardship. All eight therefore sustain closed liability on that bounded basis. No entitlement to a new evidentiary opportunity arises merely because the employer's existing proof fails.

**Affirm with a mandatory experiment or exhaustive negotiations.** Its strongest practical point is that a short trial might have supplied better knowledge. O2 forecloses turning that observation into a legal condition: supported predictions can establish hardship before an experiment, and effective accommodation or sufficient hardship ends the employer's duty. All eight refuse that rationale. The accepted finding of a week of good-faith discussion independently prevents treating this case as a refusal to negotiate at all.

**Selected path: affirm the judgment on O1–O5.** It preserves the accommodation obligation, lawful predictive proof and Rule 52 while identifying why this actual defense fails. The appellate direction for appropriate relief survives without importing each rationale below. The choice neither guarantees a placement nor directs a particular temporary schedule or search for an unidentified vacancy.
