### Refund limitation and appellate finality

For these mailed refund checks, the refund is made when the taxpayer receives the check, not when the Government merely writes or mails it. Wurts construes the same operative refund language by distinguishing payment from administrative allowance and connecting limitations to an enforceable recovery right. It supports, but did not itself adjudicate, the present receipt-versus-mailing question. The children conceded receipt July 9, 1990, and the suits filed July 9, 1992 were within the two-year period. July 6 is the check-writing date; no mailing date is found. No rule for every different payment mechanism is necessary.

The strongest competing construction treats dispatch as the Government's completed act and supplies an administratively simple date. ## 16. United States v. Sahhar, Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-50186 and 94-50356

### Component positions and remedy

Each Associate supports affirmance of the Ninth Circuit judgment rejecting the automatic five-year ceiling and requiring further proceedings on state placement, with the precise statutory and constitutional limits below. The District Court's unappealed dismissal of the indictment remains untouched. The decision neither reinstates charges nor itself orders new confinement, final release or transfer to Arizona. The lower proceedings must apply current lawful predicates and the state-placement duty; historical clinical findings do not become Supreme Court medical findings as of December 1996.

1. **Statutory authority after this dismissal.** A valid §4246 civil commitment arising from the statutory competency channel does not end solely because the incompetency-related prosecution has ended. Section 4246(a) covers a person whose sentence is about to expire, one committed under §4241(d), or one whose charges were dismissed solely for reasons related to mental condition, subject to the director's certification of present illness-caused statutory danger and unavailable suitable state arrangements. A former charge by itself is insufficient. Filing the required certificate stays release pending the section's procedures and requires a hearing.
2. **Due process and the hypothetical sentence.** Civil custody is justified by its present lawful treatment/protection grounds, not unadjudicated criminal guilt. The maximum sentence for the former threat charge therefore supplies no automatic endpoint for otherwise valid civil commitment. Jones rejects a hypothetical-sentence ceiling; it does not allow the Court to treat an incompetent defendant as an insanity acquittee or dispense with independent civil grounds. Foucha, Jackson and Addington require a continuing relationship between custody and proper civil purposes and adequate grounds and procedures. Substantial governmental interests or a bare assertion of nonpunitive intent do not alone establish lawful custody.
3. **Equal protection.** The claimed disparity based on the five-year criminal maximum does not compel release: sentence severity and the duration of an established illness-caused risk perform different legal work. The federal custody origin and statutory transition to suitable state responsibility are relevant to the limited classification. Heller requires a relation to the actual procedural disadvantage; it does not impose the criminal maximum or select a universal heightened tier. The result does not adopt the Ninth Circuit's suggestion that satisfaction of equal protection necessarily resolves due process. The two protections require separate attention. No general scrutiny tier is chosen.
4. **State responsibility and further proceedings.** Section 4246(d) commands all reasonable efforts to obtain assumption of custody, care and treatment by the domicile or trial State, continued periodically. Actual state assumption requires release to the appropriate state official. Only if neither relevant State assumes responsibility despite those efforts may treatment continue in a suitable federal facility until assumption or a condition permitting safe unconditional or prescribed-regimen release, whichever comes first. Arizona was not asked after dismissal; its earlier terminated placement establishes neither present acceptance nor refusal. The lower court must determine its actual response and whether the statutory efforts and continuing predicates support the lawful disposition.

### Burdens, release routes and safeguards retained by every commitment

Section 4246(d)'s commitment finding requires **clear and convincing evidence** of present mental disease or defect causing a substantial risk of bodily injury to another person or serious damage to another's property. A free-standing danger-to-self ground is not substituted. That rule must not be rewritten as a new allocation governing every subsequent release hearing.

Under §4246(e), a director who finds sufficient recovery must promptly certify it. The court orders discharge or holds the hearing authorized on the Government's motion or its own motion. The statute's post-hearing recovery finding is by **a preponderance of the evidence**: sufficiently safe unconditional release requires immediate discharge; sufficiently safe conditional release requires an individually prepared regimen, certified appropriate by the director, found appropriate by the court, and imposed with an express compliance condition. After a hearing on the same criteria the court may modify or eliminate the regimen. This case does not adjudicate an unpresented constitutional attack on that release provision or substitute subsection (d)'s initial burden for it.

Section 4246(f) addresses conditional-release violations through notice or other probable cause, possible arrest, prompt presentation and a hearing on whether noncompliance makes continued release create the statutory danger. It supplies no new indefinite commitment power over every former federal defendant.

Section 4246(g) separately governs charges dismissed for reasons **not related to mental condition**: state referral for institution of civil-commitment proceedings, followed by release if neither relevant State assumes responsibility, upon refusal notice and no later than ten days after the director's certification. The present packet establishes no distinct unrelated-dismissal premise triggering that route. The holding cannot erase it, label every dismissal mental-condition-related, or permit subsection (d) hospitalization in a case governed by subsection (g).

Section 4247(d) provides representation by counsel, appointment when needed, and opportunities to testify, present evidence, subpoena, confront and cross-examine. Section 4247(e) requires annual reports and continued-hospitalization recommendations for §4246 confinement; it is not a mandatory annual judicial hearing. Section 4247(g) preserves habeas. Section 4247(h) independently permits a discharge motion by counsel or a guardian without a director's certificate, subject to its bar on another such motion within 180 days of a court determination that hospitalization should continue; it does not compel hearings every 180 days.

## 19. Old Chief v. United States

### Admissibility alternatives and distinct remedial law

**Path A — Rule 403 error.** The prior judgment is relevant under Rule 401 despite the offer. Rule 403 evaluates probative value in context, including a legally adequate available admission. Where the sole legitimate purpose is the qualifying prior-conviction status, an admission establishing that legal status supplies the needed proof without disclosing a similar violent offense. The prior assault's name and nature add little legitimate value for this status element while presenting a substantial propensity danger in this assault/firearm prosecution. The strongest objection is the Government's ordinary right to offer coherent, credible evidence rather than a defendant's abstract substitute, and the trial judge's Rule 403 discretion. A is limited to the status proof and this unfair-prejudice comparison; ordinary narrative proof of the charged conduct remains different.

**Path B — no Rule 403 abuse.** Congress made a qualifying conviction part of the offense; an authentic judgment is direct evidence of that element. An offer does not delete the element or render its normal proof irrelevant. The trial judge may rely on ordinary limiting safeguards and the prosecution's interest in proving its case, without being compelled to take a defendant's preferred substitute. The strongest objection is that an element's existence does not place every manner of proving it beyond Rule 403, especially where the judge treats prosecutorial choice as conclusive and the unnecessary information resembles the pending violent charges. A B commitment must defend admissibility in this specific balance, not assert that relevant evidence is always admissible.

For both paths, the offer must be clarified to admit a conviction satisfying §922(g)(1) as legally defined in §921(a)(20), including the exclusions and conviction-status qualifications reproduced in the validated packet. No excluded or extinguished conviction is found here. The wording problem was not the trial court's stated ground and is not an invented independent basis for affirmance. A legally adequate admission does not allow the defendant to withdraw status from the jury without proof or stipulation/instruction establishing that element.

**Remedy is separate.** Williamson's controlling Part III already governs preserved nonconstitutional evidentiary error: Rule 52(a)/Kotteakos require the Government to establish harmlessness through whole-record fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness. The Ninth Circuit never assessed harmlessness, and the supplied record cannot support a final harmlessness decision. Error therefore leads to vacatur and remand for that inquiry and lawful consequences, not automatic retrial. ## 23. Abebe-Jira v. Negewo — lower judgment 72 F.3d 844; Supreme Court identifier 95-8873

### Event and controlling boundaries

The established counterfactual grant permits review under §1254(1) on the three packet questions. Under the authorized scheduling premise the case is **submitted for merits consideration January 14, 1997 and scheduled for disposition that day**. No earlier oral argument or historical Supreme Court grant is inferred. Three plaintiffs obtained, after a bench trial, **$200,000 compensation and $300,000 punitive damages each**, totaling $1,500,000. No official-title-only or general superior-liability rule is supplied.

**Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses**, present operation effective **Suter, March 25, 1992**, requires a source-specific right/action inquiry; it does not abolish historically received common-law actions. **Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination**, present operation effective **Franklin, February 26, 1992**, operates only once a cognizable action exists and leaves punitive authority open. Central Bank recognizes bounded development of a received securities action, not a free power to create any desirable liability. ### Components and competing sufficient grounds

**B1 — Statutory forum.** Section 1350 expressly confers original jurisdiction over an alien's civil tort action alleging violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty. The supported official-torture allegations and findings are within the statute's described category, and the asserted action is not insubstantial. Whether this category supplies an enforceable domestic damages action remains a distinct question. The jurisdictional sentence is not by itself a damages judgment, a waiver of sovereign immunity or a command to recognize every asserted international norm.

**B2 — Confined domestic action, affirmative path.** Recognize an action against the individual for his own deliberate participation in or supervision of official torture under the narrow, received international-tort jurisdiction. The operative substantive norm forbids severe physical or mental suffering intentionally inflicted under official authority for interrogation, punishment or intimidation; the proved personal conduct here falls within that established prohibition. The domestic action rests on §1350's historical reception of judicial adjudication of law-of-nations torts together with the common-law judicial treatment of that particular prohibition, not on the bare jurisdictional label alone. International law identifies the wrong; domestic common law supplies the enforceable tort action within the congressionally provided forum. This statutory and received-law basis distinguishes Suter's administrative plan requirements and O'Melveny's proposed new receiver preference. Filartiga is persuasive evidence of the established particular application, not a binding command to adopt every part of its rationale. The Court need not declare that international custom invariably creates privately enforceable federal rights, equate every form of cruel treatment with torture, or decide private nonstate wrongdoing, corporate liability, absent-supervisor liability or every possible foreign-relations defense.

The strongest negative path accepts the absolute substantive prohibition and the statutory forum but denies that either establishes congressional authorization to create a new private federal damages action. Filartiga's modern lower-court recognition is not the same as congressional enactment; the historical jurisdiction could be used for independently supplied actions without authorizing judicial development of all later norms. Central Bank's majority rule is confined to a long-received securities remedy; its rationale does not compel an ATS extension. On that path the present §1350 judgment must be reversed, without treating the torture as lawful or adding an unpleaded TVPA claim.

The affirmative response is source specific: this is a statute referring directly to torts violating the law of nations, enacted against common-law adjudication of such wrongs, and the actual prohibition is settled, rather than an inference from a general administrative duty or a new class of secondary securities defendants. The decisive disagreement is whether that historical function sufficiently authorizes this domestic action, not the moral appraisal of torture. No later Supreme Court ATS doctrine supplies the answer.

**B3 — Compensation.** For Justices accepting B2, permit compensation for proved injury caused by the defendant's qualifying personal conduct. Reparation is an ordinary incident of this actionable intentional tort, supported by traditional tort remedies and Franklin's distinction between action and appropriate relief. The completed trial findings, not the mere accusation or official title, support the present judgment. This resolves the categorical authority objection; it does not reweigh medical or emotional-loss evidence or announce a damages schedule. On the negative B2 path no compensation is available on this asserted action, irrespective of the general power to remedy a valid action.

**B4 — Punitive authority independently justified.** For Justices accepting B2, hold that the domestic common-law action permits punishment of the individual for established malicious, willful or equivalently reckless aggravated torture; it does not require punitive damages whenever liability exists. Arms's traditional aggravated-tort rule provides independent authority for the remedial type; the 1984 Filartiga decision supplies a directly analogous, persuasive application of punitive relief to personal official torture. The action's source and common-law remedial character, rather than Franklin's silence or international condemnation alone, justify the domestic remedy. The proved intentional personal torture supplies the culpable wrong needed for its availability here. No sovereign is punished, no criminal conviction is entered and no liability of a people or government is inferred.

The strongest contrary remedial argument is that recognition of a compensatory cause does not itself license penal relief with international consequences. That argument requires an express justification, but traditional punishment of aggravated personal torts supplies one once the confined domestic action is recognized; international law need not prescribe the exact domestic dollar remedy. The 1984 Filartiga default and its finding about inability to prosecute are not necessary conditions or facts imported here. Punitive entitlement, lawful considerations, safeguards and amount remain distinct. TXO and BMW retain their actual authority and disagreements; the grants here do not present a developed independent challenge to the exact amount or assessment method. Affirmance against the categorical authority attack does not certify every possible amount calculation, impose a ratio, or eliminate a properly presented constitutional limit.

**B5 — Political question.** Reject the categorical objection. This individual tort claim asks a court to determine proved conduct under an established prohibition and apply ordinary remedial law; no supported issue requires deciding the legitimacy of the Ethiopian government, directing American diplomatic policy or resolving a constitutionally committed war-powers dispute. Baker, as applied in Montana and Nixon, requires the identified commitment or manageability obstacle. Sanchez-Espinoza's challenge to United States foreign-policy operations, immunity reasoning and reserved foreign-officer distinction do not justify wholesale abstention here. This conclusion neither supplies the cause of action nor disposes of defenses not presented.

**B6 — Judgment choices and limits.** The affirmative path affirms the Eleventh Circuit's damages judgment against the three granted categorical objections, retaining each plaintiff's separate compensatory and punitive components. It adopts no independent rule for cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment beyond the supported torture ground, and no general superior-liability rule. The negative path reverses the §1350 damages judgment and remands for disposition consistent with absence of that action; it does not authorize a new TVPA theory, retroactive pleading repair or an evidentiary trial on an invented claim. Limitations and appointed-counsel objections remain outside the grant. No precise amount recalculation, factual allocation finding or new defense is made.

## 24. Walters v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises; EEOC v. Metropolitan Educational Enterprises — Nos. 95-259 and 95-779

### Event, source and components

Argued November 6, 1996; scheduled decision January 14, 1997. Review under §1254(1) of the combined Seventh Circuit litigation. The EEOC sued; Walters intervened and added a promotion claim. The lower courts dismissed using a compensation-based numerical count. Their subject-matter-jurisdiction label is preserved as procedural history, not adopted as a new holding about the legal character of numerosity.

**W1 — Relationship, not daily compensation.** An employer has an individual as an employee on a working day when the employment relationship exists on that day, even if the individual neither works nor receives compensation that day. Section 2000e(b) requires fifteen or more qualifying employees for each working day in each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year. The day-specific words measure persistence of the employment relationships, not an added daily-work or daily-pay condition. Hiring and termination still matter on the actual days they occur. Darden's agency construction of a circular employee definition and Town & Country's attention to the actual compatible relationship support this reading without independently deciding the numerical phrase.

**W2 — Payroll evidence and classifications.** Payroll records are an ordinary evidentiary way to identify relationships; a list is not an irrebuttable legal rule. A person whose relationship has not begun or has ended does not count for a day merely because a name appears on payroll. Nor does payroll status conclusively make an independent contractor or a person expressly excluded by §2000e(f) an employee. The common-law agency inquiry examines the whole relationship without making compensation on the particular day decisive. No disputed independent-contractor or public-officer classification is invented for these two hourly part-time workers.

**W3 — Enacted limitations remain.** The industry-affecting-commerce requirement, statutory agent language and §2000e(b) exclusions remain. They include the United States, wholly federally owned corporations, Indian tribes, the specified District of Columbia competitive-service departments/agencies, and qualifying tax-exempt bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations. The historical first-year twenty-five-person transition is not applied as a current threshold. Section 2000e(f)'s separate exclusions for elected state/local officers, their selected personal staff, policymaking appointees and immediate constitutional/legal advisers retain their civil-service qualification. Its United States-citizen foreign-employment language changes no domestic daily count here. A counting construction neither eliminates an express exclusion nor decides that one applies.

**W4 — Bounded disposition.** Reverse the compensation-based judgment in both consolidated dockets and remand for application of the employment-relationship method and further proceedings. No qualifying period existed in 1989. For most of 1990 there were fifteen to seventeen payroll employees each working day, while only nine weeks met the rejected compensation approach because of the two part-time schedules. Those stipulated recitations support rejecting the legal method; this Court need not invent an exact qualifying-week total to remand. Neither retaliation/promotion discrimination, Title VII liability, damages nor the jurisdictional classification of numerosity is decided.

### Alternative path and individual commitments

The employer's strongest construction makes “for each working day” a daily compensation condition and invokes a readily checked rule used below. Paid leave counted under that rule, so it is not an actual-work-only test. Its weakness is statutory: Congress counted employees, whose relationships persist through unpaid off-days, rather than persons compensated each day. A relationship method gives the temporal words work to do by excluding days before hiring or after termination, while avoiding an unstated distinction between salaried leave and an hourly employee's recurring unpaid day. Administrative convenience confirms an interpretation grounded in the text; it is not permission to count nonemployees or ignore exclusions.
