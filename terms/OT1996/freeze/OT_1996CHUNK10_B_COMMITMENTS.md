## 115. Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash — retained collateral

### Competing statutory resolutions

**R1, replacement:** Section 506(a)'s first sentence identifies what is valued—the creditor's lien in the estate's actual collateral—and the second supplies the context: purpose and the proposed disposition or use. Under the chosen retention/use option, the appropriate value is the price of comparable property for that use in the relevant market, with actual age and condition respected. Section 1325(a)(5)'s retention and surrender alternatives need not yield identical amounts. Replacement valuation is not compensation for lost immediate foreclosure under Timbers.

**R2, foreclosure/wholesale:** The first sentence values the creditor's security, whose economic realization is foreclosure recovery. The second allows changes in context, physical use and purpose without transferring the debtor's acquisition advantage to the creditor. The creditor's allowed secured amount should not expand simply because reorganization lets the debtor retain useful property. Timbers' allocation of reorganization costs and the rights/security distinction support this reading.

The strongest R1 objection is that R2 makes ordinary retention and surrender use the same market despite the second sentence. The strongest R2 objection is that R1 may include services, warranties and dealer transactions the creditor never supplied or owned. A sustainable R1 answer excludes separately priced benefits not embodied in the retained collateral; it does not mechanically deduct every dealer overhead or profit item from a properly established market price. Neither theory permits invented disposal expenses or valuation by the debtor's business income.

## 116. Hughes Aircraft v. Schumer — statutory temporal reach

**H1:** Apply former §3730(b)(4), reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand for dismissal under the government-knowledge bar. The supplied predicate is government possession before enactment and suit, not a finding that the fraud allegations are true or false. Removing a complete bar to this relator-initiated action changes the legal consequences of the completed alleged 1982–1984 conduct. Neither the amendment's jurisdictional wording nor the post-enactment filing date makes this only a change of forum. No sufficient congressional instruction for that retrospective operation is identified.

**H2, strongest rival:** The amendment regulates who may invoke jurisdiction after its enactment; fraud and exposure to a United States suit already existed. The answer to H2 is narrow: the formerly unavailable private enforcement action is itself new legal exposure, even though a different public claimant could sue. The conclusion does not make all jurisdictional amendments substantive or retrospectively inapplicable.

## 117. Mazurek v. Armstrong — preliminary relief under actual Casey

### Complete governing inquiry and alternatives

**M1:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand for application of the complete controlling Casey framework and ordinary preliminary-relief standards. The District Court's all-legislators purpose premise was erroneous; the Ninth Circuit correctly required objective attention to purpose and actual hardships, but its substantial-obstacle/purpose formulation is incomplete in this Court's law. This is a correction of the remand instructions, not a direction to enter an injunction or find an unconstitutional purpose.

The framework that M1 requires is:

1. Identify the actual physician-only duty, its authoritative construction, exceptions and sanctions, the regulated clinical work and the protected baseline. Previability choice may not be prohibited, placed under third-party control or deprived of timely care necessary for life or health.
2. Determine the duty's actual function and objectively supported obstructive purpose from text, design, context and operation. An established prohibited purpose independently invalidates the duty. An abortion opponent's sponsorship, opposition to abortion or knowledge that Cahill alone is affected is relevant context but not a conclusive substitute for that inquiry.
3. Require challengers to establish the actual added burden, stable affected population and causal connection through concrete, nontrivial evidence, including reliable prospective evidence. Evaluate genuinely usable alternatives, capacity, travel, cost and delay against actual conditions without double counting or merely assuming that another provider can absorb the work.
4. An established baseline violation, prohibited purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid and cannot be rescued by balancing benefits. Remaining severe interference requires compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful lesser interference requires the State to substantiate a permissible justification and fit and answer a concretely identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose and other independently preserved constitutional claims remain. No new-study, scientific-certainty, universal numerical facial threshold or perfect-legislation requirement applies.
5. Give competent medical evidence and professional judgment their appropriate weight without a professional veto. Clinical judgment and necessary care remain protected; prior approval cannot obstruct timely necessary care. The present case supplies no separate compelled-communication question.
6. Match preliminary or final relief to the duty, applications and population for which a violation and the relevant equitable predicates are supported. Preserve separable lawful duties; broader relief requires corresponding proof. This is not an automatic all-applications barrier or automatic facial invalidity.

Cahill's long-standing supervised, board-approved practice supplies a concrete alternative for examination under the applicable tier. It does not itself prove a meaningful patient burden, a feasible alternative constitutionally sufficient in every circumstance, absence of health benefit, or unconstitutional targeting. The absence of a proved substantial obstacle does not settle all other branches.

Casey's separate invalidation of exclusive physician delivery of routine consent information did not decide who may perform an abortion. Its protection of individualized diagnosis and professional responsibility likewise did not categorically approve every physician-only performance rule. The actual challenged clinical duty must be examined under the complete framework; neither that informational holding nor historical Casey wording supplies an automatic answer.

**M2, rival:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit and reinstate denial of preliminary relief. The existing showing does not warrant disrupting a reasonable legislative physician-qualification requirement; individual targeting and opposition sponsorship are insufficient, and the asserted effects have not shown loss of practical access. Kennedy's published reasonable-regulation approach and Scalia's/Thomas's constitutional position support this result independently. Their method is not the governing complete framework. A narrower M2 under that framework would require a supported determination that every preserved ground fails, not merely absence of a substantial obstacle.

## 118. Crawford-El v. Britton — proof quantum, immunity and discovery

### Components and adversarial alternatives

**C1:** Reject a special clear-and-convincing burden for unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation action. Ordinary legally competent direct or circumstantial proof remains available. Kimberlin already separated the underlying constitutional state of mind from the objective immunity defense and preserved ordinary procedural burdens; it expressly did not adopt the alternative heightened quantum. Deciding this reserved quantum question now requires the additional conclusion that neither §1983, Harlow's objective defense, this underlying First Amendment claim nor the governing Rules supplies it.

**C2:** Reject a categorical rule demanding proof meeting that new quantum before the claimant can obtain necessary, properly limited discovery. The lower court's absolute no-discovery proposal was not a majority holding; its competing approaches must be evaluated accurately. Courts may resolve a dispositive legal immunity defense first, insist on specific nonconclusory facts identifying the challenged conduct, use rule-authorized clarification and replies, stage discovery, and enter protective orders. Rule 56's affidavit-based procedures and ordinary evidentiary duties remain real. No unlimited discovery or categorical right to depose an official follows.

**C3:** Vacate the lower judgment to the extent it imposes the heightened quantum and dependent directions, and remand with the existing retaliation/conversion remand otherwise preserved. No disputed factual account is selected; no final immunity, liability, motive, causal connection, injury or damages is adjudicated.

**Rival A, heightened proof:** Harlow protects against litigation burdens, not simply adverse liability. Motive allegations are cheap and subjective; a higher quantum could protect officials from costly meritless suits. New York Times and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby show that heightened substantive burdens can influence summary judgment. The answer is that the speech-protective constitutional basis of the defamation burden is absent here, and Liberty Lobby applies an independently valid burden rather than authorizing its creation. Ordinary rules plus early legal immunity rulings respond to the legitimate concern without changing what proves this constitutional wrong.

**Rival B, plausible lawful purpose:** Britton's handling could reflect safekeeping and transfer policy, so an objectively reasonable lawful explanation should end the case regardless of actual motive. Settle's unanimous causation ruling defeats that categorical alternative: a merely possible lawful reason is not a proved same-decision defense. Claimant must still establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; defendant may prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. Neither mixed motives nor an asserted bad purpose automatically establishes recovery.

**Rival C, immediate alternative dismissal:** The interval between alleged threats and property delivery, comparable treatment of other prisoners, small losses and alleged legitimate explanation may defeat the claim under ordinary rules. On this grant, however, the lower court's new-proof-dependent assessment is not an independent finding that permits this Court to resolve contested inferences and credibility. Neither the constitutional protection nor the injury issue is rewritten merely to avoid the granted legal question.
