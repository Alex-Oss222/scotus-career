## 2. Verified historical comparison

Historically, the Court reversed and remanded specifically for reinstatement of the district judgment, including the one-dollar Title VII award. Souter's opinion had the joins of Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas dissented with Scalia. These historical authorship and joinder facts establish the comparator only; they make no present assignment.

The historical majority recognized liability for an actionable hostile environment created by a supervisor with immediate or successively higher authority. Without tangible employment action it allowed the employer to prove, by a preponderance, both reasonable preventive and prompt corrective care and the employee's unreasonable failure to use protective opportunities or otherwise avoid harm. A policy was not invariably required; unreasonable failure to use a procedure normally established the second component but was not its exclusive form. A tangible action, such as discharge, demotion or undesirable reassignment, foreclosed the defense. The City failed the reasonable-care component on its actual dispersed-workforce record. With only nominal damages awarded, mitigation supplied no reason for further litigation. Negligence and imputed knowledge were unnecessary alternative grounds.

Thomas and Scalia rejected vicarious liability without an adverse employment consequence. They would allow a negligence remand, including consideration of a reasonably available route to a superior official or whether dissemination would have revealed the misconduct. Their incorporated Ellerth position emphasized the employer's own fault, distinguished completed company employment acts, and questioned the majority's expansion of agency responsibility and its new defense. Those objections cannot be reduced to a generic preference for employers or for remand.

The verification receipt records full primary reading of the Faragher Court opinion and dissent, every footnote, and the incorporated Thomas Ellerth dissent, with official-reporter cross-checks. This reconciliation uses that completed verification; it does not claim a fresh complete retrieval of the original merits record or of every other Ellerth writing.

## 3. Reconciled common propositions and operative scope

All eight Associates support the following four propositions at the stated level of generality. These are their commitments, not a declaration of the final Court's composition or holding.

**F1 — actual authority materially aiding the environment.** An employer may be responsible under Title VII when actual delegated supervisory authority materially aided creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. The plaintiff must establish that connection; supervisory rank and workplace proximity alone do not suffice. Higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably necessary. Negligence, apparent authority and ordinary scope of employment remain distinct routes with their own predicates. This is the unanimous Andrade rule, reaffirmed in unanimous Oncale, not a new presumption of responsibility for every supervisor's act.

**F2 — this supported bench record satisfies that rule.** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision, in the remote unit's actual command structure, materially aided maintenance of these accepted hostile working conditions. The district court made an agency determination from the workplace structure and powers, independently of its disputed constructive-notice ground. The connection lies in the supervisors' operative control of the conditions in which the repeated abuse was maintained; it is not inferred from a title or the existence of harassment alone. Remoteness and ineffective policy communication help explain this structure but become neither universal required elements nor freestanding liability rules.

The accepted historical findings receive clear-error review; their legal significance receives independent review. The conclusion adds no finding of subjective fear, an order to submit, threatened discharge, a particular unreported interaction or an employment penalty. It does not classify every personal act as within ordinary employment scope. The appellate emphasis on missing personnel threats or consequences cannot make those facts indispensable after Andrade.

**F3 — this policy contention fails; a general affirmative defense remains open.** A formal policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, the employee's failure to complain to distant higher management, and correction after another employee's eventual report do not, in this combination, defeat the established authority-aided liability. Nonuse of an undistributed procedure does not itself establish unreasonable employee conduct. The Court need not decide whether an employer that actually provided effective protection could establish a complete defense, the elements or burden of any such defense, its relationship to tangible action, or a distinct limitation on particular relief.

This is a reservation, not rejection of all complete defenses. It does not find that no alternative route to an official ever existed, that the unprovided policy contained a particular promise, or that a particular employee held an unproved belief. The historical dissent's alternative-route and dissemination-causation proposals concern its negligence-only approach; they are not silently decided as adverse facts here. No such negligence determination is necessary to the sufficient actual-authority ground. Effective procedures, knowledge, corrective conduct and avoidable loss retain their possible relevance within their proper legal inquiries. No special statutory duty to publish a particular procedure or universal employee duty to complain is created.

**F4 — restore only the existing reviewed judgment.** Reverse the court of appeals only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and remand for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment. No new merits trial or factfinding is required on the sufficient agency ground. The separate individual §1983 and battery awards, other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions remain outside the grant. Landgraf forecloses enlarging this pre-1991 claim into new §102 damages or a derivative jury right. The decision fixes no fee entitlement or amount; Farrar concerns a distinct fee statute and supplies only its confined analogy. No new injunction, constructive-discharge finding, reinstatement, compensation, deadline or retained Supreme Court proceeding follows.

## 4. Rival paths and why they do not change the common disposition

**The real material-aid objection.** Proving that a person possessed supervisory powers does not by itself prove that those powers aided harassment. That is the strongest objection to restoration, particularly for Scalia and Thomas. It remains an express join condition. Here the supported bench findings concern actual operation of the work unit and control of its daily conditions, together with the district court's agency determination. On this basis the Court can identify a legal connection without adding a threat or fear finding. The adjudicated environment and operative authority distinguish this case from an allegation-only claim or a bare title-based inference. Assembly must retain that connection; deleting it would exceed the reconciled joins.

**Targeted remand.** Vacatur for an identified missing material-aid finding is a lawful alternative. Meritor's unresolved harassment/unwelcomeness matters and Andrade's unresolved Rule 50, instruction and sufficiency questions illustrate its function. They do not establish a mandatory new factual proceeding after every correction of agency law. The supported findings here permit the bounded legal application. No present commitment depends on a new fact, an omitted OCR sentence or the proposition that the City's separate negligence defense necessarily fails. If assembly introduces such a dependency, this clearance no longer supplies it: return the affected objective material for a clean refresh and affected modeling.

**Affirmance for the City.** Personal purpose, no tied personnel detriment, remoteness from City Hall, the characterization of Gordon's knowledge, and eventual corrective action may matter to separate agency or negligence theories. They cannot restore a categorical no-notice or adverse-action bar to the actual-authority route. A genuinely unsupported aid connection could warrant relief for the City; the accepted operational findings defeat that objection here. Severity sufficient for Harris is never substituted for constructive notice.

**Adopting the historical defense now.** The grant permits a general answer, and the historical majority supplies strong evidence for considering prevention and reasonable avoidance within the agency framework. Prior reservation in Andrade does not establish that a defense is forbidden, unnecessary forever, or rejected by any Justice. The narrower present decision is supportable because Andrade already established the actual-aid rule through a plaintiff-proved connection, all eight subsequently reaffirmed it in Oncale, and the City's present contention fails without deciding the legal treatment of proved effective precautions. The historical majority addressed the agency rule and its general limiting defense together. This Court can apply the already established, fact-dependent rule without simultaneously defining all defenses. That changed decisional setting supports reservation; it does not mechanically compel it or imply that a future defense would overrule Andrade.

The distinction is not that historical Faragher abandoned agency analysis or imposed liability on every title-holder. It adapted agency principles and coupled its general supervisor rule to a preventive-care defense. The concrete difference is the earlier completed, unanimous actual-material-aid holding and its application here, not a false contrast between agency reasoning and no agency reasoning.
