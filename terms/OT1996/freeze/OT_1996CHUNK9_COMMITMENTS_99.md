## Entering authority at its exact scope

- **Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination** — **Present operation effective: Franklin, February 26, 1992**. Compensation is available in the established action for proved intentional discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and loss. Franklin rejects a categorical funding-notice bar to that remedy but reserves the recipient-attribution rule. Scalia and Thomas joined only the judgment on their narrower ground; O'Connor, Kennedy, Stevens and Souter joined the Court's rationale.
- **Knowing supervisory acquiescence in school-authority sexual abuse** — **Present operation effective: Doe v. Taylor ISD, October 16, 1995**. Actual knowledge of abuse or a substantial risk, deliberate and unreasonable disregard despite protective authority/opportunity, and causal contribution are necessary. Negligence, constructive notice, a policy breach or office alone does not suffice. Ginsburg, Stevens, Souter and Breyer joined the controlling rationale; the other four Associates opposed the Lankford disposition. All eight joined Caplinger's distinct conduct-time immunity rationale, which did not decide his constitutional merits.
- **Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite** — **Present operation effective: Harris, November 9, 1993**. Objective and subjective abuse, sufficient severity or pervasiveness and full context matter; no psychiatric injury or indispensable single factor is required. This employment rule informs educational analysis without supplying recipient attribution.
- **Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review** — **Present operation effective: Elder, February 23, 1994**. All relevant conduct-time authority must be considered; later cases cannot supply earlier notice. Lanier, March 31, 1997, confirms particularized apparent unlawfulness without a factual twin. Because the proposed constitutional judgment rests on insufficient merits proof, these notice rules are retained rather than used to award new immunity.
- **Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer, February 24, 1997**, requires discrimination because of sex and reserves comprehensive recipient attribution. **Andrade, February 26**, unanimously preserves actual delegated-authority/material-aid and negligence as distinct Title VII routes. **Oncale, March 19**, unanimously rejects opposite-sex, desire, orientation and actual-comparator prerequisites under Title VII while retaining every causation and harassment element. The textual analogy bears on coverage; neither case automatically imports Title VII attribution into Title IX.
- **Nabozny, March 10**, distinguishes purposeful discriminatory nonprotection, personal conduct and municipal attribution; it supplies no new equal-protection claim here. **Bryan County, April 28**, is only a reargument order and supplies no merits holding. Monell, Hafer, and the existing personal-attribution rules remain effective.

## Common coverage and record rules

**Same-sex coverage and causation.** Title IX's protection of any person from sex-based exclusion, denial of benefits or discrimination under a federally funded education program contains no opposite-sex limitation. Brown supplies the because-of-sex requirement; Oncale's reading of analogous discrimination language confirms that the sex of the harasser and target cannot alone exclude a claim. The reported evidence that McDougall selected Kinman as a woman and directed no comparable attentions to male students supports this element at summary judgment. That evidence is neither a universal comparator requirement nor a finding of every defendant's intent. Sexual conduct or severity alone does not establish discriminatory causation. No sexual-orientation coverage rule is decided.

**Welcome and educational harm.** Meritor distinguishes voluntary participation from welcome. Kinman's account that the initial advances were unwanted, considered with the teacher/student authority relationship and the surrounding evidence, creates a factual dispute despite later participation and postgraduation continuation. The Court may not decide credibility or assume every later act remained unwelcome. Sex-based harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive in the educational context to create an objectively abusive educational environment that the student actually experienced as abusive. Frequency, gravity, coercion, humiliation, authority and educational interference inform the whole-context inquiry; psychological diagnosis, measurable grade loss or one mandatory form of harm is not required. This is a concrete educational application of Harris, not transplantation of every employment rule. The alleged exploitation and sexual conduct can satisfy that inquiry if proved; this ruling does not establish injury or its amount.

**Institution, individual and time remain distinct.** The District's funding-recipient responsibility, each supervisor's own constitutional conduct and the teacher's personal liability cannot be merged. The school policy covers welcome and unwelcome conduct, on and off duty and school grounds, and two years after departure; those policy terms are not the federal liability test. Graduation does not bar compensation for a proved earlier violation, but does not extend Title IX to every later private relationship. The original default against McDougall was vacated. No default or damages amount is reinstated. The granted supervisory questions do not authorize a new merits dismissal or reinstatement of an independently grounded direct constitutional claim against the teacher.

## Recipient attribution: incompatible proposed rules

### Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

**Rule and judgment.** A funding recipient may be responsible for a teacher's intentional sex-based harassment when actual educational authority delegated to the teacher materially aided the harassment, or when responsible school officials knew or reasonably should have known of the harassment and negligently failed to take appropriate corrective steps, causing injury or continuation. Teacher status or proximity alone does not establish agency attribution. These are distinct routes; proof of a forbidden environment does not itself prove either. They would affirm the removal of summary judgment on the recipient Title IX claim and remand under this clarified standard, without commanding a trial before ordinary remaining legal issues are addressed.

**Sufficient grounds.** Section 1681 regulates discrimination under the funded program, which the recipient operates through its teachers and administrators. Franklin recognizes intentional teacher harassment as capable of supporting compensation while reserving attribution. Meritor and Andrade supply a reasoned agency analogy: a recipient cannot necessarily avoid responsibility for intentional discrimination materially enabled by the authority it entrusted merely because senior management lacked notice. The teacher's intent remains the underlying discriminatory wrong; negligent failure to correct that known or reasonably knowable wrong is a separate institutional connection, not compensation for unintended disparate effects alone. Appropriate corrective efforts remain relevant to the negligence route, but do not automatically defeat a proved actual-authority route.

**Application.** Calling Kinman out of study hall and using the teacher relationship to obtain intimate disclosures and access can support the actual-authority connection; friendship alone could not. Repeated reports, the transfer request, the investigation's timing and asymmetry, the alleged wrong-line trace and lack of protective separation permit a dispute over reasonable institutional correction even if they do not establish constitutional deliberate disregard. The school's later termination does not conclusively answer responsibility for earlier preventable injury. The trier must identify the relevant period, knowledge, authority, response and causation.

**Strongest objection and answer.** Title VII expressly refers to employer agents, while Title IX concerns funding recipients and has a notice-and-voluntary-compliance administrative mechanism. These four do not disregard that distinction or import all employment rules. They conclude that ordinary program/agency responsibility is compatible with the existing private action and express nondiscrimination condition. Section 1682 governs federal administrative enforcement and does not expressly make its notice/hearing sequence a prerequisite to private compensation. Franklin prevents turning funding notice into a categorical compensation defense, while leaving the Court to identify the actual institutional connection. These four reject strict liability for every teacher's private act and an automatic award whenever a school policy was violated.

### O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas

**Rule and judgment.** Private compensation from a funding recipient for a teacher's harassment requires actual notice to an official empowered to take corrective action of the harassment or its substantial risk, followed by deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that known danger that causes injury or continuation. The wrongdoer's knowledge of her own conduct is not alone institutional notice to an independent corrective authority. Merely constructive notice and ordinary agency assistance are insufficient for this compensatory recipient action. They would reverse the Title IX remand and restore recipient summary judgment on this record because deliberate disregard is not established even assuming the relevant actual knowledge.

**Sufficient grounds.** Franklin reserved attribution; its compensation rule does not decide when intentional teacher misconduct becomes a recipient's intentional breach. Title IX's recipient-centered funding obligation lacks Title VII's express agent language. Section 1682's enforcement structure addresses the recipient's own noncompliance and provides notice and opportunity for voluntary correction before federal enforcement action. That scheme, together with Pennhurst's notice principle at the reserved attribution scope, supports requiring conscious institutional nonresponse rather than imposing private compensation for what the recipient should have discovered. These four do not import the administrative hearing, federal-agency notice or thirty-day congressional-report provisions as literal prerequisites to a private suit; they derive an attribution distinction from the recipient's role. Nor do they apply qualified immunity to the District.

**Application.** Whitehouse and Mackiel investigated the asserted relationship through interviews, attempted telephone monitoring, two polygraph examinations and confrontation of McDougall, followed by authentication and termination when the journal, cards and photographs arrived. The favorable account permits criticism of delay, technique and failure to use available interim protection. It does not supply deliberate refusal to address the known danger rather than an imperfect effort to ascertain and stop it. An allegation that the trace used the wrong line is not proof that the error was deliberately chosen. The officials were not entitled to demand conclusive proof before any response; they did undertake responses before such proof arrived. Their use of denials and adverse tests is not treated as conclusive truth or a finding that Kinman lied.

**Strongest objection and answer.** Entrusting educational authority can materially enable a teacher's intentional discrimination; refusing ordinary agency attribution may leave a student without recipient compensation even where that authority facilitated the injury. These four accept the concern but distinguish program authority from the recipient's own compensable noncompliance under the funding scheme. Andrade answers an express employer-agent statute, while Franklin and Brown reserved this different question. They do not approve teacher misconduct, eliminate direct personal remedies, require an exact prior warning of each act, or make the mere existence of an investigation conclusive in every case.

## Constitutional supervisory and municipal disposition

All eight affirm the reviewed section 1983 summary judgment at the following bounded grounds. They do **not** make agreement on the result an implicit unanimous endorsement of every earlier separate position in Doe.

**Whitehouse and Mackiel.** Apply the controlling Doe rule to each official and the favorable record. Assume the reports supplied awareness of a substantial risk; do not demand a confession or treat adverse polygraphs as proof of falsity. Whitehouse received the several student/staff reports and the mother's complaint; Mackiel received Grosse's October 16 report including the attempted suicide and participated in the ensuing inquiry. Their actual interviews, trace effort, testing, confrontation and later authentication/termination course distinguish this record from knowing acquiescence or a deliberate refusal to protect. Earlier warning signs, refusal of a transfer, nonsuspension and imperfect investigation can support negligence. Without additional evidence of deliberate disregard and causally effective nonresponse, they do not suffice for the constitutional wrong. The Court does not announce that any investigation necessarily defeats deliberate indifference or that breach of school policy is immaterial evidence.

**District.** Monell requires an unconstitutional policy or custom, or a decision attributable to an official possessing final policymaking authority, with the necessary causal connection. The evidence does not establish a persistent District practice of permitting sexual abuse, an attributable policy directing this abuse, or final policymaking authority in either supervisor for the asserted decision. Ordinary supervisory rank cannot fill that gap. The District is not vicariously liable for the teacher or personally immune under Harlow. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no additional rule. This decision does not transform absence of a broader pattern into a universal bar to every single-final-decision claim; the attribution predicate for that distinct route is unestablished here.

**Immunity and limits.** No new personal-immunity holding is required after failure of the reviewed supervisory merits claims. Doe's 1995 rule, Lanier and Nabozny cannot be used as 1988–1990 notice. The constitutional and Title IX claims have different fault and attribution questions; four Associates can sustain recipient negligence/agency review while joining this constitutional result without inconsistency. No new selective-enforcement or danger-creation claim is added. The unappealed Nebraska tort dismissal remains undisturbed.

## Alternative paths, topology and validation receipt

The broad four's strongest route to maintaining the Title IX remand is the supported dispute about ordinary reasonable correction, with a separately available actual-authority route. The narrow four's strongest route is a recipient-specific culpability rule and the failure to show deliberate disregard in this response record. Neither rationale is a logical subset of the other across every case: agency liability can exist without institutional notice, and negligent correction can exist without conscious disregard. No Marks synthesis or constructed attribution consensus is available from these eight commitments. Their common same-sex/welcome conclusions can appear in a shared portion; the recipient rule, application and associated mandate require separate votes and substantive separate-writing treatment.

For section 1983, the strongest contrary path would return the inquiry because the delay, transfer refusal and unequal investigative treatment could represent deliberate avoidance. The record supports imperfect or negligent response but supplies no additional deliberate-disregard fact beyond the shortcomings themselves. Every Associate selects the bounded merits affirmance; none relies solely on counting investigative acts, accepting defendants' denials, a lack of custody or a general school immunity.
