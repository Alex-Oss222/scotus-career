## 112. Thomasson v. Perry

**Event and research cutoff:** June 9, 1997 merits decision; argument February 19, 1997. The supplied grant concerns the statement provision's equal-protection and First Amendment validity and military deference. Independent APA relief, general procedural due process, directive invalidation and an independent substantive-liberty claim are not added.

### Historical comparison and governing scope

T1 confirms a nine-judge affirmance: Wilkinson, Russell, Widener, Murnaghan, Wilkins, Niemeyer, Hamilton, Luttig and Williams. Murnaghan wrote separately for himself. Luttig's separate opinion was joined by Russell, Widener, Wilkins, Hamilton and Williams. Hall dissented with Ervin, Michael and Motz. This completes the abbreviated sanitized comparator's description; it changes no neutral fact or modeled Justice commitment.

Wilkinson sustained the statement inference under ordinary rationality and substantial military deference, treated the statement as evidence rather than a speech offense, and rejected the due-process and APA objections discussed below. Murnaghan found the unrebutted rational presumption sufficient without the majority's extended deference account; his proposed nonmilitary breadth is not adopted by the reconciled affirmers. Luttig would invalidate the directive's narrowing definition and uphold a broader statutory exclusion based on known homosexuality. His preservation discussion, including note 14, identifies competing party accounts. It supplies neither an uncontested grant nor an entered invalidation. No reconciled Justice selects that separate route.

Hall contests the disclosure-to-misconduct inference, gives independent constitutional review decisive weight, and would reverse. His opinion supplies a substantial contrary argument but is not an undisputed finding that the offered evidence disproved every relevant propensity. His broader reliance on absence of empirical or individualized harm is not imported into the reconciled minority's rule. The controlling simulated Walmer rule expressly permits reasonable predictions without either showing.

All eight Associates apply ordinary rationality. Walmer permits conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable military predictions and imperfect classifications, while requiring a connection to the actual disadvantage and excluding bare harm or irrational prejudice. It did not resolve §654(b)(2), an orientation-only inference or this final discharge. Romer's broad civil disability and Virginia's sex-classification burden therefore provide neither an automatic result nor a new orientation tier. Bowers does not independently settle this equality question.

The legal route is §654(b)(2): a specified statement requires separation under approved procedures unless the further finding establishes that the member is not a person who engages in, attempts to engage in, has a propensity to engage in, or intends to engage in homosexual acts. The reproduced directive defines propensity as a likelihood of acts beyond abstract preference or desire. Its listed rebuttal considerations and the actual two-day Board proceeding matter. Thomasson's service record, supporting accounts and expert evidence were received; the Board found the presumption unrebutted and he declined further specific rebuttal. There is no established exclusion of his evidence, forced recantation, denied hearing or new Board instruction.

The statute's other terms retain their separate operation. Section 654(b)(1)'s actual-act exception requires all five additional findings: departure from usual behavior, unlikely recurrence, no force/coercion/intimidation, consistency of continued service with discipline/good order/morale, and no propensity or intent. They are not (b)(2)'s rebuttal requirements. Section 654(b)(3) addresses same-sex marriage or attempted marriage; it is not this discharge ground. Section 654(e) removes a processing obligation only when both service-avoidance purpose and the military's contrary best interest are determined; it permits omission of processing rather than commanding omission. Neither predicate is established here. Congress's §654(a) findings remain legislative judgments rather than individualized findings about Thomasson.

### Alternatives tested

**T-A — Affirm the conduct-related, genuinely rebuttable inference and its evidentiary use.** The voluntary declaration rationally bears on likely conduct Congress may address preventively. Ordinary rationality does not require proof of a completed act or impaired unit. This Board received the offered rebuttal, and general excellence does not necessarily negate the separate likelihood predicate. Dawson and Mitchell permit relevant expression to prove a lawful predicate; policy criticism itself remains protected. The strongest counterargument is that the policy allows orientation while burdening only its disclosure and that exemplary obedience undermines the asserted likelihood. T-A accepts imperfect prediction, distinguishes private attraction from a conclusive status disqualification, and relies on the actual opportunity for rebuttal.

**T-R — Reverse this application on ordinary equal-protection grounds.** This statement discloses identity and policy disagreement without admitting conduct or intent to violate a conduct rule. In its actual context, those words do not supply the relationship needed to put the burden of disproving likely prohibited conduct on this officer and sustain his discharge. Service evidence is relevant context rather than a universal performance defense. The strongest counterargument is Walmer's permission for classwide predictions and the challenger's ordinary burden. T-R accepts those rules but finds this particular identity-to-noncompliance inference insufficient; it does not require a completed act in every preventive personnel decision. A contextual admission of intended acts or other evidence of likely conduct would present a different case.

**T-S — Independent as-applied speech ground.** Stevens additionally concludes that using the protected disclosure to supply the missing disqualifying fact penalizes expression without the constitutionally relevant connection Dawson and Mitchell require. The strongest objection is that voluntary words can furnish evidence and that the military need not wait for harm. His answer concerns the inadequacy of the particular inference, not a categorical evidentiary privilege. Souter and Ginsburg do not join this independent constitutional ground once equality supplies complete relief.

No construction-only remand is available merely by assuming a Board error the sources do not establish. No Justice selects a facial invalidation of every §654 route, a newly transferred Virginia tier, an actual-purpose proof burden, a universal individualized-impairment condition, military immunity from review or an independent directive-invalidity judgment.

## 114. Chalmers v. Tulon Co. of Richmond

**Event and research cutoff:** June 12, 1997 merits decision. The vehicle is review of summary judgment for Tulon on the religious-accommodation claim. The abandoned disparate-treatment theory supplies no independent ground to resolve accommodation. No constitutional, tort, mixed-motive or privacy claim is added.

### Historical comparison, record and common doctrine

C1 confirms Motz's majority, joined by Michael, and Niemeyer's dissent. Motz relied on inadequate notice for these personal home letters and an independent inability-to-accommodate ground. Majority note 3 expressly preserves the possibility that general religious knowledge may give notice of some practices; it does not prohibit all indirect notice. Niemeyer would vacate and remand for trial, disputing both the notice requirement and the use of adverse inferences on summary judgment. His proposed prima facie formulation and trial direction are not automatically the reconciled remanders' rule.

Both letters and their different relationships remain material. The LaMantia letter did not expressly accuse him of adultery; Chalmers explained that she meant inaccurate customer-turnaround statements. His wife's distress, interrupted business presentation, reported marital strain and request for termination are nevertheless part of the record. The Combs letter expressly criticized adultery and described possible illness from sin, while disclaiming that this explained Combs's illness. She was a subordinate convalescing at home. The supervisory relationship permits a concrete pressure concern without establishing a completed harassment claim.

Combs's later no-offense/no-relationship-damage statement is unsworn. The separate unsworn Faber account attached to his affidavit was expressly not relied upon by the majority. The Bible-study-invitation account was not formally attached to the summary-judgment response. No reconciled position silently treats those materials as competent Rule 56 proof. Chalmers's own account of unchanged behavior and workplace relations, the letters themselves, prior ordinary religious exchanges and her explanation/apology remain available at their supported scope. Neither side treats an inference as an adjudicated historical fact.

All eight directly joined Beadle, Brown's statutory accommodation part and Opuku-Boateng. Their common law requires the sincere practice, employment conflict, employer knowledge and adverse treatment; the employer must supply an effective reasonable accommodation or demonstrate inability without more than a de minimis business hardship under the then-governing rule. Concrete noncash costs, supported operational predictions, supervisory pressure and third-party burdens may count without an actual accident, complaint or completed harassment violation. No mandatory negotiation, experiment, every-alternative inquiry or lesser-sanction duty is added. A genuine dispute of material fact or inference must be resolved under Rule 56.

**N — Notice:** Knowledge can be direct or indirect and needs no ritual request or prescribed words. General familiarity with faith does not establish notice of every practice. The timing and content of knowledge matter to the accommodation sought and opportunity to avoid or limit the burden. Neither all indirect notice nor all knowledge acquired before discipline is categorically insufficient.

**H — Hardship:** The employer must connect this practice and any supported materially distinct accommodation to the conduct of its business. Supported forecasts and legitimate noncash burdens count; status as a supervisor, offense or a possible lawsuit alone is not automatically sufficient. A legitimate nondiscriminatory disciplinary reason is not itself the distinct statutory defense.

### Alternatives tested

**C-A — Affirm on specific notice and independently sufficient hardship.** Tolerated conversations did not reasonably identify a need for these personally corrective home letters in time to avoid their effects. Later religious knowledge does not itself require retroactive forgiveness. Independently, the concrete distress, interrupted business presentation, reported break in the managerial relationship and supervisory letter support more than a minimal operational burden. The strongest contrary argument is that the employer understood the religion before discharge and could retain Chalmers under prospective boundaries. C-A finds no established effective alternative in that suggestion and requires no experiment or mandatory warning; it does not adopt a categorical religious-letter prohibition.

**C-R — Vacate and remand for the proper notice and hardship application.** Prior exchanges and unmistakable religious knowledge before the employment decision prevent the claim's conclusive rejection for lack of an exact advance request here. The content of the letters, Chalmers's supported account, explanation and apology, and the distinction between prospective limits and permission to repeat the letters leave the categorical inability conclusion inadequate on this summary-judgment record. The strongest objection is that injury already occurred and a broken relationship need not be tested again. C-R accepts predictive hardship and the real reactions but finds the decisive inference about inability to continue employment under limits unresolved; it does not hold a warning feasible or require trial regardless of lawful further proceedings.
