### Shared affirming ground and direct joins

The six affirmers directly support this confined holding: allocating administration expenses to postmortem income does not by itself compel reduction of the marital or charitable deduction for every dollar paid. The governing valuation regulation requires account of a material limitation on the spouse's income rights; it does not make every income charge automatically dispositive. In this case the Commissioner bore Rule 142(a)'s burden on the omitted-notice adjustment, maintained categorical reduction, and did not offer and preserve a quantitative-materiality basis for overturning the Tax Court's finding. That unsuccessful legal theory supplies no basis for reversal, and the Court does not reopen the proof simply to allow a different theory now.

The six accept the net-value and material-limitation language together. The regulation's example says the rule may apply to income used for administration; it is not rewritten as an unconditional command or categorical exemption. Auer and TJU require deference within the regulation, not automatic acceptance of an interpretation that removes the material-limitation inquiry. The mere fact that another reading is reasonable would not defeat the Commissioner; the affirmers find the asserted inevitable consequence incompatible with the actual qualified rule. Section 2056(b)(4)(B) requires accounting for encumbrances but does not itself establish this universal calculation. State-law allocation permission and the exclusion of actual postmortem income from the gross estate do not alone establish immateriality. Revenue Ruling 93-48 addresses deferred-tax interest; its different treatment of income burdens may inform the regulatory reading but is not a holding that it expressly decides all administration expenses or wholly withdraws Revenue Ruling 73-98. The narrower common opinion need not decide the complete equivalence of those expense categories.

This is a direct common ground, not a Marks synthesis of historical opinion groups. O'Connor, Souter and Thomas do not join a generalized rule limiting every pertinent fact to what could have been predicted at death, a universal discounted-annuity method, or merger of qualification and valuation. Kennedy, Stevens and Ginsburg may accept the common disposition without requiring those disputed additions. The frozen provisional opinion already chose this narrower sufficient ground for those Justices; comparison confirms that their material-limitation and no-basis-to-reverse positions permit it. A broader valuation proposition requires its own joins and may not be attributed to all six.

The affirmation does not establish that $1.5 million is invariably immaterial, use total estate size as the decisive denominator, or establish any numerical safe harbor, percentage, mandatory factor list, exhaustive formula or automatic new hearing. The deferred-tax-interest ruling is not an abandonment of the administration-expense position. ## 55. Nabozny v. Podlesny — March 10, 1997

**Disposition by component:** All eight Associates affirm reinstatement of the sex and sexual-orientation equal-protection claims against Podlesny, Davis, Blauert and the District, affirm denial of individual qualified immunity on those supported equality claims, and affirm the rejection of the two preserved due-process theories. The case returns for ordinary proceedings on the surviving equality claims. No official's liability, municipal policy, damages, or particular injunction is established. The District has no qualified immunity; the surviving municipal claim still requires Monell attribution and causation.

**Scope and assumed record:** This is review of a final summary judgment, not a defendant-only collateral appeal. The whole record is available for equality because the district court ventured outside the moving grounds; for due process, assume the whole record in Nabozny's favor without resolving the separate local-rule question. Defendants' contrary account that they investigated and punished complaints does not displace the favorable account. The generalized special-relationship theory was expressly disavowed below; the Court declines to revive it. Lebron permits limited consideration of an abandoned included legal argument but imposes no duty and supplies no new custody facts. The preserved increased-risk and harmful-policy theories receive their own analysis; no custody requirement disposes of them by itself.

**Equality rule and application:** When public officials intentionally withhold ordinarily available protection from a student because of sex or sexual orientation, the unequal administration is subject to equal protection even though substantive due process imposes no general duty to protect from private violence. Intentional sex discrimination must substantially serve an important governmental objective; orientation discrimination here must at least rationally relate to a legitimate purpose, without deciding a heightened tier. Unequal disciplinary outcomes, negligence, breach of state policy, knowledge or deliberate indifference without the required discriminatory purpose do not alone establish the equality violation. Feeney supplies purposeful selection because of the adverse effect, Arlington Heights permits proof through departures and contextual evidence, and Yick Wo forbids intentionally unequal administration.

The alleged statements linking nonprotection to being male or openly gay, ordinary enforcement admitted by defendants, differential treatment of male-on-female abuse and repeated departures permit a jury to find purposeful discrimination. The missing names and dates in some comparator examples bear on proof; they do not erase the more direct evidence at summary judgment. No legitimate disciplinary objective explains knowingly treating this student as less entitled to protection because he is male or gay on the favorable account. Neither every complaint nor every student must receive identical discipline. Romer and Walmer govern the present ordinary-review inquiry at their actual scopes: conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit remain permissible, but bare harm and irrational prejudice are not. Walmer's military service relationship supplies no rationale for abandoning ordinary school protection. Virginia supplies no orientation tier. The school-presentation Brown and optional-program Curtis holdings decide no immunity from intentional discriminatory treatment.

**Equality immunity:** On that supported account, the individual officials had notice from conduct-time equality law without a factually identical school-harassment decision. Before 1988, Reed, Weinberger, Hogan, Yick Wo, Feeney and Arlington Heights already prohibited arbitrary sex stereotypes and purposeful, irrational unequal administration. Bohen, 799 F.2d 1180 (1986), is relevant circuit authority for intentional sex discrimination; its employment setting is preserved. Cleburne and Moreno reject irrational prejudice and bare harm. The state antidiscrimination policy corroborates knowledge of the protective practice and the classification; its violation is not by itself a federal violation or the reason immunity fails. DeShaney's later express equality reservation confirms the distinction for conduct after its date but is not needed to furnish pre-1989 notice. Bowers concerned regulation of specified sexual conduct and did not authorize private battery or intentional denial of the ordinary protection against it. No inference of future sexual conduct or criminal offense is supplied. Romer, Virginia, Elder and current-term cases govern today's analysis but cannot retroactively give notice for earlier acts. Individual responsibility, purpose and causation remain disputed, and defendants may prevail on their factual account.

**Due-process rule and application:** Assume without deciding that sufficiently culpable official creation or increase of a danger, or an attributable policy affirmatively encouraging private injury, can establish an official deprivation. The preserved theory that failure to punish emboldened assailants does not itself establish that affirmative contribution. Mere omission does not become an affirmative deprivation by repeating it as a policy. The conclusion does not rest on the district court's broader proposition that private assailants can never be connected to state action. Pinder expressly preserves affirmative deprivation of available protection; Uhlrig leaves the merits open. Neither supplies a five-part test or mandatory assurance/reliance element.

The full favorable recitation was tested, including acts beyond silence. Podlesny promised protection, responded to the mock assault with the alleged stereotypes, and the school counseled Nabozny for leaving without permission and returned him to his ordinary schedule. That is serious evidence of discriminatory nonprotection, but this preserved record does not establish a separate official act directing an identified new assault or depriving otherwise available protection. Davis and Blauert referred Nabozny to Reeder to change his schedule to reduce exposure; the school later placed him in special education with Huntley and Grande. The favorable facts do not identify a remaining named defendant's deliberate placement decision or establish that this rearrangement, as distinct from the ongoing exposure and nonpunishment, increased the danger that produced a particular later injury. Calling a scheduling change affirmative does not by itself establish the constitutional contribution or culpability. This is a limitation of what the authenticated favorable account establishes, not an inference from unavailable research papers.

Social Services' return order is a different actor's act; the record does not attribute it to Podlesny, Davis, Blauert or a District policy. The parents' promise of a different school is not an official undertaking. Crawford discouraged charges after Huntley's beating, so that discouragement cannot have caused the completed beating. He is not a remaining defendant, and neither his employer, final policymaking role, attributable District policy nor a later injury caused by the discouragement is established. The Court does not adjudicate a new Crawford claim or hold that obstruction of police protection could never violate due process. The pleaded harmful-climate theory remains failure to discipline; no separate affirmative instruction to assailants or attributable encouragement beyond nonenforcement is established. The equality claim survives independently. No due-process immunity holding is needed after affirmance on these confined merits grounds.

**Adversarial paths:** For equality, defendants' strongest path treats the evidence as inconsistent enforcement and imperfect comparators; that path fails at summary judgment because the statements and admitted ordinary practices support purposeful selection when credited. For immunity, absence of a factual twin and uncertainty about the ultimate orientation tier do not obscure this narrower prohibition on intentional irrational nonprotection. For due process, Nabozny's strongest path combines assurances, forced attendance, scheduling, discouragement of charges and known violence as official increased vulnerability. Pinder prevents categorical rejection merely for lack of custody. But the actual actors, preserved theories, sequence and causal connections cannot be merged: the referral's stated protective objective, unidentified placement decision, separate Social Services act and post-beating Crawford act do not establish the required attributable affirmative deprivation on this record. A remand solely to develop new attribution or a different claim would not correct an identified legal error in the preserved failure-to-punish theory. No rule makes assurances and reliance universally indispensable; their absence here is relevant only to distinguishing Pinder's actual alleged interference with available aid.

## Shared operative commitments

All eight Associates support the following propositions at the same level of generality and support affirming the judgment of the Tenth Circuit on that basis. These are provisional direct opinion joins, not merely identical judgment votes. No separate writing is needed to express a supplied material disagreement.

### 1. Community conditional liberty

When the State actually releases a prisoner to live at his own residence, work in the community, and conduct ordinary community life subject to parole-like supervision and conditions, termination of that conditional liberty requires the protections of Morrissey v. Brewer, 408 U.S. 471, 480–490 (1972). The State cannot avoid that protection merely by calling the release preparole, retaining legal custody, identifying overcrowding as a program purpose, or reserving a separate decision about formal parole.

Morrissey protects the existing conditional freedom to live outside prison. It already accounts for the original conviction, continuing sentence, restrictions, supervision, and the State's need to return a person to custody on legally sufficient grounds. Harper's residence, work, and reporting arrangement present that liberty, rather than a routine transfer between prison settings. Five months of release reinforce the concrete nature of the deprivation but are not a new minimum duration for constitutional protection.

Greenholtz v. Inmates, 442 U.S. 1, 7–12 (1979), distinguishes a request for release not yet obtained from the loss of conditional freedom already possessed. The Governor's refusal of formal parole is not automatically a constitutionally adequate revocation proceeding for existing preparole liberty. This conclusion does not hold that Harper was entitled to receive formal parole or that every form of temporary absence from prison has the same constitutional incidents.

Sandin's controlling sufficient, nonexclusive rule requires genuine substantive constraints and a meaningful deprivation for the state-created prison-entitlement route; it expressly preserves directly protected liberty. The Court applies that preservation and Morrissey's more specific rule. It adopts neither the historical atypical-and-significant-hardship formulation nor an inference that mandatory words alone create protected liberty. No new finding that the entire Oklahoma program independently satisfies Sandin's state-entitlement route is necessary.

### 2. Original conditions and the later procedure

Harper's October 1990 orientation and executed acknowledgment tied community status to compliance with release conditions. The Board simultaneously recommended parole and released him. The supported account describes five **apparently** uneventful months, followed by the Governor's denial and the March 14, 1991 telephone direction to return. Those facts support conditional community liberty; they do not conclusively establish that Harper violated no condition, that release was unconditional, or that he personally was then ineligible for parole.

The procedure on which the State relies was promulgated in August 1991, after the return to prison. The earlier procedure in effect at release was silent about the effect of a later parole denial. The August procedure therefore does not establish a parole-denial return condition communicated to Harper in October 1990. Nor can silence about that single issue establish the complete content of the governing scheme, forbid all other lawful return grounds, or make community release irrevocable. The Court rejects reliance on the later procedure to defeat this process claim; it does not invalidate the August procedure prospectively or resolve every possible substantive basis for revocation.

### 3. Process and its operative qualifications

Morrissey's ordinary protections govern termination of this liberty. They protect reliable determination of the legally asserted ground and the decision whether revocation is warranted, while preserving the State's authority to use informal administrative proceedings. The case does not add a criminal-trial model, a new evidentiary burden, a fixed numerical deadline, or an absolute requirement that every final revocation hearing precede initial arrest or detention.

**Preliminary stage.** When the participant is arrested and detained pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the place of the alleged violation or arrest determines whether probable cause or reasonable ground supports detention for a parole-condition violation. A person not directly involved in the case makes that determination; the officer need not be a judge and may be a different parole officer. The participant receives notice of the inquiry, its purpose, and the alleged violations; may appear, speak, and provide relevant documents or witnesses; and, on request, may question an adverse informant in his presence. If the hearing officer determines that disclosure of the informant's identity would expose the informant to a risk of harm, confrontation and cross-examination of that informant may be withheld. The officer summarizes the proceedings and gives the reasons and evidence supporting the probable-cause determination. Formal judicial findings are not required. A supported probable-cause determination permits continued detention and return to the institution pending the final decision; no such determination is manufactured here.

**Final stage.** The State must afford an opportunity for a final revocation hearing, if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before the effective final revocation decision. The inquiry resolves contested relevant facts and whether the established circumstances warrant revocation; the participant may deny a violation or present mitigation explaining why a violation should not result in revocation. Its minimum protections are:

1. Written notice of the claimed violations or asserted revocation grounds.
2. Disclosure of the evidence against the participant.
3. An opportunity to be heard in person and to present witnesses and documentary evidence.
4. Confrontation and cross-examination of adverse witnesses, unless the hearing officer specifically finds good cause for withholding confrontation.
5. A neutral and detached decisionmaker, such as a parole board whose members need not be judges or lawyers.
6. A written statement of the evidence relied on and reasons for revocation.

These proceedings may consider letters, affidavits, and other material that a criminal trial would exclude. Morrissey's reference to two months as not apparently unreasonable on the circumstances it discussed is not a universal deadline or safe harbor. A parolee cannot relitigate issues already determined against him in another forum, including a criminal conviction that supplies a revocation ground. No such prior determination, valid waiver, admission disposing of an actual violation, risk-of-harm finding, or good-cause finding is supplied for Harper, and none is presumed. The preliminary informant-risk qualification and final-stage specific-good-cause qualification remain distinct. Morrissey itself reserved the counsel question; this case announces no separate counsel holding or claim that all later date-eligible counsel doctrine is unresolved.

### 4. Judgment, remedy, and limits

Affirm the Tenth Circuit's judgment that this preparole status requires Morrissey process and its reversal of the denial of habeas relief. The case returns through the ordinary mandate for proceedings consistent with that protected-liberty and process determination. Oklahoma remains able to establish and act upon a legally available revocation ground through constitutionally adequate procedures. The Court does not grant formal parole, order unconditional release, decide that Harper's sentence expired, establish a particular release date, declare every possible return ground invalid, or itself prescribe a new deadline for state proceedings.

This is a merits application of procedural due process, not an exhaustion remand based on hypothetical litigation defects. The question whether any particular subsequent revocation or continuing custody is lawful is left to proceedings applying the governing law and record. The original telephone instruction without the required opportunity for process cannot itself supply the constitutionally adequate revocation determination.
