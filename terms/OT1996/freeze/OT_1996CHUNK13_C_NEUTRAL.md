# 152. Faragher v. City of Boca Raton

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether an employer is vicariously liable under Title VII when a supervisor uses actual or apparent supervisory authority to create a sexually hostile work environment, even though the harassment is outside the ordinary scope of the supervisor's employment.

2. Whether liability for supervisory harassment requires actual or constructive knowledge by higher management, or may arise independently when the agency relationship materially facilitates the harassment.

3. Whether, in the absence of a tangible adverse employment action, an employer may establish an affirmative defense based on reasonable preventive and corrective measures and the employee's unreasonable failure to use available complaint procedures.

4. Whether the existence of a formal sexual-harassment policy is sufficient to defeat liability when the record raises questions about the policy's dissemination, implementation, or effectiveness.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

Faragher worked intermittently as a municipal ocean lifeguard during 1985–1990. After a bench trial the court found repeated unwelcome sex-based touching and humiliating conduct by Terry and Silverman. Their command positions controlled assignments, discipline and training in a unit remote from higher city officials. The City failed to communicate its harassment policy to that unit. Faragher complained to Gordon, another supervisor, but not higher management. The City investigated promptly after another former employee's letter. The trial court awarded Faragher one dollar on her Title VII claim. Its agency reasoning relied on supervisory authority and workplace structure, with constructive notice and Gordon's knowledge as additional grounds. Separate individual constitutional and state claims are outside the assigned employer-liability questions. [Trial findings](https://openjurist.org/864/fsupp/1552/faragher-v-city-of-boca-raton-1459086).

The amended en banc decision displaced the earlier panel treatment and rejected Title VII liability. Faragher seeks restoration of that nominal judgment; the City seeks to retain its appellate victory. The review concerns attribution of an established hostile environment, not a new trial over every act. [Operative appellate opinion, all writings](../sources/chunk13_c/faragher_ca11_complete.txt).
