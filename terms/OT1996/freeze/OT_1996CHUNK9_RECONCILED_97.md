### Components supported by all eight

1. **Public instrument and technical assistance.** The claim must be read as part of the patent, in light of the specification and any relevant prosecution history properly before the court. Technical dictionaries, publications and testimony may explain terminology, actual technical usage and the technology; genuine construction-related technical or credibility disputes remain for the judge under Markman. Such material cannot replace the meaning the complete public instrument establishes. The priority of the instrument's legally operative meaning is not a categorical ban on hearing explanatory evidence or a mandatory temporal sequence forbidding consultation until a formal ambiguity finding. No disclaimer is invented from the absence of prosecution history.
2. **This claim's meaning.** In claim 1 the term denotes peak reflow temperature, at which the solder flows in the disclosed process, rather than the temperature at which it first melts. The specification distinguishes liquidus around 190 degrees C from peak reflow around 210–218 degrees C and describes devices around 195 degrees while solder and board reach about 210 degrees. The requirement that devices remain below solder reflow temperature thus uses the latter meaning. Liquidus would exclude the disclosed integrated process and contradict that contextual use. These numbers explain the embodiment; they are not fixed numerical limits for every covered solder.
3. **Effect of opposing evidence.** The expert testimony, employees' statements, technical paper and litigation memorandum support use of the disputed phrase as liquidus in some contexts. Taking that evidence as a truthful account of such usage does not establish its use in this patent. Hall distinguished his generic usage from the patent; the technical paper also described heating above melting for effective flow. The public instrument's specific use resolves the legal question without disbelieving a witness. The judge's actual rationale was unstated; no finding that he expressly selected or discredited a particular witness is supplied. No universal standard of appellate review for subsidiary facts is necessary to correct this construction.
4. **Infringement and remedy.** Vitronics conceded noninfringement only under the rejected liquidus construction. That concession cannot sustain the judgment under the correct meaning. Conceptronic's alternative contentions about untested devices and temperatures above peak are disputed with supporting evidence and were not decided by the trial judge; the Court cannot resolve them through appellate weighing. Affirmance leaves the reversal/remand operative for adjudication of the actual infringement questions under the correct construction and ordinary procedures. It declares neither infringement nor an automatic new trial, and preserves every material claim element, including the emitter limitation outside this appeal. No equivalents claim is added; the separate unappealed patent verdict remains undisturbed.

## Adversarial alternative paths

**Affirmance with bounded construction/evidence rule:** This path reads the claim and specification together, accepts that liquidus terminology exists outside this patent, and identifies the patent's contrary contextual usage. The strongest objection is that intrinsic clarity may itself depend on expert explanation; the response preserves such explanation and judicial assessment rather than treating labels as dispositive. All eight select this path.

**Reversal restoring liquidus judgment:** This would be lawful if the patent left the generic technical meaning controlling or supported a fixed liquidus limitation. The disclosed relationship between solder, devices and temperature defeats that reading without resolving credibility. A generalized preference for expert testimony cannot supply the missing instrument support. No Associate selects it.

**Vacatur for new construction findings:** Markman permits genuine technical disputes to be resolved by the judge, so this path is available when an unresolved material technical premise or credibility choice is necessary. Here the correct meaning follows even accepting the opponent's generic-usage evidence, and the trial judge supplied no subsidiary finding requiring a chosen review standard. Further construction proceedings are unnecessary; disputed infringement facts do require the ordinary remand already ordered.
