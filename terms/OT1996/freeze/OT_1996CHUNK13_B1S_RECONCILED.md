## 1. Foreman v. Dallas County — Nos. 96-987 and 96-1389

### Reconciled components and operative grounds

**F1 — Direct review and the earlier appeal.** Section 1253 permits a qualifying interlocutory appeal from refusal of an injunction. The later final dismissal absorbs this same prospective-relief dispute under Shaffer v. Carter, 252 U.S. 37, 44 (1920). Dismiss No. 96-987 on that merger/supersession ground, while reviewing the final judgment in No. 96-1389. Neither nonfinality alone nor an invented temporary-restraining-order characterization supplies the ground. No distinct surviving interlocutory controversy is established.

**F2 — The relevant kind of practice.** A changed formula for appointing precinct officials who supervise election-day voting has the direct voting connection required by Presley. County discretion under a precleared enabling statute does not categorically exempt the resulting election-administration practice. Allen and Hampton County preserve practical electoral coverage; Monterey and Young now directly reject the relevant administration/discretion exemption. This decides the asserted legal exemption, not whether the current formula in fact differs from the coverage-date practice.

**F3 — Actual submission, not blanket approval.** The 1985 submission identified appointment timing and duration, not the County's particular partisan formulas. It therefore does not establish preclearance of those undisclosed methods. McCain, Clark, Monterey and Young require sufficient identification of the change actually submitted; receipt of the complete code does not compel the Attorney General to infer every possible local arrangement. A sufficiently identified local method could be submitted with state legislation; the existing recodification approval is neither rescinded nor enlarged.

**F4 — Missing baseline and remedy.** Vacate the final dismissal in No. 96-1389 and remand the consolidated cases for identification of the actual November 1, 1972 appointment practice, comparison with the challenged practices and determination of relevant submission status. A legally relevant covered change lacking preclearance cannot be administered prospectively, but the missing baseline prevents a final finding that such a change occurred here. The remand orders neither an immediate universal injunction nor permission to continue an established unprecleared practice. The enforcement court decides coverage and submission and supplies lawful fitted relief; substantive purpose/effect review remains with D.D.C. or the Attorney General. No completed-election consequence, appointment cancellation, damages, new partisan formula or unnecessary evidentiary hearing is directed.

## 2. Pounders v. Watson — No. 96-1383

### Reconciled petition and decision positions

**P1 — Petition stage.** All eight Associates would grant review, but on different procedural terms. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg support a grant with reasoned summary reversal on the bounded ground below. Stevens and Breyer would grant for plenary briefing and argument and oppose disposing of the merits summarily. Their opposition to summary reversal is not a vote to affirm habeas relief. These petition positions remain internal unless a Justice supplies a lawfully published statement; the public render must not publish a conference or certiorari poll.

**P2 — Six-Justice summary-process ground.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg conclude that the Ninth Circuit imposed an excessive restriction on summary contempt in its treatment of this trial. The received narrow power is not limited to noisy or physical obstruction, a lengthy pattern pervading the trial, or a separate finding that misconduct would recur without immediate punishment. Wilson shows that conduct threatening an ongoing trial may obstruct without noise or violence; Sacher permits protective deferral of punishment. The trial judge found willful defiance and serious jury prejudice, and the second punishment question followed an immediate admonition. Those circumstances support correction of the appellate summary-process ground without comprehensively redefining the constitutional exception.

The six retain Oliver's limits: misconduct openly before the judge, essential elements actually within the judge's observation, disturbance of the court's business and need to vindicate judicial authority. Personal observation, a petty sanction or irritation alone is insufficient. The trial judge's prejudice characterization is a finding relied on, not independent proof of jurors' mental states or a command in the contempt statute. The Court need not attribute a formally announced universal pattern rule to the Ninth Circuit to reject its excessive treatment of duration and future repetition here. It adopts neither automatic summary punishment for every evidentiary violation nor a newly mandatory checklist.

**P3 — Unaddressed notice/willfulness claims and factual review.** The Ninth Circuit expressly did not decide whether Watson knew a sufficiently definite order or willfully disobeyed it. The summary-process correction therefore does not finally resolve those claims. The trial judge's findings exist, but appellate nondecision is not acceptance. Lindh makes former §2254(d) applicable to this pre-enactment noncapital application. Its factual presumption depends on a qualifying state factual determination and remains subject to its statutory exceptions; it does not conclusively determine the constitutional legality of summary process. Thompson's law/fact distinction supplies the relevant discipline at its actual scope, without classifying every contempt issue automatically.

Further review must retain the former statute's conditions: a competent state court's factual determination after a merits hearing involving the applicant and State or its agent, adequately evidenced in writing; the exceptions for unresolved factual merits, inadequate factfinding procedure, inadequate development, absence of jurisdiction, unconstitutional denial of appointed counsel, lack of a full and fair hearing, other denial of due process, and lack of fair support in the pertinent record as a whole. Where its presumption properly applies and no exception appears, the statutory convincing-evidence burden remains. No exception, new hearing or wholesale factual relitigation is ordered merely because Watson disputes intent. A defense actually requiring facts beyond observation must receive the applicable due-process assessment; the Court does not decide that predicate here.

**P4 — Formal remedy and stage.** The six would grant certiorari, reverse the Ninth Circuit's summary-process ruling and its dependent instruction to grant the writ, and remand for the claims that court left unresolved and lawful further disposition. This does not order a new contempt prosecution, a second service of the completed two-day sentence, an automatic disciplinary result or final denial of all habeas relief. Stevens and Breyer oppose this present summary merits disposition and request plenary review; their preferred procedural route establishes no eventual writ or affirmance.

**P5 — Temporal law and nonretroactivity.** All eight apply Lindh's governing result, including those who dissented from its construction. None supplies a Teague disposition here. ### Adversarial paths and historical reconciliation

**Six-Justice correction path.** The strongest argument against summary correction is that prejudicial questioning followed by curative instructions and trial continuation can be addressed through ordinary criminal process, and neither Wilson nor Sacher makes necessity disappear. The six answer at the received exception's bounded scope: the renewed question after an immediate warning differs from McConnell's unfulfilled threat, protection of the jury's lawful decision process is part of protecting trial administration, and later continuation does not prove that the action already taken was unnecessary. Wilson defeats a physical-stoppage requirement; Sacher defeats a rule that delayed service alone destroys summary power. Remaining notice/intent challenges are expressly reserved. No finding of actual bias is made or rejected merely because the same judge observed the act.

**Stevens/Breyer plenary-review path.** They assume for purposes of this procedural objection that Watson defied the order and committed contempt. The issue is whether summary adjudication's validity is sufficiently clear to justify reversal without full briefing and argument. Two questions in a long trial, continuation without identified further incident and punishment for completed misconduct leave substantial questions about necessity, ordinary safeguards and whether a disinterested judge should hear the charge. McConnell and the procedural concerns expressed in Frankfurter's Sacher dissent support plenary examination; the dissent is an argument about the governing safeguards, not controlling authority displacing Sacher. They acknowledge the immediate warning, Wilson's protection against nonphysical obstruction and Sacher's allowance of delay. They assert neither a minimum number of violations nor a conclusive constitutional violation on this record. No unsupported embroilment fact, new prosecution, hearing entitlement or eventual habeas merits position is supplied.
