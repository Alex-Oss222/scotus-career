## Preserved issue, supporting evidence, and competing procedural claims

The United States objects specifically to additional alleged disciplinary deductions and witness-understanding testimony used to establish practical exposure to deductions. It says petitioners did not bring those materials to the court of appeals or present them at the petition stage, and that their appellate presentation focused on Guzy. It characterizes the presented question as assuming no applications of the personnel rules. Its footnote reports respondents' assertion that 288 of 289 petitioners had never experienced an alleged improper deduction in the prior fourteen years and reports that no petition-stage reply disputed the assertion. Those are verified **amicus representations about the proceedings**, not independently established statistical findings or a judicial forfeiture ruling. The footnote is partly damaged; do not reconstruct its missing words.

Petitioners' reply directly disputes exclusion of the record evidence. It argues that the manual expressly applied to commissioned officers including sergeants; employee and management testimony established actual understanding of exposure; and an employer invoking the exemption cannot rely on an assumed compliant policy without evidence. Its footnote distinguishes waiver of an unraised issue from citation of additional record evidence supporting a raised issue. The reply identifies record locations, including petition-appendix and JA pages. Those citations demonstrate a supported contention about existing evidence; the present extraction does not reproduce the cited testimony in full or establish the truth, scope, or credibility of every assertion.

These sources confirm, rather than resolve, the preservation dispute identified in the neutral packet. Salary basis, practical exposure, and correction are the pursued issues. The exact evidence allegedly added and its prior presentation are distinct subsidiary questions. It would be inaccurate either to call all salary-basis issues waived or to declare every proffered example unquestionably preserved merely because the broader issue was raised. Likewise, a refusal to consider disputed additional examples would not automatically eliminate the undisputed manual/Guzy record or the legal question whether a communicated policy can matter before an actual deduction.

The petitioners' related “core agreement” argument contends that the employer never satisfied §541.118(a)'s initial salary agreement requirement, so exceptions or correction cannot save the claim. That is an attributed legal construction of the preserved salary-basis issue, not an adjudicated absence of a compensation agreement. The complete regulatory text in the main neutral packet remains essential. The reply's narrower reading of correction cannot erase the regulation's disjunctive inadvertence/other-than-lack-of-work language.

## What the lower court actually decided about correction

The appellate opinion assumes without deciding that the salary-basis regulation validly applies to the public sector. It treats a merely contingent possibility of an improper deduction as insufficient. It expressly determines that Guzy's city-residency violation is not a major-safety rule within §541.118(a)(5). Its discussion treats the suspension as a negotiated substitute for possible termination, a one-time event rather than an established deduction policy, and notes the June 1, 1991 change making termination the sole sanction for violating residency. Its statement that the event cannot recur concerns that residency sanction, not a finding that every challenged disciplinary authorization was repealed.

The opinion reproduces paragraph (a)(6)'s alternative grounds and its cumulative reimbursement and future-compliance conditions. It concludes that it will not reverse the salary-basis determination and says the regulation requires reimbursement of the lost wages. This establishes an **express lower-court reimbursement requirement in the reasoning**. It does not establish payment, a completed compliance promise, elimination of all policies, or an express ruling that the regulation's future-compliance condition is dispensable. The United States' separate source statement that the conditions have not all been fulfilled remains unrebutted by these extracts.

The formal appellate conclusion affirms the district judgment on the appeal and reverses on the cross-appeal. It contains no express remand language, conditional dismissal instruction, retained proceeding, or implementation schedule. Therefore replace any suggestion that a formal remand expressly ordering cure has been verified with this precise distinction: **the reasoning requires reimbursement; the reproduced formal disposition is affirmance on appeal and reversal on cross-appeal; the United States proposes remand implementation and nondismissal until correction is complete.** The latter proposal must not be recast as text already in the lower mandate.

## The now-verified district judgment and remaining scope

The August 26, 1994 district final judgment awarded $9,284, inclusive of attorneys' fees and costs, to the particular Bomb and Arson Squad and Accident Reconstruction Squad sergeants previously designated partially nonexempt. It entered judgment for defendants on all other issues. This corrects the main packet's source limitation that no damages amount or final damages award had been established: **a particular pre-appeal district award is now documented**. It is not a computation of the entire petitioners' claimed unpaid overtime, a Guzy reimbursement award, a payment receipt, or proof of a currently enforceable award after the appellate reversal on the cross-appeal. The main packet's source-supported appellate posture—all petitioners ultimately treated as exempt—remains the posture under review.

The uncontested duties predicate must stay distinct from the salary-basis challenge. These lower-court sources explain the appeal/cross-appeal structure and the former district award; they do not introduce a new preserved Supreme Court challenge to the duties rulings or authorize reconsidering them independently.
