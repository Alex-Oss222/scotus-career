## Proposition-level support and public-law constraints

- **Eight:** Supported imminent personal school exposure permits prospective adjudication; completed injury is unnecessary. Neither generalized taxpayer standing nor a finding of completed coercion is established.
- **Six — Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer:** Preserve supported interim protection against school-directed worship, remove graduation's categorical exemption, and remand for provision-sensitive and application-sensitive narrowing. Scalia and Thomas oppose the retained restraint and its extension to graduation at this preliminary stage.
- **Five — Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer:** Official organization, adoption, direction, or religious supervision of school-event worship supplies a sufficient constitutional ground without an additional quantified pressure finding. Kennedy does not join this ground.
- **Six, the same remedial majority:** School-directed religious exercise in compulsory or comparably pressured school settings independently supplies the constitutional injury. Actual pressure from the official setting, rather than mere offense, is required by Kennedy's join. Scalia and Thomas do not join that indirect-pressure application.
- **Eight on the legal limit, with distinct remedial votes:** Relief must preserve genuinely private expression and independently lawful provisions; Section 1(3), Section 2, and severability cannot be disregarded merely because Section 1(2) permits unlawful operation. No new private-speech forum is compelled, and no broader access entitlement is created by deleting the content restrictions.
- **Eight separately on each intervention component:** Affirm denial as of right because the Attorney General actually advanced the identified private-expression interests and no omitted relevant interest is established, retaining Trbovich's minimal inadequacy burden. Affirm permissive denial within discretion on this duplication/delay record; a common question permits but does not compel intervention. Amicus participation does not replace an otherwise established intervention right.

Section 1(2)'s event permission and content eligibility limits remain distinct from Section 2's denial of authority to prescribe prayer's form or content. The latter is a statutory command; predicted implementation is a lower-court inference. The preliminary remedy does not finally invalidate every application or establish a universal code for school prayer. Both the supported restraint and the command to narrow must be stated so the lower court knows what remains protected during remand.
