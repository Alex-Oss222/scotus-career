## 66. Young v. Fordice

**Current-law correction.** Lopez v. Monterey County, November 6, 1996, is completed effective law, not an unresolved future packet. The copied holdings address covered administration despite compulsion by a noncovered State, the need specifically to identify submitted changes, the nonuse rule, separate substantive-preclearance channels, and the distinction between a genuinely judicial temporary remedy and judicial adoption of governmental policy. Apply each at its actual scope. Lopez does not itself decide whether an exclusively compelled federal implementation choice exists here or select Mississippi's comparison baseline.

Morse, Presley and Voting Rights Coalition remain controlling at the scopes copied in the slice. Exact operative register entries are “Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority,” “Voting Rights Act preclearance of primary-like party participation fees,” “The designated channel for preclearance review,” and “Federal election registration duties through state agencies.” Their present-operation authorities and qualifications are preserved verbatim. The NVRA non-displacement provision and §5 require independent attention; the duty to perform federal registration does not automatically preclear an independently chosen implementation feature.

**Neutral alternatives.** Mississippi can argue that the 41-day provisional procedure never became the lawful operative benchmark and that the later state-registration rules merely restored the old rules. It can separately argue that its prior submission identified the challenged changes or that a particular feature reflects a federal command leaving no implementation choice. The challengers can compare the post-February 10 system with the relevant lawful baseline, identify forms, communications, agency instructions or dual-status administration that changed voting practice, and dispute both the scope of submission and claimed necessity. A conclusion that the provisional plan is not the baseline does not establish that the new dual arrangement contains no changes from the older baseline. Coverage is not a finding of discriminatory purpose or effect. Each challenged change and each claim of prior submission require identification.

**Remedy and limit.** If covered changes lack preclearance, the enforcement court's authority is to enforce §5's sequence and fashion lawful relief; it may not grant substantive preclearance. Neither wholesale invalidation of federal registration nor a judicially selected identical federal/state roll follows automatically. The intake supplies the district court's summary-judgment grounds and objective implementation history. No new Mississippi statutory construction, actual DOJ approval beyond the described submission, or particular interim election plan is inferred. ## 67. Opuku-Boateng v. California

**Current-law correction.** Brown v. Polk County, March 17, 1997, is now effective. Its statutory Part II is joined by all nine Justices; its practice-specific hardship rule and refusal to require realized injury, an experiment, an actual complaint, a preferred accommodation or negotiation after effective accommodation/sufficient hardship govern at their exact scope. Beadle remains the term-opening rule, with eight Associate joins. The copied register entry is “Religious accommodation and demonstrated operational hardship,” effective February 5, 1996, now supplemented by Brown. Brown's free-exercise and mixed-motive holdings are not automatically additional claims in this solely statutory accommodation grant.

**Primary-record clarifications.** The complete Ninth Circuit opinion, dissent and footnotes at 95 F.3d 1461 were read. The source is saved by exact web-render copying at `sources/chunk6-neutral-b/opuku_appellate_web.txt` ([published appellate opinion](https://openjurist.org/95/f3d/1461/opuku-boateng-v-state-of-california)). It reports these material distinctions:

- The 1982 station had 15 inspectors, including 5 supervisors, and operated three eight-hour shifts, with staffing and shifts increasing seasonally. The policy sought an equal annual number of undesirable shifts and varying assignments to discourage collusion and expose inspectors to different commodities.
- The Court of Appeals accepted that California negotiated in good faith for a week. Its judgment did not rest on the absence of any negotiation; it found that no employer accommodation proposal was made and that the hardship showing failed. The EEOC's contrary characterization of negotiation is a reported agency determination, not an uncontested trial fact.
- The appellate opinion states that no collective agreement or contractual seniority right governed the station's shift assignments. The earlier packet's reference to “bargaining constraints” should not be read as an established constraint at this station.
- The district court admitted Ingham's account of the employee poll only to establish that a poll occurred, not for the truth of the responses, but later relied on its results. Lozano understood the request as involving three days, rather than the actual continuous 24-hour Sabbath. Whitacre could not recall the question or number asked. The appellate court's broader assumption about other employees' understanding is its inference, not independent testimony from each employee.
- The appellate court considered equal undesirable-shift schedules, voluntary trades and combinations separately. Existing testimony described scheduling flexibility for semester-long courses and a minister's Sunday mornings. That evidence concerns the actual comparative arrangements; it does not establish any unlimited right to an exemption.
- Judge King's dissent would affirm for the district judge's reasons. The reported appellate judgment remands for appropriate relief, not a particular staffing arrangement or fixed monetary award.

**Neutral alternatives and limits.** The employee can defend the appellate result through specific failures of proof without defending every sentence suggesting a required temporary experiment. California can invoke Rule 52's protection of reasonable predictive findings and argue that the actual equal-rotation, security and coworker burden evidence meets Hardison. Beadle and Brown permit concrete noncash and professional predictions; a finding does not become speculative merely because harm has not occurred. Conversely, neutral policy or an inadmissible, materially misdescribed poll cannot automatically prove actual hardship. A lawful remand may be required if the proper legal standard cannot be applied to existing findings; that does not confer a new evidence opportunity as of right. The judgment must explain whether it affirms completed liability, restores the trial judgment, or remands an unresolved question.

Conduct occurred in 1982. No new 1991-Act compensatory/punitive damages or jury right is imported retrospectively; Brown preserves the temporal and source-specific distinction. Exact backpay, mitigation, equitable hiring/reinstatement and any changed staffing facts remain remedial questions. No Free Exercise or mixed-motive claim is invented.

## 71. New Mexico DHS v. Joseph A.

**Scope preserved.** The granted 1995 appellate judgment, 69 F.3d 1081, concerns termination of the 1983 decree. **Primary-record qualifications.** The decree provides for twelve consecutive months of substantial and continuous compliance. Its stated purposes include fair, reasonable and timely adoption decisionmaking and adequate procedures securing access to permanent homes. The Special Master's 1993 report organized ten areas, summarized evidence and recommended dissolution; the district court adopted both reports and terminated on June 30, 1994. The children disputed the account of ADAPT data and dismissal of adverse case examples. The source identifies the following as report evidence or contested findings, not fresh Supreme Court findings: 30-day conference measures ranging from 6.2–63 percent; content measures of 31–97 percent; timely attorney notification of 26 percent and nonnotification of 8 percent; and testimony that time in custody rose from 1.5 to 2.1 years. The Master did not explain their relation to his compliance conclusions. The State asserted opposing evidence. The appellate court declined to weigh that evidence itself.

The later State Auditor report and proposed supplemental study were excluded because they were not considered by the Master. Other timely quality-assurance evidence was distinguished from those excluded materials. The State argued that the children challenged only one of the two reports; the appellate court rejected that vehicle argument because the appealed termination order rested on both and the first report had previously been deemed insufficient standing alone. These are the actual lower-court rulings available for review.

**Law and alternatives.** Rule 52 governs findings adopted from a master; ITT Continental Baking supplies the contractual interpretation setting, and the decree's actual obligations and essential purposes frame substantial compliance. The Department can argue that the adopted findings, read as a whole with the report, permit meaningful review and establish its contractual release condition; the children can identify the missing subsidiary findings, unexplained conflicting measures and inadequately connected methodology. An explanation need not recite every evidentiary item, but appellate factfinding cannot cure genuinely missing material findings. The lawful choices include sustaining termination on adequate supported findings or remanding for an adequate application and explanation without deciding actual compliance. A numerical score alone is not a universal discharge threshold, and immaterial deviations are not automatically equivalent to failure of essential obligations.

Rufo governs a different request to change still-binding duties because of changed circumstances; the Department need not show a Rufo change merely to prove performance of its existing termination clause. Freeman and Jenkins concern desegregation at their actual scopes and reinforce the need for operative remedial findings; they do not replace this decree's release condition with an unqualified constitutional-minimum standard or additional universal checklist.
