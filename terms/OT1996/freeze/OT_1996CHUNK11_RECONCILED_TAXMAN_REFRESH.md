## Why the clarification matters, and what it does not decide

Hopwood was decided November 25, 1996 and already applied at the original Taxman cutoff. The error being repaired is the omission of that existing authority from the earlier affected analysis, not a new ruling enacted after the original handoff. The complete public text confirms an affirmative six-Justice educational-interest holding, a unanimous invalidity judgment for the actual admissions machinery, Kennedy's distinct reservation, Scalia and Thomas's Section A disagreement, and Thomas's separate join in the bounded remedial-duty holding. Those distinctions now inform the individual positions below.

The prior reconciliation's immediate federal reversal rested on an overconclusive inference: the admitted educational purpose, sole Black teacher and exact employment tie were treated as enough to establish statutory permission. Hopwood's interest/means distinction is especially relevant to evaluating that inference through each Justice's own published position. It does not itself set a new employment evidentiary burden. Weber and Johnson remain the sources of the statutory voluntary-action inquiry, and the contested extension in C–D below is an interpretation proposed in this case, not an already controlling Hopwood employment rule.

The strongest case for retaining immediate reversal is substantial: Stevens's faculty-specific Wygant reasoning and Johnson's treatment of forward-looking purposes could support a confined permission tied to actual equality. Hopwood expressly reserves employment and therefore does not logically compel abandonment of that position. Nevertheless, the refreshed source-based inference is better supported at the stated scope. Each of the four vacating Justices joined both Hopwood's recognition of a possible interest and its rejection of the actual process despite that interest. Their prior and current sources support a possible statutory justification, while no controlling source makes this particular purpose/tie combination a complete defense to an existing-job loss. The grant asks about purpose and unnecessary trammeling; a reasoned answer may define the permissible inquiry and return its actual application rather than resolve it through an assumed educational benefit or an assumed absence of individual burden.

That remand is not based on missing operator files, a finding that the Board withheld evidence, a new mandatory educational study, or a presumption that the existing record is insufficient. The lower majority applied the purpose and burden grounds reproduced in the neutral packet; the vacating Justices reject their dispositive legal reach under the interpretation they adopt. Lower courts must apply that interpretation to the actual record, preserving ordinary procedure and the possibility that it still yields liability. No new evidentiary hearing is mandated. Conversely, the four affirming Justices identify complete statutory grounds for decision without reaching an unpresented constitutional claim; they do not treat Hopwood's employment reservation as an adverse employment holding.

## Entering law and matters common to all eight

**R0 — The employment question remains statutory.** Race selected which of two equally qualified, equally senior teachers lost an existing job in a necessary reduction. The tie does not make the decision race-neutral or eliminate the individual statutory interest. Title VII prohibits covered discharge because of race; Weber and Johnson recognize a bounded voluntary-action accommodation without invariably requiring an adjudication that the employer itself discriminated. The purpose and burdens of this admitted nonremedial plan remain the statutory questions. No constitutional claim was before the lower court; the fourth granted question permits comparison with constitutional principles, not entry of an unpresented equal-protection judgment.

**R1 — Exact Hopwood effect and limits.** Hopwood Part II holds by six direct votes that a public university's interest in concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be an important objective on a properly supported record. The university must establish its educational objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between it and the actual racial classification. A diversity label, racial balance or assumed racial viewpoint is insufficient. Part I independently held the particular separate admissions machinery unlawful, unanimously. O'Connor authored Part II; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined it. Kennedy supported removal of the categorical prohibition but reserved the affirmative interest holding. Scalia and Thomas opposed Part II and would disapprove nonremedial preference permission at their published scope.

Hopwood expressly reserved race-conscious employment and admissions at other educational levels. It neither amends Title VII nor supplies a finding of educational benefit, necessity or appropriate burden for this department. Its affirmative current holding must not be reduced to unsettled Bakke dicta or attributed to a nonexistent earlier majority for all Powell's reasoning. Its actual Bakke account preserves the five-Justice Part V-C competitive-consideration proposition and then adopts a more specific present rule. Adarand's common standard, which retains Croson's evidentiary demands and Wygant's attention to burdens, keeps its constitutional scope.

**R2 — No imported remedial or damages shortcut.** Hopwood's Fordice discussion does not establish a traceable continuing violation, remedial imbalance or dismantlement duty in this concededly nonremedial employment record. Its Title VI intent holding confirms at its own scope that good purpose does not make purposeful racial treatment unintentional; it does not answer Title VII's separate permission question. Its admissions same-decision burden and claim-specific remedies do not automatically replace the employment burden allocations or the existing federal/NJLAD judgment. No litigant loses because the operator lacks a document or because an employment question was reserved in a different case.

All eight adopt R0–R2 as the legal baseline. Recognition of Hopwood's controlling force does not make Scalia or Thomas join its affirmative rule as their preferred view, and does not make Kennedy a prior supporter of that rule.

## Competing statutory propositions

**A — Remedial boundary sufficient for affirmance.** The voluntary accommodation recognized by Weber and Johnson does not authorize this concededly nonremedial race-selected discharge merely to preserve department diversity. A manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category can support voluntary action without proof of the employer's own actionable discrimination. That premise is absent here. The constitutional availability of a supported interest in a different setting does not supply the distinct statutory authorization. This conclusion does not hold that educational diversity is inherently illegitimate, erase Hopwood, require a prior judicial discrimination finding in every affirmative-action case, or decide every constitutional faculty-diversity issue.

**B — Actual burden independently sufficient for affirmance.** Selecting an existing tenured employee for layoff because of race under this nonremedial policy, without its having a remedial limiting goal, unnecessarily trammels the interests Title VII protects. A necessary staff reduction and an equal tie do not make loss of an existing job an immaterial burden; the prior nonracial tie procedure could allocate the necessary loss without racial selection. This case-specific conclusion does not decide every remedial layoff plan, every hiring preference or a categorical constitutional rule about layoffs. Hopwood's distinction between opportunity injury and eventual success also defeats the proposition that absence of superior entitlement means absence of an individual interest, although it does not itself decide the statutory trammeling issue.

**C — Nonremedial purpose is not categorically disqualifying.** Weber and Johnson do not make their sufficient remedial premises the exclusive permissible purposes of every voluntary action. A public educational employer may offer a genuinely supported educational-integration purpose under Title VII even without alleging its own past discrimination or remedial imbalance. The statutory accommodation still requires examination of the actual practice, its connection to that educational purpose and its effect on the protected employment interests. An assertion that retaining a Black teacher adds racial diversity is not, by itself, a finding of the concrete educational benefit or a sufficient justification for selecting another teacher for discharge. Conversely, a properly supported integration interest is not rejected solely because it looks forward rather than remedies an identified employment wrong. Hopwood supports this distinction by analogy; C is a proposed Title VII construction, not a declaration that Hopwood already decided it.

**D — The equal tie defeats an automatic burden rule but does not establish lawfulness.** Actual equality of qualifications and seniority, the necessity of eliminating one position and preservation of the department's sole Black teacher are relevant to purpose, fit and the affected interests. They permit distinguishing this case from displacing a better-qualified or more-senior worker, imposing a rigid numerical quota, or barring a group from employment. They do not establish that Taxman had no protected interest or that the layoff imposed no burden. The proper statutory inquiry must consider the serious loss of an existing tenured position, the racial basis of the selection, the nonracial tie alternative, what educational benefit the actual decision seeks, and whether the practice is a confined integration measure or racial representation maintained for its own sake. These are applications of the existing purpose-and-burden inquiry, not an invented numerical score, a constitutional strict-scrutiny test transplanted wholesale into Title VII, or a newly prescribed evidentiary ritual.

Under C–D, an educational purpose is not categorically excluded and layoffs are not categorically invalid, but the current handoff does not decide that this practice is lawful. No universal fixed termination date is invented for a continuing educational objective; the claimed purpose nevertheless cannot excuse a perpetual racial quota or make the plan's operation irrelevant. The lower courts must evaluate the actual record under the statutory rule rather than either declare diversity inherently unavailable or presume that good intentions and exact employee equality settle the defense.

## Judgment and remedy alternatives

**E — Affirmance.** Affirm the reviewed judgment. The existing federal award of $134,014.62 in backpay, benefits and prejudgment interest remains, as does the separate $10,000 NJLAD emotional-suffering award and restored seniority. Taxman had already been rehired; no new reinstatement is ordered. The denial of the broader prospective injunction and punitive damages is not disturbed. Hopwood supplies no new same-decision defense, damages reallocation or burden shift in this proceeding.

**F — Vacatur and remand.** Vacate the federal liability determination and dependent relief for reconsideration under C–D, without entering judgment that the Board's decision was lawful. On remand distinguish the independent NJLAD ground and identify which relief it independently supports. The state-law liability and $10,000 award are not reversed merely by changing the federal rule, and no construction of NJLAD is made here. Existing monetary and seniority relief must be addressed according to the legal ground supporting it; neither automatic cancellation of all relief nor unconditional preservation of federally dependent relief follows. The remand orders no new reinstatement, punitive award, broader injunction or new constitutional claim. It does not predetermine whether further proceedings beyond the established record are necessary under ordinary procedure.

All eight accept the federal/state separation and the limits stated in both remedial descriptions; E and F implement their different federal commitments.

## Enhanced adversarial comparison

The strongest affirmance path begins with the individual's statutory protection from racial discharge and the remedial setting of Weber/Johnson's recognized permission. Hopwood's express employment reservation supports keeping the statutory question separate; its actual invalidity judgment demonstrates that even an available important educational objective does not validate the chosen means. The strongest objection is that a reserved question is not an adverse holding: Johnson left voluntary action's outer boundary open, and Hopwood now directly refutes a categorical claim that nonremedial educational diversity is always constitutionally illegitimate. The affirming Justices answer that objection with statutory text and the particular employment burden, not by denying Hopwood or treating its reservation as a ban.

The strongest contrary path relies on Stevens's specific Johnson and Wygant writings and on the five Associates' own affirmative Hopwood joins. It would be unsound to turn precedent sustaining one kind of voluntary integration into a categorical prohibition of every other educational effort. Its strongest objection is equally concrete: Hopwood requires substantiated purpose and fit, and the tie removes neither an individual statutory right nor the concentration of an existing-job loss. The vacating Justices answer by adopting a possible statutory route and remitting its actual application; they do not award the Board a constitutional or statutory safe harbor. That bounded route corrects the original handoff's unwarranted jump from asserted educational purpose to established permission.

A third lawful proposal—affirm solely on the existing-job burden while reserving permissible purposes—was tested separately. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas regard B as independently sufficient and can join such a narrower opinion without abandoning A. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not accept the lower court's concentrated-loss conclusion as categorically dispositive given the actual tie and the still-unexamined scope of an eligible educational purpose. They do not deny that a developed application may ultimately find unnecessary trammeling.

No path here decides constitutional validity. A route applying Hopwood's constitutional standard directly to enter a constitutional judgment is unavailable on the supplied grant and cause. A route treating diversity as a universal employment permission is unsupported by any Associate commitment.

### Alternative-path and remedy validation

**Affirmance under A:** The strongest challenge is that Weber/Johnson's sufficient remedial settings do not necessarily exhaust their permissible applications, particularly after this Court has recognized a genuinely supported educational objective in Hopwood. The affirming four answer with the statute's individual prohibition and the established remedial accommodation; they do not misstate Hopwood. The vacating four reject that exclusive-purpose conclusion as a construction of Title VII, not because Hopwood already enacted a faculty-employment exception.

**Affirmance under B alone:** The strongest ground is the concrete loss of an existing tenured position selected by race, with an available nonracial tie procedure and no remedial limiting goal. Each affirming Justice treats it as independently sufficient, so an opinion may reserve the outer purpose question. The strongest objection is the exact qualification/seniority tie and the possibility that a confined educational objective affects whether the burden is unnecessary. Each vacating Justice treats that as requiring the actual statutory inquiry, while conceding the job loss is serious and that a tie does not erase the protected interest. No vote for C automatically supplies a vote for a no-trammeling finding.

**Vacatur and remand under C–D/F:** The strongest challenge is that the court below already addressed burden and that Hopwood reserved employment. This route does not reopen facts simply because a different outcome is possible. It rejects the dispositive statutory reach of the lower grounds and directs application of the construction the vacating Justices actually adopt. It may preserve the existing judgment on a proper application; it does not instruct the lower courts to grant the Board an educational safe harbor or presume an evidentiary omission. Its source is a supported Title VII interpretation informed by, and carefully distinguished from, the Justices' own Hopwood positions.

**Immediate reversal of federal liability:** This route has the original handoff's direct educational-tie argument in its favor.
