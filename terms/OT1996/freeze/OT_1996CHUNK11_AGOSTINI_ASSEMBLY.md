# Agostini judgment, opinion relations and assignment

Event: Agostini v. Felton; consolidated Nos. 96-552 and 96-553; June 23, 1997; OT1996 chunk 11, No. 127.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the Opinion of the Court for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All five join Parts I-IV in full, including the modification of the governing Lemon test. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent. No separate concurrence is filed.

The Court updates Lemon into one inquiry with three routes: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives attribution-and-function analysis. Purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test. Aguilar's categorical onsite rule and Ball's corresponding rationale are displaced at the stated scope; Ball's completed judgment and unaddressed holdings remain intact. The present change supplies Rule 60(b)(5) relief, while Rufo, Rodriguez de Quijas and the concrete holdings expressly preserved in the Record remain operative.

Souter's Part I is joined by Stevens and Ginsburg; Part II also by Breyer. Ginsburg's independent procedural dissent is joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer. Stevens assigns the principal dissent to Souter. Their positions retain the distinct structural, stare-decisis and Rule 60 grounds; Breyer does not join Souter Part I's affirmative direct-aid theory.

## Judgment and remedy

**Vacated and remanded in both consolidated dockets, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Second Circuit judgments affirming denial of relief are vacated. Proceedings return for a district-court order removing the injunction's onsite prohibition to the extent it rests on the categorical ground rejected today. Any genuinely independent basis must be identified and decided under the surviving law; absent such a basis the prohibition must be dissolved. The City must obey the operative decree until judicial modification. Existing lawful services may continue during prompt implementation, and children currently receiving them should continue to receive them with existing statutory duties preserved. No new service entitlement, fixed timetable, damages or repayment is imposed.

## Opinion assignment

The Court issues one signed opinion in four parts for the two consolidated dockets. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela is in the majority and assigns the Opinion of the Court to himself. All five members join every part. Justice Stevens, the most senior dissenter, assigns the principal dissent to Justice Souter; Justice Ginsburg writes separately on the Rule 60 vehicle ground, with the joins stated above. No separate concurrence is filed.
