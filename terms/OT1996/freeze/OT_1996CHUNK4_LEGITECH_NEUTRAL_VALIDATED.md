## Supported record and lower judgment

The complete primary-text D.C. Circuit opinion and footnotes were read from the CAP reporter copy. The FEC investigated from 1986 Legi-Tech's computerized campaign-contribution information service. Some subscribers allegedly used the information for solicitation. The Commission found probable cause, attempted statutory conciliation, and filed a civil enforcement action seeking declaratory/injunctive relief and monetary penalties. The text of then-2 U.S.C. §438(a)(4), as quoted in the lower opinion, prohibits sale or use of information copied from FEC reports for soliciting contributions or commercial purposes, except use of a political committee's name and address to solicit contributions from that committee. The present dispute does not determine whether Legi-Tech itself violated that provision.

While cross-motions for summary judgment were pending, the D.C. Circuit's NRA decision held the nonvoting ex officio membership unconstitutional and severable. Four days after that opinion the Commission excluded the congressional officers from all proceedings. A few weeks later general counsel submitted recommendations on pending matters; after three days of deliberation the lawfully composed Commission found probable cause concerning Legi-Tech and authorized counsel to continue the litigation. These are reported acts; this packet does not claim possession of commission minutes or adjudicate the quality of individual deliberation.

The district court dismissed without prejudice to a fresh enforcement proceeding because it understood retroactivity to demand the NRA remedy despite ratification. The D.C. Circuit disagreed: it accepted the corrective act as adequate in these circumstances, distinguished rule retroactivity from remedies, and reversed. It did not enter liability or assess a penalty.

The FEC raised waiver because Legi-Tech did not plead the structural defense in its answer and never formally amended. The appellate court regarded it as an affirmative defense, rejected the contention that the objection was unavailable before NRA, but held waiver not automatic. The defense was submitted before judgment; absent prejudice to the FEC, the district court could treat supplemental authority as the functional equivalent of an amendment under Rule 15. This is a preserved defense on the appellate record, not a source gap that may be converted to default. A different threshold resolution needs legal support for overturning that treatment, not an invented missing filing.

Legi-Tech contended that the entire administrative course was structurally invalid, ratification could not replace all statutory safeguards, and the new decision was a rubber stamp. The FEC asserted that lawful reconsideration removed continuing prejudice and that repetition risked a limitations bar. The latter was a concern, not an established adjudication that time had expired. No actual bias by a commissioner was alleged according to the lower opinion; that does not mean no constitutional injury occurred. The court distinguished review of a decision to sue from statutory review of a refusal to act under §437g(a)(8). These reviewability and remedial points cannot be converted into immunity from all judicial examination of constitutional cure.

## Entering law and competing lawful paths

The exact annex contains the controlling titles, rules, effective dates and authority limits. Harper requires equal application of a governing rule to still-open direct review but leaves genuinely valid remedies distinct. Reynoldsville Casket permits an independently valid remedial/procedural basis while forbidding disguised selective prospectivity; reliance alone is insufficient. Simulated FEC v. NRA decided only Supreme Court representation and untimely authorization; it did not decide congressional membership, structural severability, structural ratification or the enforcement merits. A claim that it controls those merits would be incorrect. Ryder gives a timely substantial appointment challenger fresh adjudication by lawfully appointed adjudicators and rejects de facto validation of a defective judgment; its adjudicative-review context and limits must be addressed rather than assuming it either governs all executive investigation steps or is irrelevant to structural cure.

The path supporting appellate affirmance treats the valid Commission's own probable-cause finding and decision to proceed as a prospective lawful exercise of authority that adequately cures the enforcement defect. Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1, supplies the underlying appointments/separation principle and demonstrates that structural defects do not invariably nullify every prior act; it does not automatically resolve a timely challenger after Ryder. The strongest objection is that an endpoint vote may not replace the fair, lawful investigation and conciliation Congress made prerequisites, particularly when the same officials rely on an earlier tainted course. The model must state which functions the new Commission genuinely performed on the reported record and why those suffice; a presumption that it would inevitably reach the same result cannot alone establish constitutional cure.

The path supporting reversal requires lawful renewed performance of material statutory steps, or at least a remand to determine whether ratification actually supplied their protected functions, because the challenge concerns a structurally invalid exercise of executive enforcement power. Ryder supports the requirement of real lawful replacement where an earlier step's distinct function was lost. The strongest objection is that the Commission is enforcing, not adjudicating liability; the new Commission did reconsider probable cause and authorize pending litigation, and the district court dismissed as a supposed automatic consequence of retroactivity rather than finding a deficient cure. Demanding a complete restart regardless of what the valid Commission performed must be justified under statute and constitutional remedy, not merely labeled structural. Neither path establishes that the constitutional violation was harmless solely because personalized prejudice is absent.

A narrower remand can distinguish the governing structural rule from adequate cure, correct the district court's automatic-remedy premise and preserve any unresolved legally material inquiry. But the absence of original minutes in the operator's research is not itself a basis for an in-world production order, a finding that ratification was defective, or compulsory proceedings. Conversely, treating the reported ratification at face value is a judicial choice requiring reasons, not a newly discovered historical fact. Any de facto-officer ground is separate: the appellate court expressly did not need to reach it.

#### Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-794.
**Decided:** June 18, 1993.

##### An applied federal rule governs still-open direct review

**Holding:** When this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs all still-open direct-review cases concerning events within its substantive scope, whether those events predate or postdate announcement. A State cannot use selective prospectivity to withhold the rule; Chevron Oil is displaced to the extent it permits that practice, without reopening final judgments or deciding every conceivable prospective disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, An applied federal rule governs still-open direct review portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Davis, 489 U.S. 803 — nondiscrimination rule applied to pending claims.
- Griffith, 479 U.S. 314 — equal direct-review application extended to this civil setting.
- Beam, 501 U.S. 529 — its rejection of selective application clarified through a direct majority rule.
- Chevron Oil, 404 U.S. 97 — displaced only insofar as it permits selective prospectivity after application to the original litigants.

##### Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate

**Holding:** The state courts must apply Davis and determine the correction state law and federal due process require. If the State compelled payment before an adequate opportunity to contest the tax, meaningful backward-looking relief is required; lawful refunds, retroactive nondiscriminatory assessments or a combination may suffice, while a meaningful prepayment opportunity presents a different remedial setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final judgments are not reopened; pure prospectivity where application to the original litigants is reserved is not decided. Genuine procedural and remedial rules cannot reintroduce selective prospectivity. Virginia’s refund premise is not an independent state ground insulating the federal error.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for application of Davis and lawful correction under state law and due process; no fixed refund amount, appropriation or assessment of absent taxpayers without lawful process.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McKesson, 496 U.S. 18 — pay-first systems require meaningful backward-looking relief; alternatives preserved.
- Davis, 489 U.S. 803 — correction of unequal taxation distinguished from a particular refund formula.
- Beam, 501 U.S. 529 — procedural and remedial questions kept distinct from temporal governing law.

#### Reynoldsville Casket Co. v. Hyde

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-3.
**Decided:** May 15, 1995.

##### Reliance alone does not permit withholding a governing federal rule

**Holding:** A state court must apply a controlling federal constitutional rule to a case still open on direct review and cannot withhold that rule merely because a litigant relied on the invalidated statute. Independently valid procedural or remedial rules may determine relief, but cannot reproduce selective prospectivity under a different label.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: seven direct votes; Kennedy and O'Connor concur in the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Finality, valid procedural bars, immunity, an independently justified equitable rule and a constitutional remedial requirement remain distinct possible grounds. Reliance must satisfy an actual lawful doctrine's prerequisites; this decision establishes none and does not guarantee survival of the tort claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bendix Autolite, 486 U.S. 888: applies its invalidation of Ohio's interstate-commerce burden; no expansion of that constitutional holding.
- Harper, June 18, 1993: applies equal rule treatment on open direct review; final judgments and independently valid remedies remain distinct.
- Beam, 501 U.S. 529: applies rejection of selective retroactivity and preserves actual procedural/remedial questions.
- Chevron Oil, 404 U.S. 97: cannot support the selective temporal exception Harper displaces; no additional displacement is decided.
- Reich, December 6, 1994: distinguishes its apparent tax-remedy withdrawal; creates no automatic tort-claim preservation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ohio Supreme Court and remand for application of Bendix without the invalid tolling provision. Independently valid defenses or remedies remain available; the Court makes no equitable-tolling finding and orders no final dismissal.

#### Federal Election Commission v. NRA Political Victory Fund

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1151.
**Decided:** December 6, 1994.

##### The Commission lacks independent certiorari authority for this FECA enforcement action

**Holding:** Section 437d(a)(6)'s authority to litigate and appeal civil enforcement actions through the Commission's general counsel does not displace 28 U.S.C. §518(a)'s allocation of Supreme Court representation in this FECA corporate-contribution action. The Attorney General's particular-case direction exception remains available, and the express certiorari powers in 26 U.S.C. §§9010(d) and 9040(d) remain confined to actions in which the Commission appears under those respective funding sections.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six of eight participating Justices. Ginsburg did not participate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Providence Journal Co., 485 U.S. 693 (1988): applies its separation of lower-court litigation authority from Supreme Court representation while retaining ordinary statutory construction of congressional exceptions; no special verbal formula is imposed.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, 503 U.S. 249 (1992): applies its preservation of coexisting statutory routes and their conditions; enforcement authority and Supreme Court representation can operate together.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns this affirmative FECA enforcement petition. It does not deny the Commission the express powers supplied for funding litigation, construe every agency's litigation statute, or eliminate section 518(a)'s particular-case direction exception.

##### Authorization after the statutory petition period does not ratify this filing

**Holding:** Even assuming that the Solicitor General's May 26, 1994 authorization fully adopted the Commission's January 18 petition, it cannot relate back to validate this unauthorized invocation after the period in which an authorized petition could be filed had expired. Section 2101(c) allows ninety days and permits a Justice, for good cause, to extend the application period by no more than sixty days; the asserted authorization falls outside even that maximum under either supplied judgment date.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: six of eight participating Justices. Ginsburg did not participate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cook v. Tullis, 85 U.S. (18 Wall.) 332 (1873): applies the requirement that a ratifying principal have capacity both at the original act and at ratification, with intervening rights preserved; relation back does not supply capacity after this statutory petition period.
- Dodge v. United States, 272 U.S. 530 (1926): distinguishes adoption of an unauthorized seizure where the forfeiture prerequisites were satisfied from authorization of appellate review after the statutory filing period.
- Smith v. Barry, 502 U.S. 244 (1992): distinguishes a timely document that functionally supplies required appellate notice from a filing lacking governmental representation authority; the functional-notice holding remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no universal rule that all representation defects are incurable. It makes no finding that a Justice granted an extension or that the amended lower opinion restarted time. The Solicitor General's authorization is distinct from any Commission resolution addressing its composition; neither constitutional validity nor structural ratification is reached.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The writ is dismissed for want of jurisdiction. The D.C. Circuit judgment remains in place without Supreme Court endorsement of its constitutional merits. No ruling is made on congressional membership, structural severability or ratification, contribution liability or renewed enforcement.

#### Ryder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-431.

##### A timely direct appointment challenger receives lawful adjudication

**Holding:** A litigant who timely challenges a substantial constitutional defect in the appointments of officers adjudicating his own case and pursues that objection on direct review is entitled to a fresh adjudication by lawfully appointed officers; the de facto officer doctrine cannot validate the defective panel’s judgment against him. Ryder must receive new Article 66 review by a properly appointed military appellate panel, without automatic dismissal or reversal of his trial conviction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; nine Justices adopt this bounded rule and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns a timely direct challenge to substantial constitutional appointment defects. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckley v. Valeo is distinguished as a universal de facto remedial defense. Glidden Co. v. United States and Ex parte Ward are distinguished by collateral or technical-assignment settings. The completed Weiss decision retains its commissioned-officer/germane-assignment scope and leaves civilian appointments open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for fresh Article 66 review by a lawfully appointed military appellate panel. No acquittal, dismissal, trial-conviction reversal or decision of the underlying drug, evidence or sentence merits is ordered.
