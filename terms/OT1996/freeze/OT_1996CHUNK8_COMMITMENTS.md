## 84. Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board; Price v. Bossier Parish School Board

**Event and reach.** Consolidated direct appeals, Nos. 95-1455 and 95-1508; argued December 9, 1996; May 12, 1997 consideration. Both appeals concern the same preclearance judgment. No threshold prevents review. Section 2 liability was not adjudicated below. The stipulated absence of retrogression is an effects premise, not a finding that the Board acted without an unlawful purpose.

### Components and competing lawful grounds

**B1 — Section 2 and the effects condition.** The nonincorporation path applies *Beer* and *Lockhart*: §5 compares the proposed change with the operative benchmark, whereas §2 independently asks whether the resulting electoral arrangement affords equal opportunity. *De Grandy* preserves totality and rejects maximization. The strongest incorporation argument is that §5's language protects against racial denial or abridgment and Congress cannot be supposed to authorize through preclearance a change violating the Act's substantive guarantee. Its answer is that clearance is permission to implement a change under a limited inquiry, not immunity from §2; a separate §2 action remains available. Conversely, the strongest answer to nonincorporation is that keeping different standards does not itself establish that a proved §2 violation is irrelevant to whether the change may be approved. The incorporation position therefore concerns a **proved** §2 violation, not the mere feasibility of two additional majority-Black districts. Neither position supports this Court's first-instance determination of §2 liability.

**B2 — Purpose evidence and ultimate clearance.** Impact, sequence, departures, statements, rejected alternatives and pretext can bear on the intent with which a change was selected. *Arlington Heights* supplies that evidentiary inquiry; *Pleasant Grove* and *Richmond* prevent nonretrogressive effect from doing the entire work of purpose review. The remand path vacates ultimate clearance because a categorical exclusion of relevant evidence or uncertainty whether the factfinder applied that exclusion must be corrected. Its strongest objection is that if intentional dilution itself is outside the statutory prohibition, a §2-type comparison cannot establish prohibited purpose merely by being renamed evidence. The remand ground answers that an evidentiary inference and the substantive ultimate question are distinct; the factfinder must assess the evidence under a valid purpose rule, not presume unlawful intent from additional feasible districts. No present finding of racial purpose follows.

The opposing path sustains clearance where nonretrogression is established and the proffered dilution theory does not concern denial of the individual vote or prohibited backward movement. Its strongest objection is that it narrows statutory purpose before the lower court has evaluated the alleged sequence, departures and explanations, despite the statute's separate purpose language. The Justice-specific limits below identify the disagreement rather than treating an effects stipulation as an automatic intent finding.

**B3 — Scope of prohibited purpose.** A broader rule would deny clearance for intentional racial vote dilution even without intended retrogression. A narrower disposition can correct the exclusion of probative evidence while reserving the ultimate breadth of §5's purpose condition. The former follows the unqualified racial-purpose language and the separate work performed by purpose; its difficulty is defining that reach without importing every §2 results violation. The latter respects the need for factual findings but must not instruct the lower court to disregard discriminatory evidence under a presupposed retrogression-only rule. The commitments distinguish those choices explicitly.

### O — The contemporaneous numerical-pager search

**Accepted ground available.** A lawful custodial arrest permits a contemporaneous search of the person and immediately associated personal effects under *Robinson*. On this record, immediate retrieval of the stored numerical messages from the pager taken from Ortiz's person falls within that authority. The information is protected and retrieval is a search: neither lawful hardware possession nor a no-search label supplies the result. The categorical incident authority, rather than proof of impending overwrite in this particular pager, justifies this search.

**Strongest opposing path.** *Chimel*, *Chadwick*, *Pinson* and *Wellons* support distinguishing seizure from examination of concealed contents once officers have secure possession. Electronic information cannot itself injure an officer, and the record does not demonstrate an imminent incoming page, this pager's capacity or its inability to preserve data without reading. A warrant-required path would vacate the suppression ground and remand for preserved alternative justifications and harmlessness. Its central obstacle is that *Robinson* authorizes contents searches of objects on the person without a separate danger finding, while *Chadwick* concerns property not immediately associated with the person and a search remote from arrest. Treating every secured personal effect as outside *Robinson* would change that doctrine, not merely apply *Chadwick*. The bounded ruling below concerns this immediately searched numerical pager; it does not announce a rule for every electronic device, delayed data retrieval or remotely stored information.

**Exigency separated.** General propositions about some pagers do not establish an independent particularized destruction exigency here. The immediate incident ground is sufficient without adopting the Seventh Circuit's broader statement that every pager must immediately be searched to prevent destruction. *Richards* rejects an unsupported category in a different warrant-execution setting but expressly leaves justified categories in other settings intact. *Chandler* similarly does not replace *Robinson* with a universal case-specific necessity rule.

### H — Hurtado's most analogous contempt guideline

Sections 2J1.1 and 2X5.1 require the **most analogous** guideline, not automatic use of obstruction whenever refusal impedes a prosecution. Under the authoritative §2J1.1 commentary, nature, circumstances, effect on administration and vindication of judicial authority inform the choice; obstruction is analogous in some cases. *Stinson* makes that consistent explanation authoritative. *Koon* independently corrects interpretation errors and unsupported grounds while respecting supported factual findings and sentencing judgments in their proper domains.

**Obstruction path and objection.** Knowing refusal after immunity and a direct judicial order can obstruct a prosecution even if the defendant is ultimately convicted and the witness's motive is to avoid being an informer. Actual success and a sole purpose to procure acquittal are not requirements of every obstruction analogy. The strongest objection is that an unparticularized inference from every refusal bypasses the commanded comparison, and this record lacks support for the district court's additional characterization of an effort to help Ortiz escape punishment. Nor may the court treat §2X3.1's title as proof that Hurtado was an actual statutory accessory.

**Material-witness path and objection.** A refusal to supply testimony, without additional acts designed to aid the accused and against a record of earlier and limited present assistance to the Government, closely resembles failure of a material witness to appear. The strongest objection is that presence plus defiance after immunity may have a different obstructive character from nonappearance, and guideline analogy does not require proof of every element of the comparison offense. The lower court's sentence comparisons cannot replace the required analysis or compel the gentler range merely because it seems fairer.

**Two lawful remedial positions.** One position affirms the definite direction to use §2J1.5 on the supported record. The other preserves vacatur of the existing 37-month sentence but removes the categorical appellate direction: remand requires a properly supported comparison of the guidelines, without a requirement of acquittal or a special motive beyond what the applicable guideline and analogy actually demand. It permits obstruction if justified by the actual conduct and commentary; it does not predetermine a replacement guideline, adjustment or sentence.

## 86. Reyes-Hernandez v. INS

**Event and reach.** Review of the Seventh Circuit judgment denying the petition in No. 95-3677, 89 F.3d 490. The authorized merits hearing/submission and consideration are May 12, 1997; timely filing and grant scheduling are express neutral stipulations. The October 1995 BIA order is the operative administrative final order. No new agency election, reopening disposition, habeas filing, stay or execution event is assumed.

### R1 — Commenced IIRIRA §242(g)

All eight apply *Yang*'s controlling April 1, 1997 commencement rule and recognize that §242(g) is now operative. Pub. L. 104-302 §2 corrects §306(c)(1) to apply the general amendments as provided under §309, **except** that §242(g) reaches claims arising from past, pending and future proceedings. This is real cross-temporal reach, not a basis for treating the entire permanent review system as applicable. Corrected §309(c)(1) saves existing proceedings and their review subject to specific exceptions. Section 309(c)(4)'s administrative-final-order trigger is absent: the 1995 order was not entered more than thirty days after September 30, 1996. Section 306(c)(2) protects existing §106 judgments from invalidation or required reconsideration by paragraph (1), but creates no new appellate jurisdiction.

**Narrow construction.** Section 242(g)'s three objects—decisions or actions to commence proceedings, adjudicate cases, or execute removal orders—protect those identified executive choices, rather than every legal issue generated by a removal proceeding. A direct challenge to the legality of the completed adjudication under the saved review system is not automatically a challenge to the executive choice to adjudicate. This construction gives §242(g) work across grandfathered proceedings without erasing the detailed saving and transitional rules; the opening exception for review provided in §242 retains its channeling function within the new system. Former §106 review is not falsely declared to be textually “provided in” new §242; it survives through §309 only for claims outside §242(g)'s specified withdrawal. Here Reyes challenges temporal eligibility, the legality of the actual discretionary reasons and treatment of a supplemental brief, not the selection of him for proceedings or a discretionary decision when to execute an order.

**Broader construction and strongest objection.** “Any cause or claim” and “notwithstanding any other provision of law” can support withdrawal of every challenge arising from an adjudicative decision, with only new §242's own review preserved. Its strongest difficulty is that this reading would make the listed executive acts practically coextensive with the whole removal process and displace saved judicial review far beyond the particular exceptions Congress wrote. Conversely, the strongest objection to the narrow reading is that denial of a waiver is an action adjudicating a case and that a surviving old-law review route is not among §242(g)'s express exceptions. The narrow answer rests on the statute's enumeration, the difference between action **to** adjudicate and review of an adjudicated legal result, and the transitional scheme as a whole. No rule is announced that every discretionary action is reviewable or every constitutional claim necessarily survives.

**Commitment:** All eight accept the narrow construction at the stated scope and find §242(g) no bar to these claims. This is a new construction, not something *Yang* already decided. Their common *Yang* join supplies the enacted-timing and provision-separation method; their common *Felker* join requires identification of the particular withdrawn and surviving channels. They do not rely on an assumed adequate §2241 alternative or on §1651 to create jurisdiction. The broader construction would produce a threshold dismissal, not a merits denial; it is rejected here for the textual and structural reasons given.

### R2 — AEDPA §440(a), R3 — §440(d), and R4 — the colorable defense

Section 440(a)'s present restriction on review and §440(d)'s restriction on eligibility are examined independently. Section 440(f) directs the reach of §440(e) and supplies no explicit temporal instruction for either challenged subsection. IIRIRA §306(d)'s dual-offense technical correction supplies no additional conviction finding.

**Preserved-review path.** Reyes completed a litigation choice before enactment: he surrendered a colorable deportability defense while pursuing a discretionary waiver and the associated ordinary review. For §440(d), removing eligibility would change the legal options attached to that completed surrender, even though an eligible person never had an entitlement to mercy. For §440(a), the claimed new consequence is separate: eliminating the then-available opportunity to obtain judicial correction of an unlawful denial after the defense was surrendered. This is not merely a litigant's general hope that the current jurisdiction will persist. *Landgraf* requires examination of that concrete change and does not let the jurisdictional label decide it. Neither subsection clearly directs that consequence for this completed concession. The narrow conclusion preserves the original claim and review; it does not decide all preenactment convictions, contested deportability findings, future reopening requests or every reliance theory.

**Withdrawn-review path.** Section 440(a) addresses a court's present authority to review a covered final order. Present adjudication is the relevant activity; reliance on possible review does not fix federal jurisdiction permanently. Under this approach, even a strong temporal objection to eliminating eligibility under §440(d) does not reopen a review route Congress removed. Its strongest objection is *Landgraf*'s rejection of labels and the supported surrender of an actual option, as opposed to mere pendency. The response is provision-specific: the underlying legal status of the concession and eligibility need not change merely because the forum for further ordinary review changes. *Felker* supports respecting a specified withdrawal but does not itself decide these pending immigration proceedings or establish the adequacy of a hypothetical habeas remedy.

**Colorable defense.** The Government did not dispute the predicate used by the Seventh Circuit. No particular defense is invented, and no new factfinding is needed merely because the published opinion does not name it. The preserved-review rationale depends on surrender of a genuine legal option: an utterly insubstantial defense would not establish that particular lost-option premise. This is not a universal requirement of subjective reliance, a rule that only pleas or concessions can invoke *Landgraf*, or a decision that all persons without a colorable defense receive the new law. Other possible retroactivity predicates are reserved.

### R5 — Waiver discretion and R6 — supplemental evidence, if reached

*Yang* unanimously separates statutory eligibility from individualized discretion and requires actual, reasoned administration without pretext, disregard of binding rules or unexplained departures from settled policy. Here the agency considered the favorable rehabilitation and family hardship against serious, recent and repeated drug offenses. The BIA could adopt the immigration judge's adequate reasons without writing an independent extended balance. No actual policy reversal, irrational factor or legally forbidden presumption is established. Stronger favorable equities do not authorize this Court to reweigh the record.

The supplemental brief did not compel original agency appellate factfinding. The ordinary motion-to-supplement, remand or reopening route distinguishes adjudicating the record from receiving new material. *Abudu* supplies the relevant predivergence reopening framework; *Doherty*'s fractured result supplies no controlling general rationale. The strongest contrary argument is that four years of developments could materially change the equitable balance and an appellate agency should not disregard them solely for a label. But the agency did not accept the evidence and then overlook it: it required the known procedural route, indicated reopening remained possible, and was not obliged on this record to transform the brief into a different motion. No later motion's timeliness, eligibility, success or disposition is adjudicated.

## 88. Edmond v. United States and the five Rule 12.4 petitioners

**Event and reach.** No. 96-262, encompassing Edmond, Lazenby, Leaver, Leonard, Nichols and Venable; argued February 24, 1997; consideration May 19, 1997. The challenge concerns the Secretary's actual appointments, not an assumption that the General Counsel's earlier assignments became valid by continued service.

### E1 — Statutory appointment authority

Section 323(a) expressly permits the Secretary to appoint departmental officers and employees and prescribe duties and powers. Article 66 expressly permits civilian appellate military judges and creates the appellate judicial structure. Read together, these provisions authorize these appointments; an additional statute repeating the office's name in an appointment clause is unnecessary. Assignment of an existing judge to a panel is nevertheless distinct from appointment to an office. *Weiss*'s germane-duty rule for already commissioned officers does not itself validate a civilian appointment.

The contrary statutory path would require more office-specific authorization, reading §323 as general personnel administration rather than a grant reaching adjudicative offices. Its strongest argument is constitutional caution against allowing a department head to create offices through a general personnel provision. The accepted ground answers that the office and civilian service are already supplied by the UCMJ, while §323 contains genuine appointment language; no power to create an unrelated office or appoint a principal officer is inferred. Constitutional classification remains independently necessary.

### E2 — Inferior-officer classification and E3 — remedy

The accepted classification rests on the **combined** statutory structure: direction over the appellate organization and rules; assignment and removal from judicial assignments by the Judge Advocate General; superior review by presidentially appointed, Senate-confirmed judges; and the absence of final authority free from that supervisory system. Decisional independence within assigned adjudication does not itself create a principal office. Article 66's important fact, credibility and sentence functions remain real. Article 67 reaches matters of law, with review obligatory in specified classes and otherwise requiring the accused's petition and good cause; it is not guaranteed plenary factual rehearing. Those limitations do not erase the combined hierarchical relationship.

The principal-officer path emphasizes the importance and substantial independence of Article 66 judgment, its factfinding authority, and the limits on Article 67 review. Its strongest objection to the accepted path is that merely placing an officer somewhere below another cannot determine constitutional rank, particularly when superior judges cannot reweigh the same facts. The accepted path therefore does not rely on importance, appellate review, removal or assignment alone; *Morrison* and *Freytag* support attention to the actual office, and *Weiss/Ryder* preserve the structure rather than a blanket military exception. The Court need not declare supervision alone sufficient for every office or necessary in exactly this form for every inferior officer. Constitutional independence and inferior status can coexist.

If either E1 or E2 failed, *Ryder* would require fresh Article 66 review for a qualifying timely substantial defect, not acquittal, automatic trial-conviction reversal or an assertion that Article 67 supplied the lost fact review. Under the accepted E1/E2 conclusions, no such defect exists and the six judgments are affirmed within the granted questions.

## 89. Edwards v. Balisok

**Event and reach.** No. 95-1352; argued November 13, 1996; consideration May 19, 1997. The claim concerns an in-custody disciplinary deprivation of thirty days' earned good-time credit. No record fact establishes a claimant outside *Heck*'s decided custody/opportunity category. The allegations of misconduct are not findings. The Ninth Circuit's categorical rule allowing all procedural claims is the legal error reviewed.

### D1 — Necessary implication, D2 — independent procedure claims, D3 — disposition and D4 — prospective relief

**Invalidity-dependent ground.** Success on the claim that the decisionmaker deliberately concealed exculpatory evidence and acted dishonestly or with disabling bias would establish that the disciplinary adjudication supporting the credit loss was invalid. Calling the injury procedural or declining to request restoration does not avoid that necessary implication. *Heck*'s operative rule therefore requires qualifying invalidation before damages or retrospective declaratory relief on that theory. *Preiser* separately places restoration in habeas. *Sandin* and *Young v. Harper* confirm meaningful liberty and process, not an unrestricted civil channel for an invalidity-dependent remedy.

**Strongest contrary path.** *Wolff* preserves civil relief for compatible procedural injuries and *Carey* permits nominal damages for process denied even if the substantive result would have been the same. The strongest argument for allowing this claim is that a fair rehearing could reach the same misconduct finding, so the prisoner seeks process rather than innocence. The response is that *Heck* asks whether the existing adjudication must be invalid, not whether every future lawful adjudication must exonerate him. Disabling bias and deliberate suppression undermine the adjudication itself. Conversely, the State's request to dismiss every pleaded defect fails if the claim does not necessarily establish invalidity. An inadequate statement of supporting facts, a particular refusal of questions or another independent procedural omission cannot be classified solely by its label; the actual theory, applicable process and relation to the basis of discipline determine implication. An omitted statement might permit a compatible procedural injury even though a bias claim does not. No automatic harmlessness or procedural entitlement to every requested question is announced.

**Required distinction.** The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's categorical rule and remands. The deceit/bias/concealment theory is subject to dismissal without prejudice absent qualifying invalidation. Any independently pleaded statement-of-reasons, questioning or other theory must be examined separately; the common ground does not hold that all of them necessarily fall within *Heck*, or that each necessarily states a due-process claim. On a genuinely separable failure-to-state-supporting-facts theory, success need not establish that the loss of credits was itself unlawful, so blanket dismissal is impermissible. Ordinary pleading, liability, personal responsibility, immunity and remedial requirements remain.

**Disposition/accrual.** Qualifying invalidation means reversal on direct review, executive expungement, authorized state invalidation or a federal writ calling the custody judgment into question. For a claim within the prerequisite, accrual awaits that event and dismissal is without prejudice. Failed review, exhaustion or expiration of an opportunity is not favorable invalidation. The Court does not impose a judicial state-remedy exhaustion rule or order a stay to obtain one; *Patsy* remains operative. Independently applicable statutory administrative exhaustion is a different issue and is not decided. Former prisoners and those without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain reserved rather than supplied as invented facts here.

**Prospective relief.** A future date-stamping practice ordinarily need not invalidate the completed disciplinary judgment and is not automatically barred by *Heck*. The request was not adjudicated below. Remand must consider live standing, equitable predicates and the actual claim; no injunction issues here. A prospective label alone cannot save a declaration whose substance invalidates the past adjudication.

## 1. Reach, operative statute, and boundaries

The camp challenges its own property-tax treatment after timely state refund and prospective-exemption proceedings. Review of the Maine Supreme Judicial Court's final judgment sustaining that treatment is the supplied channel. All eight Associates participate; no threshold obstacle or case-specific exclusion is established. The commitments reach the granted Commerce Clause question and lawful consequences of its resolution. They do not decide an independent equal-protection, religious-liberty, or right-to-travel claim.

The general exemption concerns real and personal property owned and occupied or used solely for the purposes of benevolent and charitable institutions incorporated by Maine. Its protection against loss of exemption because of funding sources or beneficiary-class limitations must be read with the express nonresident subparagraph. For an institution actually operated principally for nonresidents, the subparagraph permits exemption of **no more than $50,000 of current just property value only when the statutory average weekly charge per person does not exceed $30**; above that computed rate it permits no exemption. The $50,000 is exempt property value, not tax dollars. The rate is total tax-year stipends or charges divided by total persons receiving the services, benefits or advantages, then divided by the number of weeks during that tax year when the institution is actually operated principally for nonresidents; the tax year is defined by section 502. An advertised weekly price is not a substitute for this calculation when the calculation is disputed.

The subparagraph does not apply to a nonprofit corporation incorporated solely to conduct medical research. That exception removes this subparagraph's restriction, not the general exemption's other requirements. The quoted paragraph also includes the listed nonprofit nursing, boarding, boarding-care, community mental-health and child-care facilities with their stated licensing or incorporation conditions, and defines nonprofit by reference to Internal Revenue Code section 501(c)(3). This camp is not found to satisfy the medical-research exception. Neither incorporation nor nonprofit status alone establishes every requirement for exemption.

The reported roughly $400 weekly charge, approximately 95 percent nonresident clientele, property use, national recruitment and tax treatment remain supplied facts. No new statutory average, tax-year tally, exact refund, or entitlement to the capped exemption is calculated here. The question is the validity of the residence condition used to deny the camp equal tax treatment, not whether all other exemption conditions are satisfied. ### A. Actual comparability under Tracy

**Path A1 — comparable private charitable service:** The same otherwise qualifying camp activity receives different property-tax treatment according to the residence of the people it serves. National recruitment and interstate attendance identify the affected service transactions. The statute adds no compulsory rate, reliability, universal-service or other corresponding obligation to the favored institution's offering. Relief of local needs may be a legitimate public objective, but it does not itself establish an actual difference between otherwise comparable private services. Tracy therefore requires examination, followed here by recognition of comparability; it does not bar that conclusion.

**Strongest objection:** A resident-serving charity contributes a local public benefit that the government might otherwise have to provide, whereas service to nonresidents does not discharge that community responsibility. Different beneficiaries could thus identify different public-service relationships, even without utility-style regulation.

**Path A2 — materially different local public service:** If the favored activity actually substitutes for an identified governmental service or entails material service duties, Tracy permits attention to that difference before finding discrimination. Its strongest difficulty here is that the supplied statute and record identify beneficiary residence, not an established difference of that kind. Describing all resident charity as local public assistance would make the challenged residence criterion establish its own exemption from review. Utility obligations are not the sole possible relevant distinction; the deficiency is the absence of a supported material distinction in this application, not the absence of a utility label.

**Bounded remand alternative:** An unresolved fact about the compared services could warrant a remand rather than merits invalidation. A remand is not warranted merely because the parties dispute the legal significance of residence. No particular unresolved service fact capable of changing this comparison is established in the supplied record. This conclusion does not treat an unretrieved document as proof of litigant default and creates no new evidentiary burden.

### B. Interstate commerce and nonprofit activity

**Path B1 — coverage of these transactions:** A camp that recruits nationally and supplies paid services to visitors crossing state lines participates in interstate commerce despite its charitable organization and the local consumption of its services. Heart of Atlanta supplies the travel-and-services principle; the practical service-market analysis in Carbone and Tracy is consistent with examining the transaction rather than institutional labels.

**Strongest objection:** Property located in Maine and charitable services performed there could be characterized as local nonprofit activity, with interstate travel only an incidental consequence.

**Path B2 — distinct noncommercial/public activity outside this comparison:** An actual direct public-service program or materially different noncommercial arrangement might raise a different question. It does not describe the supplied nationally marketed, paid camp services. B1 does not imply that every charitable act or every donation is commerce, or decide all governmental benefits under the Commerce Clause. ### C. Facial tax distinction, justification and compensation

**Path C1 — discriminatory condition requiring justification:** For otherwise comparable camp services, the beneficiary-residence condition disadvantages the institution's interstate clientele and makes changing the geographic mix of customers a route to more favorable tax treatment. A local taxpayer and local property do not erase the classification, just as local shareholder residence did not erase Fulton's geographic tax deduction. This conclusion concerns the condition's operation, not an invented finding that a particular rival lost a customer.

**Strongest objection:** All institutions are Maine institutions; the State can reserve public assistance to its own community, and the exemption benefits the residents rather than shielding local producers from outside producers.

**Path C2 — local public assistance rather than discriminatory taxation:** A genuine government benefit or purchased service directed to residents may be analyzed differently. The present measure instead changes the tax on a private provider according to the residence of its customers. If comparability is established, preserving state resources or supporting local charity does not alone establish that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives are inadequate. Neutral charitable-property rules or lawful direct assistance remain analytical alternatives, not ordered replacements. The low-charge cap and sole-purpose medical-research exception do not make the camp's residence condition neutral; neither must be declared independently unconstitutional to resolve this application.

Compensation is separate from public assistance and service comparison. The tax cannot be justified merely by residents' general contributions or an unquantified assertion that their charities relieve state expenditures. Fulton and Oregon Waste require an identified intrastate burden, a roughly equivalent and nonexcessive charge, and substantially equivalent taxable events. No such matching tax is established. The analysis does not assert a numerical disproportionality that has not been calculated.

**Path E1 — reversal of the federal validation and remand for lawful relief:** Once the residence condition fails the governing Commerce Clause rules in this application, the state judgment sustaining it cannot stand. ## 90. United States v. Thomas — May 19, 1997

### Components and supported disposition paths

**T1 — offense-date §1465 coverage.** The challenged acts are knowing interstate transportation for sale or distribution of the charged obscene pictures/images through the controlled bulletin-board service. **T2 — receiving-community obscenity standard.** Miller and Hamling permit the receiving community's standards for prurient appeal and patent offensiveness in this knowing, controlled commercial distribution. ## 94. United States v. Hyde — May 27, 1997

### Components, alternatives and common operative position

**H1 — accepted plea and deferred agreement.** After the district court has accepted a guilty plea following the required Rule 11 inquiry, former Rule 32(e)'s fair-and-just-reason requirement governs the defendant's presentence withdrawal motion even when the court has deferred acceptance of the associated plea agreement. The Government's path gives the accepted plea its separate legal effect. Its strongest objection is that the defendant is bound while the court remains free to reject the inducing agreement. ## The statutory fork and the surviving limits

### Path A — the legally applicable enhanced maximum governs

For a defendant in §994(h)'s stated categories, the maximum term authorized means the legally applicable statutory maximum, including an invoked recidivist enhancement. Where the statute permits thirty years following the required enhancement procedure, twenty years is not that defendant's maximum authorized term. The instruction is directed to the Commission, but remains an instruction it must carry out. Amendment 506's categorical exclusion of the increase conflicts with that direction and is not authoritative under Stinson.

The strongest counterargument is textual and institutional, not simply a plea for lower sentences. Congress referred to categories, placed implementation with the Commission and allowed sentences near the maximum; those choices afford discretion to develop a uniform category benchmark. Path A answers that the enumerated categories establish who receives the near-maximum treatment, while the enacted penalty law establishes which maximum is legally authorized. Classification discretion cannot make an applicable enhanced ceiling disappear. The statute's flexibility about proximity, adjustments and implementation does not transfer the separate power to select a ceiling that the penalty law makes inapplicable as the maximum for the affected offender.

The Commission's concern about prosecutorial variation has force as policy. It does not make every difference created by the statutory enhancement procedure unwarranted in law. Congress left prosecutors a choice whether to invoke the enhancement, and this construction leaves that choice in place: without a lawfully applicable enhancement, the enhanced maximum does not become the benchmark merely because a prosecutor could have sought it. The actual notices are established here. No constitutional approval of every charging decision, categorical immunity from selective-prosecution review, or prosecutorial obligation to enhance follows.

Stinson supplies deference to permissible commentary and expressly withholds it when commentary violates a statute. Neal keeps a statutory sentencing command effective despite a different guideline calculation, while preserving lawful Commission discretion within its domain. MCI requires attention to the particular obligation and the scope of delegated adjustment rather than invoking general policy to remove that obligation. Auer concerns a permissible own-regulation interpretation and gives the Commission no authority to alter Congress's command. Gonzales likewise gives effect to the specific sentencing command while preserving lawful discretion on distinct matters. None of these decisions alone supplies the meaning of §994(h); the textual relation between categories, maximum and near performs that work here.

### Path B — Congress permitted the Commission's categorical benchmark

Section 994(h) speaks to the Commission's construction of guidelines for categories, not a direct command that each defendant receive his personal statutory maximum. Read with the Commission's assigned task of producing consistent sentencing ranges, the provision permits using the ordinary offense maximum as a common benchmark for career offenders while leaving every actual statutory floor and ceiling intact. Amendment 506 continues to produce a substantial career-offender increase; it does not erase the career-offender category or forbid sentences within otherwise available enhanced statutory limits. Its uniformity rationale therefore implements, rather than replaces, the statutory instruction on this reading.

The strongest objection is that a benchmark excluding a legally applicable increase is no longer the maximum term authorized, and that general consistency policy cannot override a specific near-maximum command. Path B's answer rests on the actual words categories and at or near and the Commission-directed structure: the disputed statutory referent is a categorical guideline benchmark, not necessarily each individual's enhanced ceiling. This is a construction of the instruction before deference, not a claim that deference excuses a contradiction already established. The statutory enhancements remain ceilings and, where applicable, floors; a guideline policy can operate inside them without guaranteeing that charging choices independently determine the career-offender table's benchmark.

On this reading, Stinson's permission for authoritative commentary matters because Amendment 506 is within the statute's interpretive space. Neal and MCI remain binding when a clear statutory command is actually displaced. Neal's simultaneous preservation of a distinct lawful guideline measure illustrates why a statutory ceiling need not answer every guideline-calculation question by itself; it does not establish a freestanding power to disregard §994(h). Auer similarly preserves consistency-limited interpretive authority rather than deciding this statute. This path does not depend on a free-floating judicial preference for lenity, proportionality or Commission expertise.

### The independent proximity question

Selecting the benchmark and testing sufficient proximity are distinct issues. The lower majority and dissent supply different arguments concerning both. No Justice treats the words at or near as requiring imposition of the maximum in every case, excluding every acceptance-of-responsibility adjustment or foreclosing an otherwise authorized departure. Conversely, substantial punishment is not necessarily the same instruction as punishment at or near the designated maximum.

The Path A Associates conclude that measuring the career-offender table against a categorically reduced benchmark is itself contrary to §994(h). They do not add a numerical minimum percentage for near or independently invalidate every sentence that falls below one. Some coincidentally overlapping or substantial sentences cannot validate the commentary's express wrong-benchmark direction. They do not approve or disapprove every unchanged adjustment, departure or other guideline rule not before the Court. This leaves genuine implementation discretion while resolving the presented exclusion.

The Path B Associates also address the Government's independent argument that even the unenhanced benchmark yields insufficiently near sentences. They reject a comparison based solely on these respondents' final adjusted sentences. A career-offender table is one stage in the sentencing structure, and the scheme retains authorized adjustments and statutory limits. The lower opinion's calculations give substantive support for treating the ranges as near the unenhanced ceiling; they do not require a universal judicial percentage formula. In the present record, the asserted proximity objection supplies no independent reason to invalidate Amendment 506 under Path B. These Associates do not hold every possible later table or distant sentence permissible merely because the Commission labels it near.
