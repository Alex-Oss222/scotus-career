## Proposed public formulation

The judgment of the Eleventh Circuit on the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the following rule. The claim does not seek a new rule under Teague for Lambrix's November 24, 1986 finality: the preexisting federal requirement of meaningful capital guidance applies to the jury recommendation's legally consequential contribution established by the prefinality Florida authorities. The State's independent judicial sentencing function remains intact, but does not make the required jury contribution legally nonexistent. This ruling neither finds the instructions constitutionally deficient on their merits nor determines harmful transmission or the adequacy of a cure.

On remand, the lower court must first resolve the genuinely preserved and unresolved state procedural-default defense, including its independence, adequacy when compliance was required, preservation and any applicable cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice exception. If the claim is barred and unexcused, relief is denied on that ground. If it is available, the court proceeds to the actual merits, effect and lawful correction under the existing Espinosa/Stringer rules, together with any genuinely applicable, unresolved statutory condition. The Teague new-rule ground may not again be used to reject this particular claim. No present writ, automatic evidentiary hearing, new penalty proceeding, life sentence, release, stay or execution authorization is ordered; unrelated guilt and other claims are untouched.

The decision to reach Teague in this Court is distinct from the order of the remaining tasks on remand. A Justice may maintain a dissent from reaching Teague now while expressly joining the alternative old-rule proposition and the remand subject to it. Publication must preserve that scope; it may not relabel such a Justice as agreeing to the initial sequence or as casting a formal reversal vote.
