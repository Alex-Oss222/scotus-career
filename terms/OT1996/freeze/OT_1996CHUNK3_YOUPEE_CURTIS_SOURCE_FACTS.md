## Youpee

Full dated lower-court primary opinion text recovered from the Caselaw Access Project: [67 F.3d 194 (1995)](https://static.case.law/f3d/67/cases/0194-01.json), saved as [lower opinion](sources/CH3A_Youpee_lower_lower_opinion.txt). Cross-check text: [OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/67/f3d/194/youpee-v-babbitt). This is the September 5, 1995 Ninth Circuit opinion, not a Supreme Court opinion.

The opinion reports William Youpee's October 19, 1990 death, a 1981 will, Bureau of Indian Affairs certifications of interests of 2% or less, devisees without existing shares in the affected allotments, no evidence rebutting the escheat presumption, and an April 1992 administrative partial-distribution decree. The administrative appeals board declined the constitutional challenge. The District Court granted declaratory and injunctive relief; the Ninth Circuit affirmed. Government conceded the children's standing in that appeal. The opinion values the fractional interests at $1,239. Those propositions are reported court facts or litigation positions, not newly authenticated underlying probate documents.

The opinion's footnote 2 quotes all three subsections of §2206. The [official 1994 Code text and amendment notes](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title25/html/USCODE-1994-title25-chap24-sec2206.htm) have been saved [verbatim by text extraction](sources/CH3A_Youpee_2206_1994_exact.txt). Subsection (a) contains both a prospective capacity-to-earn test and a rebuttable presumption based on prior earnings. Subsection (b) permits devise to another owner of an undivided interest in the same parcel. Subsection (c) authorizes Secretary-approved tribal codes taking precedence over (a), subject to the stated condition concerning further descent or fractionation. The official amendment notes identify a November 29, 1990 change to the opening ownership/jurisdiction wording; Youpee died before that change. The 1984 income, devise and tribal-code provisions predate the death. The fresh validator must preserve this temporal distinction if quoting the death-date statute, rather than labeling every word of the 1994 text as already operative on October 19.

No underlying probate file or standalone tribal-code instrument was recovered. The initial packet's propositions about each devised interest going to a single descendant and absence of an operative tribal code require the validator to identify their authorized sanitized factual support, or retain a specific source limitation. This extractor does not strengthen them through a Supreme Court source.

## Curtis

Full July 17, 1995 Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court primary opinion text recovered from CAP: [420 Mass. 749](https://static.case.law/mass/420/cases/0749-01.json), saved as [lower opinion](sources/CH3A_Curtis_lower_lower_opinion.txt). Direct masscases.com retrieval returned 403. The lower opinion contains the superintendent-memorandum quotation, summary-judgment posture, legal positions and footnotes; no separate memorandum was recovered.

The quoted January 2, 1992 memorandum provides free junior-high condoms through the nurse, with counseling before receipt and pamphlets. At high school, condoms are available free through the nurse or for 75 cents from restroom vending machines; counseling is provided to students requesting it. The opinion separately characterizes participation as voluntary, without required collection of condoms, classroom attendance concerning the program, reading, or disciplinary consequences for abstaining. These statements must remain distinct: voluntary participation does not erase the quoted junior-high sequence. There is no parental opt-out or notification provision. The memorandum directs staff to reserve personal opinions and presentation to emphasize abstinence as the only certain prevention of sexually transmitted disease.

Plaintiffs sought declaratory and injunctive relief under §1983 and Massachusetts civil-rights law; the challenged judgment is summary judgment for defendants. The federal parental-liberty/familial-privacy and free-exercise claims were presented. Footnote 1 lists waived state statutory claims concerning accomplice liability for statutory rape, reporting abuse, and moral education. Footnote 2 declines state constitutional theories not put to the trial judge. A Massachusetts medical-consent claim is not among that enumerated waived list; discussion of medical consent appears in the comparison to a New York lower decision. The original neutral packet's generic 'state-law medical-consent and moral-education claims were waived' should therefore be verified or corrected by the fresh validator.

The lower court reports expert affidavits concerning the policy's potential negative consequences, and treats those as outside its threshold constitutional inquiry. This is the lower court's assessment of materiality, not a factual finding that the program has no adverse effects. Its judgment affirmed the summary judgment and declined to proceed to government-interest balancing. The court's reasoning is a judgment under review, not controlling Supreme Court law.
