## Clarified commitment

The last sentence of B2 rejects a chapterwide inference from the failure of the programwide theory. It does **not** preserve a freestanding individual entitlement to generally adequate staffing or computerized administration as such. The original commitments distinguish an agency's administrative duties from an individual's enforceable statutory entitlement, and B6 already rejects any automatic order to redesign staffing or accounting systems.

All eight Associate Justices retain the judgment commitment to vacate and remand. They reject the asserted individual right to programwide substantial compliance, including an attempt to restate that same demand as a general right to sufficient agency personnel or satisfactory computer administration. The statutory organization and management requirements examined below do not identify such a personal entitlement. Neither the benefit of sound administration to families nor the mandatory character of an administrative duty supplies the missing right. This is a conclusion about the asserted administrative-as-such entitlement, not a disposition of every distinct obligation situated in a staffing, service, distribution or data-processing provision.

The record supplied here alleges staffing shortages, heavy caseloads, backlogs, accounting failures and deficient recordkeeping as causes of collection failures. It does not establish a separately pleaded personal right to each standardized computer requirement or a particular staffing-level remedy. The clarification does not manufacture those claims and decide them. Those allegations can remain relevant to proof or appropriately fitted relief if an actual individual right and its violation are established; their relevance does not itself create the right or approve a structural injunction.

## Statutory application at its supported scope

**Agency organization and staffing — §654(3).** Both supplied Code versions require a single and separate organizational unit within the State to administer the plan, meeting staffing and organizational requirements prescribed by the Secretary. This directs the establishment and administration of the agency. It does not specify a beneficiary's entitlement to a particular staff complement, caseload, assigned employee or staffing-level remedy. Reading this organizational direction as a general personal right to adequate staffing would repeat B2's substitution of effective program administration for an identified individual right. The lack of an identified personal entitlement is sufficient here; no universal rule that qualitative standards are judicially unenforceable is necessary.

Section 654(3) expressly incorporates Secretary-prescribed requirements. The exact operative text of 45 C.F.R. §303.20 has not been supplied to this context. Accordingly this clarification decides no individual subparagraph of that regulation, attributes no particular staffing formula or exception to it, and announces no categorical rule about whether a regulation can elaborate an otherwise established statutory right. If a distinct claim under an identified regulatory provision becomes material, its operative text, actual asserted entitlement and preserved claim must be supplied through neutral preflight before that claim is decided. The present generalized theory does not require that broader adjudication.

**Computer administration — former and amended §654(16).** The 1994 text describes an approved statewide automated data-processing and information-retrieval system designed to assist management effectively and efficiently in administering the plan. Its specified functions include support-enforcement and paternity data, collection and distribution accounting, cost information, program interfaces, security, payment monitoring and management information. The 1996 formulation retains that management purpose, expressly requires establishment and operation by the State agency, and cross-references §654a. The substantial technical detail does not turn the system itself into every beneficiary's individual entitlement to effective administration. Precision of an administrative specification and existence of an individual federal right are distinct questions; the computer provisions are not rejected simply for being complicated or imprecise.

The 1994 paragraph's words “at the option of the State” cannot be read without §654(24). In that compilation, paragraph (24) requires a State lacking the specified system as of October 13, 1988 to submit the advance planning document by October 1, 1991, for Secretary review and approval within nine months, and have an approved operational system by October 1, 1995. The 1996 official amendment notes establish that Pub. L. 104–35, enacted October 12, 1995, replaced that latter date with October 1, 1997. The 1996 rewrite then specifies October 1, 1997 for requirements enacted on or before October 13, 1988, and October 1, 2000 for requirements enacted on or before August 22, 1996; the latter deadline extends one day for each day of the Secretary's delay beyond the specified rulemaking deadline. No present default follows from the 1994 compilation's superseded date, and no finding about Arizona's system or satisfaction of an applicable deadline is made.

**1996 §654a.** Subsections (a)–(c) require a statewide system capable of specified tasks, prescribe program-management functions and provide performance data for federal incentives and penalties. They confirm the administrative character of the general system obligation; they do not establish the asserted personal right to satisfactory computer administration. The remainder of the section contains more particular commands, including data safeguards in (d), registry records and updating in (e), information comparisons in (f), collection and distribution functions in (g), and use for expedited procedures in (h). No separately preserved claim to one of those particular protections or tasks is established here. Their individual enforceability is neither decided adversely by calling the section administrative nor affirmatively established by their specificity. In particular, no confidentiality injury, missed individual transmission deadline or other new claim is invented from this text.

The applicable dates remain indispensable. Section 654a's notes refer to Pub. L. 104–193 §395(a)–(c), reproduced in the entering-law slice: the general October 1, 1996 rule for state-law/state-plan changes, the enactment-date residual rule, the legislative grace period and the distinct constitutional-compliance grace period. Section 654a(g), added by §312(c), has the separate October 1, 1998 effective date and the described limited exception for States processing receipts through local courts as of August 22, 1996. Section 654(24)'s implementation deadlines also remain distinct from enactment and general effective dates. No missing Arizona implementation fact is assumed, and none of these provisions is silently applied before its operative time. The general-right conclusion does not depend on choosing an unresolved Arizona transition date.

The exact operative text of 45 C.F.R. §307.10 has not been supplied. No requirement or particular subparagraph of that regulation is adjudicated here. The verified statutes suffice to reject the general administrative entitlement actually at issue; they do not authorize a holding about every standardized computer requirement. A later material regulatory claim would require the exact applicable regulation and the identified claim, not an inference from the broad prayer.

**State-plan placement.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 preclude rejection merely because a provision appears among state-plan requirements. They do not dispense with B1's inquiry into an individual right. The conclusion above rests on what the examined organizational and management provisions require and on the generalized entitlement asserted, not on their placement in §654. Their specified savings clause preserves the §671(a)(15) nonenforceability result; neither that clause nor a reference to Suter supplies an unrecorded broader rule.

## Provision-specific remand and Madrid preserved

B1 and B3–B6 remain as frozen. After identifying an actual asserted statutory right, the lower court must separately address whether Congress displaced §1983 and whether the alleged conduct and requested remedy satisfy the particular provision, proper-defendant requirements and applicable temporal law. Federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions do not by themselves foreclose every otherwise existing individual right. Rejecting a general staffing or computer-administration entitlement supplies no blanket dismissal of all Title IV-D claims.

Madrid's allegation of collected support not being distributed remains an actual distinct allegation implicating former §657(b)(1). Under the supplied 1994 text, subsection (b) applies to support collected under an approved plan in the specified post-1976 fiscal years and is subject to subsection (d)'s separate foster-care distribution rules. Paragraph (b)(1) addresses the first $50 of monthly support received in that month and the first $50 for each prior month received then when the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not promise another $50 for each installment regardless of timing. This clarification establishes no individual right, violation, applicable payment facts or monetary remedy on Madrid's allegation. It preserves the inquiry already required by B5; the general administrative theory cannot absorb or erase it.

## Alternative paths and final scope check

The strongest argument for the generalized administrative right is that Congress's staffing and computer duties are mandatory, detailed and necessary for families to receive effective services. That demonstrates the duties' importance and may establish administrative noncompliance; it does not identify the asserted individual entitlement. Wright's defined rent entitlement, Wilder's identified reimbursement obligation and Livadas's specific labor right retain their force without making every operational means to statutory performance privately enforceable as such.

The opposing chapterwide path also fails: declaring every Title IV-D service, distribution or data provision unenforceable because the general staffing/computer theory fails would disregard Suter's independent-claim remand, Livadas's separate right and foreclosure inquiries, and §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10. A narrower conclusion resolves the actual general theory while preserving the lower court's provision-specific task. No new Justice-specific disagreement, changed judgment vote, additional claim, separate-writing function or adjudicated payment follows from this clarification.

For downstream use, B2's final reservation means: **Rejection of the programwide right, including the generalized staffing and computer-administration demand, does not decide whether a distinct, actually asserted provision creates a particular individual right.** It does not mean that the generalized administrative entitlement is held open for recognition on remand.
