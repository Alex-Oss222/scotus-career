## Common factual and statutory limits

CBE gave notice on March 16, 1995, to the Company, EPA and the appropriate Illinois authorities. The Company then supplied the overdue reports; the reports were current when the complaint was filed on August 7, 1995. C confirms no EPA enforcement during the notice interval. N supplies allegations that members live near or visit the facility and use the information for chemical-risk assessment and emergency preparation. N supplies no allegation of an ongoing or imminent future reporting violation. The members' interests are therefore more particular than an abstract demand that every company obey every law, but that distinction does not answer whether a judgment would redress a remaining injury.

The prayer seeks a declaration of past violation, periodic access to the facility and records at the Company's expense, copies of future compliance reports, Treasury penalties, and investigation/prosecution expenses through the litigation-cost clause. No damages entitlement, continuing withheld report or continuing information deficit is supplied. The old reporting pattern and annual obligations are relevant to the strongest deterrence argument; they are not converted into a pleaded imminent violation.

Section 11046(a)(1)(A)(iii) concerns completion and submission under §11022(a), with its tier-II alternative. Section 11022(a)(2)'s March 1 tier-I requirement does not apply when tier-II information for the same year goes to the same recipients by the same deadline. Section 11046(a)(1)(A)(iv) separately concerns the toxic-release form under §11023(a), ordinarily due July 1 for the preceding calendar year. Coverage, thresholds, reporting-frequency provisions and the information requirements retain their statutory qualifications; none is newly found satisfied beyond the supplied posture.

Section 11046(c) authorizes enforcement of the requirement concerned and imposition of its civil penalty. Section 11045(c)(1) imposes liability to the United States of no more than $25,000 for each §11022 or §11023 violation by a nongovernmental person; §11045(c)(3) counts each continuing day separately. The maximum is not a mandatory award and the recipient is not CBE.

The §11046(d)(1) notice-and-waiting condition and §11046(e) enforcement bar remain distinct: the latter requires that EPA have commenced and be diligently pursuing the specified administrative order or civil action. No defect under either provision is added. Section 11046(f) permits an appropriate award to a prevailing or substantially prevailing party when issuing a final order; it does not compel an award or expressly create damages for late information. Section 11046(g) preserves existing rights without creating a new claim. Section 11022(f)'s inspection entitlement belongs to the requesting fire department with jurisdiction; it is not an automatic CBE inspection right.

## Alternative-path assessment by component

### A. Constitutional jurisdiction and decisional sequence

**Path A1 — decide Article III first.** Lujan, Brown Group, Arizonans and Bennett preserve an independently necessary personal controversy, irrespective of statutory permission. Young distinguishes a substantial federal claim's merits from statutory adjudicatory power. The question whether this particular citizen cause reaches completed late filings therefore cannot simply be renamed lack of jurisdiction because §11046(c) uses that word. Its heading, structure and incorporation of subsection (a) must be assessed; a restriction on actionable conduct or obtainable relief remains capable of being a merits restriction.

**Strongest contrary path A2 — statutory threshold or limited merits-first decision.** Section 11046(c) expressly confers jurisdiction in actions brought under subsection (a), making a statutory-threshold reading arguable. Norton and Avrech also establish that the Court has reserved jurisdictional questions rather than exhaustively deciding them before every adverse disposition. The Company could seek resolution through a statutory ground before a difficult constitutional question.

**Assessment and limits.** Norton involved difficult appellate and statutory-route questions after a contemporaneous decision had already settled the merits; either route had the same practical effect. Avrech likewise had intervening merits authority. EPCRA's disputed reach is a live issue here, not a foreordained application. Neither case authorizes deciding this new substantive question merely because dismissal under a preferred construction seems convenient. McCardle supports the need for adjudicatory authority but involved withdrawal of a statutory appellate route; it is not silently enlarged into a decision of every possible ordering question. Arizonans preserves power to correct a lower court's jurisdictional error without adjudicating the merits. The bounded common conclusion is that Article III supplies the appropriate first and sufficient ground here. The broader sequencing positions of individual Justices appear below.

### B. Injury and traceability

**Path B1 — recognize a personal informational injury.** Required information about a facility near members' homes or activities can matter to their own preparations and assessment of risks. The loss of timely access need not be indistinguishable from an abstract interest in enforcement. Kennedy and Souter's Lujan concurrence allows Congress to identify concrete injuries and causal chains; Stevens's Lujan position and O'Connor's join in the dissent recognize concrete use interests and reasonable inferences. Bennett and Brown Group, joined by all eight Associates, preserve pleading-stage treatment of a concretely alleged interest. Section 11023(h) expressly identifies public informational uses.

**Strongest contrary path B2 — statutory entitlement is insufficient by itself.** Lujan does not permit Congress's permission to sue to replace injury. Proximity and a general desire for reports do not necessarily establish every claimed informational harm. Information already supplied before filing also distinguishes the availability of prospective relief from a past deprivation.

**Commitment and boundary.** Each Justice can assume for this disposition that the described past deprivation is concrete and fairly traceable to the Company's own nonfiling. No independent causation defect based on absent agencies is asserted. No Justice is committed here to finding informational injury categorically insufficient or to holding that every failure to meet EPCRA deadlines injures every resident. Because redressability is independently deficient, the judgment need not decide B1 versus B2 at a broader level. The distinction between a completed injury and a threatened injury remains necessary when examining particular relief.

### C. Redressability, each requested remedy, and remand

**Path C1 — jurisdictional dismissal.** Even assuming the personal past injury and statutory authority to pursue a penalty, the plaintiff must show a likely concrete benefit from the relief requested. The reports were current before filing. No alleged ongoing or imminent violation ties prospective coercion or deterrence to these members' future information needs. This conclusion does not rely on the noncontrolling three-Justice application of Lujan to absent agencies.

**Strongest contrary path C2 — allow the action or order bounded further proceedings.** Congress sought timely information and selected penalties, notice and potential costs to encourage private enforcement. A long history of nonfiling followed only by notice-induced compliance can support a practical concern about recurrence. Members retain their nearby activities and annual information interests. Church of Scientology requires only meaningful partial relief, while Bennett protects reasonable pleading inferences rather than certainty.

**Why C2 does not sustain this pleaded action.** Annual duties and past nonfiling do not, on the supplied allegations, identify a presently withheld report or an imminent repetition. The claim of concrete benefit cannot rest solely on the plaintiff's success in seeing public law enforced. Church of Scientology involved retained copies on which a judicial order could operate; Bennett involved an alleged continuing water constraint. A remedy could remove something concrete in each. Here, compliance preceded suit, and no comparable present object of relief is alleged. This is initial standing, not a postfiling voluntary-cessation case. A remand to invent recurrence, to presume a right to discovery, or to cure the operator's missing original documents is unwarranted.

| Requested relief | Operative conclusion and strongest competing argument |
|---|---|
| Declaration of completed reporting violations | A statement that the Company violated past deadlines would not supply the reports earlier or establish a pleaded ongoing legal consequence affecting these plaintiffs. The claim that recognition of the right itself vindicates the deprivation identifies a legal conclusion, but not effectual relief on these allegations. No general bar to retrospective declarations attached to a concrete remedial dispute is adopted. |
| Treasury civil penalties | Payment to the United States neither compensates CBE nor its members for the past loss. Deterrence can matter to protection against threatened injury; it is not rejected categorically. The missing premise here is a pleaded ongoing or imminent reporting injury to these plaintiffs that the penalty would likely prevent. The general enforcement incentive cannot itself replace that premise. |
| Periodic facility and record access at the Company's expense | No identified continuing reporting failure or threatened violation supports coercive monitoring relief on this complaint. Section 11022(f) does not independently give CBE the fire department's inspection right. The Court need not determine the full lawful scope of an injunction in a live enforcement action; even assuming ancillary monitoring could then be available, it does not supply this action's missing injury. |
| Copies of future compliance reports | Future reports concern future reporting periods. With no alleged imminent failure, ordering copies supplies no likely remedy for the completed late filings challenged here. The annual schedule alone does not establish threatened deprivation. This does not excuse any future statutory reporting duty. |
| Investigation and prosecution expenses requested through §11046(f) | Even treating the expense allegation as real, the clause supplies a discretionary incident of a properly maintained action and final order, not a freestanding compensation claim for late information. Expenses incurred to bring and prosecute the action cannot bootstrap a merits controversy otherwise lacking redress. No holding is needed about which prefiling expenses might count in a properly supported action or whether a particular plaintiff would substantially prevail. |
| Remand for a new standing theory or amendment | No such entitlement or specific unexamined allegation is supplied. The appropriate remand corrects the lower judgment and directs jurisdictional dismissal; it does not require an amendment opportunity, forbid every later properly supported suit or adjudicate a new claim. |

### D. EPCRA temporal scope and statutory disposition

**Path D1 — missed deadlines remain within the citizen cause after late filing.** Subsection (a) addresses failure to complete and submit forms under §§11022(a) and 11023(a), whose deadlines are part of the obligation. Unlike the Clean Water Act formulation considered in Gwaltney, EPCRA does not use the same present-violation language. Notice can correct mistakes, end accumulation of additional daily violations, permit diligent EPA enforcement and encourage settlement even if it does not erase accrued liability. C develops these textual and structural arguments; the official text preserves their actual qualifications.

**Strongest contrary path D2 — citizen enforcement requires an unperformed reporting obligation when suit begins.** The specified act is completion and submission, which was accomplished before the complaint. Section 11046(c)'s enforcement language and the notice interval can be read as a supplemental compliance mechanism, leaving wholly past penalties to government enforcement. Meghrig requires attention to the enacted private cause and remedies rather than an assumed general enforcement purpose.

**Assessment and commitment.** D1 has substantial textual force; D2 has a genuine structural argument. Meghrig's current-endangerment language and remedy limitation do not determine EPCRA's distinct formulation, while Gwaltney's result cannot be transplanted without comparing its operative text. None of the eight commitments adopts D1 or D2 as an alternative holding after concluding that Article III is absent. A Justice's willingness to discuss their relative force does not authorize a vote for a statutory judgment. The statute's temporal reach, violations, penalty amount and EPA's available enforcement remain undecided.

The strongest argument for making D2 an independent jurisdictional ground is addressed under A2. The members' lack of a redressable stake makes it unnecessary to settle every classification issue surrounding §11046(c), and no Justice is committed to rewriting the statutory merits disagreement as a jurisdictional fact.

## Justice-specific commitments and enhanced comparison

The common component assessment is incorporated only at the scope each row adopts. Each Justice's decisive authority is his or her own date-eligible writing or actual public join, not an imputed vote from a decision predating service.

### John Paul Stevens

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit's judgment permitting the action and remand for dismissal for want of Article III standing, without adjudicating EPCRA's temporal reach. Assume the pleaded past informational injury and its traceability; none of the requested remedies supplies the required effectual benefit.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** His Church of Scientology opinion requires some meaningful relief, rather than complete restoration. His Arizonans opinion requires a legally available claim and remedy and separates corrective jurisdiction from merits power. His Lujan separate position found standing because protected personal interests and meaningful consultation relief were present. His Norton dissent affirmatively establishes jurisdiction before reaching the merits and expressly identifies the need for power to enter the judgment.

**Two-path test:** His strongest path toward sustaining standing is the concrete loss of useful information, which is more immediate than a generalized enforcement interest; annual reporting and Congress's selected penalties add a plausible deterrence rationale. The decisive difference from his Lujan and Church positions is the absence of pleaded threatened deprivation or a retained object that judicial relief can change. He will not infer an unpleaded damages entitlement from the costs clause, consistent with his Arizonans insistence that a generalized prayer cannot create an unavailable damages claim.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** He supports declining to decide a new contested statutory merits question on assumed Article III power. Norton cannot be cited as his approval of its majority's assumption. His joins in Young, Brown Group and Meghrig require precise separation of claim scope, jurisdiction and remedy. D1's incorporation of deadlines is a serious construction; D2's completed-act argument does not become an obvious jurisdictional answer merely through §11046(c)'s wording. Both remain unadjudicated.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** He will not join a categorical rejection of informational injury, a holding that Treasury penalties never deter private injury, or a declaration that partial relief must restore everything lost. He supports the jurisdiction-first proposition for contested substantive merits, while preserving Norton's and Avrech's actual limited holdings. A materially alleged continuing legal consequence or imminent reporting injury would require reconsideration; neither is found here.

### Sandra Day O'Connor

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse and remand for jurisdictional dismissal on the supplied complaint. Assume personal past injury and traceability; reserve EPCRA's temporal scope.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** Her join in Blackmun's Lujan dissent preserves reasonable record inferences and procedural protection of concrete interests. Her Church of Scientology, Brown Group, Arizonans and Bennett joins preserve meaningful relief, concrete Article III limits and proof suited to the litigation stage. Her Meghrig join distinguishes the existence of a private enforcement mechanism from additional remedies.

**Two-path test:** The best contrary path treats years of nonfiling, continued annual obligations and members' nearby activities as enough at pleading to connect penalties to future compliance. Her Lujan dissent gives that argument weight. But that dissent concerned facts supporting future exposure and possible project influence; it did not eliminate the need for a threatened personal injury. The supplied complaint contains no allegation of ongoing or imminent future violation. Favorable inference does not supply a different complaint, and statutory incentives do not turn Treasury receipts into private redress.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** She adopts Article III first here because this redressability question can be resolved without deciding the genuine EPCRA dispute. Her Young join prevents casually equating a lost substantive claim with absent statutory jurisdiction. She does not need to announce a universal order for every threshold question or settle the outer reach of Norton and Avrech. D1 and D2 remain available competing statutory arguments in a proper case.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** She will join the bounded remedy analysis and the decision to reach Article III first in this case. She withholds assent from language prescribing an exhaustive, exceptionless sequencing rule or suggesting that all uncertainty must be resolved against a plaintiff at pleading. No factfinding remand is ordered because no identified factual allegation needs resolution to establish the deficiency. Her disposition does not resolve lawful amendment or relief in a future properly pleaded suit.

### Antonin Scalia

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse and remand for jurisdictional dismissal. Assume without deciding concrete past informational injury and causation; find no likely redress from the pleaded remedies; do not reach statutory liability.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** His controlling Lujan Parts II and IV distinguish citizen-suit permission from concrete injury and likely redress. His separate Part III-B shows his attention to the actual remedial chain, but its particular absent-agency analysis is not controlling here and is unnecessary. His joins in Brown Group, Young, Arizonans and Bennett preserve the same distinction even where statutory standing is broad and allegations receive favorable treatment.

**Two-path test:** D1's textual argument could justify statutory coverage, and Congress may recognize concrete injuries. That is the strongest response to a reflexive characterization of CBE's concern as wholly abstract. His disposition therefore assumes the particularized past injury rather than denying it merely because many neighbors could share it. Nonetheless, penalties paid to the government and unsupported future monitoring do not redress that past loss; litigation expenses cannot create the otherwise missing controversy.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** He supports a rule forbidding the assumption of disputed Article III standing merely to decide a contested substantive cause of action. McCardle's account of judicial power and his Arizonans join support that rule. Norton and Avrech retain their bounded previously-controlled-merits situations; they do not authorize a new EPCRA merits judgment. Young defeats the assertion that any failed federal cause is jurisdictional.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** He will not join D1 or D2 as an alternative merits holding after jurisdiction fails. Nor will he join a general exception for congressionally authorized enforcers, a free-standing public interest in deterrence or expense recovery that dispenses with likely private benefit. A concrete ongoing or imminent deprivation could change the remedial chain; this disposition does not foreclose it or decide every penalty suit.

### Anthony M. Kennedy

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse and remand for jurisdictional dismissal; assume personal informational injury and direct traceability, and rest on the absence of a supported remedial connection.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** His Lujan concurrence, joined by Souter, recognizes Congress's power to identify concrete injuries and causal chains while requiring a connection to the persons entitled to sue. He declined the absent-agency redressability section. His subsequent Brown Group, Arizonans and Bennett joins require a real controversy without turning indirect relief into a categorical impossibility.

**Two-path test:** The strongest contrary argument is that Congress specifically protected neighboring users' timely information and supplied a deterrent remedy, so its judgment should matter to the causal and remedial assessment. That consideration can distinguish CBE's alleged past harm from the public's general demand for compliance. It does not, however, identify future harm the Treasury payment would prevent on this complaint. He rejects both the automatic conclusion that governmental payment always defeats redress and the automatic conclusion that congressional authorization always establishes it.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** He supports the prohibition on assuming disputed Article III standing to resolve this contested substantive claim. His Young join requires attention to what §11046(c) actually conditions; D2 cannot evade that issue by a label. Norton and Avrech are preserved at their actual bounded settings, and this case need not decide every ordering choice among nonmerits thresholds.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** Any formulation implying that Congress can never recognize informational injury or define an adequate causal chain is unacceptable. A limited finding that this complaint supplies no threatened injury to which deterrence is connected is acceptable. D1 and D2 remain undecided; no new standing-proof document or evidentiary demand is imposed at the pleading stage.

### David H. Souter

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse and remand for jurisdictional dismissal. Assume the concrete past loss; resolve every requested form of redress as in C, without an alternative EPCRA holding.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** His Brown Group opinion permits the representative suit Congress actually authorized while preserving member injury and likely redress. His Young opinion separates a substantial federal claim from its substantive defeat. His Meghrig opinion respects the particular environmental cause and remedial scheme. His join in Kennedy's Lujan concurrence, and his Arizonans and Bennett joins, preserve congressional recognition of concrete interests without dispensing with Article III.

**Two-path test:** Brown Group provides the strongest contrary analogy: Congress chose an enforcer who need not personally receive the money. The decisive distinction is that WARN's recovery benefits the represented workers who suffered the alleged employment injury, while these penalties go to the United States and no pleaded threat connects their deterrence to CBE's members. The organizational form is not itself the problem; the missing remedial benefit is.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** He supports resolving the constitutional defect before disputed EPCRA merits, with Norton's and Avrech's confined circumstances left intact. A substantial debate over the actionable reporting period is not self-evidently an absence of adjudicatory authority. His Young distinction is especially relevant to any effort to make §11046(c) dispositive without classification.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** He will not extend Meghrig's particular current-danger language to EPCRA without textual analysis, treat its preservation clause as an independent cause, or transform discretionary cost shifting into damages. Both D1's incorporated-deadline construction and D2's supplemental-enforcement construction remain open. Properly supported ongoing harm, a different authorized beneficiary or a separate independently pleaded remedy would present another case.

### Clarence Thomas

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse and remand for dismissal for want of Article III standing. Assume past informational injury and causation; decline statutory merits.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** His joins in Scalia's Lujan opinion, including its noncontrolling redressability section, support requiring an actual link between the requested judgment and the plaintiffs' injury. His Brown Group, Young, Meghrig, Arizonans and Bennett joins preserve statutory textual limits while accepting concrete pleaded injuries and real remedial links.

**Two-path test:** The strongest alternative is that §11046(a)'s specific enforcement permission, together with §11046(c)'s penalties, supplies a remedy for the alleged violation of a personal informational interest. His Brown Group join confirms that enforcement and beneficiary roles can differ. Here, however, the specified beneficiary of payment is the United States; the members have neither compensation nor a pleaded future deprivation to avert. The legislature's creation of penalties does not alone establish the required personal benefit.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** He supports requiring Article III before a contested merits construction, recognizing the actual special settings of Norton and Avrech. His Young join prevents the mere failure of a federal cause from automatically defeating statutory jurisdiction. D1 may have textual support, but Article III remains independent; D2 is not adopted under an assumption of jurisdiction.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** He rejects making citizen enforcement or enforcement expenses alone a substitute for Article III. He does not require the Court to decide whether all informational injury is legally cognizable, to import Lujan's absent-agency application as governing law, or to decide government enforcement authority. No new theory of private or public rights is supplied beyond his verified date-eligible positions.

### Ruth Bader Ginsburg

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse and remand for jurisdictional dismissal, assuming personal past informational injury and traceability. Reserve the EPCRA merits.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** Her actual joins in Young, Brown Group, Meghrig, Arizonans and Bennett supply the relevant evidence. No Lujan vote or 1992 Church of Scientology join is attributed to her. Those later joins distinguish the procedural gateway from a claim's merits, permit Congress's removal of prudential obstacles, and preserve a live controversy with a legally available remedy.

**Two-path test:** The strongest alternative emphasizes the particular informational interests of nearby users and the pleading stage, rather than treating CBE as an uninjured general enforcer. Bennett and Brown Group support taking such allegations seriously. But Bennett's challenged constraint remained operative and Brown Group's authorized recovery would reach injured members. No requested relief here is linked to a remaining comparable injury. Arizonans also prevents an unavailable monetary theory from keeping a controversy alive.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** She supports resolving Article III before undertaking this disputed statutory merits question. The combined current-law distinctions in Young and Arizonans weigh against calling the contested §11046(a) coverage issue jurisdictional merely to avoid standing. Norton and Avrech remain confined to their actual settings; no universal ranking of every jurisdictional or nonmerits issue is adopted.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** She will not join an enlarged holding rejecting informational injury generally, disregarding Congress's express authorization, or ruling that a plaintiff must always receive a penalty personally. The adopted conclusion is the absence of likely benefit through any requested relief on these allegations. D1 and D2, lawful costs in a proper action and a later genuinely live enforcement suit remain unresolved.

### Stephen G. Breyer

**Judgment and sufficient ground:** Reverse and remand for jurisdictional dismissal because the practical benefit of the requested relief is missing on the supplied complaint. Assume past personal injury and causation; leave statutory temporal reach undecided.

**Personal jurisprudential basis:** His actual Young, Brown Group, Meghrig, Arizonans and Bennett joins provide his date-eligible anchors. No Lujan or Church of Scientology vote is imputed to him. Bennett permits an adequately supported practical remedial connection without certainty; Arizonans requires an available remedy in a continuing controversy; Brown Group respects Congress's selected enforcement structure without abandoning Article III.

**Two-path test:** The most substantial alternative is that penalties enforce timely disclosure by making the historical pattern costly, and consequently protect members who repeatedly need these reports. That practical deterrence account could matter where a future deprivation is alleged. Here its necessary personal-risk premise is absent. Recognizing a social enforcement benefit alone would erase the retained constitutional limitation in Brown Group. The costs request does not repair the missing premise.

**Sequence and statutory alternatives:** He agrees to resolve the straightforward redressability defect before the genuinely disputed EPCRA construction. He withholds assent from an exhaustive pronouncement that every jurisdictional issue must always precede every other question, since Norton and Avrech show the need to attend to the actual proceeding and already-settled merits. Neither supplies a reason to decide the novel statutory dispute here. His Young join also prevents classification by label.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** He will join the bounded standing and remedy analysis, not a categorical assertion that penalties payable to government can never redress personal risk or that minor pleading uncertainty always warrants dismissal. A concrete threatened reporting deprivation could make deterrence relevant without direct payment; no such premise is supplied. D1 and D2 remain unadjudicated, and no general remedial taxonomy beyond what resolves this complaint is required.

## Source limits, blockers and next-stage constraints

There is no unresolved gap that prevents the bounded commitments. The original filings are not required to invent a factual premise when the verified and sanitized allegations already permit disposition. A proposed different holding dependent on additional complaint language, future violation, compensation, a continuing information deficit or amendment must return through a corrected neutral packet; it may not be supplied during assembly.

The isolated model has not compared these positions with the historical Supreme Court result. That comparison belongs to reconciliation and may require Justice-specific further review. These commitments do not assert a historical departure or match. They remain intact as the independent handoff even if reconciliation later identifies a different supported commitment.

No opinion author, assignment, private circulation revision, Stone join, Stone fallback use or Court-wide vote is decided here. No original-source excerpt has been reconstructed by long retyping; the primary and statutory text remains in the linked mechanically preserved copies.
