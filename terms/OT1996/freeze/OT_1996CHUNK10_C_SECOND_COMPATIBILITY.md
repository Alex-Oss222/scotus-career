## Chalmers: the common judgment component is available

**Conclusion:** The one/four/four formal positions do not themselves require a stop. Engine §§8–9 permit the Court to set aside this summary judgment and return the accommodation claim because that concrete judgment component has five supporters: Stone, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This is not five votes for either formal vacatur or a mandatory trial. The full positions remain Stone's reversal and trial remand, the four Associates' vacatur and ordinary further proceedings, and the other four Associates' affirmance.

This conclusion rests on what the commitments require, not a rule that every reversal and vacatur vote may be combined. Here Stone expressly concludes that genuine disputes defeat this summary judgment and requires return of the accommodation claim for trial. The remanding four expressly conclude that neither notice nor the independent hardship analysis conclusively sustains the present summary judgment and require return of the same claim for proper adjudication. The relief they share is therefore definite: the present judgment cannot remain operative, and the claim returns to the lower court. Neither side merely reserves a possible remand or asks for a decision on an unrelated threshold. The differences concern the formal disposition and what the lower court must do after the judgment is displaced.

Engine §8 commands separate judgment coalitions for each "separable judgment component"; §9 separately requires the treatment of the judgment below, remedy, remand instruction and next stage. Those instructions permit identifying the common removal-and-return component while preserving disagreement about an obligatory trial. Section 4 likewise recognizes judgment-component agreement apart from opinion joins. A mandatory-trial demand necessarily includes return of the claim and removal of the judgment barring it; recording that included vote does not require Stone to accept the lesser remand as sufficient.

Section 4's ban on unauthorized substantive narrowing remains controlling. It would prohibit changing Stone's proposed judgment to "vacate and remand for further proceedings," describing him as accepting renewed summary judgment in place of trial, or treating his trial demand as optional. It does not require the Court's resulting mandate to give every Justice in a component coalition all the additional relief that Justice demands. Stone must retain his reversal/trial vote and his disagreement with a mandate that does not command trial. That is preservation of his fixed position, not a new fallback or a discretionary narrower position attributed to him.

The distinction must be carried through the actual judgment and topology, not left to an audit footnote:

The operative order can state that the judgment is set aside and the case remanded, while expressly leaving no Court instruction requiring trial. Stone's published position must make clear that he would reverse and require trial and disagrees with stopping at the common relief. No liability, feasibility of warning, right to reinstatement, or other additional relief follows from the common component. No fifth join in a four-Justice rationale follows either. Any claimed controlling proposition still needs its own compatibility test and actual majority at the same operative scope.

This does not invoke *Marks*, create a tie, or supply a general procedural rule for resolving every multiway split. It identifies an already adopted judgment component under the Engine's express component method. A bare assertion that five dislike the judgment, without this concrete shared legal effect and without the remedial disagreement, would be inadequate. ## Thomasson: revalidation is required

The existing compatibility memo correctly identifies a §4 blocker. Stone's statutory ground is independently sufficient and expressly depends on the Board having given the presumption irrebuttable effect. His supplement states both that "[t]he Board required more than the statute allows" and that a new proceeding must give the presumption genuine rebuttal effect. These are essential application and remedy premises, not optional supporting language.

The frozen neutral validation establishes receipt of service, supporting and expert evidence, a finding of insufficient rebuttal, and the refusal to supply further specific rebuttal. It establishes neither excluded proof nor compelled identity disavowal nor another identified unlawful Board instruction. It leaves a supported construction or application route available only if the particular legal error is identified. The reconciled commitments preserve that limit. Receipt followed by insufficiency does not establish an irrebuttable rule; conversely, receipt alone does not establish that every possible Board standard was lawful. The blocker is the missing support for Stone's particular essential error premise, not an affirmative factual finding that no Board error could exist.

Assembly cannot convert this ground into a different claim that the Board improperly weighed evidence or that the existing proof conclusively rebutted the presumption. Nor can it omit an expressly independent ground while retaining the rest as though Stone had approved the omission. Section 4 requires renewed approval against the corrected record, unless a supported additional source establishes the essential error and the changed neutral premise completes the affected isolated stages. His express Walmer branch addresses the intervening legal standard; it does not supply a branch for this factual/application correction. The existence of five frozen non-Stone affirmance votes does not cure incomplete revalidation of Stone's required position.
