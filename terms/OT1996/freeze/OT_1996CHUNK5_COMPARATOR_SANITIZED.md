# 49. In re Rhone-Poulenc Rorer

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL APPELLATE COMPARATOR

On March 16, 1995 the Seventh Circuit granted mandamus and directed decertification, with Judge Rovner dissenting; rehearing was denied April 27. The majority combined aggregate settlement pressure, materially varying state standards and successive-jury concerns. It did not determine tort liability. The dissent viewed the writ as circumvention of ordinary final review.

[Historical appellate opinion](https://openjurist.org/51/f3d/1293).

## B. HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT ORDER AND SUBSEQUENT CONTEXT

The historical Supreme Court denied certiorari October 2, 1995 in Grady v. Rhone-Poulenc Rorer, No. 95-147, 516 U.S. 867. The order gives no merits rationale and adopts no mandamus or certification rule. It is not an authenticated completed simulation event and is not part of the authorized counterfactual petition history.

[Official Supreme Court order](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep516/usrep516orders/usrep516orders.pdf).

A later official DOJ account identifies historical settlement-class proceedings at 169 F.R.D. 632 (1996), after decertification of the litigation class. That is no Supreme Court merits ruling and does not silently moot the counterfactual dispute expressly preserved here. No settlement, compensation or binding release is created in the simulation.

# 57. Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome:** Affirmed. The Eleventh Circuit's judgment and the Tax Court's treatment of the disputed deductions remained in place.
* **Historical Reasoning:** The decision rejected automatic dollar-for-dollar reduction on this record. The historical reasoning examined date-of-death valuation, the economic significance of limitations on income rights, and the relevant Treasury regulation and administrative rulings. The Commissioner's failure to establish an alternative quantitative-materiality challenge was material to affirmance. A definitive method for measuring materiality remained unresolved.
* **Historical Limits:** The result does not establish that income-funded administration expenses can never reduce a deduction. The treatment of expenses charged directly to qualifying principal remains distinct. The marital and charitable deductions received the same disposition on the question presented.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only; it is not simulated precedent.
* **Historical Sources:** [Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert, 520 U.S. 93](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/520/93/); [Official U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/520bv.pdf#page=188).

# 59. Bennett v. Spear

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Court reversed and remanded unanimously.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Scalia wrote for a unanimous Court.
* **Historical Holding:** Petitioners adequately alleged Article III standing; the ESA’s “any person” citizen-suit language negated the prudential zone-of-interests limitation for authorized citizen suits; the § 1536 claims were not covered by the cited citizen-suit subsection but were reviewable under the APA; and the Biological Opinion was final agency action.
