## Proposition-specific grounds and limits

**1. Forum, norm, action, responsibility and remedy remain distinct.** All eight apply Abebe-Jira: substantial allegations by aliens of torts violating the law of nations come within §1350's forum grant; whether an applicable norm binds a private individual must be answered norm by norm. Neither jurisdiction nor condemnation of the conduct by international law automatically establishes an American civil action, this defendant's responsibility or a particular remedy. The failure to allege state action cannot defeat a norm that itself binds individuals. A merely domestic assault does not become an international tort because the victim and defendant are foreigners.

**2. Genocide.** All eight accept that the prohibition applies to private individuals as well as officials. The Genocide Convention's Article IV expressly confirms individual coverage. Article II requires intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial or religious group as such, through killing members; causing serious bodily or mental harm; deliberately inflicting conditions of life calculated to bring about the group's physical destruction in whole or in part; imposing measures intended to prevent births; or forcibly transferring children to another group. Alleged ethnic cleansing, forced pregnancy, rape or murder must satisfy the relevant defined act and specific group-destruction intent; the labels do not establish those predicates. The pleaded personal planning or direction connects Karadzic to the asserted conduct sufficiently to defeat this categorical dismissal, but is not proof.

The six Justices recognize a confined received domestic civil action for that personally perpetrated, planned or directed intentional wrong. Abebe-Jira's specific §1350 reception rationale supplies the domestic source; the clear prohibition and express private coverage supply the norm, rather than a free-standing judicial power to enforce every international obligation. The Convention and criminal implementation statute are not themselves treated as civil causes. Section 1092's disclaimer of an enforceable substantive or procedural right created by that criminal chapter remains effective; it does not repeal the distinct received action. The criminal statute's then-existing territorial/nationality conditions govern its prosecution, not an unexpressed amendment of §1350. Scalia and Thomas accept that these sources establish the international norm but reject the additional domestic action: absence of repeal does not establish an action that they find never authorized. They do not treat §1092 as an affirmative jurisdictional repeal.

**3. Core war crimes.** All eight reject the proposition that the relevant prohibitions necessarily require action by a recognized state. Common Article 3 binds each party to a noninternational armed conflict occurring in the territory of a High Contracting Party. It protects persons taking no active part in hostilities, including armed-forces members who have laid down their arms and persons out of combat through sickness, wounds, detention or any other cause. It requires humane treatment without adverse distinction based on race, colour, religion or faith, sex, birth, wealth or similar criteria. Against those protected persons, its prohibitions apply at any time and place: violence to life and person, especially murder, mutilation, cruel treatment and torture; hostage-taking; outrages upon personal dignity, especially humiliating and degrading treatment; and sentences or executions without a previous judgment of a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees. It also requires collection and care of the wounded and sick. An impartial humanitarian body may offer its services; the parties should endeavour to bring other Convention provisions into force by special agreement. Application does not affect the parties' legal status. These distinct clauses are not converted into one generic criminal or civil rule.

The six recognize the received civil action here for the pleaded deliberate killing, torture and grave sexual violence against protected persons as core war crimes personally perpetrated, planned or directed by the defendant. Each claim must establish the relevant armed conflict, protected victim, prohibited act, conflict nexus and personal responsibility. Common Article 3's express application to each party defeats a categorical official-action requirement; the source of the domestic action remains Abebe-Jira's confined reception rationale. This does not recognize a civil action for every breach of every treaty clause, decide the customary status of Protocol II, or decide the alternative international-conflict theories. Scalia and Thomas accept the individual international duties but withhold the additional domestic action on the same source ground as for genocide. Yamashita supports the existence of individual obligations in war; its military adjudication does not create this civil action or decide an absent commander's civil liability. Neither group imports a securities aiding-and-abetting rule from Central Bank, assumes liability from title alone or decides a comprehensive command-responsibility standard.

**4. Official torture, ordinary extrajudicial killing and the TVPA.** The controlling Abebe-Jira action for deliberate personal participation in or supervision of official torture remains available if its own elements are established. Ordinary private torture or killing does not fall within that action merely because it is atrocious. If conduct satisfies the distinct genocide or war-crime norm, the six's received-action conclusions above govern; otherwise a valid official-action or separately authorized route must be shown. No additional independent ATS action for ordinary official execution need be decided beyond the available TVPA route and the genocide/war-crime holdings.

All eight accept the TVPA's enacted civil damages action under §2 for an individual who, under actual or apparent authority or color of law of a foreign nation, subjects an individual to defined torture or extrajudicial killing. Torture damages run to that individual; killing damages run to the individual's legal representative or a person entitled to claim in a wrongful-death action. Under §2(b), the court shall decline to hear the claim if adequate and available remedies in the place where the conduct occurred have not been exhausted. Under §2(c), suit must commence within ten years after the cause arose. Neither the absence of an identified alternative forum in the present dismissal record nor a research gap establishes satisfaction or breach of exhaustion.

Section 3 defines extrajudicial killing as a deliberated killing not authorized by a previous judgment of a regularly constituted court affording indispensable judicial guarantees, and excludes a killing lawfully carried out under foreign national authority under international law. Torture requires intentionally inflicted severe physical or mental pain or suffering directed against a person in the offender's custody or physical control; pain arising only from, inherent in or incidental to lawful sanctions is excluded. The specified purposes include obtaining information or a confession from the victim or a third person; punishing the victim for an act the victim or a third person committed or is suspected of committing; intimidating or coercing the victim or a third person; and discrimination of any kind. Mental pain means prolonged mental harm caused by intentional or threatened severe physical pain; administration, application or threatened use of mind-altering substances or procedures calculated to disrupt profoundly the senses or personality; threatened imminent death; or a threat that another person imminently will suffer death, severe physical pain or suffering, or those substances or procedures. These statutory definitions do not automatically rewrite every element of the separate received ATS action.

The district court's nonrecognition reasoning does not foreclose proof of actual governmental attributes—population, territory, governmental organization and capacity for relations—or proof of action in concert with Yugoslavia or Serbia. Legal accountability for conduct need not recognize a government diplomatically. The TVPA's actual or apparent authority/color-of-law words still require authority attributable to a foreign nation; self-description as a president or a mere semblance of authority is not enough by itself. Lugar supplies a useful concerted-action analogy, not wholesale transplantation of §1983 into the international or TVPA inquiry. Section 1331 supplies federal-question jurisdiction for the enacted TVPA claim; whether it supplies a separate universal customary-law route remains undecided.

## 3. Common legal commitments and procedural limits

All eight maintain the separation required by **Monell, Pembaur, Canton, Collins, Leatherman, and the municipal portion of Plakas**: an underlying federal deprivation; a decision properly attributable to the municipality; the necessary municipal fault; and a causal connection between that fault and the particular deprivation. Employment alone supplies none of the last three. Collins rejects an additional freestanding governmental-abuse element; it does not dispense with attribution. Leatherman rejects heightened pleading, not the proof demanded after trial. Plakas preserves a distinct municipal inquiry and requires evidence connecting the identified policy or deficiency to the deprivation. Nabozny likewise preserves actor-specific responsibility; it creates no relaxed hiring standard.

All eight reject a categorical rule that a single final hiring decision can never produce municipal liability, or that an otherwise facially lawful act is categorically immune from § 1983 scrutiny. Tuttle bars inferring a municipal policy merely from a subordinate's single misconduct; it does not erase independent evidence of the final policymaker's selection and the information accompanying it. Conversely, identifying Moore's selection as attributable to the County does not prove deliberate indifference or direct causation. The question is the demonstrated risk of the relevant constitutional abuse, not whether an applicant was generally undesirable or whether declining to hire would have prevented all his later acts.

A prior pattern may establish notice but is not an invariant prerequisite in the face of an otherwise obvious constitutional risk. None requires proof that the policymaker intended the injury or could foresee its exact time, victim, and mechanics. The stricter Canton formulation requiring actual or constructive notice of an omission substantially certain to produce constitutional violations remains material for O'Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy; it is not a demand for metaphysical certainty or proof of the County's illustrative psychiatric hypothetical. The two groups below disagree about what the proven background and proposed police duties permit a jury to find, not about converting ordinary negligent selection into federal liability.

State permission to employ an applicant is not federal immunity and is not proof that hiring him was constitutionally indifferent. The other direction is equally important: a departure from prudent hiring practice or an arguable state-paperwork defect does not itself establish a federal constitutional injury. No Justice adopts the County's requested good-faith immunity from municipal liability.

Stevens rejects that application because the record includes information about resistance and force-related misconduct, the failure to examine it, and duties presenting the corresponding risk. Pic-A-State alleges its own concrete economic injury and a present constraint on resumed operations from a criminal statute directed at the business it actually conducted. Its stated plan to resume, the enacted fit and serious penalties make the enforcement risk sufficiently real without an actual prosecution. The Government's statement that prosecution is unlikely is weighed but is not a disavowal. ### Commerce ground all eight join

Congress may prohibit a procurement business's knowing interstate or foreign transmission of information used to obtain, for a person in one State, a ticket, chance, share or interest in a lottery or similar scheme conducted by another State, subject to the enacted agreement exception and the separate statutory exemptions. The regulated conduct is an actual interstate commercial procurement transaction. Keeping the physical ticket in the conducting State does not remove that interstate business from the commerce power.

Champion v. Ames establishes that commerce in lottery interests is a permissible subject of federal prohibition, not merely federal facilitation. The challenged amendment closes the actual electronic-procurement route around the physical-ticket restriction. Gibbons supplies the affirmative power over interstate commercial intercourse; the power is not confined to moving tangible paper. Robertson's actual interstate enterprise holding confirms that a genuine cross-border business operation does not need a separately substantial effect from each transaction. Harris, Hale and American Life League preserve direct-market and economic-class grounds under the actual Lopez holding. Here the text itself requires knowing interstate transmission by a procurement business; no court-created element is added.

The strongest contrary contention is that lawful local ticket purchases and state revenue protection cannot themselves enlarge an enumerated power. That proposition is sound but does not describe the enacted target: the challenged procurement transmission is interstate commercial conduct, rather than local conduct connected to national commerce only through downstream social costs. Under Hodel, closing that commercial loophole and accommodating the States' distinct gambling decisions are rational means within the established power. Revenue preservation can be a permissible legislative objective here because that independent commercial connection exists; a generalized effect on state programs would not establish the power by itself. Congress need not condemn every lottery as immoral, prove a precise dollar loss, or supply transaction-specific legislative findings to regulate this business.

### Agreement exception and congressional authorization all eight join

The §1301 exception permits the business when an agreement exists between the States in question **or appropriate authorities of those States**. Congress may choose that accommodation while prohibiting other covered interstate procurement. Under Prudential Insurance v. Benjamin and Northeast Bancorp, the dormant Commerce Clause's restraints on unaided state action do not invalidate the federal commercial rule or the particular state choices Congress clearly authorizes. The explicit agreement condition supplies that authorization at its actual scope.

This does not approve every state lottery restriction, remove an independent constitutional right, establish that Pic-A-State has a qualifying agreement, or transfer unlimited federal lawmaking authority to States. The statutory permission and the antecedent affirmative commerce power are separate questions. Tracy's ordinary discrimination comparison and economic rationality holding do not decide the reserved congressional-authorization question; the surviving pre-divergence authorization cases supply this ground. No First Amendment, equal-protection or nondelegation merits ruling is warranted on the grant and supported record.

## Exact statutory limits retained in every join

The challenged clause requires a person engaged in the procurement business, a customer in one State, a covered scheme conducted by another State, and knowing transmission in interstate or foreign commerce of procurement information. It does not become a prohibition on all communications about any lottery or all wholly local ticket purchases. The existing physical carriage and receipt clauses remain distinct. The governing amended sanction is a fine under title 18, imprisonment up to two years, or both; no superseded $1,000 ceiling is used.

Section 1307's exemptions are distinct from the §1301 agreement exception. Section 1307(a)(1) covers specified information about a state-authorized, state-conducted lottery contained in a publication published in that State or another lottery State, or broadcast by a station licensed in such a State. Section 1307(a)(2) covers information about other schemes authorized or not prohibited in the conducting State when run by a nonprofit or governmental organization, or as a clearly occasional and ancillary commercial promotional activity. Section 1307(b) separately covers transportation or mailing to addresses within the conducting State of material concerning its state-authorized lottery, and to foreign addressees of materials designed for a lottery authorized within that foreign country. Its definitions of State and foreign country include the specified possessions and subdivisions; the subsection-(b) lottery definition concerns pooled proceeds allocated by chance and excludes sporting-event wagers. The nonprofit definition refers to qualification under Internal Revenue Code §501. No fact establishes that this business comes within any exemption, and no holding collapses them into blanket immunity for a lawful state lottery.

## Common rule, application limits and lawful bases

**Announcement and this category.** Each Justice applies his or her actual join in Thomas's Wilson v. Arkansas opinion. Announcement of identity and authority contributes to reasonable dwelling entry, with supported excuses for danger, futility, escape or threatened destruction. A warrant's authorization to search does not settle the reasonable manner of entry. Wisconsin's felony-distribution category assumes its conclusion in every included case, although the actual occupant, sought evidence and execution setting can differ. General associations between drugs, weapons and disposal do not establish that every search in that category requires surprise. The rule would remove most of Wilson's work in a large class without examining circumstances.

This conclusion does not reject every categorical Fourth Amendment rule. Buie's permission to check immediately adjoining attack spaces and Maryland v. Wilson's passenger-exit direction rest on specific relationships between the intrusion and a recurring safety need. Buie's rejection of an automatic violent-offense rule for a broader sweep is the closer analogy here. An unannounced forcible dwelling entry affects occupant safety, the opportunity for voluntary admission, property and privacy; it is not merely a limited relocation during a lawful traffic stop. Chandler confirms attention to the actual governmental justification in its distinct special-needs setting, but supplies no universal prohibition of categories.

**Operational threshold.** For the evidence-destruction ground at issue, the State must identify specific circumstances and rational inferences supporting a reasonable suspicion that the normal announcement and opportunity to admit officers would threaten preservation of the evidence. It need not establish probable cause of imminent destruction or await the sound of a toilet or actual destruction. Buie's use of articulable suspicion for a limited protective step under an existing lawful arrest supports the practicable threshold by analogy. The present step regulates the execution of an independently authorized search; it does not authorize entry or a search unsupported by a warrant or another lawful basis. The analogy does not make a protective sweep and a surprise entry identical, or transfer every Buie rule to evidence searches.

The threshold is modest but reviewable. A drug label, unsupported general firearms claim or officer hunch is inadequate. The relevant facts must be known when the challenged force is initiated; later flight or successful discovery cannot retroactively supply justification. The decision does not set the exact threshold for each other Wilson exception, define a universal waiting period, or replace the distinct knowledge-of-purpose inquiry in a claim of useless gesture with a suspicion of police identity.

**Seven-Justice application.** The credited sequence supports a particular inference that the ruse had exposed the police and that further delay risked destruction of the cocaine sought. **3. Consensual relationship.** A-1 voluntarily entered the established $12,490 subcontract with LCM, the tribally owned corporation, for reservation community-center work. The first Montana exception can support appropriate regulation of activities arising from consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements. It does not require an express forum-consent clause in every case, but it does require a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and the consensual activity; contracting with the Tribe does not submit the contractor to every conceivable reservation claim.

For this claim the six assume, favorably to petitioners without making findings, that Stockert's trip served that project and that Fredericks resided on the reservation. She nonetheless asserts the ordinary duty owed by a public-road driver to another non-Indian road user, not a tribal contract entitlement, an obligation owed to a tribal participant in the work, or control of project performance. The road is used under the public right-of-way, rather than only by permission gained through the subcontract. The contract may explain why the truck was there without making adjudication of this third-party highway claim part of governing the consensual relationship. That distinction supplies the result; absence of contractual privity is relevant but not a universal rule that no third-party tort could ever fall within Montana's first exception. A different injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a person participating in the consensual activity presents a different question.

This reasoning does not find the trip unrelated. It makes the unresolved work connection legally insufficient even on petitioners' strongest supported version, so further factfinding about the load or assignment cannot change these six commitments. No weight is given to the purported Utah forum/law clause, which was not accepted as part of the appellate record. Nor do attributed building/employment-law obligations establish blanket consent to this lawsuit. The holding neither enforces nor invalidates an unread contract provision.

**4. Tribal integrity, security, health or welfare.** Montana separately preserves civil authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. Nonmembership alone cannot eliminate every possible tribal interest. The seriousness of Fredericks's injury, her family ties to members, assumed residence, road safety and the importance of functioning tribal courts are all acknowledged. But ordinary individual injury and the resulting familial or community effects do not on this record establish the governmental threat or direct tribal effect sufficient to make this nonmember public-road litigation an exercise of necessary tribal self-government. Otherwise the exception would restore general territorial authority over essentially every local civil wrong that the external limit excludes.

No fiscal or service burden is invented, and no finding that the Tribe suffered no consequences is entered. The question is legal sufficiency of the supported claim and interests. The sons' membership does not change their mother's membership or bring their unreviewed consortium claims into the judgment. A filed protective state action is consistent with another forum being available, but forum availability is not the independent legal reason that tribal power is absent. No state merits or limitations outcome is decided.

## Souter's distinct ground and Breyer's narrower remand

**Souter.** His actual join in Blackmun's Bourland dissent objected specifically to equating loss of absolute exclusion with loss of compatible regulation; his join in Breyer's Chickasaw dissent likewise attends to the concrete governmental relationship protected by a limited source. He accepts controlling Bourland on its acquisition statutes and does not restore the displaced power there. The present conditioned highway easement retains the underlying trust interests and contains no express transfer of judicial jurisdiction. Public passage can coexist with adjudication of wrongs associated with tribal economic activity. The six's road analogy therefore does not, for him, establish that otherwise supported civil forum authority has been surrendered.

He does not rest on the easement label alone or claim complete authority over any nonmember who crosses the reservation. Even taking Montana as the operative residual framework, its first exception concerns the nonmember's activities arising from a consensual relationship, not merely claims between signatories for breach of the agreement. If Stockert was carrying out the Tribe's community-center project when the collision occurred, the activity and associated road injury could supply a sufficiently direct connection to governing that reservation undertaking. The public nature of the road is important, but does not necessarily make the activity unrelated to the voluntarily assumed project. The strongest objection is that ordinary highway duties are owed to everyone; his answer is that a generally stated duty can still govern the performance and consequences of a specific consensual reservation enterprise.

The present record does not permit replacing that conditional proposition with a finding that the trip was in performance of the subcontract. Nor may a court turn the absence of proof in this motion record into a finding that it was unrelated. He therefore vacates and remands for the federal courts to apply a conduct-specific jurisdiction inquiry to the proper record and its procedural posture. The residence allegation and admission, subsequent dispute and stricken materials require their actual procedural treatment if residence affects that analysis; no historical residence finding or nonresidence finding is invented. The first exception supplies a sufficient reason for further proceedings. Generalized community welfare does not itself become an independently sufficient final jurisdiction ground.

**Breyer.** His Chickasaw income dissent rests on a particular protected governmental relationship, member/employer/work nexus and source, not generalized territorial sovereignty. He likewise regards the voluntary tribal construction undertaking and actual relationship of the driving to it as potentially material. He would not decide the broader question whether every compatible adjudicatory power survives this easement. Even assuming the six's Montana framework, a categorical exclusion of a work-related third-party accident because its claimant did not sign the subcontract is too narrow. The ordinary nature of a tort duty does not by itself settle whether regulation of the activity falls within the consensual relationship.

He therefore joins vacatur/remand at the first-exception scope, requiring proper assessment of the allegedly project-serving trip and any legally material residence issue. He does not adopt Souter's broader objection as a necessary holding, deem work purpose proved, require a finding of jurisdiction, or create a broad exception for every injured reservation resident. Montana's second exception remains a distinct inquiry rather than an automatic consequence of the first. A court must respect the actual federal pleading admission and the limits of the existing evidentiary rulings rather than order factfinding on an invented factual premise.

For both Justices, remand is to the federal courts for proceedings consistent with the corrected legal inquiry. It is not a mandatory new tribal evidentiary hearing, a command to repeat already completed exhaustion, or an immediate revival of the District Court's ultimate jurisdiction judgment. Which additional proceedings are legally needed depends on the existing record, procedural rules and the factual predicates actually material under the stated inquiry.

## Shared governing law and genuinely competing merits paths

All eight apply Anderson as carried by Burdick: examine the actual burden, precise interests and their connection. Severe restrictions demand a narrowly drawn compelling justification; reasonable nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily may rest on important regulatory interests. Neither a severe-burden label nor a least-restrictive-means obligation follows from the mere existence of a party-association claim.

**Path A — reverse and sustain the old prohibition in this application.** The restriction limits an official ballot nomination, while leaving this party able to endorse and campaign for Dawkins, voters able to vote for him, and the party able to nominate another eligible candidate. On the complete system supplied, the burden is significant but not severe. The State can preserve coherent nomination channels, distinct ballot choices and stable party identities through a general prohibition without proving that this particular consensual nomination already caused fraud. Burdick, Storer and Munro support preventive administration; Colorado Republican regulates expenditure restrictions on the party's own expression and does not convert the official ballot into a general expressive forum. The best objection is that an actual qualifying nominee is being excluded from the party's line even though there is no added candidate, consent is present and signature rules already screen weak nominations. This path answers that objection only by identifying the limited ballot burden and proportionate general nomination-system interests; it cannot rest on an interest in protecting incumbents from competition or call this nomination a fictional protest vote.

**Path B — affirm the as-applied constitutional judgment.** Choosing a willing, otherwise eligible and sufficiently supported standard bearer is a central party function. Another candidate or an endorsement without the party nomination does not furnish the same associational choice. Even if the burden is not assigned the severe label, the asserted interests do not adequately fit this consensual combination: no sore loser is being restored, the major party does not object, existing support requirements apply, and the additional designation does not add another individual to the race. Norman, Tashjian and Eu make actual association and precise fit matter. The best objection is that reasonable election rules may use general lines and anticipatory judgments without case-specific proof, and there is no entitlement to maximize a party's influence. This path accepts that premise but finds the complete bar insufficiently connected to the actual dangers asserted here. It does not require separate party-line vote totals, duplicate votes or a court-designed ballot.

The strongest objection is that medical intimacy and limited entrustment are closely analogous to Sheets and public dissemination is an imperfect measure of injury. ## The actual award: four separate legal questions

**What is established.** Benefits were paid voluntarily before the formal proceeding, but the present issue is the later adjudication. The June 2, 1992 hearing involved counsel, evidence, an additional medical deposition and briefs. Jurisdiction/status was one of five disputed questions. The ALJ expressly addressed Harbor Tug's contention that Papai might fall within the master/crew exclusion, recognized the parties' contrary civil positions, refused preclusive effect to the interlocutory district status orders, applied Wilander and a Robison-style connection formulation, and determined the crew exclusion did not apply. The order awarded some benefits, denied others and provided credit, interest, calculation and fee procedures. It was signed August 27 and filed and served August 31, 1992; neither side appealed, and its finality is accepted in the supplied litigation. This is neither an unadjudicated payment nor an imagined automatic award with no status decision.

Section 921(a) makes filing the effectiveness trigger and provides thirty-day finality absent its prescribed review proceeding. Section 921(b) supplies Board review; the sixty days in §921(c) follow a final Board order, not the initial ALJ decision. No October 27 ALJ-appeal deadline, actual last-day computation, categorical inability to appeal or claimant appellate standing ruling is invented. The conflicting February 2/February 5 benefit-date language is left intact because it does not decide this status or preclusion question. No new last-payment date or failed modification attempt is assumed.

**Statutory exclusivity.** Section 905(a) replaces other employer liability where the prescribed LHWCA employer liability applies, subject to its failure-to-secure exception and separate contractor qualification. The crew exclusion in §902(3)(G) defines the coverage boundary. Harbor Tug's independent submission is that a final formal compensation disposition conclusively activates §905(a) without needing ordinary issue-preclusion elements; Papai disputes that legal consequence. The statutory text does not say simply that receiving any payment makes a person a covered employee, and the controlling Gizoni holding forecloses such a rule for voluntary unadjudicated payments. Its reservation, however, leaves the final-order question open rather than deciding it against the employer. The issues include whether the final order conclusively establishes coverage for this purpose, what §921's prescribed review path makes binding outside compensation enforcement, and whether Congress's preservation of overlapping pursuit and credits changes that consequence. A formal-award rule must answer those questions rather than derive itself from the phrase mutually exclusive.

The strongest statutory-foreclosure argument is that final determination of LHWCA liability invokes an express exclusivity command and prescribed review scheme, with no failure to secure payment alleged. Continued payments, a lien or a second recovery's credit would not undo a final statutory classification. The strongest contrary argument is that §905(a)'s liability trigger and §902(3)(G)'s exclusion do not expressly create a new conclusive-status category based simply on the form of payment, while Gizoni preserves alternate pursuit and the underlying schemes contemplate overlap. The answer cannot be assumed from ordinary issue preclusion or from the absence of an affirmative election. Settlements raise additional issues and are not decided merely because the employer includes them in its argument. None of the eight adopts an independent final-award statutory bar here; the three status-reversal Associates reserve it, and the five remanding Associates require its distinct legal consideration without assuming either result.

**Ordinary administrative issue preclusion.** Astoria supplies a contextual presumption favoring preclusion for final administrative adjudication, subject to congressional purpose, the agency's role, the issue actually and adequately litigated, and the relevant procedural opportunity. Its statutory-intent inquiry permits implied displacement and imposes no clear-statement demand. It rejected preclusion under the ADEA's particular scheme; that outcome does not establish a universal second judicial trial after every federal administrative award. Conversely, a general preference for repose does not remove the statutory and adjudicatory predicates. Astoria is independently verified primary law and an own-writing/actual-join anchor for Souter and the four other present Associates then participating.

Applied here, the employer has a substantial claim: the same parties litigated the crew exclusion as an express prerequisite to compensation, the ALJ decided it after an evidentiary hearing and briefs, the earlier district orders remained interlocutory, and the award became final. Papai cannot answer merely that compensation began voluntarily, that counsel changed positions or that the ALJ might have erred. Neither later clarification of law nor possible §922 modification automatically destroys finality. But issue identity and an adequate opportunity on the precise status issue require more than counting hearings: the actual connection standard used, necessity and scope of the determination, contrary litigation incentives, claimed limitations on review and any applicable statutory displacement must be addressed. The ALJ cited both permanent-assignment and substantial-work alternatives; no simple finding that it applied a permanent-only rule is adopted. Nor is a material legal difference under Chandris conclusively disproved merely because Wilander was cited. The preserved objections require a source-specific comparison, not a generic assumption that every compensation hearing is the same as a Jones Act trial.

The recovered order eliminates the old missing-order reason for declining analysis. The remanding Associates do not find its predicates unknowable or demand the whole hearing transcript as a condition of decision. They reject the Ninth Circuit's categorical extension of Gizoni's voluntary-payment reasoning to a formal status adjudication, and return the actually disputed predicates and statutory question for their proper first application below. The record already establishes real adjudication and finality; an alleged defect in opportunity or issue identity requires its actual legal or evidentiary basis. A new first-instance conclusive preclusion holding would require that completed comparison and a properly supported individual judicial position; this model does not supply one by inference from the Justices' status opinions. Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer are not attributed personal joins in Astoria or Gizoni that they did not cast.

**Election, inconsistent positions and credits.** Harbor Tug disclaims an affirmative election-of-remedies defense. Initial alternative pursuit, judicial estoppel, ordinary issue preclusion and §905(a) exclusivity therefore cannot be collapsed into one election label. Years of voluntary payments and the dispute about added back symptoms prevent the lower court's forced-litigation rationale from becoming an established finding of employer manipulation. At the same time, a final adjudication does not by itself refute the claimed effects of incentives or establish that a favorable award recipient could obtain review of every disputed premise. Those objections must be analyzed on their actual grounds.

Section 903(e) expressly credits specified other compensation or Jones Act payments against LHWCA liability. Current Gizoni preserves its own overlapping-recovery credit rule. Neither provision's direction nor that holding automatically settles the collateral effect of a formal award. The employer's settlement-and-credit examples and nonsymmetry argument remain arguments, not findings about what happened here. No double recovery, refund, fee consequence or new lien amount is adjudicated.

Section 913(d) supplies tolling only after denial of tort recovery on its specified covered-employee, employer and secured-compensation grounds. It is not protection following every failed lawsuit. Section 922 permits specified factual-mistake or changed-condition modification within the last-payment or rejection periods, preserves its limits on prior payments and excludes settlement modification. It does not establish an expired one-year period measured from the award's signature. No actual stay request, withdrawal right, promised sequencing protection or unused modification opportunity is invented. ## Shared operative commitments

**Coverage.** ERISA §510's attainment clause applies to rights a participant or beneficiary may become entitled to under a covered welfare plan; absence of statutory vesting does not categorically remove those rights from its protection. The statute separately prohibits specified actions against participants or beneficiaries for exercise of covered rights and actions undertaken for the purpose of interfering with attainment. The prospective attainment language and ERISA's inclusion of welfare plans leave no textual pension-only or vested-right limitation to insert.

**Amendment and interference remain distinct.** Section 1051(1)'s exemption of welfare plans from statutory participation and vesting requirements preserves lawful power to design, amend and terminate such benefits. Section 510 does not itself make benefits immutable or promise continued employment. That power does not automatically authorize a covered discharge or discriminatory act undertaken for §510's prohibited purpose while a relevant plan right may be attained. Curtiss-Wright distinguishes amendment power from authorized exercise; Lockheed distinguishes sponsor design from implementation and independent statutory obligations; Varity's majority and dissent disagree about other functions and enforcement but neither establishes categorical immunity from §510.

**Application and remedy.** The lower court dismissed the welfare theory on the categorical absence of vesting. That ground cannot stand. The transfer and benefits reductions are reported allegations; they establish neither a valid plan amendment nor a proved statutory violation. On remand the plaintiffs must satisfy the actually applicable participant/beneficiary, plan-right, covered-action, purpose and causal requirements, and establish an available remedy through §510's reference to §502. ## Exact rule, finality and source-specific alternatives

**Rule under examination.** Where state law makes a jury's capital recommendation a legally consequential part of the weighing process, an unconstitutionally vague aggravating instruction to that jury can infect the judge's final death sentence notwithstanding the judge's separately reasoned findings of valid aggravation. A lawful correction must address that transmitted influence; independent findings or other valid aggravators do not alone answer it. The present question is whether that protection was dictated by law existing when Lambrix's judgment became final on November 24, 1986. It is not whether the jury constitutionally had to impose sentence, whether life and death recommendations had identical force, whether Florida could employ a judge, or whether the current Espinosa merits rule is sound.

Teague's operative inquiry, as applied through current Stringer, Graham, Gilmore, Caspari and Gray, asks whether existing precedent compelled that particular result at finality, allowing reasonable distinctions grounded in the earlier holdings. A later decision's date, the absence of identical facts, or a later court's disagreement does not itself show novelty. The inquiry concerns both the rule and its proposed application. Current postfinality opinions govern this Court's method and, where they so hold, the prior rule's established force; they are not fictitious authorities available to a 1986 judge. Espinosa decides the present merits principle, not this finality question.

**Prefinality Florida law is dual, with real qualifications.** LeDuc, 365 So.2d 149, 150–152 (1978), involved a death recommendation, applied a protective review standard to it if all relevant data had been considered, and independently examined the judge's findings and psychiatric information. The recommendation was legally important before 1986; its weight was conditioned on an adequately informed process, and it was not binding. The later availability of psychiatric reports and affirmance in LeDuc preclude treating the case as an automatic-new-jury rule for every information problem. Messer, 330 So.2d 137, 141–143 (1976), supplies the more specific safeguard holding: the jury had to receive relevant accomplice-sentence and proposed psychiatric mitigation, and the judge's capacity to consider those matters later did not satisfy the statutory jury function. It required a new jury sentencing hearing on those exclusions, not automatic life or the universal invalidity of every judicial cure.

Ross, 386 So.2d 1191, 1197–1198 (1980), retained recommendation weight and LeDuc's relevant-data condition while requiring independent judicial balancing. It vacated because the judge treated death as compelled and remanded to the judge to exercise reasoned judgment. Its Dixon discussion specifically assigns experienced judicial judgment a protective role against lay reactions that could describe every capital crime as heinous. That is substantive support for reasonable prefinality reliance on a judge's legal narrowing, rather than a mere advisory-jury label. But Ross involved improper judicial deference, not a holding that separate findings always purge a legally weighted vague recommendation. Its judicial remand cannot be transferred without examining the error to be corrected.

Eutzy, 458 So.2d 755, 757–760 (1984), preserved both great weight and judicial authority in sustaining an override of a life recommendation. It found no supported mitigation justifying that recommendation, rejected robbery as an unsupported aggravator and preserved the other supported findings. Its instruction discussion expressly rested on the favorable life recommendation and resulting absence of harm, without reaching the instructions' merits. It supplies no finding that a death recommendation under vague instructions is harmless, and its treatment of a factually unsupported factor is not automatically the constitutional treatment of a vague one. Its accomplice discussion depended on the absence of evidence of equal culpability; no general rejection of jury mitigation follows. The unexplained Florida separate death dissents in LeDuc and Eutzy carry no invented reasoning.

**Old-rule path and its strongest objection.** Godfrey's guidance requirement follows the actual decisionmaking work assigned to a capital actor. LeDuc and Ross make a death recommendation consequential, while Messer shows that the required jury contribution performs a distinct protective function not automatically supplied by the judge's later review. The relevant-data condition reinforces the dependence of lawful weight on an adequate jury process; it is not a finding that Lambrix's jury in fact considered everything relevant or a holding equating excluded evidence with vagueness. On this path, the constitutional rule comes from Godfrey, applied to the allocation established by state law; a violation of state law alone is not the constitutional ground. Stringer's current old-rule holding supplies the controlling rejection of an automatic exemption for consequential weighing. Baldwin's independent-sentence rationale and reservation about actual jury influence, and Spaziano's permission for judicial sentencing, do not authorize influence from an unguided constitutionally relevant recommendation. Independence and influence can coexist; no equal-weight premise is necessary.

The strongest objection is that the relevant state decisions resolved the allocation and protection of a statutory jury role, while Godfrey addressed the constitutional sentencer. Ross and Eutzy insist on a legally decisive, independently reasoned judicial sentence. Messer's state mitigation remedy does not decide whether federal constitutional guidance had to extend to the recommendation despite that judicial safeguard. Baldwin reserved the influence question rather than resolving it, and Spaziano sustained substantial judicial authority. A compelling present analogy can therefore remain an application not compelled in 1986. The old-rule Justices answer through the existing protection's actual function, rather than treating the reservations as affirmative holdings or applying Espinosa retroactively merely because it now controls.

**New-rule path and its strongest objection.** A reasonable 1986 jurist could require meaningful narrowing in the independently reasoned judicial sentence while treating the earlier recommendation's imperfect guidance as a distinct transmission problem not yet constitutionally settled. Ross's specific protective role for the judge and judicial-remand remedy, Eutzy's preserved authority to reject an unreasonable recommendation, Spaziano's allocation holding and Baldwin's distinct independent-sentencing rationale provide the substantive basis. LeDuc and Messer establish significant jury weight and a state safeguard, but do not themselves constitutionalize the particular noncurative effect of an unguided recommendation. This ground assumes no absence of influence, no equal treatment of life and death recommendations, and no automatic cure. It leaves Espinosa fully operative for an eligible merits adjudication.

The strongest objection is that calling a recommendation advisory or the judge independent cannot detach a legally weighted contribution from the resulting sentence. LeDuc squarely involved death, Ross preserved that source and its condition, and Messer rejected the very claim that a judge's later consideration always sufficed for the jury's omitted contribution. Moreover, Stringer prohibits treating a weighing mechanism as an affirmative permission to retain unconstitutional influence. The new-rule Justices must accept those propositions and identify the additional federal step, rather than suggest Florida had no prefinality death-recommendation rule. Their conclusion is confined to what the combined authorities compelled in 1986, not a current finding that the actual sentence was constitutionally cured.

Godfrey, Zant and Barclay must be read at their relevant scope: guidance was already required; Zant's nonweighing holding and reservation did not authorize all invalid weighing; Barclay's state-law invalidity and actual review were not a universal cure for constitutional aggravation. Maynard, Clemons, Walton, Florida Smith and Grossman postdate finality and cannot fill the prefinality landscape by assumption. Current Stringer and Espinosa retain their force and stated limits. HAC and cold/calculated/premeditated aggravation remain within the supplied claim; the lower court assumed inadequate guidance for retroactivity, and this handoff does not make a fresh factor-specific vagueness finding or presume juror reliance on either factor.
