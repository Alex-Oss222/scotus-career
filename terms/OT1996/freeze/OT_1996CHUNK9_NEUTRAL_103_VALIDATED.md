## Reviewed judgment and record

The Second Circuit's reported judgment is 74 F.3d 28. Its source header states January 4, 1996, which supersedes the original packet's unsupported January 11 date. The court adhered to its prior reversal of the District Court's summary judgment for the state officials after considering Travelers. It reasoned that an assessment directly depleting plan assets affects the Fund's provision of benefits, and that routing payments differently would change administration across states. Those are the reasons under review, not current Supreme Court law.

The Fund is a self-insured multiemployer employee welfare benefit plan. It owns and operates two separately incorporated nonprofit diagnostic and treatment centers in New York and one center in New Jersey. Its centers furnish medical and dental care to longshore workers, retirees and dependents, other covered ILA workers, and members covered under the Port Police and Guards Union arrangement described in the State's account. The lower appellate opinion describes services as provided to plan participants and beneficiaries; the State's cited account specifies these additional arrangements. No new finding concerning every recipient's statutory beneficiary status is necessary merely to report those accounts.

The HFA is a revenue measure applied through licensed health-facility activity. The reviewed diagnostic-center rate is 0.6 percent of assessable gross cash receipts from patient care and other operations, subject to the actual deductions and exclusions. Collections ultimately reach the State's general fund. The Fund's centers reported $1,177,670 assessable receipts for January through November 1991 and paid $7,066.02, then stopped filing assessment reports and making payments. These figures describe past payments, not a final refund or current-liability adjudication.

Employer contributions transmitted first to the Fund and then to its centers were excluded as grants; the State did not contest that exclusion in this case. Other receipts included direct outside-employer payments, employee fees and copayments, workers' compensation and PPGU payments, and miscellaneous income. The Fund challenges the levy on health-benefit payments and contributions; it does not challenge tax on miscellaneous receipts unrelated to participant health services. Its restructuring of certain receipts is an identified party practice, not proof that all taxable receipts could lawfully be recharacterized or that the State commands that restructuring.

The trustees filed this federal action on April 16, 1992, seeking declaratory and injunctive relief and repayment. The District Court granted the State summary judgment on nonpreemption and expressly did not decide the remaining tax-jurisdiction question. The State's merits footnote 3 also identifies the Eleventh Amendment refund defense as unreached. No supplied decision awards a fixed refund or finds a waiver of that defense. The State's failure to press section 1341 in later briefing does not itself supply jurisdiction.

## Entering law and distinct jurisdictional question

Read `entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK9_102_103.md` in full. Travelers sustains the reviewed insurer and HMO applications as ordinary economic influence while reserving direct self-funded application. It makes no new Tax Injunction Act holding. Dillingham now supplies current law: ERISA status does not become a legal reference merely because a covered entity encounters an independently operating state rule, and an economic incentive does not become a plan command without its actual operation establishing one. Board of Trade preserves preemption where covered-plan benefit content supplies a mandatory legal measure. Inter-Modal preserves the independent work of each enacted ERISA command and remedy, without deciding this tax. None decides whether this Fund may litigate in federal court under section 1341.

Section 1341 bars federal restraint of state-tax assessment, levy or collection where a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy may be had. These receipts support a revenue-raising assessment, not a newly inferred regulatory penalty. The District Court rejected the Fund's proposed automatic ERISA override and sovereign-instrumentality analogy, then identified competing considerations about the state remedy but did not decide them. This case has no concession of adequacy comparable to any other pending June 2 matter.

Additional primary legal sources now clarify the competing routes:

- [Travelers Insurance v. Cuomo, 14 F.3d 708, jurisdiction portion](https://openjurist.org/14/f3d/708/travelers-insurance-company-v-m-cuomo): The Second Circuit treated fiduciaries' injunctive ERISA actions as exclusively federal and therefore found no adequate state remedy. This is lower-court reasoning, not an adopted Supreme Court holding. The response is that an adequate state tax-review action need not duplicate the federal cause of action.
- [Morgan Guaranty Trust Co. v. That demonstrates state adjudication of the federal issue in that route; it does not by itself establish identical procedures or relief under this HFA. Its broad tax-preemption reasoning remains subject to current Travelers and Dillingham.

## Lawful paths and matters to keep separate

**Threshold:** The Court must decide whether the actual New York route is sufficiently certain and adequate notwithstanding ERISA's exclusive federal cause-of-action jurisdiction. A finding that it is adequate requires a jurisdictional disposition without deciding preemption. A supported finding that it is not sufficiently plain permits merits review; neither an automatic ERISA exemption from section 1341 nor State silence supplies that conclusion. The different tax in Morgan and the different taxpayer routes matter. If existing sources do not permit resolution, ordinary jurisdictional remand is an available judicial disposition, not an invented production order.

**Preemption if reached:** The State's strongest position is that provider licensing and receipts, rather than ERISA status or benefit content, trigger the ordinary tax; direct payment by the plan adds cost without dictating benefits, eligibility or internal administration. The Fund's strongest position is that the tax reaches the plan's actual delivery of benefits and their funding, and the interstate routing changes show more than an indirect market effect. General applicability alone answers neither side. The analysis must identify an actual legal command, plan-specific reference, competing enforcement remedy or effective compulsion, and distinguish required tax accounting from uniform administration of plan entitlements. The rate alone creates neither a safe harbor nor a conclusion of severe compulsion.

**Relief:** Prospective ERISA relief, refund relief, the Tax Injunction Act and the separate sovereign-immunity defense require their own treatment. A nonpreemption decision would remove only the ERISA ground and would not determine a current tax balance, new penalties or a refund amount. A preemption decision likewise would not automatically establish that federal repayment is available. Fund-to-center transfers excluded in this litigation, miscellaneous receipts the Fund did not challenge, and hypothetical materially different tax applications remain outside the case.
