# 66. Young v. Fordice

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Young v. Fordice
- **Citation or Docket:** 520 U.S. 273 (1997); No. 95-2031
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on direct appeal from a three-judge United States District Court for the Southern District of Mississippi.
- **Simulated Event Date:** March 31, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether Mississippi was required to obtain Voting Rights Act § 5 preclearance before implementing discretionary voter-registration procedures adopted in response to the National Voter Registration Act.
- **Question Granted:** Whether the post-February 10, 1995 dual federal and state registration system contained unprecleared changes from the lawful baseline.
- **Legal Field:** Voting Rights Act § 5, voter registration, federal election law, and preclearance.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The three-judge district court granted Mississippi summary judgment, treating the New System as a correction of a temporary unlawful plan and as already covered by preclearance of the NVRA implementation submission.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The private plaintiffs ask the Court to reverse and enjoin administration of the unprecleared changes. Mississippi seeks affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** Mississippi's pre-1995 Old System generally used one registration for federal and state elections. A Provisional Plan attempted to implement the NVRA for both, but the legislature did not enact needed state-law changes and officials abandoned that plan after forty-one days. The later New System retained the old state-registration rules while using different NVRA procedures for federal registration and added manuals, instructions, reporting requirements, agency choices, and voter communications not contained in the old system. Mississippi did not submit that dual system for preclearance.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The direct appeal concerns coverage and the required preclearance sequence, not whether the new practices ultimately have a discriminatory purpose or effect. The Provisional Plan's brief operation must be classified before selecting the comparison baseline.
- **Relevant Dates:** New System implemented February 10, 1995; district actions filed April 20, 1995; argued January 6, 1997; decided March 31, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** The private action was consolidated below with a similar action brought by the United States.
- **Participation Matter:** Official opinion checked: no historical case-specific nonparticipation is recorded. The term-effective simulation roster applies with Stone as Chief Justice; no case-specific exclusion or vote is inferred beyond the controlling participation record.
- **Public Sources:** Official U.S. Reports, 520 U.S. 273; NVRA implementation manuals and Department of Justice correspondence described in the opinion; three-judge district-court record.

### Issue 1: Baseline and coverage of NVRA implementation changes

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Morse v. Republican Party of Virginia (1996) has six joins for the pleaded primary-like convention-fee change and for keeping coverage and submission distinct from substantive preclearance. Presley (1992) excludes only governing changes without the required direct voting connection. Voting Rights Coalition v. Wilson (1996) unanimously upholds the challenged federal-registration duties, using Article I, §4 for congressional elections and the separate Burroughs/national-protective basis for presidential registration; it neither requires identical state and federal rolls nor alters voter qualifications.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Section 5 coverage reaches legally operative changes in voting practices and procedures. Federal registration duties administered through state offices establishes that NVRA authority does not erase the distinct operation of state election law.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Application to dual registration

Mississippi’s lawful federal-registration duty survives even though state-only qualifications and rolls remain distinct. That federal command does not establish preclearance of discretionary state forms, instructions, agency choices, or the way dual status is communicated. Those features directly affect access to registration and differ from Presley’s internal governing reallocations. Identifying the lawful operative baseline and the specific changed components precedes substantive §5 purpose-and-effect review. No assumed same-term Lopez disposition is needed to conduct that inquiry.
