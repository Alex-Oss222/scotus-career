## Decisional propositions and common limits

1. A reasonable period preceding commencement may reveal whether the defendant's business relationship is continuous and systematic. The year of filing is not an exclusive window, and an earlier ceased activity does not by itself establish present amenability. The 1987–1993 discovery limit was reasonable; unlimited retrospective discovery is unnecessary on this record. All eight support these propositions and leaving that discovery order undisturbed.
2. General jurisdiction requires substantially more than the purposeful contact sufficient for a claim arising from a particular forum transaction. International Shoe, Perkins and Helicopteros control that distinction. Quill's related collection-duty holding neither supplies all-purpose jurisdiction nor imposes physical presence as a universal prerequisite.
3. For the six Justices finding this prima-facie commercial course sufficient, the constitutional fairness objection fails here. Burger King's compelling-case requirement cannot be met merely by the foreign location of this claim, witnesses and documents, the parties' nonresidence, and a slight domestic defense burden. Those circumstances deserve consideration; their ordinary combination here does not demonstrate that requiring this deliberately established commercial actor to answer is constitutionally unfair. Actual venue/transfer questions remain separate.
4. Asahi's eight-Justice independent fairness holding remains law in its international setting. No commitment treats its stream-of-commerce plurality as a holding or treats Scalia's judgment agreement as a join in Part II-B. The present six-Justice judgment-supporting ground does not declare that sufficient general contacts defeat every conceivable due-process objection.
5. Keeton keeps choice of law and a supposed favorable limitations period from distorting personal jurisdiction. No Justice decides which limitations provision applies, its expiration, construction liability, damages, service defects other than the contacts objection, a transfer destination or an entitlement to transfer.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**General contacts: reversal versus alternative affirmance.** The strongest case for affirmance is that continuous selling is not necessarily continuous and substantial corporate operation: Glater involved resident sales representatives yet rejected all-purpose jurisdiction, and Crane carefully distinguishes general power from claim-linked business. Ginsburg and Breyer adopt that distinction as dispositive on the complete credited course. They do not ignore the dealer-support and employee facts or rely simply on a small percentage. The strongest case against their path is that Robertson's aggregate course adds recurrent engineering, authorized builders, targeted architectural solicitation, a former staffed office and sustained business beyond mere advertising or purchases. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas find those combined features sufficient under the existing, fact-sensitive standard at this procedural stage. Their conclusion treats Glater as a materially informative but nonbinding application, not a rule that no selling operation ever suffices. No Justice invokes a later corporate-home limitation.

**Independent fairness: affirmance versus rejection of its application.** Asahi and International Shoe supply the strongest case for a separate review even after adequate contacts; multiple minimal plaintiff/forum interests may matter without a formal foreign-defendant requirement. Stevens and O'Connor's own Asahi positions make categorical elimination unavailable to them, and Breyer's Pleasant Street join likewise recognizes separate review. The opposing argument is not that the factors disappear: general jurisdiction necessarily reaches some disputes without a claim-specific forum connection, and Burger King demands a compelling constitutional showing. The burden actually found is slight domestic inconvenience; the remaining circumstances identify a better trial location without the Asahi kind of demonstrated unfairness. The six reversal votes find that objection decisive. Scalia reaches the same bounded application while reserving an obligatory freestanding general-jurisdiction inquiry. Ginsburg and Breyer express no vote to affirm on fairness after assuming sufficient contacts.

**Remedy.** The six reversal votes require remand for further proceedings with the prima-facie constitutional dismissal removed, not a trial verdict or final adjudication of disputed jurisdictional facts. Remaining properly presented service, limitations, venue, transfer and merits questions retain their ordinary requirements. Ginsburg and Breyer would leave this Vermont dismissal in place without resolving those questions. All eight leave the reasonable discovery limit in place. No source gap is converted into a party default or a new production order.
