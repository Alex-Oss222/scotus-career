## Competing paths and remedial consequences

**A — affirm on a confined no-violation application while assuming the asserted medical-confidentiality interest.** The self-insured authority may review benefit utilization, possible misuse, generic substitution and expensive medications through the officials responsible for administration. The names arrived incidentally, the later communication remained among administrators and physicians involved in assessing the report, and identified reporting ended when its necessity was questioned. Even accepting the favorable evidence that identifiers were unnecessary and the initial safeguards deficient, the Constitution does not demand an optimal reporting arrangement or the least intrusive conceivable administration. This record establishes no general publication, employment penalty, treatment veto or use detached from the benefits inquiry. The strongest objection is that the question to Press sought HIV identities that Pierce admitted were not necessary; a legitimate interest in costs cannot automatically license a distinct inquiry into an intimate diagnosis. The narrower affirmance answers that limited related review, not administrative perfection or indispensability, is the constitutional question; it must not recast deliberate later access as accidental or find that all identifiers were necessary.

**B — vacate the directed defendants' judgment and remand.** Sheets's protected intimacy and purpose limitation should extend to deliberate official identification or internal sharing of a person's serious medical condition without an adequate governmental connection, even when it stops short of general publication. Receipt of unnecessary names alone does not establish the violation, but a jury may infer from the subsequent conduct that officials went beyond reasonably related benefits review. Favorable evidence includes highlighting the HIV/AIDS drugs, asking who was HIV-positive, the acknowledged lack of need for names, lack of notice and safeguards, and the confidential basis on which Doe used the program. The strongest objection is Whalen's refusal to constitutionalize administrative necessity, plus the limited audience and prompt correction. This path therefore cannot adopt a universal indispensability requirement or automatically restore damages. Its narrower rule distinguishes purposeful intimate inquiry without adequate connection from legitimate utilization review with imperfect safeguards. The appellate court must consider the record under that rule and address properly remaining damages and trial issues. No complete verdict form or instructions establishes that every newly articulated condition was found, so no unconditional reinstatement of $125,000 follows.

**C — affirm because the proposed internal-use constitutional extension lacks an adequate constitutional source.** Sheets remains binding at its actual public-disclosure scope, but does not establish general constitutional control of identified data held by a public benefits administrator. Whalen's reserved question cannot itself supply the new affirmative rule. The strongest objection is that medical intimacy and limited entrustment are closely analogous to Sheets and public dissemination is an imperfect measure of injury. This path answers through the specific guarantee's demonstrated scope, not by denying intimacy, requiring economic loss, or holding that state remedies cure an otherwise established substantive violation.

## Reconciled common ground and limits

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer support affirmance. All five can join the expressly assumed-right Path A application; Scalia and Thomas also reject the proposed extension on Path C and must not be represented as endorsing the assumed right. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support vacatur and remand through Path B. No Court-level majority or opinion assignment is supplied because Stone is outside this context.

The common affirmance does not endorse Westinghouse's seven considerations as a constitutional test, deny Sheets, make public dissemination indispensable to every conceivable future claim, or authorize all intentional employer access. It leaves the general scope of medical confidentiality undecided. The opposing rule requires a governmental connection to the deliberate intimate use without imposing universal indispensability. Both sides preserve the precise actual recipient scope and distinguish accidental acquisition from later intentional acts.

No damages ruling is required by affirmance. A vacatur would displace the constitutional direction to enter judgment for defendants and require appellate treatment of properly remaining matters, expressly including the damages-sufficiency issue the Third Circuit left unresolved. There is no new immunity defense, mandatory retrial, automatic award or assumption of missing litigant filings.
