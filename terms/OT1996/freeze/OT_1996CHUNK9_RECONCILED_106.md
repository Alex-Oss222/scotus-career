## Comparator and governing context

The historical Court reversed, with Breyer writing for Rehnquist, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor dissented separately, adopting Scalia's merits reasons while disagreeing with his criticism of the certiorari grant. Scalia's dissent was joined by Thomas. These historical writing relationships are evidence for reconciliation only. They neither assign authors nor establish final joins here.

The historical Court treated the product as the item supplied through the manufacturer and initial distribution chain to an initial user. Independent equipment added afterward remained other property after resale. It distinguished product integration during initial manufacture/distribution from later user additions. A resale warranty's theoretical availability did not itself displace the original manufacturer's existing duty concerning other property. Its discussion preserved foreseeability, legal causation, other economic-loss limits and actual contractual allocation. The opinion expressly confined its conclusion to the added equipment that itself played no causal role in the accident. Its disposition did not turn the vessel's hull into other property against the hydraulic-system supplier or classify all later replacement components as separate property.

Scalia's complete dissent selected the object of the commercial purchaser's bargain as the product. He reasoned that the purchased vessel and equipment represented Saratoga's product-value interest, for which warranty and insurance could allocate loss. He considered a rule ending the distribution chain at the last seller regularly engaged in selling that product preferable to a simple first-user rule, but ultimately adopted the purchaser-bargain rule, not that alternative. His concern that a commercial outfitter might also briefly use a product illustrated a boundary problem; he acknowledged the absence of a finding that Madruga was in that business. Neither that disputed characterization nor a new finding that Madruga was a distributor may be inserted into the neutral record. His criticism of granting review was distinct from his actual merits vote. O'Connor expressly rejected that criticism while agreeing with his reasons for affirmance.

East River, 476 U.S. 858, is unchanged governing law. Its integrated manufacturer-supplied turbine and propulsion-system treatment at 867 prevents component atomization. Its 871–875 warranty analysis protects the commercial product-value bargain, and its application at 875 gives substantive weight to downstream charterers' actual repair, as-is and insurance undertakings even though they could not assert the manufacturer's warranty. Footnote 8 preserves possible express tort-risk allocation in appropriate circumstances. East River does not already decide the precise user-addition/resale question, but its full reasoning supports the legal disagreement over the appropriate boundary; the downstream passages cannot be omitted from either side's explanation.

Simulated Exxon preserves separate legal causation and comparative allocation, with restrained review of concurrent findings and independent attention to actual contractual obligations. It supplies no new product boundary. Simulated Yamaha preserves state remedies outside the field of the pertinent exclusive maritime rules; it concerns territorial nonseafarer death and supplies no reason to select one commercial resale boundary here. None of the five May 27 public decisions changes East River or the property classification. June 2 peers are not antecedent law.

The corrected strict-liability findings are accurate descriptions of the historical lower adjudication, not a counterfactual factual change. No actual contractual term, record finding, altered review question, remedial limitation or prior published O'Connor position supplies the missing changed premise.

After reviewing her complete dissent, the full merits rationale she adopts and East River's actual downstream application, the historical affirmance remains strong Justice-specific evidence. The strongest objection is that the manufacturer's exposure to independent property should not shrink with resale. Her answer is that East River defines the commercial interest protected by contract and warranty through the purchaser's acquisition, not merely the manufacturer's original inventory. She need not endorse criticism of the certiorari grant, an unproved commercial-distributor finding, or a bar on every external item aboard.

## Components, qualifications and remedies preserved

**A. The reviewed classification.** The five reversal Associates support the proposition that independently added user equipment does not lose its other-property character merely because the user transfers it with the original product to a later user. The product includes the vessel and integrated components supplied through the manufacturer and its initial distribution chain. The rule addresses added equipment that itself played no causal role in the accident; it does not automatically decide the treatment of a causal replacement or every item acquired after delivery. This qualification limits the proposed rule and is not a new factual finding that every listed item was causally inert. No new specific provenance or causal participation is presumed. Ordinary legal causation and any valid applicable contractual allocation remain distinct.

The three affirmance Associates support treating the integrated vessel and equipment acquired in Saratoga's commercial bargain as the product. Their ground does not erase distinct catch or unrelated external-property claims, change general maritime products liability, or adopt every alternative boundary discussed in the historical dissent. The five and three positions remain opposing applications, not one common rule reached by adding unlike rationales.

**B. Adjudicated matters and review limits.** The district court found defective hydraulic design and legal causation and imposed strict products liability. The circuit affirmed those findings and the two-thirds reduction for Saratoga's own fault. Its footnote 8 rejects treating occasional negligence references as a supported independent negligence adjudication. No new defect, negligence, superseding-cause or comparative-fault finding is made. The corrected neutral packet's findings remain findings; they are not reduced to allegations or reopened wholesale on remand.

The grant does not reopen the separate hull-recovery denial against Marco or the rejection of recovery for ordinary installed replacement components. Spare inventory independently added by a user is not interchangeable with an installed replacement of an original component. The rule does not atomize the original vessel into a defective hydraulic system and other property. An actual distributor or manufacturer's integrated first-sale package remains distinct from user additions; neither the record nor reconciliation conclusively resolves all mixed assembler/user situations.

**C. Implementation.** Reversal would remove the challenged resale-only exclusion and return the case for implementation in the existing damages and interest posture. The supported item categories must be treated under the rule without a new final amount, presumed full valuation, reinstatement of every trial item by fiat or mandatory retrial. The two-thirds reduction remains. Catch, crew property, currency, rescue and the interest determinations remain undisturbed except for the recalculation that the changed principal award requires. The skiff's reported rescue use and asserted later scuttling prevent claiming that every item was burned in the fire; the legal classification does not itself find a particular item's loss mechanism.

Affirmance would retain the Ninth Circuit's classification and its existing damages/interest remand, not invent a different award. Neither disposition supplies a missing equipment ledger, new warranty language, an express waiver, a finding of distributor status, or a default from unavailable research materials. Precise new item provenance or valuation would require the targeted trial extract identified in the fresh packet; a confined legal ruling needs none of those unknown details.

**Separate-writing substance.** If contrary applications remain after assembly, the purchaser-bargain position should explain the commercial warranty/private-ordering ground, its response to the unchanged-duty argument and its rejection of the first-user boundary. O'Connor's merits support does not require her to endorse criticism of the grant. The other position should explain why resale alone does not alter the original manufacturer's other-property duty and why that rule preserves manufacturer-supplied integration.
