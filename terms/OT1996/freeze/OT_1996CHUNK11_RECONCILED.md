## 119. Fletcher v. Kalina

### Component propositions and remedy

**F1 — Protected advocacy:** Preparing and filing the information and warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case are protected advocacy under Imbler and Burns when performed in that capacity. This protection is not lost merely because the complaint alleges bad purpose or false evidence. It does not immunize a distinct evidentiary undertaking by the same person.

**F2 — Personal attestation:** When the prosecutor personally swears to the truth of factual assertions to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant, she performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness rather than the advocate's function of presenting another person's evidence. Absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect that personal certification. The functional difference, not proof that the statement was false, determines the absolute-immunity classification. No general withdrawal of immunity for courtroom witness testimony follows.

**F3 — Remaining elements and defenses:** Denial of absolute immunity for the oath establishes no damages liability. Fletcher must establish a constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality and causation under the governing Fourth Amendment law; Kalina retains qualified immunity and all other available defenses. A judicially issued warrant does not categorically sever causation when material deception procured it. A corrected truthful account may nevertheless establish probable cause, and neither culpability nor that account is decided in this interlocutory appeal. Elder and Lanier concern the legal review method, not retroactive notice from their decision dates.

**F4 — Judgment and stage:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's denial of absolute immunity for the personally executed certification, with the foregoing functional limitation, and return the case for the unresolved proceedings. No reversal of an actual damages judgment, determination of qualified immunity, new mandatory trial, or award of damages is ordered. If implementation requires separating claims resting only on protected preparation/presentation from claims resting on the personal oath, the lower court must observe that distinction; the affirmance does not authorize liability for all acts in the charging transaction.

### Enhanced comparison

The protection path treats the certificate as one component of initiating prosecution and stresses that separating preparation from presentation can expose protected advocacy through pleading. Its strongest support is Imbler's protection of initiation and Burns's protected appearance at a probable-cause hearing. Its obstacle is that the official personally furnished the evidentiary truth assurance that a separate witness could supply. Malley denies comparable absolute protection to the warrant affiant, and Antoine and Buckley require a historically supported function rather than institutional importance.

The function-specific denial path preserves the advocate's preparation and presentation but denies absolute immunity for the oath. Its strongest objection is an artificial division of one charging event or a special rule triggered by alleged dishonesty. F1 and F2 answer both: actual function, rather than timing, title, alleged motive or the truth ultimately found, supplies the line. Every Associate adopts this distinction. Kennedy and Souter do not surrender their Buckley view that genuine preparation for testimony can precede probable cause; the personal oath is different from consultation with a prospective trial expert. Scalia's historical method supports denial without revisiting Imbler or making every witness answer in damages for testimony.

## 120. Piscataway Township Board of Education v. Taxman

### The statutory fork and component definitions

**Common propositions T0:** Race determined which equally qualified, equally senior employee lost an existing job, so the employer needs statutory permission; equal qualifications do not remove the discrimination question. Weber and Johnson authorize some voluntary affirmative action without an adjudication that the employer itself violated Title VII. Neither the constitutional diversity discussion in Bakke nor the current federal constitutional rule in Adarand supplies an employment-law exemption. No constitutional claim was before the lower court, and none is decided here. All eight Associates adopt these propositions.

**T1 — Remedial boundary, supporting affirmance:** The statutory accommodation recognized by Weber and Johnson does not authorize this concededly nonremedial race-selected discharge merely to preserve department diversity. A manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category may support voluntary action without proof of the employer's own actionable past discrimination; that distinction does not make every beneficial or integration-minded purpose sufficient. On the admitted absence of that remedial premise, Title VII prohibits this decision. This is not a constitutional holding that diversity can never be important.

**T2 — Burden, independently supporting affirmance:** Making one existing tenured employee bear the loss of her employment because of race, under this nonremedial diversity policy without a remedial limiting goal, unnecessarily trammels the interests Title VII protects. The necessary staff reduction and equal seniority do not make the racial selection burdenless; the board's available nonracial tie procedure could allocate the loss without that classification. This case-specific holding does not settle every remedial layoff plan or import a rule that any hiring preference is permissible.

**T3 — Narrow educational permission, supporting reversal of federal liability:** Weber and Johnson do not make their sufficient remedial premises the exclusive permissible grounds for every voluntary decision. Title VII permits this genuinely educational choice, confined to an actual equality of qualifications and seniority in a necessary reduction, to preserve the business department's sole Black teacher and the integrated instruction that presence supplies. The employer has not displaced a superior qualification or seniority entitlement, chosen an unqualified employee, or established an absolute exclusion from employment. The serious layoff burden is not automatically unnecessary merely because the same loss falls on one of two otherwise tied employees. This rule does not permit racial balancing for its own sake, fixed representational quotas, derogation of superior seniority or qualifications, or a general diversity defense for all jobs. An expressly nonremedial objective need not have a remedial endpoint that would be conceptually incompatible with its continuing educational purpose; the limiting circumstances are the actual tie and the particular integration interest, not an asserted right to maintain a racial fraction indefinitely.

**T4 — Federal/state remedy separation:** An affirmance leaves the reviewed judgment in place: the federal award of $134,014.62 for backpay, benefits and prejudgment interest; the $10,000 state-law emotional-suffering award; and restored seniority. It creates no new reinstatement order because Taxman was already rehired, and does not disturb the denials of a broader injunction or punitive damages. A reversal of federal liability does not itself reverse the distinct NJLAD judgment. On that path, remand is necessary to identify whether the parallel state claim independently sustains liability and which monetary/seniority relief remains justified; no assumption is made that every component is federal or that all existing relief disappears. All eight accept this separation; they differ on which federal judgment it implements.

### Enhanced alternative-path test

For affirmance, the strongest argument is that Title VII protects individuals from discharge because of race, and Weber/Johnson's departure from a literal prohibition is tied to overcoming employment exclusion. The existing job loss and absence of a remedial benchmark make this plan materially different from both precedents. The strongest objection is that those decisions expressly left outer boundaries open and did not hold that a good-faith educational integration choice between fully tied teachers must be forbidden. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas resolve that objection by declining a new statutory exception whose supposed limit would depend on the employer's desirable purpose rather than the statute's established remedial accommodation.

For reversal, the strongest support is the distinction between eliminating discrimination and forbidding every voluntary integration choice, together with the absence of any superior individual claim to the remaining position. Stevens's Johnson concurrence, 480 U.S. 642–647, expressly addresses forward-looking reasons, including workforce diversity; his Wygant dissent, 476 U.S. 313–320, specifically explains integrated public instruction and rejects an automatic constitutional hiring/layoff divide. The strongest objection is that a tenured employee suffers a concentrated loss and that the nonremedial purpose provides no temporal endpoint. The reversing Associates answer by restricting permission to the actual equal-qualification/equal-seniority educational tie, acknowledging the harm, and rejecting a perpetual quota or general benign-purpose license. They do not claim that Adarand has changed Title VII, that its congressional-competence rationale applies to this board, or that Stevens's earlier separate writings were already controlling law.

**No last-predicate revival.** A predicate committed within four years of suit does not restart the period for an earlier actionable injury or permit recovery of stale damages merely because it belongs to the same RICO pattern. **Normal baseline.** Alaska's statutory three-mile submerged-land grant is measured from the Convention's normal baseline, including the ordinary low-water line and its applicable river/bay closing rules and separate island belts. The supplied historical materials establish no firm and continuing general ten-mile island-fringe rule requiring enclosure of Stefansson Sound; the 1985 Alabama/Mississippi judgment does not preclude that conclusion.

California's adoption of Convention definitions supplies the baseline framework. Article 4 permits straight baselines in an appropriate island-fringe setting; it does not command the United States to choose them, and the national choice was not made here. The international twelve-mile sea does not enlarge the statutory three-mile State grant. Alaska's separate general-practice theory is considered on its own terms, not rejected for failure to prove a specific historic-title claim it does not make. The 1930 four-mile assimilation proposal, six-mile high-seas strait rule and ten-mile inland-sea/bay rule were materially different propositions. The Chapman Line, Webb letter, Gulf submissions and 1961 letter do not establish the proposed uniform rule. Oil-leasing convenience and avoidance of federal enclaves cannot supply it.

The 1985 Alabama/Mississippi decision resolved Mississippi Sound's particular historic inland-water status on specific assertions and foreign acquiescence; it did not necessarily decide the universal formulation Alaska proposes. Issue preclusion therefore fails even assuming it could apply here. Mendoza's general rule against nonmutual estoppel against the United States need not be expanded or given a new original-jurisdiction exception. The later Alabama/Mississippi and Massachusetts decrees retain their actual geographic force, and the Nome artificial-extension permit holding remains distinct. No general authority to contract existing state territory is decided.

**Dinkum Sands.** A naturally formed feature must ordinarily remain above the agreed mean-high-water datum to qualify as an island under Article 10(1). Dinkum Sands' recurring seasonal loss of elevation below that datum defeats island status and a separate three-mile grant here; isolated emergence does not create periodically appearing and disappearing island entitlement.

The Convention's drafting history distinguishes exceptional inundation of an otherwise enduring island from an ordinarily changing gravel-and-ice formation. The approximately nineteen-year mean-high-water datum already accounts for ordinary tidal variation. The joint monitoring study and subsequent measurements support the Master's finding of frequent below-datum conditions; the 1971 chart and 1979 map depended on the earlier survey, and visual emergence does not displace calibrated elevation evidence. Borax and the California decree support the datum, not an absolute physical-permanence condition. Ordinary accretion or erosion of an established island remains distinct. Article 11 supplies no alternate entitlement at these distances.

**Petroleum Reserve — six-Justice rule.** Congress may retain submerged lands in federal ownership for an appropriate public purpose if the governing instruments clearly include them and clearly establish intent to defeat future state title. The same demanding retention inquiry governs the disputed equal-footing and statutory territorial-sea lands; §1313(a)'s express-retention requirement cannot be replaced by a presumption favoring the Federal Government simply because the Submerged Lands Act is a federal grant.

Shively supplies Congress's Property Clause power for appropriate territorial public purposes; Utah Division and Montana require separate clear inclusion and state-title defeat, not geography alone. The 1923 order's ocean-side barrier description, small-lagoon closure and express three-mile qualification include the disputed submerged estate. Its purpose of securing petroleum resources for national defense would be defeated by excluding the submerged portions of the same deposits. Unlike Montana's unneeded riverbed or Utah Division's reservoir-control rationale, federal ownership of this estate performs the reservation's central work.

Section 11(b) preserves exclusive federal legislative authority over federally owned military lands at admission and specifically names the petroleum reserve. Read with the already defined estate, that congressional treatment confirms continued federal ownership and ratifies inclusion of its submerged lands. The provision is not rewritten as an express generic submerged-title clause, and federal regulatory jurisdiction alone would not establish ownership. The precise named-reserve recognition and petroleum ownership purpose supply the title conclusion. Because Congress could retain that estate at admission and confirmed it, the result does not require deciding the disputed independent reach of the Pickett Act. No broad Executive power to defeat future state title is announced.

**Petroleum objection of Scalia and Thomas.** The Submerged Lands Act is the operative grant for this post-1953 State, and its exception demands an express retention of submerged lands, in terms embracing the land under water. In their view, necessary inclusion inferred from an exterior boundary and resource purpose cannot replace that requirement; congressional ratification cannot cure an instrument lacking it. Section 11(b)'s legislative-jurisdiction and existing-ownership language does not expressly retain the submerged estate. They would sustain Alaska's exception. They do not extend this statutory analysis to States admitted before the Act or announce that Congress lacks constitutional power to retain submerged lands with adequate language.

**Arctic Range — six-Justice rule.** The 1957 application and the operative temporary-segregation regulation set the specifically described lands apart for the proposed wildlife refuge before Alaska entered the Union; §6(e)'s exclusion for lands withdrawn or otherwise set apart as wildlife refuges therefore retained those submerged lands at statehood. The application was not a completed final reservation, but the statute's broader alternative reaches this legally effective, purpose-specific segregation.

The low-water boundary, submerged coastal features and habitat justification establish inclusion separately from retention. Federal protection of the identified aquatic habitat supports that inclusion under Utah Division/Montana's demanding test. The regulation segregates the lands from the enumerated forms of disposal only to the extent the proposed reservation, if effected, would bar them; it also preserves administrative jurisdiction. ## Competing statutory propositions

**A — Remedial boundary sufficient for affirmance.** The voluntary accommodation recognized by Weber and Johnson does not authorize this concededly nonremedial race-selected discharge merely to preserve department diversity. A manifest imbalance in a traditionally segregated job category can support voluntary action without proof of the employer's own actionable discrimination. That premise is absent here. The constitutional availability of a supported interest in a different setting does not supply the distinct statutory authorization. This conclusion does not hold that educational diversity is inherently illegitimate, erase Hopwood, require a prior judicial discrimination finding in every affirmative-action case, or decide every constitutional faculty-diversity issue.

**B — Actual burden independently sufficient for affirmance.** Selecting an existing tenured employee for layoff because of race under this nonremedial policy, without its having a remedial limiting goal, unnecessarily trammels the interests Title VII protects. A necessary staff reduction and an equal tie do not make loss of an existing job an immaterial burden; the prior nonracial tie procedure could allocate the necessary loss without racial selection. This case-specific conclusion does not decide every remedial layoff plan, every hiring preference or a categorical constitutional rule about layoffs. Hopwood's distinction between opportunity injury and eventual success also defeats the proposition that absence of superior entitlement means absence of an individual interest, although it does not itself decide the statutory trammeling issue.

**C — Nonremedial purpose is not categorically disqualifying.** Weber and Johnson do not make their sufficient remedial premises the exclusive permissible purposes of every voluntary action. A public educational employer may offer a genuinely supported educational-integration purpose under Title VII even without alleging its own past discrimination or remedial imbalance. The statutory accommodation still requires examination of the actual practice, its connection to that educational purpose and its effect on the protected employment interests. An assertion that retaining a Black teacher adds racial diversity is not, by itself, a finding of the concrete educational benefit or a sufficient justification for selecting another teacher for discharge. Conversely, a properly supported integration interest is not rejected solely because it looks forward rather than remedies an identified employment wrong. Hopwood supports this distinction by analogy; C is a proposed Title VII construction, not a declaration that Hopwood already decided it.

**D — The equal tie defeats an automatic burden rule but does not establish lawfulness.** Actual equality of qualifications and seniority, the necessity of eliminating one position and preservation of the department's sole Black teacher are relevant to purpose, fit and the affected interests. They permit distinguishing this case from displacing a better-qualified or more-senior worker, imposing a rigid numerical quota, or barring a group from employment. They do not establish that Taxman had no protected interest or that the layoff imposed no burden. The proper statutory inquiry must consider the serious loss of an existing tenured position, the racial basis of the selection, the nonracial tie alternative, what educational benefit the actual decision seeks, and whether the practice is a confined integration measure or racial representation maintained for its own sake. These are applications of the existing purpose-and-burden inquiry, not an invented numerical score, a constitutional strict-scrutiny test transplanted wholesale into Title VII, or a newly prescribed evidentiary ritual.

### Alternative-path and remedy validation

**Affirmance under A:** The strongest challenge is that Weber/Johnson's sufficient remedial settings do not necessarily exhaust their permissible applications, particularly after this Court has recognized a genuinely supported educational objective in Hopwood. The affirming four answer with the statute's individual prohibition and the established remedial accommodation; they do not misstate Hopwood. The vacating four reject that exclusive-purpose conclusion as a construction of Title VII, not because Hopwood already enacted a faculty-employment exception.

**Affirmance under B alone:** The strongest ground is the concrete loss of an existing tenured position selected by race, with an available nonracial tie procedure and no remedial limiting goal. Each affirming Justice treats it as independently sufficient, so an opinion may reserve the outer purpose question. The strongest objection is the exact qualification/seniority tie and the possibility that a confined educational objective affects whether the burden is unnecessary. Each vacating Justice treats that as requiring the actual statutory inquiry, while conceding the job loss is serious and that a tie does not erase the protected interest. No vote for C automatically supplies a vote for a no-trammeling finding.

**Vacatur and remand under C–D/F:** The strongest challenge is that the court below already addressed burden and that Hopwood reserved employment. This route does not reopen facts simply because a different outcome is possible. It rejects the dispositive statutory reach of the lower grounds and directs application of the construction the vacating Justices actually adopt. It may preserve the existing judgment on a proper application; it does not instruct the lower courts to grant the Board an educational safe harbor or presume an evidentiary omission. Its source is a supported Title VII interpretation informed by, and carefully distinguished from, the Justices' own Hopwood positions.

**Immediate reversal of federal liability:** This route has the original handoff's direct educational-tie argument in its favor. It fails the refreshed commitments' complete support test because possible permission, an actual equality of competing employees and a stated educational aim do not themselves establish that the particular layoff satisfies the proposed statutory rule. All four vacating Justices now reserve that application; none remains committed to a present declaration that the Board acted lawfully. This is a change in their modeled judgment position as well as in the certainty of an application, and must be carried into assembly explicitly.

**Constitutional and remedial substitutions:** No Associate joins constitutional merits adjudication, automatic transplantation of strict scrutiny, a presumed Fordice duty, the transfer of Hopwood's Title VI intent rule into a complete Title VII permission analysis, or an admissions same-decision burden. Neither path creates a punitive award, broader injunction, new reinstatement or extra monetary sum.
