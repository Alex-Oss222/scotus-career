### Issue 1: Asserted fundamental right to physician-assisted suicide

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** No simulated holding recognizes a fundamental right to obtain intentional assistance in death. Pre-divergence Cruzan recognizes the traditional bodily-integrity interest and assumes a competent refusal right for its disposition, while sustaining Missouri's evidentiary safeguard for an incompetent patient's prior wishes. Romer v. Evans addresses status-based political disability and does not establish the asserted substantive liberty.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No registered assisted-suicide doctrine controls. Identify the asserted liberty from the actual claim and governing sources, considering text, structure, precedent and tradition without importing the historical Glucksberg decision as an already controlling methodology. The actual simulated Casey must be addressed at its own scope.
- **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; Washington's homicide and medical-practice law; surviving principles of bodily integrity, treatment refusal, and state authority over medicine.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

Simulated Casey (June 29, 1992) preserves the patient's protected reproductive decision, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health care, and replaces the trimester timetable with its complete six-step previability framework. Those adopted holdings defeat reliance on historical Casey language as an unlimited personal-autonomy rule, but also prevent importing historical Glucksberg's particular history-and-tradition formulation backward as an already binding general method. The present liberty claim needs its own source-grounded analysis and appropriate level of description. Cruzan recognizes the traditional bodily-integrity interest, assumed for that disposition a competent person's constitutional right to refuse life-sustaining nutrition/hydration, and upheld a clear-and-convincing safeguard for an incompetent patient's wishes; it is not a categorical holding that every medical assistance causing death is identical. The plaintiffs seek protection for willing physician assistance, not compelled service or government financing. Romer supplies an equality rule and no assisted-suicide substantive right.


## C. LITIGATION POSITIONS AND LAWFUL PATHS

### Litigant positions and lawful alternatives

Washington defends the prohibition as protection of life, vulnerable patients and professional medical practice, emphasizing diagnostic error, coercion and difficulty policing the proposed terminal-illness boundary. The physicians and patients assert a liberty interest for competent terminally ill adults seeking willing medical assistance to control the manner and timing of death, drawing on treatment-refusal and bodily-autonomy principles; the asserted claim does not demand a public subsidy or compel an unwilling physician. The Court must identify the claimed liberty at the relevant level, consider the pre-divergence treatment-refusal decisions and actual simulated Casey without assuming either resolves assisted suicide, and address the prohibition's operation and asserted safeguards. Available paths include sustaining the law against this claim, recognizing a bounded right and evaluating the statute under the resulting standard, or resolving a demonstrated narrower application if the record and preservation permit it. Any remand must identify what remains for decision; a general autonomy statement or a list of conceivable risks alone does not define the asserted right.
