## 2. United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder

**Identity and posture:** Review of the Ninth Circuit's judgment at 95 F.3d 787, lower appellate Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513, reversing separate indictment dismissals and returning both prosecutions. No new Supreme Court docket number is assigned. These are not convictions, and neither guilt nor a sentence is under review. Each defendant seeks restoration of his own dismissal; the United States seeks to preserve each trial remand.

### Components and sufficient proposed grounds

**M1 — Actual offense and common constitutional question.** Section 228(a), as enacted October 25, 1992, punishes willful failure to pay a past-due support obligation with respect to a child residing in another State. Section 228(d)(1) requires an amount fixed by a court order or State-law administrative process for support of a child, or of a child and the custodial parent, which has remained unpaid longer than one year **or** exceeds $5,000. Those are alternatives, although these indictments allege both. Willfulness and the different-state condition remain elements; the word debt, the amount, or the duration alone is not a constitutional substitute for the required interstate relation.

Both January 11, 1995 indictments are included. Mussari's concerns two Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning November 1, 1988; Schroeder's concerns four Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning February 1, 1992. The Court need not resolve the one-day discrepancy in the sources describing the initial District Court dismissal to review the identified appellate judgment. It finds no ability to pay, deliberate evasion, actual interstate travel, flight, guilt, specific arrears balance, or defect in a State support order. Constitutional review proceeds on the charged class and preserved question, leaving statutory proof and lawful defenses to each prosecution.

**M2 — Direct protection of these interstate payment obligations.** All eight Associates would sustain the statute against the presented Commerce Clause objection and affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of each constitutional dismissal. Their common sufficient ground is confined to the legally fixed private payment obligation for support of children residing across State lines. Section 228 addresses willful nonpayment of that cross-border economic obligation itself. It does not seek federal power through a chain from local family conflict to poverty, welfare expenditures, crime, or national productivity. Its different-state condition does actual work in delimiting the cross-border payment relation. A transfer required by an existing support obligation does not cease to be an economic payment because State family law created it rather than a commercial bargain.

Lopez, April 26, 1995, rejects a remote effects chain for stand-alone school-zone possession and forbids inserting a missing commerce element. It does not exclude every regulation touching family life or every payment lacking a negotiated exchange. Harris, April 27, 1995, and Bishop/Stokes, February 5, 1996, sustain their actual economic vehicle-taking class while requiring the enacted vehicle element; Hale, February 12, 1996, sustains possession integral to direct market regulation. They establish a need to identify the actual regulated object and relation, not a rule that every thing with interstate history or every debt is automatically commerce. Here the object is the due cross-border payment obligation, not a locally possessed object with remote travel or hypothetical downstream economic effects.

The common ground permits protection of the specified interstate payment obligation without finding an actual completed transfer. Withholding the required payment is the conduct Congress addresses; requiring that the money already have crossed State lines would not follow from this offense. The Court adds no flight, voluntary-crossing, bank-wire, particular payment-instrument, or motive-to-evade element. It neither constitutionalizes all unpaid family obligations nor establishes federal power to create or revise the underlying support duty. The different-state condition is necessary to this rationale; no sufficiency judgment is made about a statute lacking it or about an unrelated debt with an incidental geographical feature. The opinion should not adopt an unqualified formula that every obligation is a thing in interstate commerce merely because it is called a debt.

**M3 — Private regulation, State law, and domestic relations.** All eight reject a separate Tenth Amendment or categorical domestic-relations ground for these dismissals. The federal statute directs the private obligor to satisfy an existing obligation; it does not command a State to legislate, enforce the debt, investigate for federal officers, alter an order, or assume the obligor's liability. New York preserves direct federal regulation within enumerated power while forbidding the compulsory State responsibility it addressed. Printz's qualified executive-assistance rule is neither needed to authorize this private duty nor a reason to invent a State fiscal issue.

Ankenbrandt, June 15, 1992, confines the statutory diversity exception to issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees and rejects a general Article III domestic-relations exclusion. This federal criminal prosecution issues no such decree and treats the existing support obligation as a predicate. Its federal character must still rest on the sufficient commerce ground in M2; merely showing an absence of commandeering does not establish enumerated power. Conversely, the State-law origin of the obligation does not defeat direct federal enforcement within that power. The Court does not reopen custody, recalculate support, adjudicate a modification, prescribe future State enforcement machinery, or resolve a hypothetical interference with a different State proceeding. A lawful defense bearing on an actual statutory predicate remains available.

**M4 — Temporal limit, distinct prosecution proof, and restitution reservation.** Both alleged periods begin before enactment. Federal conviction and punishment must concern willful failures after the October 25, 1992 enactment, when all applicable elements are satisfied. Completed pre-enactment nonpayment cannot itself become a federal offense. Earlier arrears may identify the fixed obligation and supply lawful background or past-due status; that use is not a judgment that the earlier omission was criminal under a statute not yet enacted. The Ex Post Facto Clause independently supplies the criminal limit. Landgraf and Rivers reinforce attention to the legal consequence attached to completed conduct, but their civil rules do not displace the independent criminal prohibition.

The common constitutional decision establishes neither defendant's willfulness, capacity to pay, different-state residence for the necessary period, fixed qualifying amount, unpaid duration, nor any sentencing fact. The return permits ordinary proceedings on each indictment subject to applicable statutory, temporal and procedural constraints. It directs no new allegation, amended indictment, conviction, particular trial instruction beyond the legal limit stated, or new prosecution for a period not charged. No comprehensive continuing-offense theory is adopted to eliminate a separate proof or notice issue.

Section 228(c) separately requires restitution at sentencing equal to the then-existing past-due obligation. This pretrial decision fixes no restitution amount and does not decide the precise lawful treatment of older arrears in an eventual restitution order. It would be error either to exclude all older arrears automatically because no pre-enactment omission can be punished as the offense, or to approve their inclusion automatically without the separate statutory and constitutional inquiry. First-offense exposure is a fine, imprisonment up to six months, or both; a subsequent offense permits a fine, imprisonment up to two years, or both. Nothing here finds a prior offense or selects a sentence.

**M5 — Disposition in both dockets.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the constitutional dismissals and its remand for ordinary further proceedings in each separate prosecution, subject to M4. Mussari's dismissal is not restored; Schroeder's dismissal is not restored. The judgment adjudicates the presented constitutional objections, not the sufficiency of all future proof or every possible defense. No detention, conviction, restitution award, State-order revision, compensation proceeding or compulsory State administrative act is ordered.

### Contrary paths and narrower or broader alternatives

**Rejecting the interstate-payment classification.** The strongest rival treats the underlying act as failure to perform a noncommercial family duty. Different residence, on that view, is a jurisdictional label that cannot change the activity's character, and the district court's rejection of AFDC or general poverty effects follows Lopez. This path is legally supported by Lopez's demand for a real commercial connection and by the fact that Harris and Hale concerned identified economic/market classes. It would reverse the Ninth Circuit on the commerce question and restore each constitutional dismissal without finding either defendant innocent on the facts.

The eight Associates reject that path here because the law reaches an existing monetary obligation with a current cross-border payment relation, rather than deriving commerce from family hardship. Their ground does not depend on AFDC effects, moral disapproval, or the fact that all conduct might influence the economy. The relevant distinction is the obligation's legally required interstate satisfaction, as presented in these charged different-state relationships. Their ruling is confined to that class and retains every statutory element. No separate broad effects rationale is added as an independently sufficient holding.

**Saving construction through travel, evasion or payment method.** A narrower offense requiring flight, deliberate State crossing, or an actual interstate instrument might present an additional connection. But those conditions are not enacted and the record establishes none. Lopez's no-rewriting rule forecloses supplying them as the basis for this judgment. The enacted willfulness condition remains real; it is not replaced with a finding of interstate evasion.

**A categorical family-law exclusion or an automatic Printz analogy.** Ankenbrandt and New York support distinguishing decrees and State administration, not forbidding all direct federal regulation affecting families. Printz does not hold that every valid federal crime may recruit State officers, and these prosecutions require no such conclusion. Neither a categorical exception nor a fiscal remand follows.

**An immediate guilt or restitution disposition.** The statutory threshold and constitutional power do not establish the charged facts. A decision sustaining the statute therefore returns each existing prosecution rather than entering convictions or calculating arrears. The offense-date limit and older-arrears restitution question remain distinct. These narrower limits are essential to the common join, not optional observations.

## 3. In re Cohen

**Identity and posture:** Review under the approved §1254(1) appellate route of the Third Circuit's February 6, 1997 judgment, 106 F.3d 52, lower appellate No. 96-5155. No separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied. The existing bankruptcy judgment includes $94,147.50 in treble damages based on $31,382.50 in fraudulent overcharges and an existing award of attorney's fees and costs whose separate amount is not supplied. The Court is reviewing that adjudicated liability and its discharge status, not assessing new damages.

### Components and sufficient proposed grounds

**C1 — Federal fraud remains a necessary predicate.** All eight apply Field v. Mans, November 28, 1995. The actual-fraud theory requires actual and justifiable reliance under the ordinary preponderance burden. Justifiability depends on the recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances. No universal duty to investigate the rent ordinance applies; personally known or obvious falsity and actually encountered warning signs remain relevant. Objective imprudence may bear on whether reliance occurred, but does not become a universal prudent-person condition. A State consumer-fraud label or statutory enhancement alone establishes none of these federal elements.

The supplied record contains federal fraud findings upheld on ordinary factual/legal review, including deliberate avoidance of the rent limits and selective knowledge of favorable provisions. Those findings are not converted into new Supreme Court factual findings. Limited tenant knowledge of the ordinance does not by itself defeat justifiable reliance. The reviewed debt-extent question may be decided on the sustained federal fraud predicate; the packet identifies no supported, material Field error requiring a new factual proceeding. The underlying findings remain subject to the ordinary scope of review and preservation, rather than being declared immune because a State statute supplied damages. No trial fact, warning, investigation, representation or missing finding is invented, and no exploratory fraud remand is ordered.

Field's scope is retained. Subsection 523(a)(2)(A) excludes a statement respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition. Subsection (B) separately requires a materially false writing of that kind, reasonable reliance, and the debtor's causing its making or publication with intent to deceive. Nothing supplied makes this a financial-statement case. The decision does not classify every possible false-pretense or representation-only claim, displace subsection (B), or presume actual fraud whenever money was retained improperly.

**C2 — The nondischargeable amount is the valid liability for the fraudulent acquisition.** All eight would hold that when money or property is obtained by the fraud within §523(a)(2)(A), the qualifying debt includes the legally enforceable liability arising from that fraudulent acquisition, rather than only the net value the debtor received. The Code defines debt through liability on a claim. The phrase concerning the extent obtained by fraud identifies the fraudulent acquisition giving rise to the debt and separates it from lawful acquisitions; it does not impose a restitution-only ceiling on the consequences of that acquisition.

This reading gives work to both debt and obtained by. There must be a qualifying acquisition of money or property and a sufficient connection between that acquisition and the liability declared nondischargeable. It does not make every obligation of a dishonest debtor nondischargeable. Here the identified overcharged rents, not all lawful rent or every landlord obligation, supply that acquisition. An enforceable enhancement imposed for those fraudulent receipts is a liability for them even though the debtor did not separately obtain the dollars represented by the enhancement. State law determines the valid liability; federal law determines whether it falls within this discharge category. The Court creates neither a new State cause nor a new federal damages remedy.

Field deliberately left debt extent and the obtained-by relationship separate. It therefore supplies the fraud prerequisite, not an already settled result here. Noland, May 13, 1996, requires observance of the Code's actual categories rather than a judicial preference about punitive obligations; it concerns priority and equitable subordination and does not itself settle §523(a)(2)(A). The present result rests on this exception's text and structure. Neither a generalized fresh start nor generalized hostility to fraud can replace that construction.

**C3 — Existing treble liability and conditional punitive characterization.** All eight would affirm nondischargeability of the existing $94,147.50 treble judgment for the $31,382.50 fraudulent acquisition. Even assuming for decision that the enhancement is wholly punitive, that characterization does not remove a valid liability for the qualifying fraud from §523(a)(2)(A). The Court does not find it wholly punitive as a matter of New Jersey law; the lower opinion used that assumption, while Cox v. Sears identified a partly compensatory purpose. No need to settle that State-law characterization arises because the federal result is the same under the stated assumption.

This is not a general nondischargeability rule for penalties. A penalty arising from unrelated conduct, a different statutory category, or an obligation lacking the qualifying fraudulent-acquisition nexus is outside this holding. Section 523(a)(4) and (6) cover distinct wrongs and need not use the same acquisition language; their broader formulations do not compel a cap on liability in (a)(2)(A) once its acquisition requirement is satisfied. The Court neither collapses those categories nor decides their independent elements.

**C4 — Existing attorney's fees and costs.** All eight would hold the existing valid State-law fee and cost obligations nondischargeable insofar as they are imposed because of, and form part of the liability for, this qualifying fraud. No separate fee or cost amount is supplied, and none is calculated or added. The Court does not award Supreme Court fees, new appellate fees, future litigation costs, interest, or an enlarged enhancement. Fees unrelated to the qualifying fraudulent acquisition are not covered merely because the same debtor owes them. A valid underlying obligation and its fraud connection remain necessary; a federal discharge ruling does not create State entitlement to fees.

**C5 — Judgment and scope.** Affirm the Third Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains the existing full fraud liability's nondischargeability, including the adjudged treble damages and existing fee/cost award at the scope of C1–C4. No new debt is assessed, the amount already obtained is not confused with the treble total, and no recalculation or additional hearing is ordered. The disposition leaves ordinary enforcement of the existing nondischargeable judgment to the proper proceedings; it does not set collection terms, distribution priority, a payment schedule or a new liability against another person.

### Competing constructions and supported alternatives

**Acquired-value ceiling.** The strongest rival concedes that the modifier concerning what was obtained attaches to the acquired money/property rather than directly to the word debt, yet argues that the resulting exception still reaches only an obligation representing what the debtor actually obtained. An enhancement or litigation expense was not received by the debtor; the acquisition words must impose a limit different from the broader debt-for-wrong language in §523(a)(4) and (6). Fresh-start policy favors confining an exception where the text reasonably permits it. This is the Greenberg construction preserved in the neutral packet, not a caricature based solely on a modifier's nearest noun. It would affirm nondischargeability of the qualifying acquired value and reverse the larger extent determination, with any necessary confined allocation of the existing award; it would not itself erase the underlying State liability.

The eight reject that construction because it turns the fraudulent-acquisition limitation into a second definition of debt as restitution, although debt remains the liability legally arising from the claim. The difference from (a)(4) and (6) is that (a)(2)(A) requires this kind of acquisition. A liability can be for that acquisition without each consequence being a second acquisition. The phrase concerning extent still prevents treatment of lawful rent or unrelated liabilities as fraud debt. The 1984 placement of the phrase supplies no clear direction to discharge the nonrestitution consequences of the very fraudulent acquisition Congress excepts; the conclusion does not depend on a policy-based expansion of every fraud-related claim. Legislative history may confirm that conclusion but is unnecessary to the common sufficient textual ground.

**Automatic punitive-debt exclusion.** Noland's insistence on enacted categories and the precise discharge text defeat a general judicial choice to discharge an enhancement simply because it is noncompensatory. That does not turn Noland into a holding on fraud discharge or decide the enhancement's State-law character. The assumed wholly punitive enhancement still belongs to the existing debt for the qualifying acquisition.

**A federal-fraud remand.** A supported, material and preserved reliance or other federal-fraud defect would require its ordinary disposition under Field. The supplied record does not establish such a defect. No Justice chooses a remand merely because more record material could theoretically exist. The affirmance relies on the existing sustained federal fraud predicate at the actual review scope, not a presumption that consumer-fraud trebling necessarily satisfies §523 or a finding that all possible factual objections were waived.

**An expansion to any consequence of any fraud.** The alternative at the other extreme would except all debts associated with the debtor's dishonesty, regardless of an acquisition within (a)(2)(A). The eight reject that expansion. The qualifying acquisition, valid liability, and causal/statutory nexus remain necessary. The financial-condition exclusion and subsection (B)'s distinct requirements are not bypassed; no claim outside the supplied fraud acquisition is decided.
