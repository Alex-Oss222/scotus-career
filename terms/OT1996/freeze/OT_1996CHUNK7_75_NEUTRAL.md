## Questions, posture and facts

The grant concerns Congress's authority to prohibit knowing interstate commercial transmissions procuring interests in another State's lottery, despite the physical tickets remaining in that other State and the underlying purchases being lawful there. The express interstate-agreement exception and the relation between affirmative commerce authority and dormant-Commerce limits are distinct issues. Pic-A-State seeks declaratory and prospective injunctive relief; the United States seeks affirmance of dismissal. Standing, ripeness and the proper equitable channel remain threshold inquiries, rather than facts settled by the brief's assertion that they are satisfied.

Pic-A-State took Pennsylvania orders for interests in other States' lottery tickets. Agents purchased and retained tickets in their State of origin; computer transactions transferred receipts and procurement information. The 1994 amendment specifically targeted this mode of business, which had avoided the earlier physical-ticket prohibition. Pic-A-State brought suit three days after enactment and ceased the challenged business. The lower court reports it would resume if the amendment were invalidated. No federal prosecution is pending. The Government said federal prosecution was unlikely and suggested Pennsylvania would prosecute under its own law; it did **not** expressly disavow federal prosecution.

Pennsylvania had enacted Act 8 of 1993 against interests in other States' lotteries. A district court invalidated that statute; the Third Circuit reversed after the federal amendment, treating Congress's permission as removing the dormant-Commerce objection. That earlier lower litigation explains the business and remedy setting, but is not an entered Supreme Court decision and does not automatically resolve this facial federal challenge or any later prosecution.

The court below found adversity from cessation and credible criminal exposure, fitness from the predominantly legal question, and utility because a declaration would affect actual plans. It separately rejected standing and equitable objections; Younger concerned no pending state prosecution here. On the merits it reasoned that interstate information transactions and the lottery market were commercial, Congress could rationally preserve revenues, accommodate state gambling choices and close the physical-ticket gap, and Congress could authorize otherwise impermissible state commerce discrimination. Its description of historical Lopez is not imported as the law of this Court: the actual inherited Lopez controls.

## Statutory law in terms

The authoritative 1994 Code text and its amendment note were fetched from GPO and saved in `sources/chunk7-neutral-a/USC18_1301_1994.txt`. The complete operative §1301 and §1307 text is mechanically copied to the end of the entering-law slice. Section 1301 targets a person **engaged in the business** of procuring for a person in one State a ticket, chance, share or interest in a lottery or similar scheme **conducted by another State**, who **knowingly transmits in interstate or foreign commerce information for procurement**. It excepts a business permitted by agreement between the States in question **or appropriate authorities of those States**. The enacted physical-ticket carriage and receipt prohibitions remain distinct. The operative fine is **fined under this title**, plus imprisonment up to two years or both; the lower quotation's former $1,000 ceiling is not the governing amended penalty.

Section 1307 has distinct exemptions: specified publications and broadcasts about state-conducted lotteries; qualifying authorized/not-prohibited nonprofit, governmental, or clearly occasional and ancillary commercial promotional schemes; transportation or mailing to addresses within the conducting State; and qualifying foreign-country lottery materials to foreign addressees. It defines State, foreign country, and lottery and excludes sporting wagers from the subsection-(b) lottery definition. Do not replace those categories with an assertion that all lawful state lottery business is exempt, or replace §1301's agreement exception with §1307's different conditions. No source establishes that this procurement business fits an exemption.

## Entering law and balanced alternatives

Use [the exact slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK7_75_ENTERING_LAW.md). The operative register is **Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects**, whose present operation includes Hale, February 12, 1996, and American Life League, November 13, 1995; the Lopez and Harris components retain April 26 and 27, 1995 dates. It preserves economic-class and direct-market regulation, rejects a stand-alone local-possession/social-cost chain, and does not create an exhaustive commerce formula or universal requirement of transaction-specific findings.

Actual Lopez's five-Justice holding cannot be replaced by an argument that all noneconomic local activity is exempt. Harris's eight-Justice economic vehicle-taking holding and unanimous Bishop/Stokes application preserve genuine commercial-class grounds; unanimous Hale permits possession integral to a direct transfer/stock regime without a transaction-specific element. Robertson unanimously treats an enterprise's actual cross-border procurement, labor and output operation as direct commerce, without requiring a separate substantial effect from each enterprise. These analogies do not mechanically decide lottery procurement, but the enacted interstate transmission is material rather than merely an object's remote history.

Champion v. Ames supplies federal authority to prohibit interstate lottery-ticket commerce; Gibbons and Hodel address the scope and rational exercise of enumerated commerce power. Prudential v. Benjamin and Northeast Bancorp permit clear congressional authorization of state burdens otherwise limited by dormant-Commerce doctrine; they do not authorize violations of independent constitutional rights. Current Tracy separates its actual discrimination comparison from ordinary economic rationality and expressly decided no congressional-authorization theory. Bennett, March 19, 1997, unanimously preserves concrete economic injury, stage-appropriate proof, traceability and likely redress rather than automatic access for every economically affected party. Its distinct ESA/APA route is not an element of this suit.

Supported paths and strongest objections:

- Reach the facial commerce question because the statute targets existing conduct, cessation is concrete, resumed operations bring serious penalties, and the Government has not disavowed enforcement. Contrary contention: cessation may be voluntary and prosecution speculative; assess the particular targeted enactment and stated resumption rather than assuming enforcement certainty. With no present state prosecution, ordinary Younger abstention does not automatically apply.
- Uphold direct regulation of interstate commercial procurement communications and the agreement exception as part of an authorized federal rule accommodating state choices. Strongest objection: the government cannot merely relabel purely local lottery sales commercial and let protection of favored state revenue answer the enumerated-power question. The precise regulated transmission and business context must supply the connection.
- Uphold on the distinct economic-market/integral-scheme rationale, even if the physical purchase is intrastate. Strongest objection: aggregation must identify the actual regulated economic class and its relation to the interstate lottery regime; generalized effects on public programs or productivity alone are insufficient under Lopez.
- Invalidate or narrow if the enacted category is construed to reach transactions lacking the required actual commerce relationship, or if the state-agreement mechanism lacks the clear congressional authorization asserted for discriminatory state action. The strongest counterargument is the text's express knowing interstate transmission by a procurement business, Champion's established prohibition power, and the express agreement condition. Do not create a missing interstate element or sever an exception without establishing why the lawful constructions fail.

State authorization of the underlying lottery does not itself confer federal immunity. Congress's affirmative regulation and permission for particular state choices must remain separate from a free-standing state restriction. The litigation presents no independent First Amendment, equal-protection or nondelegation claim established for decision. If relief is warranted, define it against this federal provision and its proper officers; do not adjudicate every state lottery law or hypothetical prosecution.
