## 61. Ingebretsen

### Supported source facts and posture

The official Fifth Circuit January 10, 1996 opinion was downloaded and read in full: https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/94/94-60631.CV0.wpd.pdf, saved as `tmp/OT1996CHUNK6_A_sources/Ingebretsen_official.pdf` and `.txt`. It supports the preliminary injunction, the graduation exception, the preimplementation challenge, the two named school witnesses and both intervention components. Its references to historical postdivergence Supreme Court law are descriptions of the lower court's reasoning, not additional in-world authority; actual Lee/Pinette and other Holdings govern.

The early hearing held intervention in abeyance and allowed amicus argument, but the appealed orders ultimately denied intervention both as of right and permissively. The as-of-right denial rested solely on adequate representation; the permissive denial rested on absence of a new issue and resulting delay. The appeal therefore cannot be disposed of as if the only action were a still-pending initial abeyance. The lower opinion says the Attorney General actually asserted the students' free-exercise/free-speech interests and that the proposed intervenors identified no right the Attorney General omitted. Those are the published lower-court account and litigation characterization, not an irrebuttable legal rule that an Attorney General always represents private parties adequately.

The district court heard the interim superintendent and a principal, and the published account links prospective injury to implementation at school events and selection/content-screening responsibilities. Actual completed coercion or punishment was not found: the statute was enjoined before implementation. The panel's prediction that implementation would inevitably involve school officials is a judicial inference disputed by the rehearing dissent; it must not be relabeled as actual historical implementation. The injunction was against the statute, not a prohibition of all independent private prayer. The graduation exception invoked Jones II's student-choice, student-delivery, nonsectarian/nonproselytizing conditions; no blanket approval of every graduation prayer follows from the lower judgment.
