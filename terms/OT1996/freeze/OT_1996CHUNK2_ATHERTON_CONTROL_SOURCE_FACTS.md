## Supported facts and assertions, kept distinct

1. The judgment below is the Third Circuit's interlocutory ruling under §1292(b), reported as Resolution Trust Corp. v. CityFed Financial Corp., 57 F.3d 1231 (1995). The appellate opinion combined the federally chartered City Federal litigation with unrelated litigation involving state-chartered United Savings. The United Savings judgment is not itself an additional Supreme Court matter in this packet.
2. City Federal's assets and claims passed through successor institutions to City Savings, F.S.B. RTC sued in its receiver capacity. FDIC succeeded RTC as receiver when RTC terminated on December 31, 1995. Sources: FDIC statement pp. 2–3, 6–7, citing 12 U.S.C. §§1441a(m)(1), (b)(4)(A).
3. City Federal was declared insolvent December 7, 1989. Claims concern consideration, approval and oversight of acquisition, development and construction loans in the mid-to-late 1980s. The receiver alleged losses exceeding $100 million. These are allegations, not findings of liability. The lower opinion does not allege self-dealing, conflict of interest, bad faith or fraud in the City Federal case. Sources: petition appendix A11–A12; petitioner brief pp. 4–5; FDIC statement p. 3.
4. The initial and first amended complaints included state and federal common-law negligence, gross negligence and fiduciary-duty theories. The parties assumed federal chartering required federal law. The District Court dismissed claims beyond §1821(k), treated RTC's state-law claims as withdrawn and allowed an amended statutory gross-negligence pleading. The receiver appealed the federal-common-law dismissal. Petitioner says the withdrawal ruling was not appealed. Sources: petitioner statement pp. 5–6; FDIC statement pp. 3–4. Do not treat rejection of federal common law as automatic reinstatement or adjudication of a preserved state claim.
5. The Third Circuit reversed the City Federal dismissal and directed that the receiver could pursue available federal-common-law negligence and fiduciary-duty claims. It **reserved the content of the federal standard**, including whether ordinary or gross negligence governed (appendix A32 n.16). The supplied neutral packet's assertion that the Third Circuit simply applied an ordinary-negligence standard is therefore incomplete. Sources: FDIC brief pp. 5–6; petitioner brief p. 8; lower opinion n.16.
6. The Third Circuit also concluded §1821(k) did not apply to federally chartered institutions, and that the receiver could not proceed under it in City Federal (appendix A37 n.17). Both parties challenge that exclusion: FDIC note 4 expressly agrees that the text encompasses state and federally chartered insured institutions. This agreement does not resolve whether federal common law survives or what state-law claims remain available.
7. Judge Mansmann agreed that stricter state-law claims survived for state-chartered institutions, but dissented from preservation of federal common law; she read the statute as supplying the exclusive gross-negligence standard in federal-charter receiver suits. Sources: lower opinion appendix A38–A50; the parties' merits statements.
8. The petition's questions primarily concern whether §1821(k) supplants federal common law and supplies an exclusive receiver standard, and whether the Third Circuit could exclude federal institutions from the section. On the merits petitioner also argues no federal common-law duty rule existed in the first place and state law governs; FDIC says that broader antecedent issue was not questioned in the petition and need not be reached, while briefing its merits. Petitioner reply expressly contests that preservation objection. This is a legal-scope dispute, not a factual default or concession supplied by the Engine.
