# 119. Fletcher v. Kalina

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL LOWER-COURT COMPARATOR

The Ninth Circuit affirmed denial of absolute immunity, treating the warrant declaration as outside protected advocacy under *Malley* and *Buckley*. It did not finally resolve the qualified-immunity dispute or damages.

This records the historical lower-court disposition only. It does not establish a completed simulated Supreme Court outcome or supply governing post-divergence Supreme Court law.

[Primary opinion](https://openjurist.org/93/f3d/653/fletcher-v-kalina)

## B. LATER HISTORICAL SUPREME COURT COMPARATOR — OUTSIDE GOVERNING SIMULATION LAW

On December 10, 1997, the historical Supreme Court unanimously affirmed in Kalina v. Fletcher, 522 U.S. 118, No. 96-792. It distinguished protected preparation and presentation of charging documents from personally attesting to facts: the oath performed a witness function and lacked absolute prosecutorial immunity.

[Historical Supreme Court opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/522/118/). This subsequent historical result supplies no simulated holding, vote, or effective precedent for this packet.

# 122. Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

The Ninth Circuit affirmed the judgment on September 13, 1996. It historically granted panel rehearing on June 26, 1997, and later left the opinion unchanged on October 28, 1997; those steps are displaced by the scenario's express appellate-abeyance setup. [Rehearing order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/118/660/587277/). The Supreme Court subsequently affirmed on May 24, 1999, in No. 97-1235, 526 U.S. 687. That later decision is a historical comparator only, not entering simulation law. [Historical Supreme Court opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/526/687/).

# 123. Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp.

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 9-0.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Breyer delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the Chief Justice and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg. Justices Scalia and Thomas joined Part III and concurred in part and in the judgment, urging adoption of the Clayton Act injury-accrual rule.
- **Historical Holding:** The last-predicate-act rule is invalid because a later predicate does not revive recovery for old injuries. Fraudulent concealment requires reasonable diligence. The Court left unresolved the choice among the remaining civil RICO accrual rules.
- **Historical Authority:** Opinion of the Court for the last-predicate-act and diligence holdings; judgment only as to the unresolved broader accrual rule.
- **Historical Sources:** Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp., 521 U.S. 179 (1997).

- **Official comparator source:** [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/521bv.pdf#page=208).

# 126. United States v. Alaska

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The Court overruled Alaska’s exceptions, sustained the United States’ exception, and adopted the Special Master’s recommendations to the extent consistent with the opinion.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O’Connor delivered the opinion. Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer joined all parts. Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Scalia and Thomas joined Parts I, II, and III. # 129. Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho

# SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. HISTORICAL COMPARATOR

- **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed in part and remanded, 5-4.
- **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy announced the judgment. Parts I, II-A, and III were joined by the Chief Justice and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, and Thomas; other portions were joined only by the Chief Justice. Justice O'Connor concurred in part and in the judgment, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas. Justice Souter dissented, joined by Justices Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
- **Historical Holding:** The officer suit could not proceed because the requested relief was the functional equivalent of quiet title to uniquely sovereign submerged lands and therefore fell outside Ex parte Young.
- **Historical Authority:** Judgment supported by a five-Justice coalition, with the narrowest controlling rationale supplied by Justice O'Connor's concurrence together with the joined portions of Justice Kennedy's opinion.
- **Historical Sources:** Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho, 521 U.S. 261 (1997).
