## 3. Shared issue analysis and competing lawful paths

### A. Actual authority, managerial rank and the instruction

**Adopted path.** Andrade, February 26, 1997, supplies a plaintiff-proved connection between actual delegated power and the actionable environment. Oncale, March 19, preserves attribution separately from sex causation and the Harris threshold. Faragher Part I, September 8, applies that rule while preserving its limits. Each Associate joined all three relevant propositions; Faragher's eight-Associate application demonstrates that daily operational power can matter without a completed personnel act. It does not establish what happened in Harrison.

Here Brown's work assignment power is not simply a title-based inference. The lower opinion, Appendix A at 3a–6a and 27a, reports evidence of orders taking Harrison from her station or buddy into isolated areas, successive work-related excursions followed by alleged sexual misconduct, disciplinary participation and a work-through-lunch order following a refusal. This evidence permits a finding of material aid and requires a legally accurate opportunity to decide that route. It remains evidence, not a new Supreme Court finding that every incident occurred or every element was proved.

The principal attribution charge, Appendix A at 24a–27a, demanded all of its listed agency, apparent-authority and reliance elements. The accompanying definition required managerial control amounting to alter-ego identity. The apparent-authority charge, 28a–31a, again conditioned liability on authority to commit the challenged conduct. Together they foreclosed the separate route even if the jury believed that actual assignment power materially aided the abuse. The phrase addressing authority otherwise than apparently did not supply a material-aid instruction or remove the cumulative requirements.

**Strongest contrary path: preserve the charge.** Its reference to control over employment conditions could encompass a shift foreman; its reference to authority beyond apparent authority might be read broadly; refusing the employee's proposed automatic-supervisor instructions was proper. Those are genuine points against treating every disputed word as error. They do not cure the entire charge: managerial control was expressly tied to alter-ego identity, apparent authority and reliance remained mandatory, and the instructions never asked whether actual delegated power materially aided the environment. The record distinguishes refusal of an inaccurate proposed sentence from refusal of any accurate instruction on the supported theory. Requested instruction 7 contained both a potentially valid aid theory and an invalid status-alone theory; instruction 24 raised aid, while instruction 25 proposed status-based responsibility. No commitment requires the court to give any proposal verbatim.

**Other broader path: impose automatic responsibility from supervisory status.** Rejected by Meritor and the three current-term decisions. Removing an alter-ego prerequisite does not turn every supervisor into the employer for every personal act. Nor does Brown's conceded employment establish the material-aid connection. This path cannot support either a rule or a directed employer judgment.

### B. Whole-charge prejudice and apparent authority

**Adopted path.** The error was prejudicial on this record. The principal instruction was given over objection; the employee requested the omitted route; the appellate court expressly considered the charge as a whole and found the route viable on the evidence, Appendix A at 24a–34a. Daily-control evidence coupled with lack of top-management status made the incorrect barrier consequential. The work-order evidence gave the omitted route a concrete basis, independent of belief that harassment was permitted. No supplied special verdict establishes that the jury necessarily rejected an untainted element independently of attribution.

**Strongest contrary path: error but harmlessness.** A jury could have rejected an actionable environment or discriminatory causation, and the separate verdict against Brown does not establish those statutory elements against Potash. Some proposed instructions were overbroad, while the employer had a policy and responded after notice. Those propositions prevent automatic liability but do not show that this jury's employer verdict rested on an independent, correctly decided ground. A possible lawful explanation for a general verdict cannot cure a charge that excluded a supported liability route. Policy knowledge after some incidents and the later response likewise do not establish that actual authority supplied no material aid. The commitment is a record-specific finding of prejudicial instructional error, not a rule that every instruction error requires reversal or that prejudice is presumed whenever a general verdict is returned.

**Narrower lawful option.** Affirm on the independently sufficient actual-authority omission without deciding whether a correctly instructed jury could find apparent authority. Each Associate accepts that limit. Restatement §27, reproduced at 30a, supports the modest clarification that the employee's reasonable belief must arise from the principal's words or conduct. Faragher did not decide apparent authority; its separate observations cannot supply a Court holding. There is no need to adopt the lower opinion's entire apparent-authority framework, its characterization of apparent permission to harass, or its suggestion that unknown policy by itself supplies sufficient apparent authority. Nor is there a need to extend Faragher's actual-delegation holding to every claimed apparent work power.

### C. Prevention, reporting and response

**Adopted path.** Preserve the distinction among actual authority, apparent authority, negligence and remedy. A policy can affect reasonable belief or employer care when the required facts are present. Prompt investigation and correction can bear on response and relief; they do not retroactively remove earlier actual aid. The account reports that Harrison learned of a posted policy after some incidents and that it was absent from her new-hire materials. It does not establish universal ignorance, no available official, unreasonable nonuse, a particular policy promise, or the legal inadequacy of every later step. The employer's belief that Brown perceived consent does not turn its finding of unwilling participation into a judicial finding that conduct was welcome.

**Strongest contrary path: complete defense from policy and response.** Faragher expressly left open a possible defense involving genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse. Harrison's eventual knowledge and Potash's remedial measures distinguish its facts from Faragher's undistributed policy. That difference permits attention to the actual evidence in appropriate inquiries; it does not establish a complete defense, its governing elements or its prerequisites here. The charge was not an accurate, separately justified instruction on a recognized defense. The reservation neither expands this grant and preservation nor supplies an appellate finding against Harrison. All eight decline to adopt a new complete defense or reject every possible such defense in deciding this instructional appeal.

### D. Independent statutory rights and the actual contract record

**Adopted path.** Gardner-Denver, 415 U.S. 36 (1974), preserves the distinction between contractual grievance rights and the independent Title VII right. Gilmer, 500 U.S. 20 (1991), enforced an actual agreement to arbitrate the statutory ADEA claim and distinguished the earlier contractual-arbitration cases. First Options, May 22, 1995, requires courts to determine the antecedent agreement and delegation rather than presume them from an assertion of arbitral power. Its scope is not expanded into a new rule about union representation.

The decisive record limitation is a litigation fact reported in Appendix A at 35a n.1 and 40a n.2: Potash supplied neither the CBA nor the district court's order on exhaustion in the appellate record, and its appellate brief did not identify an agreement to arbitrate statutory claims. That prevents the employer from establishing the statutory obligation it needs for reversal. It is not a finding that no contract ever existed, a fabricated waiver, or an inference from the operator's inability to retrieve a document. Membership in a union and the asserted existence of a grievance procedure do not establish statutory scope or delegation. No disputed contractual interpretation is necessary to decide whether sex-based conduct and delegated work power meet Title VII's elements.

**Strongest contrary path: enforce the contractual procedure.** Gilmer rejects a categorical objection to arbitration of statutory employment claims, and a proven agreement can create enforceable duties without extinguishing substantive rights. A clause could, in another case, present the question of an express collective statutory-arbitration obligation. This path fails as a basis for Potash's requested dismissal because no such clause or delegation is established in the actual appellate record. It cannot be supplied by inventing terms or presuming that a generic grievance process governs statutory claims.

**Broader alternative and limits.** A categorical ruling that no CBA may ever commit statutory claims to arbitration would exceed what is necessary here. A categorical FAA exclusion for every employment or collective agreement likewise does not follow from Gilmer, which reserved the general employment-exclusion question. The lower court's broader rationale is not adopted. Livadas, June 13, 1994, and Hawaiian Airlines, June 20, 1994, preserve independent rights where adjudication needs no disputed contract interpretation, but their distinct preemption holdings do not decide every agreement's enforceability. The narrow ground fully rejects this defense; no contract-recovery remand is ordered merely to rescue the unsupported cross-appeal.

### E. Remedy and what the jury verdicts do not establish

**Adopted path.** Affirm the appellate reversal of Potash's Title VII judgment and its remand for further proceedings; affirm rejection of Potash's cross-appeal. The district court must proceed consistently with the corrected agency rule and the resolved prejudice determination. If the surviving claim is submitted to a jury, the supported actual-authority route must be accurately instructed, without the universal alter-ego, apparent-authority or permission-to-harass conditions. Ordinary genuinely preserved grounds consistent with these holdings remain for their proper adjudication. The mandate does not order a second appellate reconsideration of the same harmlessness claim.

**Strongest narrower-remand alternative.** Vacate for the lower court to reconsider prejudice after Faragher. That adds an unnecessary intermediate proceeding: the lower opinion already identified the omitted route, preservation, supporting evidence and concrete prejudice, and this Court can resolve the reviewed legal issue on those recitations. Faragher's different bench findings do not require an exploratory remand here. The appellate further-proceedings disposition is itself adequate and remains the next stage.

**Strongest broader-remedy alternative.** Enter judgment for Harrison based on the tort verdict and strength of the alleged authority misuse. Rejected. The Brown verdict concerns another defendant and different elements, and no supplied special findings resolve every Title VII predicate against Potash. Faragher restored an existing nominal judgment on supported bench findings and an accepted environment; it creates no general restoration command for an erroneous jury charge and a defense verdict. No sum awarded against Brown becomes a Title VII award against Potash.

The 1993 incidents occur after November 21, 1991. Landgraf's temporal bar on new §102 liabilities therefore does not itself restrict Harrison to Faragher's one-dollar remedy. Available §102 compensation, its conditional jury election and punitive predicates retain their statutory limits; §706(g) relief remains distinct, including backpay and interest excluded from §102 compensation. No amount, cap calculation, punitive finding, fee entitlement, reinstatement, constructive discharge or termination liability is decided. Duplicate compensation for the same injury is not authorized. The separate Brown tort awards and unrelated dispositions are not reopened.
