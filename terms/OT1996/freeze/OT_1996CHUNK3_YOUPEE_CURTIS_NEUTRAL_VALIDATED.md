## Curtis

**Identity and route.** Review of Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth, 420 Mass.749, 652 N.E.2d580 (July17, 1995), under the established simulated grant and granted federal questions. Historical petition identifier95-617 does not supply an outcome. The supplied simulated argument date is November4, 1996 and consideration date January21, 1997. The requested relief is an injunction against continuing the program without parental exclusion and notification provisions.

**Exact program distinction.** The complete lower primary text, [CAP](https://static.case.law/mass/420/cases/0749-01.json), at `sources/CH3A_Curtis_lower_lower_opinion.txt`, quotes the January2, 1992 superintendent memorandum. Junior-high students could request free condoms from the nurse and **would be counseled before receiving them**; the nurse also supplied disease-information pamphlets. High-school students could obtain free condoms from the nurse or pay75 cents at restroom vending machines; trained-faculty counseling was available on request and pamphlets in the nurse's office. Staff were instructed to withhold their personal views in respecting privacy, and the planned presentation stressed abstinence as the only certain means of avoiding sexually transmitted disease. No opt-out or parental notification provision existed.

The lower court separately characterized overall participation as voluntary: no student had to request condoms, attend a program class, read literature or face discipline for abstention. Preserve both the memorandum's junior-high sequence and that broader characterization. The packet's unqualified statement that counseling is voluntary and its claim that the program never requires counseling must not erase the condition attached to a junior-high request. The record supports voluntary entry into the program, not a finding that a requesting junior-high student could bypass every program condition. No forced condom use, medical procedure, prescription, compelled sexual conduct or change of legal custody is established.

**Entering law corrected.** The main slice's “Proof before a potentially indefinite complete parental-contact cutoff” (A.St.P.C., November20, 1995) and supplement's “Protection against serious avoidable harm in compulsory custody transitions” (Baby Richard, June21, 1996) protect their specified severe interventions and bounded implementation interests.
