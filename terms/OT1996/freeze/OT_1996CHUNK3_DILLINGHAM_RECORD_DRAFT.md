#### I. The wage criterion does not refer to ERISA plans

**Holding and operative rule:** ERISA §514(a) does not preempt the challenged public-works wage application as a reference to ERISA plans: the lower apprentice rate turns on state approval of an apprenticeship program, not on its ERISA status or the benefit content of a covered plan. The Court accepts this program's covered status for purposes of deciding preemption without holding that every apprenticeship or training arrangement is an ERISA plan.

**Controlling explanation:** Travelers distinguishes state laws whose legal operation identifies covered plans or uses their benefits as a benchmark from laws operating independently of that status. Board of Trade supplies the contrasting case: its replacement-coverage obligation used a covered plan's benefit content as the mandatory measure. California's criterion concerns approval of apprenticeship programs as such, and programs subject to that criterion need not all be ERISA plans. Its operation therefore does not depend on the existence of a covered plan.

The contrary argument begins with this program's covered status and the fact that approval conditions affect its operation. But a law does not acquire an ERISA-specific reference merely because a covered plan encounters it. That would replace the statutory inquiry with the presence of an ERISA litigant. The particular program may be assumed covered without making that assumption the legal measure of the State's wage rule. The Court does not decide every requirement in California's apprenticeship system or the classification of other training arrangements.

**Precedent treatment:** Travelers is applied for independently operating state cost rules. Board of Trade is distinguished because its mandatory covered-plan benchmark is absent; its holding remains intact.

#### II. The wage choice does not establish a preempted plan command

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, §514(a) does not preempt California's requirement that contractors pay prevailing journeyman wages when they do not use apprentices from an approved program. The approval-linked wage incentive neither dictates covered-plan benefits, funding, structure, administration or remedies nor is shown to have economic effects so acute that the retained wage choice becomes an effective plan command; a direct command or demonstrated coercive application remains outside this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Travelers requires examination of a law's actual operation. Its hospital-payment holdings preserve ordinary economic influence while reserving effects that operate as substantive coverage commands or effectively restrict choice. A contractor here may retain an unapproved program and pay the prevailing journeyman wage. The State's incentive favors approved training, but no supported finding shows that the higher wage eliminates that alternative or requires a change in a covered plan's internal terms. Later approval of the program was not retroactive and does not erase the dispute over the earlier wages.

Dillingham's strongest contention is that the wage differential pressures programs to conform to substantive state training standards. Influence is real, but it does not by itself establish the compulsion Travelers requires. Treating every substantial cost consequence as preemption would discard that decision's distinction. Traditional state responsibility for wages and apprenticeship informs the statutory setting; it does not immunize every public-works measure or establish a percentage safe harbor. The Court applies the concrete command-and-effects inquiry, not a formula under which any verbal relationship to a covered plan suffices. It neither creates a competing ERISA enforcement remedy nor sustains regulation of every plan merely because the State acts through a contractor.

**Precedent treatment:** Travelers's indirect-effects rule and acute-effects reservation are applied without change. Board of Trade remains controlling when plan benefits supply the mandatory legal benchmark, which this wage application does not use.

#### III. Reversal of the ERISA ground leaves independent issues for remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's ERISA-preemption judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the absence of §514(a) preemption of this wage application. The mandate removes that federal obstacle to enforcement without directing final judgment on the independently unresolved NLRA theory, contingent §1983 damages, any properly remaining fee issue, nonpreemption defenses or the underlying wage calculation.

**Controlling explanation:** The District Court granted judgment to the state defendants on the ERISA and NLRA theories. The Ninth Circuit reversed on ERISA, rejected the Fitzgerald Act saving theory, and did not decide the independent NLRA issue or its contingent damages consequence. Removing the ERISA ground therefore does not supply a decision on those unreviewed questions. The separate rejection of the particular §1988 fee request based on an asserted ERISA right not sought to be enforced under §1983 is not automatically reversed, and the State's contractual-estoppel contention, declined below because it was first raised on appeal, is not revived.

The contrary remedial path would reinstate final judgment on every claim merely because the sole appellate preemption ground fails. That would decide more than the granted ERISA question and eliminate issues the Court of Appeals has not reached. Conversely, remand does not authorize a new challenge to the settled absence of §514(a) preemption on these facts. Because that provision does not reach this application, the Court need not decide §514(d) or whether the Fitzgerald Act would save an otherwise preempted rule. Nor does the State obtain blanket immunity merely from public ownership of the project.

**Precedent treatment:** Travelers governs the ERISA ground at its actual scope. Neither Boston Harbor nor Chamber of Commerce v. Reich supplies an automatic public-contract immunity or decides the independent NLRA and damages issues left for remand.

**Precedent treatment:** Travelers governs the ERISA ground at its actual scope. Neither Boston Harbor nor Chamber of Commerce v. Reich supplies an automatic public-contract immunity or decides the independent NLRA and damages issues left for remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers Insurance Co., April 26, 1995: applied to the concrete difference between ordinary economic influence and a direct or effective plan command. Its acute-effects reservation and unresolved direct-self-funded questions remain intact; no numerical threshold or general-applicability immunity is added.
- District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade, December 14, 1992: distinguished because California's wage criterion does not use covered-plan benefit content as a mandatory measure. The covered-plan benchmark holding remains binding.
- Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor), March 8, 1993: its protection of the lawful proprietary labor agreement supplies no blanket ERISA immunity for every public-works condition. The Court does not resolve the independent NLRA claim by applying a procurement label.
- Chamber of Commerce v. Reich, January 14, 1997: its particular limit on federal procurement authority does not decide this ERISA application or the separate NLRA claim and associated remedies. Its refusal to make a procurement label dispositive remains undisturbed.

## Law After Decision

California may apply the reviewed wage consequence without the ERISA bar imposed below, subject to independently unresolved issues and ordinary enforcement requirements. State approval of apprenticeship programs as such is not a reference to ERISA status, and this record's wage incentive is not an actual or effective plan command. Direct plan regulation, an ERISA-specific legal benchmark, competing enforcement remedies and demonstrated acute compulsion remain outside the holding. No categorical immunity for wage laws, apprenticeship laws, public purchasing or indirect economic effects is adopted.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed. The Court's opinion does not revive an unlimited reading under which every relationship to a covered plan establishes preemption.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit for the independent issues it did not resolve and proceedings consistent with the ERISA holding. No final wage amount, damages award, fee entitlement or new deadline is ordered. The Court need not reach §514(d) or the Fitzgerald Act saving theory because §514(a) does not preempt this application. The program's later approval remains nonretroactive.
