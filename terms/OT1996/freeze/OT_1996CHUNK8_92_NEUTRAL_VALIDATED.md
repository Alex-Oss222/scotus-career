### 3. Categorical postponement and particular accommodation

Landis v. North American Co., 299 U.S. 248, 254–258, recognizes inherent docket-control power and requires judgment weighing competing interests. If there is even a fair possibility that a requested stay will harm another, the applicant must make a clear showing of hardship or inequity in proceeding. Extraordinary public interests may warrant a delay that is moderate in length and not oppressive; a stay must be bounded when entered, and an opportunity to seek later revision does not cure an initially immoderate duration. Landis concerned awaiting another suit, so the application of its principles to presidential burdens requires explanation, not a preexisting presidential test. Its remand for current factual appraisal did not erase the stay applicant's burden.

The existing lower order stays trial for the Presidency while allowing discovery. #### Swint v. Chambers County Commission, 514 U.S. 35

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1636.
**Decided:** March 1, 1995.

##### Ordinary municipal liability remains reviewable after final judgment

**Holding:** The denial of this municipal defendant's ordinary liability defense does not qualify for immediate collateral-order review under §1291 because the defense remains effectively reviewable after final judgment. The officers' distinct immunity from suit does not give the municipality a corresponding right to avoid trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp. retains its cumulative collateral-order requirements. Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc. is applied through its independently controlling adequate-final-review ground. Mitchell v. Forsyth is distinguished because the officials' immunity from suit is not the county's liability defense. Monell v. Department of Social Services supplies no municipal immunity from all trial.

**Independent alternative holding:** An order that expressly leaves the county's final-policymaker question open for further presentation and a later legal ruling does not conclusively determine that question under Cohen. This failure independently defeats collateral-order review; the mere denial of the pending motions does not resolve a question the court expressly reserves.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's Part III, eight Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cohen is applied to the express reservation of the disputed question. Coopers & Lybrand v. Livesay is applied to the requirement of a conclusive, rather than tentative, determination. Neither decision is enlarged into a rule admitting every later definitive ruling to immediate appeal.

##### A proper immunity appeal does not authorize this separate county appeal

**Holding:** A court of appeals may not decide a separate party's nonappealable liability defense merely because a related officers' immunity appeal is properly before it and resolving the defense would save litigation. The county attribution issue here is neither inextricably intertwined with nor necessary to meaningful review of the officers' qualified-immunity questions, and no other authorized appellate route is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part II, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abney v. United States and United States v. Stanley are applied to limits on adding otherwise unauthorized issues to a proper immediate appeal. Cohen and Digital Equipment remain the source of the separate collateral-order inquiry. Earlier examples of connected review are not overruled or converted into a general pendent-party jurisdiction doctrine.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Part IV of Ginsburg’s opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices, expressly leaves undecided whether genuinely inextricable or necessary connected review is available at all and what its scope would be. Stone-Zsela’s affirmative view is separate and noncontrolling. Section 1292(b) requires the District Judge's written statement that an order involves a controlling question of law, substantial ground for difference of opinion, and that an immediate appeal may materially advance the litigation's ultimate termination. Application must be made within ten days; the court of appeals may then permit the appeal in its discretion. Application alone does not stay District Court proceedings unless the District Judge, the court of appeals, or a judge thereof orders a stay. No qualifying certification or permission is established here. Section 1292(e) permits additional interlocutory-appeal rules prescribed under §2072; §2072(c) permits rules defining finality under §1291. Those are rulemaking authorizations, subject to §2072's prohibition on abridging, enlarging, or modifying substantive rights, and are not an operative rule allowing this appeal. No mandamus relief is sought or granted here; that distinct route retains its own requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Commission's appellate disposition under §§1983 and 1985(3) is vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss its interlocutory appeal. Its surviving federal claims return to their preappeal District Court posture without a liability or summary-judgment ruling. Officers' immunity dispositions and separately declined City and individual state-law appeals are undisturbed; no county state-law count remained.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The presented committee-evidence challenge is not judicially revisable

**Controlling proposition:** The Senate’s use of a committee to receive testimony, while the full Senate receives the record, hears argument and retains deliberation and judgment, falls within the impeachment-trial procedure committed to the Senate and is not judicially revisable on this claim. This holding does not decide whether courts may enforce the oath, presidential-trial presiding-officer or two-thirds requirements, or review a purported proceeding that abandons adjudication altogether.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Six of nine Justices adopt this exact ground and its limits; White and Blackmun’s merits ground is noncontrolling, and Souter does not formally join the Court’s opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this challenge asks the Judiciary to choose the Senate’s evidentiary method rather than enforce an independently prescribed constitutional procedure. Baker requires examination of the particular constitutional commitment and the standards available for judicial decision. Article I gives the Senate sole responsibility to try impeachments but does not require every Senator to receive live testimony. The committee assembled evidence; the full Senate retained deliberation and judgment. Powell enforced qualifications independently fixed by the Constitution and therefore does not authorize appellate supervision of this method. Commerce v. ## Holdings

### Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. The court independently determines those cumulative conditions and, when they are satisfied, must reinstate without discretionary balancing, retaining independent responsibility for all claims, defenses, and remedies; this expressly retrospective entitlement does not authorize legislative findings, dictated liability, or unrestricted reopening of completed merits judgments.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and remedy stated here. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas dissent; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Morgan Stanley governs this completed limitations dismissal. Finality matters: the unappealed judgment ended the ordinary review process. But Morgan Stanley distinguishes Congress's creation of a bounded entitlement to further adjudication from legislative adjudication of the controversy itself. Section 27A(b) supplies the governing rule; judges determine whether its conditions are satisfied and decide the securities claims.

The statute's narrow coverage and exclusively retrospective operation require attention, but do not make it an individualized legislative judgment. It reaches every action satisfying objective conditions concerning commencement, dismissal, governing limitations law, and the time for seeking relief. It does not identify parties for an award or assessment of liability. A closed temporal class is still a class defined by law. Congress has directed no finding of deception, reliance, causation, or damages. Independent judicial determination remains both at the eligibility stage and on the merits. Mandatory reinstatement after eligibility is established is the consequence of the new legal rule, not a legislative finding that the plaintiffs prevail on their claims.

Robertson distinguishes a genuine change in governing law from prescribed adjudication; Morgan Stanley supplies the completed-dismissal application that Robertson left open. Klein protects the judicial function without forbidding this statutory entitlement. McCullough's protection of completed judicial determinations remains: Congress may not dictate contrary findings or declare a defendant liable. Beam's finality limit on judicial retroactivity does not erase an express statutory remedy. Wheeling Bridge and Sioux Nation concern continuing equitable relief and sovereign waiver, respectively; neither supplies an unrestricted reopening power. Chase Securities addresses due-process revival, not the Article III issue resolved here.

The February 11, 1992 motion satisfied the statutory conditions. Reinstatement therefore follows, with the securities merits left for the courts. The dissent's objection treats completion of this limitations dismissal as an absolute bar despite the retained judicial work; Morgan Stanley forecloses that rule in this setting. Other completed merits judgments, individualized legislative commands, and independent constitutional protections remain outside this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may not command findings contrary to the record, impose liability on identified defendants by legislative declaration, or disguise an individualized award as a procedural reopening law. The Court decides no general power to set aside completed judgments adjudicating the underlying merits, no constitutionality of every retrospective statute, and no independent due-process question; applicable due-process protections and other constitutional restrictions remain. Section 27A(a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, federal waiver, continuing prospective relief, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. The decision neither adjudicates other parties' completed cases nor creates criminal or innocence remedies. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses remain for the courts; reinstatement establishes none of them.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: applies its five-Justice Article III holding sustaining §27A(b)'s bounded mandatory reinstatement procedure to this qualifying completed private limitations dismissal; preserves independent eligibility and merits adjudication and all constitutional limits.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: applies its distinction between genuine legislative change and prescribed judicial adjudication; Morgan Stanley supplies the completed-dismissal rule that Robertson did not decide.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: applies its protection of the independent judicial function; a class-defined entitlement to renewed adjudication does not dictate findings or securities liability.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: preserves protection of completed judicial determinations while distinguishing the statutory entitlement to litigate a limitations-dismissed claim from a legislative declaration of facts or liability contrary to an adjudication.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529: preserves finality against reopening by later judicial doctrine alone; the entitlement here comes from an express statute whose Article III validity Morgan Stanley establishes.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes the continuing operation of prospective equitable relief; that setting does not itself decide this completed limitations dismissal.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own defense; no governmental waiver of a private defendant's defense supplies the holding here.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304: leaves its due-process revival rule intact without treating it as a controlling answer to Article III.
- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S. 350: identifies the limitations rule that produced the dismissal and the statutory response; §27A(b) supplies reinstatement without establishing the securities merits.

## Precedent Treatment

Morgan Stanley controls the constitutional question and mandatory statutory remedy. Robertson and Klein retain the distinction between legislation governing adjudication and legislative performance of adjudication. McCullough continues to protect completed judicial determinations within the distinction Morgan Stanley draws. Beam's rule of judicial retroactivity alone creates no reopening entitlement. Wheeling Bridge, Sioux Nation, and Chase Securities remain authorities for their distinct prospective-relief, sovereign-waiver, and due-process settings. Lampf remains the intervening limitations decision to which §27A(b) responds. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

A completed, unappealed private securities limitations dismissal within §27A(b)'s objective criteria must be reinstated on a timely qualifying motion. Courts determine eligibility independently and retain all adjudicative responsibility for the underlying claims, defenses, and remedies. A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability. The rule does not establish a general power to reopen completed merits judgments or remove independent constitutional protections. The dissent's contrary finality rule is noncontrolling.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, and Thomas. A dismissal with prejudice that has become final without appeal is a completed exercise of judicial power even when it rests on limitations. In their view, Congress may change the law for pending cases but may not require an Article III court to undo its conclusive disposition after ordinary review has ended. Leaving fraud and damages for a later judicial decision does not preserve the conclusiveness of the dismissal itself. The dissent acknowledges that Morgan Stanley governs the statutory setting, but rejects its distinction between a new entitlement to adjudication and interference with the completed judgment.

McCullough supplies the dissent's protection of completed adjudication, and Klein protects judicial decision from legislative control. Robertson permitted changed law in pending litigation; it did not decide completed judgments. Beam preserves finality despite later judicial doctrine. Wheeling Bridge concerns prospective equitable relief and Sioux Nation the Government's own defense. The dissent treats neither setting as authority for reopening a private final dismissal. Congress's use of objective criteria and an express temporal command does not answer the dissent's objection: every judgment within the defined class has already conclusively disposed of its case. Narrow and wholly retrospective coverage strengthens the concern about legislative interference, although the dissent's objection also reaches a broader command to reopen such completed private judgments. The dissent would affirm denial of reinstatement, without deciding §27A(a), independent due-process claims, fraud, liability, or damages.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment of the Sixth Circuit is reversed. The case is remanded for reinstatement under the satisfied conditions of §27A(b) and further judicial proceedings on the underlying securities claims. Reinstatement is required, not a discretionary remedy. The courts retain responsibility for the merits, defenses, and any relief; no merits outcome is prescribed.

### 1997-01-14 - Chamber of Commerce v. Reich; D.C. Circuit No. 95-5242; 74 F.3d 1322; no Supreme Court docket number assigned.

Authority: [Record](../records/Chamber_of_Commerce_v_Reich_merits_1997-01-14.md).

#### Holdings

**Authority common to all holdings:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, is joined throughout by the other eight Justices identified in Opinion Topology. Each proposition below therefore has nine votes; judgment agreement and reasoning agreement coincide.

##### I. Review of the implementing officer

**Holding and operative rule.** A person injured by an officer's implementation of a restriction outside delegated statutory authority may seek prospective equitable restraint under federal-question jurisdiction unless Congress has displaced that remedy. Section 702 waives sovereign immunity for this nonmonetary official-capacity suit against the implementing Secretary even without a pleaded APA review cause, while preserving other legal and equitable review limits and relief prohibitions in another consent-to-suit statute; any injunction must identify the officer or successor responsible for compliance.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 1331 supplies jurisdiction; traditional officer-directed equity supplies the action; §702 addresses sovereign immunity. None independently creates a damages claim or a cause for every statutory error. McAnnulty, Stark and Harmon establish that the absence of an express review provision does not itself eliminate prospective officer relief. Larson and Dugan support the confined distinction between authorized action and action beyond delegated power, without making every allegation of error sufficient. The employers challenge an operative disqualification imposed by the Secretary, not merely the wisdom of an authorized purchase. Their claim presents a judicially manageable boundary between two federal statutes. Dalton bars reopening its particular completed base-closure package; the Procurement Act contains no comparable structure foreclosing this action. Franklin preserves review of a completed subordinate act despite later presidential involvement. The origin of this restriction in a presidential order does not answer whether its implementing officer has statutory authority. The Court neither decides the President's APA status nor authorizes coercive relief against him.

##### II. The procurement delegation does not authorize this labor-policy sanction

**Holding and operative rule.** The Procurement Act's general economy-and-efficiency delegation does not authorize Executive Order 12954's government-wide disqualification based on permanent replacement of economic strikers, because that sanction alters the bargaining choices Congress preserved under the NLRA. This statutory holding leaves Congress free to change those choices and preserves ordinary review of materially different proprietary conditions and lawful centralized purchasing standards.

**Controlling explanation.** Mackay Radio recognizes the use of permanent replacements for economic strikers within the NLRA's remaining protections; it does not equate those strikers with unfair-labor-practice strikers. Insurance Agents and Machinists identify the economic weapons Congress left to the bargaining parties. A general purchasing delegation must operate with that allocation, not silently displace it. Boston Harbor protects a lawful bargain justified by the public purchaser's project interests; a procurement label alone does not turn general labor policy into proprietary conduct. This order makes replacement itself the disqualifying event and reaches affiliates capable of supplying the contract, even when the striking workers do not perform that contract. The Government's interest in dependable supply and retained experience is legitimate, but the policy's actual operation goes beyond a supported performance bargain. Its threshold and discretionary exceptions do not cure that statutory mismatch. The Court neither imposes a universal one-project rule nor invalidates purchasing standards because of their size. It does not guarantee any employer government business. The ground is construction of the federal enactments together, not mechanical application of state-preemption labels or a constitutional limit on Congress.

**Operative qualifications.** The order concerns contracts above $100,000. After finding permanent replacement of lawfully striking workers, the Secretary may find termination for convenience appropriate unless the contracting agency head objects; termination is not mandatory. Debarment retains exceptions when the precipitating dispute has been resolved as determined by the Secretary or the agency head finds a compelling reason to lift it. Debarment normally reaches the organizational units found to have used permanent replacements; the regulations define a unit to include the responsible prime-contracting division or element and any affiliate capable of supplying the contracted goods or services. In deciding whether a dispute ended, the regulations consider a formal settlement or agreed dispute-resolution procedure, informal resolution, the strikers' return, and other factors tending to show the dispute has ended. The Court does not adopt the lower court's reading of those factors as a universal requirement of union approval or find that every application compels unlawful recognition.

##### III. The relief follows the statutory defect

**Holding and operative rule.** The judgment reversing dismissal and rejecting the challenged implementation is affirmed, and the case is remanded for appropriate prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against the implementing Secretary confined to the unlawful procurement restriction. No injunction against the President, damages, contract award, striker reinstatement or employee-specific bargaining remedy is ordered.

**Controlling explanation.** The reviewable defect concerns the Secretary's implementation of an unauthorized restriction. Prospective officer relief addresses that defect without transferring purchasing decisions to the courts or deciding individual labor rights. The lower court must formulate the appropriate decree consistently with §702's responsible-officer requirement and retained legal and equitable limits. No independent nondelegation ruling, mandatory procurement-findings procedure or decision on a materially different proprietary measure is necessary.

- McAnnulty, 187 U.S. 94: applied for prospective review of officer action beyond delegated statutory authority despite the absence of an express review provision.
- Stark v. Wickard, 321 U.S. 288: applied for the availability of judicial protection against officer action exceeding statutory limits.
- Harmon v. Brucker, 355 U.S. 579: applied for judicial determination of an officer's statutory authority before accepting a claim of unreviewable discretion.
- Larson v. Domestic & Foreign Commerce Corp., 337 U.S. 682: applied at its confined officer-action scope; statutory error is not converted into a universal immunity exception.
- Dugan v. Rank, 372 U.S. 609: applied for the corresponding distinction between sovereign relief and action outside lawful officer authority.

Lawful proprietary measures and centralized purchasing standards remain subject to their own statutory justification; no entitlement to a government contract follows. The Court adds this statute-specific limit without changing Dalton's actual preclusion rule or deciding presidential APA status.

#### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct votes. The Chief Justice's judgment concurrence adds no vote to this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity resolves this individual damages claim without deciding the reserved constitutional merits. Harlow supplies objective protection for conduct not violating clearly established rights; Anderson requires a particularized inquiry without a factual twin. Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law, and Pinder separates nonprotection from an affirmative deprivation without making its earlier notice result automatic here.

The Court assumes the supported account that officials knowingly closed the protective unit despite grave risk to a defined staff group. That assumption does not merge Harder, Vega, Thompson, Whiteman and Lee or attribute a clinical placement decision to any of them. Cornelius supplies the strongest contrary analogy: affirmative workplace exposure to dangerous prisoners through placement, supervision and assurances. Together with Bowers, Archie and Archuleta, they do not sufficiently settle the constitutional attribution of each administrator's different acts through this clinical and institutional chain.

Warnings and professional procedures are features of that legal comparison, not proof of reasonable care, lack of awareness, no causation or constitutional waiver.
