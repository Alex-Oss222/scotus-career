### The existing appellate party identification is sufficient here

Section 1254(1) requires a petition by a party to the case in the court of appeals. Scofield, 382 U.S. 205, 208–209, supplies that requirement; Perini, 459 U.S. 297, 302–305 and footnote 13, preserves the distinction between statutory party status and injury. The Attorney General's interest in enforcing a conviction, standing alone, would not satisfy it.

The June 1995 panel opinion, September 1995 rehearing order and April 1996 en banc opinion each identify **John Van de Kamp** among the appellate respondents, alongside the custodial respondents. The petition timely seeks review in the name of the current **Attorney General Daniel Lungren**, expressly in that official capacity, as well as the People. The Office of Attorney General, not a personal claim of either officeholder, is involved. The Court accepts the court of appeals' repeated identification of that official litigant as sufficient evidence of party status in the appellate proceeding whose judgment is under review.

The contrary documents receive their full weight. The 1991 amended habeas petition names Gomez and Borg; the verified answer calls them proper respondents, and the subsequent order substitutes Merkle for Borg. Those documents establish the custodians' proper participation and replacement. They do not supply a ruling by the court of appeals eliminating the Attorney General's office from that proceeding or establish that the later repeated appellate designation must be disregarded. The opposition asserts the broader conclusion, but it does not turn the earlier custodial substitution into such an appellate ruling. Acceptance of the appellate identification is a limited adjudicative judgment on these competing documents, not a neutral certification that their history is undisputed.

The date-effective public-officer rules, independently verified in [the clean rule-source receipt](ROY_OFFICER_RULES_VALIDATED.md), supply the applicable distinction between the office and its holder. **FRAP 43(c)(2)** permits an existing official-capacity appellate party to be described by official title rather than name; **FRCP 25(d)(2)** does likewise for an official-capacity action. **FRAP 43(c)(1)** automatically substitutes a successor when an official-capacity party ceases to hold office during the appellate proceeding's pendency; **FRCP 25(d)(1)** provides the corresponding rule when that change occurs during the action's pendency. Under each automatic-substitution provision, an immaterial misnomer is disregarded and omission of a formal substitution order does not defeat the substitution. Neither provision creates a party who was absent when its operative conditions applied.

Here the decisive adjudicative premise is the appellate court's repeated identification of the Attorney General's official-capacity party, rather than proof of an imagined intervention or substitution order. On accepting that identification, Lungren's expressly official petition identifies the incumbent of the same office, not a new personal litigant. The office-title provisions make that distinction legally material. The automatic-substitution provisions confirm why a missing formal name-change order, by itself, is insufficient to defeat continuing official litigation; they are not used to invent when any disputed event happened or to dispense with their pendency conditions. Nor does the Attorney General's appearance as counsel independently establish party status. The analysis does not falsely suppose that the change of Attorney General first occurred during the appeal or while this Supreme Court proceeding was pending. The 1961 note to Civil Rule 25(d) confirms the procedural nature of official succession and its lack of effect on substantive issues; its separate warning to consult appellate substitution rules is respected here.

Thus at least the official Attorney General petitioner satisfies the requirement on this record. It is unnecessary to decide whether the People separately qualify or to manufacture a petition by unnamed custodians. No late party joins the petition under Rule 12.4. Rule 35.3 is not used to establish original party status below. Smith v. Barry supports attention to the party objectively identified by a timely document; the Court retains its requirement of an actual identified litigant. This is not a general rule that counsel is a party, that every caption is conclusive, or that any State may seek review without party status.

## 3. Current law, method and remedy common ground

**Personal-intent category.** All eight apply the State-borne Chapman rule to this preserved omission of the purpose to facilitate robbery. Current O'Neal concerned a combined instruction and prosecutorial statement permitting conviction without the defendant's own required intent. The omitted-purpose instruction here permits the same constitutional failure. An accompanying prosecutorial statement is not a persuasive boundary for this protection; extending the rule to this omission is a confined application of its personal-intent premise. No Justice's row adopts Chapman for every constitutional habeas claim, revives a resolved exhaustion issue, supplies an innocence prerequisite or resolves AEDPA's temporal application.

**Classification.** This defect permits harmlessness review. It does not invalidate the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings, as Sullivan did. Gaudin requires a jury determination of an offense element but reserved universal structural-error treatment. Rose and Carella support reviewability of element-related errors. The Associates do not adopt automatic reversal for every omission. Scalia and Ginsburg's stricter actual-findings method is a method of harmlessness review, not an unqualified structural rule.

**The disagreement concerns permissible proof, not whether Chapman was mentioned.** The Ninth Circuit expressly assessed Chapman and found the omission not harmless before separately applying historical collateral law. A remand cannot be justified by saying that it never performed any Chapman analysis. Five Associates identify a defect in making necessary findings the exclusive method here. Stevens finds the present result supportable under a proper actual-contribution inquiry. Scalia and Ginsburg defend the narrower method appropriate to this omitted-purpose element.

**Five-Justice method proposition:** O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer join only the following qualified rule. The State must show beyond a reasonable doubt, on the whole record, that the omission did not contribute to the verdict actually returned. Review must relate the instructions, evidence and findings to that verdict. Necessary equivalence between findings and the omitted purpose is sufficient but is not the exclusive route. A court may draw supported inferences concerning the error's actual effect; it may not substitute proof sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction, treat knowledge plus assistance as legally identical to purpose, or infer a particular purpose merely from general mental capacity. Under Yates, the court must examine whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence on which the proposed harmlessness inference rests. If the omission kept the relevant personal-purpose question from the jury's consideration so that the proposed whole-record inference cannot reliably address its actual verdict, the State has not carried its burden by producing strong evidence alone. This qualification is an express condition of Souter's join and is accepted by the other four.

**Scalia–Ginsburg method proposition:** For this omitted-purpose error, harmlessness requires that the jury's other determinations effectively embrace Roy's required purpose, or that the evidence and findings establish that the jury could not have reached what it did find without also finding that purpose. Independent appellate certainty that a properly instructed jury would convict is insufficient. The State must establish the requisite connection beyond a reasonable doubt under current law. Whole-record language describes what is examined; it does not eliminate the need to tie harmlessness to a finding actually made. This remains distinct from Stevens's narrower conclusion that the State fails on this record without adopting an exclusive necessary-findings rule for all omissions.

**Record and preservation limits apply to every row.** The charge required knowledge and assistance, not mere presence, but omitted the legally separate purpose to facilitate the robbery. The personal-intent constitutional claim is preserved. The special-circumstance argument concerns that existing harmlessness question rather than automatically constituting a new constitutional claim under Yee; the opposition's first-instance objection nevertheless must be addressed. Its claimed oral concession is not verified, and no forfeiture is declared simply because the opposition asserted it.

The special-circumstance instructions require an integrated analysis. The conditional nonkiller clause concerning intentional aid to first-degree murder cannot be treated as an unconditional finding of Roy's purpose to facilitate robbery. Absence of personal knife use does not itself decide every possible actual-killer theory. The robbery-related purpose of the killing in 8.81.17 is distinct from Roy's own aiding purpose. Reversal of the special circumstances prevents restoration of their operative punishment here; it does not automatically settle every possible evidentiary implication of what the jury returned. General specific-intent or diminished-capacity instructions do not silently insert the omitted clause.

The evidence includes possession of property, wet clothing, assistance testimony and reported statements, as qualified in the neutral repair. Clothing attributed by the lower recitations to McHargue cannot be placed in Roy's backpack as an uncontested fact. Hudspeth's impeachment and rebuttal, Hall's reported statements and retraction, the knife evidence, the verdicts and the state court's explanation require consideration together. The State's account is not an undisputed admission; a possible companion-assistance inference is not an additional factual finding; and the verdicts do not establish wholesale acceptance or rejection of every witness. No missing transcript or damaged OCR passage supplies default, guilt, innocence, a new juror finding or a forfeiture.

**Remedy shared by the five remand commitments:** Grant certiorari, vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for the qualified Chapman inquiry on the existing supported record, with District Court proceedings if otherwise proper. The Ninth Circuit may reach the same result. The remand concerns only the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions. If the available violation is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, conditional relief must allow the State a reasonable period to retry those affected convictions; proper harmlessness permits denial. There is no present finding of harmlessness, immediate release, retrial deadline, restoration of special-circumstance punishment or disposition of the separate Clark conviction.

**Remedy shared by the three affirmance commitments:** Grant review and affirm the habeas-relief judgment for the two Mannix convictions on their respective grounds below. Implementation remains conditional relief allowing a reasonable retrial period; no new Supreme Court deadline or immediate unconditional release is directed. The Clark conviction and reversed special-circumstance punishment remain outside the disposition.
