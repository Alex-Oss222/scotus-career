## Six-Justice controlling-ground commitment

**1. The particular public-road consequence.** Montana protects distinct authority over retained tribal/trust land and limits inherent authority over nonmember activity on alienated land, subject to its two exceptions and any specific superior federal source. Bourland applies an exclusion consequence to particular land opened to public use by federal acquisition legislation; it does not require treating all land instruments or rights-of-way alike. Here, the actual federal grant and public-road use mean the Tribe cannot condition ordinary motorists' highway passage on obtaining its discretionary permission. At that limited scope, the right-of-way has the relevant characteristic supporting Montana's nonmember framework.

This is a legal conclusion about this authorized public use, not a finding that the instrument conveyed fee title or expressly transferred every governmental power to North Dakota. Its trust-only provision, route, conditions and reservations remain effective. The easement's tenure is unlimited only while actually used for its specified purpose. It remains subject to valid existing rights or adverse claims. The United States grantor may terminate all or part after thirty days' written notice and failure to correct breach of a grant term or applicable regulation, two consecutive years of nonuse for the granted purpose, abandonment, or failure to file the required completion affidavit. Indian owners, lessees, successors and assigns retain reasonably necessary crossings, constructed and maintained at their risk with responsibility for resulting right-of-way damage. Those rights concern the grant's duration and compatible land use; they do not establish a present general power to exclude authorized public motorists or an adjudicatory entitlement over every accident. No termination trigger is found.

The acknowledged consideration is $5,546.50 plus other good consideration. The header's zero tribal acres and 87.48 individually owned acres is not a finding of the exact collision parcel's owner. The incorporated map/plats and separate consent document are not reproduced, but the grant is not attacked as invalidly issued. Neither their absence nor the trust-only clause decides a new title dispute. The result holds for the supported right-of-way setting without selecting a parcel owner, finding statutory noncompliance, construing every clause of §§323–328, or treating all reservation roads as alienated land. Sac and Fox's inclusion of qualifying rights-of-way within Indian country remains intact; territorial classification and this particular nonmember governing power are different inquiries.

**2. Forum power requires a sufficient source.** Adjudication and choice of law are analytically distinct, but compelled litigation and a binding tort judgment exercise sovereign authority over the defendants' conduct. A tribal court cannot supply the missing authority for this highway-negligence claim solely by saying it could apply state law. National Farmers and Iowa Mutual ensure tribal examination and preserve ultimate federal review; their exhaustion holdings are not independent grants of jurisdiction over every reservation occurrence. Their civil/criminal distinction remains effective: no automatic importation of Oliphant or Duro supplies this result. Williams involved an on-reservation trader's claim against resident members concerning their transaction, a concrete internal relationship missing from this claim between two non-Indian road users.

Merrion remains a substantial counterargument because it separates sovereign taxing power from a private landowner's ability to exclude and refuses to infer surrender from contractual silence. The six do not infer a general sovereign waiver from an unreserved clause here. They distinguish its taxation of mineral lessees' reservation business and governmental-services relationship from the present ordinary highway claim. Montana's external limit and its exceptions, applied to the federally authorized public-road setting, do the work; the result is not a universal rule that every government power vanishes whenever an easement is granted.

**3. Consensual relationship.** A-1 voluntarily entered the established $12,490 subcontract with LCM, the tribally owned corporation, for reservation community-center work. The first Montana exception can support appropriate regulation of activities arising from consensual commercial dealings, contracts, leases or similar arrangements. It does not require an express forum-consent clause in every case, but it does require a meaningful relation between the asserted authority and the consensual activity; contracting with the Tribe does not submit the contractor to every conceivable reservation claim.

For this claim the six assume, favorably to petitioners without making findings, that Stockert's trip served that project and that Fredericks resided on the reservation. She nonetheless asserts the ordinary duty owed by a public-road driver to another non-Indian road user, not a tribal contract entitlement, an obligation owed to a tribal participant in the work, or control of project performance. The road is used under the public right-of-way, rather than only by permission gained through the subcontract. The contract may explain why the truck was there without making adjudication of this third-party highway claim part of governing the consensual relationship. That distinction supplies the result; absence of contractual privity is relevant but not a universal rule that no third-party tort could ever fall within Montana's first exception. A different injury to the Tribe, a member, the project or a person participating in the consensual activity presents a different question.

This reasoning does not find the trip unrelated. It makes the unresolved work connection legally insufficient even on petitioners' strongest supported version, so further factfinding about the load or assignment cannot change these six commitments. No weight is given to the purported Utah forum/law clause, which was not accepted as part of the appellate record. Nor do attributed building/employment-law obligations establish blanket consent to this lawsuit. The holding neither enforces nor invalidates an unread contract provision.

**4. Tribal integrity, security, health or welfare.** Montana separately preserves civil authority over non-Indian conduct threatening or directly affecting tribal political integrity, economic security, health or welfare. Nonmembership alone cannot eliminate every possible tribal interest. The seriousness of Fredericks's injury, her family ties to members, assumed residence, road safety and the importance of functioning tribal courts are all acknowledged. But ordinary individual injury and the resulting familial or community effects do not on this record establish the governmental threat or direct tribal effect sufficient to make this nonmember public-road litigation an exercise of necessary tribal self-government. Otherwise the exception would restore general territorial authority over essentially every local civil wrong that the external limit excludes.

No fiscal or service burden is invented, and no finding that the Tribe suffered no consequences is entered. The question is legal sufficiency of the supported claim and interests. The sons' membership does not change their mother's membership or bring their unreviewed consortium claims into the judgment. A filed protective state action is consistent with another forum being available, but forum availability is not the independent legal reason that tribal power is absent. No state merits or limitations outcome is decided.

## Souter's distinct ground and Breyer's narrower remand

**Souter.** His actual join in Blackmun's Bourland dissent objected specifically to equating loss of absolute exclusion with loss of compatible regulation; his join in Breyer's Chickasaw dissent likewise attends to the concrete governmental relationship protected by a limited source. He accepts controlling Bourland on its acquisition statutes and does not restore the displaced power there. The present conditioned highway easement retains the underlying trust interests and contains no express transfer of judicial jurisdiction. Public passage can coexist with adjudication of wrongs associated with tribal economic activity. The six's road analogy therefore does not, for him, establish that otherwise supported civil forum authority has been surrendered.

He does not rest on the easement label alone or claim complete authority over any nonmember who crosses the reservation. Even taking Montana as the operative residual framework, its first exception concerns the nonmember's activities arising from a consensual relationship, not merely claims between signatories for breach of the agreement. If Stockert was carrying out the Tribe's community-center project when the collision occurred, the activity and associated road injury could supply a sufficiently direct connection to governing that reservation undertaking. The public nature of the road is important, but does not necessarily make the activity unrelated to the voluntarily assumed project. The strongest objection is that ordinary highway duties are owed to everyone; his answer is that a generally stated duty can still govern the performance and consequences of a specific consensual reservation enterprise.

The present record does not permit replacing that conditional proposition with a finding that the trip was in performance of the subcontract. Nor may a court turn the absence of proof in this motion record into a finding that it was unrelated. He therefore vacates and remands for the federal courts to apply a conduct-specific jurisdiction inquiry to the proper record and its procedural posture. The residence allegation and admission, subsequent dispute and stricken materials require their actual procedural treatment if residence affects that analysis; no historical residence finding or nonresidence finding is invented. The first exception supplies a sufficient reason for further proceedings. Generalized community welfare does not itself become an independently sufficient final jurisdiction ground.

**Breyer.** His Chickasaw income dissent rests on a particular protected governmental relationship, member/employer/work nexus and source, not generalized territorial sovereignty. He likewise regards the voluntary tribal construction undertaking and actual relationship of the driving to it as potentially material. He would not decide the broader question whether every compatible adjudicatory power survives this easement. Even assuming the six's Montana framework, a categorical exclusion of a work-related third-party accident because its claimant did not sign the subcontract is too narrow. The ordinary nature of a tort duty does not by itself settle whether regulation of the activity falls within the consensual relationship.

He therefore joins vacatur/remand at the first-exception scope, requiring proper assessment of the allegedly project-serving trip and any legally material residence issue. He does not adopt Souter's broader objection as a necessary holding, deem work purpose proved, require a finding of jurisdiction, or create a broad exception for every injured reservation resident. Montana's second exception remains a distinct inquiry rather than an automatic consequence of the first. A court must respect the actual federal pleading admission and the limits of the existing evidentiary rulings rather than order factfinding on an invented factual premise.

For both Justices, remand is to the federal courts for proceedings consistent with the corrected legal inquiry. It is not a mandatory new tribal evidentiary hearing, a command to repeat already completed exhaustion, or an immediate revival of the District Court's ultimate jurisdiction judgment. Which additional proceedings are legally needed depends on the existing record, procedural rules and the factual predicates actually material under the stated inquiry.

## Preservation, remedy and remaining source limits

All eight decline to rest independently on the newly urged 1825/1851 treaty theory or the unratified 1866 agreement. The presented highway/Montana grounds permit their decisions without deciding that contested late theory; no holding that it is necessarily forfeited is required. No unverified historical term is converted into an operative exclusive-state-jurisdiction clause, and appropriations do not silently ratify the 1866 agreement. Were a later stage to select that independent ground, its exact primary terms and the basis for reaching it would be required before reliance.

The six's affirmance permits appropriate conforming declaratory relief on remand concerning Fredericks's reviewed claim. It does not itself issue the requested injunctions or decide entitlement to every form of coercive relief, costs or fees. The amended answer expressly consents to defending declaratory relief on tribal civil jurisdiction while withholding consent to other claims/issues/relief/liability; Resolution 92-025-JJR is likewise limited to protecting and asserting jurisdiction in this case. The opinions' descriptions of limited defense of injunctive claims do not erase those instruments. The lower courts must respect the actual lawful parties, immunity limits and any otherwise established basis and equitable predicates for relief. No general waiver or independent broad immunity ruling follows. The two remand votes likewise decide no entitlement to federal coercive relief.

No material source gap blocks these commitments. The six explain why assumed work purpose and residence cannot change their result; Souter and Breyer identify precisely why the work-connection record and any legally material residence procedure require further judicial consideration. That is an adjudicative remand on an undeveloped motion record, not a research-failure default or an instruction to produce nonexistent documents. Exact parcel title, incorporated maps, separate consent and a controlling contract clause are not grounds of this freeze. No negligence, damages, personal jurisdiction, consortium jurisdiction, insurer liability or state limitations result is decided.
