**Cutoff:** June 25, 1997. **Status:** ready for fresh neutral modeling with the qualifications below. This report supplies facts and legal boundaries, not a vote or disposition. It supersedes any implication in the original neutral packet that authorized consent and a lawful replacement plan alone establish authority for this federal decree.

## Record conclusion

The complete Joint Appendix identifies the litigated original federal theory and the district court's accepted legal predicate as racial territorial design under historical Shaw/Miller. It supplies no independently sustained finding of dilution, exclusion, unequal participation or another federal violation by Plan 330. The conclusion must not be shortened to “no allegation or evidence of other injury”: complaint paragraph 9, the representation allegations, polarized-voting evidence and actual election-administration harms require the distinctions below. Their presence does not establish their elements, preservation, causal connection or remedial significance as an independent claim against Plan 330.

## Settlement, authority and judgment

The settlement, JA 17–21, expressly identifies the Shaw/Miller claim. Defendants deny unconstitutionality but agree, **for settlement only**, that the record gives the claim a reasonable factual and legal basis. Expense, unsettled law, litigation risk and electoral certainty explain compromise. The settling plaintiffs agree to dismiss their Plan 330 constitutional claims; Lawyer does not sign. Plan 386 is to govern after court approval unless Florida adopts a replacement conforming to state and federal law. These are not unconditional admissions of liability or an independently enacted districting law.

Wallace's declaration identifies House litigation authority, JA 137–138. Counsel and the court address officials' authority and legislative participation at JA 160–163, 197 and 206. The record supports a proposal submitted by state officials and the legislative chambers as litigants. It supplies no formal enactment of Plan 386 through a legislative joint resolution or a superseding state-court judgment. The district court's description of the proposal as primarily legislative does not establish either event.

The final order is dated **March 19, 1996**, JA 195–209. The November 1995 settlement proceedings must not be mistaken for that final judgment. The neutral packet's statement that the district court approved the settlement in 1995 is therefore corrected. The complaint filing day varies across sources (April 4 in the final order, April 14 in the appellant submissions); that variance has no identified material effect here.

The majority first requires a materially and fairly contestable constitutional dispute, JA 198–199. Footnote 3, JA 201–202, preserves the settlement-only nature of the concession and explains defendants' wish to defend Plan 330 if a materially different remedy were selected. It then applies historical Miller to boundaries, racial composition and race awareness, JA 202–203, and identifies that racial-design dispute as cognizable, JA 205. It does not find an independent dilution or exclusion violation. Chief Judge Tjoflat separately would adjudicate Plan 330 unconstitutional on the same historical Shaw/Miller theory, JA 208–209; that is not a majority finding of a different injury.

The operative order grants the settlement motion, denies all other pending motions, immediately redistricts six specified districts according to the incorporated Plan 386 description, and retains jurisdiction only for fees and costs, JA 208. Footnote 4 calls the arrangement a hybrid of consent concerning liability and an adversary remedial hearing. That judicial characterization does not erase the actual nonadmission terms or Lawyer's objection. Whether his properly raised valid claim was disposed of lawfully remains distinct from whether a dissenting litigant has a veto over others' settlement.

## Event-date legal dependencies

The [B entering-law slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK12_B.md) provides the governing rules: **Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993; Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993; Johnson v. Miller, June 29, 1995; Bush v. Vera and consolidated cases, June 13, 1996; Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board and Price, May 12, 1997; Agostini v. Felton, June 23, 1997.** These actual holdings govern over the historical legal premises appearing in the source litigation.

Shaw/Miller/Vera reject territorial racial predominance alone as the asserted substantive injury. They preserve ordinary pleading and independently cognizable constitutional and statutory claims; no later electoral loss or second governmental act is invariably required. Failure of that merits theory is not automatically absence of Article III jurisdiction. Vera's termination of relief dependent on its sole adjudicated design claim does not establish that this differently structured settlement has already been resolved. Growe protects timely state work and gives actual superseding state judgments legal effect; it does not turn an officials' settlement proposal into an enacted replacement. Bossier keeps preclearance and independent section 2 liability separate. Clearance neither establishes comprehensive legality nor supplies an affirmative federal claim.

The [appellee source review](OT_1996CHUNK12_LAWYER_APPELLEE_SOURCES.md) verifies the official **Local No. 93, International Association of Firefighters v. Cleveland, 478 U.S. 501 (1986), pp. 525–526, 528–530**. Consent can support relief beyond a post-trial award, but the dispute must be within subject-matter jurisdiction, the decree within the pleadings' general scope, and the decree must further the underlying law's objectives without conflicting with that law. A nonconsenter may be heard and present evidence; others' agreement cannot extinguish that party's properly raised valid claims or impose duties without consent. The Title VII ruling does not resolve every substantive objection to a decree. Accordingly, the fresh model must test the strongest consent-based route while identifying what current, legally cognizable claim and lawful governmental authority support it. Neither universal insistence on an adjudicated violation before settlement nor consent alone is supplied as the rule.

**Plyler v. Moore, June 23, 1997**, in the [public delta](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK12_JUNE23_PUBLIC_DELTA.md), distinguishes negotiated obligations from the underlying federal right and applies the original PLRA's conditional termination and current-or-ongoing-violation preservation provisions. Its recognition that Rufo permits lawful negotiated obligations beyond constitutional minima does not decide initial authority for this districting decree. Agostini likewise preserves the distinct conditions for modifying an existing injunction and voluntarily accepted obligations; it does not supply a missing original claim. No PLRA rule applies to Lawyer. The [June 25 dependency review](OT_1996CHUNK12_JUNE25_NEUTRAL_DEPENDENCY_REVIEW.md) records the other five matters' unchanged premises.
