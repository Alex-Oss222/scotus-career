## Newly effective rules and their limits

- **Reich, January 14:** The Court permits confined prospective equitable review of the implementing Secretary's action beyond delegated statutory authority, separating §1331 jurisdiction, the officer-directed equitable action and §702's immunity waiver. It holds that the Procurement Act's general delegation does not authorize Executive Order 12954's government-wide sanction for permanent replacement of economic strikers, in light of the bargaining choices preserved by the NLRA. The decision preserves materially different proprietary conditions and lawful centralized purchasing standards. It neither supplies a general action for every statutory error nor makes a procurement label dispositive, creates no universal one-project requirement and grants no damages or individualized labor remedy.
- **Castano, January 14:** The actual Rule 23(a) and (b)(3) prerequisites must be demonstrated before certification. The unsupported core-liability and punitive certification fails, but novelty creates no categorical tort-maturation bar and a materially narrower supported proposal may be considered. The Court does not change individual substantive burdens, create a federal tobacco tort, decide every issue-certification question, or reopen the unappealed Rule 23(b)(2) denial.
- **Deuel, January 14:** Section 103(a) requires a supported connection between the prior art, ordinary skill and the claimed product; neither absence of an exact printed sequence nor a general known technique determines obviousness categorically. The existing sequence and genus rejections independently fail on their stated agency grounds. The Court affirms that judgment without granting a patent or commanding reopening, while preserving independently proper examination under ordinary notice and procedure. Its holding supplies neither a copyright-equivalence rule nor a universal administrative evidentiary burden.
