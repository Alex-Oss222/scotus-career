## Question, objective record and remedy

Whether the unobjected judicial determination of criminal materiality warrants appellate correction under Rule 52(b). Johnson seeks reversal and a new trial, not acquittal for insufficient evidence. The United States seeks affirmance. The Fifth and Sixth Amendment allocation of a genuine offense element and the remedy for forfeiture remain distinct.

Johnson was prosecuted under **18 U.S.C. §1623**, rather than §1001 or §1014, for statements to a grand jury investigating Earl James Fields's drug proceeds. She attributed $80,000–$120,000 of home-improvement funds to a box of cash given to her mother by Gerald Talcott in 1985 or 1986. Trial evidence included Fields's involvement in the property purchase, cashier's checks including checks from an associated corporation, and Talcott's death in April 1982. The District Judge told the jury that materiality was for the court and that the statements were material, following the then-governing Eleventh Circuit approach. Johnson did not object to that instruction and earlier objected to materiality evidence on the ground that the issue was for the judge. The jury found her guilty. No new finding concerning deliberate relinquishment is made from those circumstances alone.

The control-supplied objective recitation reports that the Eleventh Circuit used Olano, assumed error and plainness, and denied relief at the substantial-rights step after its record review characterized the evidence of materiality as overwhelming and incapable of a reasonable contrary conclusion. **That is the lower court's assessment, not an independently recovered jury finding on materiality.** The accusation's truth or falsity and its natural tendency or capacity to influence the grand-jury investigation are different elements. Materiality need not require proof that the grand jury actually relied on the lie. If independent analysis depends on a particular disputed trial passage, concession or alternative materiality theory, the underlying record passage must be obtained through control rather than inferred from this characterization.

Gaudin intervened before completion of direct review. Griffith supplies ordinary direct-review application of the controlling constitutional rule. No established valid waiver is supplied; failure to object ordinarily presents forfeiture. Any proposed invited-error/waiver disposition must identify a preserved legal basis and actual intentional-relinquishment predicate rather than convert compliance with then-settled circuit law automatically into extinguishment of the right.

## Entering law and material current-term developments

Read [the selected entering-law slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK7_82.md) and its [current-public reading copy](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK7_B_CURRENT_PUBLIC_POSITIONS.md).

- **Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)**: present operation effective Olano, April 26, 1993. Error, plainness and substantial-rights impact are the initial conditions; defendant ordinarily bears prejudice. Intentional valid waiver extinguishes error, unlike forfeiture. Even satisfaction of those conditions permits rather than compels correction; serious effect on fairness, integrity or public reputation is distinct and actual innocence is not necessary. Olano leaves newly clarified appellate error, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions open.
- **Jury determination of criminal materiality**: present operation effective Gaudin, June 19, 1995. All nine require jury determination of an actual materiality element beyond reasonable doubt. The judge supplies law, but a mixed law/fact label cannot transfer the element. Gaudin does not classify every withheld-element error as structural.
- **A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error**: present operation effective Sullivan, June 1, 1993. Vitiation of the reasonable-doubt standard for every finding leaves no valid verdict for harmlessness analysis. Its rule does not automatically classify every element omission.
- **Roy, November 4, 1996:** All nine agreed that its preserved personal-intent omission was trial error, not automatically structural; the State bore Chapman within that protected habeas category. Souter, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer permit supported whole-record inferences tied reliably to the actual verdict, not merely sufficient evidence for a hypothetical conviction. Scalia and Ginsburg's noncontrolling position demands actual findings necessarily embracing the omitted purpose; Stevens rejects that as an exclusive rule but found the particular record harmful. Roy neither decides this materiality omission nor transfers its preserved-error burden to Rule 52(b). It materially forecloses reliance on a universal premise that every omitted element destroys the verdict automatically.
- **Wells, February 26, 1997:** Section 1014 lacks a distinct objective-materiality element at its specified scope; that statute-specific construction does not remove §1623's element or limit Gaudin. Wells preserves independent trial issues and makes no automatic harmlessness finding. Its bounded willingness to review an actually decided and granted statutory question despite the Government's earlier trial position is not a universal waiver rule for Johnson.

## Lawful analytical paths and strongest objections

1. **Affirm for insufficient substantial-rights showing.** Recognize the jury-allocation error, determine plainness at the legally appropriate time, and apply ordinary defendant-borne outcome prejudice to the supported record. Strongest objection: an appellate conclusion that evidence is overwhelming can substitute its own element finding for the jury; Gaudin, Sullivan and Roy require attention to what a real verdict establishes. The Court must explain why ordinary prejudice is the correct category and whether the record's uncontested character answers that objection.
2. **Affirm at the independent discretionary step.** Recognize or assume the preceding conditions and decide that correction is unwarranted in these established circumstances. Strongest objection: treating evidence alone as dispositive can hollow out the jury-element guarantee or collapse the fourth step into the third. Olano nevertheless gives the fourth step independent force even after substantial-rights impact; a confined decision must explain that force without announcing a categorical harmlessness rule or demanding actual innocence.
3. **Reverse for a new trial.** Hold that the particular omission affected substantial rights and seriously impaired the proceeding's fairness or integrity, either through a justified prejudice presumption or a supported application to the actual record. Strongest objection: Gaudin expressly reserved error classification, Roy rejects automatic structural treatment for every omitted element, and Olano distinguishes a protected right from automatic correction after forfeiture. A categorical reversal theory therefore requires an articulated distinction between this defect and those existing cases, not merely the label constitutional.
4. **Limited remand if an indispensable record or legal classification remains unresolved.** The court below can apply a clarified standard to a genuine unfinished issue. Strongest objection: no remand should be ordered solely because an operator lacks a document or to reconsider facts already adequately established. Identify the exact needed judicial task and its relation to the proper standard.

Newly articulated alternatives above are analytical possibilities grounded in the supported record, not invented party filings. No path may deny that materiality was a jury element merely because the resulting judgment is affirmance. Nor may a reversal create an acquittal or forbid a lawful retrial.
