## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether officers violate the Fourth Amendment by inviting news media into a private home to observe and record execution of a search warrant when their presence serves no legitimate law-enforcement purpose.

2. Whether a warrant authorizing officers to enter a home also authorizes private third parties whose presence is unrelated to locating, identifying or seizing evidence.

3. Whether media presence and recording impose an additional unreasonable intrusion notwithstanding the validity of the officers' warrant.

4. Whether Mottola is entitled to qualified immunity for the alleged media invitation and intrusion, assessed against law clearly established when the March 5, 1992 search occurred.

## C. GOVERNING SIMULATED LAW AND ITS APPLICATION

The pre-divergence Bivens action concerns an individual federal officer's alleged Fourth Amendment home-search violation. It does not make §1983 the cause against a federal officer. Simulated FDIC v. Meyer maintains the distinction among an actionable wrong, an authorized defendant and damages; it rejects a direct agency Bivens extension. The existing individual-officer channel and requested personal damages must therefore remain separate from agency or media liability.

The Fourth Amendment limits the purpose, scope and manner of execution as well as the warrant's issuance. Simulated Wilson v. Arkansas treats the manner of dwelling entry as part of constitutional reasonableness but does not constitutionalize every federal entry statute. Section 3105 permits requested aid in executing a warrant; it is not a general license for an officer to admit spectators. Simulated Soldal preserves independent possessory and privacy protections. Here the alleged wrong is added invasion of domestic privacy by government-arranged observers and recording, whether or not copying images also meets a distinct seizure definition. A valid evidentiary search does not alone decide whether the additional intrusion was reasonable.

Simulated Elder and Pinder require the separate, particularized immunity inquiry using all relevant law in existence when the officer acted. A factual twin is unnecessary, but a present merits conclusion does not establish past notice. Their procedural and notice rules govern this review; they are not themselves notice available on March 5, 1992. Soldal and Wilson likewise postdate the conduct. Before that date, Bivens described the warrant's bounds; Andresen required restraint of unnecessary intrusions in documentary searches; Anderson supplied apparent-unlawfulness analysis; and Second Circuit Matias and Rivera addressed warrant limits and intrusive treatment of persons outside the investigation. Section 3105 reinforces the execution-purpose distinction without independently creating this constitutional damages claim or automatically defeating immunity under Davis v. Scherer. Bills v. Aseltine also postdates this search and cannot supply retroactive notice.

Simulated Johnson v. Jones and Behrens confine this immediate appeal to the legal defense on properly assumed facts. The Court cannot decide whether Mottola actually invited the crew, choose contrary factual accounts or use his appeal to adjudicate other parties' defenses. Neither decision makes every immunity case subject to a mandatory merits-first sequence.

## D. POSITIONS, REMEDIES AND REMAINING LIMITS

Mottola invokes the absence of a prior decision on this precise media practice and the authority conferred by the warrant. The plaintiffs contend that the defined home-search purpose excluded nonassisting observers and that recording intensified the unauthorized intrusion. Affirming denial would permit the claim to proceed, not establish responsibility, causation or a damages amount. Reversing on immunity would protect Mottola on this claim without declaring a general media-entry entitlement. Genuine search assistance, valid occupant consent and necessary documentation for the investigation would present different facts; none is assumed to exist here. The proceeding is Court-directed appellate review of a legal collateral order.
