## 137. Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — No. 96-270

### Components and competing lawful paths

**A1 — Reach and sequencing.** Rejecting the proposed certification is logically antecedent to the standing objections produced by including putative class members within this proposed settlement. That is a refusal to authorize representative adjudication, not an assumed exercise of Article III power to resolve asbestos liability or release absent persons' claims. Steel Co. forbids assumed jurisdiction for contested statutory merits; its nonmerits-sequencing reservation permits this limited order of decision. This commitment neither establishes each named person's standing nor permits a settlement to be approved without the necessary jurisdiction. Individual injury and notice objections unnecessary to rejection remain undecided.

**A2 — Rule 23 and settlement.** Settlement eliminates the need to assess manageability at a trial that will not occur. It does not replace Rule 23(a), predominance or the actual governing-law inquiry with an overall assessment of a bargain's desirability. Castano requires a demonstrated basis for representative treatment; Matsushita and Richards separately preserve genuine adequate representation. Those cases do not make every Rule 23 defect constitutional.

**A3 — Adequacy.** Presently injured people seeking current payments and exposure-only people needing secure future protection have materially different negotiating interests under this capped, nonindexed arrangement. Nine undifferentiated representatives and common counsel do not supply the separate advocacy these interests require. A favorable aggregate bargain, notice and an opportunity to exclude oneself cannot alone cure this structural defect. The defect is sufficient to leave certification, settlement approval and its injunction vacated. This does not decide that every injured/exposure-only combination is inherently impossible, order particular subclasses or approve a replacement proposal.

**A4 — Predominance.** Common exposure to asbestos and the offer of one settlement do not establish that common legal questions predominate over materially different governing state law, exposure, injuries and claims in this proposed class. The record supplies no demonstrated lawful grouping that performs the work Castano requires. A settlement can remove proof-management obstacles; it cannot manufacture a common underlying legal entitlement. A4 is an additional ground only for Justices expressly joining it below.

**Adversarial comparison.** The strongest path to reversal/remand is that the lower court used litigation manageability or demanded identical claims despite a settlement's real common benefits. A2 rejects those errors without validating this proposal. The strongest objection to affirmance is excessive appellate substitution for the district court's evaluation of an innovative bargain. A3 answers it through the supported conflict in who bargains for future protection, rather than a different valuation of the settlement. A narrower adequacy-only affirmance avoids unnecessary predominance generalization; a broader ban on settlement-only or nationwide tort classes has no support here.

## 138. City of Boerne v. Flores — No. 95-2074

### Components and competing lawful paths

**B1 — Reach.** The accepted §1292(b) appeal properly presents Congress's authority to impose RFRA on States/localities. Prior Church of Scientology and Swanner applied the statute while expressly reserving constitutionality. Neither their application nor Agostini resolves §5. The permit's boundaries, actual substantial burden and asserted justification have not been adjudicated and are not decided here.

**B2 — Enforcement-power path.** Congress may use preventive as well as remedial means to secure the Fourteenth Amendment's guarantees, including measures reaching some constitutionally permissible conduct. The limit is that the means must enforce the constitutional right rather than substitute a new substantive standard for the right itself. With Smith still governing genuinely neutral, generally applicable laws, RFRA's universal person-specific compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means command reaches State/local burdens without a tether sufficient to identify it as prevention or remedy for unconstitutional treatment. B2 would reverse the Fifth Circuit on State/local RFRA authority and remand accordingly. This is not a requirement of a prior judicial violation for every statutory protection, nor a holding that Congress lacks predictive or preventive power.

**B3 — Constitutional-baseline path.** O'Connor's Smith concurrence applied compelling-interest review while agreeing with the judgment; Souter's public Lukumi writing called for reconsideration in a case that required it. If Smith's underlying constitutional rule is unsound, an invalidity judgment built upon its restrictive baseline may decide the wrong antecedent issue. This path calls for focused further briefing/reargument on Smith, its treatment of neutral burdens, its relation to Sherbert and Yoder, and the resulting §5 inquiry before a final merits judgment. It does not presume an unbriefed overruling, uphold RFRA now, or grant the permit.

**Adversarial comparison.** The strongest objection to B2 is the breadth of Morgan and South Carolina v. Katzenbach: constitutional enforcement is not confined to judicially identified instances, and difficult-to-prove discrimination can justify prophylaxis. The answer must preserve that discretion while distinguishing enforcement of an existing right from a general replacement standard; no invented legislative inventory or absence of discrimination is used. The strongest objection to B3 is that Smith is binding and the granted question concerns §5, so the Court can answer it now. B3's proponents regard the constitutional baseline as sufficiently consequential to require a properly framed reconsideration rather than implicit endorsement. A third path sustaining RFRA merely because Congress can expand rights beyond constitutional minima is rejected; broader statutory protection needs a valid source of federal authority.

**Relevant primary sources:** [Smith, including O'Connor's concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/494/872); [Morgan](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/384/641), which applies broad appropriate-means review and does not require case-by-case antecedent judicial invalidation; [Gregory majority](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/90-50.ZO.html) and [White/Stevens concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/90-50.ZC.html), whose federalism/interpretation dispute does not itself decide an expressly stated §5 enactment. The actual earlier public Lukumi writing is in [OT1992 chunk 8](../../OT1992/output/OT_1992CHUNK8.md). The original RFRA source is linked in preflight B; the statutory qualifications there are retained, not replaced by a general immunity.

## 139. Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc. — No. 95-1184

### Components and competing lawful paths

**G1 — Reach and characterization.** The challenged item is compelled financing of generic commodity advertising in comprehensive collective marketing orders. Compelled funding can burden expression without attribution, ideological content or suppression of a producer's own advertising. The absence of those additional burdens informs the analysis; it is not a categorical answer. Separate statutory challenges to the orders remain separate.

**G2 — Integrated-program ground for reversal.** On the supported scheme, generic promotion is germane to an otherwise lawful, substantially collective marketing arrangement governing product characteristics and common marketing decisions. A producer may be charged its share of that common economic activity without choosing each message, where its own speech remains free and no personal endorsement is demanded. This ground would reverse the First Amendment invalidation and remand the remaining statutory matters. It does not immunize every agricultural assessment, every commercial message or a stand-alone advertising association imposed on an otherwise independent market.

**G3 — Compelled-subsidy ground against the assessment.** Mere benefit, majority approval or extensive regulation does not establish the justification necessary to compel dissenters to finance an expressive campaign. Abood/Keller/Lehnert distinguish expenditures made necessary by a legally imposed representative function from promoting interests that beneficiaries could pursue voluntarily. On this record the generic advertising charge is not shown to be a necessary incident of a duty to perform an otherwise unavoidable representative service. The asserted common economic benefit does not suffice. G3 supports affirming the judgment against the advertising assessments, without treating every nonexpressive order requirement as invalid.

**G4 — Commercial-speech alternative.** If review proceeds under commercial-speech advancement and fit rather than G2/G3, the government must justify the actual compelled-support burden. The lower judgment addresses both advancement and fit. Neither a mandatory empirical-study rule nor least-restrictive-means review may be imposed. These commitments do not turn the lower court's conclusions into new Supreme Court findings about effectiveness; no Justice below relies on invented advertisements, brand dilution or a new evidentiary failure. G4 is reserved unless a row specifies an operative ground.

**Adversarial comparison.** G2's strongest objection is that one cannot define the government's project to include advertising and thereby make compelled speech self-justifying. Its proponents therefore depend on the already comprehensive economic integration and limit their conclusion to germane common marketing. G3's strongest objection is that it improperly separates one shared marketing expense from collective obligations which dissenters may lawfully be required to finance. Its proponents respond that regulation does not eliminate the independent First Amendment question and that the exclusive-representation cases require more than shared commercial benefit. Hurley concerns control of an actual private expressive product; Rosenberger expressly left compulsory fees open. Neither resolves this funding dispute. Turner II retained independent speech scrutiny, not a general economic-regulation exception.

**Direct primary anchor:** [Lehnert, 500 U.S. 507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/500/507). Stevens's separate opinion permitted broader germane public-relations expenditure. Scalia's separate opinion, joined by O'Connor and Souter and by Kennedy except its strike-preparation section, confined compulsory fees to the legally imposed representative function; Kennedy's separate writing resisted an unduly rigid treatment of reasonably incurred costs. These are different rules, not interchangeable votes for either result here. Current Gibson, 44 Liquormart and Turner II provide the later individual commitments used below.

## 140. Lawyer v. Department of Justice — No. 95-2024

### Components and competing lawful paths

**L1 — Settlement and state authority.** The supported Florida process permitted adoption of a replacement proposed by the Attorney General, with legislative counsel participation and the political branches declining special sessions. Growe's opportunity for timely State districting is not a requirement of a legislative vote in every case. The fairness hearing and preserved right to challenge Plan 386 permit approval without first adjudicating liability under the obsolete plan. This is a conclusion on the supplied State-authorized process, not universal authority for any attorney general to redraw districts.

**L2 — Objector's rights.** A nonconsenting litigant may present objections and retain independent claims; he cannot veto other parties' lawful settlement merely by refusing consent. The decree may not impose an unconsented personal obligation or adjudicate away his valid independent claims. Those protections are material conditions of affirmance, satisfied by the supplied hearing and retained challenge opportunity. [Firefighters, 478 U.S. 501](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/478/501), including O'Connor's insistence that settlement does not immunize unlawful substantive relief, supplies the distinction.

**L3 — Governing substantive law.** Under the actual Shaw/Miller/Vera line, racial territorial design or predominance alone is not the cognizable constitutional deprivation. Independent exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, inferior civic status and other recognized violations retain their own rules. None is established by the supplied demographic/design facts. Bossier does not transform the possibility of additional minority opportunity or a purpose dispute into a finding of illegality.

**L4 — Factual alternative.** The district court found that county, geographical, community and incumbency considerations were not subordinated to race. The supported findings are not clearly erroneous. The court may affirm without deciding whether predominance alone should ever suffice, because this record fails even that asserted trigger. Miller's unanimous evidence/clear-error components supply the governing review; every disputed districting fact is not reweighed de novo.

**Adversarial comparison.** The strongest route to reversal would be an actual defect in State authorization, extinguishment of the objector's independent claim, an unconsented duty, or an independently unlawful replacement. The neutral packet establishes none; refusal to settle is insufficient. The strongest objection to an L3 affirmance is the four Justices' retained view that racial classification itself may inflict constitutional harm. L4 permits those Justices to affirm without abandoning that objection, but they may not call the displaced historical theory controlling law. A demand for an old-plan liability adjudication would do no remedial work where lawful replacement and live individual challenges are preserved.

## 141. United States v. O'Hagan — No. 96-842

### Components and competing lawful paths

**O1 — Scope and actual deception.** Review covers mail fraud (counts 1–20), §10(b)/Rule 10b-5 (21–37) and §14(e)/Rule 14e-3 (38–54). The separate laundering disposition (55–57) is outside review. A fiduciary's secretly disloyal conversion of entrusted confidential information while feigning fidelity can deceive the source; unfairness, unequal market knowledge or occupational status alone cannot. Disclosure to the source may remove this theory's deception but is not a universal exemption from securities or other law.

**O2 — §10(b) nexus supporting reversal.** When the concealed conversion is accomplished through the fiduciary's securities trades, source deception is sufficiently connected to those purchases or sales even though the source is not the trading counterparty. This is primary deceptive conduct, not a general information-parity duty or automatic criminal aiding-and-abetting liability. Chiarella's duty requirement remains; its reserved misappropriation question is answered on this confined theory. The in-world Central Bank does not forbid it and supplies no criminal scienter shortcut.

**O3 — Competing §10(b) nexus ground supporting affirmance.** Deception of the employer/client concerning misuse of its property does not necessarily deceive a securities counterparty or concern the transaction in the sense required by §10(b). The fact that securities trading is the means of profiting from a general property fraud is insufficient on this reading. Source fraud can remain criminal under the mail-fraud statute even when the securities nexus fails. Carpenter's equally divided securities disposition settles neither path nor any current Justice's vote.

**O4 — Independent §14(e) authority.** Section 14(e) expressly authorizes reasonably designed preventive means. A rule need not make every covered trade independently fraudulent under §10(b). Rule 14e-3(a)'s specified tender-offer setting, source and knowledge restrictions reasonably prevent deception around confidential tender information; no separate fiduciary-duty element is necessary to its validity as applied to the alleged source-informed trading. Schreiber's footnote 11 recognizes latitude to regulate nondeceptive activity as a means of prevention while preserving the underlying statutory meaning of manipulation. MCI requires genuine delegation; this express preventive text supplies what a mere power to modify did not.

**O4 limits.** The offeror must have taken substantial steps to commence, or have commenced, a tender offer. The trader other than the offeror must possess material tender information, know or have reason to know it is nonpublic, and know or have reason to know that it came directly or indirectly from the offeror, issuer, or their officer, director, partner, employee or agent. Covered trades include the identified securities, convertible/exchangeable securities and related options or rights, including causing covered purchases or sales. Trading is not barred under this provision when the information and its source are publicly disclosed a reasonable time beforehand. This commitment neither prohibits all advantageous information trading nor adjudicates irrelevant regulatory exceptions. Criminal willfulness and any lack-of-knowledge limitation remain distinct from rule validity and are preserved for remand.

**O5 — Mail fraud and mandate.** Carpenter recognizes confidential business information as property and permits mail fraud for its deceptive appropriation when the required use of the mails furthers the scheme. It does not make any professional breach mail fraud automatically. The lower court's dependency on rejection of the securities theories cannot remain conclusive when O4, or O2, is sustained. Vacate the mail disposition and remand to examine the actual charged theory and preserved instructions, sufficiency, causation/use of mails and error-effect questions. Do not automatically reinstate convictions or decide that an independent property-fraud theory was necessarily submitted correctly. Preserve the unreviewed laundering disposition.

**Adversarial comparison.** O2's strongest objection is that the transaction may merely follow a completed source fraud, leaving §10(b) without a limiting nexus. Its proponents require deceptive appropriation through the securities use itself, not any later expenditure of stolen money. O3's strongest objection is that the enacted language does not restrict the victim to a buyer or seller and the deception here accomplishes its end in the trades. O4's strongest objection is that prevention could become an unlimited equality-of-information code; the answer must rest on the express delegation and the rule's concrete tender, source and knowledge limits, not market fairness alone. O5 separately rejects both automatic reinstatement and automatic invalidity of mail fraud whenever a securities count fails.

**Primary anchors:** [Chiarella, including Stevens's concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/445/222); [Carpenter](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/484/19); [Schreiber, including note 11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/1); [Eighth Circuit judgment](https://openjurist.org/92/f3d/612/united-states-v-ohagan), whose historical Central Bank premise is displaced in-world. For Breyer, [SEC v. MacDonald, 699 F.2d 47](https://openjurist.org/699/f2d/47) confirms his participation in statutory insider-liability and bounded disgorgement decisions without deciding source misappropriation; [Mayburg, 740 F.2d 100](https://openjurist.org/740/f2d/100/mayburg-v-secretary-of-health-and-human-services), his own opinion, distinguishes actual delegated authority from deference merely because an agency adopted a position.
