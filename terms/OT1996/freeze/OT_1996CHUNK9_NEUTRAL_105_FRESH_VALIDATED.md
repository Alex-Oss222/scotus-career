## Posture and question

The Eleventh Circuit's complete July 9, 1996 opinion, 88 F.3d 1573, states it permitted appeal of the district court's interlocutory orders under §1292(b). It reexamined the county question after its earlier Swint disposition was vacated on jurisdictional grounds. Simulated Swint likewise determines appellate reach, not sheriff attribution, and leaves a properly authorized §1292(b) route available. There is no appellate-route defect established here.

The granted question concerns whether Sheriff Tate's criminal-investigation and law-enforcement acts are attributable as final policy to Monroe County under §1983. The County was dismissed. Other individual claims and the lower opinion's hearsay and summary-judgment rulings are not independently granted questions merely because they appear in the same opinion. The lower court held that Tate was not a final County policymaker in law enforcement; it expressly did not decide whether he was a final State policymaker. The question need not be turned into a universal binary status ruling.

McMillian's murder conviction and death sentence were reversed for nondisclosure of exculpatory and impeachment evidence; the State dismissed the charges and began another investigation. He alleges officials manufactured inculpatory evidence, suppressed favorable material, and caused unlawful detention. Those allegations are not civil findings of the defendants' liability. No County liability verdict is present.

## State structure and competing characterizations

Alabama Constitution article V, §112 places a sheriff for each county in the State executive department. Article V, §138 provides election by county electors. State decisions Hereford and Parker v. Amerson treat sheriffs as State employees for the described state-law immunity and vicarious-liability purposes. That classification supplies evidence of legal structure, not an automatic federal §1983 conclusion.

Section 36-22-3 requires attendance on specified courts, obedience to lawful court orders, crime investigation and apprehension, securing evidence and reporting it to the district attorney. Section 36-22-5 requires investigation when directed in writing by the district attorney, attorney general or governor, with a prompt report to the directing officer. These duties are locally exercised; the scope of the territory alone does not identify the governmental entity represented.

County funding and administration are substantial. The sheriff keeps an office at the courthouse. The county treasury pays the salary and investigative expenses; the commission supplies reasonably needed quarters, office materials, equipment, automobiles, repairs and maintenance. The specific expense scheme requires a detailed sworn statement and approval, permits county audit of reasonable necessity, and requires allowance of the amount approved by the governor or attorney general. Association dues are discretionary. Fees go to the county general fund subject to the distinct prisoner-feeding exception and county-resolution proviso. The interleaved appendix does not establish unlimited county operational authority, and payment duties do not themselves confer power to direct investigations.

The county-attribution path emphasizes election, local accountability, funding, territorial service, and independently final authority within county law enforcement. No separate commission review is necessary if state law vested county authority directly in an elected sheriff. The state-attribution path emphasizes the constitutional executive placement, state judicial characterization, duties to state courts and prosecutorial officers, and absence of County power over this investigative function. Neither path may reduce the inquiry to a label or assume that the commission is the County's only possible policymaker.

The lower majority distinguishes Parker v. Williams's jail-personnel partnership rationale, citing counties' distinct jail powers. Judge Propst separately questions that jail analysis. His concurrence is not a controlling alteration of jail law; this criminal-investigation case does not decide every sheriff function, every Alabama county theory, or sheriffs in other States.

## Governing law and alternatives

Monell requires a policy, custom, or attributable final policymaker's decision causing the federal injury; ordinary respondeat superior is insufficient. Pembaur permits a single final policymaker's decision. Praprotnik's state-law identification approach must be read at its fractured scope, with Jett supplying the later majority adoption identified in the opinion. State and local positive law, custom with legal force, actual delegations, and the function in issue matter; federal law determines the §1983 consequence. The existence of unconstitutional conduct and its attribution to the sued institution are separate.

The entering slice supplies unanimous Collins's separation of deprivation and municipal responsibility and Swint's bounded jurisdiction rules. The actual May 27 Kinman public delta is reviewed and incorporated: Ginsburg's unanimous institutional part requires policy/custom or an attributable final decision and finds neither on its school record. It specifically preserves the single-final-policymaker route, distinguishes supervisory status and personal misconduct from institutional attribution, and confers no personal immunity on the District. Its eight-Associate personal-supervisor affirmance is a different culpability inquiry. Neither Title IX's recipient standard nor the personal deliberate-disregard rule replaces Monell's attribution test here.

Bryan County remains pending for reargument under its April 28 order. No Supreme Court hiring, fault, causation, training, or remedial merits rule from that case exists. The other four May 27 public holdings concern distinct subjects and change no sheriff-attribution rule. June 2 peers are not antecedent law.

One lawful result affirms the County dismissal because, for the challenged investigative acts, Alabama vested authority outside the County. The strongest counterargument is that a sovereign's placement and immunity classifications cannot defeat the federal remedy when an independently elected local official actually makes final local policy; lack of commission control may demonstrate finality rather than State identity. The contrary result reverses that attribution ruling and remands for the remaining elements without finding the allegations true. Its strongest counterargument is that local election, funding and territory cannot convert the actual legal authority of a State executive officer into County power. Both paths leave other sheriff functions and independent County policies or customs outside this adjudication.
