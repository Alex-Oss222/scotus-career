## Common legal propositions eligible for direct joins

**A. Same-sex coverage.** Title VII's prohibition of discrimination in employment terms or conditions because of an individual's sex does not exclude harassment because its perpetrator and victim have the same sex. The employee must still prove discrimination because of sex and every other applicable element; common sex establishes neither automatic liability nor automatic immunity. The text protects an individual and describes the prohibited reason for disadvantage, without an opposite-sex-perpetrator requirement. Meritor establishes that discriminatory harassment can alter employment conditions, and Harris applies that protection without making it depend on a particular victim/harasser sex pairing. Garcia's contrary categorical rule cannot govern the remand. No general pronouncement about the precedential status of alternative lower-court holdings is required.

**B. Proof of the prohibited reason.** Sexual attraction or desire may be evidence of sex discrimination, but neither is a necessary condition of liability. Sex-directed hostility, differential treatment, discriminatory statements, the character of threats, or other relevant circumstances may support the required inference; these are illustrative ways of proving the statutory fact, not exclusive channels, mandatory corroboration categories or automatic presumptions. Sexual content or humiliating conduct alone does not dispense with causation. The employee retains the ultimate burden under the governing proof rules. Hicks permits circumstantial proof without a special additional category and does not require the McDonnell Douglas framework when the claim can be proved otherwise. No finding of Oncale's or the alleged actors' orientation, attraction or motive is made.

**C. Single-sex workplaces and actionable conditions.** An all-male or all-female workplace does not itself bar a sex-discrimination claim or require an actual opposite-sex coworker as a comparator. Context may support or weaken an inference of discrimination and bears on the severity inquiry, but workplace custom does not make sex discrimination lawful. Under Harris, the employee must show sufficiently severe or pervasive sex-based conduct that alters employment conditions, is objectively hostile or abusive to a reasonable person in the circumstances, and is actually perceived as abusive. Frequency, severity, physical threat, humiliation and work interference are relevant without any one being indispensable. Neither medical injury, treatment, diagnosis nor measurable loss of productivity is required. Repeated threats and bodily assaults, if proved, must be evaluated as such rather than automatically treated as ordinary workplace banter; their proof and all remaining statutory requirements are for lawful proceedings below.

**D. Attribution and remaining theories.** Removing the categorical bar does not establish Sundowner's responsibility. Andrade requires an actual connection between delegated supervisory authority and creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment; supervisory title alone is insufficient and lack of higher-management notice alone is not a complete defense. An express employment threat, completed personnel action or economic loss is not invariably required for that agency route. Negligence concerning known or reasonably knowable harassment remains a distinct route. The lower court must apply that current law if attribution is reached. The quid-pro-quo theory requires its own supported showing that an employment benefit or detriment was conditioned on sexual participation; the record's label and allegations of a supervisor's abuse do not decide it. The Court imposes no strict liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker liability rule or unproved employment condition.

**E. Bounded disposition.** Reverse the judgment insofar as it rests on the categorical same-sex exclusion and remand for ordinary proceedings on the remaining issues, including the claims against Sundowner and Lyons to the extent that is their challenged ground. Leave Pippen and Johnson's independently grounded not-an-employer dismissal undisturbed because that separate ruling is outside the presented scope, without affirming a new substantive individual-liability rule. The remand determines neither liability nor entitlement to trial, constructive discharge, factual sufficiency, a damages amount, new discovery, or the availability of particular remedies for the 1991 conduct. Lyons's own statutory status remains undecided; restoration of a claim against him at the rejected ground does not hold that a supervisor is personally a statutory employer. Properly preserved independent defenses and ordinary procedural limits remain, but no missing preservation fact or new defense is manufactured.

## Enhanced comparison of supported alternatives

### Categorical coverage and a possible narrower affirmance

**Path 1 — reverse the same-sex exclusion:** The enacted provision protects each individual against conditions imposed because of sex, and Meritor/Harris already recognize harassment as a prohibited alteration of those conditions. No text restricts the perpetrator's sex. **Strongest contrary argument:** Title VII concerns discrimination between sexes rather than all sexual violence or same-sex hostility; the allegations might describe vulgarity, personal animosity or misconduct without sex discrimination. All eight accept the distinction but conclude that it calls for applying the because-of-sex element, not a categorical immunity. Their own Harris/Andrade/Brown commitments preserve that distinction. Ginsburg's Harris equality explanation and Scalia's refusal to add an administratively easier work-loss prerequisite independently reinforce the rejection of a nontextual gate; Breyer's Andrade authorship supports correction of a legal bar while leaving application unresolved.

**Path 2 — affirm Garcia's categorical rule:** The strongest support is concern that abandoning an opposite-sex model would turn any sexual act into employment discrimination. The statutory sex-causation requirement and Harris's objective/subjective alteration-of-conditions rule answer that concern without deleting same-sex claims. Each Justice rejects Path 2 at that legal level. No date-eligible own writing in the authorized record supplies a contrary requirement of an opposite-sex perpetrator.

**Narrower affirmance on another element:** It is legally possible for a same-sex claim to fail for causation, severity, attribution, defendant status or another properly preserved ground. It is not supported here as a substitute disposition against Sundowner merely because the appellate court never needed to adjudicate those issues. The lower court's categorical decision and assumed allegations do not establish an independent evidentiary failure. All eight refuse to invent a failure, a factual concession, an unpreserved defense or an appellate sufficiency record. The independent coworker-employer dismissal is different: it is expressly recorded and remains outside the granted review rather than being erased with Garcia.

### Sexual desire versus proof by other evidence

**Path 1 — no desire prerequisite:** Sex discrimination may be hostile or punitive rather than desirous. Ordinary evidence of discriminatory targeting and the surrounding circumstances can establish the statutory reason. **Strongest objection:** Without desire or opposite-sex comparison, the inference may become speculation from the act's sexual form. The common rule demands proof of the prohibited reason and permits competing non-sex explanations; it does not command the inference. Hicks's accepted no-extra-category rule and the Justices' Brown joins support keeping evidence flexible while causation remains necessary. Ginsburg relies additionally on her Harris equality analysis; Thomas and Scalia require particularly explicit preservation of the causation burden, but that qualification is shared rather than a vote withheld from the proposition.

**Path 2 — require desire, homosexuality or a specified comparator:** Desire can make a particular inference stronger, and differential evidence can be useful. Those evidentiary points do not justify making them indispensable. A rule that the harassment was actionable only if one participant were gay or bisexual would add an orientation condition absent from the sex-discrimination text and require an unnecessary adjudication of identity. All eight reject that mandatory condition. Their agreement does not hold that orientation discrimination as such is covered by Title VII; Nabozny's separate equal-protection ruling cannot supply that proposition.

### All-male workplace and the severity inquiry

**Path 1 — apply the existing totality standard:** The composition, relationships and ordinary setting inform the objective assessment and the sex-causation inquiry without replacing either. Harris supplies severe or pervasive, not severe and pervasive, and no medical-injury or work-loss gate. **Strongest objection:** Courts might impose inappropriate etiquette expectations without understanding a worksite's interactions. Context answers that concern, but physical threats and coercive bodily conduct cannot be automatically discounted as harmless solely because the site employs men. All eight accept this distinction. O'Connor's Harris opinion, Scalia and Ginsburg's distinct concurrences, the remaining Harris joins, and Breyer's Andrade application preserve the same statutory rule.

**Path 2 — categorical single-sex workplace exclusion or conclusive inference from sexual violence:** These opposite shortcuts each erase an independent statutory inquiry. Absence of an opposite-sex employee does not make discrimination logically impossible; severity alone does not establish why conduct occurred. Brown's mixed-audience analysis supports considering composition as evidence, not a defense, but its Title IX holding remains only an analogy here. All eight reject both shortcuts. No Justice commits to a finding that Oncale has already satisfied causation, objective hostility or subjective abuse.

### Employer responsibility and the mandate

**Correct the coverage rule and return unresolved elements:** Andrade independently requires an actual delegated-authority inquiry if that issue is reached. A supervisor's ability to direct the job may matter even without a completed employment action or notice upstairs, but status alone does not decide it. A claim that supervisors necessarily act privately during abuse would erase the agency route; an automatic employer-liability rule would erase its limiting predicate. All eight had directly joined Andrade, and Breyer authored it. They preserve both the agency and negligence routes without finding either satisfied here.

**Enter liability or order trial now:** The seriousness of allegations does not replace proof; reversal of the legal bar does not decide that every other defense fails or that a particular trial is required. All eight reject this disposition. The quid-pro-quo label does not establish an employment condition, and a hostile-environment theory does not acquire that additional prerequisite merely because both were pleaded. The correct bounded remand preserves these distinct questions and the independently grounded coworker dismissals.

## Components and legal premises

1. **Standing and review scope.** The hearing-based account concerns the plaintiffs' children in the affected public-school system and impending implementation at school events; the superintendent and principal supplied testimony before the opening of school. It supports threatened personal exposure rather than an abstract objection to a statute. Neither completed prayer nor punishment is required before prospective relief. Lujan's actual-or-imminent, personal-injury, traceability and redressability requirements remain. No taxpayer-standing expansion, statewide injury to every pupil, or finding of completed coercion is made. Review under the established appellate grant includes the cross-appeal about graduation and the separate intervention denials.
2. **Section 1(2) and official religious activity.** The enacted provision says qualifying invocations, benedictions or student-initiated voluntary prayer shall be permitted at compulsory and noncompulsory school-related assemblies, sporting events, graduations and other events. It is not merely a general forum-opening enactment. Its words do not themselves establish that an official has composed, led or screened an actual prayer. On the preliminary record, the event mandate and anticipated school administration support a substantial likelihood of unconstitutional official religious activity and, in the compulsory setting, religious pressure. Relief may prevent officials from organizing, adopting or directing worship as part of their own program, including choosing a devotional slot or a speaker to deliver prayer, and from applying the nonsectarian/nonproselytizing limits through official religious-content screening. Calling that activity student-initiated does not conclusively make it private.
3. **Distinct safeguards and private expression.** Section 1(3) preserves constitutionally protected expression at other times and events. Section 1(4)'s disclaimer does not decide attribution contrary to actual conduct. Section 1(5) directs severability of Section 1's provisions. Section 2 permits voluntary participation and denies teachers or school authorities authority to prescribe prayer's form or content. That denial is real and cuts against reading the Act as commanding official authorship; it does not erase Section 1(2)'s distinct eligibility filters or prove that an official event devotional exercise is private. Genuinely private prayer and actual neutral private-expression forums remain governed by Lamb's Chapel, Chabad and Good News/Good Sports. Neither the statute nor relief requires a school to establish a new forum. An injunction must not become a prohibition of private religious viewpoints.
4. **Graduation.** The lower exception is limited to Jones II's student-choice, student-delivery and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing conditions. Those conditions do not categorically establish a private forum or eliminate official adoption and pressure. A graduation may therefore receive no automatic exemption from the same constitutional inquiry. Conversely, no conclusion follows that every religious reference by a privately selected student speaker is governmental prayer. Lee's two controlling grounds remain distinct.
5. **Interim relief and severability.** The Court reviews a preliminary injunction, not a final declaration. Likely infringement of the plaintiffs' religious freedom supports irreparable injury; a tailored restraint preserves that freedom without suppressing independent private prayer. The State has no legitimate interest in official unconstitutional worship but retains an interest in lawful accommodations and expression. The blanket restraint on the Act apart from graduation must be narrowed by actual unconstitutional operation and by its separately functioning provisions; graduation's categorical safe harbor must be removed. Do not excise religious-content qualifiers and thereby invent a broader statutory access entitlement. Remand should permit application-sensitive and provision-sensitive relief under state severability law, preserving lawful independent provisions and applications, without rewriting Mississippi's program or issuing a final universal prayer code.
6. **Rule 24.** The final orders denied intervention as of right and permissively; initial abeyance is not the disposition on review. Rule 24(a)(2) requires timely application, a relevant interest and practical impairment unless existing parties adequately represent that interest. Trbovich makes the showing that representation may be inadequate minimal and rejects categorical sufficiency based on a public representative's official duty. Here the published account says the Attorney General actually pressed the students' speech and exercise interests, and applicants identified no omitted right in the statutory-validity litigation. That is affirmative record support for adequacy at this stage, not an irrebuttable presumption. Rule 24(b) makes commonality a basis for permission, not an entitlement; the court must consider undue delay or prejudice. The reported duplication and delay finding, together with allowed amicus presentation and the bounded scope of the action, supports the discretionary denial. Amicus status would not substitute for intervention if Rule 24(a)'s conditions were satisfied.

### Official activity and private speech

**Restraint path:** Enforce Lee against officials' use of the mandatory event permission to organize worship, choose devotional presentations, or screen religious content, while preserving real private expression. **Strongest objection:** Section 2 expressly denies content-prescription power; neutral accommodation, voluntary participation and student initiation may result in entirely private prayer. ## General full-power core: supported paths

### Core duties, their qualifications and their actual role

**Commercial capacity.** Section 534(b)(1)(A) requires a system with twelve or fewer usable activated channels to carry **at least three** local commercial signals. Its exemption for a system with 300 or fewer subscribers lasts only while the system does not delete any broadcast television signal, and exempts only §534 duties. A system with more than twelve channels is subject to the local-commercial obligation up to one-third of its usable activated channels, §534(b)(1)(B). Neither provision requires unavailable/ineligible stations, limits voluntary carriage to that number, or supplies a ceiling on added §535 obligations. Selection when stations exceed the required number remains the operator's, subject to the closest-city-of-license affiliate rule and the prohibition on substituting low-power carriage for a local commercial station. These terms constrain compulsion but do not make it voluntary or eliminate the burden of maintaining stations already carried.

**Educational capacity is additional.** Section 535(a) operates in addition to §534. Twelve-or-fewer-channel systems ordinarily carry one qualified local educational signal, with voluntary additional carriage and the continued-carriage rule preserved. If beyond any qualified local educational station's presence, they import one chosen station; they need not remove programming actually provided on March 29, 1990 for that importation but must use the first channel available. Systems with thirteen through thirty-six channels carry at least one and need not carry more than three qualified local educational signals, subject to continued carriage, and may carry more; absent a local qualified station they must import at least one. Growth beyond thirty-six channels on or after March 29, 1990 activates the each-requesting-qualified-local-station rule with its other conditions. Above thirty-six channels, the general rule requires each requesting qualified local educational station, with the **additional** substantially duplicative station exception when three must be carried; there is no general three-station cap.

**Existing educational carriage and signal quality.** Section 535(c), notwithstanding the other provisions, retains every qualified local educational station carried March 29, 1990; waiver for a specific operator/station requires both parties' written consent. Section 535(g)(4), also notwithstanding the other provisions, separately removes compulsory carriage when a good-quality or baseband signal is not delivered to the principal headend. Both commands remain stated; no unsupported actual conflict is decided. Continued carriage is a real constraint on future editorial changes. The affirming ground therefore does not treat the ordinary capacity categories as universal release from earlier commitments.

**Commercial and educational duplication differ.** Section 534(b)(5) permits omission of a substantially duplicative commercial signal or another affiliate of a network; it does not forbid such carriage. Every voluntarily carried duplicate or multiple affiliate counts toward the subsection (b)(1) number. Section 535(b)(3)(C), for thirteen-to-thirty-six-channel systems, permits omission of an additional affiliate of the **same State public television network only when** its programming substantially duplicates the carried affiliate. Section 535(e), for systems above thirty-six channels that must carry three, permits omission of **additional** substantially duplicative educational stations by comparison with another qualified local educational station **requesting carriage**, with FCC's definition promoting distinctive educational services. No commercial duplication rule is transplanted into §535. Section 535(f) also prevents a carried educational station from using network nonduplication rights to delete another carried local educational station's programs.

**Signal and geographic eligibility.** The commercial definition covers full-power, regularly licensed, noneducational stations in the relevant market and excludes low-power stations, translators and passive repeaters. Distant-signal treatment requires the station's increased-copyright indemnity. Below −45 dBm UHF or −49 dBm VHF at the principal headend, the station is excluded unless it agrees to bear delivery costs for a good-quality or baseband signal. Educational qualification has its separate public/nonprofit license and CPB-grant-eligibility route, municipal-ownership/predominantly-educational-programming route, and stated translator/reserved-channel inclusions. Educational locality is satisfied by a principal-community reference point within fifty miles **or** a Grade B contour encompassing the headend. Section 535(i)(2)'s indemnity condition for adding a distant educational signal expressly excepts stations already carried under subsection (c). These limits mitigate specific mismatches; they do not establish universal station vulnerability or permit deleting every costly signal.

**The speech burden includes full service and placement.** Section 534(b)(3) requires entire primary video/audio and line-21 captions, technically feasible program-related ancillary material, and the entire schedule subject to the identified exclusivity/substitution regulations. Other ancillary material is discretionary, and appropriate feasible signal enhancements may be removed for headend use. Section 535(g)(1) separately covers entire primary video/audio/captions and technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education or language, leaving other material discretionary. Required carriage occupies usable expressive capacity; it is not an empty-channel reservation. ## Common judgment and direct proposition commitments

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each would **vacate the en banc Sixth Circuit judgment and remand for application of the correct fair-warning standard and consideration of any remaining properly preserved issues**. All eight directly join P1–P5 below at their stated scope. They do not now affirm the convictions, order a new trial, reinstate the twenty-five-year sentence, or decide that all seven counts necessarily survive.

**P1 — conduct-time fair warning.** Section 242 may punish only a deprivation whose criminal prohibition was sufficiently clear when the defendant acted to give fair warning that the charged conduct was unlawful under the statute. Statutory language and authoritative pre-existing interpretation must make the applicable federal prohibition ascertainable; an unforeseeable judicial enlargement cannot retrospectively make conduct criminal. Vagueness, lenity and the bar against unexpected retrospective construction protect related notice interests but are not interchangeable automatic defenses or a license to invent a new constitutional right at trial.

**P2 — no Supreme-Court-only requirement.** The notice inquiry is not limited to Supreme Court cases. Relevant contemporaneous decisions interpreting the federal right must be considered at their actual authority and specificity. The Court need not hold that every lower-court opinion alone supplies sufficient notice, resolve an actual circuit conflict, or declare one prescribed number of cases enough. The Sixth Circuit's categorical exclusion is erroneous because it substitutes the source's court level for the substantive clarity of pre-existing law.

**P3 — no factual-twin requirement.** Prior law need not have condemned the very same act in a fundamentally similar factual setting if the existing rule makes the constitutional deprivation apparent in the charged circumstances. Particularized clarity remains essential: a bare appeal to liberty, bodily integrity or freedom from wrongful treatment cannot establish every asserted constitutional application. A rule may give clear warning in an obvious application even before another case repeats those facts. Conversely, moral offensiveness or the existence of a state assault crime does not alone establish the federal constitutional predicate.

**P4 — willfulness and the federal deprivation remain independent requirements.** Screws's limiting construction requires the willful deprivation of a federal right made definite by the Constitution, federal law or their interpretation; bad purpose alone does not cure an undefined federal prohibition. The Government must separately establish the underlying constitutional deprivation, action under color of law and the criminal willfulness element. Civil qualified immunity offers a relevant analogy concerning the clarity of law, not an automatic criminal conviction or a substitution of an objective civil defense for §242's statutory mental state and criminal proof requirements.

**P5 — bounded return.** On remand the Sixth Circuit must examine the relevant law existing at the time of the charged conduct without its categorical Supreme-Court/fundamentally-similar-facts restrictions and address the preserved issues necessary to dispose of the counts. This Court makes no final finding that each assault had the necessary official nexus, that each asserted constitutional theory was then clear, that the instructions correctly stated every element, that the evidence proves every element, or that the original sentencing disposition stands. No later simulated decision becomes retrospective notice merely because it now confirms a constitutional distinction.

## Adversarial alternatives and decisive limits

**Affirm the categorical rule.** The strongest defense is that criminal punishment demands greater precision than civil damages and that §242 incorporates broad constitutional language rather than enumerating each forbidden act. A Supreme Court/factually similar precedent requirement offers an administrable restraint on judges and prosecutors. That protection is overinclusive: it excludes otherwise unmistakable applications of settled law, treats the absence of repetition as substantive permission, and finds no such categorical limitation in the statute or Screws. Criminal precision is instead preserved by actual conduct-time clarity and willfulness. None of the eight accepts a doctrine allowing punishment merely because a later court regards conduct as intolerable.

**Affirm the result on an alternative no-notice ground.** Rejecting the categorical test does not logically establish adequate notice in this case. This competing disposition could be lawful if the conduct-time authorities and each count's essential facts established that the prosecution necessarily depended on a novel constitutional theory. The supplied neutral record does not establish that proposition, and the lower judgment used the erroneous general restriction. The eight therefore do not independently acquit, hold the constitutional theory unavailable, or use later civil immunity opinions as proof that the earlier law was either clear or uncertain.

**Reverse and reinstate convictions outright.** Direct official sexual abuse may be an especially clear application of bodily security and due process. It differs from the failure-to-protect and supervisory disputes in Pinder and Uhlrig. But recognizing that legal distinction cannot resolve the full criminal record, the official nexus of each count, every instruction, or preserved appellate objections. Doe's recognition of official sexual-abuse responsibility and Nabozny's rejection of a factual-twin requirement reinforce the current analytical distinction; they do not retroactively give notice or prove guilt. A limited remand is supported without searching for nonexistent post-order filings or manufacturing new evidence.

**Civil notice analogy and its boundary.** Anderson expressly retains particularized apparent unlawfulness while rejecting identical-facts necessity. Elder requires the complete relevant law on a preserved issue, and the unanimous Pinder notice standard repeats the conduct-date and no-factual-twin rules. Those are persuasive analytical parallels. Civil immunity protects reasonable official action under its own doctrine; §242 has a different cause, burden, willfulness requirement and criminal consequences. The present judgment does not hold the two regimes identical in every application or transfer civil pleadings assumptions into a completed criminal verdict.

## Exact common propositions available for joinder

**B1 — Identify the right, not just a violated law.** A claimant using §1983 to enforce a federal statute must identify a provision conferring the asserted individual federal right. The provision must be intended to benefit the claimant, impose a binding rather than merely precatory obligation, and define the claimed right with sufficient precision for judicial enforcement rather than leaving it vague or amorphous. Statutory benefit, state administrative duty and enforceable individual right are distinct. The inquiry is provision-specific and reads the text in its statutory setting; it does not treat an entire funding program as an indivisible promise.

**B2 — Programwide substantial compliance is not the claimed individual right.** Title IV-D's systemwide substantial-compliance measure serves federal evaluation and administration of the State program. The measure does not itself give each beneficiary an individually enforceable entitlement to compel every operation of the entire program to achieve that administrative benchmark. An aggregate level of compliance can coexist with failures affecting particular people; conversely, an individual right, if one exists in a particular provision, cannot be extinguished merely because the State's aggregate performance is satisfactory. The mandatory character of many state-plan duties therefore does not establish the broad right recognized below. This holding does not decide that every staffing, service, distribution or other particular provision fails B1.

**B3 — Foreclosure is a separate inquiry.** When an identified provision creates an individual right, §1983 supplies its statutory enforcement channel unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or establishes a sufficiently comprehensive remedial arrangement incompatible with it. Title IV-D's federal audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions do not by themselves establish blanket displacement of §1983 for every otherwise existing individual right. Administrative detail and funding withdrawal are not equivalent to an incompatible remedy for the person whose identified right is violated. A specific provision's remedial design must still be assessed on the actual claim; this conclusion does not adjudicate a hypothetical undisclosed remedy or immunize every requested injunction from ordinary limits.

**B4 — State-plan placement creates no categorical bar or entitlement.** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 forbid deeming a Social Security Act provision unenforceable merely because it appears in a section requiring a state plan or prescribing plan contents. Their enacted pending-action rules apply here. The actual Suter holdings already require provision-specific treatment and distinguish §1983 from a separately implied action; no historical Suter rationale or unrecorded state-plan rule is imported through the statutory reference.

**B5 — Concrete allegations and temporal law require examination on remand.** The complaint's broad prayer does not erase Madrid's allegation that collected support was not distributed under former §657(b)(1). An unmet criterion does not invariably defeat release if its deviation is immaterial to those purposes; conversely, a general impression of improvement cannot erase material unfulfilled requirements. No universal numerical compliance percentage supplies a safe harbor.

**J2 — Findings must permit review.** Rule 52(a) requires enough subsidiary factual findings and explanation to disclose the basis for the ultimate compliance conclusion; it applies to a master's findings to the extent adopted by the district court. Neither a long evidentiary summary nor repetition of the legal conclusion substitutes for material findings. The court need not recite every item or discuss irrelevant details, and reviewing courts must respect supported findings under clear-error review. They cannot weigh disputed evidence themselves to supply the material findings the trial tribunal omitted.

**J3 — These findings are inadequate.** The adopted report does not adequately connect its methodology, competing quantitative and qualitative evidence, and stated compliance conclusions to the decree's obligations. It does not explain the relationship between reported low conference-compliance measures and the compliance finding; nor between the attorney-notification evidence and that finding. It discounts adverse individual case examples as selected instances without explaining why they are unrepresentative rather than evidence of wider defects. It does not adequately resolve the timely challenge to ADAPT data or explain/discredit testimony that time in custody increased. These are gaps in explanation and factfinding, not Supreme Court findings accepting every adverse measure or declaring systemwide failure.

For clarity, the report describes conference measures ranging from 6.2–63 percent, other content measures from 31–97 percent, timely attorney notification of 26 percent and no notification for 8 percent, and testimony that custody length increased from 1.5 to 2.1 years. The Court identifies those as examples of evidence requiring an intelligible account in the findings. It selects no percentage, computes no overall score, credits no disputed system automatically, and does not decide whether the Department's opposing evidence is more persuasive. A reasoned explanation may establish why a particular shortfall was immaterial or why a contrary measure is reliable; it must actually be supplied by the tribunal charged with finding facts.

**J4 — Performance and modification are distinct.** The Department need not establish a significant change under Rufo merely to prove performance of the decree's existing release condition. If it instead seeks to reduce still-binding obligations because law or facts changed, Rufo supplies that different threshold and tailoring inquiry. Freeman and Jenkins reinforce the need to match findings to the actual remedy within their school-desegregation settings; they neither replace this decree's twelve-month condition with their full school-release inquiry nor create a universal constitutional-minimum exit. No PLRA prison-conditions predicate is established here.

**J5 — Remand is bounded.** The district court must apply the stated contractual condition, address material deviations and their effect on the decree's essential purposes, explain relevant methodology and resolve the material factual conflicts needed for review. It may then enter the lawful judgment the supported findings warrant. The Court requires no plaintiff consent, consensus, neutral-reviewer endorsement, guarantee against every future lapse or new institutional arrangement. Procedures contemplated by the decree must be genuinely in place; that requirement does not extend the bargained twelve-month duration or impose an independent indefinite prediction requirement. No automatic retrial of every settled fact or acceptance of newly tendered material follows from remand; further proceedings are governed by ordinary authority.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Reinstate termination on the reports read as a whole.** The strongest case is that the experienced district judge adopted lengthy reports, substantial compliance tolerates immaterial defects, and Rule 52 does not require every evidentiary detail. O'Connor's Rufo emphasis and the institutional-restraint concerns in the Jenkins dissent make that a real alternative. The reports nevertheless omit material—not merely repetitive—connections between contested evidence and essential compliance findings. The unexplained methodology and disregard of adverse cases cannot be repaired by assuming that some unarticulated weighing occurred. All eight reject reinstatement on that record without demanding a transcript of every evidentiary choice.

**Affirm because the data conclusively establish noncompliance.** Low compliance measures and prolonged custody provide substantial grounds for concern, but the State offered opposing evidence and the appellate tribunal did not resolve credibility or measurement reliability. Rule 52 prevents an appellate substitute for missing factual findings in either direction. All eight refuse a definitive noncompliance holding or permanent enforcement judgment.

**Affirm remand under the decree and Rule 52.** This selected path preserves meaningful review, the State's contractual route to release and the children's right to performance. Its strongest objection is the cost of more supervision despite years of proceedings. Neither age nor administrative burden creates the missing findings; the remand must nevertheless remain confined and may lead to termination upon the requisite supported showing. No newly created procedure may indefinitely postpone a properly established right to release.

**Require Rufo change, Freeman's school checklist or constitutional-minimum proof as the release test.** Each substitutes a different legal question for performance of this decree. All eight reject these transpositions. Rufo applies only to changed-duty relief; school-desegregation precedents illuminate review and tailoring at their actual scope.
