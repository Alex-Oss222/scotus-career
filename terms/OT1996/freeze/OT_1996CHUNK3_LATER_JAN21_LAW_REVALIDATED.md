## Newly effective law and published continuity

Youpee applies Irving and Penn Central to the amended escheat mechanism's uncompensated destruction of the presented succession interests. Restricted co-owner devises, the income provisions, retained lifetime powers and the unused tribal-code option do not cure that operation for this estate. The Court neither adopts a categorical Lucas rule for every property attribute nor establishes a universal beneficiary-choice entitlement. O'Connor's opinion has the direct joins of the seven other Justices identified in the projection, including all modeled Justices except Stevens.

Stevens's dissent regards the narrower affected-interest provisions, co-owner devise option, retained lifetime powers and nearly six-year planning opportunity as sufficient to distinguish Irving. It does not find actual notice, culpable abandonment or valueless property, decide an individualized-notice claim, or extend Lucas to every restriction on a separate attribute. Its judgment is a proposed takings reversal, not a general rule that time alone cures any deprivation.

Curtis unanimously rejects the presented parental-liberty and constitutional free-exercise claims against the described optional program. Its actual operation permits abstention without loss of ordinary education, discipline or required affirmation; the junior-high counseling condition does not require entry into the program. The decision preserves actual official-pressure and targeting rules, decides no independent RFRA claim and creates no general school-health immunity. Its distinction between constitutional permission for one parental-consent arrangement and a constitutional obligation to impose another preserves the scope of both propositions.
