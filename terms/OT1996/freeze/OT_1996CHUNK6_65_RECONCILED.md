## Proposition reconciliation and necessary precision

1. **Conduct-time notice:** retain P1. Section 242, standing alone or as authoritatively construed, must have made the charged constitutional unlawfulness reasonably clear when the defendant acted. Vagueness, lenity and the bar against unexpected retrospective construction protect fair warning in related but distinct ways. Courts construe Congress's criminal enactment; they do not create common-law crimes. Bad purpose cannot make an otherwise unknowable constitutional prohibition definite.

2. **Sources:** retain P2. Screws does not confine the relevant interpretive law to Supreme Court decisions. Lower-court authority may provide notice, and actual conflicts can undermine sufficient clarity; neither source level nor one fixed numerical consensus is a substitute for examining the law. The rejection of categorical exclusion does not make every lower-court opinion independently sufficient or controlling.

3. **Specificity:** retain P3. A prior factual twin is unnecessary when existing law applies with obvious clarity. Conversely, a general statement of liberty or bodily security cannot resolve every application. An earlier decision expressly leaving the particular application open may require substantially more factual specificity before notice is adequate. This qualification prevents the obvious-application rule from silently overruling the particularized inquiry preserved in Anderson, Elder and the actual Pinder/Uhlrig decisions.

4. **One legal-clarity protection, distinct criminal requirements:** P4's civil analogy must not be used to leave open or adopt a categorically heightened criminal **legal-clarity** threshold. The historical unanimous Court expressly rejected the proposition that §242 requires notice clearer than the civil 'clearly established' standard: both demand apparent constitutional unlawfulness in light of pre-existing law. There is no supported changed premise for shrinking that holding to an unspecified resemblance. Retain the common fair-warning threshold while keeping constitutional deprivation, color of law, criminal willfulness, criminal proof and all other statutory predicates separate. The same clarity protection does not make a civil damages conclusion an automatic criminal conviction or transplant the whole civil cause of action into §242.

5. **Return and foreclosed legal shortcuts:** retain P5's count-specific and remaining-issue remand, without reopening the categorical Supreme-Court-only and factual-twin rules just rejected. Section 242 is not categorically inapplicable to rights protected by Fourteenth Amendment due process; Screws and Price foreclose that proposition. DeShaney's limitation on a duty to protect against private violence does not establish categorical immunity for an official's own assault outside custody. Graham requires use of a specific constitutional provision when it covers the asserted claim; it does not confine every physically abusive official act to the Fourth or Eighth Amendments. These legal limits, expressly retained in the historical opinion's final footnote, do not decide the official nexus, the complete constitutional theory, conduct-time clarity or proof on each charged count. They accord with the actual Collins/Pinder/Uhlrig distinctions and supply no complete new substantive-due-process test.

The model's distinction between civil and criminal liability is therefore preserved with a precise boundary. Its 'analogy' language cannot support a broader fair-warning immunity than the historical single clarity standard.
