### Governing law and components

The applicable register entry is **Separate due-process and Commerce Clause connections for remote use-tax collection**, **Present operation effective: Quill Corp. v. North Dakota, May 26, 1992**. Its due-process component rejects indispensable physical presence for its related collection obligation; its distinct Commerce collection rule and record-specific software application do not govern civil personal jurisdiction. The relevant current-term authority is **Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Robertson-Ceco Corp., April 21, 1997**, Parts I–IV, at their actual separate coalitions: aggregate contacts and a reasonable multiyear inquiry; substantially greater affiliation for general than specific jurisdiction; an independent compelling-case fairness inquiry; and preservation of proof and ordinary defenses after removing a jurisdictional dismissal. Its all-purpose sufficiency result does not decide this claim-specific controversy. ProCD, January 27, and United States v. Thomas, May 19, show only that electronic dealings receive ordinary legal analysis; neither supplies a jurisdiction test.

1. **Purposeful affiliation.** Under International Shoe, Hanson, World-Wide Volkswagen and Burger King, the defendant must himself create the relevant purposeful forum relationship. Repeated deliberate distribution of thirty-two software files through the known Ohio provider, continuing marketing through that system, and Ohio collection and remittance of fees establish such an affiliation on this prima-facie record. The provider agreement and Ohio-law provision reinforce notice within that actual course; neither alone establishes jurisdiction, and the latter is not consent to an Ohio forum. Physical absence and the small amount sold to Ohio residents do not defeat this deliberate use of Ohio distribution and payment services.
2. **Connection to the claim.** This declaratory controversy concerns the names and marketing of the software Patterson distributed and advertised through the Ohio relationship, together with his infringement demands to that provider. The actual relationship, alleged commercial use of the names and demands establish a sufficient connection between his forum conduct and the controversy. A different hypothetical marketing arrangement does not erase the arrangement actually alleged. No adjudication that a valid trademark arose in Ohio is necessary. No holding rests on a demand letter alone, injury to an Ohio resident alone, or an all-purpose jurisdiction theory.
3. **Fairness.** Burger King's compelling-case standard and Asahi's independent fairness holding require consideration of the actual Texas-to-Ohio burden, Ohio's interest in the commercial dispute, CompuServe's effective-relief interest, Texas's interest and interstate administration. The individual defendant's scale is considered, but the ongoing knowing commercial relationship, claim connection and ordinary domestic burden shown do not establish constitutional unfairness. CompuServe's size or asserted stakes confer no jurisdictional privilege. No rule categorically excludes serious domestic hardship.
4. **Posture and remedy.** Ohio's transacting-business provision and the applicable federal service/jurisdiction rules provide the asserted route. The described provider course satisfies the statutory ground on the supplied construction as well as due process. On this papers-only Rule 12(b)(2) motion, supported jurisdictional allegations receive the favorable prima-facie treatment; conflicting affidavits cannot be resolved as trial findings against the plaintiff. The plaintiff retains its ultimate jurisdictional burden. Affirmance leaves the Sixth Circuit's remand operative for proof and remaining defenses; it orders no mandatory evidentiary hearing where none is needed. Subject-matter jurisdiction, declaratory-relief discretion, venue, transfer and merits retain their separate requirements. The unaddressed dismissal grounds are not rejected by this decision.

## Adversarial path disposition

**Affirmance path:** All eight accept the sufficiently alleged deliberate Ohio provider course, its connection to the dispute, and the absence of a compelling fairness objection. Its strongest objection is that the Ohio location belongs to CompuServe and the trademark rights could exist elsewhere. The defendant's own repeated use of that identified location for distribution, marketing and remuneration, with this dispute arising within that course, makes this more than plaintiff residence or network routing.

**Dismissal path:** Reversal restoring dismissal would be lawful if the contacts were only a resident plaintiff, a fortuitous server, passive availability or demands unrelated to a purposeful commercial course. Those are not the supported prima-facie facts. Neither direct Ohio sales below $650 nor physical absence independently defeats the actual course. No Associate selects that path on this record.

**Limited factual-remand path:** A remand without deciding even prima-facie sufficiency could be warranted by a genuine missing premise material to affiliation or claim connection. None is identified in the frozen packet; ultimate proof remains available on the ordinary remand. A mandatory new hearing would add a procedure unsupported by necessity here.
