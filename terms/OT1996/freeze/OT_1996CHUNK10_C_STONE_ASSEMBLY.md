# 112. Thomasson v. Perry

# SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

They are Stone’s positions for the simulation, not Court decisions. Approval does not establish missing evidence, participation or an intervening Court holding.

## A. STATUS

- **Approval Status:** Source-designated approved position, preserved subject to the express precedent conflicts identified below. This packet does not enter a Court decision.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's judgment and remand for as-applied relief setting aside Thomasson's discharge.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The discharge based on Thomasson's identity statement and the resulting presumption shall be set aside, and the matter returned to the Navy for further proceedings consistent with the opinion. Actual evidence of prohibited conduct or intent remains subject to otherwise constitutional military rules. No ruling is made on the separate due-process, speech, or substantive-liberty claims.

### Issue 1: Construction of the statements provision (an independently sufficient ground)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the operative construction first, because it can resolve the discharge without a constitutional holding.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A statement of homosexual identity triggers a rebuttable presumption that the member engages in, attempts, has a propensity to engage in, or intends homosexual acts. The member must be allowed to offer all relevant contrary proof, including actual conduct, credibility, context, testimony from others, service history insofar as probative, and a commitment to obey valid conduct rules, without being required to deny the stated identity itself.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 654's statements provision creates a rebuttable conduct-or-intent presumption, not an irrebuttable rule, and the member may rebut it through relevant evidence without disavowing homosexual identity. The Board required more than the statute allows. The discharge is set aside on this ground alone, and the matter is returned to the Navy for a proceeding that gives the presumption genuine rebuttal effect.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory and regulatory text identifies a presumption rather than direct discharge for status alone. Giving that form genuine effect requires considering the evidence that bears on actual conduct, propensity, and intent rather than demanding denial of the identity statement that triggered the proceeding.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent evidentiary use of a statement that actually admits regulable conduct or intent.

### Issue 2: Equal protection

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the classification and as-applied fit as a further and independent ground. The equal-protection ground also resolves the discharge, so he does not reach due process, speech, or substantive liberty.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether discharge may rest on the identity statement and resulting presumption without independent evidence of prohibited conduct, propensity, or intent.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The statements provision classifies by sexual orientation and uses identity speech as the evidentiary trigger for predicted conduct. Any authenticated earlier-effective Walmer holding would govern at its actual scope. If that holding adopts intermediate scrutiny for the relevant classification, Stone would apply it; if it adopts another controlling inquiry, that inquiry must be identified from the decision rather than inferred from the prepared packet, with Stone's own tier view stated separately. No such decision is authenticated in the supplied sources. Pending that verification, the tier view and constitutional reasoning stated here remain Stone's proposed positions, not a rule attributed to Walmer. Unit cohesion, discipline, privacy, and readiness are important, and supported contemporaneous military judgments receive substantial weight. The Government nevertheless bears the constitutional burden, and Congress's findings in the statute receive greater weight than the executive regulation did in Walmer. On this record the identity-based presumption, rather than individualized evidence, does the decisive work.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** As applied to discharge an officer without independent evidence of prohibited conduct, propensity, or intent beyond the identity statement and resulting presumption, the rule is not shown to be related to the military's important readiness and discipline interests in the way the governing standard requires.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Thomasson's service record and the absence of independent conduct-or-intent evidence leave the presumption as the sole bridge from identity to discharge. Military deference gives supported professional judgments real weight, but it does not shift the burden to the officer or make a status-to-conduct inference self-validating. The as-applied discharge therefore fails without deciding the validity of every conduct rule or every application of the statute.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not hold the presumption irrebuttable, hold the statute invalid in all applications, treat Congress's findings as pretext, immunize misconduct, order reinstatement or back pay beyond the ordinary consequences of setting aside the discharge, or decide the separate due-process, speech, or substantive-liberty questions.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

The statutory construction is an independently sufficient ground in Stone's proposed disposition and uses no new standard. Any dependence of his equal-protection reasoning on Walmer is conditional on an authenticated earlier-effective decision and its actual scope. Walmer's calendar placement supplies no holding, and no controlling tier is established by the prepared position. Stone's intermediate-scrutiny view, the stated statutory and equal-protection grounds, and the discharge disposition remain his approved proposed positions. He preserves weight for supported military judgment.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1.** Stone would construe the presumption as genuinely rebuttable through relevant evidence without requiring disavowal of identity, and would set aside this discharge on that ground alone, because the Board gave the presumption irrebuttable effect.

**Question 2.** The speech question is presented but Stone would not render a separate First Amendment holding because the statutory and as-applied equal-protection grounds already set aside the same discharge. A statement may be relevant evidence under a valid conduct rule; it cannot be deemed actual prohibited conduct solely by repeating the identity presumption. His mandate reserves the independent speech claim rather than approving or rejecting a general military restriction on orientation speech.

**Question 3.** Supported professional judgments on discipline, cohesion, privacy and readiness receive substantial evidentiary weight, not exemption from the court's chosen standard or the Government's burden. Stone would preserve otherwise constitutional conduct rules and grant only the stated as-applied discharge relief.



# 114. Chalmers v. Tulon Co. of Richmond

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s judgment and remand for trial on the Title VII accommodation claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The factfinder must resolve the religious character and scope of the practice, sufficient employer knowledge, actual workplace and third-party effects, whether a neutral prospective rule, warning, or other effective accommodation was available, and whether Tulon proves undue hardship. Stone reinstates no abandoned disparate-treatment claim and enters no liability or damages judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Notice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Notice is necessary to determine whether the employer had a meaningful opportunity to accommodate, but the court below treated it as a categorical advance-forecasting requirement not supplied by Title VII’s text.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify rather than replace the received accommodation framework.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VII requires enough information, before the challenged adverse action, for the employer reasonably to understand that a religious practice is involved and what conflict requires response. It does not invariably require advance notice before the first manifestation of an unanticipated practice. Lack of earlier notice remains relevant when harm is irreversible or no prospective accommodation can address the conflict.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Tulon knew Chalmers was evangelical, recognized the letters as religious, and discharged her after investigating them. A jury could find that the employer had notice of the religious practice before the adverse action and could have considered a prospective warning or neutral restriction. A jury could also find that general knowledge did not make the precise practice foreseeable. Rule 56 does not permit the Court to choose between those inferences.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that general awareness of faith automatically notifies an employer of every practice, excuse an employee who conceals a foreseeable scheduling conflict, or require retroactive accommodation of harm that no lawful response can cure.

### Issue 2: Actual hardship and third-party limits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Fourth Circuit’s alternative holding that accommodation was impossible independently supported summary judgment and must be reviewed.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply simulated *Beadle* and *Hardison* while giving concrete third-party burdens their proper evidentiary role.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Offense or generalized coworker discomfort does not establish undue hardship, but an employer need not subject employees, especially subordinates, to coercive, repeated, authority-backed, or materially harmful individualized religious admonition. On this disputed record, Tulon has not established as a matter of law that no reasonable prospective accommodation short of discharge existed.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The supervisor’s marital discord and the subordinate’s initial distress are relevant and may establish real hardship at trial. So are Chalmers’s supervisory authority and the private subject matter. But the subordinate later denied lasting offense or work impairment, the letters violated no identified rule, and a warning against future targeted letters might have protected all parties. The statutory burden belongs to Tulon, and summary judgment cannot rest on contested characterizations or unsupported predictions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Title VII does not require Tulon to permit recurrence, surrender neutral anti-harassment or privacy rules, tolerate supervisory coercion, or transfer material costs to coworkers. Voluntary private religious conversation and generalized expression remain different from targeted authority-backed admonition.

### Issue 3: Disposition and remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reversal is required because genuine disputes concern elements and the employer’s defense.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Remand for ordinary trial rather than decide liability from the appellate record.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When record-supported disputes remain concerning sufficient notice, actual hardship, and feasible accommodation, a court may not grant summary judgment by declaring the practice categorically unaccommodable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A trial can separate the legitimacy of protecting coworkers from a post hoc justification and determine whether a bounded prospective rule would have preserved both religious exercise and workplace function. Remedy follows only after liability and causation are proved.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone decides no damages amount, reinstatement right, constitutional claim, or state-law claim.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s user-approved notice clarification and ordinary trial remand are proposed applications beyond Beadle’s scheduling dispute. The remand leaves Tulon free to establish actual irreversible harm or a concrete inability to accommodate; it does not presume that all unsolicited religious letters must be tolerated or that every discharge without warning violates Title VII.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1–2 — retained user-approved proposal.** Stone would require enough knowledge before the adverse decision to understand the religious practice and relevant conflict, without making pre-act notice invariably essential. General belief alone is insufficient, but the employer’s actual knowledge of practice and the circumstances before discharge may supply the necessary information. Irreversible harm and lack of a workable prospective response remain material.

**Question 3 — retained user-approved proposal.** Stone would remand for trial rather than declare these disputed communications categorically unaccommodable. A neutral warning or rule may matter if it would effectively prevent recurrence; actual coercive supervisory use, serious personal harm or operational cost may defeat accommodation. The proposed result revives only the accommodation claim, not abandoned disparate treatment, and awards no damages or right to send every religious admonition.
