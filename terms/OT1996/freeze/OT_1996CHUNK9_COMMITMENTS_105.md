## Common threshold and issue divisions

All eight Associates reach the granted County-attribution question. The published lower opinion establishes a permitted §1292(b) appeal; this is not the unauthorized pendant County appeal vacated in Swint. The fact that the old Swint merits reasoning was vacated requires fresh evaluation, not automatic reversal of a later judgment through a valid channel. No separate issue about hearsay, individual immunity, the ultimate truth of misconduct allegations, or jail staffing is granted here.

All eight apply Monell's separate institutional-attribution requirement. Kinman's unanimous May 27 institutional holding confirms the policy/custom or attributable final-decision route, including a single decision if final authority is established. Its personal-supervisor culpability and Title IX recipient rules do not determine this case. Collins likewise separates federal injury from attribution. Bryan County remains pending for reargument and supplies no merits rule. All reject ordinary respondeat superior, a label-only determination, and a requirement that every County policy come from the County commission.

Jett Part IV, 491 U.S. 737–738, supplies a majority rule for judicial identification of the official who speaks with final policymaking authority concerning the particular act, using state/local positive law and legally effective custom. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy directly joined it. Pembaur's executive single-decision holding and actual Ohio application must be distinguished from its four-Justice Part II-B formula. Praprotnik's O'Connor plurality, joined by Scalia, preserves genuine delegation, ratification and custom while respecting legal allocation. Neither case decides that every locally elected sheriff represents a county, or that a State constitutional classification invariably defeats County attribution.

The separable components are: (A) the legal method and granted scope; (B) application to the sheriff's criminal-investigation/law-enforcement function; and (C) remedy and reservation of other claims/functions. Each Associate accepts A and the bounded nature of C, but the application of B divides them.

## Two supported application paths

**Affirmance path.** Alabama's constitutional allocation places the sheriff in the State executive department; its courts treat the office as State employment in the relevant structural cases; the criminal-investigation statutes require service to courts and prosecutorial officers, with State executive authority to direct investigations. The county supplies money and facilities but the record establishes no County power to direct, review, or prescribe the challenged criminal-investigative policy. This is an allocation of authority, not merely a title. The County need not answer for a different entity's policy because it pays expenses.

The strongest counterargument is that an independently elected sheriff may himself embody County government. The absence of commission control does not show absence of County authority if the law placed local final authority directly in the sheriff. Local election, jurisdiction, revenue flows, office facilities and operating expenses are part of the same governmental arrangement; the State's immunity classifications concern a different question. Treating the commission as synonymous with the County would impose a control relationship incompatible with final policymaking.

**Reversal path.** The sheriff's final criminal-investigative choices within the county are exercises of the local governmental office the State created. The State's broad executive label and ultimate oversight do not displace the local authority demonstrated by election, jurisdiction, financing, and independent operational responsibility. The existing dismissal mistakenly treats the lack of commission control as a sufficient answer; final local policy can be assigned directly to a sheriff. Reversal does not prove a federal injury or impose vicarious liability for a subordinate's act.

Its strongest counterargument is that locality and financial support do not identify the entity represented: State officials can be elected by districts and serve a local territory. Jett requires respect for the actual positive-law allocation, not a federal presumption that decentralized law enforcement is County business. The reversal position must show why the arrangement establishes a County policymaker; it cannot merely count local features or ignore the investigation-directing statutes.

## Proposition-level compatibility and possible writings

All eight can join the common method: federal §1983 liability follows policy attributable to the sued entity; state law and legally effective custom identify the relevant final authority; title, election, funding, geography and commission control are not individually dispositive; function and entity identity are separate from finality. The application cannot be combined merely because all accept that method.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas can directly join an affirmance explaining why the Alabama constitutional and investigative structure attributes the challenged acts outside the County. They need not decide that the sheriff has authority to set every kind of State policy. The lower court's explicit reservation of State-policymaker status is not rewritten as its finding.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer can directly join a contrary application identifying the sheriff's final local office as County authority and rejecting commission control as the measure of County identity. Their common ground is narrower than Stevens's previously published general view of vicarious municipal responsibility; that broader theory receives no additional join. A dissent or separate application opinion can explain the disagreement without replacing the operative Monell rule.

The internal Associate alignment is four for affirmance and four for reversal/remand on the County-attribution component. It is not a final Court count; Stone's position is neither inferred nor supplied. No author is assigned. All eight leave individual liability, State liability/immunity, other sheriff functions, different States' legal arrangements, and distinct County policies/customs outside the present holding. A reversal would not order a mandatory trial or award money; an affirmance would not approve the alleged misconduct.
