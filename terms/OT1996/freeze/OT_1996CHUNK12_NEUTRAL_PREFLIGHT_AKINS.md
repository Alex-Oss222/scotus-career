## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent political-party campaign expression and candidate connections

### Independent political-party campaign expression and candidate connections

**Governing question:** Application of the Senate party-expenditure ceiling and the alternative candidate-contribution theory to a genuinely independent party advertisement.

**Current rule:** Section 441a(d)(3)'s Senate-election expenditure ceiling cannot be applied to a party's own genuinely independent campaign advertisement merely because the speaker is an official political party. Party identity or a continuing electoral relationship also cannot replace §441a(a)(7)(B)(i)'s required cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion by a candidate, authorized political committee or agent for the asserted contribution theory. Actual independence defeats that theory; realistic corruption and circumvention concerns do not alone establish the statutory candidate connection.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent-expression protection: Independent party expression; distinct contribution predicate: The alternative contribution theory, 1996-06-26; O’Connor Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (six).

**Present operation effective:** Colorado Republican, 1996-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** The general contribution/expenditure distinction survives. Transfers between national, State, district, local and subordinate committees of the same party retain their exemption from §441a(a)(1)–(2); candidate-material republication under §441a(a)(7)(B)(ii) and earmarking or intermediary direction under §441a(a)(8) have different predicates. The rule creates no immunity for candidate cash, genuine conduits, sham independence or coordinated expression.

**Open question:** No majority either validates or invalidates all coordinated party-expression limits or resolves every interaction with §441a(d)(1)’s notwithstanding clause. Neither three-Justice further framework supplies controlling law.

**Holdings navigation:** Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission, 518 U.S. 604 (1996).

### Additional controlling decisions

- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992:** its specified five-Justice Parts II, III-A and IV govern concrete personal injury, stage-appropriate support and procedural-right limits. The absent-agency application in Part III-B is not controlling. Congress can protect concrete interests; statutory complaint permission alone is insufficient.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers Union Local 751 v. Brown Group, Inc., May 13, 1996:** the unanimous Souter opinion permits Congress to displace Hunt's prudential individual-participation condition while preserving constitutional injury and likely redress. It decides representative WARN enforcement, not whether every FECA complainant is injured.
- **Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission, June 26, 1996:** O'Connor's six-Justice Part I protects genuinely independent party expression and requires the actual statutory candidate connection for the alternative contribution theory. It neither resolves all coordinated spending nor adopts a universal express-advocacy rule or organizational major-purpose exemption.
- **Federal Election Commission v. Legi-Tech, Inc., February 18, 1997:** Thomas's unanimous opinion permits actual lawful reconsideration by a properly constituted Commission within available authority and retained statutory prerequisites. It supplies no generic review bar, automatic enforcement remedy or political-committee definition. The packet's FEC v. NRA Political Victory Fund reference remains confined to Supreme Court representation and timely ratification.
- **Bennett v. Spear, March 19, 1997:** the unanimous Court sustains a supported pleading-stage causal/remedial link despite another agency's retained choice. It requires provision-specific authorization, preserves constitutional injury, and does not turn uncertain ultimate success into a categorical standing defect. Its evidence of announced agency compliance is not an Akins fact.
- **Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co., June 2, 1997:** Part I requires resolution of disputed Article III power before this sort of contested statutory merits question, retaining O'Connor and Kennedy's threshold-sequence qualification. Part II assumes informational injury and traceability but denies standing where reports were already current and no requested remedy repaired the completed deprivation or prevented a supported future injury. It does not reject informational injury generally. Akins's allegedly still-withheld information must be assessed on its own facts; no source establishes that it has already been disclosed.

### Supported alternative paths; no selection

1. **Article III first.** One path treats the asserted denial of particular statutorily required election information as an injury to these voters' own electoral activity, with the number of similarly situated voters immaterial to concreteness. The strongest objection is that opposition to AIPAC's policy positions or an undifferentiated wish for lawful government proves no personal informational interest. The lower court connects the asserted information to elections in which complainants participate, while acknowledging that nondisclosure prevents precise identification; neither side may convert that limitation into invented evidence. The competing jurisdictional path rejects standing if the supported personal, causal or remedial connection is insufficient. That path ends judicial merits adjudication; it cannot simultaneously sustain the major-purpose interpretation on the merits. Findley's old candidacy and the Commission's failure to contest that theory do not independently establish Article III standing.
2. **Causation and redress.** One path permits review because correction of the dismissal's legal premise can restore the statutory process for obtaining information still withheld; a retained lawful enforcement choice is not necessarily fatal. Bennett supports examining the actual link rather than demanding certainty. The strongest objection is the chain through future Commission choices, third-party compliance and useful disclosure, including possible lawful settlement without the desired reports. A denial-of-standing path must explain why this is speculation on the actual record rather than treating all agency discretion as dispositive. Steel Co. requires a useful remedy for these plaintiffs; its already-filed reports and absence of future harm cannot be imported here. Past financial information may remain useful to future voting on the lower court's account, but usefulness and statutory entitlement are distinct questions.
3. **Express review versus lawful discretion.** Section 437g(a)(8) expressly authorizes review of dismissal for legal error. A legal-error path corrects the interpretation actually used and leaves lawful factual and discretionary choices to the FEC. The competing path may sustain a legally permissible construction or an independently supported lawful discretionary ground. The separate close membership question underlying the ?441b nonenforcement decision does not automatically become the political-committee dismissal's ground. Courts may not invent an unexercised discretionary rationale or equate reviewability with compelled prosecution.
4. **Major purpose and transaction character.** The voters' strongest path distinguishes candidate-connected contributions/coordinated spending from independent expression: a large lobbying budget does not itself erase covered candidate financing above the statutory threshold. The Commission's strongest path treats the major-purpose limitation as necessary to avoid imposing organizational political-committee duties and associational burdens on a predominantly issue-oriented body, relying on the breadth of the pre-divergence formulations. Neither path can rest on labels alone. The Court must determine the scope those precedents actually decided and whether their constitutional rationale extends to the covered transactions here. The lower court reports that the parties did not describe these disbursements as independent expenditures; this is not a judicial finding that every alleged activity was coordinated or legally covered. Genuine candidate connection, amount, calendar-year aggregation and each applicable exclusion remain distinct predicates.
5. **Membership and bounded relief.** The membership-communication exclusion in ?431(9)(B)(iii) has both an organizational-purpose condition and its own express-advocacy reporting proviso; exclusion from expenditure classification does not necessarily erase that distinct reporting obligation. Section 441b(b)(2) has different text and beneficiaries, and ?431(8)(B)(vi) and (9)(B)(v) separately cross-reference it. Do not substitute one provision's exception for another's. AIPAC membership and transaction application may remain for the Commission under the proper effective standard; later proposed rules supply no current rule. Correcting the major-purpose interpretation need not decide whether the threshold is actually met after exclusions, classify the entire organization conclusively, declare all lobbying to be campaign activity, order wholesale associational disclosure, or impose a penalty. A statutory-construction affirmance of the Commission position likewise requires Article III power and an answer to the contribution/independent-expression distinction.

The alternative-path test is therefore required for standing, review/remedy and the major-purpose question. An independently sufficient jurisdictional resolution avoids merits. No broader factual premise is adopted to obtain a merits result. The source limits are not a blocker to those bounded paths; a proposed final transaction classification exceeding them would require targeted support.
