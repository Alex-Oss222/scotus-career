## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Kinman v. Omaha Public School District
- **Citation or Docket:** 94 F.3d 463 (8th Cir. 1996); court-of-appeals No. 95-2809
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Procedural Category:** Simulation-assigned appellate merits
- **Lower-Court Event Date:** 1996-08-26; this is a source date, not an assigned simulation event.

- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit affirmed summary judgment on §1983 and reversed summary judgment on Title IX for trial. The review assignment includes both components; the original Nebraska tort dismissal was not appealed and is outside it.
- **Relief Requested:** Kinman seeks reinstatement of the constitutional claims and an available Title IX damages action. The District and supervisory officials seek judgment protecting them from attribution of the teacher's misconduct.
- **Material Record:** Kinman attended Bryan High School from 1986 to May 1990. She alleges that English teacher Sheryl McDougall exploited a relationship originating at school and engaged in unwelcome sexual conduct. Later participation and a postgraduation relationship are conceded; initiation, welcome, and officials' knowledge are disputed. Her mother requested a classroom transfer; several students and staff later reported a relationship. Officials interviewed Kinman and her mother, attempted a telephone trace allegedly placed on the wrong line, administered two polygraphs to Kinman, and eventually confronted the teacher, who denied misconduct. They did not initially suspend her under school policy. After receiving a journal, photographs and cards in 1992, they pursued termination and certificate revocation. Summary judgment requires supported disputes to be viewed favorably to Kinman; none of these allegations becomes a finding of liability.
- **Vehicle Limits:** Recipient liability, each supervisor's own conduct, the District's policy, and the teacher's personal responsibility are distinct. The original default against McDougall was vacated; no final damages amount is supplied.

### Questions Presented

1. Whether a school district receiving federal financial assistance may be held liable for a teacher's sexual harassment of a student under Title IX when school officials knew or reasonably should have known of the misconduct and failed to take appropriate corrective action.
2. Whether a school district's liability for teacher-student sexual harassment under Title IX should be governed by agency principles developed under Title VII, or by a distinct standard applicable to federal funding recipients.
3. Whether a school district and its supervisory officials may be held liable under 42 U.S.C. §1983 for failing to prevent a teacher's sexual misconduct when officials investigated complaints but allegedly failed to take adequate protective measures.
4. Whether same-sex harassment by a teacher constitutes discrimination on the basis of sex under Title IX, notwithstanding the absence of opposite-sex harassment.

Simulated Franklin v. Gwinnett County (February 26, 1992) permits compensation within the established Title IX action for proved intentional discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss. It rejects a categorical Spending Clause notice objection to that intentional-discrimination remedy, but expressly leaves recipient attribution open. It therefore prevents dismissing Kinman's demand merely because she graduated, while neither authorizing negligence damages against every recipient nor importing Title VII's entire agency formula.

Simulated Harris v. Forklift (November 9, 1993) requires objectively and subjectively abusive sex-based conditions sufficiently severe or pervasive, assessed through frequency, severity, threats, humiliation and interference; psychological injury is not required and no factor is indispensable. It governs employment and supplies a reasoned analogy for educational harm, not a completed Title IX attribution rule. Meritor separates unwelcome advances from merely voluntary participation. Neither prior decision establishes a same-sex exclusion.

Most directly, simulated Doe v. Taylor Independent School District (October 16, 1995) makes a supervisor personally responsible for actual knowledge of school-authority sexual abuse or a substantial risk, deliberate and unreasonable disregard despite authority and opportunity to protect, and resulting injury. Reasonable responses do not become unconstitutional merely because harm follows; constructive knowledge alone fails. Lankford's assumed knowing acquiescence and Caplinger's distinct conduct-time immunity ruling cannot be merged. Doe did not decide that Caplinger's conduct was constitutionally permissible. The school's investigation here must be evaluated for what each official actually knew and reasonably did, not counted as automatically sufficient or ignored as automatically inadequate. Doe leaves District liability and Title IX open. Monell requires institutional policy or attributable final decision; Hafer permits personal-capacity claims without erasing personal immunities, and Elder requires conduct-date notice rather than retroactive reliance on the 1995 decision.

## C. LITIGATION POSITIONS, LAWFUL PATHS, AND UNCERTAINTY

Kinman stresses repeated reports, the unfulfilled classroom-transfer request, the asymmetric inquiry and continued access. Defendants stress denial, inconclusive tests, an actual investigation and prompt removal once corroborating evidence arrived. Those facts can bear differently on constructive notice, actual knowledge and deliberate disregard. For Title IX, the competing possibilities are agency-based attribution, recipient wrongdoing, or a mixed rule tied to delegated authority; Franklin alone selects none. Same-sex targeting must still be because of sex, and education-related harm must be shown.

The Court may preserve the Title IX remand under a specified lawful standard, modify it to require a narrower recipient connection, or uphold dismissal if no supported route survives. It may affirm or reopen particular §1983 claims without reinstating every defendant. No remedy should decide credibility, award damages, regulate the school indefinitely, or reopen the unappealed state claim. The existing appellate record does not establish which supervisor possessed final District policymaking authority.

### Current law and distinct components

Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer (February24) now establishes the Title IX because-of-sex requirement while expressly reserving a comprehensive recipient-attribution rule. Oncale (March19) unanimously rejects a categorical same-sex exclusion under Title VII, without requiring desire, orientation, or a real opposite-sex comparator; it preserves causation, unwelcome treatment, contextual severity and attribution as distinct. Those Title VII holdings bear on the textual analogy here, but neither changes Title IX into Title VII or resolves recipient damages attribution. Andrade (February26) preserves an actual-delegated-authority/material-aid agency route and negligence as separate Title VII inquiries, not strict liability from supervisory title. Whether and how that rule translates to a funding recipient remains the granted question.

Nabozny (March10) preserves intentional equal-protection liability for discriminatorily withheld school protection, separates individual conduct and municipal attribution, and rejects both automatic school immunity and a general constitutional insurance duty. No newly invented selective-enforcement claim is added to Kinman's granted supervisory case. Bryan County was restored for reargument on April28 and supplies no merits rule. The opening Doe v. Taylor rule controls actual-knowledge supervisory deliberate disregard; its immunity components remain actor- and conduct-date-specific. Lanier (March31) rejects a factual-twin requirement for fair warning without supplying retrospective notice. All personal immunity analysis here must use the time each official acted in1988-1990, rather than the1995/1997 decisions as notice. A municipality does not inherit personal qualified immunity.
