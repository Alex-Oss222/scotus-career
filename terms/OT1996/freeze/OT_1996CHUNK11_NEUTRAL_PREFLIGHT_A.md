## Klehr: record distinctions for clean validation

The complete Eighth Circuit opinion in petition Appendix A was read. It reviews summary judgment, accepts the governing view of evidence favorable to the nonmovants, and treats the asserted later losses as a single continuous injury because they share type, source and pattern. That is the lower court's legal classification of the record, not a trial finding that no post-1989 communications or losses occurred.

The petition, pp.5–6, alleges later advertising caused continued use until 1991, livestock and milk-production losses, and repair expenditure through 1990. The complaint reproduced at G-24–25 identifies advertisements dated August 25 and October 25, 1989. The first date precedes the August 27 limitations boundary; the second follows it. The petition also references a fall-1989 advertisement; do not merge the advertisements or infer their exact receipt dates.

The petitioners' merits brief, pp.40–43, requests a separate later-injury recovery as an alternative to the broader accrual theory. It contests the equation of same type/source/pattern with one injury and alleges later cattle illnesses requiring treatment and reduced production. Respondents, pp.27–29, characterize all such losses as natural and foreseeable consequences of the original silo defect. Neither characterization becomes an uncontested fact merely because a brief states it.

JA volume 1 contains a narrative describing a fall-1990 unloader motor replacement. It also contains the affidavit identifying 1982 repair invoices and scattered herd problems over many years. The existence of that later repair does not itself establish fraud, a predicate act, a legally distinct injury or a recoverable amount caused by a specific post-cutoff act.

The petitioners' brief p.16 note 9 identifies Dr. Behr's loss comparison for 1974–1993 at JA960/963 and cites JA982 for later improvement after stopping the feed. These are precise follow-up references if year-specific allocation becomes material. They are not certified here as a judicially found damages segregation. The downloaded second JA OCR does not cleanly expose those numbered pages; this receipt makes **no finding that the primary record lacks them**. Inspect the corresponding image PDF and both volume tables of contents before concluding a particular citation or damage allocation is absent. A segregated newer loss is neither proved nor disproved merely by continued use or by the lower court's general classification.

## Del Monte and Rambo procedural limits

Del Monte's appellate opinion expressly distinguishes the two liability verdicts from alternative theories within a single general verdict. The takings instructions offered two alternative bases: deprivation of all economically viable use or failure substantially to advance a legitimate public purpose. The equal-protection instruction separately required differential treatment of similarly situated property without a rational basis. The appellate court explained that one adequately supported liability claim could support the common delay award; a takings defect therefore does not by itself dispose of the whole judgment. Preserve the grant's scope and the unappealed due-process disposition.

Rambo's appellate opinion discusses preservation of estoppel and nominal relief. It rejected estoppel because detrimental reliance was missing and treated opposition to downward modification as sufficient to preserve a lesser nominal award. Those are lower-court rulings, not new Supreme Court holdings. The current grant concerns nominal compensation and lawful administrative factfinding. Rambo I's actual local mandate controls the effect of the earlier Supreme Court proceeding; historical quotations in the later briefs cannot enlarge it.
