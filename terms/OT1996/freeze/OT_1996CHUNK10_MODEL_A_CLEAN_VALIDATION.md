## Doe: validated issue boundaries

The CAPTA question concerns the report-triggered duty in the conduct-time §5106a(b)(2): prompt initiation of an investigation to substantiate an abuse or neglect report. The different duty to take immediate protective steps is triggered by a finding of abuse or neglect. A preinjury finding is not established. The mandatory text has not changed between the conduct and the official compilation; the 1992 grant-development and four-year-plan changes cannot be applied to the 1989 conduct. The statute's limited waiver mechanism is preserved, but no District waiver is established. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 concern the Social Security Act and do not directly amend CAPTA.

Both live statutory paths remain available for independent assessment. One reads the provision as a funding recipient's institutional investigative obligation, comparable in the relevant respect to Suter's reporting requirement. The other reads the particular report, identified child, required investigation, and promptness command as a judicially manageable individual entitlement under Wright, Wilder, Livadas, and Blessing. Neither path may equate every mandatory duty with a right, treat plan location as dispositive, confuse §1983 with an implied action, or treat federal funding supervision as categorical foreclosure. An implied CAPTA action was not asserted; the Adoption Act claim was withdrawn below. The provisions and claims must not be merged.

The allegations of warnings, response failures, and causation remain allegations under Rule 12(b)(6). The opinion describes scalding on February 23 and reports spanning late February to early March, while the complaint and notice letter assert preinjury reports. That sequence supplies no trial finding of preinjury notice, breach, preventability, or causation; it cannot be resolved against the pleaded preinjury allegation at dismissal.

The investigative-procedure due-process question is distinct. The lower opinion's assertion that a state-created entitlement requires a preexisting substantive constitutional protection duty is too broad under the supplied law. DeShaney bars a general affirmative protection duty, not every independently created statutory interest. Sandin rejects procedures alone but preserves substantive constraints. The relevant D.C. provisions include investigation, discretionary provision or securing of services, and conditional emergency-removal duties; no finding satisfying the latter trigger before injury is established. Custody commenced after the injury.

A further local-remedy due-process theory remains reserved. The majority discusses it as a reply-brief theory; Rogers treats it as the actual procedural theory. It concerns loss of an asserted local tort remedy through §12-309 given infancy, incapacity, and District custody. Both writings leave the certified notice issue for the D.C. Court of Appeals. ## Homar: validated issue boundaries

The undisputed sequence is August 26 arrest and immediate unpaid suspension, September 1 dismissal of charges, September 11 receipt of the supplemental police report, September 18 administrative meeting, and September 23–24 demotion and further meeting. Homar disputes the report's attributed admissions; accepting that officials received the report does not establish their truth. The September 18 opportunity cannot simply be called constitutionally sufficient: the lower opinion reports that the attributed admissions and receipt of that report were not disclosed to Homar then. By the September 24 meeting he had read the report. The effective time of demotion was disputed below.

The later groundskeeper-rate backpay, and February 16, 1993 authorization of the salary difference for the suspension period, do not erase the earlier deprivation. They may bear on relief if a violation is established.

The initial and continuing deprivations require Mathews analysis. A categorical rule that every unpaid suspension requires an advance hearing is one live lower-court position, not the governing rule. Conversely, the need to remove an officer from duty does not by itself make wages immaterial. The packet's suggestion that a separate fiscal emergency is necessarily required is advocacy for one path, not an already established element. An assessment may consider the short provisional wage deprivation, independent arrest, sensitive duties, confidence in the police, government interest in withholding pay while charges are investigated, error risk, and the value of an initial response.

Mallen supports provisional action upon an important governmental interest and substantial independent assurance; its precise banking statute, ongoing indictment, and delay holding do not make 23 days automatically reasonable here. Dismissal of charges materially changes the reason for continued suspension and increases the need to assess promptly any independent employment grounds. It does not conclusively decide employment misconduct. Young supports separation of temporary action and continuing deprivation, without transplanting parole procedures.

The Court may reject the categorical advance-hearing rule and remit the promptness/adequacy of continuing process, or sustain a prehearing-pay requirement on this record while allowing immediate duty removal. Neither route resolves immunity, damages, final demotion, the separate substantive-due-process claim, or the liberty/stigma claims outside the supplied grant.
