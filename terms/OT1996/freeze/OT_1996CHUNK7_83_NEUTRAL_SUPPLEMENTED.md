## Questions, record and lower decisions

Whether an adequate and independent Florida procedural bar prevents federal habeas consideration of the Espinosa claim, and whether that claim requests a new rule unavailable under Teague at Lambrix's **November 24, 1986** finality. Sequencing these nonjurisdictional defenses is itself a live issue. The source brief's statements that default invariably must precede Teague and that considering Teague necessarily creates unnecessary constitutional law are not controlling law and are not carried forward. Conversely, ordinary respect for an adequate state ground remains relevant and cannot be dismissed solely because Teague might be easier.

After a mistrial, a second jury convicted Lambrix of two murders and recommended death. The judge found five aggravators for Moore's murder, four for Bryant's, and no mitigation, then imposed two death sentences. The challenged instructions concerned heinous, atrocious or cruel (HAC) and cold, calculated and premeditated aggravation. The Eleventh Circuit assumed insufficient jury guidance for its Teague analysis; that assumption is not a new Supreme Court merits finding. Other lower-court claims concerning assistance of counsel, double jeopardy and testimony do not enter this grant merely because the lower opinion discusses them.

The Florida Supreme Court's June 16, 1994 decision, 641 So.2d 847, No. 81941, reports that Lambrix requested a limiting HAC instruction based on State v. Dixon and therefore preserved the instruction objection at trial. He did not pursue refusal of that instruction on direct appeal. Florida held the Espinosa claim barred on that ground. Thus this is **not** an established failure to preserve at trial. Florida separately treated renewed appellate-ineffectiveness allegations as successive, and alternatively rejected ineffectiveness because the instruction claim would have failed under its then-existing approach. Those separate grounds must not be merged into a single default or treated as a federal determination of cause.

The Eleventh Circuit's panel initially stayed its appeal to allow state consideration after Espinosa, then affirmed denial of the federal petition. It followed Glock's conclusion that the particular judge/jury correction rule was new. The panel's result is evidence of its reasoning, not a binding resolution of the Teague question in this Court. The original clean neutral's attributed published-source coverage reports that Florida asserted default before the District Court, the court of appeals, and again in a postjudgment clarification/modification motion. There is therefore no supported premise that Florida simply omitted the defense from all those stages. Whether any narrower forfeiture contention survives depends on its actual basis, not the source brief's generic suggestion.

Lambrix seeks reversal and lawful further habeas proceedings or sentencing relief. The State seeks affirmance on default, Teague or an available merits ground. A rejection of Teague alone does not itself issue a writ; a default disposition does not validate the instruction. No guilt retrial, automatic life sentence, immediate release or execution authorization follows from a mere gateway determination.

## Entering law and temporal limits

- **Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error**: present operation effective Espinosa, June 29, 1992; Stringer's direct-weighing component dates to March 9, 1992. A legally influential inadequately guided recommendation can infect the judge's sentence; surviving valid aggravators alone do not cure it. Genuine authorized reweighing and Chapman harmlessness beyond reasonable doubt are different routes. These merits and cure rules do not eliminate collateral gates.
- **Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity**: present operation effective Schiro, January 19, 1994, with Caspari's preserved subsidiary-defense comparison February 23, 1994. Teague is nonjurisdictional. Discretion to decline a belated defense is not a rule requiring its rejection; preserved and fairly included are distinct requirements.
- **Capital eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims**: present operation effective Sawyer, June 22, 1992. Clear and convincing evidence must show that no reasonable juror would find death eligibility absent constitutional error. Mitigation affecting only selection does not meet that gateway, and opening it does not itself grant relief. Ordinary cause and prejudice remain separate.

Teague requires identifying the precise requested rule and finality date, testing whether then-existing precedent dictated that rule, then considering both contemporary exceptions if it is new: substantive exclusions of conduct, persons or punishment from the State's power, and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Mere importance of a capital protection does not itself meet either exception. Caspari and Gilmore preserve the finality-specific inquiry. Gray, June 20, 1996, recognizes a dictated application of the preexisting opportunity-to-answer protection at its own finality, while preserving independent claim default and provision-specific statutory restrictions.

Stringer calls the Mississippi Godfrey-based weighing claim old at February 19, 1985 and remands an unresolved default; it neither decides Florida's judge/jury structure nor imposes a universal ordering between the two defenses. Espinosa establishes the Florida recommendation rule in 1992 but does not itself decide its retroactivity for a 1986-final judgment. Maynard (1988), Clemons (1990), Walton (1990), Smith (Florida 1987), and Grossman (Florida 1988) cannot be treated as precedents already decided in November 1986; they may clarify a claimed old principle or show a later development only with that distinction explicit.

Coleman, Harris v. Reed, Ford v. Georgia and Wainwright v. Sykes preserve the separate federal inquiries into actual state reliance, independence, adequacy at the relevant time, cause/prejudice and a qualifying miscarriage of justice. Florida's express 1994 reliance identifies its ground. The rule's existence and consistent operation when compliance was required remain a distinct federal adequacy question; recent postconviction cases do not by themselves settle that historical question.

AEDPA was enacted while the older federal petition was pending. No supplied in-world holding determines chapter 153's temporal reach for this petition. Gray and current Roy preserve that issue. ## Prefinality Florida sentencing law: weight and independence together

All four additional opinions below were decided before the November 24, 1986 finality date. They are authorities describing Florida's allocation of sentencing responsibility, not new evidence of Lambrix's trial or a decision of the federal retroactivity question. Full exact saved texts, including notes and separate dispositions, are copied in [the added primary reading slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK7_83_FLORIDA_PREFINALITY_READINGS.md).

**LeDuc v. State, 365 So.2d 149 (Fla. November 22, 1978), especially 150–152.** The defendant pleaded guilty; the jury recommended death and the judge imposed it. The court described the statute as requiring both jury and judge to evaluate and weigh the enumerated considerations. At 151 it applied to a death recommendation the principle that a recommendation should stand, **if all relevant data were considered**, absent strong reasons to believe reasonable persons could not agree. It cited Tedder in doing so. Accordingly, the prefinality landscape cannot be stated as affording weight only to a recommendation of life. The condition about relevant data is indispensable. LeDuc also independently reviewed the judge's findings and psychiatric information, including materials considered after relinquishment of appellate jurisdiction, and found no required mitigation on that record. It neither bound the judge to the jury nor resolved constitutional vagueness or collateral retroactivity. The two Justices who disagreed with the death dispositions supplied no separate explanation in this text; none is inferred.

**Messer v. State, 330 So.2d 137 (Fla. March 31, 1976), especially 141–143.** The court affirmed guilt but required a new jury sentencing hearing because relevant mitigation had been withheld from the jury: the accomplice's lenient plea sentence and proposed psychiatric evidence. It rejected the proposition that the jury's advisory status made the judge's later consideration of psychiatric material enough. Under the state statutory scheme, the jury was an integral safeguard and its recommendation's value depended on the information before it. The accomplice's sentence was relevant, not determinative; the psychiatric proof had to be allowed, not automatically accepted. Messer supplies concrete prefinality evidence that an independent judge did not make every defect in jury participation immaterial. Its holding rests on state sentencing procedure and excluded mitigation. It does not itself establish the federal rule for a vague aggravator, eliminate all judicial or appellate cures, or prescribe automatic life imprisonment.

**Ross v. State, 386 So.2d 1191 (Fla. June 12, 1980; rehearing denied September 12), especially 1197–1198.** The court affirmed guilt but vacated death and remanded for judicial resentencing because the judge treated the death recommendation as compelling instead of exercising his own reasoned judgment. Ross distinguishes Tedder's demanding life-override standard from LeDuc's treatment of death recommendations. Recommendations receive great weight and serious consideration, but do not bind the judge: §921.141(3) requires his independent balancing notwithstanding the recommendation, with judicial judgment interposed against the risk of juror emotion. Ross repeated LeDuc's relevant-data qualification. It also directed the judge not to count the same robbery twice through robbery and pecuniary gain; that duplication alone did not necessarily require reversal. Adkins would have sustained the death sentence because he thought the judge had exercised judgment; Boyd would have directed life because of the aggravation and mitigation balance. The majority's particular judicial remand is not a general rule that independent findings automatically cure vague jury instructions.

**Eutzy v. State, 458 So.2d 755 (Fla. September 20, 1984; rehearing denied December 3), especially 757–760.** The court sustained a judge's death sentence overriding a life recommendation, while requiring great weight for the jury and preserving the judge's independent role. It applied Tedder rather than treating the advisory label as eliminating weight. It rejected robbery as an aggravator for lack of supporting evidence, retained prior violent felony and cold/calculated/premeditated aggravation, and found no supported mitigating basis for the recommendation. An accomplice's equal culpability could not be presumed from speculation. Its rejection of the jury-instruction challenge rested specifically on the favorable **life** recommendation and absence of resulting harm; it did not reach the instruction's merits or declare every advisory-jury error harmless. The as-applied override objection was separately unpreserved. McDonald's unexplained death dissent supplies no invented ground.

These authorities establish a dual state-law account: a recommendation of death can carry substantial legal weight, while the judge must still make an independent reasoned sentencing decision. Weight is not binding force; independence is not absence of influence. The inquiry here must use both propositions. State-law departures are not automatically federal constitutional violations, and a state opinion's particular remedy does not itself dictate the federal collateral remedy.

## Precisely opposing retroactivity arguments from that landscape

**The old-rule argument** connects Godfrey's already-established need for meaningful guidance to the actual legal role of the Florida recommendation. LeDuc addresses death recommendations before finality; Messer demonstrates that the legislature's required jury contribution is a safeguard whose defective input cannot always be dismissed merely because the judge can consider the matter later. On this view, extending guidance to a legally influential recommendation preserves the existing protection's function, as Stringer later recognized in its different weighing context. Ross's independence requirement does not erase the accompanying weight requirement. The strongest answer is that state-law relevance and a state remedy for excluded mitigation do not necessarily compel the precise federal correction rule for an advisory jury's vague aggravator; the judge's independent and law-governed sentence may remain a legally significant distinction.

**The new-rule argument** asks whether reasonable jurists in November 1986 could distinguish a recommendation from the sentencer's legally decisive findings. Ross required an independently reasoned decision and remanded to the judge; Eutzy preserved judicial authority despite the recommendation's great weight; Spaziano and Baldwin recognized substantial latitude in allocating sentencing functions. Godfrey's direct-sentencer guidance rule might therefore have left this particular transmission-of-error question unresolved. The strongest answer is that the argument cannot treat the judge as unaffected by the recommendation when LeDuc and Ross supply legal weight, or rely on Eutzy's harmlessness discussion without its favorable-life posture. An advisory label, absence of a case with identical facts, later disagreement or the mere date of Espinosa does not alone establish novelty.

Neither path requires an all-or-nothing statement that the jury controlled sentencing or had no operative role. The demanded Teague showing is dictation of the precise federal rule at finality, not merely that one analogy is attractive. Current Espinosa governs its own merits proposition; it neither answers retroactivity by itself nor permits present disregard of the prefinality authorities. Smith (1987), Grossman (1988), Maynard (1988), Clemons (1990) and Walton (1990) remain later sources and cannot supply an authority already decided in November 1986. The new Florida readings repair the prefinality institutional account without changing the supplied trial-preservation, direct-appeal omission, state-habeas or federal-defense facts.

## Balanced lawful paths

1. **Determine default first:** Establish that the specifically identified state ground was independent, adequate when applicable, preserved and unexcused, then affirm without Teague or constitutional merits. Alternatively remand an actually unfinished material default issue. Strongest objection: federal default is nonjurisdictional, the State repeatedly asserted it, and a remand solely to repeat that known history may serve no legal purpose; the court must identify the genuine unresolved predicate rather than invent a waiver or jurisdictional barrier.
2. **Resolve Teague before a difficult default dispute:** If the requested rule is new at November 24, 1986 and neither contemporary exception applies, affirm on nonretroactivity while expressly reserving default and constitutional validity. Strongest objection: the established Godfrey guidance principle and the prefinality influence shown by LeDuc and Messer may already dictate protection; Ross and Eutzy must be read with, rather than in place of, their independent-judgment qualifications. Procedural economy alone does not answer the finality analysis or erase the value of respecting an adequate state ground.
3. **Reject the new-rule bar:** Determine that the specific claim follows from the prefinality guidance and sentencing-allocation authorities, distinguish rather than silently extend Stringer, and remand remaining default/cure/statutory questions as necessary. Strongest objection: prefinality authority may have permitted reasonable jurists to rely on the judge's lawful independent sentencing findings; the distinction between an advisory jury and the legally decisive judge requires substantive analysis. An old-rule determination does not itself establish unexcused default, harmful error or entitlement to a writ.
4. **An exception-based route, only if actually supported:** Classify the rule as new but satisfy one of the two then-existing exceptions on its own terms. Strongest objection: a correction to a recommendation may regulate sentencing procedure without excluding lawful punishment, and importance alone is not a watershed accuracy showing. Do not invent a second sufficient ground without support.

Analytical alternatives are not assertions that new arguments were filed. No legal consequence is derived merely from unavailable research papers.
