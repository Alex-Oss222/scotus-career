## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The bankruptcy court shall value the truck under the replacement-value standard and reconsider confirmation and the allowed secured claim. It shall determine the relevant market and adjustments from the evidence and shall not include value for items the debtor would not receive with a comparable replacement.

### Issue 1: Valuation of retained collateral under Section 506(a)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory valuation question. The competing figures are estimates, so the Court states the legal measure and remands for valuation.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the statute's proposed-use command. When the debtor keeps and uses the collateral, the secured claim is measured by the price of property of like age and condition available for the same proposed use, not by the amount the creditor would receive in a disposition the plan prevents. Replacement value is not the cost of a new item and must exclude warranties, services, or accessories the retained collateral does not provide.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Chapter 13 debtor who retains and uses collateral under the cram-down option must value the secured claim by replacement value for like property serving the proposed use, adjusted to the collateral's age and condition, rather than by net foreclosure value.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The plan displaces Associates' immediate foreclosure right and permits continued business use of the truck. Section 506(a) expressly makes proposed disposition or use relevant, and the chosen use is retention, not sale. Foreclosure value would price a transaction that will not occur and transfer the use-value advantage entirely to the debtors. Replacement value preserves the statutory distinction between surrender and retention while leaving factual valuation to the bankruptcy court.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prescribe retail value in every case, require valuation as new property, or decide valuation when the debtor surrenders, abandons, or proposes a materially different use.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved replacement-value result identifies the transaction the plan actually selects: continued use of this truck. It does not adopt a universal retail or new-truck price, and BFP’s insistence on context supports excluding unreceived warranties and services rather than dictating a foreclosure value here.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand with instructions to dismiss.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The Ninth Circuit shall direct dismissal of Schumer's qui tam action under the pre-1986 government-possession bar. The Court does not decide the amended public-disclosure rule, whether Hughes caused loss to the public fisc, or the constitutional questions excluded from the grant.

### Issue 1: Retroactive application of the 1986 qui tam amendment

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the temporal question because it disposes of the case. The parties agree that the Government possessed the material information within the pre-1986 bar.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Landgraf. The amendment does more than assign a tribunal or alter litigation procedure. It removes a complete defense, authorizes a relator with distinct financial incentives to sue where no private action was previously permitted, and exposes completed transactions to treble-damages litigation. Congress did not clearly prescribe retroactive operation.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The 1986 qui tam amendment does not govern alleged false claims submitted before enactment when applying it would revive a relator action barred by the prior government-possession rule.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Before 1986, Hughes's disclosure to the Government foreclosed Schumer's action. Applying the amendment would change the legal consequence of that completed disclosure and the alleged billing conduct. Calling the old bar jurisdictional does not avoid Landgraf because the amendment determines whether this plaintiff may maintain the claim at all. The pre-amendment law therefore controls and requires dismissal.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide prospective application of the amended Act, Government-filed actions, or a purely forum-allocating jurisdictional amendment that leaves substantive exposure unchanged.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not finally invalidate or sustain the physician-only provision. ## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fargo leaves a record-dependent purpose inquiry open

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preliminary merits standard because it is the basis of the appellate judgment. He does not resolve the final constitutional claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Did the Ninth Circuit err in concluding that the challengers had a sufficient chance of succeeding on their claim that Montana’s physician-only abortion requirement was enacted for the purpose of placing a substantial obstacle in the path of abortion access, despite the District Court’s finding that the provision had not been shown to create such an effect?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the controlling simulated purpose-or-effect rule rather than transform Casey’s approval of another physician-only provision into a categorical immunity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A physician-only abortion provision is not invalid merely because it excludes a particular nonphysician or was supported by abortion opponents. But when record evidence could show that the provision was specifically designed to create a substantial obstacle, the independent purpose prohibition within the controlling simulated Casey sequence warrants examination; Casey does not foreclose that inquiry by label alone. The remaining steps, including review of meaningful or severe interference short of a substantial obstacle, retain independent force.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The received simulated rule requires the entire Casey analysis, including independent purpose/obstacle prohibitions and review of residual severe or meaningful interference. Medical qualification requirements must be assessed on their actual operation and supported justification; the professional label supplies no categorical immunity. Here the singular practical target, legislative sequence, and companion provisions create a legally sufficient question for provisional review. Those facts do not yet prove the claim, and ordinary preliminary-injunction burdens remain with the challengers.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that targeting Cahill establishes unconstitutional purpose, that any reduction in providers is a substantial obstacle, or that the injunction must remain in force after the District Court applies the proper standard.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

### Application of the corrected governing standard

Stone's proposed affirmance of the appellate vacatur/remand remains user-approved. He would require the District Court to apply all six Casey steps, including residual meaningful or severe interference, rather than demand only a substantial obstacle. The deliberate removal of the sole supervised nonphysician provider and legislative sequence support inquiry but do not establish the ultimate purpose, effect or equities. Montana may substantiate a genuine medical-safety justification; the court must test that justification against the actual duty and competent contrary evidence once challengers establish the triggering burden. The disposition leaves ordinary preliminary-relief factors and appropriate scope to the District Court and does not command an injunction, evidentiary hearing or final invalidation merely because the preparation record is incomplete.

## A. STATUS

- **Approval Status:** Approved by the user on October 11, 2026 as Stone's proposed position, reasoning, and stated fallback. It remains a proposal to the Court, not decided simulated law.

## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **Use the ordinary preponderance standard.** Stone would reject a clear-and-convincing motive burden created solely to reduce civil-rights litigation. The statutory remedy and ordinary civil rules provide no such category. Kimberlin has already rejected direct-only proof; replacing that barrier with an unsupported elevated burden recreates much of its exclusionary effect without a source. The protection of officials from unfounded cases warrants use of existing screening tools, not alteration of the underlying plaintiff's burden.

2. **Qualified immunity does not authorize an independent judicial evidentiary code.** Leatherman's reserved individual-officer question must be answered rather than falsely called settled. Stone would extend its source-based reasoning here: the Rules control pleading and proof, subject to their authorized particularity, reply, and case-management mechanisms. Motive remains an element where the constitutional claim makes it one. Officials may contest protected activity, personal involvement, causation, and the Mt. Healthy same-decision issue; none requires pretending motive is legally irrelevant.

3. **No categorical requirement to prove motive before obtaining the discovery needed to prove it.** Stone would permit sequencing of a genuinely dispositive legal issue and restrictions against irrelevant or disproportionate requests. The judge should identify what factual issue requires discovery and why the proposed request bears on it. The prisoner must supply a cognizable, nonconclusory claim, but circumstantial proof, comparator handling, and evidence held by Britton cannot be excluded merely because they are not yet in his possession.

**Express personal-immunity position.** Governing Harlow/Anderson and Kimberlin preserve the broader clearly-established-law defense. Stone's current Remedies commitment proposes replacing that defense with a merits-first inquiry and a narrow defense, borne by the official, for genuine reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct. Legal silence, ordinary departmental practice, or the absence of identical facts would not suffice. This is a proposed departure, not Kimberlin's holding. It preserves reasonable factual mistakes and lawful discretion within the underlying constitutional rule and protects genuine reliance on authoritative legal permission, while rejecting a second immunity barrier detached from such reliance. The present result also follows under the existing defense: its objective character does not justify the new proof burden.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Vacate the en banc judgment insofar as it imposes the clear-and-convincing burden and an unauthorized categorical pre-discovery proof barrier; remand the retaliation claim under ordinary civil standards and Kimberlin, with lawful tailored case management. Preserve independently resolved claims and unresolved defenses. Stone would state his narrow legal-reliance proposal without claiming Court adoption; if the Court retains existing immunity, he independently supports the same procedural disposition under that law. No retaliation finding, damages award, or guaranteed trial is proposed.
