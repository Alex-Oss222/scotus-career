## Operative grounds and adverse-path checks

**Common propositions — eight direct joins.** Section 1681 contains no opposite-sex condition. Brown requires because-of-sex treatment; Oncale supplies an analogy rather than a finding of discrimination. The reported sex-directed selection and absence of comparable attentions to male students support causation at this stage without creating a compulsory comparator rule. Meritor distinguishes participation from welcome; neither continuation after graduation nor acquiescence conclusively defeats the reported initial objection. A sufficiently severe or pervasive, objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive remains necessary. Harris informs context, authority, coercion, frequency, severity, humiliation and educational interference without requiring diagnosis, measurable grade loss or every listed circumstance. No orientation-coverage rule, credibility finding or damages finding follows.

**Recipient disagreement — four against four.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer treat the delegated educational relationship as a possible institutional connection to intentional harassment and reasonable correction as a distinct connection. Calling Kinman from study hall, the teacher relationship, reports, transfer request and imperfect investigation support further adjudication under those standards. Status, access or policy violation alone does not suffice. Their strongest objection is the funding-recipient structure and Title VII's express agent language; their answer remains that ordinary program responsibility and private compensation need not adopt section 1682's administrative sequence as suit prerequisites. Franklin defeats a categorical compensation objection but does not itself establish their attribution rule.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas require the recipient's actual corrective-official notice and deliberate disregard. They distinguish Franklin's remedy from attribution of the intentional wrong and rely on the recipient structure, section 1682's opportunity for correction, and Pennhurst at the reserved attribution question. They do not transplant agency notice, a hearing, congressional reporting or thirty days into the private action. Their strongest objection is the authority-enabled abuse left uncompensated by a notice prerequisite; they maintain that the enacted recipient obligation differs from Title VII's employer-agent relationship. On this record, they assume awareness of the substantial risk but find deliberate disregard insufficiently supported. Later conclusive proof is not a prerequisite to all action; the record shows earlier responses as well.

**Whitehouse and Mackiel — eight votes to affirm, on personal constitutional merits.** Doe requires actual knowledge of abuse or substantial risk, deliberate and unreasonable disregard despite protective authority and opportunity, and causation. The mother/student/staff reports to Whitehouse and Grosse's October 16 report to Mackiel are not merged into collective knowledge. Assuming sufficient awareness for each, the interviews, attempted trace, testing, confrontation and later authentication/termination distinguish the reported response from knowing acquiescence. Transfer refusal, nonsuspension, delay and asymmetry remain relevant adverse evidence; they support the statutory negligence dispute for four Associates but do not establish deliberate disregard under these commitments. An allegation of a wrong-line trace supplies no separate evidence of a deliberately chosen error. No Justice treats denials or polygraphs as conclusive truth, demands confession, or holds that any investigation necessarily defeats liability.

**District — eight votes to affirm on separate attribution grounds.** The record establishes neither an unconstitutional policy/custom causing injury nor a relevant decision by an established final policymaker. Monell does not permit ordinary rank or the teacher's conduct to supply the missing institutional link. A widespread pattern is not made indispensable to every single-final-decision theory; that alternative fails for want of its attributable decision, not because one decision could never suffice. The District receives no qualified immunity.

**Immunity and scope.** The individual supervisory merits ground makes a new immunity holding unnecessary. Doe's Caplinger ruling concerned particularized conduct-time notice and did not approve his constitutional conduct; it is not a merits shortcut here. Elder and Lanier preserve the 1988–1990 notice dates and forbid using later holdings as earlier warning. Nabozny supplies no new selective-enforcement claim. The teacher's separate responsibility, vacated default, unappealed Nebraska tort dismissal and absence of a proved damages amount remain distinct. No new direct-teacher disposition, injunction, mandatory trial or postgraduation violation is invented.
