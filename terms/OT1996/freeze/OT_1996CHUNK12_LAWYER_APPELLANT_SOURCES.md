## Original claim and requested relief

The jurisdictional statement at 2–3 and merits brief at 5–6 describe the complaint as a Fourteenth Amendment challenge to the configuration of original District 21, Plan 330. The quoted allegations concern the deliberate grouping of minority residents with different interests, irregular boundaries and asserted racial segregation in district design. The merits brief's footnote 1 at 5 describes the requested declaration, injunction against elections under that plan, and an order requiring redistricting consistent with traditional districting principles. These submissions do not describe a separate original count for vote dilution, exclusion from the franchise or unequal participation. That observation is limited to these three submissions; the separate joint-appendix review checks the actual pleading.

Lawyer's proposed constitutional objection to replacement Plan 386 likewise rests on shape, racial population distributions, the crossing of Tampa Bay and county boundaries, and the motives asserted to explain them. The merits brief at 42 n.4 analogizes the inclusion or extraction of minority populations to Gomillion; it does not identify a pleaded deprivation of the right to vote. The earlier NAACP objections recited at 3–4 and reply 13–14 concern the 1992 state redistricting process and do not establish a separate count in this action. Sections 2 and 5 of the Voting Rights Act appear in the background and in disputes about the legality of the proposed remedy and preclearance; these references are not proof of an independent original statutory claim.

## Decree and state participation

The merits brief at 6–15 and 21–34 attacks federal remedial authority as an issue distinct from Plan 386's own constitutionality. It says the Legislature did not enact Plan 386 through a joint resolution and the Florida Supreme Court did not adopt it under the state's fallback process. The asserted consent was supplied by officers and counsel in the litigation. Lawyer contends that authority to represent legislative bodies in litigation is different from authority to enact a districting plan. The reply at 1–4 gives the competing preservation account: prior comments that only a remedy remained assumed an adjudication of liability, and Lawyer continued to object to the proposed procedure and plan.

The appended lower order at jurisdictional-statement Appendix B, 7a–13a, expressly predicates federal intervention on a material and fairly contestable constitutional dispute. Its stated basis is the historical Miller racial-design standard applied to Plan 330's boundaries, racial composition and race awareness. The settlement concessions are expressly for settlement purposes; defendants deny liability and reserve their defense if a materially different remedy is adopted. The order does not purport to rely on consent alone. Its separate concurrence would instead find original Plan 330 unconstitutional under the same historical Shaw/Miller theory before approving the new plan. These descriptions identify the lower court's premise without treating that historical premise as current governing law.

The merits brief at 26–31 invokes Firefighters, City of Miami and the lower-court Perkins, Kasper and Clements cases to argue that a federal decree requires a lawful federal predicate and cannot dispose of a nonconsenting party's valid claims merely by agreement. These are the appellant's arguments; the independent neutral preflight must test their applicability against governing law and the other parties' submissions.

## Evidence and procedural limits

The merits brief at 12–15 and reply at 7–11 dispute whether the fairness hearing and final order supplied adequate findings and an opportunity to present relevant evidence. They acknowledge that Guthrie's declaration was received and that he was available for examination, but argue that examining him would not establish the negotiating officials' motives. They dispute the exclusion of examination of Justice Department attorney Mulroy. The reply at 9 n.4 withdraws reliance on Lawyer's own alternative plan as a constitutional replacement; that plan is not the relief sought in this appeal. No credibility finding or procedural default is inferred from these submissions.

The jurisdictional statement and merits brief date the complaint April 14, 1994; the appended final order says April 4. The challenged final order is March 19, 1996, and the appeal was filed April 16, 1996. The complaint-day discrepancy is preserved as a source variance and is not material to the presently identified legal-predicate question.

## Handoff limit

An independent cognizable federal claim must be verified from the actual pleading and record, not inferred from party consent, preclearance, the presence of minority intervenors, an analogy to vote dilution, or the existence of a settlement. This source review does not select among affirmance, reversal, remand or a stopped matter. Any current-law defect in the lower court's stated predicate requires neutral analysis and renewed non-Stone modeling before assembly.
