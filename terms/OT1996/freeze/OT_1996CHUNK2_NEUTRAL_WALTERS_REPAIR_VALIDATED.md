## Source basis and exact changes to the neutral premises

The [objective supplement](../sources/chunk2-neutral/Walters_OBJECTIVE_SUPPLEMENT.md) supplies control-extracted, sanitized published recitations from official U.S. Reports 519 U.S. 202, at 205–206 and 211–212, [official bound volume](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/519bv.pdf). Validation here uses that saved supplement, not the current matter's opinion, outcome or reasoning. Its recitations affirmatively support the described stipulations and filing omissions. They do not establish that the original stipulation, payrolls, petition or merits brief was independently recovered or read in this context.

1. **Promotion date and coverage years.** Walters's additional promotion claim concerns a credit-manager promotion in September 1989. The District Court used 1989 and 1990 as the relevant years. Applying the statute's current-or-preceding-calendar-year formulation to the September 1989 event identifies 1988 and 1989 for that claim. This mismatch must be identified separately from the counting-method question. The stipulated failure to reach the threshold in 1989 remains; the supplement supplies no threshold count for 1988. The District Court's selection of years is a description of its treatment, not a statutory command to use 1990 for this promotion claim.

2. **Actual filing omission.** The published recitation states that Walters did not raise the coverage-year discrepancy in her certiorari petition or merits brief. This is affirmative source support for the omission, not an inference from missing research files. It supplies a preservation and review-scope issue, not a finding of intentional waiver, a jurisdictional ruling or a predetermined consequence. No historical judicial resolution of that omission is imported.

3. **Forty-seven weekly payroll counts.** The published stipulation description identifies 47 weeks in 1990 with at least fifteen employment relationships as counted through the weekly payroll. The calculation originally counted employees appearing on the payroll during each week without determining whether each was employed on every working day of that week. Consequently, 47 cannot simply be relabeled as 47 conclusively qualifying daily-compliance weeks.

4. **Nine potentially affected midweek-change weeks and the minimum calculation.** The supplement identifies nine weeks with midweek employment changes. Excluding every one of those potentially affected weeks from the 47 weekly counts leaves **47 − 9 = 38 weeks**. Under a construction that counts genuine employment relationships on each working day, this supplies a record-supported minimum calculation above the statutory twenty-week requirement, without determining whether any of the excluded nine weeks also qualifies. It does not supply particular hiring or termination dates, continuous employment throughout all fifty-two weeks, or an individual status adjudication for each worker. The legal adoption of the relationship criterion and application of the statute remain for modeling.

5. **Two distinct uses of the number nine.** The prior handoff separately reports only nine weeks satisfying the lower courts' compensation-based method. Those are not identified as the same nine weeks with midweek employment changes. The first figure describes payment on every working day; the second identifies weeks conservatively removed from the relationship-based calculation. Neither number may be substituted for the other.

6. **Scope of respondent's objection.** The published recitation describes Metropolitan's objection as a challenge to using the existence of employment relationships as the counting criterion, rather than a challenge to the described use of payroll evidence to establish those relationships. This narrows the supported dispute. It does not concede the correct interpretation of the statute, convert payroll records into an irrebuttable legal classification, or establish universal agreement about every possible employee-status question.

The prior handoff's final exact-count warning is therefore superseded to this extent: absence of the original payrolls no longer requires treating the number of relationship-counting weeks as unknown. The 38-week minimum is a supported application path on the published stipulated premises. A new inquiry into unspecified worker identities or ordinary off-days cannot be introduced merely to restore the superseded uncertainty.

The relevant [entering-law slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK2_C.md) supplies these separate rules and limits:

- **Darden, March 24, 1992:** the common-law-agency inquiry governs ERISA's circular definition, considering all relationship incidents without making any single factor decisive. Its interpretive framework is available at that stated scope; it is not an existing decision on Title VII's daily numerosity language.
- **Town & Country, November 28, 1995:** statutory text, compatible service and actual control matter to employee classification; the decision's NLRA holding and express exclusions remain statute-specific. Neither case proves that any identified person here is an independent contractor, former employee or excluded worker. Ordinary genuine-relationship qualifications remain distinct from an unsupported new factual contest.
- **Yee, April 1, 1992, preservation and question-presented holdings:** a new argument supporting a preserved claim is distinct from a separate question outside the grant. The question-presented limit is prudential, and departing from it requires a supported exceptional basis. Relatedness alone does not establish that a separate question is fairly included. This supports analysis of the promotion-year issue without predetermining its classification or consequence.
- **United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992, reviewability holding:** a federal question expressly decided below may be reviewed in its particular circumstances despite failure to renew opposition to binding circuit precedent. This permits review within that holding's limits; it neither obliges review of every omitted question nor excuses an affirmative waiver. The statement that the District Court used two years does not by itself establish that the coverage-year discrepancy was expressly adjudicated by the court of appeals.
- **Zafiro, January 25, 1993, petitioner-specific preservation holding:** the Court's ordinary practice permits declining an individual's unpursued and unpassed-upon claim absent a supported exceptional reason, without treating that nonreview as a merits rejection, jurisdictional bar or intentional waiver. Its distinct appellate facts are not findings about Walters's litigation.

The prior handoff's mechanically recovered statutory text and verified temporal limits remain the operative textual source. No later amendment, later judicial classification rule or current-matter historical waiver holding enters this addendum.

## Supported alternative paths and remaining boundaries

These are analytical possibilities, not reported party submissions or judicial choices:

1. **Retain compensation counting.** Assess the strongest daily-compensation reading, including its treatment of paid leave and regular part-time off-days, and consider the lower dismissal within the preserved review scope. The reported compensation count remains nine weeks. This path does not supply an absent 1988 count or decide the separate promotion-year review issue by implication.

2. **Adopt relationship counting and apply the stipulated minimum.** Give the employment relationship effect on working days, use payroll as evidence of that relationship, and assess the conservative 38-week minimum for the 1990 period. The record then supports considering disposition of the numerical objection without a remand merely to discover an exact count hidden by the prior phrase “most of 1990.” Any further proceedings can remain confined to matters genuinely left unresolved, including the claims' merits and the separately addressed promotion issue.

3. **Decline the unraised promotion-year issue within the Court's review practice.** Independently assess the granted question and filing omission under the entering procedural authorities. A supported decision to leave the mismatch outside the review would not declare that 1990 is a proper statutory year for a September 1989 event, adjudicate discrimination merits or establish intentional relinquishment. The scope of any mandate must state how the promotion component is treated.

4. **Consider the promotion-year issue only on a supported review basis.** A conclusion that the issue is fairly included, or an identified exceptional basis for considering it, must be reasoned from entering law and the actual posture; the omission itself creates neither permission nor an automatic bar. If reaching it makes the 1988 count material, that count remains unknown. A limited remand or other procedurally supported treatment can preserve the correct years without inventing 1988 facts or importing the historical Court's response to the omission.

Payroll evidence remains rebuttable as a general legal matter: a person not yet hired, whose relationship has ended, or who is not an employee under the governing statute does not become one solely through a payroll entry. These qualifications preserve the chosen rule's ordinary boundaries. They are not new findings about these stipulated relationships, and do not automatically reopen the supported 38-week calculation. An outcome that actually depends on a particular exception, disputed identity, additional preserved filing or precise appellate treatment requires affirmative support for that proposition.
