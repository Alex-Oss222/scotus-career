## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **The report-triggered initiation obligation creates a bounded enforceable right on the pleaded claim.** Stone would distinguish an individualized command to begin investigating a reported endangered child from an unmeasured promise of an adequately functioning welfare system. Doe is within the identified beneficiary class; the duty is mandatory; a complete alleged failure to initiate investigation can be assessed without dictating professional conclusions or imposing a judicially invented deadline. He would allow §1983 enforcement of that limited obligation while rejecting an absolute federal guarantee of protection or a right to every service.

2. **Mandatory funding language is neither automatically enforceable nor automatically administrative only.** Stone would apply Wright/Wilder and the actual Suter limits, examining this provision, beneficiary, duty, and enforcement structure. He would find no demonstrated comprehensive remedial scheme foreclosing the asserted §1983 route. This distinguishes rather than overrules Suter's holdings concerning different statutory provisions; he would expressly reject extending Suter into a categorical state-plan bar. His prior disagreement with Suter does not itself supply the right. The proposed CAPTA conclusion must stand on its own enacted command and administrable initiation duty.

3. **Mandatory investigative procedures alone do not create the claimed liberty interest.** Stone would affirm rejection of that theory as framed, without saying government custody is the only possible source of a protected interest. Sandin requires genuine substantive entitlement rather than a procedural word count. The present allegation does not establish custody before injury or an official's affirmative infliction of the private harm. The separate statutory right identified above is enforced through its own §1983 channel; it is not automatically converted into a constitutional guarantee. The local negligence duty and unresolved notice issues remain distinct.

**Remaining personal and institutional questions.** Stone would require causation and defendant-specific responsibility on remand, not impose respondeat superior on the District. Existing Court law retains conduct-date qualified immunity for personal damages. His Master Remedies position proposes a narrower defense borne by the officer: genuine reasonable reliance on binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct. He would state that change openly if a personal immunity ruling becomes necessary, preserve actual authoritative reliance, and treat legal silence or routine inaction as insufficient. Recognition of a statutory right today does not itself prove personal culpability in 1989. No immunity outcome is decided on this undeveloped dismissal record.

## C. PRECISE PROPOSED DISPOSITION

Reverse dismissal of the narrow federal statutory claim concerning prompt initiation of investigation after a qualifying report and remand for the remaining elements and defenses. Affirm dismissal of the procedural-due-process claim insofar as it rests solely on mandatory investigative procedures. Preserve the certification of the D.C.-law notice questions and the unresolved local claims. Order no damages, compulsory service plan, or institutional decree. The distinction between an enacted, report-triggered duty and a general constitutional protection guarantee produces the different dispositions.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall authorize focused discovery reasonably directed to whether Maloney was actually biased in Bracy's case, including the asserted relationship with counsel, trial scheduling, and relevant corruption evidence. The court shall then adjudicate the habeas claim under the developed record. No finding of bias or entitlement to a new trial is made.

### Issue 1: Good cause for discovery on the judicial-bias claim

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the discovery ruling. Bracy's allegations were preserved, and the public corruption record is judicially cognizable. Final bias, prejudice, procedural default, and remedy remain for later adjudication.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies Rule 6. Good cause does not require the petitioner to prove the concealed fact before receiving the means reasonably necessary to investigate it. The petitioner must identify a legally sufficient bias theory and concrete facts connecting that theory to his own trial. Bracy has done so, while the district court retains authority to confine discovery to the relevant connection.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Bracy established good cause for focused discovery because Maloney's proved corruption, the alleged use of unbribed cases to camouflage fixed cases, counsel's relationship and rapid preparation, and the trial's placement among identified fixed cases provide a concrete basis to investigate actual bias in Bracy's own prosecution.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Maloney's corruption defeats the ordinary assumption of regular judicial conduct, but it does not alone prove Bracy's claim. The additional case-specific allegations supply the missing connection. Requiring admissible proof of the very agreement or motive alleged to be hidden would make Rule 6 circular. Discovery may still fail to substantiate the claim, and the district court may sequence depositions and documents to avoid an unfocused inquiry.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize discovery whenever a judge committed unrelated misconduct or permit merits relief based on appearance alone. Bracy must ultimately prove constitutionally material bias in his own case.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s approved focused discovery follows from the specific corruption, counsel, and trial-sequence allegations. It neither requires proof of the concealed constitutional violation before discovery nor treats any suspicious judicial ruling as sufficient on its own. His remedy leaves Rule 6 scope and subsequent habeas adjudication to the district court without ordering a new trial.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Third Circuit's reversal of summary judgment and remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court shall apply Mathews separately to immediate removal from duty and immediate cessation of pay, resolve any disputed justification for nonpayment, and determine whether the post-suspension opportunity to respond was prompt and effective. The Court does not finally decide liability, damages, qualified immunity, or the later demotion.

### Issue 1: Immediate removal and immediate nonpayment

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches whether the officials were entitled to summary judgment. The incomplete record does not permit a final ruling that immediate nonpayment was constitutionally justified.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an independent felony arrest that supports immediate removal from sensitive duties necessarily supports immediate suspension without pay.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The arrest substantially reduces the initial error risk concerning the need to remove a university police officer from sensitive duties. It does not automatically establish a separate need to stop salary before a prompt response opportunity. The government may justify immediate nonpayment through a demonstrated fiscal, integrity, evidence-preservation, flight, or comparable urgency, but no such ground is established as a matter of law on the supplied record.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An independent felony arrest can justify immediate removal from sensitive duties. Summary judgment is unavailable on an immediate unpaid suspension when the record identifies no separate need for prehearing nonpayment.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Removal protects the public and institution while the accusation is assessed. Nonpayment imposes a distinct property deprivation and requires its own place in the Mathews balance. Because the officials did not establish a separate urgency for stopping pay, the lower court properly rejected summary judgment without creating a categorical paid-suspension rule.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require paid suspension in every case. A demonstrated urgency, together with prompt corrective process, may alter the balance.

### Issue 2: Prompt post-suspension process

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone states the governing requirement but leaves final application to the lower courts because the supplied record does not establish the precise interval, content, and justification as a matter of law.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Once immediate action is taken, the employee must receive a prompt, informed opportunity to contest the employment consequence. Dismissal of the criminal charges changes the balance and requires the employer to proceed on its own employment grounds rather than rely indefinitely on the arrest.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The lower courts must determine whether effective post-suspension process was prompt in light of the continued unpaid deprivation and the dismissal of the criminal charges.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The record does not fully establish when the university obtained the relevant information, why an earlier response opportunity was impracticable, or whether the later process could correct the ongoing deprivation. Those matters belong in the district court under the correct legal separation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not require a full evidentiary trial before provisional action and does not decide the validity of the ultimate demotion.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Section 1983 does not require a state interlocutory appeal

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the procedural preemption question because it is the only issue before the Court.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does 42 U.S.C. § 1983, the Supremacy Clause, or federal qualified-immunity doctrine require a State to provide an interlocutory appeal from denial of qualified immunity, notwithstanding a neutral state final-judgment rule?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply federalism and source separation. Preserve the substantive defense while declining to transfer § 1291 into state procedure.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Neither § 1983 nor federal qualified-immunity doctrine requires a State to provide an interlocutory appeal from denial of immunity when the State applies a neutral final-judgment rule and affords meaningful adjudication and review of the defense.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mitchell’s federal appeal rests on the collateral-order construction of Congress’s federal appellate statute. Idaho is not administering § 1291. The officials remain free to assert immunity and obtain review; they face a different timing rule, not denial of the defense. Imposing federal procedure without enacted authority would displace state judicial administration unnecessarily.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves a rule discriminating against federal defenses, denying meaningful review, or compelling a state court to disregard the immunity’s substantive protection.
