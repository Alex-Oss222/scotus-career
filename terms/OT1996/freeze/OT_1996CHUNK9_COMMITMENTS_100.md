## Common legal commitments

**A. Reach and factual posture.** The timely held petition and October 7, 1996 grant are authorized procedural premises, not historical orders. The present collateral order concerns whether the alleged invitation and associated intrusion are legally actionable despite immunity. Accept that Mottola admitted the crew to observe and record the family and private materials without search assistance, investigative documentation, consent or another legitimate execution function. Whether he actually did so remains for proof. The Court must neither decide those facts against him nor replace the allegations with a benign assistance account.

**B. Fourth Amendment rule.** Officers executing a valid home-search warrant may not use that authority to admit private observers whose presence and recording serve no legitimate search or other law-enforcement function. A warrant for specified evidence does not surrender the occupants' domestic privacy to unrelated spectators; the added government-arranged intrusion must be justified on its own actual relation to lawful execution. The crew's recording of the family and private papers over objection intensifies that invasion even though the footage was never broadcast. Bivens's warrant-bounds discussion, Payton's home protection and Andresen's requirement to minimize unwarranted documentary intrusions support this conclusion. Current Wilson and Richards confirm that a valid warrant does not settle every manner-of-execution question.

This is an objective connection-to-authorized-conduct rule, not a motive-only invalidation of an otherwise lawful search. It does not prohibit genuine requested assistance, necessary investigative documentation or entry under valid occupant consent. It decides no media entitlement outside the home. Section 3105 permits service by the named or legally authorized officers and aid requested by the officer while the officer is present and executing the warrant. That assistance exception is material; the statute does not independently create the constitutional damages claim or convert every statutory violation into a Fourth Amendment violation. The holding does not require classifying all video or sound copying as a separate seizure.

**C. Immunity standard.** The question is whether pre-March 5, 1992 law made this additional nonassisting media intrusion apparently unlawful to a reasonable officer, not simply whether home privacy was broadly protected or whether the Court now rejects the practice. Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635, 639–641 (1987), permits reasonably mistaken constitutional judgments while rejecting an indispensable identical-facts requirement. Davis v. Scherer prevents an unrelated statutory violation from defeating immunity; section 3105 may inform the lawful-assistance context but cannot substitute for clear constitutional notice. Bivens, Payton, Andresen and the pre-search circuit execution/assistance cases must be considered at their actual scope. Bills v. Aseltine and every later authority identified above are excluded as notice sources.

**D. Remedy limits.** Denial-of-immunity affirmance permits continuation of the properly scoped individual-officer claim with proof of responsibility, causation and injury; it is not a liability or damages judgment. Immunity reversal would require dismissal of this reviewed claim against Mottola without validating the practice, extinguishing unrelated claims or adjudicating another party's liability. Either path leaves the unappealed and excluded matters untouched: other force, detention, pre-warrant entry and photograph issues, CBS's separate position and settlement, unserved agents and the criminal footage proceeding. No broadcast or broadcast damages are invented.

## Competing sufficient applications and opinion relationships

**Clear-notice application — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.** The particular protected interest is exclusion of government-arranged, nonassisting private observers and their unrelated recording from the interior of the home during an evidentiary search. Bivens, 403 U.S. 388, 394 n.7, confines execution to the warrant's bounds; Payton, 445 U.S. 573, 585–590, gives the home special protection; Andresen, 427 U.S. 463, 482 n.11, preserves privacy limits even when officers must examine papers. Matias, 836 F.2d 744, 747, applies the documentary-intrusion discipline; Rivera, 928 F.2d 592, 606–607, denies that a search warrant automatically authorizes additional intrusive treatment. Clouston, 623 F.2d 485, 486–487, Wright, 667 F.2d 793, 797, and the cited genuine-assistance decisions explain why aiding participants differ. Their combined restriction is sufficiently concrete on the assumed purposeless intrusion. The absence of a case about television does not create a reasonable execution justification. This is an Anderson apparent-unlawfulness application, not a rule that all unlawful home-search conduct loses immunity.

**Insufficient-notice application — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.** The same sources support the present merits conclusion, but the constitutional step from authorized search plus third-party observation to additional unreasonable invasion was not sufficiently determined before March 5, 1992. Payton and Bivens chiefly concern official entry and warrant boundaries; Andresen and Matias concern documentary searching; Rivera concerns intrusive official treatment; the assistance cases uphold genuine aid. None expressly analyzes unrelated media observation during a valid home search. Absence of an exact precedent is not dispositive by itself; here the differing legal contexts leave a reasonable interpretive question about the incremental intrusion. Section 3105's clearer service/aid boundary does not automatically resolve constitutional damages notice under Davis. The four grant immunity on that limited gap, while agreeing that the Court should now reject the alleged conduct.

Each coalition's merits agreement is direct and can support a common constitutional portion. The two immunity applications are incompatible; neither is a narrower logical subset of the other. They must not be combined through Marks or an undifferentiated judgment roster. The immunity disagreement merits a substantive separate-writing explanation in whatever final topology assembly lawfully produces. Authorship and final assignment remain unselected.
