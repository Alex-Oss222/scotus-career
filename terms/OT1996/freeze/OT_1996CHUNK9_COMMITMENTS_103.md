## Presented facts and distinct components

The Fund operates two separately incorporated, licensed New York diagnostic and treatment centers and a New Jersey center. The New York centers provide services principally to covered workers, retirees and dependents, with additional outside-employer and PPGU arrangements described by the State's record account. The Fund is a self-insured multiemployer welfare plan, not thereby a federal sovereign or federal regulatory agency.

The HFA is a revenue assessment on licensed health-facility operations. The reviewed diagnostic-center rate is 0.6 percent of assessable gross cash receipts from patient care and other operations. Receipts reach the general fund. The centers reported $1,177,670 in assessable receipts for January through November 1991 and paid $7,066.02 before ceasing reports and payments. Neither figure is a final present assessment or an adjudicated refund. Transfers from the Fund to its centers were excluded as grants, and the State did not contest that exclusion. Other receipts include direct outside-employer payments, PPGU and workers' compensation payments, registration fees and copayments, and miscellaneous revenue. The Fund does not challenge the tax on unrelated miscellaneous receipts.

The April 16, 1992 complaint requests prospective declaration and injunction and repayment. The District Court identified but expressly left undecided the state-remedy dispute under the Tax Injunction Act. It also left the separate Eleventh Amendment objection to repayment undecided. The Court of Appeals reversed on ERISA and adhered after reconsideration. Neither court's merits discussion establishes an adjudicated refund entitlement. State silence in later appellate briefing does not waive section 1341 or prove adequate or inadequate state relief.

The components are **A**, the statutory tax-jurisdiction framework and absence of an automatic ERISA exception; **B**, adequacy of the actual New York route for this prospective preemption challenge; **C**, the jurisdictional disposition and separate treatment of repayment; and **D**, the ERISA merits commitment if a lawful federal vehicle remains after the distinct jurisdictional issues. D is recorded for reconciliation and compatibility only; it is not an alternative merits holding of a court dismissing for lack of jurisdiction.

## A and B — threshold grounds adopted by all eight

**A:** The HFA is a state tax for the present section 1341 inquiry: its gross-receipts assessment raises public revenue rather than imposing a regulatory sanction adjudicated against a particular violator. The Court need not adopt the lower court's broad suggestion that every revenue collection device is necessarily a tax. Section 1341 bars federal prospective restraint where a plain, speedy and efficient state remedy may be had. ERISA's federal fiduciary cause of action does not repeal that restriction; section 1144(d) expressly preserves other federal law. Nor does federal regulation of a plan make this private Fund the United States for purposes of the sovereign exception. The holding does not decide the position of a genuine federal agency or a different express statute.

**B:** New York's independent declaratory-judgment route affords a sufficiently plain and adequate opportunity to obtain adjudication of this federal preemption objection. The District Court itself identified that affirmative route. *Tully* establishes the operative New York practice of entertaining constitutional challenges to taxation through CPLR 3001 notwithstanding tax-review exclusivity language, with ordinary preliminary relief available in a proper case. *Morgan Guaranty* demonstrates that New York's courts actually adjudicate an ERISA preemption objection to a tax. Its different refund procedure is not treated as the HFA's procedure. Together these authorities answer the presented categorical objection that ERISA prevents the state court from considering the federal question at all. Section 1132(e)(1) reserves specified ERISA civil actions to federal courts; it does not withdraw the state court's authority to apply supreme federal law when deciding a different state tax action. The adequate state cause of action need not duplicate section 1132(a)(3).

This is a legal adequacy determination on the demonstrated declaratory route, not a presumption based on the Fund's failure to produce an exhibit or a guarantee of the Fund's substantive success. No special HFA barrier to that declaratory route is established in the presented dispute, and none is invented. The State need not concede that the tax is unlawful or promise a preliminary injunction for the route to be available. Conversely, a merely hypothetical or inaccessible remedy would not suffice. *Reich* forbids a State to hold out a postpayment remedy and then withdraw it when invoked; *National Private Truck Council* depends on an actually adequate channel. Neither decision permits substitution of a label for a real hearing. Their limit is satisfied by the identified existing state adjudicative route for prospective relief, not by importing Arkansas procedure or the amount paid in another case.

The strongest contrary authority is the Second Circuit's jurisdictional analysis in lower *Travelers*, reinforced by the Fund's direct section 1132(a)(3) claim. Its inference conflates exclusivity over that federal cause of action with exclusive authority over every federal preemption issue. It does not establish that New York must refuse its own declaratory action. The earlier Supreme Court *Travelers* merits holding did not adopt that jurisdictional analysis or create an ERISA exception to section 1341. The Court's present interpretation gives effect to both section 1132 and section 1144(d), while leaving the federal question fully available in state adjudication and ordinary review.

## C — actual disposition and refund qualification, all eight

Vacate the Second Circuit's ERISA merits judgment and remand with instructions that the federal prospective declaratory and injunctive claims be dismissed under section 1341. No federal merits judgment invalidating the HFA survives this disposition. The District Court's nonpreemption judgment is not simply reinstated, because a merits judgment cannot substitute for the required jurisdictional disposition.

The repayment request requires separate treatment. The State's Eleventh Amendment defense was not adjudicated. The Court does not declare it waived, conclusively sustained, or irrelevant; it does not turn permission to sue an officer prospectively into authority to recover paid taxes from the State. On remand the remaining repayment component must receive its own threshold assessment, including the preserved sovereign-immunity objection, the actual statutory remedial basis and any applicable tax-jurisdiction or comity limit, before any federal merits adjudication. This limited direction does not announce a general rule about whether section 1341 itself covers every refund suit. The state prospective route's adequacy is not a judicial finding that a particular HFA refund application already satisfies all state procedural requirements.

No refund, new assessment, penalty, reporting deadline, compulsory hearing or tax-collection order issues. No state filing is presumed or ordered, and no tolling promise from *Tully* is reproduced as a present order. Ordinary proceedings address the remaining properly presented threshold questions. A court that determines that no federal repayment vehicle exists must dismiss that component without reaching preemption. D below describes the Associates' merits view only if a lawful federal vehicle survives; it is not permission to assume one.

## D — conditional ERISA merits commitments, all eight

If merits are lawfully reached, all eight would reject this preemption challenge and reverse the Second Circuit's contrary merits conclusion at the following scope. This position is not an operative holding accompanying C.

The assessment operates on licensed medical-provider activity and assessable receipts independently of ERISA status. ERISA coverage neither triggers the tax nor supplies a mandatory benefit level, eligibility criterion or legal measure of a plan obligation. A plan's choice to provide care through its own centers does not convert every ordinary provider cost into regulation of the plan as a plan. *Travelers* preserves ordinary economic influence while leaving direct self-funded applications open; its reservation is addressed rather than treated as an earlier holding against the Fund. *Dillingham* supplies the current inquiry into actual reference or connection, and rejects the inference that an independently operating criterion refers to ERISA merely because a covered entity encounters it. *Board of Trade* involved a state-required covered-plan benefit benchmark, which is absent here. *Inter-Modal* preserves independently enacted ERISA protections; it supplies no tax exemption or special rule for self-provided welfare services.

The Fund's strongest objection is that the tax falls on its very delivery of benefits, reduces money for promised services and induces different fund-routing and accounting choices across state lines. Those effects are real economic consequences, but the presented law does not dictate the terms of benefits, eligibility, covered beneficiaries, funding promises, fiduciary standards or the administrator's adjudication of claims. Tax reporting and a choice to route otherwise exempt grants are not themselves legal commands to redesign plan entitlements. The grant exclusion is an actual accepted application here, not a finding that every receipt could be recharacterized to avoid tax. Direct incidence and reduction in available assets do not alone distinguish the tax from ordinary costs that a plan incurs when it operates a medical facility.

Neither general applicability nor the 0.6 percent rate is a categorical safe harbor. A discriminatory reference, mandatory covered-plan benchmark, competing benefit-enforcement system or sufficiently acute economic coercion that effectively dictates choices could present a different question. This record establishes no such operation. The Court would not require the Fund to prove insolvency or pretend that any financial burden is harmless; it would require the asserted connection to be one ERISA protects against. Section 1144(b)(5)(B)(i)'s reservation concerning tax laws in the special Hawaii provision preserves application of the ordinary preemption rule to taxes; it does not preempt all taxes touching plans. The insurance saving and deemer clauses do not change this provider-tax inquiry or turn self-insured status into general immunity.

The exact state statutory qualifications remain material. Section 2801's definition and exclusions, section 2807-d(1)(a)'s personal-needs and refund deductions and paragraph-(b) exclusion, and section 2807-d(2)(c)'s specified diagnostic-center rate are not collapsed into a tax on every dollar. The appendix does not reproduce paragraph (b) or every intervening subdivision, so no complete current-liability formula is adjudicated. The administrative instructions separately exclude the specified restricted-investment income, charity/bad-debt and county grants, grants/donations/bequests/deficit financing, sales and excise taxes, identified HMO subscriber receipts and qualifying distinct-rate Medicaid family-planning receipts. They remain instructions with their stated qualifications, not newly enacted exceptions. Conditional nonpreemption decides neither an individual receipt's classification nor liability outside the challenged operations.

## Alternative dispositions and common envelope

The eight Associates reject an automatic ERISA exception, waiver-based access and an adequacy finding based only on the name of a state proceeding. They adopt the narrower demonstrated declaratory-route ground for A/B/C. If a legally supported HFA-specific obstruction to that route were established, jurisdiction would require reassessment; that is a limit on the rule, not a finding that such an obstruction exists. A remand for the prospective-remedy question alone is unnecessary once the legal exclusivity objection is answered on these authorities. The separate repayment threshold remains genuinely unresolved and is remanded without a merits assumption.

All eight support the same actual A/B/C disposition: vacate the merits judgment; dismiss prospective federal relief under section 1341; address repayment's distinct threshold defenses before any federal merits ruling. All eight conditionally support D if lawful merits adjudication remains. None joins an opinion publishing D as an independently sufficient alternative holding after dismissing the same claims for lack of jurisdiction. There is no judgment-only fracture, proposed separate writing or author assignment at this stage. A later opinion must preserve that distinction and cannot portray the eight conditional commitments as eight votes for an actual merits judgment that C does not permit.
