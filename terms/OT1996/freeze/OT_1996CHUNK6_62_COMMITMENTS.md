## Common provisional judgment and propositions

Each of Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment insofar as it invalidates the statute because subsection (5)(b) asks whether parental notification is in the petitioner's best interests rather than employing the precise abortion-best-interests formula, and remand for proceedings consistent with that limited determination. None commits to a judgment approving every provision or implementation of Montana's law. The mandate removes this textual ground for facial relief; it does not decide an unreviewed injunction ground or order enforcement notwithstanding an independent surviving ground.

**P1 — textual adequacy at the granted scope.** A notice bypass is not constitutionally deficient simply because its independent best-interests ground is expressed as waiver when notifying a parent is not in the petitioner's best interests. Montana's provision permits assessment of the consequences of notification for the minor, including effects on obtaining the abortion; it does not direct the judge to disregard those consequences or require parental approval after waiver. The maturity and best-interests routes are alternatives. The wording therefore does not itself establish the denial of independent authorization condemned in Miller.

**P2 — binding limitation.** This conclusion rests on the actual statutory route and the particular textual objection, not a rule that any bypass cures every notice burden or that all notification is less than meaningful interference. Casey/Fargo/Miller remain governing law. An actual construction or operation producing parental practical control, a substantial obstacle, or unjustified remaining interference may be challenged under their complete standard. This decision supplies no general Salerno barrier and does not adjudicate independent timing, evidentiary, appeal, abuse-investigation, emergency, adult-incapacity or penalty objections.

**P3 — remedy.** Reverse the best-interests-wording ground and return the case, with relief conformed to the questions actually resolved. No new judicial bypass, new proof burden, compulsory visit, appellate timetable, production order or evidentiary trial is ordered.

All eight Associates join P1–P3 at those precise levels. Scalia and Thomas do not by these joins affirmatively endorse the entire Casey six-step rationale; Kennedy does not thereby join that full framework. The proposition that the framework is binding law, and that this case does not reopen it, is compatible with their narrower joins.

## Material alternatives and statutory qualifications

The providers' strongest objection is that an immature minor could establish that abortion serves her interests while a court regards notification as beneficial in isolation. If the statute required the latter isolated inquiry, or denied waiver despite supported obstruction of protected care, the independent authorization route might be ineffective. The actual wording contains no command to exclude the consequences of notification for access or welfare, and the prior Ohio treatment permits a notice-focused formulation. Facial invalidation solely from the different verbal formulation therefore assumes a restrictive operation the text does not command. This does not shift the Casey burden by demanding completed lost access: reliable prospective evidence or an authoritative restrictive construction could establish a different claim.

A general remand to rebuild a burden record is the narrower competing remedial path. It is not needed to answer the granted textual distinction. Fargo involved additional access duties requiring supported application of the whole standard; Miller involved demonstrated compulsory operation and no usable independent maturity/best-interests route. Neither requires a new record whenever a notice statute exists. Conversely, the State's broadest path—complete facial approval of all provisions merely because a bypass exists—exceeds this question and these commitments.

The following are statutory facts, not a ruling that every safeguard is indispensable: confidentiality and sealed records; counsel on request; preference for prompt consideration; written specific findings; maturity and the two subsection (5) alternatives; and no fee for a pregnant minor's waiver petition or appeal. The forty-eight-hour ruling deadline may be extended at the petitioner's request, and automatic grant applies if the court fails to rule within forty-eight hours **and time has not been extended**. The abuse ground concerns evidence of a pattern of physical, sexual or emotional abuse and is independent of best interests; alleging abuse also activates the separate reporting provision. Subsection (8) permits state supreme court rules for expedited confidential appeal and does not establish a particular appeal timetable. An authorization order is unappealable. No conclusion here validates those distinct details against an unpresented claim.
