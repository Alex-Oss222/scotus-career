**Principal injury rule and limits.** Individual Members whose votes were counted and given full effect lack standing here to litigate the abstract dilution of Congress's institutional lawmaking power. Their statutory permission to sue removes applicable prudential barriers but cannot supply the constitutionally required concrete personal injury. Powell concerns personal exclusion; Coleman concerns complete nullification of votes sufficient to determine a particular result. Neither describes this suit. Akins prevents treating numerical sharing alone as disqualifying; these plaintiffs lack its actual denial of information useful to their own participation. The absence of House authorization, retained legislative remedies and availability of a later concrete cancellation claim reinforce the limited conclusion; they are not a legislative-exhaustion requirement or a universal bar to official injury. No holding decides an authorized institutional plaintiff, actual exclusion, an outcome-determinative nullified vote, or a later beneficiary's standing. Traceability concerns may be noted as additional uncertainty, but the source does not support converting its historical footnote into an independent fully developed holding.

**Souter's substantive separate ground, joined by Ginsburg.** Official injury is not categorically outside Article III: Coleman and authorized governmental representation prevent that shortcut. The Members' interest in their legislative power is more direct than an ordinary citizen's demand for constitutional government, though broader and less specific than decisive-vote nullification. In this setting, Allen's separation-of-powers foundation and Valley Forge's last-resort principle counsel against judicial entry into an internal and interbranch contest. A beneficiary deprived of an actual spending or tax benefit can present a traditionally concrete controversy, with the usual injury, causation and redressability predicates. That available route supports restraint here; it is not a universal requirement to find another plaintiff, a political-question ruling on all Article I controversies, or an adjudication that any future plaintiff necessarily prevails. His conclusion remains Article III insufficiency under these conditions, not merely prudential abstention.

**Stevens's substantive dissent.** A Member's opportunity to vote on the law's ultimate text is an individual constitutional function. On his view, the Act alters that function immediately by authorizing a truncated law without a congressional vote on the truncation; a hypothetical later cancellation is unnecessary to the asserted present injury. Bender does not permit representation without authorization, but this complaint asserts the Member's own voting role. On the merits, a signed bill first becomes law; subsequent cancellation removes enacted legal force. Chadha's bicameralism and presentment requirements and Stevens's allocation-of-function precedents cannot be avoided by labeling that repeal execution or by providing broad intelligible standards. Field's contingent execution and ordinary impoundment do not answer all three statutory cancellation categories, especially removal of direct-spending obligations and tax provisions. This is his sufficient Article I ground, not an additional holding of insufficiently intelligible delegation standards, a presidential secret-reservation theory, or a conclusion about a future cancellation's remedy.

**Breyer's substantive dissent.** Statutory authorization does not enlarge Article III, but removes the applicable prudential obstacle and reduces interbranch-conflict concerns. The alleged systematic impairment of an existing constitutional function is concrete and focused, and the adverse parties present familiar constitutional questions. Coleman rejected an absolute official-injury exclusion; its one legislative event does not justify rejecting an allegedly continuing impairment of many votes. The possibility of a later private plaintiff does not erase an immediate injury if it exists. He therefore would reach the merits, while expressly declining to decide them in a dissent from the Court's jurisdictional disposition. The complete Raines merits supplement eliminates a research-gap rationale for that reservation; it does not force him to resolve a question unnecessary to his published disagreement.

**Statutory and remedial precision.** Assembly must preserve the enacted Act and the neutral merits supplement's qualifications whenever the dissent discusses them: permission rather than a duty to cancel; cancellation in whole of each defined statutory category; deficit, essential-function and national-interest conditions; the five-calendar-day period excluding Sundays; message requirements; the disapproval-bill exception; receipt by both Houses and restoration upon enacted disapproval; thirty calendar days **of session** under the enacted rules; the distinct definitions, thresholds, exclusions and identification restrictions; and the sunset. No actual cancellation is found. The majority vacates the single district judge's declaration and directs jurisdictional dismissal; it does not decide ripeness independently, approve the Act or invalidate it. The special statutory direct appeal is not a three-judge-court appeal.

### Controlling-ground content available for assembly

The CDA selects speech because of its content and reaches substantial protected adult expression. The compelling purpose of protecting minors does not establish the necessity or fit of the means. Sable supplies that distinction and the rule against reducing adults to material suitable for children. Ginsberg addressed a defined commercial sale to minors under variable-obscenity limits, including the work's value to minors; it does not validate these broader definitions. Pacifica's broadcast context, scarcity and intrusive reception do not describe this Internet record. Actual ACT/Pacifica already requires independent review even within broadcasting. Turner concerns content-neutral cable carriage and preserves classification and independent evidentiary review. Actual Denver's seven-Justice §10(b) rule supplies an analogy about compulsory adult-access burdens; its fractured permission judgments establish no universal cable rule and no Internet classification.

The uncertainty of “indecent” and the contextual patently-offensive standard, backed by criminal sanctions, chills protected discussion and enlarges the overbreadth problem. The Court need not decide whether the provisions independently violate Fifth Amendment vagueness requirements. The knowledge and specified-person wording do not prevent liability in mixed adult/minor communication. A serious-value or harmful-to-minors exception cannot be inserted into text that lacks it. The defenses do not cure the demonstrated burdens: practicable verification is unavailable or costly for many small and noncommercial speakers, and proposed tagging does not establish the required effective protection under the then-existing record. This is not a finding that every future age screen is unconstitutional or that all control technology is ineffective.

The complete offense and defense terms in the frozen neutral appendix remain operative descriptions and must not be collapsed. R1's telecommunications-device language, including the enacted exclusion of an interactive computer service from that definition, is distinct from R2/R3's interactive-computer-service language. Facility offenses require knowing permission **and** intent that the facility be used for prohibited activity. Section (e)(1) protects mere access/connection without content creation under its control limitation. Section (e)(2) withdraws that defense from a person who is a conspirator **with an entity actively involved in creation or knowing distribution**, or who knowingly advertises the prohibited communications; it does not require the conspirator personally to create the content. Section (e)(3) separately concerns ownership/control of the offending network. Section (e)(4) retains the within-scope conduct and authorization/ratification or reckless-disregard conditions for an employer. Section (e)(5)(A)'s good-faith reasonable, effective and appropriate measures and (B)'s verified-card/account/adult-code/PIN access restriction are **alternative** defenses. The FCC **may** describe measures under (e)(6); it cannot compel, approve, enforce or endorse them, and their use is evidence of good faith rather than a licensing scheme. The distinct savings provisions do not supply a missing narrow content definition.

The majority rejects the requested judicial narrowing because the enacted offenses and defended construction suppress a substantial protected range and no readily available textual construction supplies the proposed new offense. Severance of the discrete words “or indecent” preserves the obscenity portion of (a); a general severability provision does not authorize rewriting the remaining coverage or inventing an effective verification regime. The decision leaves open the broader proposition that protected Internet indecency could never be regulated; it does not decide the proposed blanket prohibition of all such messages to seventeen-year-olds or the role of parental approval, and does not decide the separate extraterritorial efficacy/scope question. Those reservations must not be replaced by O'Connor's contrary minors-overbreadth conclusion.

### O'Connor's separate ground and precise disagreement

O'Connor accepts protection of adults as a limit on any Internet adult-zone approach. Butler, Sable and Bolger prohibit suppressing adult access to protect children; Ginsberg permits genuine age-confined restrictions. The present Internet lacks generally available identity gates, and mixed conversations cannot reliably exclude minors. The display offense therefore forces silence or children's-level expression, and the transmission offenses cannot be enforced against conversations involving another adult merely because a known minor participates. She agrees with that relief.

She would construe (d)(1)(A) to require the sender's knowledge of the recipient's minority, consistently with its wording and constitutional avoidance under DeBartolo. Where the initiating adult knows every recipient is a minor, there is no other adult audience whose access is impaired. Brockett's application severability and the Act's severability provision support retaining that confined application. She recognizes that CDA indecency is broader than Ginsberg's obscenity-as-to-minors category, but finds the challengers have not shown that the additional protected minor speech is **substantial** relative to the legitimate sweep under Broadrick. This is a distinct substantive reason for her narrower remedy, not a holding that minors have no protected speech or that the statute can survive any individual vagueness challenge. Her framework evaluates technology as it exists in 1997; future improvement is not a factual premise curing current burdens. No other Associate joins this writing after reconciliation.

### Principal ground and limits

No suspect classification or established fundamental entitlement to the requested intentional assistance triggers heightened equality review of this claim. That conclusion is reached independently from the existing law and claim; it cannot be borrowed from the same-day Glucksberg decision. Cruzan's protection against unwanted bodily intervention does not itself establish the requested assistance entitlement, and actual Casey has its own reproductive scope.

The State allows competent patients to refuse treatment and prohibits intentional assistance in suicide. The practical difference for terminally ill patients who do or do not depend on life-sustaining treatment must still be assessed; identical wording is not the end of equal protection. Bodily consent, legal causation and purpose explain a reasonable general line. Withdrawal respects a refusal of intervention and ordinarily leaves the underlying disease to cause death; intentional lethal assistance supplies an additional means intended to cause death. Good-faith pain treatment may foresee a life-shortening effect without having that as its prohibited purpose. The State may reasonably protect life, guard against mistake, pressure and abuse, and preserve professional responsibility while respecting refusal. It need not eliminate the refusal protection to equalize all consequences, nor extend it to every different act that may hasten death.

The line need not describe every patient's or physician's actual intent in every case. Foreseeable death is not automatically purpose to kill, and neither a patient's wish nor the label “withdrawal” establishes all relevant intent. Legal uncertainty at a boundary does not itself defeat a reasonable general distinction. Actual Beach permits relevant generalization; actual Heller and Romer still require connection to the real disadvantage and preserve Moreno/Cleburne's limit. No finding of hostility is an indispensable prerequisite to review. The Court does not apply the historical Heller result, presume every assisted-death request involuntary, treat a less restrictive alternative as automatically required by ordinary review, or foreclose a materially different individual challenge.

### Substantive separate positions

**Stevens.** The State has a sufficient basis for the general distinction, but not an invariably true physiological or psychological taxonomy. A patient withdrawing support and a patient requesting medication can share a wish to hasten impending death; a physician's purpose can likewise concern suffering in both settings. Terminal sedation further complicates a categorical intent/causation account. His Cruzan dissent grounds the importance of individual dignity and the manner of impending death. Actual Casey supplies a supported analogy concerning defined personal choice, real burdens and clinical circumstances, not the historical opinion's unbounded autonomy language and not a newly controlling assisted-death right. Protective interests justify a general rule without necessarily resolving the exceptional competent patient's case. His writing reserves such a question; it does not find a particular application unconstitutional or require a hearing.

**O'Connor, with Ginsburg's substantial adoption and Breyer's qualified join.** There is no general entitlement to suicide, but the narrower interest of a competent person enduring great suffering near imminent death need not be decided here. New York legally permits qualified physicians to alleviate that suffering, including medication causing unconsciousness and potentially hastening death. Even assuming a cognizable narrower interest, safeguards against mistaken judgments of competence, terminal status and voluntariness justify the prohibition presented. Her Cruzan concurrence permits States to develop safeguards in the first instance. This is neither a finding that pain relief is universally delivered or successful, nor a rule making legislative consideration an exhaustion requirement, nor a new pain-treatment test. Ginsburg adopts substantially these reasons and nothing broader by inference. Breyer's qualified join excludes the principal-opinion joins.

**Souter.** The physicians' and patients' interest is important enough to demand commensurate justification even without recognizing a fundamental assistance right. Harlan's Poe approach, bodily integrity and the actual reproductive and privacy precedents require more than an unexplained label or abstract disapproval. Protection against mistaken or involuntary suicide and euthanasia supplies the decisive explanation: whether safeguards can reliably preserve responsible voluntary choice and contain the practice presents a serious unresolved empirical problem. Palliation and refusal differ in purpose, bodily intrusion and the likelihood of implementing a responsible choice, even though each may hasten death. Those reasons support the permitted/prohibited line. His reasoning is independently adopted for this case; a cross-reference to a contemporaneous separate writing may avoid repetition but cannot make that writing intervening precedent. He publishes his separate sufficient ground instead of silently joining a lower level of review.

**Breyer.** He agrees that the identified interests justify the distinction, while describing the potential liberty more carefully: personal control over the manner of death, professional medical assistance and avoidance of unnecessary severe physical suffering together. He does not decide whether that combined interest is fundamental. Its central suffering concern is not directly prohibited by the state law, which permits sufficient pain medication despite foreseeable death. Failures in delivery or the possibility of sedation do not establish a legal ban, and he does not assert that every person's suffering can be relieved. A law actually forbidding necessary palliation would present a different question. His qualified O'Connor join and his own explanation do not join a categorical denial of all end-of-life liberty.

**M2 — Direct protection of these interstate payment obligations.** All eight Associates sustain the statute against the presented Commerce Clause objection and would affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of each separate district-court indictment dismissal, preserving the trial remand in each prosecution. Their ground does not depend on AFDC effects, moral disapproval, or the fact that all conduct might influence the economy. The relevant distinction is the obligation's legally required interstate satisfaction, as presented in these charged different-state relationships. Their ruling is confined to that class and retains every statutory element. No separate broad effects rationale is added as an independently sufficient holding.

**Saving construction through travel, evasion or payment method.** A narrower offense requiring flight, deliberate State crossing, or an actual interstate instrument might present an additional connection. But those conditions are not enacted and the record establishes none. Lopez's no-rewriting rule forecloses supplying them as the basis for this judgment. The enacted willfulness condition remains real; it is not replaced with a finding of interstate evasion.

**A categorical family-law exclusion or an automatic Printz analogy.** Ankenbrandt and New York support distinguishing decrees and State administration, not forbidding all direct federal regulation affecting families. Printz does not hold that every valid federal crime may recruit State officers, and these prosecutions require no such conclusion. Neither a categorical exception nor a fiscal remand follows.

**An immediate guilt or restitution disposition.** The statutory threshold and constitutional power do not establish the charged facts. A decision sustaining the statute therefore returns each existing prosecution rather than entering convictions or calculating arrears. The offense-date limit and older-arrears restitution question remain distinct. These narrower limits are essential to the common join, not optional observations.

## 3. In re Cohen

**Identity and posture:** Review under the approved §1254(1) appellate route of the Third Circuit's February 6, 1997 judgment, 106 F.3d 52, lower appellate No. 96-5155. No separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied. The existing bankruptcy judgment includes $94,147.50 in treble damages based on $31,382.50 in fraudulent overcharges and an existing award of attorney's fees and costs whose separate amount is not supplied. The Court is reviewing that adjudicated liability and its discharge status, not assessing new damages.

### Reconciled components and sufficient grounds

**Statutory frame retained.** Section 523(a)(2) concerns a debt for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal, or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by the conduct specified in its subparagraphs. Subparagraph (A) identifies false pretenses, a false representation, or actual fraud, other than a statement respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition. The actual-fraud claim and fraudulently acquired rents presented here supply the application; no independent rule for every other acquisition or representation theory is decided. The separately stated subsection (B) conditions remain intact as specified in C1.

**C1 — Federal fraud remains a necessary predicate.** All eight apply Field v. Mans, November 28, 1995. The actual-fraud theory requires actual and justifiable reliance under the ordinary preponderance burden. Justifiability depends on the recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances. No universal duty to investigate the rent ordinance applies; personally known or obvious falsity and actually encountered warning signs remain relevant. Objective imprudence may bear on whether reliance occurred, but does not become a universal prudent-person condition. A State consumer-fraud label or statutory enhancement alone establishes none of these federal elements.

The supplied record contains federal fraud findings upheld on ordinary factual/legal review, including deliberate avoidance of the rent limits and selective knowledge of favorable provisions. Those findings are not converted into new Supreme Court factual findings. Limited tenant knowledge of the ordinance does not by itself defeat justifiable reliance. The reviewed debt-extent question may be decided on the sustained federal fraud predicate; the packet identifies no supported, material Field error requiring a new factual proceeding. The underlying findings remain subject to the ordinary scope of review and preservation, rather than being declared immune because a State statute supplied damages. No trial fact, warning, investigation, representation or missing finding is invented, and no exploratory fraud remand is ordered.

Field's scope is retained. Subsection 523(a)(2)(A) excludes a statement respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition. Subsection (B) separately requires a materially false writing of that kind, reasonable reliance, and the debtor's causing its making or publication with intent to deceive. Nothing supplied makes this a financial-statement case. The decision does not classify every possible false-pretense or representation-only claim, displace subsection (B), or presume actual fraud whenever money was retained improperly.

**C2 — The nondischargeable amount is the valid liability for the fraudulent acquisition.** All eight would hold that when money or property is obtained by the fraud within §523(a)(2)(A), the qualifying debt includes the legally enforceable liability arising from that fraudulent acquisition, rather than only the net value the debtor received. The Code defines debt through liability on a claim. The phrase concerning the extent obtained by fraud identifies the fraudulent acquisition giving rise to the debt and separates it from lawful acquisitions; it does not impose a restitution-only ceiling on the consequences of that acquisition.

This reading gives work to both debt and obtained by. There must be a qualifying acquisition of money or property and a sufficient connection between that acquisition and the liability declared nondischargeable. It does not make every obligation of a dishonest debtor nondischargeable. Here the identified overcharged rents, not all lawful rent or every landlord obligation, supply that acquisition. An enforceable enhancement imposed for those fraudulent receipts is a liability for them even though the debtor did not separately obtain the dollars represented by the enhancement. State law determines the valid liability; federal law determines whether it falls within this discharge category. The Court creates neither a new State cause nor a new federal damages remedy.

Field deliberately left debt extent and the obtained-by relationship separate. It therefore supplies the fraud prerequisite, not an already settled result here. Noland, May 13, 1996, requires observance of the Code's actual categories rather than a judicial preference about punitive obligations; it concerns priority and equitable subordination and does not itself settle §523(a)(2)(A). The present result rests on this exception's text and structure. Neither a generalized fresh start nor generalized hostility to fraud can replace that construction.

**C3 — Existing treble liability and conditional punitive characterization.** All eight would affirm nondischargeability of the existing $94,147.50 treble judgment for the $31,382.50 fraudulent acquisition. Even assuming for decision that the enhancement is wholly punitive, that characterization does not remove a valid liability for the qualifying fraud from §523(a)(2)(A). The Court does not find it wholly punitive as a matter of New Jersey law; the lower opinion used that assumption, while Cox v. Sears identified a partly compensatory purpose. No need to settle that State-law characterization arises because the federal result is the same under the stated assumption.

This is not a general nondischargeability rule for penalties. A penalty arising from unrelated conduct, a different statutory category, or an obligation lacking the qualifying fraudulent-acquisition nexus is outside this holding. Section 523(a)(4) and (6) cover distinct wrongs and need not use the same acquisition language; their broader formulations do not compel a cap on liability in (a)(2)(A) once its acquisition requirement is satisfied. The Court neither collapses those categories nor decides their independent elements.

**C4 — Existing attorney's fees and costs.** All eight would hold the existing valid State-law fee and cost obligations nondischargeable insofar as they are imposed because of, and form part of the liability for, this qualifying fraud. No separate fee or cost amount is supplied, and none is calculated or added. The Court does not award Supreme Court fees, new appellate fees, future litigation costs, interest, or an enlarged enhancement. Fees unrelated to the qualifying fraudulent acquisition are not covered merely because the same debtor owes them. A valid underlying obligation and its fraud connection remain necessary; a federal discharge ruling does not create State entitlement to fees.

**C5 — Judgment and scope.** Affirm the Third Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains the existing full fraud liability's nondischargeability, including the adjudged treble damages and existing fee/cost award at the scope of C1–C4. No new debt is assessed, the amount already obtained is not confused with the treble total, and no recalculation or additional hearing is ordered. The disposition leaves ordinary enforcement of the existing nondischargeable judgment to the proper proceedings; it does not set collection terms, distribution priority, a payment schedule or a new liability against another person.

### Competing constructions and supported alternatives

**Acquired-value ceiling.** The strongest rival concedes that the modifier concerning what was obtained attaches to the acquired money/property rather than directly to the word debt, yet argues that the resulting exception still reaches only an obligation representing what the debtor actually obtained. An enhancement or litigation expense was not received by the debtor; the acquisition words must impose a limit different from the broader debt-for-wrong language in §523(a)(4) and (6). Fresh-start policy favors confining an exception where the text reasonably permits it. This is the Greenberg construction preserved in the neutral packet, not a caricature based solely on a modifier's nearest noun. It would affirm nondischargeability of the qualifying acquired value and reverse the larger extent determination, with any necessary confined allocation of the existing award; it would not itself erase the underlying State liability.

The eight reject that construction because it turns the fraudulent-acquisition limitation into a second definition of debt as restitution, although debt remains the liability legally arising from the claim. The difference from (a)(4) and (6) is that (a)(2)(A) requires this kind of acquisition. A liability can be for that acquisition without each consequence being a second acquisition. The phrase concerning extent still prevents treatment of lawful rent or unrelated liabilities as fraud debt. The 1984 placement of the phrase supplies no clear direction to discharge the nonrestitution consequences of the very fraudulent acquisition Congress excepts; the conclusion does not depend on a policy-based expansion of every fraud-related claim. Legislative history may confirm that conclusion but is unnecessary to the common sufficient textual ground.

**Automatic punitive-debt exclusion.** Noland's insistence on enacted categories and the precise discharge text defeat a general judicial choice to discharge an enhancement simply because it is noncompensatory. That does not turn Noland into a holding on fraud discharge or decide the enhancement's State-law character. The assumed wholly punitive enhancement still belongs to the existing debt for the qualifying acquisition.

**A federal-fraud remand.** A supported, material and preserved reliance or other federal-fraud defect would require its ordinary disposition under Field. The supplied record does not establish such a defect. No Justice chooses a remand merely because more record material could theoretically exist. The affirmance relies on the existing sustained federal fraud predicate at the actual review scope, not a presumption that consumer-fraud trebling necessarily satisfies §523 or a finding that all possible factual objections were waived.

**An expansion to any consequence of any fraud.** The alternative at the other extreme would except all debts associated with the debtor's dishonesty, regardless of an acquisition within (a)(2)(A). The eight reject that expansion. The qualifying acquisition, valid liability, and causal/statutory nexus remain necessary. The financial-condition exclusion and subsection (B)'s distinct requirements are not bypassed; no claim outside the supplied fraud acquisition is decided.

## 3. Shared issue analysis and competing lawful paths

### A. Actual authority, managerial rank and the instruction

**Adopted path.** Andrade, February 26, 1997, supplies a plaintiff-proved connection between actual delegated power and the actionable environment. Oncale, March 19, preserves attribution separately from sex causation and the Harris threshold. Faragher Part I, September 8, applies that rule while preserving its limits. Each Associate joined all three relevant propositions; Faragher's eight-Associate application demonstrates that daily operational power can matter without a completed personnel act. It does not establish what happened in Harrison.

Here Brown's work assignment power is not simply a title-based inference. The lower opinion, Appendix A at 3a–6a and 27a, reports evidence of orders taking Harrison from her station or buddy into isolated areas, successive work-related excursions followed by alleged sexual misconduct, disciplinary participation and a work-through-lunch order following a refusal. This evidence permits a finding of material aid and requires a legally accurate opportunity to decide that route. It remains evidence, not a new Supreme Court finding that every incident occurred or every element was proved.

The principal attribution charge, Appendix A at 24a–27a, demanded all of its listed agency, apparent-authority and reliance elements. The accompanying definition required managerial control amounting to alter-ego identity. The apparent-authority charge, 28a–31a, again conditioned liability on authority to commit the challenged conduct. Together they foreclosed the separate route even if the jury believed that actual assignment power materially aided the abuse. The phrase addressing authority otherwise than apparently did not supply a material-aid instruction or remove the cumulative requirements.

**Strongest contrary path: preserve the charge.** Its reference to control over employment conditions could encompass a shift foreman; its reference to authority beyond apparent authority might be read broadly; refusing the employee's proposed automatic-supervisor instructions was proper. Those are genuine points against treating every disputed word as error. They do not cure the entire charge: managerial control was expressly tied to alter-ego identity, apparent authority and reliance remained mandatory, and the instructions never asked whether actual delegated power materially aided the environment. The record distinguishes refusal of an inaccurate proposed sentence from refusal of any accurate instruction on the supported theory. Requested instruction 7 contained both a potentially valid aid theory and an invalid status-alone theory; instruction 24 raised aid, while instruction 25 proposed status-based responsibility. No commitment requires the court to give any proposal verbatim.

**Other broader path: impose automatic responsibility from supervisory status.** Rejected by Meritor and the three current-term decisions. Removing an alter-ego prerequisite does not turn every supervisor into the employer for every personal act. Nor does Brown's conceded employment establish the material-aid connection. This path cannot support either a rule or a directed employer judgment.

### B. Whole-charge prejudice and apparent authority

**Adopted path.** The error was prejudicial on this record. The principal instruction was given over objection; the employee requested the omitted route; the appellate court expressly considered the charge as a whole and found the route viable on the evidence, Appendix A at 24a–34a. Daily-control evidence coupled with lack of top-management status made the incorrect barrier consequential. The work-order evidence gave the omitted route a concrete basis, independent of belief that harassment was permitted. No supplied special verdict establishes that the jury necessarily rejected an untainted element independently of attribution.

**Strongest contrary path: error but harmlessness.** A jury could have rejected an actionable environment or discriminatory causation, and the separate verdict against Brown does not establish those statutory elements against Potash. Some proposed instructions were overbroad, while the employer had a policy and responded after notice. Those propositions prevent automatic liability but do not show that this jury's employer verdict rested on an independent, correctly decided ground. A possible lawful explanation for a general verdict cannot cure a charge that excluded a supported liability route. Policy knowledge after some incidents and the later response likewise do not establish that actual authority supplied no material aid. The commitment is a record-specific finding of prejudicial instructional error, not a rule that every instruction error requires reversal or that prejudice is presumed whenever a general verdict is returned.

**Narrower lawful option.** Affirm on the independently sufficient actual-authority omission without deciding whether a correctly instructed jury could find apparent authority. Each Associate accepts that limit. Restatement §27, reproduced at 30a, supports the modest clarification that the employee's reasonable belief must arise from the principal's words or conduct. Faragher did not decide apparent authority; its separate observations cannot supply a Court holding. There is no need to adopt the lower opinion's entire apparent-authority framework, its characterization of apparent permission to harass, or its suggestion that unknown policy by itself supplies sufficient apparent authority. Nor is there a need to extend Faragher's actual-delegation holding to every claimed apparent work power.

### C. Prevention, reporting and response

**Adopted path.** Preserve the distinction among actual authority, apparent authority, negligence and remedy. A policy can affect reasonable belief or employer care when the required facts are present. Prompt investigation and correction can bear on response and relief; they do not retroactively remove earlier actual aid. The account reports that Harrison learned of a posted policy after some incidents and that it was absent from her new-hire materials. It does not establish universal ignorance, no available official, unreasonable nonuse, a particular policy promise, or the legal inadequacy of every later step. The employer's belief that Brown perceived consent does not turn its finding of unwilling participation into a judicial finding that conduct was welcome.

**Strongest contrary path: complete defense from policy and response.** Faragher expressly left open a possible defense involving genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse. Harrison's eventual knowledge and Potash's remedial measures distinguish its facts from Faragher's undistributed policy. That difference permits attention to the actual evidence in appropriate inquiries; it does not establish a complete defense, its governing elements or its prerequisites here. The charge was not an accurate, separately justified instruction on a recognized defense. The reservation neither expands this grant and preservation nor supplies an appellate finding against Harrison. All eight decline to adopt a new complete defense or reject every possible such defense in deciding this instructional appeal.

### D. Independent statutory rights and the actual contract record

**Adopted path.** Gardner-Denver, 415 U.S. 36 (1974), preserves the distinction between contractual grievance rights and the independent Title VII right. Gilmer, 500 U.S. 20 (1991), enforced an actual agreement to arbitrate the statutory ADEA claim and distinguished the earlier contractual-arbitration cases. First Options, May 22, 1995, requires courts to determine the antecedent agreement and delegation rather than presume them from an assertion of arbitral power. Its scope is not expanded into a new rule about union representation.

The decisive record limitation is a litigation fact reported in Appendix A at 35a n.1 and 40a n.2: Potash supplied neither the CBA nor the district court's order on exhaustion in the appellate record, and its appellate brief did not identify an agreement to arbitrate statutory claims. That prevents the employer from establishing the statutory obligation it needs for reversal. It is not a finding that no contract ever existed, a fabricated waiver, or an inference from the operator's inability to retrieve a document. Membership in a union and the asserted existence of a grievance procedure do not establish statutory scope or delegation. No disputed contractual interpretation is necessary to decide whether sex-based conduct and delegated work power meet Title VII's elements.

**Strongest contrary path: enforce the contractual procedure.** Gilmer rejects a categorical objection to arbitration of statutory employment claims, and a proven agreement can create enforceable duties without extinguishing substantive rights. A clause could, in another case, present the question of an express collective statutory-arbitration obligation. This path fails as a basis for Potash's requested dismissal because no such clause or delegation is established in the actual appellate record. It cannot be supplied by inventing terms or presuming that a generic grievance process governs statutory claims.

**Broader alternative and limits.** A categorical ruling that no CBA may ever commit statutory claims to arbitration would exceed what is necessary here. A categorical FAA exclusion for every employment or collective agreement likewise does not follow from Gilmer, which reserved the general employment-exclusion question. The lower court's broader rationale is not adopted. Livadas, June 13, 1994, and Hawaiian Airlines, June 20, 1994, preserve independent rights where adjudication needs no disputed contract interpretation, but their distinct preemption holdings do not decide every agreement's enforceability. The narrow ground fully rejects this defense; no contract-recovery remand is ordered merely to rescue the unsupported cross-appeal.

### E. Remedy and what the jury verdicts do not establish

The district court must proceed consistently with the corrected agency rule and the resolved prejudice determination. If the surviving claim is submitted to a jury, the supported actual-authority route must be accurately instructed, without the universal alter-ego, apparent-authority or permission-to-harass conditions. Ordinary genuinely preserved grounds consistent with these holdings remain for their proper adjudication. The mandate does not order a second appellate reconsideration of the same harmlessness claim.

**Strongest narrower-remand alternative.** Vacate for the lower court to reconsider prejudice after Faragher. That adds an unnecessary intermediate proceeding: the lower opinion already identified the omitted route, preservation, supporting evidence and concrete prejudice, and this Court can resolve the reviewed legal issue on those recitations. Faragher's different bench findings do not require an exploratory remand here. The appellate further-proceedings disposition is itself adequate and remains the next stage.

**Strongest broader-remedy alternative.** Enter judgment for Harrison based on the tort verdict and strength of the alleged authority misuse. Rejected. The Brown verdict concerns another defendant and different elements, and no supplied special findings resolve every Title VII predicate against Potash.
