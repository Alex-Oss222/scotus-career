## Shared operative commitments

**Coverage.** ERISA §510's attainment clause applies to rights a participant or beneficiary may become entitled to under a covered welfare plan; absence of statutory vesting does not categorically remove those rights from its protection. The statute separately prohibits specified actions against participants or beneficiaries for exercise of covered rights and actions undertaken for the purpose of interfering with attainment. The prospective attainment language and ERISA's inclusion of welfare plans leave no textual pension-only or vested-right limitation to insert.

**Amendment and interference remain distinct.** Section 1051(1)'s exemption of welfare plans from statutory participation and vesting requirements preserves lawful power to design, amend and terminate such benefits. Section 510 does not itself make benefits immutable or promise continued employment. That power does not automatically authorize a covered discharge or discriminatory act undertaken for §510's prohibited purpose while a relevant plan right may be attained. Curtiss-Wright distinguishes amendment power from authorized exercise; Lockheed distinguishes sponsor design from implementation and independent statutory obligations; Varity's majority and dissent disagree about other functions and enforcement but neither establishes categorical immunity from §510.

**Application and remedy.** The lower court dismissed the welfare theory on the categorical absence of vesting. That ground cannot stand. The transfer and benefits reductions are reported allegations; they establish neither a valid plan amendment nor a proved statutory violation. On remand the plaintiffs must satisfy the actually applicable participant/beneficiary, plan-right, covered-action, purpose and causal requirements, and establish an available remedy through §510's reference to §502. The Court need not formulate every possible causation or business-restructuring rule to remove the erroneous exclusion. Mertens and Varity's remedy limits remain intact; the decision authorizes no compensatory-damages award or automatic reinstatement.

## Alternative-path comparison

**The strongest affirmance path** reads unvested welfare expectations as outside protected future entitlement because the employer could amend the package directly; extending §510 would otherwise convert ordinary cost-saving restructuring into compulsory benefits. This is a substantial structural objection, not merely a policy preference. It fails as a categorical construction because it would deprive the prospective attainment language of work for a defined class of covered plans without an exemption in §510. A lawful revision of plan terms and a prohibited interference with participants' attainment are different statutory acts. Courts must enforce the purpose and action requirements rather than erase the category of benefits.

**The strongest objection to vacatur** is that the complaint may ultimately concern only a permissible business transfer with incidental benefit consequences. That possibility remains available below. The reported allegations expressly assert benefits-avoidance purpose, and the grant tests the categorical legal ground, not an established concession of lawful purpose or authorized amendment. An alternative affirmance would need an actual preserved and supported basis; none is supplied. The remand creates no presumption that cost reduction proves prohibited purpose and no independent duty to preserve a particular subsidiary or work arrangement.
