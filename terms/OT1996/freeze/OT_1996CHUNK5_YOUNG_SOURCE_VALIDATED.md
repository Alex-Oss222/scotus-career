## Program chronology and legal limits

The published recitation supports release in October 1990, simultaneous Board recommendation for parole, review of release conditions during orientation, and an executed acknowledgment tying community status to compliance. It describes five **apparently** uneventful months, followed by the Governor's denial and the telephone direction to return on March 14, 1991. That account does not conclusively find that no condition was ever violated or establish an unconditional promise of release.

The procedure on which the State relied was promulgated in August 1991, after the return. The earlier version in effect when Harper entered the program was silent about the effect of a later parole denial. Preserve both dates and this precise subject of silence. The later procedure cannot simply be attributed to the operative October 1990 rules or treated as proof of Harper's actual advance understanding. Conversely, silence about parole denial does not alone establish an irrevocable entitlement, bar every other legally available return ground, or prove the content of the whole governing scheme. The simultaneous parole recommendation also counsels against treating the packet's general description of early-release eligibility as a proved finding that Harper himself was ineligible for parole when released.

Sandin's controlling rule requires genuine substantive constraints and a meaningful deprivation when the claim rests on state-created prison liberty, and expressly preserves directly protected liberty. It adopts no atypical-and-significant-hardship test. The model must assess the actual community liberty under Morrissey separately from a claimed right to receive formal parole. The new extract supports the temporal and actual-conditions comparison; it does not decide that constitutional comparison, the proper process, or ultimate relief.

## Rehearing and petition sequence

The petition for rehearing was filed two days late, but the Tenth Circuit expressly granted leave, treated the filing as timely, and withheld its mandate until after denial. The certiorari petition was filed within 90 days of that denial. These are supported facts for applying the term-effective review-timing rules. It would be inaccurate to describe the rehearing as merely an unauthorized late paper or to measure timeliness from the original judgment while ignoring the court's acceptance and treatment of the rehearing.

The extract supplies no exact rehearing, denial, or certiorari filing dates from which this validator could independently recalculate elapsed days. The model must apply the governing rule to the authorized late rehearing; these facts do not establish a universal rule that every untimely submission restarts review time. ## Release and return

In October 1990, after reviewing respondent Ernest Eugene Harper's
 criminal record and conduct while incarcerated, the Pardon and Parole Board
 simultaneously recommended him for parole and released him under the Program.
 At that time, respondent had served 15 years of a life sentence for two
 murders. Before his release, respondent underwent orientation, during which
 he reviewed the "Rules and Conditions of Pre-Parole Conditional Supervision,"
 see App. 7, and after which he executed a document indicating that he "underst[ood]
 that being classified to community level depend[ed] upon [his] compliance
 with each of these expectations," App. 6. He spent five apparently uneventful
 months outside the penitentiary. Nonetheless, the Governor of Oklahoma
 denied respondent parole. On March 14, 1991, respondent was telephoned
 by his parole officer, informed of the Governor's decision, and told to
 report back to prison, which he did later that day.
