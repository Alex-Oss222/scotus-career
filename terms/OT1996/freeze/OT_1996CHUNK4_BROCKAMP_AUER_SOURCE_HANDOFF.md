# 40. Auer v. Robbins

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Auer v. Robbins
- **Citation or Docket:** 519 U.S. 452 (1997); No. 95-897
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Entry Stage and Event:** Argued merits case on review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit.
- **Simulated Event Date:** February 19, 1997.
- **Question Presented:** Whether the Department of Labor's salary-basis regulation validly applies to public employees, how the phrase 'subject to reduction' should be interpreted, and what weight courts give an agency's interpretation of its own regulation.
- **Question Granted:** Whether St. Louis police sergeants and a lieutenant are salaried executive employees exempt from FLSA overtime under 29 C.F.R. § 541.118(a).
- **Legal Field:** Administrative law, statutory interpretation, labor law, and agency interpretation of regulations.
- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit held that all petitioners satisfied the salary-basis and duties tests and were exempt from overtime.
- **Requested Supreme Court Judgment and Relief:** The officers ask the Court to reverse and award further proceedings on overtime. The police commissioners seek affirmance.
- **Material Facts and Record:** The police manual authorized disciplinary sanctions that could include pay deductions, but such deductions were not the general practice. One deduction implicated the salary-basis rule. The Secretary interpreted 'subject to reduction' to require either an actual practice of improper deductions or a significant likelihood created by a clear and particularized policy, and recognized a reimbursement or correction window for isolated improper deductions.
- **Preservation, Threshold, and Vehicle Matters:** The Secretary participated as amicus and presented an authoritative agency interpretation. Courts assess the regulation’s text within Thomas Jefferson University’s controlling-weight rule unless this Court expressly replaces it. The case does not require deciding damages before liability. The duties question is preserved.
- **Relevant Dates:** Argued December 10, 1996; decided February 19, 1997.
- **Companion or Consolidated Matters:** None.
