## Exact common support and mandate

**All eight — Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer — directly support each proposition:**

1. Title VII does not exclude discriminatory harassment because the actors share a sex. The individual must prove discrimination because of sex and the remaining statutory requirements.
2. Sexual desire is possible evidence, not a necessary condition. Sex-directed hostility, differential treatment, discriminatory statements, threats, and context may supply proof; neither that list nor any item is indispensable. Sexual content alone is insufficient, and no orientation fact is found.
3. A single-sex workplace supplies context, not categorical immunity. Harris requires sufficiently severe **or** pervasive sex-based conduct, objectively hostile or abusive in context and actually perceived as abusive. No medical injury, diagnosis, treatment, measurable productivity loss, or single mandatory factor is added.
4. Apply Andrade if employer attribution is reached: actual delegated authority must materially aid the environment; title alone does not suffice; lack of higher-management notice alone does not defeat the agency route; negligence is distinct. No employment action or economic loss is invariably necessary for that agency route. The separate quid-pro-quo theory retains its own employment-condition predicate.
5. Reverse only the judgment resting on the same-sex exclusion and remand the affected claims, including Sundowner and Lyons at that rejected ground. Preserve the independently grounded Pippen/Johnson not-an-employer dismissal outside the grant without adopting a new substantive individual-liability rule. Lyons's statutory status remains undecided.
6. No decision of ultimate liability, factual sufficiency, constructive discharge, damages, entitlement to trial, mandatory discovery, or available remedies for the 1991 conduct. Properly presented independent defenses remain available under ordinary procedure.
