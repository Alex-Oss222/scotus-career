# 152. Faragher v. City of Boca Raton

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether an employer is vicariously liable under Title VII when a supervisor uses actual or apparent supervisory authority to create a sexually hostile work environment, even though the harassment is outside the ordinary scope of the supervisor's employment.

2. Whether liability for supervisory harassment requires actual or constructive knowledge by higher management, or may arise independently when the agency relationship materially facilitates the harassment.

3. Whether, in the absence of a tangible adverse employment action, an employer may establish an affirmative defense based on reasonable preventive and corrective measures and the employee's unreasonable failure to use available complaint procedures.

4. Whether the existence of a formal sexual-harassment policy is sufficient to defeat liability when the record raises questions about the policy's dissemination, implementation, or effectiveness.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

Faragher worked intermittently as a municipal ocean lifeguard during 1985–1990. After a bench trial the court found repeated unwelcome sex-based touching and humiliating conduct by Terry and Silverman. Their command positions controlled assignments, discipline and training in a unit remote from higher city officials. The City failed to communicate its harassment policy to that unit. Faragher complained to Gordon, another supervisor, but not higher management. The City investigated promptly after another former employee's letter. The trial court awarded Faragher one dollar on her Title VII claim. Its agency reasoning relied on supervisory authority and workplace structure, with constructive notice and Gordon's knowledge as additional grounds. Separate individual constitutional and state claims are outside the assigned employer-liability questions. [Trial findings](https://openjurist.org/864/fsupp/1552/faragher-v-city-of-boca-raton-1459086).

The amended en banc decision displaced the earlier panel treatment and rejected Title VII liability. Faragher seeks restoration of that nominal judgment; the City seeks to retain its appellate victory. The review concerns attribution of an established hostile environment, not a new trial over every act. [Operative appellate opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/111/1530/630363/).

## D. ENTERING LAW APPLIED

The operative simulation registers, coordinated October 7, 2026, supply completed law through July 1, 1996. No completed same-term decision establishing this agency rule has been authenticated. Earlier prepared Andrade and Harrison positions are not holdings, however their scheduling is arranged.

Title VII prohibits sex discrimination in employment conditions and includes an employer's agent in its definition. Pre-divergence Meritor directs attention to agency principles while rejecting automatic employer liability for every supervisor's harassment and rejecting absence of management notice as an invariably sufficient defense. Scope of employment, employer negligence, apparent authority and an agency relationship that aids misconduct require separate treatment. The question is how those principles apply where the harasser's delegated control creates or sustains discriminatory working conditions without a completed firing or demotion. A label such as “personal conduct” does not answer every agency route; a supervisor's title alone also does not answer the required connection.

Simulated Harris unanimously requires severe or pervasive sex-based conduct creating both an objectively hostile environment and subjective abuse. It eliminates psychological injury, treatment and lost productivity as prerequisites but explicitly leaves employer attribution open. It therefore supports the trial's totality inquiry despite Faragher's continued work and limited treatment; it cannot make the City responsible simply because abuse occurred. Severity sufficient for Harris does not necessarily prove what distant management knew or reasonably should have known.

Actual notice, imputed knowledge, preventive care and remedial response concern distinct propositions. A properly implemented policy may bear on reasonable care, a particular assertion of apparent authority, and preventable loss. Whether it supplies a complete affirmative defense to materially aided harassment is the open third question. Neither a two-part preventive-care defense nor a tangible-employment-action prerequisite is inherited simulation law.

Simulated Landgraf's controlling five-Justice §102 application denies the new compensatory and punitive damages for pre-November 21, 1991 conduct; three other Justices separately reach their unavailability. Accordingly this review cannot enlarge the pre-1991 Title VII award into §102 damages or generate a new §102 jury right. The original nominal claim remains the concrete stake. A restored judgment does not itself determine collateral fees: simulated Farrar separates enforceable nominal relief from reasonable fee amount under §1988, a useful limited analogy to the separate Title VII fee statute, not an independent source of Title VII damages or an automatic fee award.

## E. PARTY POSITIONS AND AVAILABLE OUTCOMES

Faragher argues that delegated command power facilitated the hostile conditions and that ineffective policy dissemination defeats the City's attempt to shift responsibility to her noncomplaint. The City separates private harassment from its business, disputes any use of delegated power to obtain submission, and relies on absent management notice and prompt correction once notified. Its objection that the trial treated severe harassment and constructive notice as equivalent requires distinct consideration if negligence becomes dispositive.

The Court may affirm under a legally sufficient attribution rule, reverse and restore the nominal judgment if existing findings establish every necessary ground, or vacate for application of a corrected agency rule where the findings do not resolve its material requirements. A limited remand must identify the missing agency or negligence determination. Original personal judgments, unrelated claims and unpresented individual immunity issues are not automatically reopened. No court may assume either a future affirmative defense or a future Supreme Court outcome.

# 153. Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc.

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether an employer may be held liable under Title VII when a supervisor uses authority over an employee's work assignments and working conditions to facilitate sexually harassing conduct.

2. Whether an employee must prove that the offending supervisor possessed substantial managerial authority, including authority to hire or fire, before employer liability may be imposed under agency-law principles.

3. Whether a federal court commits reversible error by instructing a jury that supervisory harassment can create employer liability only if the supervisor possessed actual or apparent authority to commit the harassment, rather than authority over the employee that aided its commission.

4. Whether an employee covered by a collective bargaining agreement must exhaust contractual grievance or arbitration procedures before pursuing an independent Title VII discrimination action in federal court.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

Harrison, an underground miner, alleged that shift foreman Brown used work orders to take her into isolated mine areas and subjected her to repeated unwanted sexual conduct. Brown controlled assignments and participated in discipline. The employer later investigated, credited her unwillingness but Brown's account of perceived consent, and prescribed leave, treatment, reassignment and restrictions. Its harassment policy had not been included in her new-hire materials. A jury awarded $102,500 compensatory and $40,000 punitive damages against Brown on state torts, but found for Eddy Potash on Title VII.

Harrison challenged employer-attribution instructions. Potash cross-appealed on contractual nonexhaustion. The Tenth Circuit ordered further proceedings on Title VII and rejected the cross-appeal. Potash omitted the CBA and the order addressing exhaustion from the appellate record; no identified clause committed statutory claims to arbitration. [Appellate opinion, including footnotes 1–2](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/112/1437/585308/).

## D. ENTERING LAW APPLIED

The authoritative Holdings and Standards register edition of October 7, 2026 supplies completed law through July 1, 1996. No authenticated completed same-term decision resolves these assigned questions. Even if Andrade or Faragher appears earlier in the schedule, its prepared position does not become governing law. No later historical harassment or arbitration decision supplies the rule.

Title VII's employer definition includes agents. Pre-divergence Meritor requires agency analysis, rejects liability based solely on every supervisor's misconduct, and does not make management notice universally necessary. The distinct possibilities include scope of employment, employer negligence, apparent authority, and aid supplied by delegated power outside ordinary employment scope. The instruction issue is whether demanding alter-ego managerial rank and apparent authority to commit harassment improperly excludes the last route. Assignment power has a concrete causal role if a supervisor uses work instructions to isolate a subordinate; merely being present at the same workplace is different. Legal instructional error still must be prejudicial when the charge is considered as a whole.

Simulated Harris supplies the unanimous objective-and-subjective severe-or-pervasive standard, without mandatory psychological injury, therapy or lost output. The alleged physical coercion and repeated work-related isolation concern severity, humiliation and conditions of work. The remedial response does not substitute for determining those elements or attribution. The tort verdict cannot automatically become a Title VII employer judgment: it concerns another defendant and different elements.

On Question 4, pre-divergence Alexander v. Gardner-Denver preserves an independent Title VII action despite contractual arbitration; Gilmer enforces an actual individual agreement to arbitrate the statutory ADEA claim and does not automatically overrule the collective-contract distinction. Simulated First Options requires judicial determination of agreement and clear, unmistakable delegation under applicable contract principles. Its antecedent rule has nine joins; the conditional limited-review rule where delegation actually exists has eight, with Stone reserving that proposition. The absent contract here cannot establish delegation or scope by assertion.

Simulated Livadas holds that an independent, nonnegotiable wage claim requiring only contractual arithmetic is not displaced by §301; its additional holding rejects withholding state enforcement solely because protected bargaining produced an arbitration agreement. Simulated Hawaiian Airlines similarly preserves independent rights not requiring disputed contract interpretation, despite overlapping facts or unfinished grievances. Their particular state-right and labor-statute holdings do not themselves decide Title VII arbitration. Their relevant discipline is to identify the right's actual source and necessary elements: whether Brown used assignment power to impose sex-based conditions can be resolved without deciding disputed contractual terms. A genuine separate contractual obligation remains distinct; no blanket exemption of every CBA from the FAA follows.

## E. PARTY POSITIONS AND LAWFUL ALTERNATIVES

Harrison seeks a charge permitting attribution through abused delegated authority and argues that a contractual grievance process cannot extinguish her independent statutory action. Potash defends a narrower attribution rule and invokes the asserted CBA before federal suit.

The Court may affirm the appellate remand if the instructions excluded a supported, legally valid liability route; reverse it if the charge was legally adequate or any error harmless; or narrow remand directions without imposing employer liability. It can reject the asserted exhaustion defense on the actual contract record while leaving other valid statutory-arbitration agreements for cases presenting them. A verdict against the employer requires the ordinary factfinding and remedial predicates, with no duplicate recovery for the same injury. The separate state awards against Brown are not set aside simply because Title VII requires further proceedings.
