### Independent judicial purpose and legal basis

The opposition raises an express statutory objection supported by actual pleadings and a substitution order. The appellate opinion supplies materially contrary party-caption evidence. Those are competing sources about a prerequisite to this Court's review. Resolving their legal effect before a merits order protects the Court's jurisdiction and the identity of the litigant invoking it. The request would be appropriate even if every trial transcript page were readily available to the operator. It requests a focused account of the existing party record and legal capacity, not production of a missing merits archive, and imposes no merits consequence for an operator's research gap.

Section 1254(1), as applied in **UAW v. Scofield, 382 U.S. 205, 208–209 (1965)**, distinguishes a party entitled to seek merits review from an interested nonparty. **Director, OWCP v. Perini North River Associates, 459 U.S. 297, 302–305 & n.13 (1983)**, distinguishes statutory party status from Article III injury; its statutory premise was a conceded party below, which is disputed here. Neither decision makes the Attorney General's representation alone sufficient.

The 1995 Rules reinforce the need for a bounded answer. Rule 12.4 does not permit a party omitted from the petition at filing to join it later. Rule 12.6 addresses parties below and their status here. Rules 14.1(b) and 15.2 make party identification and the timely disclosure of objections material. Rule 35.3 supplies automatic succession for a public officer already party in an official capacity to a proceeding in this Court, and permits disregarding an immaterial misnomer; it does not determine whether that officer was a party below. Rule 16.1 permits an appropriate order after consideration of the submitted papers. Nothing in these provisions establishes the disputed predicate by itself.

The strongest immediate-reach position is that the appellate caption, naming the former Attorney General as a respondent, provides sufficient evidence of an official litigant whose present holder seeks review, or that the petition objectively acts for the custodial respondents. The strongest immediate-dismissal position is that the amended pleading, verified answer and substitution establish that only the custodial officers were respondents and that the named petitioners cannot join them now. The first position does not explain the contrary party documents; the second treats the later appellate caption as a mistake without a sufficient source for that conclusion. The adopted course decides neither disputed premise against a party and leaves both legal arguments available at the specified return.

**Smith v. Barry**, January 14, 1992, is a relevant but limited analogy: functional compliance can overcome a filing's title, while required party-identifying information remains necessary. It does not transform the Attorney General as counsel into a party or adjudicate the actual contents of this petition. Its reasoning supports examining the timely filing and party record before choosing either formal dismissal or disregard of the discrepancy.

### Governing components and common boundaries

**Existing law.** The Standards entry *Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review*, present operation effective **O'Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995**, places the State's whole-record *Chapman* burden on its defined personal-intent instructional/argument category. The precise prior violation combined instructions with prosecutorial argument. Applying it here requires explaining that both defects permit guilt without the defendant's own legally required intent; an additional prosecutorial statement is not what gives that particular personal-intent protection constitutional importance. That is a bounded application to this preserved purpose omission, not a rule for all habeas error.

**Classification.** *Sullivan*, June 1, 1993, addresses a charge vitiating the reasonable-doubt standard for every jury finding. *Gaudin*, June 19, 1995, requires jury determination of an element and reserves universal structural-error treatment. The omission here does not invalidate the reasonable-doubt standard for all findings. *Rose v. Clark*, 478 U.S. 570 (1986), and the Court's *Carella*, 491 U.S. 263 (1989), permit some harmlessness review of element-related instructional defects. The Associates' alternative positions do not adopt automatic reversal for every omitted element. Whether the permissible review is narrow enough to require relief here is distinct from classification.

**Actual lower ruling.** The Ninth Circuit expressly concluded that the omission failed *Chapman* on direct review before proceeding through its historical collateral-standard analysis. It permitted harmlessness only through necessary jury findings. A merits remand therefore needs a justified defect in that method or a distinct unfinished application; the assertion that no *Chapman* assessment occurred is false. The historical collateral rule in the parties' papers and lower opinion is not current law.

**Record.** The jury could not convict on mere knowledge or presence alone, but knowledge plus assistance and purpose to facilitate robbery are not identical legal elements. The separate Clark verdict and capacity reasoning cannot supply the missing Mannix purpose by themselves. The special-circumstance instructions present a serious actual-findings argument, despite the later reversal of those findings: intentional aid to the actual killer under 8.80 and the robbery-related purpose of the murder under 8.81.17 have to be connected to Roy's own purpose and to the instructions defining first-degree murder. The conditional nonkiller clause must not be treated as an unconditional finding on every possible route; lack of personal knife use does not itself establish that Roy could not have been an actual killer by another means. This is a limitation on the inference sought, not a finding that Roy drowned Mannix. Nor does the single adverb concerning intentional aid automatically establish robbery purpose when the jury was directed elsewhere for the definition of aiding and abetting.

The State may rely on the watch, cash, wet clothing, assistance testimony and reported statements, but Hudspeth's account, Hall's statements and retraction, the verdicts and the corroboration disputes must be considered together. Clothing specifically allocated by the lower recitations to McHargue cannot be moved into Roy's backpack. The medical evidence does not identify Roy as the source of the fatal wound. A conviction on one component does not imply wholesale acceptance or rejection of every witness. These limits arise from the supported record, not from a missing research file.

**Preservation.** The personal-intent constitutional claim is preserved under the authorized intake. The opposition's distinct objection to first-instance use of special-circumstance findings remains material, but the claimed oral concession is not verified. Under current *Yee*, April 1, 1992, a new argument can support a preserved claim, subject to the question presented. The findings argument concerns the preserved harmlessness issue and is not categorically a new constitutional claim. That does not require this Court to make a disputed record application in the first instance, nor does Rule 15.2 itself establish forfeiture merely because an objection was raised. Any remand below must permit consideration of genuine preservation limits; no default is invented.

### Qualified method and remedy that the six remand votes can join

For this preserved purpose-to-facilitate omission, the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt, using the whole record, that the omission did not contribute to the verdict actually returned. The reviewing court must consider the instructions, findings and evidence in relation to that verdict; it may draw supported inferences about the omission's effect and is not confined to locating an express or logically necessary finding of the omitted words. But proof sufficient to convict at a hypothetical correctly instructed trial, a finding of general criminal capacity, or a conclusion that knowing aid always equals purposeful aid does not discharge the burden.

The existing en banc *Chapman* discussion treats absence of necessary findings as conclusive. The legal defect identified by these six is that categorical restriction, not the absence of a *Chapman* discussion and not a finding that the evidence is necessarily sufficient for harmlessness. On remand the court must decide, in light of the instructions and the evidence actually put before the jury on knowledge, assistance and the related mental states, whether the omission could have contributed to these verdicts. *Yates* supplies the requirement to attend to the evidence the jury was instructed to consider and the error's contribution; *Rose* supplies review of the record rather than a per se rule whenever intent was contested. *Carella*'s Court opinion supplies reviewability and leaves room for necessary-finding equivalence; Scalia's narrower concurrence remains an argument, not an existing majority rule for every omission. *Sullivan* excludes substituting an entirely hypothetical verdict for a constitutionally nonexistent one; it does not declare this otherwise instructed jury incapable of meaningful review.

The remand covers only the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions. The Ninth Circuit may undertake the required review and use District Court proceedings if otherwise proper. If the available constitutional violation is not harmless beyond a reasonable doubt, conditional relief must allow the State a reasonable period to retry the affected convictions. A finding of harmlessness under that rule permits denial of the writ. No immediate release, present retrial deadline, restoration of special-circumstance punishment, disposition of the Clark conviction, independent innocence requirement or new AEDPA temporal rule is supplied.

### Enhanced individual comparison: merits alternatives, objections and limits

**Stevens.** His own *Rose* concurrence, 478 U.S. at 586–591, supports some harmlessness review while insisting on the State's rigorous burden and constitutional interests beyond accurate guilt. His join in Souter's *Yates* opinion, 500 U.S. at 402–411, supports examining what this jury considered and prohibits replacing its missing determination with a sufficient prosecution case. His current *Gaudin* join protects the jury's element determination but does not compel universal structural error. The strongest remand argument is that the Ninth Circuit made its necessary-findings method exclusive. His alternative affirmance accepts that the statement is too broad for all omissions but regards the result here as supportable under the proper inquiry: a legally distinct purpose was omitted; the reported assistance to a companion allows a purpose distinction; the accomplice and special-circumstance instructions do not conclusively assign the robbery purpose to Roy on every route; and the evidence of an advance plan is disputed in ways reflected in the verdicts and lower recitations. This is not acceptance of every impeachment assertion or a finding that Hudspeth was wholly disbelieved. The State's additional findings argument is within the harmlessness issue, yet supplies no sufficiently established independent purpose finding to compel reversal here. His narrower join is affirmance for this record under *Chapman*, with ordinary conditional habeas implementation, not wholesale adoption of the Ninth Circuit's formula. He could accept a remand identifying a genuinely unperformed material task, but not a remand based on an absent *Chapman* assessment or an unavailable operator file.

**O'Connor.** Her majority join in *Rose*, her *Yates* join, and her own controlling *O'Neal* Parts II and IV support classification and whole-record *Chapman* review without deciding harmlessness at this stage. Her *O'Neal* personal-intent premise covers the reason this error matters; presence of an additional prosecutorial statement in that earlier case is not a persuasive basis to deny its protection here. The strongest affirmance argument is that the jury was never told to find purpose and that the Ninth Circuit already ruled under *Chapman*. Her answer is limited: knowledge and assistance were genuinely submitted, so the jury's consideration and the relation between its findings and omitted purpose remain assessable; the necessary-findings-only rule cuts off that inquiry prematurely. She joins neither an equation of the two mental states nor a conclusive harmlessness finding based on the State's account. A categorical structural extension would conflict with the distinctions retained in *Sullivan*, *Gaudin* and her own *O'Neal* opinion. The narrower acceptable course is the stated method remand, with the State retaining the entire burden and with the special-circumstance argument's availability and effect left for proper consideration.

**Scalia.** His *Carella* concurrence, 491 U.S. at 267–273, expressly distinguishes independent judicial proof from jury findings and their necessary equivalents; his *Yates* separate opinion, 500 U.S. at 411–416, objects to asking whether a properly instructed jury would have found the missing element. His current authorship in *Sullivan* and *Gaudin* reinforces that concern while preserving their exact limits. The strongest remand argument is the whole-record wording of governing *O'Neal* and *Rose*. He reads that wording with the actual-verdict constraint: an ambiguous instruction about a submitted mental state can permit a broader effect inquiry than total omission of a distinct purpose. In this record, neither the actual knowledge-and-assistance findings nor the Clark findings logically require Roy's purpose to assist robbery. The special-circumstance argument must include the actual-killer alternative and the cross-reference to the defective aiding definitions; intentional assistance to a killer and the robbery-related character of a killing cannot be silently substituted for the missing personal purpose. A genuine finding necessarily equivalent to that purpose could render the error harmless despite its omission, so this is not automatic structural reversal. His alternative affirmance rests on failure to establish such a valid equivalent or another supported ground rendering the omission irrelevant, not on wholesale acceptance of disputed defense assertions. He does not join the six's rejection of a necessary-findings limit appropriate to this error. His narrower possible remand would have to identify a real unresolved actual-finding question; no such remand is frozen here merely because a source is unread.

**Kennedy.** His *Yates* majority join supports its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficiency of untainted evidence; his current *Sullivan* and *Gaudin* joins leave this classification question open. His earlier *O'Neal* vote for further proceedings is established, but the supplied current-law source identifies it as resting on a different harmlessness ground, not as support for the controlling burden. He now applies the defined controlling rule without importing the historical collateral standard. The strongest affirmance position is Scalia's jury-allocation argument. Kennedy accepts its prohibition on substituting a judicial verdict but does not read it to preclude inquiry into whether this omission affected the verdict when related mental-state facts were submitted. Knowledge and assistance are not the omitted purpose, but the whole charge and the evidence may establish the omission's lack of contribution without a formal express purpose finding. The proper disposition on this record is renewed review, not reinstatement of the denial: the contradictory accounts and special-circumstance connections must be addressed under *Chapman*. He joins the six only with those limits and would refuse a draft treating the state court's functional-equivalence assessment as a binding fact.

**Souter.** His own *Yates* opinion is the primary methodological source: identify the evidence the instructions put before the jury, then assess the constitutional error's effect rather than ask only whether evidence could support guilt. His current *Brecht* and *O'Neal* joins establish the State's burden in the defined categories. The strongest affirmance argument is *Yates*'s warning that an instruction can narrow the jury's focus so much that whole-record review cannot reliably speak to the actual verdict. He does not dismiss that warning. Here the jury was directed to knowledge, affirmative aid and several related intent issues; the special-circumstance directions may have additional significance, but their effect requires an integrated reading. The Ninth Circuit's categorical necessary-findings formula does not itself perform that examination. His alternative remand therefore directs a *Yates*-qualified inquiry, leaving a nonharmless result available if the omission kept the relevant personal-purpose issue from the jury's consideration. He will not join a statement that all overwhelming evidence is necessarily enough, a speculative purpose finding, or the claim that the lower court never assessed *Chapman*. A narrower actual-verdict explanation is a condition of his join, not an extra burden invented for this case.

**Thomas.** His own current *Gaudin* join establishes that an element belongs to the jury and leaves the universal error-classification issue undecided. His *Sullivan* join covers the different defect in the governing reasonable-doubt standard. The supplied sources establish that he did not join the *O'Neal* judgment; they do not supply the content of his earlier separate rationale, and none is invented. His alternative commitment applies the existing personal-intent rule rather than deriving a vote mechanically from that dissent. The strongest competing course would distinguish an omission from combined instructions and prosecutorial argument to reopen the collateral standard. The difference in the mechanism does not supply a sufficient distinction from the controlling premise that conviction was permitted without the defendant's required personal intent. The stronger disposition is a method remand under existing law: the jury did decide knowledge and aid, and the State may attempt to establish lack of contribution on the whole record. He will not join a universal *Chapman* extension or restoration of the state habeas denial based on capacity, possession of property or Hudspeth's disputed account alone. This is a precedent-bound commitment, not a claim that his earlier dissent has been withdrawn or republished.

**Ginsburg.** Her current *O'Neal* Parts II and IV join is the direct source for a narrow category and conditional remedy, and her *Gaudin* join protects jury allocation while reserving universal structural treatment. The strongest affirmance position is that the Court should not send Roy through another harmlessness inquiry when the en banc court already found the error harmful and the proposed cure argument was not resolved below. Her alternative remand identifies a narrower legal task: replace the exclusive necessary-findings condition with an actual-contribution inquiry under the same State-borne burden. It does not demand a fresh evidentiary record or allow the State to benefit from an assumed concession. *Yee* permits distinguishing the new argument from a new claim, while the first-instance objection and the tainted special-circumstance findings still require careful treatment. Her join requires the express distinction between intentional acts, capacity, the perpetrator's purpose and Roy's own purpose. She will not join any declaration that the special circumstances necessarily cure the omission, that the State has proved harmlessness, or that *O'Neal* governs every habeas error.

**Breyer.** His current *O'Neal* Parts II and IV join supplies both the personal-intent category and the rule that use of an incorrect standard alone commands neither a writ nor release. His *Gaudin* join preserves jury allocation without a universal structural consequence. The strongest affirmance argument is that the jury's omitted purpose cannot be recovered through an appellate prediction and the evidence admits the companion-assistance inference. He accepts both as serious constraints but favors deciding their significance by an integrated effect inquiry rather than treating any logically conceivable alternative as automatically decisive. That inquiry must address the actual reported evidence, impeachment and rebuttal, the ordinary instructions and the special-circumstance connections; it cannot accept the State's summaries as uncontested. Conversely, a possible innocent-purpose account remains an inference to assess, not an additional jury finding. He joins the qualified method remand and no present record-specific harmlessness result. His narrower alternative permits the lower court to sustain relief under *Chapman* after the correct inquiry; it does not promise that the same or a different result must follow.
