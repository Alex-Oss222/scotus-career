## Questions, record and review boundaries

The granted questions concern a separate constitutional reasonableness inquiry after sufficient general contacts, the relevance of the forum's absence of connection to the dispute, and the period and nature of contacts used to establish general jurisdiction. Met Life seeks restoration of its Vermont action; Robertson seeks dismissal, including on its alternative insufficient-contacts ground. Construction liability, limitations, venue and transfer are distinct questions.

The action was filed August 31, 1993. Met Life is a New York corporation headquartered there; Robertson is incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in Pennsylvania. The alleged faulty curtain-wall design, manufacture and installation concerns a Miami building and acts in Missouri, Texas and Florida. None of the claim-generating activities occurred in Vermont. United Dominion obtained separate summary judgment March 14, 1995 and is not a party to the appeal. The later Met Life notice of appeal therefore presents the final jurisdictional dismissal, rather than an unexplained year-long delay after a still-partial judgment.

Jurisdictional discovery covered 1987–1993. The plaintiff's submissions report approximately $4 million in Vermont sales over the multiyear period; $226,319 in 1993; five Star dealers and four Ceco authorized builders; product support and trademarks; national and direct marketing reaching Vermont, including particular architectural firms; over 150 employee/engineer visits; an employee resident with an office during 1989–1990; registration, sales identification and tax filings. Robertson emphasizes lack of current property, office or agents, independent dealers and 1993 sales below .07% of total sales. Those percentages do not themselves decide either constitutional category. The lower source inconsistently describes the $4 million interval in one passage as 1989–1993 and elsewhere 1987–1993 (one OCR passage says 1998). Do not manufacture a year-by-year series; the supported proposition is sustained approximately $4 million sales during the discovered multiyear course.

No evidentiary hearing was held after extensive discovery. The Second Circuit credited the plaintiff's jurisdictional factual averments for its prima-facie assessment and reviewed dismissal de novo. It upheld the reasonable discovery limit, rejected confining the contacts inquiry mechanically to filing-year 1993, found continuous and systematic contacts when considered together, but affirmed dismissal under a separate fairness analysis. It treated domestic defense burden as only slightly adverse, but Vermont's and the plaintiff's interests and location of proof as strongly adverse. It excluded the perceived favorable limitation period from the jurisdictional analysis under Keeton. These are the lower court's legal determinations, not already Supreme Court law.

Judge Walker dissented: general contacts sufficient to render the corporation amenable to unrelated suits should not be defeated by interests other than the defendant's protected liberty; litigation convenience belongs primarily to forum non conveniens or transfer. His proposed distinction and the majority's contrary extension of Asahi both remain live arguments. He agreed that Vermont was an inconvenient trial forum.

## Entering law and lawful paths

Quill's May 26, 1992 due-process holding establishes purposeful nonphysical market affiliation for a related tax duty; it does not decide general all-purpose jurisdiction. The operative register is **Separate due-process and Commerce Clause connections for remote use-tax collection**, at its actual tax scope. No relevant current-term change has been identified in the sanitized projection.

International Shoe supplies quality, nature and fair-play limits; Perkins permits unrelated litigation on sufficiently continuous and systematic operations; Helicopteros rejects treating regular purchases and associated training as sufficient general contacts. Burger King requires a compelling unreasonableness showing once purposeful contacts exist and keeps possible venue relief distinct. Asahi's eight-Justice Part II-B supplies an independent fairness holding in an international specific-jurisdiction setting; its stream-of-commerce theories are fractured, and Scalia did not join II-B. Keeton separates the choice-of-law/limitations question from jurisdiction. Vermont's long-arm rule reaches the constitutional limit as construed below; its exact quoted statute remains in the saved lower opinion.

Supported alternatives, without a selected result:

- Sustain separate fairness review in general-jurisdiction cases and affirm if the actual cumulative burden and missing forum/plaintiff interests meet the demanding constitutional threshold. The strongest objection is that ordinary unrelatedness defines general jurisdiction and that a slight domestic burden plus ordinary inconvenience may fail to be compelling.
- Sustain a separate inquiry but reverse or vacate its application: the continuing deliberate commercial relationship supports fair notice, and constitutional unfairness may not be established by ordinary inconvenience. The strongest objection is that Asahi treats plaintiff, forum and system interests as constitutionally relevant, rather than always leaving them to venue.
- Reject a freestanding inquiry that allows the absence of case-specific forum interests to undo adequate general affiliation, while leaving actual defendant unfairness and venue remedies available. The strongest objection is that International Shoe's fairness requirement is not expressly confined to specific jurisdiction.
- Affirm on insufficient continuous and systematic contacts, or remand that issue under a corrected standard, if the quality and duration of the commercial course are insufficient. The strongest objection is that the total contacts go beyond Helicopteros's purchases and cannot be reduced to isolated categories, percentages or a filing-day snapshot.

The discovery limit can stand independently even if the dismissal fails. Any jurisdictional reversal restores only the opportunity to litigate remaining threshold and merits issues; it orders no damages, limitations finding or transfer.
