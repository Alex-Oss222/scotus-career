# 38. FEC v. Legi-Tech, Inc.

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** FEC v. Legi-Tech, Inc.
- **Citation or Docket:** 75 F.3d 704 (D.C. Cir. 1996); court-of-appeals Nos. 94-5379 and 95-5085
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Procedural Category:** Simulation-assigned appellate merits
- **Lower-Court Event Date:** 1996-02-16; this is a source date, not an assigned simulation event.

The FEC investigated Legi-Tech's commercial use of campaign-contribution information, found probable cause, pursued conciliation, and filed this civil enforcement action. After circuit litigation invalidated the Commission's inclusion of congressional officers as nonvoting ex officio members, the FEC removed those officers and reconsidered pending enforcement matters. The lawfully composed commissioners received counsel's recommendations, deliberated, and voted to ratify continuation against Legi-Tech.

The district court dismissed without prejudice, concluding that the structural ruling and retroactivity required dismissal despite ratification. The FEC appealed. The dispute concerns whether the new action cured the defect, not whether Legi-Tech actually violated the disclosure-information restriction or owes a particular penalty.

### Questions Presented

1. Whether an agency whose original enforcement proceedings were undertaken while it was unconstitutionally constituted may cure that structural constitutional defect through subsequent ratification by a properly constituted agency.
2. Whether the participation of congressional officers as nonvoting ex officio members of an independent executive agency renders the agency's prior investigations, probable-cause determinations, and enforcement decisions void from their inception, notwithstanding subsequent lawful reconstitution.
3. Whether a properly constituted agency may ratify an earlier enforcement decision without reopening the original investigation, conducting new conciliation proceedings, and independently repeating every statutorily required step.
4. Whether a party challenging an enforcement action initiated by an unconstitutionally structured agency is entitled to dismissal without demonstrating continuing prejudice, or whether an adequate subsequent corrective procedure may justify allowing the action to proceed.
5. Whether retroactive application of a judicial decision identifying a structural separation-of-powers violation necessarily requires the same remedy in every pending enforcement proceeding affected by that violation.
