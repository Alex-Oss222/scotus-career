## Reconciled P/I/E positions

**T — Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas:** Reverse the rejection of preemption of these testament-derived claims. The alleged ownership arose in then-undistributed plan benefits and was assertedly transferred by Dorothy's testament. Later payment, rollover or stock delivery does not supply a valid antecedent ownership transfer where ERISA's beneficiary protections and anti-alienation scheme exclude that asserted transfer. The claims do not arise from a later independent bargain, tort, loan, gift or other obligation. The rule therefore turns on their legal source, not perpetual immunity of every asset traceable to a pension.

*Guidry* supplies the specific pension protection and refusal to invent equitable exceptions. *Mackey* preserves the pension/welfare distinction. The particular enacted beneficiary and domestic-relations provisions supply the conflict; neither a generalized interest in national uniformity nor the mere mention of a plan is enough. *Travelers* and *Dillingham* require an actual legal conflict rather than unlimited relatedness, but do not erase this conflict. *Board of Trade* shows that a separately administered obligation can still intrude on a protected plan entitlement; *Inter-Modal* preserves the independent force of an enacted command. These authorities do not decide every later recipient obligation.

For P, calling the action an accounting does not change the asserted prepayment ownership. For I, rollover and later beneficiary designation do not validate the earlier transfer, and no holding that the IRA itself remains an ERISA plan is necessary. For E, delivery changes possession and form, not the asserted legal source. The five preserve genuinely applicable statutory exceptions at their actual predicates and adopt no fictional administrator action. They would reverse and remand at this scope without fixing property fractions or amounts.

**U — O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Affirm rejection of preemption for P/I/E insofar as the remedy is confined to recipient or succession accounting for those already-distributed assets, leaves the plan's future payments and duties unchanged, and does not reach S. Louisiana's ownership and usufruct rules may impose such an obligation without making the recipient a new federally recognized plan beneficiary. Section 1056 protects delivery and restricts an assignment enforceable against the plan; its detailed QDRO route does not necessarily occupy every later property relationship. The statutory protection must be applied at its actual scope rather than made a general federal rule extinguishing state ownership after distribution.

The strongest T objection is that the state-law title itself originates in an impermissible prepayment transfer. The U response is that recognizing an obligation between recipients after lawful distribution differs from enforcing an assignment against the pension. *Guidry* forbids a constructive trust on the pension but does not decide every later accounting; *Mackey* requires attention to the statutory categories. *Travelers* and *Dillingham* require an actual interference with protected plan operation. *Board of Trade* involved a mandated covered-plan benchmark, not ordinary succession accounting simply because an amount can be traced to benefits. These three accept neither an automatic domestic-relations exemption nor a safe harbor merely because the plan is not named. Their surviving position is a proposed legal limitation on relief, not a factual stipulation that the sons abandoned S.
