## 42. Maryland v. Wilson — February 19, 1997

**Retained event and facts.** No. 95-1268; argued December 11, 1996; categorical passenger-exit authority during a lawful traffic stop. The suppression judgment, observed movement/nervousness and delayed stop, falling cocaine, and lack of a preserved independent case-specific safety ground are retained as supplied source-verified neutral facts. This task did not independently inspect the suppression transcript or Maryland appellate opinion; their absence from this task is not a finding of litigation default.

**Updated law.** Whren, June 10, 1996, supports an objectively justified ordinary initial traffic stop but separately preserves manner, scope and duration limits. Ornelas, May 28, 1996, concerns independent review of ultimate reasonable suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause, with historical-fact protection and genuine contextual due weight. It is not a universal independent-review rule for every Fourth Amendment question. The Standards titles are **Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop** and **Independent appellate review of suspicion and nonwarrant probable cause**, with the effective dates and authority in the slice.

Robinette, November 18, 1996, applies Mimms to a driver during lawful traffic business and rejects invalidation based on the officer's undisclosed motive. It does not itself extend the categorical command to passengers. It separately requires independent justification for detention after traffic grounds end and preserves genuine independent state law. This public holding is now part of the entering snapshot, not merely a pending earlier packet.

**Live paths and limits.** One supported legal path extends Mimms's officer-safety/incremental-intrusion reasoning from drivers to passengers during the lawful stop; the competing path distinguishes a passenger's responsibility for the traffic violation, freedom of movement and individual justification. Brignoni-Ponce, Terry and Prouse remain date-eligible sources for their actual seizure and safety principles, not holdings on this exact categorical order. No rule that the passenger was necessarily free to depart, or necessarily already seized by the initial stop, is assumed. Rejecting the State's categorical rule does not erase the observed facts, but neither may the Court supply the independent justification the approved neutral packet identifies as unpreserved. No automatic broader passenger detention, frisk or search authority follows from an exit-order ruling.

## 43. Regents v. Doe — February 19, 1997

## 44. Schenck — February 19, 1997

Use the linked complete lower-court validation with the exact decree and footnotes. ## 45. Brown — February 24, 1997

**Record status.** All facts are well-pleaded allegations accepted at Rule 12(b)(6), not trial findings. Full 68 F.3d 525, including every footnote, was read. The complaint alleged one required ninety-minute program for these fifteen-year-old plaintiffs; the source later describes two school presentations, which does not establish that either plaintiff attended two. Neither plaintiff alleged personal selection for a skit or direct targeting by the presenter. The complaint also alleged weeks of coarse imitation afterward; do not compress the record into an assertion that nothing occurred after the assembly. It alleged no factual account connecting that later hostility to the plaintiffs' sex rather than their disagreement with the message.

**Material lower grounds and qualifications.**

1. The lower court separately rejected conscience-shocking conduct, an infringement of the parents' educational liberty, and a student's claimed privacy right against exposure to offensive words. It did not hold that words can never shock the conscience, nor treat Fraser/Pacifica permission to restrict speech as an individual federal damages entitlement to censorship.
2. The procedural due-process claim invoked both the school’s mandatory written-positive-subscription policy and Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 71, section 1, described in footnote 8 as allowing a religious opt-out from instruction on disease. Defendants assumed these interests for the motion; this is neither a holding that the Constitution creates them nor a finding that the state provision and school policy have identical terms.
3. The lower court characterized the deprivation as failure to supply the required notice/permission opportunity, not the program itself, and applied Parratt/Hudson. It found no pleaded delegation to bypass the policy and no pleaded facts satisfying Zinermon's foreseeability/delegated-deprivation distinction. The plaintiffs' contrary possibility is that planning and institutional authority allowed advance safeguards. Treat that as a legal argument from allegations, not a finding of authorized policy change.
4. The lower court separately found a developed challenge to the adequacy of postdeprivation remedies waived; its conclusion is not proof that a remedy compensates every alleged harm. The unpreserved general assertion that exposure cannot be undone does not itself establish a preserved remedy-inadequacy theory.
5. The lower court addressed Free Exercise damages under Smith/Yoder and declined retroactive RFRA damages; it separately dismissed prospective declaratory Free Exercise relief for lack of an allegation of likely recurrence under Lyons. Its footnote alternative discussed conduct-date qualified immunity, with a possible School Committee exception. No universal immunity for all private contractors, officials or entity defendants may be inferred.
6. The Title IX analysis separately addressed severe-or-pervasive hostility and the requirement that discrimination be **because of sex**. It expressly disclaimed a rule that one episode is categorically insufficient. It identified context and lack of personal targeting without establishing indispensable prerequisites of physical touching, personal targeting, measurable academic loss or psychological harm.

**Scope.** The stated grant lists parental liberty, student privacy/bodily integrity, and Title IX. Section I also supplies procedural and Free Exercise theories; preserve them as existing lower-judgment components and legal grounds to be assessed under the grant and question-presentation rules. This preparation does not add a new independently granted RFRA question, adjudicate its temporal reach, or silently reopen an ungranted state claim. Any disposition of those distinct components must state why they are reached, fairly included, left undisturbed or otherwise properly treated.

**Updated law.** Curtis is now controlling public law effective January 21, 1997, with unanimous reasons, but expressly reserves compulsory sex education and genuine religious-compulsion cases. Brown's required attendance is a material distinction; lack of compelled personal performance, affirmation, disclosure or bodily contact remains a separate factual distinction, not an automatic answer. Curtis supplies no categorical school immunity. Settle's genuine-pedagogy rule, June 25, 1996, does not authorize every event bearing a curricular label; Sherman concerns official religious observance, not a general opt-out rule.

Harris, November 9, 1993, and DeAngelis, January 6, 1997, preserve the totality-based severe-or-pervasive standard without required tangible loss or physical advances. Their employment setting is an analogy, not automatic incorporation of a Title VII attribution formula into Title IX. Franklin, February 26, 1992, permits compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination but reserves final recipient attribution, punitive relief and unintentional effects. Neither curriculum content nor offense alone answers the because-of-sex element.

Original RFRA was enacted **November 16, 1993**, not 1994 as the lower transcription says; the official three-page Public Law 103-141 text was downloaded and read in full. ## 46. Dunn — February 25, 1997

**Objective date addition.** Argued **November 13, 1996**. Control supplied this date alone after checking the Cornell syllabus for No. 95-1181. This context did not receive or open that syllabus or any historical result, reasons or lineup.

**Verified lower posture.** Full 58 F.3d 50, June 23, 1995, was read. This is an interlocutory appeal from appointment of a temporary equity receiver, not a final fraud judgment. The district court froze assets April 5, 1994, and appointed the temporary receiver after hearings. The appellants' hearing argument concerned absence of CFTC authority. The lower court also rejected abuse of discretion in the receiver appointment, conditional on the agency's statutory authority. These preliminary recitations and affidavits do not establish final fraud liability.

Investors supplied money for defendants to trade off-exchange currency options in defendants' names; the lower source expressly says that options or participations were not sold directly to investors. This distinction matters if a proposed rationale depends on counterparty or customer status. Do not recast every solicitation as a direct currency-option sale to the investor.

**Statute and competing interpretations.** The complete Treasury Amendment, including all listed categories and the board-of-trade/future-delivery exception, is mechanically copied into the source excerpts from the lower court's quotation of 7 U.S.C. section 2(ii). The court considered itself bound by American Board of Trade, 803 F.2d 1242, which distinguishes trading an option from exercising it and treats exercise as the currency transaction. It acknowledged contrary Fourth Circuit treatment in Salomon Forex, 8 F.3d 966. This reports lower-court reasoning; historical Supreme Court petition treatment cited within those sources is not adopted as an in-world event.

Petitioners argue that the whole transaction and its underlying commodity fall within the ordinary words and that the explicit exception limits restoration of CFTC coverage. The CFTC's contrary reading distinguishes a right to transact from a transaction in currency and invokes the regulatory structure. Its existing trade-option exemption, 17 C.F.R. section 32.4, and its lower-argument representation that interbank options qualify, are relevant limits on an unqualified claim that its position necessarily regulates all interbank currency options. The quoted exemption has its own commercial-actor and business-purpose conditions; it is neither the Treasury Amendment nor a universal exemption for every petitioner's trade.

The fraud allegations cannot create statutory authority. Conversely, exclusion from this agency's authority does not establish immunity from every other antifraud law. The Court may affirm the agency-authority determination or reverse/vacate for implementation under the proper scope; either route must respect the interlocutory receiver posture and any grounds genuinely remaining. No new fraud finding, restitution amount or independent enforcement action is presumed.

**Source limit and readiness.** The full primary lower opinion supplies the complete quoted operative proviso, transaction description and litigation posture. The original complaint, complete transaction contracts and briefs were not separately read. Attempts to fetch the Treasury text from guessed GovInfo 1994 paths returned a Page Not Found page; those error files were removed and are not sources. No missing filing or default is inferred. ## 47. United States v. Wells — February 26, 1997

**Objective date addition.** Argued **November 4, 1996**. Control supplied this date alone after checking the Cornell syllabus for No. 95-1228; this context did not open the syllabus or receive its historical disposition, reasons or lineup.

**Verified record and lower reasoning.** Full 63 F.3d 745, August 21, 1995, including every footnote, was read. Two substantive counts were removed by judgments of acquittal; the jury convicted on Count I conspiracy and Count II bank false statement. The appealed judgment vacated those convictions for a new trial. Those acquittals are not subject to reinstatement through a ruling on the two convictions.

The lower court records that defendants raised the jury-materiality issue at trial and in their new-trial motion. It requested and received supplemental briefs after Gaudin and addressed the Government's no-materiality argument. The trial judge considered materiality a jury question but believed then-binding circuit law required judicial determination. This supports a genuine preservation/review issue without describing a source gap as waiver or converting acquiescence in existing law into an immutable statutory element.

The lower court adopted a materiality element through the statute's established judicial construction and comparison with other fraud/false-statement provisions. It also addressed conspiracy pleading and evidentiary sufficiency, rejected the legal-opinion portion of proposed expert testimony, and left other materiality-expert, multiple-conspiracy-instruction and Government sentencing questions unaddressed because it ordered retrial. The approved neutral packet separately preserves unresolved indictment/instruction theories. A Supreme Court correction of the element question must not be written as automatic rejection of every remaining appeal or an automatic restoration of a particular sentence.

**Statutory source and genuine fork.** The official 1994 section 1014 text and relevant revision note are saved and mechanically excerpted. The operative phrase addresses knowing false statements/reports or willful overvaluation, purpose to influence specified institutions, and specified credit transactions. It contains no express materiality term. The official revision notes also state that the 1948 consolidation changed no substance except the changes described there. Thus respondents' continuity/common-meaning argument has a statutory-history source; it should not be reduced to a plea that materiality would be desirable. Conversely, the omission, express surrounding elements, and differing predecessor provisions remain interpretive issues to be resolved rather than assumed away. This preparation does not claim to have verified a precise count of predecessor statutes containing materiality or every predecessor's text.

The 1994 Code's later penalties and institutional amendments are not automatically the law governing punishment for all earlier conduct. Its amendment notes identify the 1989 and 1990 penalty increases and later changes. No new penalty holding is proposed here; materiality and the disposition of the reviewed convictions are the prepared questions.

**Entering law.** Gaudin, June 19, 1995, unanimously requires jury determination beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an offense element. It does not decide which materially different statutes contain that element, and it reserves universal structural-error treatment. Lebron, February 21, 1995, permits consideration of a disavowed legal argument within the same preserved claim and question on an adequate existing record; it establishes discretion, not an obligation or universal abolition of waiver/invited-error doctrine. The supplied statement that invited error “do[es] not transform” the premise into an unreviewable rule is a proposed application, not an independently established categorical rule.

Roy, November 4, 1996, is included for its actual omitted-intent/actual-verdict harmlessness holdings and published disagreement, if an error/remedy path is reached. Its preservation, habeas category, burden and limits must remain distinct; it does not itself prove this different error harmless or authorize appellate factfinding to replace a jury finding. The Government's reviewability and textual path, defendants' historical-meaning/lenity and trial-specific objections, and the option to remand properly unresolved matters remain available for independent modeling. Reinstatement of all relief is not presumed from success on the statutory question.
