## Chamber of Commerce v. Reich — ready

Sources: full [D.C. Circuit opinion, February 2, 1996](https://static.case.law/f3d/74/cases/1322-01.json), saved [here](sources/CH3A_Chamber_lower_opinion.txt), with [OpenJurist cross-check](https://openjurist.org/74/f3d/1322/chamber-of-commerce-of-united-states-v-b-reich). Their source URLs are embedded in the files.

The lower opinion substantiates the March 15 suit against the implementing Secretary, earlier ripeness appeal, subsequent final regulations, no amended APA cause, and the District Court's alternative statutory merits ruling. The government's appellate reviewability concession was limited and qualified; do not turn it into a concession that all challenged action is reviewable or unlawful. Its nondelegation/finding objections are separate from the NLRA-conflict route and need not be decided if another sufficient route disposes of the case.

Add the order's actual qualifications to any factual and remedial account: it concerns contracts above $100,000; termination is not automatic and is subject to the agency head's objection; debarment retains the dispute-resolution and compelling-reason qualifications; its organizational-unit reach includes specified affiliates. The lower opinion's later discussion of the Secretary's regulatory treatment of a dispute's end is a judicial interpretation, not statutory language or a finding that every conceivable application violates the NLRA. The permitted replacement question concerns economic strikers, not workers striking against unfair labor practices.

Keep §1331 jurisdiction, the equitable officer-review route, §702 immunity waiver, and the merits distinct. Section 702 preserves other legal and equitable review limits, requires an injunction to identify the responsible officer or successor, and does not authorize relief another consent-to-suit statute forbids. Neither §1331 nor the waiver independently supplies the equitable cause. The strongest opposing path is statutory harmony: broad procurement authority and genuine operational interests could permit choices a private purchaser may make. The challengers' path relies on Congress's preserved economic weapons and the distinction between project purchasing and broad labor policy. The Court must decide that statutory fit, not infer invalidity from the policy's popularity or characterize the federal statutory conflict as state preemption alone.

Simulated Franklin reserves presidential APA status; Dalton's scheme-specific preclusion remains controlling; Boston Harbor's actual project-specific rule supplies the purchasing analogy. The earlier current-term Yang holding reinforces only the separation of statutory discretion from arbitrary administration at its proper scope; it does not decide procurement review. A sufficient route is available without coercive presidential relief or a new ruling on the President's APA status. No material factual blocker remains on that confined route.

## Castano v. American Tobacco Co. — ready

Sources: full [Fifth Circuit opinion, May 23, 1996](https://static.case.law/f3d/84/cases/0734-01.json), saved [here](sources/CH3A_Castano_lower_opinion.txt), with [OpenJurist cross-check](https://openjurist.org/84/f3d/734/castano-v-american-tobacco-company). The report includes the trial proposal, the certification order's scope, Rule 23 text, relevant qualifications and all footnotes.

Correct scope: review is by §1292(b), not mandamus and not a later Rule 23(f) procedure. The class claims concern nicotine addiction and asserted misconduct, covering smokers and specified derivative or representative claimants since 1943. The district court certified core liability and punitive questions; it excluded injury, causation, reliance, defenses and individual compensatory damages. It denied Rule 23(b)(2) equitable/medical-monitoring certification, and that denial was not cross-appealed. No current Supreme Court relief should reinstate that separate category implicitly.

The proposed plan and what the district court actually adopted must remain distinct: the lower opinion states that the court did not adopt the entire plan, though its order incorporated many elements, including the punitive-ratio arrangement. The appellate judgment ordered dismissal of the class complaint. It did not adjudicate the manufacturers' tort liability or extinguish individual claims on their merits. Liggett's requested conditional appeal dismissal was declined below; a pending proposed settlement is not a completed release of the entire controversy.

The legal choices are concrete. Falcon permits the inquiry necessary to establish Rule 23 prerequisites; Eisen's merits limit does not make that inquiry pleadings-only. Defendants identify legal variations, individual proof, and possible repeated jury fact determinations. Proponents may argue for genuinely separable common issues, workable legal groupings and efficiency despite expensive individual proof. A trial plan is evidence of manageability, not an independently enacted universal requirement for a document carrying that title. Neither novelty nor the amount at stake alone appears in Rule 23 as a categorical prohibition. The opinion's general predictions about settlement pressure and jury behavior remain that court's reasoning, not established trial findings for every class action.

Do not decide independently the constitutionality of every punitive ratio, an Article III severance objection or all claimed Erie defects: the lower court expressly left those additional grounds unresolved. Its Seventh Amendment concern was a risk of reexamining common facts in this proposed sequence; mere repetition of evidence must not be equated automatically with unconstitutional reexamination. The narrower Rule 23 paths avoid those constitutional questions. Matsushita and Richards preserve adequate representation but do not themselves resolve prospective certification. Cipollone, in the ProCD supplement, matters to identifying viable smoking-related claim categories and statutory periods if that substantive-law map is reached; do not certify or reject nonexistent causes by treating all pleaded tobacco duties alike. Pending Amchem and Adams are not precedent. No material source blocker remains for review of this certification order.

## In re Deuel — ready

Sources: full [Federal Circuit opinion, March 28, 1995](https://static.case.law/f3d/51/cases/1552-01.json), saved [here](sources/CH3A_Deuel_lower_opinion.txt), with [OpenJurist text](https://openjurist.org/51/f3d/1552/in-re-deuel); official [1994 §103](sources/CH3A_35_103_1994_exact.txt) and [1996 §103 with amendment/effective-date notes](sources/CH3A_35_103_1996_exact.txt).

The packet's claim-specific record is substantiated. Claims 4 and 6 define broad encoding genera; claims 5 and 7 identify the particular 961- and 1196-base-pair cDNAs. Bohlen supplies only the first nineteen amino acids and brain-tissue proteins; Maniatis supplies a general screening technique. The agency asserted protein identity and tissue/species interchangeability without the separate evidentiary explanation described in the appellate opinion. A useful target and an available technique are relevant asserted premises, not proof that every target-specific step was routine. The genus claims do not inherit every narrow-sequence defense automatically, but no separate agency rejection ground was supplied for them. Section 112 defects were reserved, not adjudicated.

Preserve both paths: §103 does not require anticipation or printing the exact molecule in prior art; a supported predictable route and expectation of success may matter. Conversely, incentive to conduct a broad research program cannot alone establish obviousness of every ultimate product. The Court can examine whether the appellate court's categorical statements went beyond what this record supports without deciding that the unsupported agency rejection was correct. New factual findings from litigation counsel cannot cure the missing agency explanation. No eligibility, infringement, priority or automatic patent-issuance judgment follows from the review.

Pub. L. 104–41, effective November 1, 1995 for filed and pending applications, redesignated the retained product-obviousness rule as §103(a) and added a qualified permission concerning biotechnological process claims in subsection (b). The product claims here are not transformed into process claims, and no applicant election is invented. The statutory change supplies no new product-obviousness outcome. Markman's judge/jury allocation is at most an aid to identifying the claims' scope; it does not replace Graham, determine all PTO factual review or create a construction hearing. The scenario's pending controversy supplies the procedural basis; no further historical petition document is required merely to restipulate the authorized premise.

Clear opportunity to inspect and reject is supported here; no broader website or future software practice needs adjudication.

The independent federal inquiry must apply both copyright subject matter and equivalence. Lack of copyright in facts does not itself remove a fixed compilation from §301's subject-matter inquiry. A genuine party-specific undertaking may be qualitatively different from exclusive rights against the world, while a mere contract label or duplicative restriction may fail to establish that difference. Wolens and Cipollone support examining the source of the undertaking under their different statutory clauses, not automatic §301 immunity. Kewanee, Aronson, Feist and Bonito Boats remain available at their pre-divergence scopes. No nonparty is bound and no copyright arises by enforcing a promise. Generally applicable defenses remain legally possible, but an unpreserved new defense is not invented as a reason for additional proceedings.
