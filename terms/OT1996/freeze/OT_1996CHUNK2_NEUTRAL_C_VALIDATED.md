## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether 28 U.S.C. §1350 merely confers subject-matter jurisdiction over specified international-law torts, or also authorizes federal courts to recognize and enforce private civil causes of action for violations of customary international law.

2. Whether federal courts possess authority to fashion compensatory and punitive remedies for established international-law violations in an action under the Alien Tort Statute.

3. Whether the political-question doctrine bars adjudication of individual claims for torture committed abroad by officials of a foreign government.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

Three Ethiopian women sued a former local official for his personal participation in torture during the 1977–78 Red Terror. Their federal action followed his discovery in Atlanta. After a bench trial the court awarded each plaintiff $200,000 compensatory and $300,000 punitive damages; the Eleventh Circuit affirmed. The selected appeal concerns §1350's remedial authority and political-question objections. The unpreserved limitations argument does not become an additional granted issue. The defendant seeks reversal of the damages judgment; the plaintiffs seek affirmance. This is a civil tort judgment, not criminal prosecution or an immigration-removal proceeding.

## C. ENTERING LAW APPLIED TO THE RECORD

The text of §1350 supplies district-court jurisdiction over an alien's tort committed in violation of the law of nations or a United States treaty. Subject-matter jurisdiction, a legally enforceable substantive norm, a private action and the available remedy require separate analysis. Pre-divergence Baker v. Carr requires a concrete textual commitment or judicially unmanageable decision, not merely a foreign setting. Official personal torture presents a materially different question from reviewing the legitimacy of an entire foreign government.

Simulated Suter rejects deriving an individual right and an implied statutory action merely from an administrative statutory obligation; its majority, despite Stone's disagreement, requires a source-specific inquiry here. It does not decide §1350 or abolish historically received common-law actions. Simulated Franklin v. Gwinnett permits appropriate relief only once a cognizable action exists and specifically reserves punitive damages. It cannot itself authorize either this cause or this punitive award. Simulated Central Bank preserves a confined received securities remedy for culpable substantial assistance; that holding supports attention to the historical received action, not an unrestricted judicial power to enforce every international norm. The question therefore turns on the meaning and historical function of §1350 and the specificity of the asserted prohibition, with remedial authority separately justified. Later historical ATS decisions, including Sosa, are not entering law.

## D. PARTY POSITIONS, AVAILABLE DISPOSITIONS AND LIMITS

The plaintiffs characterize the statute as recognizing judicial enforcement of a narrow, established international tort and emphasize the defendant's proved personal conduct. The defendant distinguishes a jurisdictional grant from congressional creation of damages liability and invokes foreign-relations limits. An affirmance must identify the action and remedies independently; jurisdiction alone is insufficient. Reversal on the action issue would not approve torture. A limited remand could be needed if an alternative pleaded statute requires temporal or preservation analysis, but no unverified TVPA claim or retroactivity premise is inserted to save this judgment. No foreign sovereign is made a defendant by this packet.

### Record, lower judgment, and review boundaries

The Eleventh Circuit's January 10, 1996 opinion, 72 F.3d 844, was read in full through its footnote in two independent reproductions (OpenJurist and FindLaw). Its public recitation supplies the trial facts under Engine §14(2). Three plaintiffs filed in September 1990, following Taye's encounter with Negewo in an Atlanta hotel in 1989. Following a two-day bench trial the District Court found liability for torture and cruel, inhuman and degrading treatment and awarded **each** plaintiff $200,000 compensatory and $300,000 punitive damages. The total judgment is therefore $1,500,000, but the individual components must not be merged for remedy analysis.

The findings distinguish conduct: Negewo personally supervised and directly participated in some torture of Abebe-Jira and Taye; the finding concerning Demissie is personal supervision of some interrogation and torture. Do not transform the last finding into a finding that he personally inflicted every injury, or make official title alone the basis of liability. The action concerns the individual's own participation/supervision; it does not add a sovereign, organization, absent supervisor, new plaintiff or new claim.

The lower court reads §1350 as allowing a federal forum and judicial common-law remedies for customary-international-law violations, relies on Filartiga and its progeny, invokes the analogy to Lincoln Mills, and reads the TVPA legislative history as supporting its account. It rejects a political-question objection under Baker and its circuit's Linder decision. These are the **lower court's reasons**, not controlling Supreme Court law. The lower opinion is not a separate detailed justification for every kind or amount of damages.

Two separate footnote matters stay distinct: the Eleventh Circuit declined the proposed Georgia two-year limitations defense because it was not raised below; it also rejected the denial-of-appointed-counsel challenge under the exceptional-circumstances standard. ### Supported analytical alternatives, without assigning votes

An affirmance path can distinguish a narrow action enforcing an already specific and obligatory prohibition of official torture from open-ended creation of substantive international norms, treating the jurisdictional enactment's historical function and received judicial remedy as the necessary source inquiry. It must independently justify compensatory and punitive authority and the relationship of each plaintiff's supported findings to the narrow rule. The strongest contrary argument is that a jurisdictional sentence confers adjudicative power without delegating creation of new private liability; the later enactment of a specifically defined cause with conditions does not automatically settle the earlier statute's function.

A reversal or narrower-remand path can distinguish forum jurisdiction from an actionable duty and question whether this statute authorizes the asserted judicial action/remedy. Its strongest contrary argument is that denying every cognizable tort action may ignore what the jurisdictional enactment presupposed and the received action demonstrated by pre-event cases. An action-source objection does not deny the substantive prohibition of torture. If only punitive authority is unsupported, that component must be analyzed separately from compensation. A bounded opinion may confine adjudication to the proved official torture rather than generalize to every form of cruel treatment, nonstate violence or universal civil jurisdiction; the lower judgment cannot be enlarged to support those applications.

The foreign setting alone supplies no political-question ground. Any such ground must identify the concrete constitutionally committed decision or unmanageable legal determination in this individual tort adjudication and confront the ordinary judicial character of the facts and damages issues. Recognition of a cognizable action likewise does not establish all possible foreign-relations limitations; unresolved defenses are not manufactured from silence.
