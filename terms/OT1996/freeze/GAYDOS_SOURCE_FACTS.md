## Original paper

The corrected August 1996 petition seeks an order causing the District of New Jersey clerk to file and docket a FOIA/Privacy Act complaint originally tendered in December 1995, or exercise of Supreme Court original jurisdiction over it. It also requests clerk disqualification and issuance of summons. It alleges access denial and false notices of court orders. These are allegations, not findings of misconduct. Its exhibits include a 1993 district-court order directing a deputy clerk to act in a particular different action; that exhibit does not establish a universal disqualification.

An authenticated published recitation supplies the independent lower-court fact that the FOIA complaint was docketed on September 9, 1996, and dismissed in its entirety the following week. Treat that as a lower-court procedural fact; it does not adjudicate the accuracy of the allegations or every later request. The reported docket number appears as 96-CV-42435 and should not be silently regularized without the underlying docket. The original paper itself predates that docketing.

## Additional contention in the October supplement

The October 8 petition and October 22 supplement, associated with No. 96-5831 in the archive, additionally seek a supporting opinion from the district court in Gaydos v. Sisk, No. 96-698. The applicant asserts she cannot perfect review because a clerk repeatedly demanded that opinion although none beyond the supplied order exists. She invokes access to review, Supreme Court Rules 20.3 and 14.1, and the All Writs Act. This contention is distinct from the already-docketed FOIA complaint and must be evaluated in its own terms.

The exhibits establish:

- A February 28, 1996 district-court letter order, filed February 29 and entered March 6, denies reconsideration of an earlier IFP denial and dismisses the complaint with prejudice under former 28 U.S.C. §1915(d). It identifies frivolousness and related dismissals and refers a possible violation of an earlier filing order. That is an existing written statement of reasons; it is not proof of any additional opinion.
- A July 30, 1996 Supreme Court clerk letter returns a separate certiorari paper because the district-court opinion and a service declaration were missing, requests correction, and states a 60-day Rule 14.5 interval. A September 20 letter again returns it for the district-court opinion in No. 96-00698. These are authenticated administrative correspondence, not Court adjudications. Do not infer that any deadline expired, that service was defective after correction, or that the requested opinion was legally indispensable without the governing rules and the relevant papers.
- The applicant's October 10 response says the corrected paper is being resubmitted with mandamus seeking the repeatedly requested opinion; she asserts the other corrections were timely. The receipt stamp is October 15. These documents establish her contention and the correspondence, not the truth of every claim of compliance.
- The October 8 sworn financial affidavit reports no current employment, annual pension/alimony income of $6,480, about $300 in cash/accounts, a car worth about $200, and no dependents. The originally attached affidavit had an earlier date; the October affidavit supplies the current sworn representation. No contradictory financial evidence was located in these documents.
