## 137. Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor

**Validated posture and dates.** No. 96-270; argued February 18, 1997; scheduled decision June 25. Review concerns the Third Circuit's vacatur of certification, settlement approval and the related injunction. The lower opinion is May 10, 1996, with a June 27 rehearing disposition. O'Connor's established nonparticipation remains; the eight other seated Justices constitute a quorum. The precise threshold is a majority of participants, with an appellate equal division leaving the lower judgment in place without Supreme Court precedent. No vote is proposed.

**Entering law.** Matsushita (February 27, 1996) and Richards (June 10, 1996) keep adequate representation separate from notice and exclusion rights. They do not constitutionalize every Rule 23 requirement. The completed Castano decision (January 14, 1997) now requires an actual law-and-proof showing before certification, rejects both automatic national-class bans and a tort-maturation prerequisite, and preserves every Rule 23(a) prerequisite. Rhone-Poulenc (February 26) preserves these requirements while distinguishing them from the extraordinary mandamus threshold. Its settlement-pressure discussion does not approve a class. Adams (March 3) is a DIG supplying no merits approval of a compulsory settlement. Steel Co. (June 2) forbids assumed Article III power merely to decide contested statutory merits but does not exhaust legitimate ordering of nonmerits thresholds. The model must address whether certification is logically antecedent to the particular putative-class standing objection, rather than assume either that all standing questions must precede certification or that constitutional power can simply be ignored.

**Live alternatives.** A court may reject this proposed class because materially different applicable law, injuries and remedial interests defeat predominance or because present and future claimants lack genuinely adequate structural representation. Settlement removes a trial-management problem; it does not automatically answer these different prerequisites. Conversely, a properly supported settlement-class analysis could consider the settlement's elimination of trial-management obstacles and the common resolution actually offered, without substituting general settlement desirability for predominance or representation. The strongest objection to affirmance is that litigation-specific manageability concerns might have been imported into a settlement where no trial is proposed. The strongest objection to reversal is that a negotiated package cannot establish adequate advocacy for conflicting groups merely by being beneficial on average. Separate representatives or subclasses are possible structures to assess, not an order or a finding that a new proposal would succeed.

**Justice-specific source anchors.** Ginsburg authored Castano; every other participating Associate joined its four holdings. All participating Associates joined the controlling representation rule in Matsushita and the unanimous Richards rules, and all joined Rhone-Poulenc's retained certification constraints. These concrete shared commitments supply each Justice's starting point without dictating a settlement-class result. Breyer's published Steel Co. concurrence permits an exceptional merits-first approach on its stated conditions; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg have distinct statutory-threshold positions there; Scalia and Thomas joined the jurisdiction-first Court part, and Kennedy joined with a reservation. Preserve those differences if sequencing matters. Falcon, Hansberry and Shutts supply the date-eligible underlying rules, not a current-matter outcome.

**Limits and sources.** The nine representatives, present/future interests, caps and absence of inflation adjustment are supported packet facts. No new factual conclusion about adequacy, class members' individual standing, notice delivery or contractual severability is made. ## 138. City of Boerne v. Flores

**Validated posture and corrected date.** No. 95-2074; argued February 19, 1997; scheduled decision June 25. The §1292(b) appeal concerns Congress's authority to impose RFRA on States and localities, not an adjudicated failure of this permit denial under RFRA. The Fifth Circuit opinion is **January 23, 1996**, as shown by 73 F.3d 1352. The packet's February 19, 1996 lower-judgment date was corrected in the frozen neutral derivative only. The source brief and runtime split are untouched. No specific nonparticipation is established.

**Entering law.** Smith (April 17, 1990) remains the constitutional rule for genuinely neutral, generally applicable laws. Lukumi (June 11, 1993) preserves separate targeting, secular-comparator and narrow-tailoring inquiries. Church of Scientology (December 5, 1994) and Swanner (March 27, 1995) applied original RFRA prospectively while expressly reserving its constitutionality. Their application-specific burden sequence does not decide Congress's §5 power by implication. The Standards' current wording also incorporates American Life League (November 13, 1995): later federal enactments remain subject to RFRA unless they expressly exclude its application by reference to RFRA. That federal clause does not itself resolve the State-power question. Agostini (June 23, 1997) overrules Aguilar's categorical treatment of the safeguarded onsite public service and displaces Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale, while retaining the Community Education result and Lemon's general structure; it neither changes Smith nor decides RFRA's validity.

**Live alternatives.** Congress may be understood to have adopted a preventive remedy for hard-to-detect unconstitutional religious discrimination, with the strict statutory standard serving constitutional enforcement. The strongest objection is that a universal exemption standard untethered to demonstrated unconstitutional conduct effectively changes the judicially enforceable right. Alternatively, §5 may authorize remedial and preventive protection but not the asserted statewide and local replacement of Smith's substantive baseline. The strongest objection to that limitation is the breadth of preventive authority recognized in South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan; Congress need not wait for case-by-case adjudication of every constitutional violation. Oregon v. Mitchell supplies an additional boundary to examine, with its actual fractures preserved. Reconsideration of Smith is a distinct possible constitutional path only if lawfully within the question and adequately supported; it is not entailed by prior RFRA application or by disagreement with the statute's breadth.

**Justice-specific source anchors.** Scalia authored Smith's constitutional rule, joined by Stevens and Kennedy; O'Connor's separately reasoned Smith concurrence supplies her direct objection to the neutral-law approach. Their actual grounds must be read, not inferred from the judgment alone. O'Connor authored both simulated RFRA holdings; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas joined the application-specific Swanner opinion, while Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer did not join that disposition. All eight Associates joined Church of Scientology's statutory inquiry and constitutional reservation. Kennedy's controlling Lukumi components distinguish targeting and underinclusion from neutral regulation; Souter's direct joins have the narrower scope recorded in Holdings. Agostini's published writings supply current evidence of the Justices' treatment of precedent and religion, but neither its establishment result nor its coalitions answer §5. Further direct, date-eligible Justice-specific enforcement-power authority may be researched just in time if needed to resolve a material fork.

**Statutory limits and sources.** Original RFRA imposes a substantial-burden threshold, then governmental production and persuasion on the person-specific compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means justification. It authorizes appropriate relief subject to ordinary Article III standing; it preserves religious belief and the Establishment Clause. The saved [enactment text](../sources/chunk4_b/rfra_enactment.txt) and [official-source PDF](../sources/chunk4_b/rfra_enactment.pdf) contain §§1–7. The text has visible OCR defects: use the PDF if verbatim wording matters, and copy a verified extraction rather than retyping a quotation. A ruling on State/local application must identify its effect on prospective RFRA claims; it does not silently reopen final judgments, dispose of independent free-exercise claims, grant a permit or decide federal RFRA validity. [Fifth Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/73/f3d/1352); [Smith](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/494/872).

## 139. Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott

**Validated posture and dates.** No. 95-1184; argued December 2, 1996; scheduled decision June 25. The lower opinion issued June 27, 1995, was amended on denial of rehearing September 18, and amended again October 3. Its speech holding concerned both advancement and fit. No specific nonparticipation is established.

**Entering law.** Gibson (December 4, 1991) preserves germane compulsory bar charges but requires protection against financing nongermane ideological activity once an objection is known. Hurley (June 19, 1995) protects actual private expressive composition without exempting ordinary goods and services. Rosenberger (June 29, 1995) expressly leaves compelled-fee claims unresolved. The commercial-speech Standards preserve actual advancement and reasonable fit, without least-restrictive-means or compulsory empirical-study requirements; Lanphere supplied the added access trigger on April 18, 1995. The proposition-level 44 Liquormart holdings (May 13, 1996) remain distinct from its nonmajority theories. Turner II (March 31, 1997) permits supported preventive regulation of the particular cable bottleneck under independent intermediate review; it supplies no general economic-regulation immunity for compelled speech funding.

**Live alternatives.** The assessment may be a germane term of an integrated, otherwise lawful collective marketing arrangement, leaving producers' own speech and attribution free; that permits a confined reversal without treating all compelled advertising as ordinary regulation. The opposing path is that compelled financing itself burdens protected expression even without personal attribution, ideological content or suppression of independent advertising. The latter path must be considered expressly: absence of personal attribution is not a complete answer to every compelled-subsidy theory. Abood, Keller and Lehnert require examining the reason for compulsory association and the speech's relation to it; a bare free-rider assertion is not automatically sufficient. If commercial-speech review applies, identify the actual burden and evidence before deciding advancement, fit or the need for remand. Do not invent objectionable advertisements, branding effects or findings about program effectiveness.

**Justice-specific source anchors.** Souter authored Hurley's unanimous rule. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg adopted 44 Liquormart's actual advancement ground; O'Connor and Breyer joined the fit ground while assuming advancement; Scalia and Thomas did not supply additional balancing votes. In Turner II, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer joined the preservation/diversity rationale; O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg disputed the showing and fit, while preserving the granted-question boundary. These distinctions support separate consideration of classification and application. For Scalia, Kennedy and the other then-participating Justices, Lehnert's actual individual writings and joins are relevant direct pre-divergence compelled-dues authority; its different statutory-duty and germaneness approaches must not be conflated.

**Limits and sources.** The material supported scheme is comprehensive collective regulation, generic commodity promotion, no personal author attribution and retained independent advertising. Those facts do not prove all possible claims against a marketing order invalid or valid. [Ninth Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/58/f3d/1367); [Lehnert](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/500/507). The AMAA and then-effective orders remain the primary sources for any exact statutory delegation or assessment exception necessary to a new holding; the supplied scope does not adjudicate the separate statutory challenges.

## 141. United States v. O'Hagan

**Validated posture and dates.** No. 96-842; Eighth Circuit judgment August 2, 1996, rehearing denied November 13; argued April 16, 1997; scheduled decision June 25. The lower court vacated convictions in a 57-count indictment. Counts 1–20 were mail fraud, 21–37 §10(b)/Rule 10b-5, 38–54 §14(e)/Rule 14e-3, and 55–57 laundering. The supplied review scope excludes the separate laundering disposition. No specific nonparticipation is established.

**Entering law.** Central Bank (April 19, 1994) recognizes a confined private affirmative-assistance remedy; its accepted private-action rationale is materially different from the historical premise quoted in the lower opinion. The lower court's quotations of a contrary historical Central Bank result are not entering Supreme Court law. The simulated holding still requires underlying deception and preserves primary responsibility as a separate category; it supplies no criminal scienter rule. MCI (June 17, 1994) limits agencies to actual delegation while preserving Chevron within that boundary. Chiarella requires a duty making silence deceptive and leaves a general equality-of-information rule unavailable. Carpenter supports fraud against the source of entrusted information; its divided securities disposition supplies no controlling resolution of this §10(b) question. Section 14(e)'s preventive delegation requires independent analysis rather than automatic assimilation to §10(b).

**Live alternatives.** Concealed disloyal use of entrusted information may constitute source deception consummated through the security transaction; alternatively, that breach may lack the required statutory connection to a securities transaction or victim. Test actual deception separately from fiduciary unfairness, then the connection requirement; neither market inequality nor professional status alone establishes the offense. Full disclosure to the source may remove the alleged deception but does not itself create an exemption from every other law. Independently, §14(e) may authorize a reasonably preventive rule encompassing specified trading without a separate fiduciary-breach element, or the rule may exceed a delegation confined more closely to statutory fraud. Support for one securities theory does not decide the other. Mail-fraud treatment must follow the charged theory and the actual appellate dependency; recognition of a legal theory does not establish that every instruction, element or harmless-error issue is resolved. Preserve the unreviewed laundering result.

**Justice-specific source anchors.** Stevens authored the simulated Central Bank majority joined by Souter and Ginsburg; its limited private-remedy reasoning does not decide this prosecution. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg joined MCI's actual statutory-boundary ground. Chiarella includes Stevens's separately stated reservation, and Carpenter supplies date-eligible participation for Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia. Neither source contains positions from Justices not then on the Court. For Breyer and for any material distinction among the other Justices not answered by these sources, the independent model should retrieve a direct date-eligible statutory or agency-power writing rather than infer a securities position from a predecessor or from the current historical result. This is a source task, not a presumption against either path.

**Rule and remedy limits.** The lower opinion reproduces Rule 14e-3(a)'s substantial-step/commencement condition; material nonpublic tender-offer information; knowledge or reason-to-know conditions about both nonpublic status and specified sources; covered purchases, sales and related instruments; and the public-disclosure alternative within a reasonable time before trading. Preserve these distinct conditions and any actually relevant exceptions in a proposed holding. No general trading prohibition or equal-information duty follows. Criminal willfulness, instruction, sufficiency and error-effect questions must remain distinct from statutory validity; unresolved issues remain for ordinary remand rather than automatic reinstatement of every conviction. [Eighth Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/92/f3d/612/united-states-v-ohagan). Use enacted §§10(b), 14(e), the then-effective rules and applicable criminal provisions if exact language becomes decisive.
