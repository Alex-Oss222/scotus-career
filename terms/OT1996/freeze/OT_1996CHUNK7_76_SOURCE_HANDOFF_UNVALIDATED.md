## Source-present factual distinctions requiring preservation

1. **Warrant and no-knock request:** The application and warrant at JA 2–10 identify Room 138 and controlled-substance evidence. The requested no-knock language is visibly struck in the text extraction and Pharo confirms the issuing judge refused the request at JA 42. The warrant's authority to search and the manner of execution are distinct. A fresh reader can inspect the original separated PDF for the precise strikethrough if that matters; the transcript independently confirms the refusal.

2. **Advance information:** The warrant affidavit describes the informant's report, cash rentals, repeated calls, refusal of maid service, packaging material in an abandoned room, and the earlier arrest with 63 packets. These are information offered to establish probable cause; they are not findings that this particular room contained a weapon. The later seizure cannot silently become information known before entry.

3. **Uniformed officer and recognition:** Pharo testified that, when Richards partly opened the door, he looked over Pharo's right shoulder toward a police officer in full uniform and appeared surprised; the door then slammed and locked. JA 38–43. Richards expressly testified that he saw only Pharo and no uniformed officer, and that he shut the door when Pharo tried to force it. JA 45–48. Petitioner merits p.6 says his field of vision *permitted* him to see Pharo and the uniformed officer. That is not an express admission that he actually saw and recognized the officer. Respondent merits pp.1–2 preserves the denial and the trial court's credibility treatment. Do not replace these primary distinctions with the more categorical description in control's secondary objective extract.

4. **Trial ruling:** JA 54–55 discounts Richards's testimony as self-serving and lacking some credibility, draws an inference of awareness of the uniformed officer, and accepts announcement before crossing the threshold. It refers to the door closure, lock, surprised expression, failed ruse, disposable controlled substances, and the defendant's flight. It should be reported as the trial court's inference and reasoning, not as an uncontested admission or an independently found intention to destroy evidence. What facts the officers knew before forcing entry must remain distinct from the court's later evidentiary inference.

5. **Timing and content of announcement:** Pharo said police identity was announced two or three seconds after the door shut, before the eventual successful forcing of the door; the testimony also describes unsuccessful manipulation and kicking. JA 39–45. On supplemental testimony, he said he heard other officers shout "search warrant" when he heard the bang of the door opening, and agreed it was simultaneous. JA 64–68. The trial court allowed the supplemental hearing over the defendant's objection and said the added evidence did not change its ruling. JA 60–69. Do not merge announcement of identity with announcement of purpose, or entry across the threshold with the first application of force.

6. **Flight and seizure:** The testimony and complaint place breaking glass and window flight as entry occurred; JA 18–32 and 47 describe the defendant, the other occupant, and the sequence. Respondent's characterization that both were going out is not a substitute for the transcript, which says Dates remained inside. Drugs and money were found above the bathroom ceiling; they were not in plain view. These later discoveries cannot prove that officers heard evidence destruction before entry.

7. **Lower-court grounds:** The Wisconsin Court of Appeals affirmed under the drug-house rule; the state Supreme Court majority reaffirmed a categorical exception for warrants seeking evidence of *felonious drug delivery or possession with intent to deliver*, not every drug offense. The majority did not decide the quantum of justification outside that category. Abrahamson concurred in the judgment based on the trial facts and the useless-gesture theory while rejecting the categorical rule. That distinction matters to any accurate statement of the judgment below.

8. **Preservation and remedy are source-present:** Respondent merits pp.38–46 requests consideration of useless gesture and suppression independently, says both were briefed below and in the opposition, and asks either this Court or the state court on remand to address them if necessary. Its proposed suppression grounds invoke valid-warrant authority, independent source and avoiding a worse position for police. Those are litigant submissions, not adopted law. The reply and petition still need full reading before completing the adversarial assessment or a preservation conclusion. Suppression and invalidity of the blanket rule must not be merged automatically.
