## Complete category and amendment account

Former §2510(2) requires an exhibited expectation against interception under circumstances justifying that expectation **and excludes electronic communication**. The latter exclusion was added in 1986; it was not added by the 1994 cordless amendment. The [official 1994 Code](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title18/pdf/USCODE-1994-title18-partI-chap119-sec2510.pdf), printed pages 441–443, supplies the complete definition and amendment history.

The pre-amendment wire definition separately excluded the radio portion transmitted between a cordless handset and base. Former electronic-definition subparagraph (12)(A) separately excluded that portion; former (12)(B) excluded wire or oral communications, followed by the tone-only-paging and tracking-device exclusions. Public Law 103-414 §202(a) removed the cordless exclusion from the wire definition, removed electronic subparagraph (12)(A), and redesignated the remaining electronic exclusions. It did not amend the oral definition. Section 202(b) separately changed penalty provisions; §203 separately added §2510(16)(F). Those changes must not be collapsed into a claim that every paragraph of the 1994 Code already governed the interception.

The category inquiry must apply each definition as then written. It cannot call all radio transmission electronic by applying the broad introductory language while ignoring the express cordless exclusion. Conversely, exclusion from the wire and electronic categories does not itself prove that the communication satisfies the oral definition's expectation requirement. The electronic exclusion within the oral definition must be tested against the statutory meaning of electronic communication, with the then-operative exclusions. Neither categorical oral coverage nor its categorical impossibility is certified by this repair.

The official Act was approved October 25, 1994. Section 111(b)'s four-year delay expressly concerns Title I §§103 and 105, not Title II §202. Section 111(a) likewise speaks of its own title. The Act contains no express instruction retroactively reclassifying the earlier interceptions for this litigation. The absence of a §202 delay does not itself answer every temporal consequence of its enactment.
