## LeDuc v. State, 365 So. 2d 149 (Fla. 1978)

**Primary text:** https://static.case.law/so2d/365/html/0149-01.html ; particularly 150–152, with the pertinent recommendation standard at 151. No. 47953, November 22, 1978.

LeDuc pleaded guilty to rape and first-degree murder; the advisory jury unanimously recommended death on both offenses, and the judge imposed death. The Florida court previously required written findings and certification concerning psychiatric material. On review of the ensuing record it affirmed. Its account of the statutory system required the advisory jury and the judge to weigh the enumerated considerations.

At page 151 the court applied a standard giving weight to a jury's recommended sentence where all relevant information had been considered: the recommendation should stand absent strong reasons to believe reasonable persons could not agree. It cited Tedder while reviewing a recommendation of death. Thus the state-law materials cannot be described as recognizing weight only for a life recommendation. The condition concerning consideration of relevant information is part of the standard, not an optional qualification. The court separately reviewed the judge's findings and the asserted mitigation. Psychiatric material received by the judge but not the jury did not compel mitigation on that record.

**Limits:** This is a direct-review application with a jury recommendation of death and independent judicial consideration. It does not hold that the judge is bound by that recommendation; it decides no federal retroactivity issue or collateral consequence of a vague aggravating instruction. Its separate propositions concerning presumptions and particular aggravation are not supplied as present federal constitutional rules.

## Messer v. State, 330 So. 2d 137 (Fla. 1976)

**Primary text:** https://static.case.law/so2d/330/html/0137-01.html ; particularly 140–142. No. 46849, March 31, 1976.

The court affirmed the convictions but required a new sentencing proceeding before a jury because relevant penalty evidence was improperly excluded. The excluded matters included the accomplice's comparatively lenient plea disposition and psychiatric testimony the defense sought to present. At page 142 the court rejected the trial judge's view that, because the jury's recommendation was advisory, his own later consideration of psychiatric material made its exclusion from the jury unimportant. The legislature had made the jury an integral part of the sentencing process; the value of its recommendation depended on the information before it, and the sentencing process employed both jury and judge as safeguards.

**Limits:** The decision concerns state statutory sentencing procedure and the admissibility of mitigation. It supports an independent legal role for the jury, but does not itself decide whether every defect in jury consideration violates the Federal Constitution, whether a particular aggravating instruction is vague, whether a judge or appellate court can supply a constitutionally sufficient cure in another case, or whether any federal rule was dictated for Teague purposes. The remedy was new sentencing, not automatic life imprisonment or reversal of the guilt determinations.

## Ross v. State, 386 So. 2d 1191 (Fla. 1980)

**Primary text:** https://static.case.law/so2d/386/html/1191-01.html ; particularly 1197–1198 and the separate writings. No. 52929, June 12, 1980; rehearing denied September 12, 1980.

The court affirmed the convictions, vacated the death sentence, and remanded for resentencing by the judge. The judge had given undue weight to the jury's death recommendation instead of making the required independent reasoned determination. At pages 1197–1198 the court distinguished Tedder's demanding standard for overriding a recommendation of life from LeDuc's treatment of a recommendation of death. It explained that recommendations carry great weight without binding the judge, who must independently weigh aggravation and mitigation under section 921.141(3), notwithstanding the recommendation. That duty includes guarding against an emotionally driven sentencing result. The court also disapproved duplicative aggravation based on the same robbery and pecuniary-gain circumstance; it did not make duplication alone invariably dispositive.

The separate views disputed the sentencing application: one would uphold death because the judge had adequately exercised judgment; another would require life because mitigation outweighed aggravation. They do not eliminate the majority's distinction between weight and binding force.

**Limits:** Ross supplies both sides of the institutional account: a jury recommendation matters, and judicial sentencing must remain independent. The remand was for judicial resentencing on this record. It did not announce a general harmlessness rule for errors in jury instructions or decide collateral federal retroactivity.

## Eutzy v. State, 458 So. 2d 755 (Fla. 1984)

**Primary text:** https://static.case.law/so2d/458/html/0755-01.html ; particularly 757–760 and notes. No. 64212, September 20, 1984; rehearing denied December 3, 1984.

The court affirmed a conviction and a judicial death sentence imposed despite a jury recommendation of life. It rejected robbery as an aggravator on the record, sustained the prior-violent-felony and cold/calculated/premeditated aggravators, and found no mitigation sufficient to support the life recommendation. At page 759 it recognized that the recommendation must receive great weight but cannot displace the judge's independent weighing duty. It applied Tedder's demanding test to the life override and examined the alleged mitigation, including whether a companion could reasonably be regarded as equally culpable. That factual possibility could not simply be assumed.

The treatment of allegedly erroneous penalty instructions at page 757 rested on the jury's favorable life recommendation and the absence of prejudice in that posture. It is not a general statement that an advisory jury may always receive invalid aggravating instructions. An as-applied challenge to the statutory override mechanism was unpreserved. One Justice concurred in guilt and dissented from death without a separate explanatory opinion; no reason should be invented for that disposition.

**Limits:** This is a life-override case, not a holding that a recommendation of death is irrelevant. Its independent-duty language must be read with its great-weight language and the particular Tedder application. It supplies no automatic federal Teague classification or resolution of Lambrix's procedural defenses.
