## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Maryland Court of Special Appeals.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The suppression order remains in force. No remand is authorized for a new justification the State failed to preserve.

### Issue 1: Categorical passenger-exit authority

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the categorical question and rejects it. He does not reach the unpreserved alternative justification.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether officer safety permits a suspicionless order directed to every passenger, rather than an order based on any objective circumstance in the actual stop.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone proposes the narrow rule required by the Fourth Amendment's demand for reasoned seizure. An officer may order a passenger out when any objective circumstance reasonably supports the order for safety, to facilitate a lawful search, or to conduct the stop. The showing is modest and may arise from movements, numbers, location, conduct, or other practical facts. A passenger who committed no traffic offense may not be subjected to an additional command solely because the driver was lawfully stopped. General statistics and administrative ease do not replace an objective reason in the case.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Fourth Amendment does not authorize a categorical passenger-exit order in every traffic stop. An officer must identify an objective circumstance making the order reasonable for safety or lawful investigation. Because Maryland preserved and presented only the categorical rule, the suppression judgment stands.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Mimms rests partly on the driver's own offense and control of the vehicle. A passenger has not supplied that predicate. Officer safety is weighty, and Stone's rule gives officers broad practical latitude without requiring probable cause or a formalized suspicion label. It requires only a reason connected to the stop. That modest discipline prevents Whren's broad stop authority from becoming arbitrary control over millions of passengers. The actual movements and nervousness might support the order, but the State did not preserve that ground and the Court cannot rewrite the litigated case.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not prevent an officer from ordering a passenger out on minimal objective safety grounds, controlling occupants during a lawful search, or arresting on probable cause. It does not decide a command to remain in the vehicle.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone’s narrower passenger-exit rule is an approved proposed extension, not a Whren holding. His affirmance depends on the preserved categorical theory and the supplied lower-court treatment of the observations. Any claim that the particular movements independently justified this order should be addressed only if properly available on this record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

Leave Doe’s alternative arm-status challenge, waiver, abrogation, individual-defendant claims, and contract merits unresolved except to the extent lawfully open below.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal indemnification and immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the narrow granted question. The indemnity issue is legal and dispositive on the assumption that the University otherwise qualifies as an arm of California.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply and clarify simulated *Hess* without changing its multi-consideration inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A third party’s contractual promise to indemnify an otherwise immune state instrumentality for litigation costs or judgments does not divest the instrumentality of Eleventh Amendment immunity. Arm status turns on the entity’s complete legal relationship to the State and its primary legal obligations, not on collateral reimbursement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The University remains the defendant against which judgment would run. The federal contract may require the Department of Energy to reimburse the expense, but that agreement neither substitutes the United States as obligor nor changes California law defining the University’s governmental status. Treating indemnity as dispositive would make constitutional identity depend on insurance and contracting arrangements that can change case by case, contrary to *Hess*’s institutional inquiry.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold treasury exposure irrelevant. Direct state responsibility remains highly probative. He reserves whether the University is in fact an arm of California, whether California waived immunity, whether Congress validly abrogated it, and whether prospective or individual-capacity relief remains available.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the fixed entrance and driveway zones and the cease-and-desist condition governing counselors permitted within those zones; reverse the unconditional person and vehicle floating zones; vacate the automatic fifteen-foot retreat outside the fixed zones; remand.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The district court may retain the fixed zones and the limited counseling exception. It shall strike the unconditional floating zones and the automatic fifteen-foot retreat outside the fixed zones. On remand it may adopt a narrower provision that preserves an initial peaceful approach but, after an objectively clear verbal or nonverbal refusal, requires the enjoined defendants to stop following and maintain a modest, precisely defined separation supported by the record.

### Issue 1: Fixed zones and the counseling exception

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the fixed zones and the conditions governing counselors whom the injunction permits to enter them.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** The fixed zones are tied to repeated obstruction and crowding at identified entrances and driveways, failed lesser commands, and impaired access. Fifteen feet provides a stable access corridor while leaving visible and audible advocacy nearby. The two-counselor exception may be conditioned on ending the approach after an objectively clear refusal so the exception does not recreate the crowding and pursuit the zone remedies.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The fixed entrance and driveway zones and the cease-and-desist condition governing counselors admitted within them satisfy Madsen on this record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The findings connect the fixed geography to the actual places where access failed. Protesters retain signs, leaflets, chanting, and conversation immediately outside. The counseling exception expands access beyond the clear-zone baseline, and its refusal condition is a conduct-based limit on that exception rather than a general right to avoid unwelcome public speech.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not validate a larger unsupported zone or create a general listener's veto on a public sidewalk.

### Issue 2: Floating zones and the post-refusal condition outside fixed zones

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches both the unconditional floating zones and the linked automatic retreat requirement.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** A mobile fifteen-foot zone around every person or vehicle is operationally uncertain, prevents ordinary conversational distance and leaflet transfer, and burdens peaceful speakers far beyond the access points. The record does support a narrower response to persistent close pursuit by these adjudicated parties: after an objectively clear verbal or nonverbal refusal, the injunction may require them to stop following and maintain a modest, specifically defined separation. Refusal ends targeted pursuit; it does not silence stationary or generally addressed advocacy.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The unconditional person and vehicle floating zones and automatic fifteen-foot retreat burden more speech than necessary. On remand, the injunction may preserve an initial peaceful approach yet require the enjoined defendants, after an objectively clear refusal, to stop following and maintain a modest, precisely defined separation supported by the record.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** Direct conduct rules can address obstruction, touching, threats, and harassment without forcing every speaker to calculate a moving fifteen-foot boundary. At the same time, the record of repeated close pursuit permits a precise post-refusal disengagement rule. Severance and narrowing preserve complete relief without converting refusal into a general power to suppress advocacy.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding creates no undefined right to personal space, no automatic fifteen-foot distance, and no general listener's veto. It restricts neither nonparties, willing conversation, an initial peaceful approach, stationary advocacy, nor speech addressed generally.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone may preserve his approved distinction between fixed corridors, unconstitutional automatic floating zones, and a narrower post-refusal remedy. Madsen makes room for independently lawful, precisely limited conduct relief but does not command his proposed replacement distance or cessation rule. Those additional Schenck instructions are his own proposed application to the supported pursuit evidence, with stationary and generally addressed advocacy preserved.

## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal claims.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** No federal remedial instruction is required. Any remedy for violation of state law or the School Committee's positive-subscription policy remains outside the granted federal questions.

### Issue 1: Parental liberty, student privacy, procedural due process, and Free Exercise

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the pleaded constitutional theories. No personal participation, compelled affirmation, bodily intrusion, or religiously forbidden act is alleged. Assuming the written positive-subscription policy created a state-law interest, the procedural claim fails because the plaintiffs did not preserve an argument that Massachusetts postdeprivation remedies were inadequate. Stone does not decide whether the planned departure from the policy was random and unauthorized. Because no constitutional violation is established, Stone does not reach qualified immunity.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether compulsory attendance at this school-selected AIDS-awareness presentation, without compelled participation or affirmation, violated the identified federal rights.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Meyer and Pierce protect parental choice among lawful educational paths and parental instruction outside school; they do not give each parent authority to prescribe the lessons taught in a chosen public school. The students were required to attend, but not to perform a skit, affirm a message, engage in conduct, disclose private information, or undergo bodily intrusion. Exposure to offensive curricular speech creates no freestanding constitutional privacy right. The attendance rule was neutral and generally applicable, and no religious exercise was prohibited, penalized, or conditioned on abandoning belief.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Parental liberty secures parents' choice among lawful educational paths and their own instruction; it does not confer a constitutional right to prescribe or opt out of each public-school curricular presentation reasonably related to a legitimate pedagogical objective. Compulsory exposure to ideas, without compelled participation, affirmation, religious conduct, or bodily intrusion, violates neither student privacy nor Free Exercise. Assuming the positive-subscription policy created a state-law interest, failure to preserve a challenge to available postdeprivation remedies defeats the procedural-due-process claim.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The program may have been crude and the alleged policy violation may support state-law relief. Federal liability still requires an identified constitutional deprivation. Listening is not compelled profession, public-school enrollment is not a waiver of parental instruction outside school, and compulsory attendance does not make every idea a bodily or religious imposition. The unpreserved challenge to state remedies prevents the Court from converting the local policy dispute into a federal procedural judgment.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not authorize compelled profession or participation, bodily intrusion, religious establishment or targeting, discrimination because of sex, pretextual curricular action, or disregard of state law without the remedies that source provides.

### Issue 2: Title IX

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the missing statutory element and does not reach severity or pervasiveness.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Neither the presentation nor the alleged later ridicule was imposed on the plaintiffs because of sex. Sexual subject matter, without sex-based targeting, exclusion, or hostility, is not Title IX discrimination.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The complaint fails under Title IX because it alleges no discrimination, exclusion, or hostile educational condition imposed on the plaintiffs because of sex.
- **Stone's Reasoning and Record Application:** The presentation was delivered to the student audience generally, and the named students were not selected for the skits. The missing because-of-sex element disposes of the claim without announcing a broader recipient-liability rule.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not govern sex-specific humiliation, school-authorized sexual targeting, or deliberate indifference to known sex-based harassment.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Questions 1–2 — preserved approved answers.** Stone would affirm dismissal because parental educational liberty does not authorize control over each reasonably related public-school lesson, and these plaintiffs were not compelled to perform, affirm, disclose intimate information or undergo bodily invasion. He does not approve the presenter’s alleged conduct as sound pedagogy or decide rights of students personally subjected to a materially different act. A breached local permission policy does not itself create the requested federal substantive right.

**Question 3 — preserved approved answer.** The plaintiffs’ complaint lacks a condition imposed on them because of sex, rather than sexual content offensive to them. Stone would dismiss on that element without inventing a comprehensive Title IX damages or harassment framework unnecessary to the outcome. The source-approved procedural and religion dispositions remain separately preserved.

### Issue 1: Off-exchange foreign-currency options fall within the exclusion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches statutory authority before the fraud allegations or remedy.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Commodity Exchange Act’s Treasury Amendment excluded off-exchange options to buy or sell foreign currency from CFTC regulation as “transactions in foreign currency.”
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary textual and structural interpretation; do not repair a perceived regulatory gap judicially.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Treasury Amendment’s exclusion of off-exchange “transactions in foreign currency” includes options whose underlying commodity is foreign currency, subject to the amendment’s board-of-trade proviso.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** In ordinary market language, the option is a transaction in the commodity whose fluctuating value gives the contract its substance. A narrower reading threatens to make the recognized futures exclusion textually unstable and reduces the proviso’s work. The alleged fraud may demonstrate a need for legislation, but consequence cannot create agency jurisdiction the words do not supply.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not protect fraud, decide the reach of other statutes or state law, or govern transactions conducted on a board of trade within the proviso.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Hold that materiality is not an element, then permit the Eighth Circuit to determine whether the indictment, falsity instruction, preservation, or another independent trial error requires further proceedings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The Government may present the statutory argument

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone resolves preservation before merits because respondents assert it as a bar.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether materiality is an element of 18 U.S.C. § 1014, and whether preservation, invited-error, indictment, or instruction issues prevent the Government from obtaining review after it previously treated materiality as an element.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary preservation rules; do not use invited error to freeze a legal element that the appellate court actually decided.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Government may argue that § 1014 lacks a materiality element where that statutory question was presented to and decided by the court of appeals and is included in the certiorari question, notwithstanding its earlier instructions under then-prevailing circuit law.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendants had notice and a full opportunity to litigate the issue. The Court corrects law, not counsel’s consistency. Any case-specific prejudice from the indictment or instructions remains distinct and can be considered below.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether the Government may raise an issue never presented below or whether the actual instructions were harmless.

### Issue 2: Materiality is not an element of § 1014

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the element question because it controls the Gaudin premise.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether materiality is an element of 18 U.S.C. § 1014, and whether preservation, invited-error, indictment, or instruction issues prevent the Government from obtaining review after it previously treated materiality as an element.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply enacted text and statutory structure. Gaudin is fully preserved for statutes that actually contain materiality.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1014 does not require proof that the charged false statement was material. The Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the defendant knowingly made a false statement or report for the purpose of influencing the identified financial institution or transaction.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress used “any false statement,” expressly required purpose to influence, and omitted materiality while using it in neighboring provisions and some predecessor statutes. Adding the element would not be interpretation of an established term; it would be judicial supplementation. The severe penalty cannot create an omitted element when the text is clear, though it reinforces the need to enforce knowledge, falsity, purpose, charging restraint, and sentencing law accurately.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide the independent effect of alleging materiality in this indictment, whether the instruction distorted the jury’s falsity or purpose findings, or whether any error was harmless.
