# 39. Ehrlich v. City of Culver City

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether the essential-nexus and rough-proportionality requirements established by Nollan v. California Coastal Commission and Dolan v. City of Tigard apply to monetary exactions imposed as conditions of approving a particular land-development proposal.

2. Whether a municipality may require a property owner to pay recreational-facility mitigation fees as a condition of redevelopment without demonstrating a sufficient relationship between the development's anticipated impacts and the particular financial burden imposed.

3. What distinguishes individualized monetary permit conditions subject to heightened takings scrutiny from generally applicable taxes, assessments, and regulatory fees.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

Redevelopment of a defunct private recreation facility for townhouses carried a $280,000 recreation charge and a $33,200 public-art condition; a separate $30,000 park fee was not challenged. California's Supreme Court returned the recreation fee for justification and sustained the art condition. Its reasoning was fractured. The state court accepted a nexus to the rezoning's loss of the recreation use but found the amount insufficiently supported. The art ordinance allowed an approved work kept on the project site instead of a payment or donation; the successor used that onsite option while the preserved challenge continued. The owner contests the charges; the City invokes replacement recreational needs and its general arts program. Review is of the California judgment and its remand, not direct review of a completed taking or a compensation award. Under §1257, the federal question must be sufficiently final under the applicable exception and not defeated by an adequate independent state ground.

Lower-court opinion extraction (12 Cal.4th 854, March 5, 1996; reproduced in the petition appendix): Arabian, Lucas and George supply the lead opinion. Mosk concurs in the judgment, expressly agrees with Parts V and VI, and emphasizes that heightened scrutiny ordinarily does not cover general development fees, but may cover an individualized ad hoc permit exaction. Werdegar concurs in the judgment and the lead reasoning except Part II on the Mitigation Fee Act, which she declines to construe. Kennard, joined by Baxter, agrees on the art condition and on applying the Nollan/Dolan inquiry to individualized monetary conditions, but would reject the recreation fee entirely; she rejects charging the owner for replacing a voluntarily furnished private benefit or for the City's costs of obtaining alternative private facilities. The lead opinion says the City may charge a supported amount reflecting costs of inducing comparable private recreational opportunity, such as appropriate redesignation costs or lawful incentives; it rejects measuring the charge by the value of public replacement facilities. Its remand requires the City to determine whether and to what extent any recreation fee is justified, with findings supported by substantial evidence and no exact mathematical requirement. The separate $30,000 park fee is outside the case. The public art option allowed art on the project site; the successor selected that option.
