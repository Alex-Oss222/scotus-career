## Question and actual procedural scope

The granted municipal hiring question asks whether Sheriff Moore's selection of reserve deputy Stacy Burns can establish the County's own policy, fault and causal responsibility under §1983 for the constitutional injury to Jill Brown. The County seeks relief from municipal liability; Brown seeks preservation of her judgment. An adjudication must distinguish that reviewed hiring issue from the distinct training and state-law grounds actually returned by the jury. Their presence does not enlarge the grant, but it prevents an unexplained inference that rejecting hiring alone necessarily eliminates every basis of County liability.

The operative Fifth Circuit opinion is **67 F.3d 1174, October 23, 1995**, which withdrew **53 F.3d 1410** in full. Rehearing was denied November 29, 1995. The substituted opinion sustained the federal hiring basis without analyzing the separate training verdict; the withdrawn opinion's training analysis is not an operative appellate ruling. The County argues that its rejected-training analysis was correct and that its deletion did not create an independent basis for affirmance. Brown argues that the undisturbed training verdict independently supports the judgment and was not properly challenged in the petition. These are opposed positions on the actual review and remedy questions, not predetermined resolutions.

Moore and Morrison received pretrial judgments on December 9, 1992; the Eighth Amendment claim against Burns was also dismissed. The surviving claims included the Fourth Amendment claims against Burns and municipal hiring, training and state claims. The intentional-infliction claim was dismissed in December. This municipal review does not reopen those dismissed defendants or silently retry the established underlying officer violations.

## Incident and proof categories

On May 12, 1991, the Browns traveled from Texas toward their Oklahoma home. Todd Brown turned away from the checkpoint near Cartwright and returned across Denison Dam into Texas. Morrison and Burns followed, and the stop occurred in Grayson County, Texas. Speed, the Browns' perception of pursuit, compliance with commands and Jill Brown's movements were disputed. On her account she offered no threat and raised her other hand while trying to comply. Burns used an arm-bar extraction, brought her to the ground and handcuffed her; she also described being raised by the handcuff chain. Her knees sustained serious injury, with four operations and evidence of future bilateral replacement. Detention estimates differed, and she received no charge or citation. Weapons found later do not supply Burns with preforce knowledge he did not possess.

The plaintiff's expert called an arm-bar a low-level physical extraction method while disputing its necessity and the manner in which it was used here. That description is not a finding that this force was lawful. The underlying jury findings and the separate municipal inquiries must each receive their proper effect; the mere occurrence of an officer violation does not prove municipal fault or cause.

## Verdict, judgment and remaining grounds

The April 19, 1993 verdict found that Burns falsely arrested Brown, lacked qualified immunity for that arrest, used excessive force, lacked qualified immunity for that force, and falsely imprisoned her. It then answered four separate County questions affirmatively:

| Interrogatory | Ground actually found |
|---|---|
| 6 | Deliberately indifferent hiring policy in the case of Burns, instituted by Moore |
| 7 | Deliberately indifferent training policy in the case of Burns, instituted by Moore |
| 8 | Negligent hiring through Moore |
| 9 | Negligent training through Moore |

The verdict did not separately find the number of earlier assaults, identify which criminal entry Moore actually read, or select a particular causal explanation. The accompanying instructions required inadequate hiring or training, deliberate indifference and direct causation for the federal claims; the state claims required negligence and proximate cause. Question 10 asked for damages proximately caused by Burns or by the County acting through Moore. Do not treat these separate federal and state findings as one undifferentiated hiring verdict.

The fifteen compensatory items total **$927,302**. The district court removed **$36,000** for past income and **$180,000** for future earning capacity, leaving **$711,302** actual damages. Its signed September 20, 1993 judgment made that actual award and **$65,000 trial attorney's fees jointly and severally payable by the County and Burns**. It imposed **$20,000 punitive damages on Burns alone**, with 3.43% annual interest from judgment and costs. It separately provided joint and several conditional appellate fees: $12,500 for a Fifth Circuit appeal, $5,000 for making or responding to a certiorari application, and $5,000 if certiorari was granted. Thus $87,500 is the combined trial and all specified appellate fee figure, not the trial fee alone. The actual itemized verdict and signed judgment govern over compressed or inconsistent totals in counsel's narratives.

The Fifth Circuit affirmed the judgment, including Burns's liability, immunity and punitive issues, while sustaining County liability on hiring. On the earnings cross-appeal, defendants' earlier Rule 50 motion did not raise the damages insufficiency ground; nevertheless Brown's failure to object meant plain-error review, under which the reduction stood. This distinct preservation history supplies no waiver of the County's municipal sufficiency and charge objections.

The district court applied Oklahoma tort law by comity to Moore's Oklahoma selection and deployment acts. It rejected §155(5)'s discretionary-function defense because it viewed the particular selection and deployment as operational implementation rather than protected initial planning. It rejected §155(28)'s recognized-standards defense because it found conformity with such standards unproved. Neither conclusion is a finding that Burns violated a particular state certification bar. The Fifth Circuit did not reach the state issues after sustaining federal liability. Any final mandate must account for the surviving training and state findings and the actual extent of review before directing judgment, further proceedings, or relief from particular monetary components.

## Criminal history: what is proved, disputed and unavailable

Schweizer's testimony enumerated one assault-and-battery conviction, nine traffic convictions, false identification, public drunkenness, expired tags, suspended-license violations and an alcohol-related actual-physical-control offense. Actual physical control is not automatically driving while intoxicated. A resisting-arrest charge must not be relabeled a conviction.

Burns described a September 1989 campus confrontation: members of a fraternity-pledge group struck his car, he confronted them, and pushing followed. He acknowledged the assault plea in January 1990 and described only one other lifetime fight, in his high-school freshman year. That is his account, not a jury finding that his conduct was justified. The related public-drunkenness and false-ID matters were described as arising from the campus episode; counsel expressly acknowledged uncertainty whether the traffic offenses arose then or earlier. His grandfather's arrangement to dispose of cases together to save trips does not establish that every offense occurred in one episode. Counsel's out-of-court statements were admitted, where allowed, to explain actions rather than prove their truth.

The OSBI printout supplies a separate notice question. Barnes read a February 25, 1989 alcohol/APC entry; September 7, 1989 assault, resisting and public-drunkenness entries; and January 4, 1990 entries and dispositions. **Four printed assault references were read aloud.** Barnes explained that some could be duplicated references to the same charge. Counsel suggested separate complainants; Barnes allowed that possibility but said multiple counts ordinarily receive a distinct designation. One assault conviction was expressly enumerated. The respondent's two-count characterization, the four printed references and Burns's campus account therefore cannot become four proved violent attacks, or an uncontested single harmless episode. Exhibit 29's complete original image is absent from the supplied appendix; a decisive claim about its precise layout would require that image.

Probation materials described nonpayment, community-service failures and alleged further offenses. Conditions included avoiding offenses, criminal associates and intoxicants, paying the fine and completing three days' community service. Burns admitted an outstanding warrant on the incident date while claiming not to know he was on probation. Testimony suggesting that undercover work replaced punishment was struck with an instruction to disregard it; it supplies no established arrangement. Moore testified that the NCIC check disclosed no outstanding warrant. Existence of a warrant is not proof that NCIC disclosed it to him.

The County maintained objections to criminal-history evidence as prejudicial, outside state standards and insufficiently causal. The court admitted the history for the propriety of permitting Burns to serve, **not as propensity proof that he committed the acts at issue on May 12**; it repeated a limiting instruction concerning probation. Counsel's later credibility arguments cannot enlarge the admitted purpose.

## Moore's knowledge, screening, training and authority

Burns was **the son of Moore's nephew**; his grandfather had worked with the department for sixteen years. Moore described limited contact with Burns while he was growing up. He knew of fast driving, tickets and an actual-physical-control arrest, but denied knowledge of fighting or an aggressive reputation. Family relationship does not itself prove knowledge of every offense.

Moore obtained a driving history and an NCIC check; the record describes an OSBI printout and a May 8, 1991 applicant entry. Barnes could not tell whether that entry documented a request for the report or submission of applicant information. Moore acknowledged noticing or probably noticing suspensions, denied noticing the assault, false-ID and resisting entries, and made no further disposition or probation inquiry. The lower majority's inference that he closed his eyes or knowingly disregarded the risk for a relative is an inference from the record, not a special jury finding that he read and understood every entry.

Schweizer relied on the combined recent course over roughly two and a half years as showing risks of abuse and excessive force and a need for better screening. His opinion was not that one old traffic ticket invariably predicts unconstitutional force. He conceded aspects of state eligibility while criticizing the hiring decision. Barnes agreed that the record warranted concern and inquiry and said he doubted he would have hired Burns; he did not give an unconditional never-hire opinion. Whether this proof establishes the required constitutional risk rather than general unfitness remains the legal and evidentiary dispute.

The CLEET custodian's record placed both application and commencement of training on **May 6, 1991**, six days before the incident. Barnes withdrew his earlier six-to-seven-week estimate. Respondent's at-most-three-class or possibly-no-class argument is an inference from scheduling and uncertain recollection, not a finding of the exact classes attended. Burns had no prior patrol experience. His trial account added LETN broadcasts to his earlier description of no County training. The petitioner cites trial pages 579–580 for ride-along instruction by Howell and Calclazier; the respondent contests its adequacy. The appendix supports absence of roadblock, interstate-pursuit and Texas-arrest instruction. Brief recitations of trial pages omitted from the appendix remain identified recitations, not independently reread transcript pages.

Burns could not carry a firearm or drive the patrol car. Moore said he could arrest only while a deputy was present and directed him to arrest or assist, with observation and learning as his role. Burns said the accompanying officer was in charge, but also said he received no particular direction for the pursuit, approach, extraction, takedown or handcuffing and believed Moore had authorized his actions. The record supports neither an uncontested license to act without limits nor an undisputed prohibition of all coercive conduct.

The respondent cites trial pages 628 and 394 for five takedowns among thirteen earlier arrests; the reply stresses prior arm-bar uses without complaints. A hearsay objection sustained to a different question at JA44–45 does not establish exclusion of all later testimony. Those later pages are supplied through counsel's recitations. Postselection activity does not automatically establish preselection knowledge. The County's account of no prior police-abuse complaints during Moore's tenure is distinct from Brown's account of prior checkpoint delay or harassment complaints; do not compress these into no complaint of any kind ever.

Other deputies had undergone adequate checks and certification. Oklahoma's quoted eligibility provisions contain a **felony or moral-turpitude** bar, psychological evaluation and suitability certification, education requirements and application to reserves. Trial testimony also addressed minimum age, fingerprints/background investigation, certification within one year and annual instruction. No felony alone does not prove compliance with every condition. The timing of employment-status notice and other paperwork was contested. Conversely, the expert's asserted duty to arrest on an outstanding warrant and the district court's recognized-standards ruling are not automatic findings of a state certification violation. State compliance is evidence to assess separately from its disputed federal effect.

## Instructions, objections and attribution

The stipulation identifies Moore as the County policymaker regarding the Sheriff's Department. The charge identifies his acts as final official policy and separately requires inadequacy, deliberate indifference and direct causation. For hiring, it asks whether closer scrutiny was obviously needed and the inadequate scrutiny was so likely to result in constitutional violations that Moore could reasonably be called deliberately indifferent. The exact saved wording is copied below, preserving the charge's use of constitutional needs rather than silently rewriting it as a different formula.

The County objected under Rule 51 to an undefined custom; treatment of the single Burns hire as policy; failure to identify a specific hiring or training deficiency and its causal connection; lack of a pattern; disregard of state standards and the requested good-faith defense; the formulation of questions 6 and 7; and use of needs instead of rights. It sought a general-policy formulation and objected to the assigned-authority account and several arrest and pursuit instructions. Those objections were overruled. The actual transcript does not support an assertion that the County made no municipal-policy or causation objection.

The lower majority treated Moore's final-policymaker identity as stipulated and the description of his status as unobjected to. The petitioner and reply contest both scope and preservation, quoting the earlier appellate brief and referring to the actual charge objections. **Who possesses final authority, the sphere of that authority, whether a particular choice constitutes policy, and whether policy caused this injury with sufficient fault are separate inquiries.** The objection to one hire as policy is not automatically an objection to every aspect of the policymaker stipulation. Nor does the stipulation alone establish every municipal element. Any narrower preservation ruling must identify the particular claim and the actual language supporting it.

The County moved under Rule 50 at the close of plaintiff's proof on municipal sufficiency, policy, pattern, causation, standards and state exemptions; the judge denied the federal request and took state matters under advisement. The petitioner reports renewal at the close of all proof. The September order addressed municipal sufficiency, the single-incident objection, the two state exemptions and Burns's immunity. Thus a clarified-rule remedy must distinguish a preserved legal sufficiency question from instructional error and from an unpreserved newly formulated objection; no category is decided merely by labeling all of them causation.

## Entering law and balanced paths

Use [the exact slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK7_74_ENTERING_LAW.md). **Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation**, effective Collins, February 26, 1992, separates constitutional injury from municipal attribution and rejects an additional freestanding governmental-abuse element. **Municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules**, effective Leatherman, March 3, 1993, governs pleading, not trial sufficiency. **Municipal liability defenses and connected interlocutory review**, effective Swint, March 1, 1995, preserves the county/officer distinction and does not add a merits immunity. The exact register qualifications and dates are copied.

Plakas, January 9, 1995, is an eight-Justice municipal holding: cognizable federal deprivation, municipal attribution, applicable culpability and causation remain separately necessary; reasonable final force does not answer every independent municipal wrong. Its rejected equipment/training theory establishes no universal hiring rule. Nabozny, March 10, 1997, unanimously preserves municipal attribution and causation apart from the individual's wrongdoing. Blessing's April 21 statutory-rights decision does not replace Monell's constitutional municipal framework or overrule §1983. Its noncontrolling funding-contract reservation is not a county hiring rule.

Monell bars employer-based vicarious liability; Pembaur permits a final policymaker's single deliberate choice to constitute policy in appropriate circumstances, without making every employee act policy. Canton rejects a universal rule limiting liability to policies unconstitutional on their face, while demanding municipal deliberate indifference and an actual close causal link for failure-to-train liability. Its objective municipal inquiry remains distinct from the employee's underlying constitutional culpability. Tuttle guards against inferring a culpable policy merely from a single employee violation.

## Both paths at their strongest; no outcome selected

**County's argument:** Monell forbids liability based on employment; Tuttle demands independent policy and causal proof; Pembaur's direct official order differs from this facially lawful hire; Canton requires municipal fault and a very close constitutional causal connection. The County disputes both single-hire attribution and applicant-specific risk. Its evidence includes the campus account, ambiguous printout entries, state eligibility, the training and supervision precautions, prior checks for other hires and the absence of comparable abuse complaints. General aggression can include lawful police action; a mere potential disposition, more prudent investigation or but-for employment cannot alone establish the County's own constitutional responsibility. Federalism supports a bounded §1983 construction but does not create an already-established municipal immunity. The reply conditionally contemplates exceptionally serious known violent history certain to recur if single-hire liability is legally possible; that hypothetical neither describes Burns nor concedes the respondent's proposed threshold.

**Brown's argument:** A deliberate choice by the official with final authority can itself be County action, and this case has proof beyond the employee injury. She relies on the combined preemployment history, available inquiries, Moore's asserted disregard, preparation for foreseeable coercive duties and the relation of the very risk ignored to the injury suffered. Requiring a second hire or a prior on-duty victim in every case would exclude the first injury despite adequate preemployment notice. She disclaims respondeat superior, simple negligence, all bad hires and every act outside the policymaker's sphere. Her causation argument requires the particular kind of constitutional injury threatened, distinguishing violence from unrelated discrimination, sexual misconduct or retaliation. She reads Canton's moral-certainty example as addressing recurring duties requiring judgment, not requiring that a constitutional injury be intended or inevitable. The body of her brief accepts deliberate indifference; its footnote alternative that demanding causation might dispense with separate fault is a separate submission, not governing law. State minimum rules do not require hiring every eligible applicant and do not displace federal rights.

**Affirmance path:** The final authorized choice, complete favorable proof of known or obvious risk, deliberate screening failure and actual assigned duties could support the jury's findings under the correct municipal standard. The strongest objection is that proof of general unfitness or negligent investigation may still fall short of a particular constitutional risk and close causation. Jury deference does not answer a legal-standard deficiency.

**Hiring-insufficiency path:** Even crediting favorable proof and reasonable inferences, the identified applicant-specific record may fail the necessary constitutional risk and close causal link. The strongest objection is that isolating a misdemeanor or crediting Burns's explanations as settled fact may improperly discard the cumulative course, printout notice, expert testimony and jury-supported inference. A rule may not demand intent while calling the standard deliberate indifference. Any relief must separately account for training, state liability and the grant's limits.

**Clarified-standard path:** If the charge or appellate approach materially differs from the permissible hiring-specific rule, lawful further proceedings may be considered. The strongest objection is that a full evidentiary opportunity plus insufficiency may require judgment on the affected claim rather than another chance to prove it. Conversely, a new articulation of the rule, actual Rule 50/51 preservation and independent surviving grounds may affect the remedy. The packet does not choose among those procedural consequences.

A single final choice is neither categorically immune nor sufficient without the necessary elements. No path can derive municipal liability solely from the proposition that the injury would not have happened without employment. No path can erase Burns's independently established wrong or assign his punitive award to the County.
