## Common legal propositions eligible for direct joins

**A. Same-sex coverage.** Title VII's prohibition of discrimination in employment terms or conditions because of an individual's sex does not exclude harassment because its perpetrator and victim have the same sex. The employee must still prove discrimination because of sex and every other applicable element; common sex establishes neither automatic liability nor automatic immunity. The text protects an individual and describes the prohibited reason for disadvantage, without an opposite-sex-perpetrator requirement. Meritor establishes that discriminatory harassment can alter employment conditions, and Harris applies that protection without making it depend on a particular victim/harasser sex pairing. Garcia's contrary categorical rule cannot govern the remand. No general pronouncement about the precedential status of alternative lower-court holdings is required.

**B. Proof of the prohibited reason.** Sexual attraction or desire may be evidence of sex discrimination, but neither is a necessary condition of liability. Sex-directed hostility, differential treatment, discriminatory statements, the character of threats, or other relevant circumstances may support the required inference; these are illustrative ways of proving the statutory fact, not exclusive channels, mandatory corroboration categories or automatic presumptions. Sexual content or humiliating conduct alone does not dispense with causation. The employee retains the ultimate burden under the governing proof rules. Hicks permits circumstantial proof without a special additional category and does not require the McDonnell Douglas framework when the claim can be proved otherwise. No finding of Oncale's or the alleged actors' orientation, attraction or motive is made.

**C. Single-sex workplaces and actionable conditions.** An all-male or all-female workplace does not itself bar a sex-discrimination claim or require an actual opposite-sex coworker as a comparator. Context may support or weaken an inference of discrimination and bears on the severity inquiry, but workplace custom does not make sex discrimination lawful. Under Harris, the employee must show sufficiently severe or pervasive sex-based conduct that alters employment conditions, is objectively hostile or abusive to a reasonable person in the circumstances, and is actually perceived as abusive. Frequency, severity, physical threat, humiliation and work interference are relevant without any one being indispensable. Neither medical injury, treatment, diagnosis nor measurable loss of productivity is required. Repeated threats and bodily assaults, if proved, must be evaluated as such rather than automatically treated as ordinary workplace banter; their proof and all remaining statutory requirements are for lawful proceedings below.

**D. Attribution and remaining theories.** Removing the categorical bar does not establish Sundowner's responsibility. Andrade requires an actual connection between delegated supervisory authority and creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment; supervisory title alone is insufficient and lack of higher-management notice alone is not a complete defense. An express employment threat, completed personnel action or economic loss is not invariably required for that agency route. Negligence concerning known or reasonably knowable harassment remains a distinct route. The lower court must apply that current law if attribution is reached. The quid-pro-quo theory requires its own supported showing that an employment benefit or detriment was conditioned on sexual participation; the record's label and allegations of a supervisor's abuse do not decide it. The Court imposes no strict liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker liability rule or unproved employment condition.

**E. Bounded disposition.** Reverse the judgment insofar as it rests on the categorical same-sex exclusion and remand for ordinary proceedings on the remaining issues, including the claims against Sundowner and Lyons to the extent that is their challenged ground. Leave Pippen and Johnson's independently grounded not-an-employer dismissal undisturbed because that separate ruling is outside the presented scope, without affirming a new substantive individual-liability rule. The remand determines neither liability nor entitlement to trial, constructive discharge, factual sufficiency, a damages amount, new discovery, or the availability of particular remedies for the 1991 conduct. Lyons's own statutory status remains undecided; restoration of a claim against him at the rejected ground does not hold that a supervisor is personally a statutory employer. Properly preserved independent defenses and ordinary procedural limits remain, but no missing preservation fact or new defense is manufactured.

## Enhanced comparison of supported alternatives

### Categorical coverage and a possible narrower affirmance

**Path 1 — reverse the same-sex exclusion:** The enacted provision protects each individual against conditions imposed because of sex, and Meritor/Harris already recognize harassment as a prohibited alteration of those conditions. No text restricts the perpetrator's sex. **Strongest contrary argument:** Title VII concerns discrimination between sexes rather than all sexual violence or same-sex hostility; the allegations might describe vulgarity, personal animosity or misconduct without sex discrimination. All eight accept the distinction but conclude that it calls for applying the because-of-sex element, not a categorical immunity. Their own Harris/Andrade/Brown commitments preserve that distinction. Ginsburg's Harris equality explanation and Scalia's refusal to add an administratively easier work-loss prerequisite independently reinforce the rejection of a nontextual gate; Breyer's Andrade authorship supports correction of a legal bar while leaving application unresolved.

**Path 2 — affirm Garcia's categorical rule:** The strongest support is concern that abandoning an opposite-sex model would turn any sexual act into employment discrimination. The statutory sex-causation requirement and Harris's objective/subjective alteration-of-conditions rule answer that concern without deleting same-sex claims. Each Justice rejects Path 2 at that legal level. No date-eligible own writing in the authorized record supplies a contrary requirement of an opposite-sex perpetrator.

**Narrower affirmance on another element:** It is legally possible for a same-sex claim to fail for causation, severity, attribution, defendant status or another properly preserved ground. It is not supported here as a substitute disposition against Sundowner merely because the appellate court never needed to adjudicate those issues. The lower court's categorical decision and assumed allegations do not establish an independent evidentiary failure. All eight refuse to invent a failure, a factual concession, an unpreserved defense or an appellate sufficiency record. The independent coworker-employer dismissal is different: it is expressly recorded and remains outside the granted review rather than being erased with Garcia.

### Sexual desire versus proof by other evidence

**Path 1 — no desire prerequisite:** Sex discrimination may be hostile or punitive rather than desirous. Ordinary evidence of discriminatory targeting and the surrounding circumstances can establish the statutory reason. **Strongest objection:** Without desire or opposite-sex comparison, the inference may become speculation from the act's sexual form. The common rule demands proof of the prohibited reason and permits competing non-sex explanations; it does not command the inference. Hicks's accepted no-extra-category rule and the Justices' Brown joins support keeping evidence flexible while causation remains necessary. Ginsburg relies additionally on her Harris equality analysis; Thomas and Scalia require particularly explicit preservation of the causation burden, but that qualification is shared rather than a vote withheld from the proposition.

**Path 2 — require desire, homosexuality or a specified comparator:** Desire can make a particular inference stronger, and differential evidence can be useful. Those evidentiary points do not justify making them indispensable. A rule that the harassment was actionable only if one participant were gay or bisexual would add an orientation condition absent from the sex-discrimination text and require an unnecessary adjudication of identity. All eight reject that mandatory condition. Their agreement does not hold that orientation discrimination as such is covered by Title VII; Nabozny's separate equal-protection ruling cannot supply that proposition.

### All-male workplace and the severity inquiry

**Path 1 — apply the existing totality standard:** The composition, relationships and ordinary setting inform the objective assessment and the sex-causation inquiry without replacing either. Harris supplies severe or pervasive, not severe and pervasive, and no medical-injury or work-loss gate. **Strongest objection:** Courts might impose inappropriate etiquette expectations without understanding a worksite's interactions. Context answers that concern, but physical threats and coercive bodily conduct cannot be automatically discounted as harmless solely because the site employs men. All eight accept this distinction. O'Connor's Harris opinion, Scalia and Ginsburg's distinct concurrences, the remaining Harris joins, and Breyer's Andrade application preserve the same statutory rule.

**Path 2 — categorical single-sex workplace exclusion or conclusive inference from sexual violence:** These opposite shortcuts each erase an independent statutory inquiry. Absence of an opposite-sex employee does not make discrimination logically impossible; severity alone does not establish why conduct occurred. Brown's mixed-audience analysis supports considering composition as evidence, not a defense, but its Title IX holding remains only an analogy here. All eight reject both shortcuts. No Justice commits to a finding that Oncale has already satisfied causation, objective hostility or subjective abuse.

### Employer responsibility and the mandate

**Correct the coverage rule and return unresolved elements:** Andrade independently requires an actual delegated-authority inquiry if that issue is reached. A supervisor's ability to direct the job may matter even without a completed employment action or notice upstairs, but status alone does not decide it. A claim that supervisors necessarily act privately during abuse would erase the agency route; an automatic employer-liability rule would erase its limiting predicate. All eight had directly joined Andrade, and Breyer authored it. They preserve both the agency and negligence routes without finding either satisfied here.

**Enter liability or order trial now:** The seriousness of allegations does not replace proof; reversal of the legal bar does not decide that every other defense fails or that a particular trial is required. All eight reject this disposition. The quid-pro-quo label does not establish an employment condition, and a hostile-environment theory does not acquire that additional prerequisite merely because both were pleaded. The correct bounded remand preserves these distinct questions and the independently grounded coworker dismissals.
