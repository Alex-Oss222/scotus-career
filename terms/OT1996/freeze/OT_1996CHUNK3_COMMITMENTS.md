## 25. Chamber of Commerce v. Reich

### Reach, components, and common supported ground

The established merits grant under §1254 concerns the D.C. Circuit's reversal of the district court's dismissal and alternative merits ruling. The employer organizations challenge the implementing Secretary. The live restriction and subsequent implementing regulations permit adjudication of prospective officer relief without inventing an amended APA cause or suing the President directly.

The following four components define the exact common ground offered for joining:

**C1 — jurisdiction and equitable route.** Section 1331 supplies jurisdiction over the federal statutory dispute. Traditional equity permits a person injured by an officer's implementation of action outside delegated statutory authority to seek prospective restraint unless Congress has displaced that route. McAnnulty, Stark, and Harmon establish that the absence of an express statutory review cause does not by itself eliminate this confined officer remedy. Neither §1331 nor the immunity waiver independently creates a damages claim or a free-standing cause for every statutory error. The claim here concerns authority to impose an operative disqualification; it is not merely disagreement with the wisdom of an authorized purchasing choice.

**C2 — immunity and preclusion.** Section 702's nonmonetary-relief waiver applies to the official-capacity claim against the implementing Secretary independently of whether the complaint invokes the APA's review cause. Its savings clauses preserve other legal and equitable review limits and prohibitions on relief contained in another consent-to-suit statute. Any injunction must identify the responsible officer or successor. Larson and Dugan corroborate the confined officer-action tradition; there is no need to hold that every allegation of unlawfulness automatically satisfies their exception. The Procurement Act establishes no scheme displacing this challenge comparable to the completed package in simulated Dalton. Simulated Franklin reserves the President's APA status and does not insulate implementation solely because it originates in a presidential order. Neither that status nor coercive relief against the President is decided.

**C3 — statutory fit.** The Procurement Act's broad authority to promote economical and efficient government purchasing must operate consistently with the NLRA. Mackay Radio recognizes permanent replacement of economic strikers subject to the NLRA's retained protections; Insurance Agents and Machinists preserve the economic weapons Congress left to bargaining parties. A general procurement delegation does not authorize a government-wide labor-policy sanction on that preserved choice. This order makes the replacement decision itself the basis of a contracting disqualification and reaches organizational affiliates capable of supplying the contracted goods or services, without requiring that the striking workers be performing the Government's particular contract. The performance rationale and discretionary qualifications do not convert that rule into Boston Harbor's specifically justified project bargain. Reading the two statutes together sustains extensive lawful procurement discretion while withholding this unsupported alteration of the NLRA arrangement. Congress remains free to enact a different rule; the President may not infer it from general procurement language. This is federal statutory construction informed by labor decisions, not mechanical state-law preemption or a constitutional prohibition on Congress regulating labor relations.

**C4 — remedy and scope.** Affirm the judgment reversing dismissal and rejecting this program, and remand for appropriate prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against the implementing Secretary consistent with C1–C3. The Court need not itself formulate every decree term. No damages, contractor award, striker reinstatement, employee-specific bargaining finding, or injunction directed to the President follows. No independent constitutional nondelegation holding or prescribed procurement-findings requirement is necessary.

The policy's qualifications must remain express: it covers contracts above $100,000; termination is permissive and subject to the contracting agency head's objection; debarment has dispute-resolution and compelling-reason qualifications; the organizational-unit definition reaches the specified affiliates. These qualifications matter to scope but do not remove the common legal defect. The Court does not hold that every application necessarily compels unlawful recognition of a minority union. The lower court's construction of the regulations governing the end of a dispute is not a statutory command or an independent factual finding that every application conflicts with the NLRA. Unfair-labor-practice strikers have different replacement protection and are not equated with economic strikers.

### Alternative paths and their limiting objections

**No-review path:** Presidential procurement discretion, the absence of a pleaded APA cause, and concern about indirect presidential restraint could support dismissal if Congress had committed this precise challenge to an exclusive scheme or if only unreviewable policy discretion were at issue. Its strongest objection is the established equitable officer route, the enacted §702 waiver, and the absence of Dalton's preclusive structure. The claim concerns the boundary between two statutes, a judicially manageable issue, and the Secretary is the operative defendant. Each Associate rejects dismissal on this record while preserving valid statutory preclusion in other settings.

**Statutory-harmony path sustaining the order:** Private buyers may respond to strike disruption; retained skill and dependable supply are legitimate purchasing interests; the Procurement Act permits centralized policy rather than requiring every choice to be made contract by contract. Its strongest objection is the actual disqualification's reach beyond performance of a particular bargain and its direct use of a protected economic choice as a government-wide eligibility criterion. Each Associate rejects this order on that distinction. They do not require proof of presidential bad motive or hold that a policy is invalid merely because it is broad or affects labor.

**Invalidation path adopted:** C3 permits harmonious operation of the two enactments without impliedly repealing either. Its strongest objection is that it might silently prohibit every aggregate purchasing standard or turn NLRA permissions into guaranteed government business. The commitment therefore excludes both implications. A concrete lawful project condition, supplier-performance decision, or specifically authorized federal condition remains a different question. The Government's large purchasing share illustrates this order's operation but is not an independent size-based rule.

## 26. Castano v. American Tobacco Co.

### Reach, components, and common supported ground

The established review is of an accepted §1292(b) interlocutory appeal; it is neither mandamus nor a Rule 23(f) proceeding. The denied Rule 23(b)(2) equitable and medical-monitoring certification was not cross-appealed and is outside the relief now considered. The pending proposed Liggett settlement does not moot the entire litigation or establish a release.

**K1 — required analysis.** Before certifying this Rule 23(b)(3) class, the court must determine from the actual claims, applicable law, and anticipated proof that common questions predominate and that class treatment is superior to the other available methods. Falcon requires rigorous analysis of the prerequisites; Eisen forbids a preliminary merits decision used to award or deny certification merely because of predicted success, but does not prohibit examining the substantive questions necessary to decide whether class adjudication can work. Conditional certification does not postpone satisfaction of those prerequisites.

**K2 — law, proof, and the actual proposal.** The present nationwide core-liability certification cannot stand. The court deferred the choice-of-law and state-law-variation inquiry, did not establish workable groupings for the actual duties and defenses, and assumed that common manufacturer conduct would significantly advance the claims without explaining its relationship to individual addiction, reliance, causation, and defenses. Proponents bear the certification burden. Shutts prevents assuming one State's law for materially disconnected claims; it does not compel a particular state-law answer here. Differences among state laws are neither always fatal nor automatically curable by subclass labels. A credible claim-and-proof structure can establish manageability; no independent formal-document requirement called a trial plan is enacted.

**K3 — punitive issues, superiority, and retained questions.** The punitive-ratio component also falls because its state-law predicates, burdens, liability relationships, and coordination with individual proceedings were not adequately demonstrated. The record does not establish that one jury may lawfully decide every punitive question before the remaining liability work. That failure is a Rule 23 ground, not a holding that every ratio, issue class, or bifurcated trial is unconstitutional. The same unanswered questions defeat the actual superiority finding: predicted savings from millions of individual trials cannot substitute for knowing what the proposed class trial and later proceedings would resolve. Settlement pressure attributable to an improperly structured all-or-nothing proceeding can reinforce those concrete concerns; size or pressure alone does not prohibit certification. The novelty of the asserted addiction theory calls for careful examination but is not an independent categorical bar. Neither a history of prior verdicts nor a preexisting judicial-management crisis is an additional prerequisite in Rule 23.

**K4 — remedy.** Affirm the rejection of the existing core-liability and punitive certification. Vacate the mandate's categorical no-reconsideration direction to the extent it requires dismissal of the class pleading merely because the tort has not matured through individual trials, and remand for ordinary lawful proceedings. The present class remains uncertified. No narrower class is now certified or presumed adequate; any renewed, materially narrower proposal must establish the applicable prerequisites. Individual claims receive no merits adjudication. The Rule 23(b)(2) denial remains outside review. This partial-vacatur component is distinct from the unanimous conclusion that the existing certification fails.

Matsushita's independent adequate-representation requirement and Richards's nonparty-binding rule remain law. Neither convenience, notice, nor opt-out alone would cure an actual representation defect. No new adequacy finding, universal collateral-review rule, or finding that these named representatives actually conflict on every issue is necessary to this decision. A renewed proposal must meet Rule 23(a) as well as the applicable subdivision of (b).

### Alternative paths and their limiting objections

**Reinstatement of this conditional issue class:** Common course-of-conduct evidence is substantial, litigation is expensive, and Rule 23(c)(4) permits appropriate issue treatment. The strongest objection is that the court did not establish which law governs or how the selected common issues connect to the remaining elements and defenses. Deferred refinement cannot supply the missing present findings. Each Associate therefore rejects reinstatement.

**Affirmance of every lower-court proposition and mandatory dismissal until maturation:** The individual questions, very large aggregate exposure, and uncertain legal theory make cautious decertification appropriate. The strongest objection is that the Rule contains no prior-verdict or immaturity prohibition, while actual legal groupings or genuinely separable issues could change the certification inquiry. Each Associate rejects that categorical remedial limit. The Court decides this proposal's demonstrated defects rather than certifying or forbidding a hypothetical replacement.

**K1–K4 adopted:** Its strongest objection is that remand might encourage repeated nominal amendments without curing anything. The answer is the proponents' continuing burden and the requirement of a materially supported proposal; remand grants no presumption, deadlines, discovery entitlement, or certification. Whether Rule 23(c)(4) ever permits certification where the complete cause of action fails an action-wide predominance inquiry need not be resolved to reject this inadequately mapped proposal. Repeated evidence before successive juries is not automatically reexamination of a decided fact; no new Seventh Amendment holding is adopted.

## 27. In re Deuel

### Reach, claim components, and common supported ground

The authorized government petition reviews the Federal Circuit's reversal of the Board's rejection of application claims 4–7. This is not an infringement action or a request that the Court issue a patent. The stipulated live application and ordinary postgrant procedure support the established §1254 route. No actual later patent disposition, waiver, or mootness event is imported.

**D1 — §103's product inquiry.** The claimed subject matter as a whole must be compared with the prior art and the capabilities of a person of ordinary skill at the invention date under Graham. A product is not nonobvious merely because the prior art did not print its exact sequence; §103 expressly addresses inventions not identically disclosed under §102. A demonstrated, predictable route to the claimed product may be relevant even if its precise structure was not written out in advance. Conversely, a useful research target and an available general technique do not by themselves prove that the particular claimed product would have been obvious. The necessary connection must be supported, not inferred retrospectively from the applicant's success. The manner of invention is not itself disqualifying.

**D2 — claims 5 and 7.** Affirm the reversal of the rejection of the two specific cDNA sequences. Bohlen discloses a nineteen-amino-acid fragment and brain mitogens; Maniatis describes general screening, not the necessary target-specific library choice and probe design. The agency did not support its asserted identity of the proteins or its extension across tissue sources. Genetic-code redundancy is relevant to that gap, not a categorical immunity from §103. On the agency grounds and factual explanation supplied, a generalized incentive to clone does not establish a supported expectation of obtaining these specific products.

**D3 — claims 4 and 6.** Separately affirm reversal of the encoding-genus rejections on this record. Their breadth is not treated as identical to the two specific sequences; an obvious species within the claimed genus can matter without advance identification of every member. The agency supplied no separately supported genus ground and did not cure the target-identity and recovery premises underlying its common rationale. The rejection cannot be sustained by a new technical finding supplied by counsel or by assuming the genus inherits every argument about a specific sequence. The Court does not hold that the entire genus is affirmatively proved nonobvious under every properly supported record.

**D4 — effect and examination.** Affirm the Federal Circuit's judgment reversing the existing §103 rejections, while rejecting any categorical suggestion that known methods can never establish product obviousness without prior exact structural disclosure. Ordinary further lawful examination remains available under applicable notice and procedure; this judgment is not an order to allow the application, a new rejection, or a ruling on §112 disclosure or enablement. Section 101 eligibility, priority, infringement, and appellate review of every subsidiary PTO finding remain outside the decision. No invention of commercial success, unexpected results, failed research, or other objective evidence fills the record.

Public Law 104–41, effective November 1, 1995 for pending as well as newly filed applications, retained the product rule in §103(a) and added qualified biotechnological-process treatment in (b). These product claims do not become process claims; the rule dependent on a timely applicant election and its linked claims, ownership, and patent-term conditions does not establish a different product-obviousness result. No election is assumed.

Markman's operative-claim discipline requires preserving the sequence/genus distinction. Its allocation of embedded construction questions does not turn ordinary-skill, prior-art, or laboratory capabilities into facts this Court may invent. This administrative case presents no jury issue or compulsory construction hearing.

### Alternative paths and their limiting objections

**Restore the Board's rejection:** The protein is useful, a corresponding gene must exist, and routine cloning can sometimes make obtaining the product predictable even before its sequence is known. The strongest objection is the missing support connecting the disclosed fragment, target identity, relevant tissues and libraries, and techniques to these claimed products. The record's actual technical gaps cannot be cured by the word routine or an assumption based on later biotechnology. Each Associate rejects restoration of this rejection.

**Vacate for a new claim-specific assessment rather than affirm:** The Federal Circuit used categorical language separating obvious methods from product obviousness and invoked inability to conceive the exact structure. Correcting that legal approach can ordinarily warrant renewed assessment. The strongest objection here is that the Board's own unexplained factual premises already fail to sustain either set of rejections and the Federal Circuit independently identified the lack of any separate genus basis. The existing agency judgment therefore cannot be restored even after correcting the legal overstatement. Each Associate chooses affirmance of its reversal with the precise D1–D4 limits; a remand for lawful further agency action is not prohibited, but no mandatory reopening to rehabilitate the rejected grounds is ordered.

**Affirm a categorical sequence-disclosure rule:** Product claims and redundancy guard against treating every useful protein as disclosure of every encoding molecule. The strongest objection is §103's explicit distinction from exact disclosure and its ordinary-skill inquiry. Each Associate rejects an anticipation-like prerequisite or a special DNA exception. The resulting judgment rests on the actual inadequacy of this combination and agency explanation, not an inflexible structural rule.

## 30. ProCD, Inc. v. Zeidenberg — January 27, 1997

### Components and supported disposition

P1 is formation and manifestation of assent under Wisconsin law; P2 is copyright subject matter and equivalence under §301; P3 is the resulting mandate. Reach both granted questions and affirm the Seventh Circuit's judgment within the actual bargain and parties. Its remand for judgment enforcing the noncommercial-use restriction stands; this Court fixes neither a new dollar amount nor an injunction extending beyond the proved undertaking. The reported absence of a developed ordinary contract defense supplies no occasion to reopen the case merely to invent one. No separate corporate-exemption argument was pressed for Silken Mountain.

**P1 rule:** Section 2-204 permits agreement manifested by conduct. This package warned that enclosed restrictions applied; the terms appeared before continued use and required an indication of assent; a real return-and-refund opportunity permitted rejection. Continued use with those opportunities establishes this undertaking. Payment does not invariably exhaust every possible formation sequence, but a seller cannot unilaterally amend an already completed bargain by inserting terms later. Acceptance of goods under §2-606 is not itself assent to every proposed term. Section 2-207 is not adjudicated for materially different exchanged-form transactions.

**P2 rule:** A fixed factual compilation can enter §301's subject-matter inquiry even when the particular facts or compilation lack copyright protection. Enforcement here nevertheless rests on a genuine party-specific promise governing the purchased access, qualitatively different from the exclusive rights §106 grants against persons without an undertaking. Neither a contract label nor an extra pleading form invariably defeats equivalence. Section 301(b)(3) preserves nonequivalent rights; it is not a categorical contract exception. Enforcement creates no copyright in the facts, no duty for nonassenting persons and no exemption from an independently applicable federal prohibition. The assumption concerning this database does not adjudicate copyrightability of every component, and the distinct program copyright is not enlarged.

The relevant voluntary-undertaking rules are Wolens (January 18, 1995) and Cipollone's warranty component (June 24, 1992), at their clause-specific scopes. Atherton's January 14 state-law holding supports keeping the contract source distinct from the federal displacement inquiry; it does not decide either question. Copyright's originality limits remain Feist's. Kewanee and Aronson, as recited in the primary lower opinion, support the distinction between independently available information and an actual personal undertaking; they supply no universal §301 rule.

### Adversarial alternatives and join conditions

The strongest formation objection is that retail payment already completed the bargain and the later form cannot become a unilateral amendment. The supported response is the actual announced sequence and affirmative continued use after review with a refund option, especially the repeated purchases; it does not rest merely on opening plastic or a prediction that market competition protects all buyers. A reversal or defense-only remand would be supported if the asserted notice or effective rejection opportunity were missing or a material preserved defense remained. The validated record supplies those opportunities and no such developed defense.

The strongest preemption objection is that widespread form restrictions could recreate exclusive control of facts despite Feist. That concern bars a blanket contract exemption. It does not make this actual promise equivalent to a rule binding independent acquirers: breach requires the undertaking and governs the agreed exchange. Refusal to protect facts by copyright is preserved, not treated as a mandate to disregard all contracts concerning them. No constitutional private power to recreate copyright is announced. Particular first-sale, reverse-engineering, fair-use, nonparty and compulsory-license questions are outside the grant and record.

## 33. General Motors Corp. v. Tracy — February 18, 1997

### Fixed neutral premises and distinct questions

T1 concerns GM's own standing; T2 concerns discrimination and comparability; T3 concerns any compensatory justification; T4 concerns equal protection and disposition. The proceeding reviews an adverse use-tax assessment for purchases from October 1, 1986 through June 30, 1990. It is not shown to be a paid-tax refund proceeding. Assessment amount and payment are not supplied. All commitments permit deciding the constitutional challenge without deciding those monetary facts.

The statutory category is a natural gas company as authoritatively construed to require distribution assets and delivery, not an express seller-residence classification. Both local and out-of-state nonqualifying marketers' sales are taxed. That does not alone answer practical discrimination. GM bought virtually all its gas from interstate marketers, often using LDC delivery. LDCs bore rate, service, reliability and human-needs obligations that marketers did not and could not select only profitable customers. The accepted source recitation says the industrial competitive market is sparsely described; the assertion of unregulated special-contract LDC sales during the relevant tax period has no record support. The 1996 enactment supplies no retroactive fact about the 1986–1990 transactions.

The governing standards are **Discriminatory routing, charges and linked subsidies in interstate commerce** (present operation SSC, March 11, 1996, with the discrimination rule unchanged); **Compensatory taxes and the actual equivalent local burden** (Fulton, February 21, 1996); and **Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications** (Beach, June 1, 1993). West Lynn examines the integrated preference; Oregon Waste's demanding justification follows discrimination; Fulton preserves three cumulative compensation predicates. No independent Pike challenge or congressional-authorization theory is introduced. The state classification and penalty remission are not additional granted federal questions.

### T1 — customer standing

All eight reject the lower court's standing bar. The adverse assessment is GM's own concrete economic burden. Its asserted unequal treatment in purchasing interstate gas is not converted into a stranger's claim merely because competing sellers also suffer. Causation runs directly from the tax rule to the assessment; relief from the allegedly unlawful assessment would redress that burden. This recognizes a litigant's claim, not the merits of the asserted comparison.

### T2 — independent enhanced comparison

**Path U, sustain the challenged distinction:** Compare the regulated LDC service business, including its compulsory service responsibilities, with the independent marketer's commodity business. The actual obligations materially affect what the LDC must offer and maintain. The fact that both can supply gas to a large buyer does not establish that the relevant businesses are similarly situated for this asserted discrimination, or permit excising the regulated service from the LDC's commodity activity by unsupported assumption. The thin industrial record supplies no established distinct unregulated sales category. This is a bounded rejection of the discrimination claim shown, not categorical constitutional immunity for utilities or a finding that any particular rate exactly funds public duties.

**Strongest objection to U:** A State cannot defeat scrutiny simply by attaching regulation to favored local sellers, and direct competition for the same industrial sale can exist despite captive customers elsewhere. Carbone and West Lynn require practical competitive analysis rather than a label. U therefore must rest on the established substantive service differences and present record, not the words utility, public interest or taxed local marketers alone. It leaves a materially different supported industrial transaction for its own case.

**Path C, vacate for a competitive-transaction inquiry:** Treat the industrial purchaser's ability to obtain the same commodity from either class as a potentially material comparator. The existing duties do not automatically answer whether the exemption advantages LDC commodity sales in that market. Correct the lower court's reliance on nominal equal treatment of local and interstate marketers and its standing bar, and remand for the needed transaction-specific comparison and, if discrimination is established, justification. This path does not declare the asserted unregulated contracts to exist or finally invalidate every exempt sale.

**Strongest objection to C:** A remand may separate industrial sales from a public-service business without evidence that their supply obligations are actually separable. Shared commodity identity alone does not show equivalent offerings. C must leave the issue open below rather than assume that an ordinary competitive-gas tax comparison erases the LDC's network obligations. The record supports a bounded remand request; it does not support judicial calculation of cross-subsidy costs or automatic narrow invalidation.

## 35. Robinson v. Shell Oil Co. — February 18, 1997
