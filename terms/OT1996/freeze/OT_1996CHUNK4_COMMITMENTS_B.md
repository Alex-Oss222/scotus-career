## 46. Dunn v. CFTC — February 25, 1997

**Reach and operative text:** All eight reach the preserved statutory question before fraud merits. The exclusion covers transactions in foreign currency, security warrants, security rights, resales of installment loan contracts, repurchase options, government securities, or mortgages and mortgage purchase commitments, except when the transactions involve their sale for future delivery conducted on a board of trade. The complete list and the conjunctive future-delivery/board-of-trade exception remain part of the source; no bank-only condition is enacted in this text.

**Common conclusion:** Off-exchange options to buy or sell foreign currency are transactions in foreign currency within this exclusion. Treating the option as only a transaction in a right, while treating exercise as the only currency transaction, gives the statutory category an artificially truncated scope. The express exception restores coverage only on its stated terms. Fraud allegations cannot create authority withheld by the statute. The separate regulatory trade-option exemption, with its commercial-actor and business-purpose conditions, does not define or narrow the Treasury Amendment. The CFTC's representation that interbank options qualify under that exemption therefore does not answer statutory exclusion.

**Two paths and objections:** The agency path distinguishes the option right from later purchase of currency and reads the exclusion against the Act's broader option regulation, with the regulatory exemption softening the claimed consequences for banks. Its strongest objection is that it removes an ordinary way of transacting in the underlying currency from the broad enacted proviso and uses an administrative exception to displace a statutory boundary. The exclusion path respects the whole transaction and the explicit restoration clause. Its strongest objection is a regulatory gap for alleged fraud. Congress's chosen coverage, rather than the desirability of enforcement or a claim of immunity from all law, answers that objection. No conclusion rests on an erroneous assertion that the agency admits regulating every interbank option.
