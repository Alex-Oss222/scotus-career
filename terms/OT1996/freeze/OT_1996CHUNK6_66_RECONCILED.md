## Reconciled propositions and direct joins

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join each of the following and reverse the summary judgment for Mississippi with a bounded remand.

**Y1 — Discretionary NVRA implementation remains covered.** A covered jurisdiction must preclear new voting-registration practices embodying its policy choices even when adopted to comply with federal legislation. The NVRA preserves the Voting Rights Act, so its federal-registration obligations do not exempt Mississippi's discretionary registration machinery or communications. The disposition does not decide a feature compelled in every detail by federal law and leaving no state policy choice. Actual Lopez's rule concerning administration despite state-law compulsion is preserved without mechanically extending it to that distinct federal hypothetical.

**Y2a — The actual Provisional Plan did not become the baseline.** The Provisional Plan did not become a practice in force or effect for §5 comparison where its registration use lasted only forty-one days, only about one-third of registration officials began using it, no election had occurred or was imminent, and officials abandoned the tentative arrangement promptly when the anticipated state-law changes would not be enacted. Those circumstances operate together; no one circumstance establishes a general numerical, temporal, election-use, intent or state-law-validity threshold. The Old System is the operative comparison for this New System challenge.

State-law unlawfulness **alone** does not disqualify an actually operative practice. A practice may remain the baseline despite a technical or other conflict with state law, as Perkins and Lockhart show. Y2a neither declares all registrations under the Provisional Plan void nor finds nobody registered or relied on it. It resolves the §5 benchmark for this system, not every individual registration's legal status or a generic rule that tentative practices never count.

**Y2b — The New System nevertheless changes the Old System.** Retaining old state-election qualifications does not restore all the prior registration practices. The New System includes materially different instructions, manuals, reporting, agency choices, assistance and communication of federal-only versus combined registration status; those discretionary practices were absent from the Old System. Both minor and major covered changes require submission. Coverage does not depend on proof that a change actually harms minority voters; whether it is nondiscriminatory is for substantive preclearance through the designated channels.

**Y3 — Prior approval is specific, not a blanket NVRA exemption.** Preclearance of the earlier submitted implementation arrangement does not approve later discretionary changes it did not identify. A submission must identify its proposed changes sufficiently to obtain their preclearance; ambiguity does not shift to the Attorney General the burden to infer and approve every contingency. The later dual arrangement's new features were not submitted as such. The actual terms govern any genuine question about a particular submission on remand, and no unrecovered correspondence is invented. The approval is not rescinded or treated as judicially reviewable merely because it does not cover this later arrangement.

**Y4 — Statutory sequence and fitted remedy.** The enforcement court must give effect to §5's nonuse requirement for covered, unprecleared state practices, identify the actual changes and submission status, and fashion lawful relief within its authority; the Attorney General or District Court for the District of Columbia decides substantive preclearance. The ordinary remand preserves the NVRA's valid federal duties and actual Lopez's remedial qualifications. It orders no particular interim plan, merged or identical rolls, racial-purpose or retrogression finding, damages, cancellation of registrations or completed-election consequence. A judicial label does not exempt the State's policy choices from §5, while an independently lawful judicial interim arrangement requires its own predicates rather than an invented absolute ban.

The provisional Y1, Y3 and Y4 survive at these clarified scopes. Y2 is replaced by Y2a and Y2b; Y2a is an actual conclusion rather than an assumed premise. All eight retain the same reversal/remand judgment. No separate writing is required to express these common commitments.
