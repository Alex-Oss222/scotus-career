## Count rule and reviewed edge cases

Engine section 8 counts each signed lead delivered after plenary merits review once for every Justice who delivers a part; it excludes separate writings, summary dispositions, unsigned/per-curiam actions, ordinary procedural orders, source records and stopped assignments.

- California v. Roy is signed but expressly a summary disposition: Souter receives no count. Its older assignment tally does not alter the current rule.
- LAVAS and Lynce count. Their signed leads complete plenary merits-docket review after argument. Lynce's assignment statement expressly distinguishes these threshold/statutory-remand decisions from ordinary procedural actions. A threshold disposition alone does not exclude a plenary signed lead.
- FEC v. Legi-Tech and Opuku-Boateng have no plenary date in their public Event blocks, but each original assignment form statement expressly establishes signed plenary review. Their published authors are Thomas and Stone-Zsela, respectively.
- Kinman supplies two author credits: one each to Stone-Zsela and Ginsburg. Multiple portions delivered by the same author are not counted again.
- Lords Landing contributes zero. The Bryan County reargument order, DIGs, financial-leave actions and admitted-source entries also contribute zero.
- All other included authors were checked against their public lead-opinion cells or prose equivalents. No unresolved countability question remains under the existing Records' stated review form.
