### Issue 1: Best-interests bypass for parental notification

- **Controlling Simulated Law:** Planned Parenthood, Sioux Falls Clinic v. Miller (1996) applies simulated Casey’s complete six-step standard, as applied in Fargo, to compulsory minor notice. Challengers first establish interference caused by the duty; demonstrated parental practical control or substantial obstruction cannot be justified by a general welfare interest. The decision enjoined the indivisible compulsory condition because the enacted law supplied no usable independent maturity or best-interests route. It did not equate every notice law with consent or forbid every forty-eight-hour period.
- **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Casey’s five-Justice six-step rule requires identification of the actual duty and protected baseline; the duty’s function and purpose; its causal added interference and independently defined affected population; ordered substantive review; clinical and truthful-communication safeguards; and violation-specific relief. A baseline violation, objectively established obstructive purpose, or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring; remaining meaningful interference requires a substantiated permissible justification and fit, including an answer to a concrete practicable substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference, that heightened access inquiry ends, without foreclosing other constitutional claims. Usable exceptions and bypasses matter; absence of an obstacle alone does not validate every burden. Fargo applies the complete rule to facial and cumulative claims, and Miller applies it to compulsory minor notice.
- **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment; Bellotti v. Baird; Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health; Mont. Code Ann. § 50-20-212.

## F. VERIFIED SIMULATED-LAW APPLICATION

### Application to the Montana bypass

Unlike Miller’s unusable alternative, the supplied Montana text gives an independent court authority to waive notice, sets a confidential preference for prompt consideration, requires counsel upon request, and provides automatic waiver if no ruling issues within forty-eight hours and the petitioner has not obtained an extension. Those safeguards bear directly on whether the wording actually permits protected access rather than giving a parent practical control. The precise best-interests wording must be construed in that framework; its sufficiency does not resolve any distinct, preserved burden concerning proof, timing, or implementation.

## G. FRESH NEUTRAL VALIDATION — STATUTE, PATHS, AND LIMITS

Source: Mont. Code Ann. §50-20-212 (1995), reproduced verbatim in the official U.S. Reports statutory appendix at 520 U.S. 299–300 and mechanically extracted in ../sources/chunk6-neutral-control/MONTANA_50_20_212_EXACT.txt. This fresh review read only that statutory extraction, this neutral packet, and the paired entering-law slice; it did not consult the present matter's historical Supreme Court reasoning or disposition.

The statute applies to minors and incompetent persons, residents and nonresidents. A petitioner may seek youth-court waiver and participate personally; the petition must state pregnancy and lack of emancipation. The court may appoint a guardian ad litem, who must preserve confidentiality. It must advise the petitioner about appointed counsel and provide counsel on request. An allegation of abuse as a waiver basis must also be treated as a report under §41-3-202, with investigation governed by Title 41, chapter 3, part 2. That reporting provision is distinct from the independent waiver grounds; no unquoted investigative rule is invented.

Proceedings are confidential and anonymous, sealed, and afforded preference necessary for a prompt decision. A pseudonym or initials may be used; documents are unavailable to the public. The court must issue written findings and conclusions and rule within forty-eight hours **unless the time is extended at the petitioner's request**. Automatic grant and waiver occur only if the court fails to rule within forty-eight hours **and time has not been extended**. The text does not authorize a court-initiated extension merely for administrative convenience.

Under subsection (4), a clear-and-convincing finding that the petitioner is sufficiently mature to decide whether to have an abortion requires an order authorizing the minor to consent without parental or guardian notification. Subsection (5) independently requires authorization on a clear-and-convincing finding either that **there is evidence of a pattern of physical, sexual, or emotional abuse** by one or both parents, a guardian, or a custodian, or that **“the notification of a parent or guardian is not in the best interests of the petitioner.”** The abuse and best-interests routes are alternatives to maturity, not cumulative requirements. If neither subsection (4) nor (5) finding is made, the petition must be dismissed. Written specific findings and conclusions and a confidential evidentiary record are required.

Subsection (8) says the state supreme court **may adopt rules** for an expedited confidential appeal after denial; that permissive rulemaking language is not itself a finding that a particular appellate timetable exists. An order authorizing abortion without notice is unappealable. Filing fees may not be charged to a pregnant minor seeking waiver or appealing denial. These statutory details do not enlarge the granted best-interests question to every adult-incapacity, investigative, appeal, emergency, penalty, or implementation issue.

The controlling Standards entries are **Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation** and **One-parent informed consent with an independent judicial bypass**, each effective June 29, 1992 under Casey; their complete current text is in the paired slice. Fargo and Miller apply the complete framework and preserve its causal showing, stable affected population, independent baseline/obstacle/purpose prohibitions, residual severe and meaningful interference branches, and relief matched to the violation. The particular Pennsylvania safeguards sustained in Casey are not all constitutionally indispensable everywhere; Miller supplies no categorical equation of notice and consent or prohibition of every forty-eight-hour period.

The State's strongest available construction is that best interests can encompass the consequences of notification, including its effect on a minor's ability to obtain protected care, alongside the independent maturity route. The providers' strongest available objection is that the different textual question could withhold authorization from an immature minor for whom abortion is in her best interests when a court considers parental notification beneficial in isolation.
