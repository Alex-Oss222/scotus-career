## 1. Event, law and component boundaries

The authorized intake is summary appellate review of the Ninth Circuit en banc habeas judgment concerning the Mannix robbery and first-degree felony-murder convictions. The supplied question asks whether the purpose-to-facilitate omission is structural or trial error and what harmlessness standard governs it on federal habeas. The historical printed petition instead frames a conflict between the necessary-findings method and historical Brecht. That advocacy does not override the authorized question or the governing law. An intermediate historical Supreme Court grant or briefing event is not reconstructed here.

The opening [A entering-law slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK1_A.md) supplies these separate rules:

- **Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review**, present operation effective *O'Neal*, February 21, 1995: the State bears the whole-record Chapman burden for the defined preserved personal-intent instructional/argument category properly available on habeas. Its exact antecedent involved combined instructions and prosecutorial argument. Application to this omitted-purpose instruction needs explanation; neither a universal collateral Chapman rule nor an exception for every omission is inherited. Kotteakos cannot be substituted merely because the historical filings assume it.
- **A vitiated jury reasonable-doubt standard is structural error**, effective *Sullivan*, June 1, 1993: a charge invalidating the standard for all jury findings leaves no valid reasonable-doubt verdict for harmlessness review. It does not classify all element omissions as structural.
- **Jury determination of criminal materiality**, effective *Gaudin*, June 19, 1995: jury determination of an offense element is constitutionally required; the universal structural-error question is reserved.

The authenticated [public law entering Roy](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK1_PRIOR_TO_ROY.md), supplied after the October 21 LAVAS decision became canonical, and the [sanitized projection](../workspace/neutral-projection.md), read with its October 21 completed-event cursor, supply the only earlier effective current-term Court event. LAVAS applies Lawrence to an intervening enactment and reserves statutory/temporal merits; it changes none of Roy's harmlessness, party-status, preservation or instruction rules. Nine Justices are seated; no case-specific nonparticipation is established. November 4 is the research cutoff.

The separate Clark second-degree-murder conviction is outside this federal challenge. The state court reversed both special-circumstance findings and the sentence resting on them while affirming the remaining judgment. Their evidentiary relevance, if any, is different from their operative validity. Nothing here restores them, reaches the Clark conviction or imposes immediate release or a retrial deadline.

## 2. Corrected lower-court account

**The en banc court made an express Chapman assessment.** Appendix A, pp14–16, and the complete published *Roy v. Gomez*, 81 F.3d 863, 867–869, establish that it first concluded that the omission would not be harmless beyond a reasonable doubt on direct review and that reversal would follow there. It then separately considered the historical collateral-review inquiry. A disposition cannot truthfully rest on the proposition that the Ninth Circuit never assessed Chapman harmlessness. Whether its restrictive necessary-findings method is the legally correct Chapman method remains a separate live question.

The en banc majority recognized harmless-error review and rejected an unqualified structural rule. It distinguished an instruction establishing an element as a matter of law from failure to alert the jury to an element and allowed harmlessness when actual findings necessarily supplied the omitted element. It reasoned that Roy could knowingly help McHargue without the purpose of facilitating robbery. The two stated examples were helping a companion avoid defeat in a fight and keeping Clark from assisting Mannix without intending the resulting assistance to the robbery. These were possible inferences, not findings that either event occurred. Footnote 6 mentions other proposed alternatives without describing them; none may be invented. The dissent agreed about reviewability but disputed the restrictive method and its record application. Its historical Brecht premise is not current governing law.

**The state court reached the contrary Chapman result.** Appendix J's complete OCR contains the January 27, 1989 majority and Evans dissent, including the unpublished Beeman discussion at pp37–47. The majority considered Roy's account denying assistance, the prosecution's prior-plan account and the remaining felony-murder account. It reasoned that knowing assistance under the actual circumstances adequately conveyed the missing purpose because no evidence supplied a contrary intent. That is the state court's legal harmlessness assessment, not an independently established fact or a new constitutional definition of aiding and abetting.

At p45 the state court inferred from the Clark second-degree verdict, Clark robbery acquittal and Mannix knife-use finding that the jury rejected Hudspeth's account. The Evans dissent also treated his testimony as disbelieved, while finding the remaining evidence sufficient for its own harmlessness conclusion. Neither statement is an additional express jury verdict identifying every piece of testimony rejected. Conversely, the State's later reliance on Hudspeth cannot be treated as an undisputed account without addressing the verdicts, these lower explanations and the impeachment evidence.

The district court's reproduced opinion, Appendix D, reaches harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt through functional equivalence between knowing assistance and purpose under these facts. Its incorporation of the magistrate's findings does not convert every appellate inference into an express jury finding. Appendix E supplies a detailed trial-evidence recitation, including defense arguments; its final recommendation states no constitutional error, and its reasoning also discusses harmlessness. Those formulations should not be collapsed into a fabricated unqualified factual finding that purpose was expressly returned.

The state majority held that linking the felony-murder and special-circumstance instructions permitted intentional aid to robbery to support the aggravating finding without the intent to kill required for a nonkiller. The Evans dissent read intentional assistance to first-degree murder as requiring intentional aid to a killing and would have upheld the special circumstances. The majority's reversal therefore does not itself resolve the distinct question whether any properly understood component of those findings bears on the omitted robbery-purpose requirement. Nor does the adverb in 8.80, read in isolation, establish that the jury necessarily supplied that requirement. Both arguments require the complete connection among instruction, finding and specific intent claimed.

The state opinion reports Mannix robbery and first-degree murder with possession but no personal knife use; Clark second-degree murder with personal knife use; and acquittal of Clark robbery. Its two special-circumstance findings concerned robbery murder and multiple murder. The en banc opinion's shorthand calling weapon findings special circumstances must not obscure the difference between weapon findings and the reversed aggravating findings.

## 4. Evidence: judicial recitation, advocacy and concrete conflicts

The recovered sources supply substantial evidence on both sides. They do not permit describing the trial evidence as wholly unavailable.

- Appendix J pp8–12 and Appendix E pp8–15 report Mannix's watch and cash among Roy's possessions, wet clothing and backpacks, testimony about the fight and assistance, and the testimony attributed to Roy by Hall and Hudspeth. These reports support the existence of that evidence, not automatic acceptance of the witnesses or the prosecution's inferences.
- **Backpack conflict:** petition p8 puts Mannix's vest and moccasins in Roy's backpack. Appendix J p8 and Appendix E pp8–9 put them in McHargue's backpack; the latter supplies RT2424–2425 and 2451. The neutral packet must not attribute those items to Roy as an uncontested fact. The common supported account is that each had some of Mannix's property, with the lower recitations specifically allocating the clothing to McHargue.
- Appendix E pp12–14 recounts both Hudspeth's truck-departure story and a statement that the truck was stuck and the men walked until picked up. It also reports his wife's testimony about a promised benefit and his rebuttal explanation that the promise concerned transfer out of California's prisons for safety; he denied promises inducing his testimony and hoped for employment. The Opposition's impeachment account must retain that recovered rebuttal when the promise is material. No credibility resolution is made here.
- Appendix J p10 and Appendix E pp14–15 report Hall's account that Roy went to help McHargue without saying how, the statement that Mannix had to die because he was a witness, its later retraction, and Hall's report that Roy never said he stabbed Mannix. The petition's characterization as an admission of personal assistance in drowning is not an uncontested verbatim admission.
- Appendix J pp11–12 and Appendix E pp11,15 corroborate that either knife could fit the wounds, that the type-A blood could match Roy or Mannix, and the defense challenge to the blood inference. They do not identify Roy as the source of a fatal wound.
- Appendix E pp23–24 records defense arguments that the cash was earned rather than stolen, McHargue supplied the watch after the killing, and the keys were not Mannix's. These are arguments, not findings. The Opposition's additional assertions about the informants' convictions, outings, prosecutor contact, keys failing locks and transcript details retain party-assertion status where not independently corroborated by the material actually read. Their supplied RT pinpoints identify a targeted source if a proposed result depends on them.

The disagreement between the en banc majority's possible innocent-purpose inferences and the state/district courts' contrary assessment is a live legal and record-application issue. ## 5. Preservation and petitioner identity

**Distinct preservation questions.** The underlying constitutional personal-intent claim is the preserved claim authorized by the packet. The State's petition p5 confirms repeated state/federal presentation. The amended habeas petition reproduced in the Opposition, pp4–6, identifies the proof-beyond-reasonable-doubt and jury-trial theories and reports their state presentation. Appendix E footnote 2 records an earlier State exhaustion objection to the directed-verdict theory, but its OCR continuation is damaged. That fragment alone does not establish a surviving bar, defeat the approved intake or authorize reopening a resolved habeas gate.

The Opposition pp14–15 separately objects that the special-circumstance cure argument was not presented below and says State counsel acknowledged that at the en banc argument. The filing establishes the objection and claimed concession. No en banc argument transcript has been independently read here; the concession is not certified. The Court must distinguish a newly presented question from an additional argument within a preserved question and consider any first-instance factual difficulty. It may not deem the special-circumstance issue forfeited solely because an Opposition asserts it, nor pretend no objection exists.

**Petitioner identity.** The certiorari petition names the People of California and Attorney General Daniel Lungren. The Opposition pp1–2 and 5 contends neither was a party below and invokes §1254(1) and the Rules. Its attachments establish that the September 1991 amended habeas caption named Gomez and Borg, the verified answer described them as the proper respondents, and the later stipulation/order substituted Merkle for Borg. The answer's footnote describes the original respondent Rowland's replacement; the Attorney General appears as counsel on the reproduced district-court answer. The en banc opinion's own caption nevertheless includes John Van de Kamp alongside Gomez and Merkle. The sources therefore do not justify a neutral finding that the Supreme Court petitioners indisputably lacked party status or that automatic succession indisputably cures the discrepancy.

The official 1995 Rules were checked: 12.4 permits separate/joint petitions by interested parties and prevents later joinder in a petition by an unlisted party; 12.6 addresses parties below and their status here; 14.1(a) includes subsidiary questions fairly within the presented question, and 14.1(b) requires the party list; 15.2 requires timely disclosure of the relevant objections and allows certain unraised nonjurisdictional objections to be treated as waived. These provisions do not themselves decide this objection. Rule 35.3 provides automatic succession for a public officer who is a party in an official capacity to a proceeding **in this Court**, and disregards misnomer not affecting substantial rights; it does not establish an officer's original or continuing party status below. A succession or representative-petition theory must identify its actual predicate and applicable authority. The question of party status must be resolved if the chosen merits path requires it; a merely formal error cannot be presumed fatal, and a jurisdictional prerequisite cannot be assumed away.

## 6. Supported competing paths and limits

These are neutral analytical possibilities under current law, not assertions of new party filings or choices by any Justice.

1. **Method correction with further review:** treat the omission as assessable trial error, apply or explain the defined O'Neal category, and determine whether the Ninth Circuit's necessary-findings limitation is legally too restrictive. A confined remand may then permit whole-record Chapman review. Its stated ground must be a justified method/scope defect or unresolved decisional task, not the false assertion that Chapman was never considered or that an operator lacks a file.
2. **Sustain the lower result under Chapman:** explain why the actual findings and the legally permissible whole-record inquiry do not establish beyond reasonable doubt that the error made no contribution, addressing the State's charge, special-circumstance, evidence and capacity arguments and the preserved objection to first-instance use. The state court's contrary legal conclusion does not itself foreclose this route.
3. **Find harmlessness on a sufficient supported record:** address the defense evidence, competing inferences, instruction relationships and the narrower actual finding claimed. Such a result cannot rest on automatic identity between knowledge and purpose, intent to kill and purpose to rob, or general capacity and the particular purpose. It requires a real conclusion under the governing standard, not substitution of historical Brecht or an invented jury finding.
4. **Structural or different-standard treatment:** explain the exact distinction or extension from Sullivan, Gaudin and O'Neal rather than declaring every element omission structural or silently restoring historical collateral law. This source validation supplies no answer to that legal choice.
5. **Threshold or vehicle disposition:** resolve the petitioner-identity objection and the preservation/first-instance issue on their actual legal predicates. The accepted scheduled merits posture does not eliminate a genuine jurisdictional defect. Conversely, a source uncertainty or imperfect caption does not itself establish one. Any further proceeding must have an independent judicial purpose rather than serving solely as an operator research request.

If relief follows, it concerns only the affected Mannix convictions and must use the governing conditional-relief/reasonable-retrial framework. Neither a finding of error nor identification of a wrong review method alone commands immediate release, a new deadline or reopening of Clark/special-circumstance punishment.
