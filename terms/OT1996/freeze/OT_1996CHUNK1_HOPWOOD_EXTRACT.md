- **Question Presented:** (1) Whether achieving racial and ethnic diversity in higher education constitutes a compelling governmental interest sufficient to justify considering an applicant's race in admissions under the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.; (2) Whether a public university may employ race-conscious admissions preferences to remedy the effects of past discrimination, and what showing of prior discrimination and remedial necessity the Constitution requires.; (3) Whether the University of Texas School of Law's admissions system satisfies the constitutional requirements governing racial classifications, and what relief is available to applicants subjected to an unconstitutional admissions process.
- **Questions granted:** Review is granted on the questions stated in Section I. Companion scope and excluded issues are identified below.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit reversed and remanded in No. 94-50664. It held that the Law School could not use race as an admissions factor to obtain diversity or remedy discrimination outside the Law School, directed reconsideration of damages, and left the plaintiffs entitled to reapply under a constitutionally lawful system. It dismissed the related intervention appeal for want of jurisdiction.
* **Relief Requested:** Texas and the university defendants seek rejection of the Fifth Circuit’s categorical prohibition and preservation of lawful authority to consider race in a properly designed program. The rejected applicants seek affirmance of the judgment that the 1992 process was unconstitutional, appropriate declaratory and prospective relief, and compensatory relief supported by causation.
* **Material Record:** The 1992 process used a Texas Index based principally on grade-point average and LSAT score. It employed materially different presumptive-admit, discretionary-review, and presumptive-deny thresholds for Black and Mexican-American applicants than for white and other nonpreferred applicants; color-coded applications; used a separate minority admissions subcommittee and separate waiting-list practices; and pursued stated enrollment aspirations of approximately ten percent Mexican-American and five percent Black students. Four white Texas residents were denied admission. After a bench trial, the District Court held that the system violated equal protection in its operation, but it regarded educational diversity and remediation as permissible interests, declined a broad injunction, awarded each plaintiff nominal damages, and allowed fee-free reapplication. The challenged 1992 system had been discontinued by the time of Supreme Court review. The record does not establish the design or operation of a later admissions system and does not establish that any plaintiff necessarily would have been admitted under every lawful race-neutral process.

The discontinued program and undeveloped later policy materially limit broad prospective relief. The intervention appeal was separately dismissed below and is outside the granted questions in this case. Remedy, causation, proper defendant, immunity, and the distinct statutory channels must remain separate.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Permissible objectives and standard

* **Reasoning Below:** The District Court applied strict scrutiny but accepted diversity and remediation as compelling; the Fifth Circuit rejected diversity as a permissible admissions justification and limited remediation to the Law School’s own proved discrimination and present effects.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** Texas argues that the Fifth Circuit improperly converted a challenge to one obsolete system into a categorical prohibition, ignored *Bakke*, and prevented universities from pursuing educational diversity or remedying the present consequences of a documented segregated educational system.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** The applicants argue that admissions are discrete, zero-sum governmental benefits, the classifications therefore receive strict scrutiny, racial balancing and generalized remediation cannot justify them, and no sufficiently bounded remedial predicate or individualized design supported the 1992 process.
* **Material Alternative or Unreached Ground:** The Court may invalidate the 1992 system for lack of narrow tailoring without categorically resolving every possible diversity-based program.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Recognition of a potentially permissible objective would not validate separate racial tracks, lower thresholds, numerical balancing, stereotypes, or a program lacking individual consideration and temporal limits.

### Issue 2: Application

* **Reasoning Below:** Both courts found serious constitutional defects in the 1992 machinery, though they differed about permissible interests and the scope of the resulting legal rule.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The defendants emphasize the individualized elements retained within the process, Texas’s history of de jure educational exclusion, and the educational need to broaden participation.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** The plaintiffs emphasize that race changed presumptive thresholds and review channels, applications were visibly sorted by race, and the system pursued target percentages without the individualized, bounded fit strict scrutiny requires.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** A court may not supply missing findings about present effects, necessity, or alternatives.

### Issue 3: Relief

* **Reasoning Below:** The District Court awarded nominal damages and fee-free reapplication but denied broader relief; the Fifth Circuit remanded for reconsideration of damages and announced a broad prohibition on race as an admissions factor.
* **Petitioner or Appellant:** The defendants argue that the obsolete policy and uncertain admission outcomes preclude broad prospective relief and compensatory awards not tied to a lawful counterfactual.
* **Respondent or Appellee:** The plaintiffs argue that unequal processing itself is cognizable and that factual uncertainty created by the unlawful system should not erase nominal, declaratory, or causally supported relief.
* **Necessary Concession or Limiting Principle:** Neither invalidity nor a same-decision showing authorizes future use of the challenged process; neither invalidity nor competitive injury automatically proves entitlement to admission or compensatory damages.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY
