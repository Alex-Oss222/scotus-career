## 55. Nabozny v. Podlesny — March 10, 1997

**Disposition by component:** All eight Associates affirm reinstatement of the sex and sexual-orientation equal-protection claims against Podlesny, Davis, Blauert and the District, affirm denial of individual qualified immunity on those supported equality claims, and affirm the rejection of the two preserved due-process theories. The case returns for ordinary proceedings on the surviving equality claims. No official's liability, municipal policy, damages, or particular injunction is established. The District has no qualified immunity; the surviving municipal claim still requires Monell attribution and causation.

**Scope and assumed record:** This is review of a final summary judgment, not a defendant-only collateral appeal. The whole record is available for equality because the district court ventured outside the moving grounds; for due process, assume the whole record in Nabozny's favor without resolving the separate local-rule question. Defendants' contrary account that they investigated and punished complaints does not displace the favorable account. The preserved increased-risk and harmful-policy theories receive their own analysis; no custody requirement disposes of them by itself.

**Equality rule and application:** When public officials intentionally withhold ordinarily available protection from a student because of sex or sexual orientation, the unequal administration is subject to equal protection even though substantive due process imposes no general duty to protect from private violence. Intentional sex discrimination must substantially serve an important governmental objective; orientation discrimination here must at least rationally relate to a legitimate purpose, without deciding a heightened tier. Unequal disciplinary outcomes, negligence, breach of state policy, knowledge or deliberate indifference without the required discriminatory purpose do not alone establish the equality violation. Feeney supplies purposeful selection because of the adverse effect, Arlington Heights permits proof through departures and contextual evidence, and Yick Wo forbids intentionally unequal administration.

The alleged statements linking nonprotection to being male or openly gay, ordinary enforcement admitted by defendants, differential treatment of male-on-female abuse and repeated departures permit a jury to find purposeful discrimination. The missing names and dates in some comparator examples bear on proof; they do not erase the more direct evidence at summary judgment. No legitimate disciplinary objective explains knowingly treating this student as less entitled to protection because he is male or gay on the favorable account. Neither every complaint nor every student must receive identical discipline. Romer and Walmer govern the present ordinary-review inquiry at their actual scopes: conceivable legitimate grounds, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit remain permissible, but bare harm and irrational prejudice are not. Walmer's military service relationship supplies no rationale for abandoning ordinary school protection. Virginia supplies no orientation tier. The school-presentation Brown and optional-program Curtis holdings decide no immunity from intentional discriminatory treatment.

**Equality immunity:** On that supported account, the individual officials had notice from conduct-time equality law without a factually identical school-harassment decision. Before 1988, Reed, Weinberger, Hogan, Yick Wo, Feeney and Arlington Heights already prohibited arbitrary sex stereotypes and purposeful, irrational unequal administration. Bohen, 799 F.2d 1180 (1986), is relevant circuit authority for intentional sex discrimination; its employment setting is preserved. Cleburne and Moreno reject irrational prejudice and bare harm. The state antidiscrimination policy corroborates knowledge of the protective practice and the classification; its violation is not by itself a federal violation or the reason immunity fails. DeShaney's later express equality reservation confirms the distinction for conduct after its date but is not needed to furnish pre-1989 notice. Bowers concerned regulation of specified sexual conduct and did not authorize private battery or intentional denial of the ordinary protection against it. No inference of future sexual conduct or criminal offense is supplied. Romer, Virginia, Elder and current-term cases govern today's analysis but cannot retroactively give notice for earlier acts. Individual responsibility, purpose and causation remain disputed, and defendants may prevail on their factual account.

**Due-process rule and application:** Assume without deciding that sufficiently culpable official creation or increase of a danger, or an attributable policy affirmatively encouraging private injury, can establish an official deprivation. The preserved theory that failure to punish emboldened assailants does not itself establish that affirmative contribution. Mere omission does not become an affirmative deprivation by repeating it as a policy. The conclusion does not rest on the district court's broader proposition that private assailants can never be connected to state action. Pinder expressly preserves affirmative deprivation of available protection; Uhlrig leaves the merits open. Neither supplies a five-part test or mandatory assurance/reliance element.

The full favorable recitation was tested, including acts beyond silence. Podlesny promised protection, responded to the mock assault with the alleged stereotypes, and the school counseled Nabozny for leaving without permission and returned him to his ordinary schedule. That is serious evidence of discriminatory nonprotection, but this preserved record does not establish a separate official act directing an identified new assault or depriving otherwise available protection. Davis and Blauert referred Nabozny to Reeder to change his schedule to reduce exposure; the school later placed him in special education with Huntley and Grande. The favorable facts do not identify a remaining named defendant's deliberate placement decision or establish that this rearrangement, as distinct from the ongoing exposure and nonpunishment, increased the danger that produced a particular later injury. Calling a scheduling change affirmative does not by itself establish the constitutional contribution or culpability. This is a limitation of what the authenticated favorable account establishes, not an inference from unavailable research papers.

## 56. Brown v. Polk County — March 17, 1997

**Disposition:** All eight Associates affirm the reversal of judgment for the County on the reviewed broad religious restrictions and Title VII accommodation/discrimination issues, with the constitutional and statutory scope stated below and ordinary remedial proceedings on return. Affirmance does not approve a right to use subordinates or public resources for worship, determine every prayer session's voluntariness, resolve every individual defendant's damages exposure, or disturb unreviewed claims. No damages sum or unconditional reinstatement is ordered.

**Statutory rule:** A public employer must reasonably accommodate a known sincere religious observance or practice unless it demonstrates inability to do so without undue hardship under then-governing Hardison: more than a de minimis cost, including concrete noncash operational burdens. An effective reasonable accommodation suffices under Ansonia even if not the employee's preferred arrangement; there is no independent duty to negotiate or try every conceivable alternative once effective accommodation or adequate hardship exists. When the conflict remains unresolved, the justification must address supported materially distinct practices and accommodations rather than transfer the burden from one activity to all religious conduct. Beadle governs with eight direct Associate joins.

The County may restrict use of paid secretarial labor for Bible-study materials, work disruption, harassment and coerced participation through appropriate rules. An established burden from one of those practices would not justify removing a privately kept Bible from a desk or banning all voluntary, nondisruptive personal religious conversation. The record's broad directives swept across that distinction. Mere discomfort with religious subject matter or an abstract Establishment Clause concern supplies no hardship finding. Concrete supervision, efficiency and coercion concerns may justify narrower controls through evidence and reasonable operational inferences; neither an actual accident nor an experiment is necessary.

**Constitutional rule:** Public employment does not make every private religious act governmental worship. A religion-specific directive extending to private possession and voluntary personal expression must satisfy the applicable speech and free-exercise limits; the blanket restriction here is not sustained merely by invoking separation of church and state. The County may prevent actual official sponsorship, supervisory coercion and misuse of public resources. The distinction depends on the actual act and context, including who directs it and whether subordinates are pressured, rather than a conclusory label of private or official speech. No universal disclaimer, monitoring requirement, open-forum status for the workplace or right to lead prayers at department meetings is imposed.

Lukumi's objective-targeting and tailoring rules prevent banning private religious practice merely because religious. Smith's neutral-law rule preserves genuine resource and duty restrictions. Pickering, Connick and Givhan retain the actual public-employee expression inquiry; NTEU supports matching a broad prospective restriction to its reach but does not make every supervisory instruction citizen speech. No constitutional claim is won solely because Title VII is violated. Pinette, Chabad and Good News reject automatic government attribution on public premises while retaining genuine establishment concerns; none supplies categorical forum immunity. The private desk Bible is materially different from a government-sponsored display or a supervisor's workplace prayer, including for Justices who rejected the forum application in those cases.

**Causation and relief:** The administrator's testimony that the religion-related reprimand was one factor in the discharge precludes ignoring the protected component as irrelevant; it does not establish that every reason was unlawful or decide all causal defenses. The lawful and unlawful conduct must be separated on the actual trial record under the governing pre-1991 statutory law. Constitutional retaliation retains Mt. Healthy's motivating-factor requirement and the employer's preponderance same-decision defense; Waters's genuine-and-reasonable-account protection applies only to actual reliance on an otherwise lawful ground, not a post hoc lawful explanation. Neither rule automatically establishes Title VII causation. Landgraf forbids retrospective use of the 1991 Act's new compensatory/punitive damages and dependent jury election for the preenactment conduct. Preexisting equitable/employment remedies remain available on their own conditions; municipal attribution, personal participation, immunity and the appropriate relief are not assumed.

**Adversarial paths:** The County's strongest path treats a department head's religious activities as one course of supervisory conduct: staff labor and meeting prayer may reasonably suggest coercion and official affiliation, making prospective restrictions and accommodation refusal lawful. That supports tailored rules, not the breadth encompassing a private Bible and genuinely voluntary conversation. Brown's strongest path treats private expression as constitutionally protected and free from categorical establishment exclusion. It does not sustain an entitlement to public financing, subordinate labor or pressure-free status by assertion. The supported common disposition corrects the blanket treatment while preserving the County's lawful operational authority and distinct causation defenses. Remanding remedial and genuinely unresolved applications supplies no judicial finding that attendance at every actual prayer was voluntary.

## 57. Commissioner v. Estate of Hubert — March 18, 1997

**Disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the reviewed deductions on a shared limited rationale rejecting automatic dollar-for-dollar reduction and preserving the Tax Court's material-limitation finding under the Commissioner's particular burden. Scalia and Thomas reverse and remand for deduction adjustment under the Commissioner's permissible categorical construction. Both groups address the marital and charitable deductions only on the expense question the parties litigated alike; no new qualification issue is opened.

**Affirming rule:** Payment of administration expenses from postmortem income attributable to qualifying property does not, by that fact alone, require reducing the estate-tax deduction dollar for dollar. The valuation inquiry concerns the net date-of-death value of the interest passing and the effect of a material limitation on its income rights, not a universal exemption for income-funded expenses. The regulation's example says that the rule may apply to income used for administration; it does not delete the material-limitation condition or prescribe automatic subtraction of every subsequently paid dollar. A charge can affect value even though postmortem income itself is not in the gross estate, so that circumstance alone does not defeat the Commissioner. Conversely, state-law authority to charge income does not establish immateriality.

The six affirmers apply Auer's eight-Associate rule and TJU's controlling-weight standard; none joins an independent-interpretation replacement. An agency's considered permissible reading can control even when presented in litigation. Here the automatic dollar-for-dollar position treats the regulatory example as a mandatory measurement rule and gives no work to the actual value effect of a material limitation. The regulatory words, date-of-death valuation structure and distinction between valuation and qualification prevent that categorical construction. An alternative reasonable judicial reading alone would not suffice; the defect is that this proposed automatic consequence bypasses conditions the regulation retains. Section 2056(b)(4)(B)'s net-value principle does not itself supply the proposed universal postmortem-income measure. Revenue Ruling 93-48 concerns deferred-tax interest and does not expressly decide every administration expense or wholly erase Revenue Ruling 73-98. It is considered at that scope, not used as a dispositive inconsistency finding or universal administrative concession.

**Particular burden and remedy:** Rule 142(a) places this omitted-notice adjustment on the Commissioner, not the estate. The Commissioner maintained automatic reduction and did not offer and preserve an alternative quantitative-materiality challenge to the Tax Court's finding. Rejecting automatic reduction therefore leaves no established alternative basis to reverse that finding. Affirmance does not establish that $1.5 million is always immaterial, use total estate size as the sole denominator, guarantee an identical result for another income interest, or adopt a universal present-value formula. Amount, duration and the nature of the income right can be relevant to actual value, but the six do not impose an exhaustive factor test, numerical safe harbor, fixed denominator or mandatory new hearing. Remand to develop a different quantitative case is not justified merely because another theory might have been litigated. Expenses charged to principal retain their established reduction treatment. The parties' parallel treatment resolves this disputed charitable expense adjustment without declaring §§2055 and 2056 or their regulations identical.

**Dissenting rule and remedy:** Scalia and Thomas regard the Commissioner's construction as at least permissible under TJU and Auer. In their reading, “material” can mean consequential to the value transferred; income diverted from the spouse or charity is unavailable to that beneficiary and the regulation expressly selects administration-funded income as its example. The legal principle of net value supports recognizing each established diversion, without a separate judicial substantiality threshold or a gift of taxable-estate value merely because local fiduciary law calls the source income. They do not read the example's “may” as an unconditional textual “must”; their ground is that the administering agency permissibly supplies the uniform construction within the text. The actual expense payment is established and satisfies the Commissioner's burden on that legal theory; they do not shift Rule 142(a) to the estate or treat nonexistent evidence as produced. They would reverse the no-reduction rule and remand for lawful computation of the deductions, not enter a newly calculated tax deficiency or decide trust qualification. They reject a court-created quantitative-materiality burden as the premise of affirmance, while recognizing that a different unsupported factual theory could not be supplied on appeal.

**Adversarial comparison:** The strongest challenge to affirmance is deference: the example directly concerns these expenses and a consequence-for-value understanding of materiality need not be unreasonable merely because another reading seems better. The affirmers answer that actual date-of-death value, a material limitation and optional application cannot be converted into automatic future-dollar subtraction; the particular litigation burden then resolves the case without a judicial formula. The strongest challenge to reversal is the same text's preserved limitation and difference between future income and date-of-death transferred value. Scalia and Thomas answer that the Commissioner reasonably regards actual diversion from the beneficial income stream as precisely the relevant value consequence, and the example confirms interpretive permission. The split concerns this construction's fit, not whether Auer controls, whether litigation form automatically disqualifies an agency, or whether the estate bears this adjustment's burden.

## 59. Bennett v. Spear — March 19, 1997

**Disposition:** All eight Associates reverse the dismissal of the reviewed ESA/APA claims and remand for their lawful adjudication. The unchallenged dismissal of the fourth, NEPA environmental-assessment claim is undisturbed. No biological conclusion, critical-habitat designation, statutory violation, replacement water level, damages, fee amount or substantive injunction is ordered.

**Article III:** The alleged reduction of petitioners' own irrigation water is a concrete economic injury, not a generalized interest in statutory compliance. At the pleading stage the Biological Opinion's operational restrictions, incidental-take conditions and the Bureau's announced compliance support traceability and likely beneficial relief. The agency's legal and practical influence can cause injury through another agency's predictable choices; no rule requires the Service itself to deliver the water or certainty that every ultimate choice changes. Petitioners must prove standing with the evidence required at later stages. Lujan's controlling elements and procedural-right limits remain; its three-Justice absent-agency application is not a categorical bar. Humane Society lacked a link between the later permit amendment and the completed transfer or useful relief; the pleaded Bureau response here supplies the distinct link. Tracy confirms that one's own economic injury is cognizable, not that every economic plaintiff necessarily has standing.

**Citizen-suit channel and statutory zone:** Section 1540(g)'s authorization to any person, read with its particular enforcement channels and broad remedial setting, removes an additional species-preservation zone restriction for a claim that subsection actually authorizes. It does not erase Article III or permit every challenge to administration. Section (g)(1)(C) permits an action alleging failure of the Secretary to perform a nondiscretionary act or duty under §1533; petitioners' allegation of a de facto critical-habitat designation without §1533(b)(2)'s required consideration of economic effects is within that asserted duty channel at this threshold stage. The characterization and ultimate merits remain disputed: no actual designation, triggered duty or omitted legally required consideration is found.

Section (g)(1)(A), addressing a person alleged to violate the Act or regulations, is not a general authorization to challenge every alleged maladministration by the Secretary of §1536. Read in context with the distinct Secretary-duty provision, it does not replace ordinary APA review of these consultation claims. Section (g)(1)(B)'s emergency enforcement mechanism is different and is not invoked. Section 1533(b)(2)'s mandatory consideration of economic and other relevant impacts when designating or revising critical habitat remains distinct from discretionary exclusion when exclusion benefits outweigh inclusion benefits, subject to the prohibition on exclusion where the best scientific and commercial data show failure to designate will result in extinction. Reviewability does not command a particular exclusion or import that economic-balancing rule wholesale into §1536 consultation.

**APA route and provision-specific zone:** Sections 702 and 704 supply review of these §1536 consultation claims because the ESA establishes no scheme-specific preclusion of that route and the citizen-suit channel supplies no other adequate judicial remedy for these particular administrative claims. Section 1540(g)(5) preserves other available enforcement/remedial rights; it does not itself establish every APA prerequisite. Dalton's unanimous preclusion holding rested on the indivisible base-closure package, deadlines and confined review scheme, which this ESA challenge does not present. Its four-Justice recommendation-finality theory is not law. Section 1536(a)(2)'s best-scientific-and-commercial-data command serves sound decisionmaking and protects affected interests against restrictions unsupported by the required data; the irrigators' alleged injury from such restrictions is at least arguably within that provision's zone. The inquiry concerns the provision sued upon, not an assumption that every ESA provision protects only species or that every economically affected person may enforce every conservation duty. Agency scientific judgment and the merits of the data claim remain for lawful review, not de novo judicial biology.

**Final agency action:** The Service's Biological Opinion completes its consultation determination and has legal consequences through the incidental-take statement: compliance with its conditions changes the taking liability regime under §1536(o)(2). It is not merely tentative advice. The Bureau retains its own obligations and operational choices, but that does not erase the completed Service action or the conditions attached to the protection from prohibited-taking liability. Franklin's controlling five-Justice finality holding requires examination of completed subordinate action despite a later governmental step; its presidential-status reservation remains irrelevant here. This reasoning satisfies both completion and consequence requirements without holding that every advisory report or prediction is final. The Bureau's stated compliance separately reinforces the pleaded causal account; practical influence alone is not silently made a substitute for finality's legal consequences.

**Preserved statutory qualifications:** Under §1536(a)(2), agencies must avoid likely jeopardy and destruction or adverse modification of designated critical habitat unless a Committee exemption under (h) has been granted, using the best scientific and commercial data available. No exemption is found here. Under (b)(4), an incidental-take statement requires the Secretary's specified no-violation/alternative and incidental-take conclusions, and, for a marine mammal, the separate statutory taking authorization; it specifies impact, reasonable/prudent minimization measures, applicable marine-mammal measures and implementing terms including reporting. Under (o)(2), taking complying with those terms is not prohibited taking; this is not an unconditional immunity from every ESA obligation. The distinct exemption-based protection in (o)(1) is not this statement's source. No marine-mammal finding is needed or made for these fish.

The citizen-suit notice limits also remain intact: (g)(1)(A) generally requires 60 days' written notice to the Secretary and alleged violator and is barred by a commenced civil penalty action or a commenced diligently prosecuted United States criminal action addressing the violation. The (B) channel separately requires 60 days' notice explaining the claimed emergency and is barred by the Secretary's commenced diligent emergency-determination action. The (C) channel requires 60 days' notice to the Secretary, except suit respecting an emergency posing significant risk to the well-being of a fish, wildlife or plant species may follow notification immediately. No unproved compliance, applicable emergency exception or enforcement bar is adjudicated. Section 702's nonmoney-relief waiver preserves other legal/equitable limits and statutory prohibitions; §704 retains its no-other-adequate-remedy and finality rules, including its specified treatment of reconsideration and administrative appeals. This reviewability disposition neither invents a notice defect nor silently waives a preserved one.

**Adversarial paths:** Respondents' strongest Article III path identifies the Bureau's independent choice and uncertain ultimate water outcome; it does not defeat sufficiently pleaded predictable compliance and the possibility of concrete relief from the challenged constraints. Their statutory path correctly rejects using (g)(1)(A) to supervise every Secretary act, but it does not establish preclusion of a distinct APA action. Their finality path emphasizes that a biological opinion does not itself dictate every Bureau operation; it fails to account for the completed consultation and conditional taking protection. Petitioners' broadest path would treat “any person” as enough for all claims and relief; it fails because Article III, each citizen-suit channel, provision-specific APA interests and finality remain independent requirements. The shared reverse-and-remand path gives each limitation its work and preserves all merits disputes.
