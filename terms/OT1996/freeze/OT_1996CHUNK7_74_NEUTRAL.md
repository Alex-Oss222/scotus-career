## Questions, record and scope

The granted municipal question is whether Sheriff Moore's single hiring decision, as stipulated final county policymaker, supplies the culpability and causal responsibility required by §1983 for Deputy Burns's established constitutional injury to Jill Brown. The County requests reversal and judgment in its favor; Brown seeks affirmance. The underlying force verdict is not retried through this municipal question. The sheriff's individual liability was dismissed before trial; the defendant remaining with the County below was Burns.

The operative reported lower decision is **67 F.3d 1174, October 23, 1995**, a substitute panel opinion withdrawing **53 F.3d 1410** in full. The packet's September 8 date is not the date of this reported judgment. Brown and her husband were stopped in Texas after crossing from Oklahoma; the pursuit and arrest circumstances were disputed, and the jury accepted the account supporting excessive force, false arrest and false imprisonment. Burns used an arm-bar extraction that seriously injured Brown's knees. The record's later-discovered weapons do not retroactively supply justification. The county question assumes the underlying constitutional injury; no new seizure theory is introduced here.

Moore ordered but did not fully examine a criminal-record printout and made no further inquiries. The lower recitation lists nine moving violations, intoxicated control of a vehicle, suspended-license events, an assault-and-battery arrest, false-identification conviction and resisting-arrest event, with testimony about public drunkenness and unexamined probation status. Do not silently turn each arrest into a conviction or infer the particular facts of prior assaults. Burns was **the son of Moore's nephew**, not Moore's nephew. Burns's grandfather had worked with the department for over sixteen years. Burns was not authorized to carry a firearm or drive a squad car, but was assigned law-enforcement duties. The sheriff had performed adequate checks and assured certification for other deputies.

Brown's expert described the history as showing disregard for law and risks of abuse/excessive force; the County's expert agreed it warranted concern and further inquiry and doubted he would have hired Burns. These are expert opinions, not independent findings that a particular later constitutional injury was inevitable. Oklahoma prohibited hiring persons convicted of a felony **or a crime involving moral turpitude** under the law quoted below; the absence of felonies alone does not establish compliance with every state-law hiring limit. The federal issue remains distinct from a state negligence or eligibility rule.

The jury charge used a deliberate-indifference formulation requiring an obvious need for closer scrutiny and inadequate scrutiny so likely to result in constitutional violations that Moore could reasonably be found deliberately indifferent to the plaintiff's constitutional needs. It is reproduced in the saved lower opinion's footnotes. The County stipulated policymaker status and did not object to instructions describing that status. The granted sufficiency/fault/causation objections are distinct from the lost-income cross-appeal, where a different preservation failure occurred. Do not transfer that waiver to the county hiring question.

The lower majority held that the hiring decision could constitute policy, that evidence permitted an inference of knowingly disregarded screening risk, and that authorizing an unqualified applicant to make forcible arrests supplied a direct constitutional causal link. Emilio Garza agreed evidence could show deliberate indifference in the background inquiry but dissented on municipal policy and causation: a facially lawful hire must not become constitutional liability from one employee injury and but-for employment. He invoked Tuttle's greater proof requirement and Pembaur's direct-order distinction. Both positions must be tested at their actual strength.

## Entering law and balanced paths

Use [the exact slice](../entering-law/OT_1996CHUNK7_74_ENTERING_LAW.md). **Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation**, effective Collins, February 26, 1992, separates constitutional injury from municipal attribution and rejects an additional freestanding governmental-abuse element. **Municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules**, effective Leatherman, March 3, 1993, governs pleading, not trial sufficiency. **Municipal liability defenses and connected interlocutory review**, effective Swint, March 1, 1995, preserves the county/officer distinction and does not add a merits immunity. The exact register qualifications and dates are copied.

Plakas, January 9, 1995, is an eight-Justice municipal holding: cognizable federal deprivation, municipal attribution, applicable culpability and causation remain separately necessary; reasonable final force does not answer every independent municipal wrong. Its rejected equipment/training theory establishes no universal hiring rule. Nabozny, March 10, 1997, unanimously preserves municipal attribution and causation apart from the individual's wrongdoing. Blessing's April 21 statutory-rights decision does not replace Monell's constitutional municipal framework or overrule §1983. Its noncontrolling funding-contract reservation is not a county hiring rule.

Monell bars employer-based vicarious liability; Pembaur permits a final policymaker's single deliberate choice to constitute policy in appropriate circumstances, without making every employee act policy. Canton rejects a universal rule limiting liability to policies unconstitutional on their face, while demanding municipal deliberate indifference and an actual close causal link for failure-to-train liability. Its objective municipal inquiry remains distinct from the employee's underlying constitutional culpability. Tuttle guards against inferring a culpable policy merely from a single employee violation.

Supported alternatives:

- Affirm if final hiring attribution, the specific known record, deliberate failure to investigate and the assigned arrest duties reasonably support the jury's municipal fault and causal findings under the correct demanding rule. Strongest objection: general unfitness, failure to investigate and family favoritism do not necessarily show an obvious risk of this constitutional injury or more than but-for employment.
- Reverse for legal insufficiency if, even crediting favorable evidence and reasonable jury inferences, the applicant-specific facts do not establish the required constitutional risk and close causal link. Strongest objection: treating the record as merely a lawful misdemeanor history may discount the complete course, expert testimony, obvious screening need and actual authorization to use coercive authority.
- Vacate and remand under a clarified hiring-specific standard if the verdict or appellate analysis used a materially broader culpability/causation rule and lawful further proceedings remain available. Strongest objection: a full opportunity to prove the necessary municipal elements may make judgment rather than a new evidentiary chance appropriate. The sufficiency/instruction distinction must be resolved before choosing remedy.

A single decision is neither categorically immune nor categorically sufficient. No path may impose liability solely because better hiring could have prevented employment. No path may erase the independently established employee injury merely because municipal attribution fails. Reserved individual, state-law and earnings issues remain outside the granted question unless control establishes a separate granted component.
