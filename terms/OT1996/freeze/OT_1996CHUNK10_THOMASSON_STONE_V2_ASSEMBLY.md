## B. JUDGMENT, REASONING, AND REMEDY

- **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand to set aside Thomasson's discharge on as-applied Fifth Amendment equal-protection grounds. Stone expects to dissent.
- **Stone's Remedy and Remand Instruction:** The discharge based on Thomasson's identity statement and the resulting presumption shall be set aside, and the matter returned to the Navy for further proceedings consistent with the opinion. Actual evidence of prohibited conduct or intent remains subject to otherwise constitutional military rules. No ruling is made on the separate due-process, speech, or substantive-liberty claims.

### Issue 1: Construction of the statements provision (no independent statutory error)

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory question and finds no independent statutory error.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** I accept that the Navy Board followed the prescribed procedures, considered Thomasson's evidence, and found the statutory presumption unrebutted. I find no independent statutory error.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** None; the statutory claim supplies no independent ground for relief.

### Issue 2: Equal protection

- **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the classification and its as-applied fit. This ground alone resolves the discharge, so he does not reach due process, speech, or substantive liberty.
- **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether discharge may rest on the identity statement and resulting presumption without independent evidence of prohibited conduct, propensity, or intent.
- **Stone's Legal Position:** Congress has legitimate interests in military readiness, discipline, privacy, and unit cohesion. Its military judgments deserve substantial deference. However, accommodating hostility or prejudice toward gay service members is not itself a legitimate governmental objective. Under our simulated *Romer v. Evans* (1996), ordinary rational-basis review permits reasonable predictions and imperfect classifications, but a class-specific disadvantage must bear a rational relationship to legitimate governmental interests. *Romer* establishes no heightened scrutiny for sexual orientation, and its broad denial of civil protections differs from this military policy.
- **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Here, Thomasson's declaration of identity and the resulting unrebutted presumption were decisive. I would conclude that using his identity as a proxy for prohibited conduct, propensity, or intent lacks a sufficient rational connection to the military interests asserted in this particular application. The absence of independent evidence informs that conclusion but does not automatically establish a constitutional violation.
- **Boundary or Reserved Question:** I would therefore set aside the discharge without invalidating all military conduct restrictions or deciding the separate speech, due-process, and substantive-liberty claims. The position does not hold the presumption irrebuttable, hold the statute invalid in all applications, treat Congress's findings as pretext, immunize misconduct, order reinstatement or back pay beyond the ordinary consequences of setting aside the discharge, or decide the separate due-process, speech, or substantive-liberty questions.

## D. VERIFIED PRECEDENT APPLICATION AND POSITION STATUS

Stone's resolution rests on the as-applied equal-protection ground under ordinary rational-basis review as applied in the simulated *Romer v. Evans* (1996); it uses no heightened scrutiny and no new standard. He finds no independent statutory error and preserves substantial deference to supported military judgment.

## E. REBRIEF ANSWERS AND POSITION STATUS

**Question 1.** I accept that the Navy Board followed the prescribed procedures, considered Thomasson's evidence, and found the statutory presumption unrebutted. I find no independent statutory error. Stone would set aside the discharge on the as-applied equal-protection ground.

**Question 2.** The speech question is presented but Stone would not render a separate First Amendment holding because the as-applied equal-protection ground already sets aside the same discharge. A statement may be relevant evidence under a valid conduct rule; it cannot be deemed actual prohibited conduct solely by repeating the identity presumption. His mandate reserves the independent speech claim rather than approving or rejecting a general military restriction on orientation speech.

**Question 3.** Congress's military judgments on discipline, cohesion, privacy and readiness deserve substantial deference; accommodating hostility or prejudice toward gay service members is not itself a legitimate governmental objective. Stone would preserve otherwise constitutional conduct rules and grant only the stated as-applied discharge relief.
