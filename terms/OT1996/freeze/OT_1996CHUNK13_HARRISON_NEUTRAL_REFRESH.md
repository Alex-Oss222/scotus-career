## Actual Faragher law mapped to the issues

**Questions 1–2: delegated power and managerial rank.** Faragher Part I, joined by all nine Justices, applies Andrade's plaintiff-proved connection between actual delegated supervisory authority and creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. Rank or proximity alone is insufficient. Higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably necessary. Daily work control can supply the required aid even when the conduct serves personal purposes or violates policy. The actual-authority route remains distinct from scope of employment, negligence and apparent authority.

That law excludes a universal condition that the harasser must be the employer's top-management alter ego or have permission to harass before the actual-authority route can be considered. It likewise excludes automatic employer liability solely from significant supervisory status. Brown's authority over assignments is relevant only with the required connection to the challenged conditions. Faragher does not supply that connection as a fact in Harrison.

**Question 3: instruction, support and prejudice.** Faragher supplies the substantive actual-authority rule, not a rule making every instructional defect reversible. Harrison still presents whether the charge as a whole conveyed a supported lawful route and whether its defect prejudiced the employee. The reproduced charge makes agency, apparent authority in committing the acts, and reasonable reliance cumulative requirements, then defines agency through managerial-control/alter-ego status. The independent actual-authority-aid route must be assessed without making apparent-authority reliance an extra universal prerequisite.

The lower court separately criticized the apparent-authority definition for focusing on Harrison's knowledge without requiring an objectively reasonable belief traceable to the employer's words or conduct. Faragher's Court opinion left apparent authority undecided. Its published separate observations do not convert that alternative into a controlling holding. The lower court's distinct apparent-authority reasoning therefore remains a review issue under the appropriate agency sources, not a new rule supplied by Faragher. Likewise, the lower court's broader references to actual or apparent work authority must not silently enlarge Faragher's actual-delegated-authority holding.

**Policy and response.** Faragher Part III has the eight Associates' full joins. Its ineffective-policy contention failed on its proved record: an uncommunicated policy, noncomplaint to distant higher management and eventual correction did not defeat established actual-authority attribution. The Court reserved a general complete prevention/nonuse defense, its elements, burden, tangible-action relationship and distinct remedial limits. It adopted neither a two-part defense nor a rule forbidding every such defense. Failed dissemination is not negligence per se; later correction does not erase earlier attributable conduct.

Harrison requires its own chronology and policy evidence. Faragher does not establish that Harrison never knew the policy, that every reporting route was unavailable, that her nonuse was unreasonable, or that Potash's response was legally sufficient or insufficient. Effective policy can remain relevant to a properly framed apparent-authority, negligence or remedy inquiry. The reservation does not itself expand Harrison's grant or preservation; any consideration of a broader defense must fit the presented issues and supported record.

**Question 4: contractual exhaustion.** Faragher decided no arbitration, CBA, FAA or statutory-exhaustion issue. Gardner-Denver's independent Title VII right, Gilmer's actual statutory-arbitration agreement and stated reservations, and First Options's antecedent agreement/delegation inquiry remain at their supplied scope. Livadas and Hawaiian Airlines distinguish independent rights from necessary interpretation of disputed contract terms; their preemption holdings do not themselves decide enforceability of every statutory-arbitration agreement.

## What the actual Harrison record establishes

The May 8 appellate opinion reports a jury verdict against Brown for intentional infliction of emotional distress and battery, with $102,500 compensatory and $40,000 punitive damages, and a verdict for Potash on Title VII. It does not supply a set of special findings establishing every Title VII predicate against Potash. Do not infer either that the jury accepted all of Harrison's account or that its employer verdict rejected the underlying harassment rather than attribution.

Brown's employment was uncontroverted. The appellate court described evidence of his significant control over daily work and his lack of top-management status. Harrison's account describes work orders that took her away from her buddy or usual station into isolated areas, repeated refusals and sexual misconduct, and an order to work through lunch after one refusal. These recitations provide concrete evidence relevant to the proposed authority-aid route, rather than a title-only hypothesis. They are not new Supreme Court findings or automatic proof of all elements.

Potash's investigator found Harrison's participation unwilling while accepting Brown's asserted perception of consent. That administrative assessment is neither a judicial finding of welcome conduct nor a complete Title VII disposition. The opinion reports an existing policy, Harrison's discovery of it in another area after several incidents, and its omission from her new-hire materials. It identifies genuine questions concerning prior knowledge. Her July report and Potash's ensuing investigation, promised compensation/treatment, crew change, reprimand, probation and contact restrictions remain distinct from the conditions before notice. The subsequent reduction-in-force termination is not newly adjudicated by this review.

**Preservation and prejudice are concretely presented.** The opinion states that the principal charge was given over Harrison's objection and reproduces her requested instructions 7, 24 and 25. The court rejected the proposed automatic-supervisor-liability theory, while finding that an appropriate authority-aid instruction was required. Thus refusal of every requested sentence and refusal of any instruction on a supported route are different questions. No instruction is owed merely because a party requested it.

The court reviewed the legal charge as a whole independently, the decision whether to give a particular instruction for abuse of discretion, and required prejudice. Its stated prejudice grounds were the alter-ego barrier despite daily-control evidence and non-top-management status, omission of the authority-aid route, and defects in the apparent-authority instruction in light of disputed prior policy knowledge. Those are the lower court's reasons to assess, not a presumed current result. A contrary harmlessness conclusion needs an actual record basis; no unreported curative instruction, special verdict, conceded element or unpreserved objection may be invented. The court's approval of considering an effectively implemented policy within apparent-authority analysis is not approval of an omnibus policy defense.
