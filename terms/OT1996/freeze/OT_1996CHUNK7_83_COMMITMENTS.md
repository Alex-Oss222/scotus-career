## Component A — sequence and actual default

This is permission, not a requirement to bypass state grounds, and it does not permit assumptions about Article III jurisdiction.

The two strongest paths are default first, with a verified adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused state ground, or Teague first, leaving default unresolved. The first would avoid a retroactivity decision if its actual premises were established. On this supplied record, express state reliance and repeated federal assertion do not settle the historical adequacy question or cause/prejudice. The second is lawful because nonretroactivity is itself a granted issue and deciding whether 1986 precedent dictated the claimed rule does not require deciding a new constitutional merits rule. The Court therefore may take that route without declaring the default defense waived or inadequate.

## Component B — the precise rule and finality comparison

The requested rule is not simply that vague capital aggravators require guidance. It is that, in Florida's judge/jury arrangement, constitutionally inadequate guidance to a recommendation with legally consequential influence can infect the final death sentence, so the judge's own valid narrowed findings do not by themselves establish a cure. Espinosa supplies that rule as current law. The question is whether it was dictated, rather than merely supported, by November 24, 1986.

**New-rule path.** Godfrey prohibited unguided capital aggravation. Zant and Barclay preserved distinctions concerning eligibility, weighing and correction; Stringer now establishes the old character of its particular direct-weighing claim but does not itself decide a two-stage recommendation. Spaziano, 468 U.S. 447, 457–467, sustained judicial sentencing responsibility and independent findings despite a jury's advisory role. Baldwin, 472 U.S. 372, 382–388 and note 8, sustained an actually independent judge and reserved a different case of jury influence rather than deciding it. A reasonable 1986 jurist could therefore understand the judge's narrowed independent determination as constitutionally sufficient without the further rule concerning transmitted recommendation error. The rule is new at this finality even though it is governing merits law now.

The strongest objection is functional: a State cannot avoid Godfrey by putting the unguided factor in a body whose recommendation influences the actual sentencer, and Baldwin's reasoning turns precisely on absence of influence. The new-rule answer is not that influence is irrelevant. It is that a holding sustaining independent judicial action and leaving the distinct influence issue open, together with Spaziano's allocation of authority, allowed a reasonable distinction before Espinosa. The later 1987 Smith and 1988 Grossman formulations cannot be silently inserted into 1986. Tedder's 1975 great-weight statement and stringent life-override rule are part of the earlier setting, but the correction rule for a death recommendation still required a further constitutional conclusion on this view.

**Old-rule path.** Godfrey already requires meaningful guidance for a decisional contribution used to select death. Stringer's binding interpretation of that preexisting protection rejects reliance on surviving factors or a formal distinction that leaves invalid aggravation on the actual scale. Tedder, 322 So.2d 908, 910, establishes the recommendation's legally consequential role; the holding arose from a life recommendation, a limit that must be acknowledged. Baldwin's independent-sentencer rationale and Spaziano's approval of assigning final authority to a judge do not authorize constitutionally unguided advice given real weight. Espinosa applies that established function to the transmitted channel; it does not invent the underlying guidance duty or require jury-exclusive sentencing.

The strongest objection is that neither Baldwin's reservation nor Tedder's life-override application dictated the exact death-recommendation cure in 1986. This path treats the relevant rule at its operative functional level: a judge must not give legally operative influence to an unguided aggravation process, regardless of whether the advisory body or judge first encounters the invalid criterion. It does not convert Baldwin's reservation into an affirmative holding, depend on Smith or Grossman as prefinality law, or impose a new single correction mechanism. The old-rule conclusion permits consideration of the claim; it does not establish the particular instructions' invalidity, actual injury, lawful state cure or freedom from default.

Maynard, Clemons, Walton, Smith and Grossman are later than finality. They may identify the subsequently articulated rule or aid explanation of the distinction, but cannot supply a November 1986 precedent. No present Teague approach rests on counting later judicial disagreement or assuming that a later opinion date makes its rule new.

## Component C — exceptions

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject **both then-existing Teague exceptions**. The requested correction governs sentencing procedure; it does not exclude these murders, a class of persons, or an otherwise authorized punishment from substantive state power. Nor is correcting this recommendation channel, in a system with separate judicial findings, the watershed procedure essential to fundamental accuracy and fairness that the second exception requires. Its constitutional importance alone does not establish that exceptional classification. Both exceptions remain part of current law; no future abolition is imported.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on the old-rule conclusion and **do not reach or adopt an exception as an independent ground**. Their votes cannot be combined with an exception theory to create a different controlling rule. Stevens's Gilmore watershed position does not automatically transfer from a route that bypassed the only lesser-offense explanation to this judge/jury correction question. No Justice supplies an exception-based writ in this packet.
