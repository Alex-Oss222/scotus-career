## Decisions and Dispositions

### Raines v. Byrd, No. 96-1671

Merits decision, June 26, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued May 27, 1997; decided June 26, 1997. On direct appeal under 2 U.S.C. §692(b) from the judgment of a single District Judge for the District of Columbia, reported at 956 F. Supp. 25. The questions are whether six Members of Congress have Article III standing and, only if they do, whether the Line Item Veto Act violates Article I's lawmaking requirements or nondelegation principles.

The Act was enacted April 9, 1996, effective January 1, 1997; the Members sued January 2. The District Court found standing and ripeness and declared the Act unconstitutional on April 10; appeal followed April 11. Four Senators and two Representatives had voted against the Act. Their votes were counted, no cancellation had injured them personally, and neither House authorized them to represent Congress. The congressional amici defended the Act's validity without taking a standing position. Senator Hatfield subsequently retired; five plaintiffs remained Members.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss for lack of Article III standing, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Breyer.

Stevens would affirm the constitutional declaration. Breyer would recognize jurisdiction and proceed to the merits, which he reserves. Souter's judgment support does not join the principal injury analysis.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg | Vacatur and jurisdictional dismissal | Entire opinion |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Souter | Ginsburg | Same disposition, separate Article III ground | Entire concurrence; Ginsburg also joins the Court opinion |
| Dissent | Stevens | Author alone | Would affirm the District Court | Standing, ripeness and Article I merits |
| Dissent | Breyer | Author alone | Would reject jurisdictional dismissal | Standing and ripeness; merits expressly reserved |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. These individual legislators lack a concrete personal injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual Members of Congress whose votes were counted and given full effect lack Article III standing to challenge the Line Item Veto Act on the asserted abstract dilution of Congress's institutional power and alteration of future votes. Express statutory permission to sue removes the applicable prudential obstacle but cannot supply the missing concrete personal injury.

**Authority:** The six-Justice opinion of the Court identified above adopts this rule, its explanation, limits and remedy. Souter supplies a seventh judgment vote on a separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires a plaintiff's own concrete injury, with causation and redressability; legislative permission cannot replace that constitutional stake. These Members cast fully effective votes against the Act and lost. They retain office and their voting entitlement, apart from Hatfield's subsequent retirement, and identify no cancellation injuring them personally. Powell concerned exclusion from a seat, an individual deprivation absent here. Coleman concerned complete nullification of votes sufficient to determine a legislative outcome; it does not turn every change in the practical force of future votes into that injury.

Akins establishes that numerical sharing does not make a concrete deprivation abstract. Its voters were denied particular information useful to their own electoral participation. These Members instead assert an institutional allocation of power. Their strongest argument is that cancellation authority presently deprives them of voting on the ultimate legal text. But no vote of theirs was overridden or denied legal effect; the asserted change belongs to the legislative institution, which has not authorized their representation. Available legislative remedies and a later concrete cancellation controversy reinforce this bounded conclusion. They create no legislative-exhaustion requirement and do not guarantee any future plaintiff standing. The Court decides no universal rule against official injury or legislator litigation.

##### II. The jurisdictional defect requires vacatur and dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** Because these plaintiffs lack Article III standing, the District Court's constitutional declaration must be vacated and the complaint dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The Court does not decide the Act's constitutionality, nondelegation, Presentment or the independent ripeness question.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion controls the jurisdictional disposition and its limits; Souter separately agrees with vacatur and dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. requires resolution of disputed Article III power before disputed constitutional merits in this setting. Congress's special direct-review and expedition provisions supply a route for review, not jurisdiction over an uninjured plaintiff's constitutional complaint. Vacating the declaration removes a merits judgment the District Court lacked power to enter; it does not sustain the Act on its merits. Personal exclusion, lost salary, complete nullification of decisive votes, an authorized institutional suit meeting Article III, and an actual cancellation causing concrete injury remain distinct questions. The Court neither adjudicates any such future controversy nor adds an independent holding on traceability.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lujan supplies the concrete-personal-injury requirement and the distinction between statutory permission and constitutional injury; its rule is applied without enlarging its noncontrolling redressability analysis.
- Akins preserves standing for concrete injury shared by many; its informational deprivation is distinguished from the institutional dilution alleged here.
- Powell remains authority for personal exclusion from office, a circumstance not presented.
- Coleman remains authority for complete nullification of decisive votes; it is distinguished from votes counted and lost.
- Steel Co. supplies the requirement to decide disputed Article III power before the disputed merits in this setting.
- Bender and Allen support the distinction between an individual's legally cognizable stake and unauthorized institutional litigation and the separation-of-powers limits on jurisdiction. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

These individual legislators cannot invoke Article III on this institutional-dilution claim. Congress's authorization and expedition provisions do not change that conclusion. The Act's constitutional validity remains undecided, and concrete personal or properly authorized institutional injuries remain subject to their own jurisdictional analysis.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Souter, concurring in the judgment, joined by Ginsburg.** Official injury is not categorically excluded by Article III: Coleman and authorized institutional representation prevent that shortcut. Whether this injury is personal rather than official, or concrete rather than abstract, is fairly debatable. Souter instead gives decisive weight to Allen's separation-of-powers foundation and Valley Forge's restraint against unnecessary constitutional adjudication. The Members seek judicial intervention in an unauthorized internal and interbranch contest, while a beneficiary deprived of an actual spending or tax benefit may present a conventional controversy satisfying injury, causation and redressability. These conditions make the present claim constitutionally insufficient. This is neither prudential abstention, an absolute political-question bar, nor a universal requirement that another plaintiff exist.

**Stevens, dissenting.** A Member's opportunity to vote on the law's ultimate text is an individual constitutional function. The Act's present alteration of that function is concrete without waiting for a particular cancellation; Coleman supports protection of official voting power, and Bender's prohibition on unauthorized representation does not defeat a Member's own injury. Stevens finds the claim ripe and would affirm the declaration on Article I grounds. The Act permits the President, after a bill becomes law, to cancel in whole a defined discretionary dollar amount, new-direct-spending item or limited tax benefit under its conditions. Chadha requires bicameralism and presentment for repeal. Giving cancellation a different name or intelligible standards does not turn removal of enacted legal force into execution; Field's contingent execution and ordinary impoundment do not answer the distinct spending-obligation and tax effects. His ground is allocation of lawmaking authority, not a finding that the delegation's standards are insufficiently intelligible. He adds no private presidential-reservation theory and decides no later cancellation remedy.

The cancellation permission requires determinations that the action reduces the deficit, does not impair essential Government functions and does not harm the national interest; the special message must be sent within five calendar days excluding Sundays. Cancellation takes effect upon both Houses' receipt; enacted disapproval nullifies it and restores the provision from its original effective date. A disapproval bill cannot itself be canceled. Disapproval requires legislation, and the special procedures do not abolish ordinary later legislation. The Act has no force or effect on or after January 1, 2005. None of these limitations, in Stevens's view, changes the Article I character of a repeal after enactment.

**Breyer, dissenting.** The systematic impairment of the Members' existing constitutional work is sufficiently concrete. Coleman does not limit official injury to one decisive voting event, and express congressional authorization removes a prudential barrier while reducing separation-of-powers concerns; it does not enlarge Article III. The prospect of a later private suit cannot erase present injury if that injury exists. Breyer finds standing and ripeness and would proceed to the merits. He expressly reserves the Act's constitutionality and does not join Stevens's merits conclusion or vote to sustain the Act.

#### Source Notes

The Line Item Veto Act is Pub. L. 104-130, April 9, 1996, 110 Stat. 1200, including the special direct-appeal provision in §3. The single-judge judgment and notice of appeal establish the review posture. No actual cancellation or resulting private injury is found. Statutory authority: [enacted Act](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ130/html/PLAW-104publ130.htm).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The constitutional declaration is vacated. The case returns to the District Court with instructions to dismiss the complaint for lack of jurisdiction. No merits remand, injunction, cancellation adjudication or new deadline is ordered.

**End of entry: Raines v. Byrd, merits decision, June 26, 1997.**

### Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union, No. 96-511

Merits decision, June 26, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 19, 1997; decided June 26, 1997. On direct appeal from a three-judge District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania under §561 of the Telecommunications Act of 1996. The question is whether the CDA's indecency and patently-offensive restrictions are facially invalid under the First Amendment because they are content based, vague, substantially overbroad and insufficiently tailored to protecting minors.

The Act was enacted February 8, 1996. The district court's preliminary-injunction order was dated June 11 and entered June 12, 1996; its June 28 clarification preserved June 12 as the appeal-clock entry date. The plaintiffs demonstrated present speech restrictions and practical compliance burdens. The record describes diverse lawful Internet expression and age-verification costs and barriers, especially for noncommercial and small speakers.

Section 223(a)(1)(B) reaches knowing creation or solicitation and initiation of transmission by a telecommunications device of an obscene or indecent communication, knowing its recipient is under eighteen, regardless of who placed the call or initiated the communication. The enacted definition of telecommunications device excludes an interactive computer service. Section (d)(1)(A) separately reaches knowing use of an interactive computer service to send to specified persons under eighteen; (d)(1)(B) reaches display available to a person under eighteen. Their content category covers contextual depictions or descriptions of sexual or excretory activities or organs in terms patently offensive under contemporary community standards. The corresponding facility offenses require knowing permission and intent that the facility be used for the prohibited activity. Violations carry fines, imprisonment of up to two years, or both.

Section (e)(1) protects mere access or connection to a facility, system or network outside the provider's control, including incidental functions that do not create content. Section (e)(2) denies that defense to a conspirator with an entity actively creating or knowingly distributing prohibited communications, or one knowingly advertising them; the conspirator need not personally create content. Section (e)(3) separately denies it for access to an offending network the provider owns or controls. Employer liability under (e)(4) requires within-scope employee or agent conduct plus knowing authorization or ratification, or reckless disregard. Section (e)(5) provides alternative defenses: good-faith reasonable, effective and appropriate measures under the circumstances to restrict minors' access, or restricted access requiring a verified credit card, debit account, adult access code or adult personal identification number. Under (e)(6), the FCC may describe suitable measures; it may not approve, sanction, compel or enforce their use or endorse particular products. Their use is evidence of good faith. Interactive services do not thereby become common or telecommunications carriers.

Section (f)(1) protects lawful good-faith implementation of defenses or access restrictions against suit. Section (f)(2) bars inconsistent state or local liability for the identified commercial, library and higher-education activities, while allowing complementary regulation confined to intrastate services without inconsistent interstate duties and preserving regulation of conduct outside this section. Section (g) preserves other federal laws. Section (h)'s device definition adds no broadcast-licensee or cable-operator obligations under other indecency provisions; its access-software definition covers content-neutral filtering, selection and transmission tools that do not create or provide the communication's content. These distinct savings provisions supply no absent narrow content definition.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Authority for both holdings:** The eight-Justice opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts the following propositions, explanations, limits and remedy. O'Connor's agreement with part of the judgment does not join this opinion.

##### I. The challenged content restrictions violate the First Amendment

**Holding and operative rule:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when uncertain and substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the Government fails to establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. On this Internet record, §223(a)(1)(B)'s indecency prohibition and §223(d)(1)'s patently-offensive transmission and display prohibitions, together with their corresponding facility-permitting offenses, fail that requirement.

**Controlling explanation:** Sable recognizes the compelling interest in protecting children but forbids reducing adults to children's speech when less restrictive means are available. Ginsberg sustained a confined commercial restriction on material obscene for minors, including a value limitation absent here. Pacifica's scarcity, intrusiveness and broadcast setting do not describe this medium; ACT/Pacifica requires independent protection of adult access even there. Turner concerns content-neutral carriage, not this direct selection of prohibited content. Denver Area's compulsory-blocking holding supplies the practical adult-access analogy, without extending its fractured permission judgments into a universal rule.

The criminal categories reach serious discussion of health, sexuality, art, politics and personal experience. Uncertainty about content and community standards chills speech; scienter cannot tell a speaker which protected discussion is criminal. Knowledge of a minor or reference to specified recipients does not prevent the prohibition from reaching conversations also intended for adults. Other communication channels do not cure exclusion from this one.

The defenses do not remove those burdens. Many small and noncommercial speakers lack feasible age verification; costs, loss of anonymity and exclusion of lawful adults persist. Proposed tagging does not establish the effective protection the defense requires on the present record. User-controlled filtering and precise regulation of unprotected material offer materially less restrictive approaches. The Government has not justified this broad criminal suppression in light of those alternatives. The Court does not find every age screen invalid or all future controls ineffective. Vagueness contributes to the First Amendment holding; no independent Fifth Amendment holding is needed.

##### II. Textual severance preserves obscenity law; judicial rewriting cannot save the remaining restrictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The discrete words imposing indecency liability in §223(a)(1)(B) are severable from its obscenity prohibition. The Court affirms the clarified preliminary injunction against that indecency branch and its corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2); the general severability provision does not authorize creating a new minor-only offense or inserting a value exception absent from the enacted text.

**Controlling explanation:** The word choice permits removal of the indecency branch without disabling the distinct obscenity rule. The proposed further construction would instead change the offenses' substantive coverage. Knowledge and audience limits do not provide the missing narrow content definition, and a serious-value or harmful-to-minors exception cannot be supplied by judicial legislation. The statutory defenses preserve their own terms but do not establish workable protection for the record's diverse speakers. The challenge's varied activities and substantial protected reach make a selective judicial reconstruction inappropriate. O'Connor would retain knowing transmissions solely to minors; the Court does not adopt her conclusion that minors' protected-speech overbreadth is insubstantial or her proposed application severance.

The ruling does not hold that Congress can never regulate protected Internet indecency, decide the proposed blanket ban on such messages to seventeen-year-olds or the role of parental approval, or resolve the separate question of extraterritorial efficacy and scope. It does not require one constitutional classification for every Internet service. Independently lawful obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, true-threat, fraud, unlawful-solicitation and direct-harmful-contact provisions remain governed by their own predicates; they do not save these overbroad restrictions. A future precisely drawn rule using effective, nonexclusionary age assurance remains open.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Sable, Butler and Bolger preserve adult access despite the compelling interest in protecting minors; child protection does not alone justify suppressing adult expression.
- Ginsberg's commercial restriction on material obscene for minors is distinguished because the CDA reaches broader protected categories without its value limitation.
- Pacifica retains its contextual broadcast force and supplies no reduced Internet protection.
- ACT/Pacifica's independent review of content-selected restrictions and adult access remains intact; the Internet record is separately examined.
- Turner retains the distinction between content-neutral carriage and content discrimination, with independent scrutiny of the evidentiary fit.
- Denver Area's compulsory-segregation holding is applied by analogy to practical adult-access burdens; its separate permission judgments create no universal Internet rule.
- Broadrick supplies the substantial-overbreadth framework; the enacted breadth cannot be repaired by an unavailable narrowing construction. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The challenged indecency and patently-offensive restrictions cannot be enforced at the clarified scope. Content-based criminal regulation of this medium must protect lawful adult expression and satisfy narrow tailoring on the actual record. Obscenity and other independently unprotected conduct retain their own legal treatment. Future, precisely drawn regulation and genuinely effective nonexclusionary age controls remain open; no general Internet-service classification or freestanding Fifth Amendment rule is adopted.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part.** Butler, Sable and Bolger protect adult access; Ginsberg permits genuine restrictions confined to minors. The Internet presently lacks generally usable identity gates, so the display offense and mixed adult/minor conversations cannot be saved by treating the network as divided into adult and child zones. She agrees with the injunction to that extent and with preservation of independent obscenity enforcement.

She would retain the transmission provisions only where the initiating adult knows all recipients are minors, with corresponding facility liability confined to those underlying communications. She construes §223(d)(1)(A) to require knowledge of minority, relying on its wording and DeBartolo's constitutional-avoidance principle. Such a communication has no other adult audience whose access is lost. Brockett's application-severability principle and the statutory severability provision support preserving that application. Although the CDA's content categories are broader than Ginsberg's obscenity-for-minors rule, she finds no showing under Broadrick that the additional protected minor speech is substantial relative to the provisions' legitimate sweep. She does not deny minors all First Amendment protection or foreclose individual vagueness challenges. Her view relies on current technology, not predicted improvements. No other Justice joins her separate writing.

Pub. L. 104-104 §§502 and 561 supply the enacted offense, defense and direct-review terms: [Telecommunications Act of 1996](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ104/html/PLAW-104publ104.htm). The District Court's findings are reported at 929 F. Supp. 824; the June 11 order entered June 12 and June 28 clarification establish the exact preliminary relief. The technological conclusions concern the existing record, not future capabilities.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The clarified preliminary injunction remains in force against enforcement, prosecution, investigation or review at the stated scope. It remains preliminary, requires no bond, and retains the lower court's direction that the parties advise concerning further proceedings at the later of thirty-one days after that order or ten days after final appellate review. The clarification did not restart the June 12 appeal clock. The Court adds no damages, new deadline, mandated technology or permanent decree.

### Vacco v. Quill, No. 95-1858

Merits decision, June 26, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 8, 1997; decided June 26, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit. Its April 2, 1996 judgment, 80 F.3d 716, held New York's distinction unconstitutional as applied to competent terminally ill persons in the circumstances presented. The question is whether allowing refusal of unwanted treatment while prohibiting intentional assistance in suicide denies equal protection. New York seeks reversal; the physician respondents seek affirmance. The three initially named patients died before the District Court's decision; the operative appellants below were three physicians. The related due-process case, Washington v. Glucksberg, is separately decided today.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Second Circuit's equal-protection invalidation is reversed. On remand the courts must reject the presented challenge to New York's treatment-refusal/intentional-assistance distinction. Independent treatment-refusal, lawful pain-care and good-faith palliation protections are undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy | Reversal and remand | Entire opinion; five direct joins including the author |
| Concurrence | O'Connor | Breyer, except insofar as the concurrence joins the principal opinions; Ginsburg adopts substantially its separate reasons in her own concurrence | Joins the Court's opinion and judgment | Separate imminent-death, suffering and palliation analysis; the qualified adopters do not join the Court's opinion |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stevens | Author alone | Reversal and remand | General prohibition sustained; particular individual interests reserved |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Souter | Author alone | Reversal and remand | Independent substantive-liberty and commensurate-justification ground |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Ginsburg | Author alone | Reversal and remand | Substantially O'Connor's separate reasons; no principal-opinion join |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Breyer | Author alone | Reversal and remand | Combined manner-of-death, medical-assistance and severe-suffering interest; also the qualified O'Connor join stated above |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### New York may distinguish treatment refusal from intentional suicide assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A State does not deny equal protection by permitting a competent person to refuse unwanted medical treatment while prohibiting intentional assistance in suicide when the distinction reasonably rests on bodily consent, legal causation, intent and professional responsibility and serves legitimate protective interests. New York's distinction satisfies ordinary rational-basis review on the physicians' claim for competent, terminally ill patients; neither the shared eventual consequence of death nor the different practical options of patients dependent on life support requires the State to permit lethal assistance.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion of the Court identified above directly supports the rule, explanation and limits; the unanimous judgment does not make the four judgment concurrences full rationale joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The law draws no suspect classification, and the presented claim identifies no established fundamental entitlement to the intentional assistance sought. Cruzan grounds treatment refusal in bodily integrity and informed consent; its assumed competent-refusal premise does not establish a right to another person's lethal assistance. Casey preserves a defined reproductive choice and its complete regulation standard, not an unrestricted right to every medical choice.

Equal wording alone does not answer the physicians' objection: patients who depend on life support have a practical option others lack. Heller requires a relationship to that actual disadvantage, and Romer rejects an unexplained disability without demanding a separate hostility finding. Here the patient's refusal ends an unwanted intervention and ordinarily lets the underlying condition take its course. Providing lethal means intentionally to cause death involves a different purpose, causal role and professional responsibility. Good-faith palliation can foresee a shorter life without adopting death as its purpose. The State may preserve bodily consent while protecting life, preventing suicide, guarding vulnerable patients against error, pressure and abuse, and maintaining medicine's responsibility to care.

Beach permits reasonable generalizations without formal findings or empirical certainty. The distinction need not describe every patient's or physician's intention in every instance: a patient's wish to die, or the label attached to an intervention, does not alone settle purpose or causation. Those possible overlaps do not make the general protective line irrational. The availability of narrower regulation does not require its adoption under ordinary review. Moreno and Cleburne continue to forbid bare harm and irrational prejudice; the supported protective connection here supplies a legitimate explanation for the actual difference in treatment.

**Operative limits:** The Court does not authorize forced treatment, displace competent refusal or valid advance directives, permit discriminatory denial of lawful pain care, or punish good-faith palliation merely because death is foreseen. A materially different individual claim, including discriminatory administration, remains open on its own facts and legal grounds. No individual entitlement to a lethal prescription or new hearing is awarded. Cruzan's assumption concerning artificial nutrition and hydration is not enlarged into a comprehensive holding on every refusal decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cruzan v. Director, Missouri Department of Health: applies its bodily-integrity and informed-consent foundation to explain the refusal/assistance distinction. Its assumption of a competent person's constitutional refusal right and its approval of Missouri's evidentiary safeguard for an incompetent patient's wishes retain their actual scope.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey: preserves protected reproductive choice, individualized viability, timely necessary life-and-health care and the complete six-step standard. That established liberty supplies no general right to intentional death assistance; no component is displaced.
- FCC v. Beach Communications: applies its permission for relevant generalizations and conceivable legitimate grounds without formal findings or certainty, while retaining its limits against irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications. Its economic setting does not itself decide this medical distinction.
- Heller v. Doe: applies the proof-burden holding's requirement of a relationship to the actual disadvantage and the separate family-participation holding's acceptance of relevant generalization without a least-restrictive-means requirement. Neither component is changed.
- Romer v. Evans: applies ordinary rationality to the law's actual operation without a separate hostility-proof prerequisite; distinguishes its unexplained blanket withdrawal of ordinary protection from the justified conduct distinction here.
- Department of Agriculture v. Moreno: preserves the prohibition on a bare desire to harm a disfavored group as a justification.
- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center: preserves the requirement of a rational connection and the limit against irrational prejudice.

#### Law After Decision

New York may enforce its intentional-assistance prohibition against the equality claim presented while continuing to respect lawful treatment refusal and palliation. The controlling addition is the rationality of this particular distinction, effective June 26, 1997. Ordinary rational-basis doctrine is applied without a new scrutiny tier, proof burden or general medical-regulation exemption. The judgment neither recognizes an assisted-death entitlement nor decides every individual application.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring.** She joins the Court's opinion and also explains why the narrower interest of a competent person suffering greatly near imminent death need not be decided. New York permits qualified physicians to relieve pain, even with medication producing unconsciousness and a foreseeable hastening of death. Given that legal availability, protection against mistaken judgments of competence, terminal condition and voluntariness sufficiently supports the prohibition even if that narrower interest is assumed. Her Cruzan concurrence supports States' initial development of safeguards. She does not find that pain care is universally delivered or invariably successful, require legislative exhaustion, or adopt a freestanding constitutional pain-treatment standard. Ginsburg substantially adopts these separate reasons. Breyer joins them except insofar as the concurrence joins the principal opinions.

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** He accepts a sufficient basis for the general distinction but does not join the Court's ordinary-review and causation/intent account in full. A patient withdrawing treatment and one requesting lethal medication may share an intention to hasten impending death; physicians may seek relief of suffering in both settings. Terminal sedation further complicates a universal distinction. His Cruzan dissent treats dignity and the manner of unavoidable death as serious individual interests; Casey's protected reproductive choice supplies a bounded analogy requiring attention to real burdens and clinical circumstances. Neither creates an unrestricted autonomy rule or an assisted-death right. Protective concerns sustain a general prohibition while potentially carrying different weight in an exceptional competent patient's well-supported case. He finds no such entitlement established here and orders no hearing.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment.** The important patient and physician interests require justification commensurate with their weight, rather than a merely formal same-treatment syllogism. Harlan's Poe analysis, bodily-integrity principles and the defined reproductive and medical-privacy interests support that substantive inquiry. Protection against mistaken or involuntary suicide and euthanasia supplies the sufficient ground: reliably identifying responsible voluntary choice and containing a legalized lethal practice present serious unresolved problems. Palliation and treatment refusal differ in purpose, bodily intrusion and the likelihood of implementing a responsible patient's choice, though both can shorten life. Those differences and protective concerns justify this line. He reaches that conclusion through the stated substantive inquiry and does not join the Court's ordinary rational-basis method.

**Ginsburg, concurring in the judgment.** She adopts substantially O'Connor's separate reasons concerning the reserved imminent-death and suffering interest and the laws' permission for palliation. She does not join the principal opinion, a declaration validating every future application, or every word of O'Connor's principal-opinion joins.

**Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** He agrees that the identified protective interests justify the distinction, while describing the potential liberty as the combination of control over the manner of death, willing professional assistance and avoidance of unnecessary severe physical suffering. He does not decide whether that interest is fundamental. The law permits the pain treatment central to it despite foreseeable death; failures in delivery and the possibility of sedation do not establish a legal prohibition. He does not assert that every person's suffering can be relieved. A law forbidding necessary palliation would raise a different question. These reasons accompany his qualified O'Connor join and do not adopt the principal opinion or deny every end-of-life liberty.

The relevant state-law operation and lower-court history are reflected in 80 F.3d 716 and the public record recitations at 521 U.S. 793. New York's prohibition targets intentional assistance; treatment-refusal law and permission for good-faith palliation have their separate sources and scope. The possibility of overlapping intent in a particular case is preserved, and permission for pain treatment is not a finding that every patient receives effective relief.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Second Circuit for proceedings consistent with rejection of the presented equal-protection challenge. No new clinical procedure, evidentiary hearing, injunction authorizing lethal prescriptions or revived deceased patient's claim is ordered. Any independently preserved matter retains its own ordinary predicates; the reservation alone creates no pending claim.

### Washington v. Glucksberg, No. 96-110

Merits decision, June 26, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 8, 1997; decided June 26, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. Its March 6, 1996 en banc judgment, 79 F.3d 790, invalidated Washington's assisted-suicide prohibition as applied to competent terminally ill adults seeking physician assistance. The question is whether the Due Process Clause protects that asserted right against the prohibition on knowingly causing or aiding suicide. Washington seeks reversal; the physician respondents seek affirmance. Compassion in Dying was a party below and is not a party in this Court. Vacco v. Quill, concerning New York's equal-protection distinction, is separately decided today.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Ninth Circuit's class-wide constitutional invalidation is reversed. On remand it must direct entry of judgment for Washington on the asserted substantive-due-process right to physician-assisted suicide. Forced treatment, valid refusal, lawful pain relief, good-faith palliation and discriminatory administration are outside this disposition.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The asserted physician-assistance right is not fundamental

**Holding and operative rule:** The Due Process Clause does not establish a fundamental right for a competent, terminally ill adult to obtain a willing physician's intentional assistance in suicide through a lethal prescription. The asserted class-wide entitlement therefore receives no heightened protection; a materially different, more particularized claim is not foreclosed merely by this disposition.

**Authority:** The five-Justice opinion of the Court identified above directly supports both holdings and their limits. The other four Justices concur in the judgment on the separate grounds identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court addresses the claim actually made: a competent person approaching death seeks willing medical assistance to cause death, without demanding compulsory service or public financing. Its terminal-illness and competence limits matter. Neither an unrestricted wish to die nor a generic appeal to autonomy adequately describes it.

The text and structure of the Due Process Clause, the relevant precedents and the enduring legal treatment of assistance in suicide do not establish this entitlement as a fundamental liberty. The tradition of prohibiting such assistance bears on this claimed right; the Court does not adopt an exclusive rule requiring the most specifically imaginable historical analogue for every constitutional liberty. Cruzan explains refusal of unwanted treatment through bodily integrity, battery and informed consent. It assumed a competent person's right to refuse lifesaving nutrition and hydration and upheld Missouri's evidentiary safeguard for an incompetent patient's wishes. Ending an unwanted intervention does not itself establish permission for another person intentionally to supply lethal means.

Casey retains the protected reproductive decision, individualized viability, timely necessary life-and-health care and its complete six-step regulation standard. Mazurek requires application of that entire standard, including its purpose and residual-interference protections. Those established holdings are not reduced to a generic substantial-obstacle rule or displaced by history alone. They concern a defined reproductive liberty, however, and do not recognize every important medical decision as fundamental. The involvement of a willing professional is not itself dispositive: protected reproductive care also involves assistance. What remains unestablished here is constitutional protection for this distinct intentional lethal act. The refusal and reproductive precedents therefore do not require the asserted extension.

**Limits:** The Court does not decide forced treatment, interference with competent refusal or valid directives, denial of lawful pain care, punishment of good-faith palliation merely because death is foreseen, or discriminatory administration. The reserved claim must be materially different from the class entitlement rejected here; the reservation neither recognizes that entitlement nor creates a new pending action.

##### II. Washington's prohibition rationally serves its protective interests

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the asserted assistance entitlement is not fundamental, Washington's prohibition is valid against this substantive-due-process challenge if it is rationally related to legitimate state interests. It meets that standard by protecting life, preventing suicide, guarding vulnerable people against error and pressure, maintaining medical responsibility and containing the risks of a legalized lethal practice; the Ninth Circuit's class-wide invalidation cannot stand.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Opinion of the Court supplies direct majority support. This rationality determination completes the rejection of the claim; the absence of a fundamental right alone is not treated as sufficient to dispose of every constitutional objection.

**Controlling explanation:** The State may protect human life without grading the value of particular lives. Terminal illness does not eliminate the risks of mistaken diagnosis, depression, coercion, family pressure, unequal care or indifference toward vulnerable persons. A physician's involvement does not guarantee a reliable determination that a request is informed, responsible and voluntary. Washington may also preserve the professional duty to care and guard against expansion from the proposed class into voluntary or involuntary euthanasia.

The physicians answer that competent adults near death can choose deliberately and that safeguards could confine the practice. The Court does not find that any named person lacked capacity or that any physician acted improperly. The issue is whether the State can reasonably address risks attending authorization of the practice as a class. Difficulty assessing voluntariness, predicting terminal decline and enforcing the proposed boundary supplies the required connection. Rational review does not require certainty that every risk will occur, a finding that every request is involuntary, or proof that prohibition is the best available policy. Nor must the State adopt a less restrictive scheme solely because one can be proposed.

The legal availability of treatment refusal and good-faith pain relief preserves their distinct functions; the challenged prohibition does not require unwanted intervention or forbid palliation because a shorter life is foreseeable. That permission is not a guarantee that every patient's suffering is relieved. States remain free to authorize and regulate assisted death within applicable constitutional limits. This judgment sustains Washington's chosen protective response to the claim before the Court without imposing a national legislative policy.

**Remedy and scope:** The Ninth Circuit must direct entry of judgment for Washington on the asserted substantive-due-process right to physician-assisted suicide. The remand does not authorize lethal prescriptions, command a new patient-specific hearing or adjudicate a materially different claim merely because it could arise.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cruzan v. Director, Missouri Department of Health: preserves its bodily-integrity and informed-consent foundation, its assumed competent-refusal premise concerning nutrition and hydration, and its approval of Missouri's clear-and-convincing safeguard for an incompetent patient's wishes. The Court declines to extend that premise to intentional lethal assistance.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey: leaves the protected reproductive choice, individualized viability, required life-and-health protection and complete six-step standard intact. Its established liberty is distinguished from the asserted assisted-suicide entitlement; neither its constitutional foundation nor any regulatory component is overruled.
- Mazurek v. Armstrong: preserves its requirement to apply the full Casey standard, including independent purpose review and the severe- and meaningful-interference inquiries that remain below a substantial obstacle. That abortion-regulation rule is not transplanted into a universal medical-liberty test.

#### Law After Decision

The asserted class-wide entitlement to willing physician assistance in suicide is not a fundamental right, and Washington's prohibition survives rational review against that claim. These are the controlling additions effective June 26, 1997. Existing refusal and reproductive precedents retain their scope. No exclusive history-only method governing all fundamental liberties, universal medical-regulation standard, constitutional pain-care test or prohibition on legislative authorization of assisted death is established. A materially different particularized claim remains subject to its own facts and governing law.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring.** She joins the Court's opinion but does not decide whether a competent person facing imminent death and great suffering has a cognizable interest in controlling those circumstances. Washington permits qualified physicians to alleviate suffering with medication even to unconsciousness and despite foreseeable hastening of death. Given that legal availability, the risks of erroneous judgments about terminal condition, competence and voluntariness sufficiently support the prohibition even if the narrower interest is assumed. Her Cruzan concurrence supports States' initial development of safeguards and democratic consideration. This neither guarantees effective delivery of pain care, eliminates suffering associated with sedation, requires legislative exhaustion nor creates a freestanding constitutional pain-care standard. Ginsburg adopts substantially these separate reasons. Breyer joins the separate analysis except insofar as it joins the principal opinions.

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** No person has complete autonomy to end life regardless of others' interests, and the State may maintain a general prohibition. That conclusion does not decide a competent individual's well-supported claim concerning the manner of an unavoidable imminent death. His Cruzan dissent and Casey's defined protected choice support attention to dignity, intolerable suffering and genuine justification. Preservation of life does not automatically outweigh every such interest. Mistake, coercion and professional-integrity concerns justify the general rule but may carry different weight in an exceptional case. Palliation does not logically eliminate every form of suffering or the distinction between conscious relief and terminal sedation. He also questions whether intent and causation invariably distinguish treatment withdrawal, sedation and lethal prescriptions; those overlaps do not establish this class-wide entitlement. He finds no individual entitlement proved here and orders no additional hearing.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment.** Harlan's Poe analysis asks whether the restraint is arbitrary or purposeless in light of the relative importance and fit of the individual and state interests. Traditions retained and abandoned inform that inquiry; neither longstanding prohibition alone nor an abstract autonomy formula resolves it. Responsible terminally ill patients seeking medical counsel and assistance present an important interest connected to bodily integrity, treatment refusal and defined reproductive choice. Casey's complete framework is not a universal medical test. He accepts the competent and voluntary premises and provisionally accepts the professional premises for this claim without inventing incapacity or misconduct.

Protection against mistaken or involuntary suicide and expansion into euthanasia supplies the sufficient justification. Family pressure alone would not settle the matter, because proposed physician screening is intended to answer it. The harder problem is reliably assessing a knowing, responsible decision and ensuring that physicians preserve the asserted boundaries. The disputed evidence concerning safeguards and the Dutch experience establishes a serious empirical uncertainty, not an incontrovertible foreign-law conclusion. Courts cannot presently displace the State's protective judgment with adequate assurance; legislatures can investigate and develop experience.

His conclusion rejects this entitlement now without permanently foreclosing recognition, demanding legislative experiments, imposing a judicial moratorium or assuming inevitable logical expansion of every protected right. Legislative foot-dragging and a materially different individual record remain for another case. Incidental difficulty does not automatically require compelling justification, and not every liberty claim demands elaborate balancing: the importance of the interest and the burden determine the inquiry. His view does not become the Court's general standard.

**Ginsburg, concurring in the judgment.** She adopts substantially O'Connor's separate reasons about the unresolved imminent-death and suffering interest and the legal availability of palliation. She does not join the principal opinion or a declaration sustaining every future application. Her agreement creates no additional independent theory.

**Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** The potential liberty combines personal control over the manner of death, willing medical assistance and avoidance of unnecessary severe physical suffering. Describing it merely as any right to suicide misses the strongest claim without establishing that claim as fundamental. Poe, Cruzan and the defined personal-liberty authorities support serious consideration. Because the law does not forbid the pain treatment central to that interest, the present case need not decide its status; the stated protective interests suffice here. Delivery failures are not the same as a legal prohibition. He recognizes sedation and does not find every person's suffering treatable. A prohibition of necessary palliation would present a materially different case. These reasons supplement his qualified O'Connor join; they do not adopt the principal opinion or one universal liberty method for all judgment supporters.

#### Source Notes

The lower-court judgment is reported at 79 F.3d 790. The state-law operation, public procedural history and party status are reflected in the record recitations at 521 U.S. 702. The prohibition on knowingly causing or aiding suicide coexists with distinct treatment-refusal, advance-directive and palliative-care rules. Legal permission for palliation is not proof that every patient receives effective relief; disputed evidence about safeguards is not a finding that any named physician or patient acted improperly.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit, which must direct entry of judgment for Washington on the asserted physician-assisted-suicide claim. No new patient action, clinical procedure, legislative study, compulsory experiment, additional evidentiary hearing or injunction authorizing prescriptions is ordered. The Court's reservations establish no actual pending claim or automatic further proceedings beyond implementing the mandate.

### Foreman v. Dallas County, Nos. 96-987 and 96-1389

Summary disposition, June 27, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided without oral argument. Direct appeals under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from a three-judge District Court for the Northern District of Texas. No. 96-987 concerns refusal of injunctive relief; No. 96-1389 concerns the subsequent Rule 12(b)(6) final dismissal. The questions concern §5 coverage of county discretion in appointing precinct election judges, the reach of Texas's 1985 preclearance submission, and the consequence of the absent coverage-date baseline.

Dallas County changed its partisan appointment formulas in 1983 and later years, most recently in 1996. Precinct election judges supervise election-day voting. The state recodification and thirty-page change summary submitted in 1985 identified appointment beginning dates and duration but not the County's particular formulas. The parties agree the record does not establish the appointment method in force on November 1, 1972. The District Court treated statutory discretion and the recodification's approval as sufficient grounds to reject the challenge.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The final judgment supersedes the earlier injunction appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** A qualifying interlocutory refusal of an injunction is appealable under 28 U.S.C. §1253, but the subsequent final dismissal absorbs the same prospective-relief dispute presented here. No. 96-987 is dismissed on that ground; the final judgment remains reviewable in No. 96-1389.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela concurs only in the judgment on No. 96-987 and does not join this rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1253 expressly reaches qualifying interlocutory injunction orders. Nonfinality alone therefore cannot defeat this route. Shaffer v. Carter supplies the merger principle when the later final decree embraces the same injunction controversy. The subsequent dismissal places the prospective-relief dispute before the Court through the final-judgment appeal, and no distinct surviving interlocutory issue is established. This ruling neither treats the earlier request as a temporary restraining order nor imposes a universal finality requirement under §1253.

##### II. County discretion does not exempt a changed election-administration practice

**Holding and operative rule:** A covered county's change in the formula for selecting precinct officials who supervise election-day voting is subject to §5 when it differs from the operative coverage-date practice. Authority to exercise discretion under a precleared state statute does not exempt the new local method from preclearance.

**Authority:** Parts II–IV of the per curiam opinion are joined by all nine participating Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Presley requires a direct connection to voting, rather than the mere redistribution of governing authority. Election judges supervise polling; the formula selecting them concerns administration of voting itself. That connection distinguishes the County's methods from an internal allocation of road-government powers. Allen and Hampton County cover practical electoral changes, formal or informal. Monterey and Young establish that administration and discretionary implementation do not escape §5 through an enabling enactment. The contrary theory would make unexamined local choices precleared merely because state law permits them. The Court rejects that exemption without finding that the challenged method actually differs from the November 1, 1972 practice.

##### III. The 1985 submission did not preclear undisclosed county formulas

**Holding and operative rule:** Preclearance of the state recodification and its identified changes to appointment timing and duration did not approve the County's partisan-selection formulas, which neither the code nor the explanatory submission identified. A distinct local method is approved only if the submission fairly identifies that method for review; a separate sufficiently specific submission or approval, if established, remains relevant.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition for Parts II–IV controls this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** McCain and Clark require identification of the actual submitted change. Monterey and Young apply that principle to administration under related legislation. The 1985 materials described when election-judge appointments would begin and how long they would last; they did not describe the County's evolving partisan formulas. Receipt of the complete code does not oblige the Attorney General to infer and approve every possible exercise of local discretion. This conclusion neither rescinds approval of the identified recodification nor prevents a jurisdiction from submitting a sufficiently described local implementation with state legislation.

##### IV. The absent 1972 baseline requires remand and conditionally governs nonuse

**Holding and operative rule:** The final dismissal must be vacated because the erroneous discretion and submission grounds do not resolve whether the County changed its November 1, 1972 appointment practice. On remand the District Court must identify that practice, compare each challenged later method and determine actual submission status; it must enjoin prospective use of any legally relevant covered change that lacks preclearance.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition for Parts II–IV controls this holding and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Morse requires an actual baseline comparison, and Young prevents a legally unsupported benchmark from replacing it. The parties agree that this record does not establish the 1972 method. Later changes in formulas and insufficient submission do not themselves prove the missing comparison. The County may establish that no legally relevant change occurred; the voters retain the statutory right to nonuse if a changed method lacks preclearance. Clark supplies that prospective consequence, while Monterey and Young require fitted relief within lawful authority. The Court orders neither an immediate universal injunction before the necessary findings nor permission to use an established unprecleared change. The local court decides coverage and submission; substantive discriminatory purpose and effect remain for the Attorney General or the District Court for the District of Columbia. No completed-election consequence, cancellation of appointments, new partisan formula, damages or automatic evidentiary hearing is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Shaffer v. Carter supplies merger of the earlier injunction controversy into the subsequent final decree; it does not erase §1253's interlocutory route.
- Presley supplies the direct-voting boundary; selection of officials supervising polls is distinguished from internal governing allocation.
- Allen and Hampton County preserve coverage of actual voting practices, including informal administrative changes.
- Monterey and Young reject the pertinent administration/discretion exemption and preserve submission-specific approval and lawful prospective enforcement.
- McCain and Clark require identification of the submitted change; mere receipt of related legislation is not blanket approval.
- Morse preserves a supported baseline and the designated substantive-preclearance channels. No precedent is overruled.

County discretion under a precleared statute does not exempt a changed polling-administration method. Approval remains confined to identified changes. Whether Dallas County changed its operative 1972 practice remains unresolved; prospective nonuse follows only upon establishing a covered change without preclearance. The decision does not adjudicate discriminatory purpose or effect.

Stone-Zsela concurs only in the judgment dismissing No. 96-987, without a separate opinion, and joins the coverage, submission and remand portions in full. No separate substantive ground is announced.

The two lower rulings and the 1985 submission's identified subject matter are established. The parties agree that the record does not establish the appointment method on November 1, 1972; no finding fills that baseline gap. Section 1253 and Shaffer v. Carter, 252 U.S. 37, 44, govern the distinct appellate question.

### Pounders v. Watson, No. 96-1383

Certiorari and summary disposition, June 27, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided without oral argument, reviewing the Ninth Circuit's December 13, 1996 judgment reported at 102 F.3d 433. The petition presents the constitutional limits on summary contempt for the specified in-court questioning and the operation of former §2254(d) on the state findings and the constitutional inquiry. There was no prior plenary grant or merits argument.

During a six-defendant murder trial, Watson asked her client whether he had faced the death penalty and, after an immediate admonition, whether he faced life without parole. The judge found deliberate disobedience and serious prejudice to the jury. Watson disputed notice and intent and explained that the testimony concerned her client's state of mind. The judge adjudged contempt in her presence and imposed two days' confinement by written order dated June 22, 1994, with service deferred to protect trial continuity. She later had another opportunity to explain through counsel. The jury received an instruction to disregard the punishment questions; the record does not independently establish its effect.

The state appellate habeas orders supplied no explanatory rationale. The federal District Court denied habeas on September 8, 1994. Watson served the two-day sentence September 16–17; possible State Bar consequences keep the dispute live, without a finding of completed discipline. The Ninth Circuit directed entry of habeas relief because it found insufficient disruption to justify summary procedure, expressly leaving notice and willfulness undecided. Its opinion did not accept those findings merely by reserving them.

All nine Justices participated in the submitted matter and decision.

The Court grants certiorari and issues a per curiam summary disposition.

#### Judgment

**Summary reversal and remand, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Breyer.

Stone-Zsela would affirm relief on his narrower due-process ground. Stevens and Breyer would obtain full briefing and argument before deciding the merits; they do not supply two further merits votes for affirmance. The Court's remand concerns the notice and willfulness claims left unresolved below, not a new contempt prosecution or renewed service of the completed sentence.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per curiam opinion | The Court, unsigned | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Summary reversal and remaining-claims remand | Entire opinion |
| Dissent | Stone-Zsela | Author alone | Would affirm habeas relief | Distinct due-process necessity ground; accepts the state notice and deliberate-violation findings for his analysis |
| Dissent from summary disposition | Stevens | Breyer | Would grant plenary consideration instead of disposing of the merits now | Procedural objection; eventual merits reserved |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Authority for the following holdings:** The six-Justice per curiam opinion identified above controls the summary reversal and the bounded grounds below. Stone-Zsela dissents on the merits; Stevens and Breyer oppose summary merits disposition and request plenary consideration without deciding the eventual merits.

##### I. The appellate court unduly restricted the direct-contempt power

**Holding and operative rule:** The narrow power to adjudicate direct contempt summarily is not confined to noisy or physical obstruction, a pattern of violations pervading the trial, or a separate finding that misconduct would recur without immediate punishment. The Ninth Circuit's excessive reliance on the limited duration of Watson's questioning and absence of demonstrated future repetition cannot sustain its summary-process ruling on this record; the Court does not finally resolve her unaddressed notice and willfulness claims.

**Controlling explanation:** Oliver retains the exceptional power for misconduct openly before the judge, with its essential elements actually observed, disturbing the court's business and requiring vindication of judicial authority. Mere observation, irritation or a short sanction is insufficient. Wilson establishes that intentional conduct may obstruct an ongoing trial without noise or violence. Sacher permits deferral of punishment to protect trial continuity and representation; delay alone does not defeat qualifying summary action. McConnell involved announced questioning that was not carried out after a recess, unlike the renewed punishment question here after an immediate admonition.

The trial judge found deliberate defiance and serious jury prejudice. Those are the trial court's findings, not an independently established account of the jurors' minds. At the scope of the process question decided below, the renewed question and the threat to the jury's lawful consideration support correction of the appellate restriction. Protecting that decision process is part of trial administration. The strongest contrary argument is that instructions and ordinary later proceedings could address completed misconduct. But continuation after the contempt finding does not establish that the action already taken was unnecessary; neither Wilson nor Sacher makes a lengthy pattern or immediate imprisonment essential. The Court need not attribute a formally announced categorical rule to the Ninth Circuit to reject its reasoning here. It does not approve summary punishment for every evidentiary violation, eliminate Oliver's limits, or comprehensively redefine the exception.

##### II. Former §2254(d) governs the remaining claims without converting constitutional legality into fact

**Holding and operative rule:** Former 28 U.S.C. §2254(d) governs this noncapital habeas application filed before AEDPA. Its conditional protection of qualifying state historical findings does not conclusively determine the constitutional legality of summary procedure, and the Ninth Circuit's express nondecision of notice and willfulness does not amount to accepting those findings.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh supplies the application-date rule for chapter 153. Sumner preserves the former statute's factual presumption, while Thompson distinguishes historical circumstances from an independently reviewed ultimate constitutional question in its setting. Neither decides every contempt issue by label.

The presumption concerns a competent state court's factual determination after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State or its officer or agent, evidenced by findings, opinion or other reliable adequate writing. The exceptions concern unresolved factual merits; inadequate factfinding procedure; inadequately developed material facts; lack of jurisdiction over subject matter or applicant; unconstitutional failure to appoint counsel for an indigent applicant; absence of a full, fair and adequate hearing; other denial of due process; or a pertinent state record, considered as a whole, failing fairly to support the finding. At a federal evidentiary hearing, after due proof of the state determination, the applicant must establish error by convincing evidence unless one of the first seven circumstances is established, otherwise appears or is admitted, or the federal court finds the eighth.

The trial judge's written findings therefore retain whatever protection the statute gives them. Watson's disagreement does not automatically establish an exception or require a hearing. Conversely, the appellate court left her knowledge of a sufficiently definite order and deliberate disobedience undecided. Those claims cannot disappear through characterization of its silence as assent. A defense depending on facts beyond the judge's observation requires its applicable due-process assessment if that predicate is established. The Court decides no such predicate and makes no finding of judicial bias.

##### III. No additional nonretroactivity ground is introduced

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court declines to introduce a Teague defense that is not established as preserved and fairly included in this review record. That choice is not a finding of categorical waiver, a determination of finality or novelty, or a change to either contemporary Teague exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Schiro treats nonretroactivity as a nonjurisdictional defense and permits discretionary refusal of a late presentation; it does not command refusal in every case. Caspari concerns a consistently preserved subsidiary defense limiting the constitutional rule and writ under review. That premise is not established here. Preservation and fair inclusion have not been established; no ruling treats silence as waiver or determines finality. The Court resolves the presented summary-process ground without creating a new collateral bar or adjudicating an unestablished finality date.

##### IV. Reversal returns the claims the court of appeals did not decide

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's summary-process ruling and dependent instruction to grant habeas relief are reversed. The case is remanded for consideration of the notice and willfulness claims expressly left unresolved and lawful further disposition under the governing former habeas statute.

**Controlling explanation:** Removing the appellate court's sufficient ground for the writ does not resolve every other claim. Its own opinion reserved notice and deliberate disobedience, and this Court's confined review supplies no substituted merits decision on them. The mandate therefore neither finally validates all aspects of the contempt judgment nor directs final denial of habeas relief. Watson has served the two-day sentence; the mandate orders no repeated confinement, new contempt prosecution, automatic disciplinary result, new evidentiary hearing or particular custody deadline. The reported possible professional consequences preserve a live controversy without establishing a final disciplinary disposition.

- Oliver and Cooke retain the narrow, observed direct-contempt exception and ordinary notice-and-hearing protections outside it; observation or a contempt label alone is insufficient.
- Wilson is applied to interference with ongoing trial administration without a requirement of noise or violence.
- Sacher preserves protective deferral of qualifying punishment; it does not impose a universal repeated-violation requirement.
- McConnell is distinguished because its threatened questioning was not carried out after the recess.

- Schiro permits declining a late nonjurisdictional defense; Caspari's consistently preserved-defense premise is not established. No precedent is overruled or narrowed by the Court.

#### Law After Decision

The received direct-contempt power cannot be restricted by treating a pervasive pattern, physical stoppage, future recurrence or immediate imprisonment as indispensable. Its existing observation and institutional-need limits remain. This appellate process ruling no longer supports habeas relief, but the unresolved notice and intent claims remain open under the former statute. Stone-Zsela's proposed added necessity boundary and the plenary-review dissent are not controlling law.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** Stone would affirm habeas relief on a narrower due-process ground. He accepts the findings that Watson knew the order and deliberately violated it, preserves the underlying trial order, and takes the judge's assessment of serious jury prejudice seriously. Watson explained herself at the bench and again through counsel; his objection is not lack of every warning or opportunity to speak. A single event may warrant contempt, without repeated or pervasive misconduct.

He would require a particularized connection between summary adjudication and protection of an ongoing proceeding or authority that cannot practicably await ordinary process. Observation establishes what the judge saw, but does not alone show why an ordinary hearing cannot reliably adjudicate punishment. Here the questioning ended, curative trial-management measures were available, the trial continued for roughly two weeks without another violation and confinement was deferred. Summary adjudication could not undo the completed prejudice. Taken together, those circumstances fail to establish the need to dispense with ordinary criminal-contempt protections.

Terry and Oliver preserve an exceptional power tied to judicial protection. McConnell requires no more power than the occasion warrants. Wilson concerned refusal of immunized testimony needed for an ongoing trial, which he distinguishes from completed questioning. Sacher permits deferred punishment where summary process is otherwise justified; delay is therefore not independently dispositive. To the extent those authorities permit this summary conviction without the additional necessity showing, Stone would narrow them. His proposed boundary rests on notice and an effective opportunity to defend while preserving immediate action against violence, refusal, ongoing obstruction and comparably urgent threats. A preference for procedural speed supplies no concrete reliance sufficient to defeat that protection, and the proposal does not itself reopen completed contempt judgments.

Stone agrees that the former habeas statute applies. He would not initiate an omitted nonjurisdictional Teague defense under Schiro merely to defeat this claim, and preservation is not established here. If a preserved and fairly included defense were properly available, Caspari would require examining the law at finality: an additional necessity rule not dictated by the earlier cases would be procedural, would create no substantive immunity from contempt and would not qualify as a watershed accuracy rule. If a preserved and fairly included bar applies to his proposed new rule, he would reject relief on that ground, without approving every feature of the summary contempt. No such bar is decided on this record. He would leave California free to pursue ordinary contempt proceedings with adequate notice and opportunity to defend if state law permits, and require another judge only if the governing neutrality or embroilment rule requires one.

**Stevens, dissenting from summary disposition, joined by Breyer.** They would receive full briefing and argument before deciding the merits. Assuming for this procedural objection that Watson defied an order and committed contempt, they question whether summary adjudication is so clearly proper that the habeas judgment should be reversed without plenary consideration. Two questions in a long trial, subsequent continuation and punishment directed at completed conduct raise substantial issues concerning necessity and ordinary criminal safeguards.

McConnell's attention to actual interference and the concerns expressed in Frankfurter's Sacher dissent warrant fuller consideration, including the substantial question whether a disinterested judge should hear a charge in these circumstances. The dissent in Sacher supplies their argument, not a controlling rule displacing its majority. They acknowledge the immediate warning, Wilson's recognition of nonphysical obstruction and Sacher's permission to defer punishment. They impose no minimum number of violations, find no judicial bias and make no final determination that due process was violated. Their requested plenary review is not a vote to affirm habeas relief or a direction to conduct a new contempt prosecution.

### Printz v. United States, No. 95-1478; Mack v. United States, No. 95-1503

Merits decision, June 27, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Consolidated cases. Argued December 3, 1996; decided June 27, 1997. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit. Its September 8, 1995 judgment, 66 F.3d 1025, sustained the Brady Act's interim duties imposed on chief law-enforcement officers and dismissed the sheriffs' severability appeals as moot. The questions are whether Congress may impose the mandatory interim background inquiry and related administrative duties, and what statutory consequences follow if a command is invalid.

The District Courts had invalidated the mandatory inquiry while leaving the conditional destruction and written-reasons provisions standing and severing rather than invalidating the entire Act. Both sides appealed. The sheriffs seek relief from compulsory officer duties; the United States seeks affirmance. Neither action is a class action. The officers assert an absence of budget provision for the federal work, exposure to liability for unauthorized expenditures, and state-law prohibitions on undertaking the responsibilities. Those assertions are not findings of cost, liability or state law. Montana and Arizona did not require implementation, and the officers chose not to implement the program; that general posture establishes neither receipt nor nonreceipt of a particular statement, records, determination or request.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The officers may challenge the compulsory duties imposed on their offices

**Holding and operative rule:** A federal command requiring these plaintiff officers to perform identified work through their offices presents a concrete injury redressable by relief against that command. Their official status does not bar review, but it supplies no standing to obtain an advisory decision about independent obligations imposed only on absent dealers or purchasers.

**Authority:** The jurisdictional section of Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Raines distinguishes concrete injury from unauthorized legislators' abstract institutional dilution after fully effective votes. These officers instead challenge duties assigned directly to their offices. The Government's disavowal of criminal prosecution does not remove the asserted compulsory civil work. The Court reaches that controversy without inventing an actual prosecution or deciding the independent obligations of persons who are not parties. The grant does not reopen the separate lower-court vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment dispositions.

##### Part I — Appropriate temporary executive records assistance is not categorically forbidden

**Holding and operative rule:** In implementing an otherwise valid direct regulation of private transactions, Congress is not categorically barred from requiring appropriate, temporary records-based assistance within a state executive officer's proper function under an affirmative statutory direction, while federal law supplies the substantive transaction rule and federal authorities retain responsibility for coercive enforcement. That source-and-function rule defeats the asserted categorical incapacity to require the confined assistance presented here, but does not decide whether the actual compulsory arrangement is permissible in light of the officers' unresolved fiscal and unauthorized-expenditure objections.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five directly adopt this limited rule; their agreement does not extend to unconditional validity of the officer obligations, a right to fair compensation, or the ultimate disposition. The four Justices who reject Part I contribute no vote to it through their agreement with the remand.

**Controlling explanation:** New York forbids compelling a State to legislate for private actors or assume their property and liabilities. It did not decide every directive to an executive officer. Here Congress directly regulates commercial handgun transfers, supplies the transaction rule and expressly directs interim assistance. The executive task concerns receiving prescribed information, consulting available records and making the eligibility assessment those records support; it leaves state policymaking and general coercive enforcement with their respective governments. Garcia and Baker preserve federal regulation affecting state activity, and FERC and Testa show that federal and state responsibilities need not always be performed through wholly separate institutions. Their distinct settings do not themselves decide this case.

The structural objection correctly identifies real work and use of state resources. Part I neither treats that work as free nor makes convenience, low cost or temporary duration a constitutional power. It instead distinguishes the source, function and responsibility assigned. Voting Rights Coalition rests on separate election powers and supplies no general commerce exception. Lopez limits commerce power and forbids inserting a missing statutory element; this transaction regime has its own commercial basis, and the Necessary and Proper Clause implements that established power.

The reasonable-effort term, available-record language, interim context and absence of a command to arrest or prevent a transfer support the confined question decided. The word including does not mean only; the Court makes no categorical textual excision of every conceivable nonrecord act and supplies no independent investigative power. It decides no compulsory field-investigation, permanent state enforcement-organization or general policy-administration command. Nor does the officer's oath independently create a task absent enacted direction. The remaining fiscal objection concerns whether this actual arrangement may finally be sustained, and is genuinely left open.

**Statutory scope and conditions:** Section 922(s)(2) directs reasonable effort within five business days after the specified dealer notice, including research in available state and local systems and the designated national system. It does not require an arrest, prosecution, prevention of a transfer or adverse dealer notification. Section (6)(B) concerns a transmitted purchaser statement: unless the officer determines the transaction unlawful, the statement, derived-information records and notice-created records must be destroyed within twenty business days after the purchaser made the statement; disclosure is limited to a person needing the information to carry out the subsection, and use is limited to that purpose. The same unlawful-transaction exception qualifies those information restrictions. Section (6)(C) separately requires written reasons only after an ineligibility determination and the individual's request, within twenty business days after receipt of that request. No transaction-specific trigger is found satisfied or unsatisfied here.

**Limits:** The holding preserves New York's prohibition, the distinct sources for state-court and election duties, valid preemption, generally applicable regulation, voluntary cooperation and lawful spending arrangements. It establishes no general cheap-command exception, permanent compulsory records power, state-law veto of valid federal law or constitutional payment entitlement. No categorical holding validates or invalidates every ancillary information duty or independent dealer obligation.

##### Part II — The limited mandate binds without a majority fiscal rationale

**Operative disposition:** In both dockets, vacate the appellate judgments only insofar as they unconditionally sustain the challenged officer obligations, and remand for development and consideration of the existing budget and unauthorized-expenditure objections under Part I. No substantive entitlement to compensation, compensation cause of action, measure of fair value, state-law liability or taking is decided.

**Authority:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supply five votes for that judgment and its confined mandate. Souter's affirmative fair-value rationale has no other join. The other four support the remand only under the independently controlling Part I rule, while maintaining that compulsory administration should instead be invalidated. There is no majority fiscal rule and no Marks synthesis.

**Scope of the return:** The courts must genuinely consider the asserted objections and their legal consequences; ultimate constitutional validity has not already been decided against the officers. Development remains confined to the existing controversy and whatever ordinary proceedings are necessary. The mandate neither requires a new money claim or automatic hearing nor finds waiver, expenditure, liability, receipt of a form, retained information, an ineligibility determination or a request for reasons. It does not automatically revive every severability issue, decide absent parties' rights or declare that particular lower interim relief has revived or expired. The criminal-vagueness, unraised civil-vagueness and Thirteenth Amendment matters outside the grant remain undisturbed.

- New York v. United States: retains the prohibition on compulsory state legislation or ownership/liability and its lawful-inducement and generally applicable regulation boundaries. **Souter, Part II of the opinion announcing the judgment.** His reason for remand is that appropriate executive assistance cannot, in his view, mean compulsory administrative support without an obligation to pay fair value. **Stevens, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer join.** They would affirm the confined interim duties and their conditional safeguards. Congress directly governs commercial handgun transfers, retains the substantive policy and coercive consequences, and requires temporary, resource-sensitive information assistance. The scheme requires no state legislation, assumption of private property and liability, independent enforcement regime, arrest or prosecution. Election duties retain their own sources.

The budget and state-law assertions deserve an answer, but do not establish a constitutional payment condition or justify returning this constitutional judgment. The duty's actual functions and the responsibilities left to the State supply their answer; they do not presume free implementation or nonexistent exposure. Reasonable effort can account for resources without becoming optional whenever a budget lacks a line item. Their affirmance decides no state-law liability, taking or every possible fiscal claim. It requires no severance and provides no general permission for permanent state conscription.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment.** He would affirm both judgments and deny the requested injunction against the confined duties. Congress may require temporary, narrowly bounded assistance using lawfully available government records as an incident of otherwise valid private-transaction regulation, with federal substantive rules and federal responsibility for coercive enforcement. The mandatory eligibility assessment is real; calling supporting tasks ministerial does not eliminate it or its cost.

He reads reasonable effort in the whole interim scheme, its available-record tasks and the absence of a command to stop transfers or arrest purchasers. Including does not itself mean only. Ordinary checking and interpretation remain required; the construction adds no commerce element or administrative power and does not excuse all inquiry. He would authorize no compulsory independent field investigation, permanent state enforcement organization or transfer of general federal regulatory responsibility. The conditional explanations, destruction and information protections retain their separate statutory predicates; adverse dealer notification remains discretionary.

His position narrows his earlier categorical objection to compelled executive administration. The reason for that refinement is the difference between incidental records work and independent regulatory or coercive responsibility, not convenience or a claim that state work is costless. New York's compulsory-legislation prohibition, Lopez's source and no-rewriting limits, and the distinct election, state-court, preemption and voluntary-cooperation rules remain. His position creates no permanent duty, retroactive officer liability or reopening of a completed judgment. The asserted expenditures do not change his conclusion on these limited duties, and no severance or further fiscal proceeding is required. He leaves materially different commands and unpresented applications for another case.

**Breyer, additional opinion concurring in Part I and dissenting from the judgment.** Dividing execution between federal and state officials can preserve state autonomy and avoid expansion of a centralized federal bureaucracy. Comparative federal arrangements can illustrate that possibility, but are neither American constitutional authority nor proof that a foreign system can be transplanted here. His sufficient domestic grounds remain valid commerce power, necessary implementation, reasonable effort and the State's retained responsibilities. This supplements his full join in Stevens's opinion; it creates no general cost-benefit override, cheap-command exception or compensation cure.

### In re Cohen, Third Circuit No. 96-5155

Merits decision, June 30, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued and decided June 30, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to review the Third Circuit's February 6, 1997 judgment, 106 F.3d 52, lower appellate No. 96-5155. The questions are whether §523(a)(2)(A) limits nondischargeability to the money acquired by fraud or includes the full legally established liability, and whether the existing treble damages, fees and costs fall within that liability.

Cohen co-owned a rent-controlled Hoboken apartment building. The administrator ordered $31,382.50 in refunds; the owners neither paid nor perfected an administrative appeal and sought Chapter 7 relief. The tenants' adversary proceeding produced federal fraud findings and a $94,147.50 treble judgment, with attorney's fees and costs already awarded. The courts below sustained the fraud determination and discharge treatment. This review concerns that existing liability, not a new damages assessment.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The existing $94,147.50 treble judgment and the existing attributable attorney's-fee and cost award remain nondischargeable. No new award, recalculation or additional hearing is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each joins every controlling proposition and the affirmance. No separate writing issues.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Federal fraud remains a necessary predicate

**Holding and operative rule:** On the actual-fraud theory presented under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(2)(A), a qualifying acquisition requires actual and justifiable reliance proved by a preponderance of the evidence. The sustained federal fraud findings permit decision of this debt's extent; a State consumer-fraud label or treble award alone would not establish that predicate.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court identified above controls all four holdings and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 523(a)(2) addresses debts for money, property, services, or an extension, renewal or refinancing of credit, to the extent obtained by its specified conduct. Subparagraph (A) covers false pretenses, false representation or actual fraud, but excludes statements respecting the debtor's or an insider's financial condition. Subparagraph (B) separately requires a materially false writing respecting that financial condition, reasonable reliance, and the debtor's causing its making or publication with intent to deceive. This is an actual-fraud acquisition of rents, not a financial-statement case or a decision of every representation theory.

Field v. Mans supplies actual and individualized justifiable reliance; Grogan supplies the preponderance burden. The recipient's knowledge, capacities and circumstances matter. There is no universal duty to investigate the ordinance. A recipient cannot rely on falsity personally known or obvious to that recipient; an actually encountered warning may require inquiry under that recipient-specific standard. Objective imprudence may bear on whether reliance actually occurred, without becoming a prudent-person test. Limited tenant knowledge of the ordinance therefore does not itself defeat reliance. The bankruptcy findings, sustained below, include Cohen's selective knowledge of favorable provisions and deliberate avoidance of rent limits. The Court makes no new findings and identifies no supported material federal-fraud error requiring remand. A material preserved defect would receive its ordinary disposition under Field; the possibility of additional facts supplies no basis for an exploratory proceeding.

##### II. The exception covers valid liability for the fraudulent acquisition

**Holding and operative rule:** Once money or property is obtained through the fraud covered by §523(a)(2)(A), the nondischargeable debt includes the legally enforceable liability arising from that acquisition, not merely the value the debtor received. A qualifying acquisition and a legal connection between it and the liability remain necessary; lawful receipts and unrelated debts are outside the rule.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part II in full.

**Controlling explanation:** The Code defines debt as liability on a claim. The acquisition language identifies which receipts give rise to the excepted liability; it does not redefine that liability as restitution alone. The overcharged rents supply the qualifying acquisition here, while lawfully received rents do not. State law establishes enforceable liability, and federal law independently determines its discharge treatment.

Cohen's strongest reading accepts that the modifier attaches to the acquired money or property but argues that unreceived enhancements cannot represent what was obtained. That reading gives the acquisition words a role but too little work to debt. Liability can arise from the qualifying acquisition without every remedial consequence being another acquisition. Sections 523(a)(4) and (6) address distinct wrongs; their different language does not impose a restitution cap on (a)(2)(A) after its acquisition condition is met. Their independent elements are not decided. Neither the placement of the acquisition phrase nor a general fresh-start policy replaces the Code's liability definition. Text and structure suffice; legislative history is not essential to the result.

Field expressly left debt extent and the obtained-by relationship open. Noland's adherence to statutory categories supports disciplined construction, but its priority and equitable-subordination holding does not resolve this discharge question. The Court creates no State cause of action or federal damages remedy and does not except every obligation of a dishonest debtor.

##### III. The existing treble judgment is nondischargeable even assuming a wholly punitive enhancement

**Holding and operative rule:** The existing $94,147.50 treble judgment for $31,382.50 in fraudulently acquired rent is nondischargeable in full. Even if the enhancement is assumed wholly punitive, that character does not remove valid liability for this qualifying acquisition from §523(a)(2)(A).

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part III in full.

**Controlling explanation:** The enhancement is an enforceable consequence of the same fraudulent receipts. A court cannot add a general equitable exception for punitive consequences to the category Congress enacted. Noland reinforces respect for statutory categories, without making every penalty nondischargeable. An unrelated penalty, or an obligation outside this qualifying acquisition, remains outside this holding. The Court does not decide that New Jersey's enhancement is wholly punitive: the lower court assumed that characterization, while Cox v. Sears recognized a partly compensatory purpose. The federal result is the same under the assumption, so settling that State-law question is unnecessary. The $94,147.50 figure is the existing total, not a new addition to the $31,382.50 acquisition.

##### IV. Existing attributable fees and costs share the debt's treatment; no new award is made

**Holding and operative rule:** Valid State-law attorney's fees and costs imposed because of, and forming part of liability for, this qualifying fraudulent acquisition are also nondischargeable. The Court affirms the existing connected fee-and-cost award with the treble judgment, without adding an amount, awarding new relief or directing further factfinding.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part IV and the judgment in full.

**Controlling explanation:** The same liability construction governs the existing fees and costs attributable to establishing or recovering this debt. A valid underlying entitlement and the fraud connection are necessary; federal discharge law supplies neither a new fee entitlement nor coverage for unrelated claims. No separate fee-and-cost amount is specified here, and none is calculated. The decision awards no Supreme Court fees, new appellate fees, future costs, interest or enlarged enhancement. Ordinary enforcement remains in the proper proceedings. The Court sets no distribution priority, collection terms or payment schedule, assesses no liability against another person and orders no recalculation or additional hearing.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Field v. Mans preserves actual and justifiable reliance for the actual-fraud theory and its individualized limits. Its express reservation of debt extent is resolved here for this qualifying acquisition; its distinct financial-condition rules remain intact.
- Grogan v. Garner supplies the preponderance burden for the federal discharge exception.
- United States v. Noland reinforces adherence to the Code's enacted categories. Its priority and equitable-subordination rule is not extended into a direct holding on fraud discharge or every punitive debt.
- Cox v. Sears identifies a partly compensatory purpose in New Jersey's treble remedy; the Court leaves the exact State-law characterization undecided because even the wholly punitive assumption does not change the result. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The actual-fraud acquisition and federal reliance requirements remain necessary. Once satisfied, §523(a)(2)(A) reaches valid liability for that acquisition, including its enforceable enhancement and attributable fees and costs. It is not an exception for every debt associated with dishonesty or every penalty. State law supplies the existing liability; the Code determines its discharge status.

### United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder, Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513

Merits decision, June 30, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Consolidated review; district cases CR-95-00009-PGR and CR-95-00010-1-PGR. Argued May 27, 1997; decided June 30, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to review the Ninth Circuit's September 5, 1996 judgment, 95 F.3d 787, reversing each separate indictment dismissal and remanding both prosecutions. The questions concern Congress's commerce power over the interstate support-payment obligation, the Tenth Amendment and domestic-relations objections, and the temporal limit necessary to lawful further proceedings.

Both indictments were returned January 11, 1995. Mussari's concerns support for two Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning November 1, 1988; Schroeder's concerns four Arizona children and alleges nonpayment beginning February 1, 1992. Both allege different-state residence and obligations exceeding $5,000 and unpaid longer than one year. Those are allegations, not findings of the offense. Each defendant seeks restoration of his dismissal; the United States seeks to preserve both trial remands.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States v. Mussari | Ninth Circuit reversal of the indictment dismissal affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | Dismissal remains reversed; trial remand proceeds subject to post-enactment and proof limits |
| United States v. Schroeder | Ninth Circuit reversal of the separate indictment dismissal affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | Separate dismissal remains reversed; separate trial remand proceeds under the same limits |

Neither affirmance enters a conviction, alters a State order or fixes a sentence or restitution amount.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each joins every controlling proposition and both disposition components. No separate writing issues.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Congress may protect the legally fixed interstate payment obligation

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may punish willful post-enactment failure to pay a legally fixed past-due child-support obligation when the obligor and child reside in different States, because §228 directly protects that cross-border economic payment obligation. The different-state relation is essential to this holding; the Court sustains neither regulation of every family debt nor federal creation or revision of the underlying support duty.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court identified above controls every holding and the affirmance in both prosecutions.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 228(a) requires willful failure to pay a past-due support obligation for a child residing in another State. Section 228(d)(1) requires an amount fixed by a court order or State-law administrative process for support of a child, or a child and custodial parent, unpaid longer than one year or exceeding $5,000. Those thresholds are alternatives; the indictments happen to allege both. Neither the amount nor duration substitutes for the interstate relation.

The object regulated is the required cross-border payment itself. Different residence does constitutional work because the existing obligation calls for satisfaction between the obligor and children in another State. That is a legal characterization of the charged class, not a finding of actual transfer. A completed payment need not precede an offense addressing its willful withholding. The State-law origin and absence of a negotiated bargain do not erase the obligation's economic payment character.

The strongest objection treats the default as a local family failure with a geographical label. Lopez rejects deriving federal power from remote chains through poverty, welfare expense, crime or productivity. The Court relies on none. Harris and Bishop/Stokes protect the covered vehicle as an interstate instrumentality against direct forcible taking and require proof of the enacted vehicle element; Hale addresses possession integral to direct market regulation. They require attention to the actual object and relation, not an automatic commerce label for every debt or object with interstate history. The present due cross-border payment supplies that relation independently.

Lopez also forbids rewriting a statute to supply a missing element. No flight, travel, voluntary State crossing, bank wire, particular instrument or motive to evade is added here. The Court decides no statute lacking the different-state condition and no unrelated obligation bearing only an incidental geographical feature. It makes no finding of deliberate evasion or ability to pay.

##### II. Direct regulation of these private obligors neither commandeers a State nor issues a domestic-relations decree

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 228's direct regulation of the private obligor does not compel State administration, and the limited domestic-relations exception does not bar these federal prosecutions. The existing operative support order supplies a predicate; federal courts may apply the offense's elements and lawful defenses but may not create or modify that obligation, custody, parentage or domestic status.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part II in full.

**Controlling explanation:** New York v. United States distinguishes valid direct private regulation from compelled State responsibility. Section 228 commands no State legislature, court or executive officer to legislate, enforce the debt, investigate for federal officials, alter an order or assume the obligor's liability. Printz's qualified executive-assistance rule concerns a different means of implementation. This prosecution requires no approval of compulsory State assistance and presents no analogous fiscal question. The absence of commandeering alone supplies no commerce power; Part I independently supplies it.

Ankenbrandt v. Richards confines the statutory diversity exception to issuance of divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees, rather than imposing a general Article III exclusion for all matters involving families. This federal criminal proceeding issues no such decree. The State-law origin of the debt therefore neither defeats the established federal power nor transfers family-law authority to the federal tribunal. The Court does not recalculate support, determine a modification or prescribe State enforcement machinery. A cognizable challenge to whether the predicate order exists and remains operative is not foreclosed. Hypothetical interference with a different State proceeding is not decided.

##### III. Both prosecutions remain confined to post-enactment failures and proof of every element

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal conviction and punishment must rest on willful failures after §228's October 25, 1992 enactment, with every applicable element proved beyond a reasonable doubt. Earlier arrears may establish lawful background or past-due status, but a completed pre-enactment failure cannot itself constitute the federal offense.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part III in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Mussari's alleged period starts November 1, 1988, and Schroeder's February 1, 1992. Neither indictment's earlier starting date enlarges the enacted offense. The Ex Post Facto Clause independently prohibits punishment for conduct completed before enactment. Landgraf and Rivers reinforce attention to the legal consequences attached to completed conduct; their distinct civil approaches do not replace that criminal prohibition or require agreement on every earlier temporal rationale.

The constitutional ruling establishes neither defendant's willfulness, capacity to pay, qualifying fixed amount, required unpaid duration, different-state residence for the necessary period, or any sentencing fact. Ability-related defenses recognized by law and other lawful defenses remain available. Each existing prosecution returns for ordinary proceedings within its charges and applicable statutory, temporal and procedural limits. No new allegation, amended indictment, offense period, conviction or special trial instruction beyond this legal boundary is directed. The Court adopts no comprehensive continuing-offense theory to eliminate a separate proof or notice question.

##### IV. Each appellate trial remand stands; any restitution remains a separate conditional issue

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment reversing each district-court indictment dismissal and remanding each prosecution is affirmed, subject to the post-enactment limit and ordinary proof requirements. Section 228(c) requires restitution under §3663 upon conviction equal to the past-due obligation existing at sentencing; this pretrial decision fixes no amount and does not decide the precise lawful treatment of older arrears in such an order.

**Authority:** The common nine-Justice coalition joins Part IV and both disposition components in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Sustaining the presented constitutional basis for prosecution does not decide guilt or sentencing. The statutory first-offense exposure is a fine, imprisonment up to six months, or both; a subsequent offense permits a fine, imprisonment up to two years, or both. No prior offense or sentence is found here. Restitution's separate command is conditional on conviction. The rule against punishing a completed pre-enactment omission does not automatically exclude every older arrear from a later restitution calculation; nor does the sentencing-balance language automatically approve inclusion without the separate statutory and constitutional inquiry. Both questions remain for any proceeding in which they actually arise. Neither dismissal is restored. The Court orders no detention, conviction, restitution award, compensation proceeding, State-order revision or compulsory State administrative act.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Lopez is applied through its connection-before-magnitude sequence: §228's concrete statutory cross-border payment mechanism supplies the connection Lopez requires, without aggregation or a downstream-effects chain. Lopez's rejection of that chain for stand-alone school-zone possession and its prohibition on supplying a missing statutory commerce element are retained.
- United States v. Harris and Bishop/Stokes preserve their actual direct-instrumentality ground and proof of the enacted vehicle nexus. Their result is not converted into an automatic instrumentality rule or a holding about every debt.
- United States v. Hale preserves its integral-market ground and rejection of a universal transaction-specific nexus requirement; the present payment relation must independently support federal power.
- New York v. United States preserves the distinction between direct private regulation within enumerated power and compulsory State responsibility.
- Printz/Mack's qualified source-and-function rule and confined fiscal mandate concern compelled public administration. They supply neither the commerce premise nor a fiscal remand for these private obligations; no compensation rule is adopted.
- Ankenbrandt v. Richards preserves the statutory diversity-decree exception and rejects a general Article III family-law exclusion. Federal enforcement here issues no domestic-relations decree.
- Landgraf and Rivers retain their civil temporal rules at their own scope; the independent Ex Post Facto prohibition controls completed pre-enactment criminal conduct. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Section 228 may directly protect the legally fixed payment obligation owed across State lines on the narrow ground stated. Its actual elements remain necessary; no generalized economic-effects rationale, universal debt rule or judicially added travel requirement governs. State authority to establish and modify the underlying support duty remains intact. The criminal offense-date boundary and any eventual treatment of older arrears in restitution remain distinct.

Conviction and punishment must concern post-enactment willful failures. No guilt, detention, sentence or restitution disposition is ordered, and the precise lawful restitution treatment of older arrears remains open.

### Faragher v. City of Boca Raton, Eleventh Circuit No. 94-4878

Merits decision, September 8, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued and decided September 8, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to review the en banc judgment reported at 111 F.3d 1530, entered April 15, 1997 and amended April 28. The petition was filed June 2; the Court granted review July 14 on four employer-liability questions and received expedited merits briefing through August 29 for this sitting.

The questions concern responsibility for a hostile environment materially aided by supervisory authority outside ordinary employment scope, whether higher-management knowledge is necessary, whether preventive care and unreasonable employee nonuse can supply a complete defense without tangible employment action, and whether a formal policy suffices without effective dissemination or implementation.

Faragher worked intermittently as a City ocean lifeguard during 1985–1990. A bench trial established repeated unwelcome sex-based touching and humiliating conduct by Terry and Silverman, who exercised command over assignments, discipline, training and daily work in a unit remote from higher officials. The City did not communicate its harassment policy to that unit. Faragher complained to Gordon, another supervisor, but not higher management; the City investigated promptly after another former employee's letter. The district court awarded her one dollar on the Title VII claim against the City. The appellate court accepted the hostile-environment finding but reversed that award on employer-attribution grounds. Faragher seeks restoration; the City seeks affirmance.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed in the reviewed Title VII respect and remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, who would vacate the reviewed Title VII disposition and remand for a determination of actual material aid under the correct rule, restoring the one-dollar judgment only if that connection is established and otherwise reaching the preserved negligence and notice grounds.

The Court reverses only the portion of the appellate judgment rejecting Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City. Its mandate requires reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further attribution findings. The separate individual §1983 and battery awards, other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions are outside this review. No new monetary award or fee determination is made.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope and relationship to judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in full; Stone-Zsela joins Part I only | All eight full joiners support the restoration mandate. Part I's governing actual-authority rule has nine joins; Parts II–IV have eight. |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | Author alone | Joins Part I; would require limited correct-rule review of the aid connection and would reject, rather than reserve, a complete preventive-care/noncomplaint defense once that connection is proved. Agrees separately with the policy principles identified below. |

There is a majority rationale for every controlling holding. Stone-Zsela's agreement with the governing rule and particular policy principles is not agreement with the Court's conclusive application or restoration mandate.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Actual delegated authority can establish responsibility without higher-management notice

**Holding and operative rule:** Actual delegated supervisory authority materially aiding creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment supplies an independent ground for employer responsibility under Title VII; the plaintiff must establish that connection. Supervisory rank or workplace proximity alone is insufficient; higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel decision and tangible economic loss are not invariably required.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion has nine direct joins, as stated above. Those nine directly support this rule. The eight Associates join Parts II–IV and the judgment in full; that common eight-Justice coalition controls the remaining holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII protects employment conditions and includes an employer's agent in its definition. Meritor directs an agency inquiry while rejecting both automatic responsibility for every supervisor's act and automatic immunity without management notice. Andrade supplies the required actual-power connection, and Oncale applies it while preserving discriminatory causation, an actionable environment and attribution as separate predicates.

Delegated control over daily working conditions may materially aid the discriminatory conditions even when the misconduct serves a personal purpose and violates employer policy. An employer's prohibition of harassment therefore does not alone answer whether its actual delegation aided the wrong. But employment merely providing an opportunity to encounter the plaintiff is not enough. The plaintiff must prove the connection.

Ordinary scope of employment, apparent authority and employer negligence remain distinct routes. Higher-management knowledge matters to negligence without becoming an additional element of proved actual-authority attribution. Harris and DeAngelis govern the separate contextual hostile-environment threshold; proof meeting that threshold does not itself establish agency or constructive notice. The Court applies the actual-authority route here and leaves the alternative routes undecided.

##### II. The supported operational findings establish the required connection here

**Holding and operative rule:** The established allocation and exercise of assignments, discipline, daily duties and supervision in this work unit materially aided maintenance of its accepted hostile conditions. These supported bench findings establish the actual-authority ground without a new threat, fear or employment-penalty finding, and do not require a further attribution proceeding.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part II. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela does not join this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry controlled assignments, reprimands and personnel-file discipline and selected recruits subject to higher approval. Silverman controlled daily duties, assignments and fitness routines. Their powers actually governed the working conditions of a geographically separated unit organized through their command. The district court's agency determination rested on those powers and the workplace structure, independently of its disputed constructive-notice ground.

This was more than supervisory capacity accompanied by an opportunity for misconduct. The operative control of Faragher's daily duties and on-site working conditions supplied the authority through which these supervisors maintained the repeated abusive environment. Remoteness and ineffective policy communication reinforce that practical structure; neither becomes a required element for every future plaintiff or a substitute for actual aid. The Court attributes this established course of hostile working conditions, not every personal act of every supervisor.

Historical facts receive clear-error review; their legal significance receives independent review. The Court adds no finding that Faragher feared discharge, received an order to submit, faced a particular threat, lost wages or suffered an employment penalty. Making a firing threat or completed adverse decision indispensable would restore the restriction Andrade rejects.

The strongest remand objection is that a list of powers shows only capacity and that the trial's agency label did not expressly distinguish facilitation from rank. The supported findings go further: they describe the actual organization and exercise of control over the conditions in which the established abuse was maintained. Their legal consequence can be decided without inventing another historical fact. Meritor's unresolved harassment findings and Andrade's unresolved verdict, instruction and sufficiency questions warranted their own remands; neither requires a new proceeding on this adequate bench record. Whether constructive notice or Gordon's knowledge independently establishes liability is unnecessary to decide.

##### III. This ineffective-policy contention does not defeat liability; a general defense remains open

**Holding and operative rule:** In this case, the combination of a policy not communicated to the relevant workforce, failure to complain to distant higher management and correction after another employee's eventual report does not defeat the proved authority-aided liability. The Court leaves open whether genuinely effective protection and unreasonable employee nonuse could establish a complete defense, including its elements, burden, relationship to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on particular relief.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part III. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees separately that an undistributed policy does not establish unreasonable nonuse and that later correction does not erase earlier attributable misconduct, but would reject the proposed complete defense rather than reserve it.

**Controlling explanation:** Meritor does not make a paper grievance policy and noncomplaint conclusive. An uncommunicated procedure does not establish available protection that an employee unreasonably declined. Prompt action after eventual notice matters to the adequacy of that response; it does not establish effective protection during the earlier authority-aided environment or retroactively erase that conduct.

The grant permits consideration of a complete defense, and effective prevention and reasonable avoidance present serious interests. But Andrade already supplies a plaintiff-proved actual-aid limitation, and the City's present contention fails without deciding how genuinely effective measures would affect responsibility. The Court neither adopts a new burden-shifting formula nor rejects every possible complete defense. Nor does it decide a tangible-action exception or a general duty to complain.

No finding is made that no alternative official could ever have been approached, that the policy contained a particular promise, or that Faragher held an unproved belief. Questions about alternative reporting and whether dissemination would have exposed the misconduct belong to their proper agency, negligence or remedial inquiries; they need not be resolved against the City to sustain this independent ground. Failure to disseminate a policy alone does not prove every negligence element or create a freestanding statutory publication duty. Effective procedures, knowledge, correction and avoidable loss retain their possible legal significance where their actual predicates are presented.

##### IV. The existing nominal judgment is reinstated within the grant's limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The appellate judgment is reversed only insofar as it reversed Faragher's Title VII judgment against the City, and the case is remanded for reinstatement of the existing one-dollar judgment without a new merits trial or further agency findings. No individual or state-law award, remaining appellate disposition, fee entitlement or additional remedy is adjudicated.

**Authority:** The common eight-Justice coalition joins Part IV and this mandate. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela instead supports the distinct conditional remand described in his separate writing.

**Controlling explanation:** The sufficient actual-authority ground supports the existing judgment independently of constructive notice, imputation of Gordon's knowledge, ordinary employment scope or apparent authority. Those alternatives remain undecided; a defect in an unnecessary alternative does not require an exploratory proceeding on the sufficient ground.

Landgraf bars attaching the 1991 Act's new §102 compensatory and punitive liabilities to this 1985–1990 conduct; its dependent jury provision creates no new jury right here. Restoration of nominal relief does not enlarge that remedy. The separate $10,000 §1983 judgment against Terry and Silverman and $500 battery punitive award against Terry remain outside the grant, along with other state claims and remaining appellate dispositions.

Farrar separates enforceable nominal relief from the reasonable fee amount under §1988. That distinct statute supplies only a limited analogy; any Title VII fee question requires its own entitlement and reasonableness analysis. The Court fixes no fee, new damages, prospective injunction, constructive-discharge finding or employment reinstatement. The appellate remand implements the existing one-dollar judgment and sets no special deadline or retained proceeding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson preserves agency-based attribution, the rejection of automatic liability for every supervisor's conduct, and the rejection of a conclusive paper-policy/noncomplaint defense. Its unresolved findings do not require a new trial on this supported record; no comprehensive defense is supplied by its reservation.
- Andrade v. Mayfair Management applies its actual-delegated-authority/material-aid rule, including the plaintiff's connection requirement and rejection of categorical management-notice or tangible-action prerequisites. Its distinct Rule 50 remand does not require renewed factfinding here; its reservation of general defenses remains open.
- Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services applies its reaffirmation of Andrade and preserves separate sex-causation, hostile-environment and attribution predicates. Its allegation-stage remand supplies no contrary command for these tried findings.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems retains the contextual objective-and-subjective hostile-environment inquiry without mandatory psychological or economic injury. It does not establish employer attribution or constructive notice merely from severity.
- DeAngelis v. El Paso Municipal Police Officers' Association preserves its application of that contextual threshold without extra injury or hierarchy prerequisites. Its distinct newsletter record does not resolve the present attribution inquiry.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products preserves the unavailability of new §102 damages for this pre-enactment conduct and the dependent jury limit. No new damages or jury entitlement is created.
- Farrar v. Hobby retains the distinction between enforceable nominal relief and a reasonable fee amount under §1988. It does not decide the separate Title VII fee question. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The actual-authority route remains a fact-dependent ground separate from negligence, apparent authority and ordinary employment scope. Existing findings of the operational control that materially aided these hostile conditions suffice for the nominal City judgment. A paper policy, noncomplaint to higher management and eventual correction supply no defense on this record. The Court adopts neither a general preventive-care/nonuse defense nor a rule forbidding every such defense; its elements, burden, tangible-action relationship and any distinct remedial limitation remain open. No title-based liability, automatic knowledge imputation or general complaint duty follows.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no other Justice joins.** He joins Part I but would vacate for a limited agency determination. Meritor's agency instruction and Restatement §219(2)(d)'s aid principle give delegated power distinct work beyond ordinary scope or permission to harass. Assignments, discipline and access can facilitate coercive conditions beyond coworker proximity; actual connection remains necessary. Apparent authority separately requires employer-attributable manifestations and reasonable reliance, not belief that assault was lawfully authorized. Unsupported supervisory claims or proximity do not establish either route.

He rejects mandatory higher-management notice or a firing threat for actual-authority attribution. Harris severity does not establish constructive notice; that asks what reasonable employer care would have discovered. He would require correct-rule application to the existing findings, with further findings only if a material issue remains. If they establish material aid, the one-dollar judgment should be restored without reopening the accepted environment; otherwise the preserved negligence and notice grounds must be resolved before judgment for either party. Clarification alone requires no new evidentiary trial.

Unlike the Court, he would reject a freestanding complete preventive-care/noncomplaint defense after materially aided misuse is proved. Title VII and Meritor supply no general immunity extinguishing that attributed discrimination. Reasonable care may defeat negligence; appropriate facts may defeat apparent authority or causation or limit recoverable avoidable loss under governing remedial law. Those inquiries do not erase proved actual-authority attribution. An undistributed policy cannot establish unreasonable nonuse, and later correction cannot erase earlier attributable misconduct. Failed dissemination alone is not negligence per se.

Landgraf bars new post-1991 damages. Personal constitutional and state claims remain outside his proposed disposition; collateral fees require their own statutory and reasonableness inquiry. His conditional restoration is not a vote for the Court's present restoration mandate.

### Harrison v. Eddy Potash, Inc., Tenth Circuit Nos. 96-2045 and 96-2065

Merits decision, September 15, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued and decided September 15, 1997. On writ of certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to review the May 8, 1997 judgment, 112 F.3d 1437. Eddy Potash is the employer-petitioner. The lower mandate was held for this review; no intervening retrial or settlement occurred.

The four questions concern employer responsibility when delegated work authority facilitates harassment; a proposed managerial or hire-and-fire threshold; the prejudicial charge requiring authority to commit harassment and reliance rather than permitting actual-authority aid; and contractual exhaustion before an independent Title VII action. Harrison defends the appellate remand and access to court; Potash seeks preservation of its Title VII verdict or dismissal for nonexhaustion.

Harrison alleged that foreman Brown used work assignments to isolate her and engaged in repeated unwanted sexual conduct in 1993. Brown controlled daily assignments and participated in discipline. A jury awarded $102,500 compensatory and $40,000 punitive damages against him on state tort claims but found for Potash on Title VII. The appellate court reversed the employer verdict for prejudicial instructional error and rejected Potash's cross-appeal. The tort verdict supplies no special finding resolving all statutory elements against the employer.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No. 96-2045, employee appeal | Reversal of Potash's Title VII verdict and remand affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | — | Further district-court proceedings under the corrected rule and resolved prejudice determination |

All nine join the further-proceedings mandate contemplating retrial subject to lawful resolution of genuinely preserved independent grounds. Any jury submission must use the corrected instructions. The separate Brown awards remain undisturbed; no employer liability or new award is entered.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, Parts I–V, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly join all five holdings, both appellate affirmances and the complete common mandate. No separate writing issues.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Material aid from actual delegated power does not require alter-ego rank

**Holding and operative rule:** An employer may be responsible under Title VII when actual delegated supervisory power materially aids creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment; the plaintiff must prove that connection by a preponderance of the evidence. Hiring or firing authority, alter-ego identity, higher-management notice, an express threat, a completed personnel action and tangible economic loss are not invariably required, while rank, employment or workplace proximity alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court identified above controls all five holdings and the mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Title VII's inclusion of an employer's agents and Meritor require an actual agency inquiry, distinct from ordinary employment scope and negligence. Andrade, Oncale and Faragher establish the plaintiff-proved connection between delegated authority and the unlawful conditions. Personal purpose or violation of a prohibition does not alone negate that connection; permission to harass is not its prerequisite.

Harrison's account describes Brown using work orders to move her away from her station or buddy into isolated areas, where misconduct allegedly followed. Control over where, with whom and on what task she worked could materially aid abuse even without top-management powers. That evidence supports submission of the route; the Court does not find every incident proved. Conversely, impressive powers do not establish that they aided any particular wrong.

The agency definition required managerial control making Brown the employer's alter ego. Its reference to control over employment conditions did not remove that categorical restriction. Meritor rejects the opposite status-alone rule too, so rejection of Harrison's overbroad proposed formulations was proper. She was entitled to an accurate instruction on a supported route, not every requested sentence.

An actionable environment and sex-based causation remain separate. Harris requires conditions sufficiently severe or pervasive, in all the circumstances, to be objectively hostile or abusive and actually perceived as abusive. Frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation and interference with work inform that inquiry; none is indispensable, and psychological injury, treatment and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Oncale preserves proof that the conditions were imposed because of sex. Grave misconduct alone supplies neither causation nor employer attribution.

##### II. The charge prejudicially withheld the supported actual-authority route

**Holding and operative rule:** Considered as a whole against this record, the preserved charge was prejudicially incomplete because it made apparent authority and reasonable reliance cumulative requirements, imposed an alter-ego barrier and omitted the supported actual-authority/material-aid route. If a distinct apparent-authority theory is properly submitted, it requires reasonable belief traceable to the employer's words or conduct and reasonable reliance on that appearance, not merely the supervisor's claimed power; that alternative is not a condition of actual-authority attribution.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II. The actual-authority omission independently suffices for affirmance of the instructional reversal; factual sufficiency of apparent authority is not decided.

**Controlling explanation:** The principal charge was given over objection, and the requested instructions presented the aid issue as well as overbroad status-based theories. Independent review of the whole charge, with prejudice required, differs from reviewing the choice of a particular requested instruction for abuse of discretion. Neither inquiry requires verbatim use of an inaccurate proposal.

The charge's reference to authority otherwise than apparently did not explain material aid or remove the mandatory apparent-authority and reliance elements. Brown's daily control and lack of top-management status made those barriers consequential. Work-order evidence concretely supported the omitted route. A possible jury rejection of another Title VII element does not establish that this general employer verdict rested on an independent, correctly decided ground; no special finding supplies that conclusion. Nor does the separate tort verdict against Brown establish the statutory elements against Potash. This is a record-specific prejudice determination, not automatic reversal for any agency error.

Restatement §27 supplies the limited principal-manifestation clarification. Faragher left apparent authority undecided; its separate observations are not a Court holding. An employee's knowledge of a supervisor's assertion is not a substitute for manifestations attributable to the employer. Knowledge that harassment is forbidden does not erase actual command power. An effectively implemented policy may bear on a properly framed apparent-authority inquiry, but the Court decides neither that route's factual sufficiency nor that ignorance of policy alone establishes it. It does not adopt every apparent-authority formulation below or extend Faragher's actual-delegation rule to every claimed apparent power.

##### III. Policy and response do not cure the omitted route; a general complete defense remains open

**Holding and operative rule:** A prohibition of harassment and correction after notice do not themselves negate earlier material aid from actual delegated power or cure the prejudicially incomplete charge. The Court preserves Faragher's reservation concerning a general complete defense based on genuinely effective prevention and unreasonable employee nonuse, including its elements, burden, relation to tangible employment action and any distinct limitation on relief.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part III, including its limited reservation of the Court's general complete-defense question. No complete defense is adopted or supplied to Potash.

**Controlling explanation:** Faragher rejected the ineffective-policy contention on its own proved record while adopting neither a general defense nor categorical rejection of every such defense. Harrison requires her own policy chronology. She discovered a posted policy after some incidents, and it was absent from her new-hire materials. Those facts establish neither universal ignorance nor that every reporting route was unavailable, nonuse was unreasonable or every remedial step was inadequate.

After her July report, Potash investigated, promised lost-time compensation and treatment, changed her crew, reprimanded Brown, placed him on permanent probation for recurrence and restricted contact. Those measures retain their potential relevance to employer care, response and relief; they do not retroactively remove earlier actual aid. Failed dissemination alone is not negligence per se. The investigator's acceptance of Harrison's unwilling participation alongside Brown's asserted perception of consent is not a judicial finding that the conduct was welcome; Meritor distinguishes unwelcome conduct from apparently voluntary participation.

The different policy facts permit their proper consideration, but establish no complete defense here. The charge was not an accurate, separately justified instruction on an established defense. Faragher's reservation does not expand the granted or preserved issues, create a reporting duty or supply an appellate finding against Harrison. Appropriate apparent-authority, negligence and remedial inquiries remain distinct. No omnibus policy immunity, new burden-shifting formula or freestanding policy-publication duty is adopted.

##### IV. This appellate record establishes no contractual bar to the independent Title VII action

**Holding and operative rule:** A contractual grievance assertion does not remove federal subject-matter jurisdiction over an independent Title VII claim. Potash cannot obtain dismissal for nonexhaustion on this appellate record, which includes neither its CBA nor the district court's exhaustion ruling and identifies no agreement covering arbitration of statutory claims or delegation of the antecedent question.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part IV and affirmance of the rejection of the employer's cross-appeal.

**Controlling explanation:** Gardner-Denver preserves independent Title VII rights despite contractual grievance arbitration. Gilmer enforces an actual agreement to arbitrate the statutory ADEA claim; it does not supply a missing agreement here or overrule the contractual/statutory distinction. First Options requires courts independently to determine agreement and clear, unmistakable delegation under applicable contract law. A bargaining relationship or asserted grievance process proves neither statutory scope nor delegation. Its conditional rule for review after actual delegation is not reached.

The appellate opinion's two footnotes report the CBA and order omissions and that Potash's brief did not identify statutory arbitration. These are actual limits of its appellate presentation, not a finding that no contract ever existed or a determination of waiver. The strongest enforcement argument recognizes that a proven forum agreement can create duties without extinguishing substantive rights; it still requires the agreement this record does not establish.

Livadas and Hawaiian Airlines require attention to the actual source of a right and whether disputed contract interpretation is necessary. Whether sex-based conditions and delegated power meet Title VII's elements does not require such interpretation here. Their distinct preemption holdings do not establish immunity from every arbitration agreement. The Court declines the lower court's blanket CBA/FAA rationale. Gilmer reserved the general employment-contract exclusion; that question and the validity of every explicit collective statutory-arbitration agreement remain unresolved. No contract-recovery or new exhaustion proceeding is ordered merely to rescue the unsupported cross-appeal.

##### V. Both appellate results stand, with liability and relief for proper further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** The Tenth Circuit's reversal of Potash's Title VII verdict and remand in No. 96-2045, and its rejection of Potash's cross-appeal in No. 96-2065, are affirmed. The remand contemplates a new Title VII trial under corrected instructions, subject to lawful resolution of genuinely preserved independent grounds; if the surviving claim is submitted to a jury, the supported actual-authority route must be accurately instructed, with the prejudicial error already resolved.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part V and the common further-proceedings mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** No second appellate reconsideration of the same prejudice question is required. The lower opinion identifies the preserved omission, supporting evidence and resulting prejudice; Faragher's intervening rule now governs directly. Its restoration of a nominal judgment on supported bench findings and an accepted environment does not transfer findings to Harrison's jury case. Brown's separate $102,500 compensatory and $40,000 punitive tort awards concern a different defendant and different elements; they remain undisturbed and do not become an award against Potash.

The 1993 incidents postdate November 21, 1991, so Landgraf's temporal bar does not confine Harrison to Faragher's one-dollar remedy. This establishes no entitlement or amount. Section 102 compensation remains distinct from §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by §706(g). Punitive relief requires malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights; no such finding is made. When available §102 compensatory or punitive damages are sought, either party may demand a jury, which must not be told the statutory monetary caps. No cap calculation is made here. Ordinary protection against duplicate compensation applies.

The Court awards no damages, fees, reinstatement or other new relief and adjudicates no new termination or constructive-discharge claim. Independent fee requirements remain for a properly presented collateral issue. The preserved defenses and remaining statutory elements are not displaced by reversal of a defective charge.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Meritor supplies agency analysis, preserves the distinction between unwelcome conduct and voluntary participation, and rejects automatic supervisor liability and conclusive paper-policy immunity.
- Andrade supplies the actual-delegation/material-aid route and its connection requirement; its separate Rule 50 remand does not require another appellate prejudice proceeding here.
- Oncale preserves sex causation, actionable conditions and attribution as separate predicates, and applies Andrade without a title-only rule.
- Faragher supplies the actual-authority rule and preserves the general-defense reservation. Its bench findings, nominal restoration and pre-1991 remedy setting are distinguished; its apparent-authority alternative remains undecided.
- Harris supplies the contextual objective-and-subjective severe-or-pervasive standard without injury or productivity prerequisites. DeAngelis preserves that standard without adding hierarchy, physical advance or work-loss requirements. Neither establishes attribution from severity alone.
- Gardner-Denver preserves the independent Title VII right despite contractual grievance arbitration. Gilmer enforces an actual statutory-arbitration agreement without supplying absent terms or deciding the general FAA employment-contract exclusion.
- First Options requires antecedent agreement and clear, unmistakable delegation; its conditional post-delegation review rule is not reached.
- Livadas and Hawaiian Airlines preserve independent rights where disputed contract interpretation is unnecessary. Their preemption holdings do not decide every statutory-arbitration agreement.
- Landgraf's pre-enactment restriction does not impose a nominal-only remedy for 1993 conduct. Its distinct §102 compensation, punitive and jury conditions remain applicable. No earlier decision is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The actual-authority route must be available when concrete evidence supports material aid from delegated work power, without mandatory alter-ego status or apparent-authority reliance. This charge prejudicially excluded that route. Policy and response retain their proper, fact-dependent roles; the general complete-defense question remains open. This unsupported contractual-exhaustion assertion does not bar the independent statutory action, but no universal CBA/FAA or collective statutory-arbitration rule follows. Employer liability and relief remain for the proper further proceedings.
