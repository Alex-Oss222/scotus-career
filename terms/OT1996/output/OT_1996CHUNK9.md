### *Ayeni v. Mottola*, merits decision

1997-05-27

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Second Circuit's judgment denying dismissal of a Fourth Amendment damages action arising from media attendance and recording during a residential search. The allegations are not findings of liability. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

The case returns for further proceedings; no liability or damages is determined.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Ginsburg delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

The Fourth Amendment claim may proceed where a warrant-authorized search allegedly admitted and recorded an unrelated media audience that did not assist the search. Qualified immunity remains a distinct, date-specific inquiry into whether the constitutional limit was sufficiently clear when the conduct occurred. The warrant's execution purpose supplies the boundary; the Court does not create a general rule against all official documentation or assistance.

The Court applies the warrant-scope and particularized qualified-immunity principles supplied by the entering law. Later decisions are not used as notice for the 1992 conduct.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia's dissent would dismiss because the pleaded law did not give adequate conduct-date notice of the asserted boundary. Kennedy and Thomas joined that judgment and ground.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The judgment is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the judgment. The pleaded claim proceeds to ordinary merits and immunity development. No finding of liability, damages, or categorical media rule follows.

The supplied Second Circuit record establishes the pleading posture and challenged search. The public account does not treat allegations as findings.

**End of entry: *Ayeni v. Mottola*, merits decision, 1997-05-27.**

### *CompuServe, Inc. v. Patterson*, merits decision

1997-05-27

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Sixth Circuit's judgment concerning specific personal jurisdiction over a commercial dispute involving an electronic software-provider relationship. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia.

The action proceeds in the lower court; liability, venue, and general jurisdiction are not decided.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

On a prima facie motion record, a continuing and deliberate commercial relationship centered on an Ohio network, together with the relationship between those contacts and the dispute, supports specific jurisdiction. Physical travel is not an indispensable condition of purposeful electronic commercial affiliation. The showing is provisional and does not resolve disputed facts or the merits.

International Shoe supplies the requirement of purposeful affiliation, and Burger King supplies the connection between the forum contacts and the claim. The Court does not adopt a new Internet-specific test or a universal physical-presence rule.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia's dissent would require a more substantial forum connection on this record and would reinstate the Rule 12(b)(2) dismissal.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case is remanded for further proceedings. Electronic commercial contacts are assessed by their actual course and forum connection, and a prima facie ruling remains subject to later factual procedure.

The supplied Sixth Circuit opinion and neutral packet establish the contacts and provisional posture.

**End of entry: *CompuServe, Inc. v. Patterson*, merits decision, 1997-05-27.**

### *Hincapie-Nieto v. INS*, merits decision

1997-05-27

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Second Circuit's dismissal of a direct petition from a deportation order after the statutory review-channel change. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

The judgment does not adjudicate an unfiled habeas petition or order a new waiver hearing.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

The enacted withdrawal of ordinary direct review applies to the pending petition where it changes the review channel rather than the elements of the underlying conviction or completed agency consideration. The Court preserves a qualifying §2241 channel for substantial legal and constitutional challenges, but a claimant must invoke that channel and satisfy its actual predicates; the present record does not establish its failure.

Landgraf supplies the temporal inquiry, and Felker supplies provision-specific treatment of review restrictions. Neither creates automatic retroactivity or a guaranteed merits review of discretionary relief.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia's dissent would preserve the direct petition because applying the restriction to this pending filing would attach a new consequence to the earlier filing path. Kennedy and Thomas joined that judgment and ground.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The Court does not adjudicate an unfiled habeas petition or order a new waiver hearing. The present record does not establish failure of the qualifying §2241 channel's predicates.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The direct petition is dismissed; no separate habeas proceeding is entered. Direct review remains unavailable under §440(a), subject to the retained legal and constitutional review channel described above.

The supplied Second Circuit record establishes the filing history, concession of deportability, and limited detention concession.

**End of entry: *Hincapie-Nieto v. INS*, merits decision, 1997-05-27.**

### *Kinman v. Omaha Public School District*, merits decision

1997-05-27

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of an Eighth Circuit judgment affirming §1983 summary judgments, reversing on Title IX, and leaving the original state tort dismissal outside review. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part, and remanded.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title IX | Vacated and remanded, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Apply recipient-specific responsibility and response rules; decide no liability |
| Supervisory §1983 claims | Vacated and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Reassess actual notice, response, causation, and defenses individually |
| Municipal §1983 dismissal | Affirmed, 9–0 | All participating Justices | — | No municipal liability without a supported policy attribution |

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivered the Opinion of the Court for the mixed judgment, joined by the Justices supporting each component as stated. Justice Scalia dissented from the vacatur of the Title IX and supervisory components; Kennedy joined that dissent in full, and O'Connor joined only as to the supervisory component.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

Title IX recipient responsibility requires proof tied to the recipient's own awareness, authority, response, and causal role; the record cannot be resolved by automatic vicarious liability or by a same-sex categorical bar. Supervisory §1983 claims require individualized review of what each official knew, could do, did, and caused, together with the applicable date-specific defense. The municipal claim remains governed by Monell's policy-attribution requirement.

Franklin supplies the availability of a damages remedy without deciding the recipient-attribution question. Farmer and Doe supply the actual-awareness and response analogy used for this record. Monell and Pembaur remain distinct municipal-liability rules.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The remand does not establish liability, damages, or a general duty to discover misconduct. The original state tort claim remains outside the judgment.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

The dissent would leave the lower court's disposition intact, reasoning that the existing investigation and preexisting law did not support the ordered reconsideration.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for component-specific proceedings. The Title IX component requires recipient-specific responsibility and response analysis; the supervisory claims require individualized factual and defense analysis; and the municipal claim remains subject to Monell's policy-attribution requirement.

The supplied Eighth Circuit record establishes the disputed reports, investigations, and procedural posture; allegations remain unresolved.

**End of entry: *Kinman v. Omaha Public School District*, merits decision, 1997-05-27.**

### *Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.*, merits decision

1997-05-27

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Federal Circuit's judgment concerning construction of “solder reflow temperature.” Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The remand concerns construction and subsequent infringement analysis only.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

Claim construction begins with the intrinsic record, including the claim, specification, and prosecution history when part of the record. Technical dictionaries, scientific publications, and expert evidence may assist with terminology and genuine usage disputes, but may not contradict the intrinsic meaning. The judge resolves construction; the Court decides no infringement, validity, damages, or equivalents issue here.

Markman supplies judicial responsibility for claim construction and preserves the factfinder's role on infringement facts. This decision rejects an artificial rule that useful technical evidence must disappear whenever a judge sees no ambiguity.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia's dissent would give the intrinsic record controlling exclusivity and would not permit extrinsic material to alter a clear construction. Thomas joined the dissent.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The Court decides no infringement, validity, damages, or equivalents issue.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case is remanded for proceedings consistent with the construction ruling. The Federal Circuit's construction governs the remand, subject to the retained separation between construction and infringement.

The supplied Federal Circuit opinion establishes the construction dispute and the absence of a jury infringement verdict.

**End of entry: *Vitronics Corp. v. Conceptronic, Inc.*, merits decision, 1997-05-27.**

### *Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas*, merits decision

1997-06-02

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Eighth Circuit's judgment allowing a federal action against Arkansas taxes to proceed. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The federal tax action is barred; the Court does not decide the separate tax-immunity merits.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by all other participating Justices.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

A federally chartered but privately owned cooperative is not the United States for the Tax Injunction Act's exception merely because federal law created and regulates it. Where the State provides a plain, speedy, and efficient remedy, §1341 bars the federal injunction action. The statutory exception and any underlying tax immunity remain distinct questions.

The Court applies the Tax Injunction Act according to the plaintiffs' legal status and the adequacy of the state remedy, without equating federal regulation with sovereign identity.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The Court does not decide the separate tax-immunity merits.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for dismissal consistent with the judgment. The associations' federal tax-injunction action cannot proceed on the United States exception presented here.

The supplied Eighth Circuit record establishes the entities' private cooperative status and asserted federal-instrumentality theory.

**End of entry: *Arkansas v. Farm Credit Services of Central Arkansas*, merits decision, 1997-06-02.**

### *Boggs v. Boggs*, merits decision

1997-06-02

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Fifth Circuit's judgment concerning testamentary community-property claims to pension benefits. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas.

The judgment stands on the supplied claim; post-distribution assets and independent obligations are not decided.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Ginsburg. Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Scalia, Souter, and Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

ERISA does not preempt the testamentary transfer of the deceased spouse's community-property interest on this record where the claim does not direct plan administration, redefine a plan beneficiary, or transfer undistributed plan benefits contrary to the plan's governing allocation. The Court does not decide claims concerning post-distribution assets or independent obligations.

ERISA's survivor and anti-alienation provisions remain controlling within their terms; they do not automatically displace every state-law incident of a separate community-property interest.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens's dissent would treat the testamentary transfer as an impermissible attempt to alter ERISA's allocation of the pension benefits and would reverse. Scalia, Souter, and Thomas joined the dissent.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

Claims concerning post-distribution assets and independent obligations are not decided. Plan administration and prohibited alienation remain governed by ERISA.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for proceedings consistent with affirmance. The challenged testamentary claim may proceed within the limits stated.

The supplied Fifth Circuit record establishes the plan, testamentary claim, and requested ERISA-preemption ruling.

**End of entry: *Boggs v. Boggs*, merits decision, 1997-06-02.**

### *De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund*, merits decision

1997-06-02

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Second Circuit's judgment enjoining New York's assessment of plan-owned health facilities. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia.

The assessment may be applied subject to ordinary proceedings; no refund or liability calculation is fixed.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

A generally applicable tax on health-care facilities is not preempted under ERISA merely because an ERISA plan owns a taxed facility and bears the economic cost directly. The assessment does not govern plan benefits, eligibility, or administration. Direct economic impact is not itself a plan command.

Travelers distinguishes ordinary economic regulation from laws that govern plan administration, and Fort Halifax supplies the same boundary for regulation that does not require an ongoing plan. The Court resolves the previously reserved direct-ownership application without converting economic incidence into administrative preemption.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia's dissent would treat the direct depletion of plan assets as materially different from the indirect effects in Travelers.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case is remanded for further proceedings consistent with the preemption ruling. The challenged assessment is not preempted on the stated ground and the injunction falls; no refund or liability calculation is fixed.

The supplied Second Circuit record establishes the assessment, plan ownership, and procedural history after Travelers.

**End of entry: *De Buono v. NYSA-ILA Medical and Clinical Services Fund*, merits decision, 1997-06-02.**

### *McMillian v. Monroe County*, merits decision

1997-06-02

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Eleventh Circuit's dismissal of the county-policy theory under §1983. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

The remand concerns policymaker attribution and remaining Monell requirements; it does not establish liability.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice O'Connor dissented, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

Whether an official is a final policymaker for §1983 purposes is a function-specific question of state law. On the supplied Alabama allocation, the sheriff acts for the county when setting the challenged law-enforcement policy, so the county may not obtain dismissal solely on the contrary state-attribution theory. Underlying deprivation, causation, and other Monell requirements remain open.

Monell supplies the municipal-liability requirement, Pembaur supplies the role of a policymaker's decision, Praprotnik supplies the state-law and authorization inquiry, and Jett supplies the function-specific attribution framework. The Court does not treat an official state label as conclusive or decide functions beyond law enforcement.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor's dissent would affirm because Alabama law makes the sheriff a state policymaker for law enforcement and the county cannot be liable on that theory. Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas joined the dissent.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The decision does not resolve the merits of the alleged misconduct or individual-capacity claims, and it does not decide functions beyond law enforcement. Underlying deprivation, causation, and other Monell requirements remain open.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for further proceedings on the county claim. County attribution may proceed for the challenged function, but the remand does not resolve liability.

The supplied Eleventh Circuit record establishes the dismissal and the Alabama-law attribution dispute.

**End of entry: *McMillian v. Monroe County*, merits decision, 1997-06-02.**

### *Saratoga Fishing Co. v. J.M. Martinac & Co.*, merits decision

1997-06-02

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Ninth Circuit's application of the maritime economic-loss rule to a skiff, net, and other user-added equipment. Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The remand addresses the remaining tort elements and relief.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Kennedy delivered the Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Thomas.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

Under East River, equipment added by the initial user after the manufacturer's sale is other property rather than part of the original product when the defective vessel later destroys it. Transfer of the vessel and additions as a package to a later purchaser does not erase that distinction. Defect, causation, and damages remain undecided.

East River preserves the contract-tort boundary for damage to the product itself while allowing tort recovery for distinct other property. The Court applies that boundary to user-added equipment.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia's dissent would treat the vessel and transferred equipment as one integrated product and would affirm. Thomas joined the dissent.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

Defect, causation, and damages remain undecided.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case is remanded for further proceedings. The user-added equipment may support a maritime tort claim subject to proof of the remaining elements.

The supplied Ninth Circuit record establishes the integrated-vessel theory and the equipment-classification question.

**End of entry: *Saratoga Fishing Co. v. J.M. Martinac & Co.*, merits decision, 1997-06-02.**
