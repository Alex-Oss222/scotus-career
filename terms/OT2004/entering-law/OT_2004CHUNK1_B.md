#### Waters v. Churchill, 511 U.S. 661

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1450.

**Decided:** 1994-05-31.

##### A genuine, reasonable account of disciplinable speech supplies a limited safe harbor

**Authority of the judgment:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated and the case remanded, 7–2; defendants' summary judgment remains set aside. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg support vacatur. No complete controlling rationale exists under Marks; the express majority propositions below have only their stated scope.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** A public employer does not violate the employee's substantive speech right by disciplining her because of a genuinely held, objectively reasonable account of speech that the Pickering–Connick balance permits it to discipline, even if that account is later shown mistaken. The protection requires actual reliance on the permissible ground; it does not protect a reasonable investigation used to conceal retaliation for different protected speech.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, adopts this sufficient condition; Scalia's concurrence in the judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, expressly accepts it under their broader genuine-ground rule. Seven Justices agree at this precise level; only four adopt the full objective-inquiry framework.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563 (1968): applies the public-employee speech balance, adding this limited factual-error permission.
- Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138 (1983): applies contextual public-concern analysis and reasonable prospective assessment of institutional disruption; personal embarrassment alone is insufficient.
- Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): preserves private speech protection and the relevance of manner, time and place.
- Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): preserves actual causation; a potentially permissible ground is not proof of the ground actually used.

##### An unreasonable false account cannot defeat actually protected speech

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Discipline because of an unreasonable and incorrect report of speech that was actually protected violates the substantive speech right when the protected speech bears the required causal relation to the action, subject to distinct causation defenses and remedial immunity. This agreement does not establish liability for every investigative imperfection, for an unreasonable report of actually unprotected speech, or without proof of the underlying protected conduct and causal action.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor, Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg accept this proposition under objective factual care; Stevens's dissent, joined by Blackmun, expressly accepts it under their broader actual-speech rule. Six Justices agree on this proposition, but the two dissenters do not join the reasonable-error safe harbor or the judgment vacating the appellate rule.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pickering, Connick and Givhan: preserve the substantive protection of the actual public-concern speech within the employment balance.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): distinguishes a property-based hearing entitlement from this substantive speech claim.
- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): retains the distinct causation and same-decision requirements; unreasonableness alone is not an automatic recovery rule.

##### The motive dispute requires remand without restoring defendants' summary judgment

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** The Seventh Circuit's categorical actual-words rule is vacated, but defendants' summary judgment remains set aside because genuine reliance and retaliation for other protected criticism are materially disputed. On remand the employee must show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor; the employer may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred without it, and individual qualified immunity remains available and, if necessary, must be assessed as a distinct defense under the law relevant to the 1987 conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's four-Justice plurality and Scalia's three-Justice concurrence in the judgment support this common vacatur and limited remand. Blackmun and Stevens would affirm the appellate reversal under their different actual-speech rule; their votes are not counted as support for vacatur.

**Current force:** Controlling only at the precise scope of this express majority agreement; no complete rationale is added by combining the writings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mt. Healthy, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), and Givhan, 439 U.S. 410 (1979): apply the employee's causal showing and the employer's preponderance same-decision defense.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800 (1982): preserves individual qualified immunity as distinct from the substantive speech rule.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: requires considering relevant authority on the preserved immunity issue, without expanding the claim or supplying notice from later law.
- Rankin v. McPherson, June 24, 1987, and Anderson v. Creighton, June 25, 1987: may inform later doctrine but cannot themselves establish notice for the January and February 1987 actions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete factual-investigation theory commands five votes. The seven-Justice reasonable-and-genuine safe harbor and six-Justice unreasonable-false-actually-protected proposition are express agreements, not a unified Marks framework; the latter includes two judgment dissenters and is not selected as a judgment-supporting narrowest rationale. No universal freestanding investigation duty or rule for unreasonable investigation of actually unprotected speech is established. Personal immunity remains tied to law giving notice at the 1987 conduct, not the 1994 decision itself.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 7–2, without restoring defendants' summary judgment. Determine genuine reliance, actual motivation and the protection of causally relevant other speech under ordinary summary-judgment and Mt. Healthy rules. The completed investigation's conditional objective adequacy and the reported account's conditional permissibility are not reopened as wholly unresolved issues. Personal immunity is considered if necessary; no damages, reinstatement, municipal-liability finding or revived association claim is ordered.

#### United States v. National Treasury Employees Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1170.
**Decided:** February 22, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Scope of public-employee speech relief (The existing ban is unenforceable throughout the certified class; The injunction does not adjudicate the claims of unrepresented senior employees; Crane retains his individual relief without a grade-only remand).

##### A broad prospective honoraria ban requires justification for its actual speech burden

**Holding:** When the Government broadly restricts future compensated expression by its employees acting as citizens, Pickering requires a justification commensurate with the restriction's actual reach and burden on employees and listeners. Section 501(b)'s honoraria ban is unconstitutional as applied to the represented lower-level employees' protected expression unrelated to their employment because the record does not adequately connect that burden to actual or apparent misuse of office; narrower valid ethics restrictions remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pickering v. Board of Education, 391 U.S. 563, supplies the employment balance, applied to the breadth of a prospective restriction. Connick v. Myers, 461 U.S. 138, supplies public-concern principles without limiting protected subjects to politics or workplace criticism. Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994, retains its distinct particular-speech and reasonable-account rules. Simon & Schuster v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, supplies the relevance of financial burdens. Turner Broadcasting System v. FCC, June 27, 1994, preserves substantial but reviewable legislative predictive judgments; it does not decide this employment case.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 501(b) prohibits receipt of an honorarium by a Member, officer or employee; §505(2) excludes special Government employees as defined in 18 U.S.C. §202. Section 505(3) covers money or anything of value for an appearance, speech or article; a series falls within the definition if its subject directly relates to official duties or payment is because of governmental status. That parenthesis does not already restrict every single honorarium. Actual and necessary travel expenses for the individual and one relative are excluded to the extent another person pays or reimburses them, and otherwise deducted to the extent they are not paid or reimbursed. Travel expenses mean transportation, lodging and meals away from residence or principal employment.

Section 501(c) treats a payment on the individual's behalf to a §170(c) charitable organization as not received, subject to a $2,000 ceiling on each payment and no financial benefit to the individual, parent, sibling, spouse, child or dependent relative. Section 504(a) permits an Attorney General civil action and a civil penalty up to the greater of $10,000 or compensation received. Under §504(b), a written opinion from an entity identified by §503 protects its recipient and another covered person whose facts are materially indistinguishable from subsection (a)'s sanction only for conduct after issuance, in good faith and in accordance with the opinion's provisions and findings. These are qualified provisions, not general exemptions. The separate §501(a) high-level outside-income ceiling is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. Nonenforcement of the existing ban throughout the certified below-GS–16 class is preserved by five votes; Crane retains individual relief by six; relief for unrepresented senior employees is removed. Congressional and judicial coverage, §501(a)'s outside-income ceiling and valid independent ethics restrictions are not invalidated. No penalty or compensation award is entered.

#### Saucier v. Katz

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1977; judgment below, 194 F.3d 962.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### The binding remand instruction and its limited authority

**Authority of the judgment:** Souter announces a 5–4 vacatur and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the exact instruction to apply existing objective qualified immunity separately from the force merits on proper interlocutory assumptions, leaving both grant and denial of immunity available. No other Justice joins Souter's full reasoning. His distinct objective-defense rationale, Stone-Zsela's authoritative-reliance replacement and the three-Justice concurrence's force-specific objection are not logical subsets. No controlling Marks rationale establishes a new general immunity rule or mandatory merits-first sequence. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would direct immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harlow, Anderson and Graham retain their existing operation. Agreement between Souter and dissenters creates no new controlling sequence rule. The preserved legal inquiry uses September 24, 1994 law and plaintiff-favorable interlocutory assumptions, including disputed participation; the Parker-only account is not an appellate finding. There is no ultimate force, violation, participation, immunity, causation or injury determination. First Amendment, false-arrest, supervisory and prospective-relief branches are not reopened; the federal-officer damages channel is assumed without a new cause of action or institutional remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must perform the specified existing-law inquiry. Separable legal issues may be decided on the proper assumptions; genuinely material factual disputes remain for the appropriate forum. The mandate requires no actual-authoritative-reliance proof, additional reliance submissions, automatic trial, hearing, liability finding or damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Graham v. Connor:** Its complete, objective, on-scene force inquiry remains the merits rule, including the circumstances and reasonable factual perceptions confronting the officer. No physical-injury prerequisite, least-dangerous-tactic command or categorical security exception is added.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Its existing objective personal-immunity defense remains in force; a separate proposal to replace it is not the Court's law.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Its particularized legal-notice inquiry remains available under the remand; neither an identical-case requirement nor proof of actual authoritative reliance replaces it.
- **Hunter v. Bryant:** Its judicial legal inquiry under proper summary-judgment assumptions governs the return; the court may not choose a more favorable competing factual account for the officer.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its consideration of all relevant preconduct law and ordinary preservation limits remain; later decisions cannot supply September 24, 1994 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its exclusion of evidentiary-sufficiency disputes from immediate immunity review remains applicable to disputed participation.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its distinction between a separable assumed-fact legal defense and a factual-sufficiency appeal remains; the present judgment creates no additional appellate jurisdiction.
- **Sterling v. Borough of Minersville:** Its unanimous participation-review rule applies to the supported two-officer account. Its different confidentiality and April 1997 notice holdings do not answer this force claim or supply earlier notice.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Its contextual force analysis remains; its supported immediate armed attack and absence of a contrary material account do not establish the facts here. Its later decision supplies no September 1994 notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its distinction between constitutional violation and conduct-date personal immunity remains, without requiring a factual twin; its 1999 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap:** Its retention of the existing objective defense remains; its distinct disclosure claims do not settle this force application, and its 2001 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Conn v. Gabbert:** Its reservation of mandatory immunity sequencing is not displaced by a controlling sequence holding here.
- **Marks v. United States:** No common narrower rationale is identified among the materially different judgment-supporting writings; it supplies no basis to combine them into a new rule.

#### Konop v. Hawaiian Airlines, Inc., 302 F.3d 868

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 99-55106.
**Decided:** January 27, 2003.

##### Whether later retrieval of the stored website pages constitutes interception

**Holding:** For the later retrieval of stored website pages shown here, an interception requires acquisition contemporaneous with transmission, rather than subsequent access to stored content. Konop bears the burden to establish an interception, and the undisputed retrieval sequence does not satisfy that element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by all eight Associate Justices; nine complete joins support both holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1995–1996 conduct-date ECPA controls. The decision concerns subsequent retrieval of stored pages; it leaves transient buffers and materially different transmission-storage arrangements undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Wiretap Act summary judgment for Hawaiian affirmed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union concerns constitutional protection for Internet expression and is distinguished from the ECPA's acquisition and consent definitions.

##### Whether an eligible nonuser can authorize access under §2701(c)(2)

**Holding:** Section 2701(c)(2) requires authorization by a person who actually uses the service, is duly authorized by its provider to do so, and gives consent concerning a communication of or intended for that user. Hawaiian cannot obtain summary judgment on this exception without establishing those predicates free of a genuine material dispute; unused eligibility does not establish user status.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls.

**Material application:** Wong had not used the website and could not supply actual-user status. Gardner’s use and its timing require access-specific findings. Electronic storage, the relevant service and otherwise unauthorized access were assumed for the exception question, not adjudicated as completed claim elements.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The claimant must still prove the remaining statutory violation by a preponderance. Contractual website restrictions do not replace the statutory consent inquiry, and failure of the consent defense does not itself establish liability or a remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the appellate reversal of SCA summary judgment and remand for each access, purported user, use chronology, communication-specific consent, remaining elements, defenses and authorized remedy. The separate labor and unappealed state dispositions are preserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank, N.A., 530 U.S. 1, supplies the source-specific approach that gives enacted actor and remedy conditions independent effect; its bankruptcy holding is unchanged.

#### Kansas v. Colorado

**Docket or dockets:** No. 105, Original.
**Decided:** May 15, 1995.

##### Pumping liability is established without choosing a general proof quantum

**Holding:** Colorado’s post-Compact well development, including increased use of existing wells, materially depleted water protected by Article IV-D in usable quantity or availability. The substantial pumping violation is established even by clear and convincing evidence, while the separate WWSP claim fails even by a preponderance; the Court therefore overrules Colorado’s proof-standard exception without choosing the general quantum governing disputed Compact-enforcement facts or fixing a compensable quantity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: preserves the distinction between enforcement of an established decree right and modification requiring clear and convincing real and substantial injury; it does not replace this Compact’s material-depletion element or settle its general factual quantum.
- Colorado v. New Mexico, 467 U.S. 310 (1984): distinguishes the proposed new-diversion setting while applying its explanation that clear and convincing proof requires high probability rather than certainty; the general Compact standard remains open.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article III-B and IV-A concern waters originating in the natural Arkansas drainage basin above the Stateline, including tributaries, and exclude imported waters; Article IV-B preserves New Mexico rights in Trinchera Creek and other Purgatoire tributaries there. Actual imported return flows may nevertheless reduce the measured loss and remain in causal accounting.

##### The protected pumping baseline is actual use, not hypothetical capacity

**Holding:** Article IV-D’s protection against material depletion extends to the improved or prolonged functioning of existing works, and Article VI-A(2) preserves Colorado’s administration only subject to the Compact’s other provisions. The supported 15,000-acre-foot historical actual-use benchmark governs this liability accounting rather than later decreed quantities or hypothetical maximum capacity; it is not an unconditional annual pumping cap or a final damages quantity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies the duty to enforce the congressionally approved Compact as written; neither state decrees nor preferred allocation policy replaces its express conditions.

##### The laches exception fails for lack of established inexcusable delay

**Holding:** Assuming laches could apply, Colorado has not established Kansas’s inexcusable lack of diligence and therefore cannot obtain the requested bar to monetary liability for periods before March 1985. The Court leaves laches’s categorical availability in Compact enforcement open, makes no independently sufficient prejudice holding, and reserves the eventual form of relief and properly supported remedy-stage equities.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Current-force modification:** Kansas v. Colorado, June 11, 2001, resolves the reserved compensation questions without disturbing the rejection of an established laches bar or finding culpable Kansas delay. Its five-Justice Part V-A permits the Compact-specific denial of additional lost-investment interest for the earliest losses while preserving appropriate inflation correction. The component judgments require the Master's 1969 commencement, never before the underlying compensable loss; no majority rationale selects that exact cutoff. Settled liability and the Compact allocation remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Kentucky, 500 U.S. 380 (1991): distinguishes its concrete prescription and acquiescence setting; its general laches observation does not establish an absolute Compact-enforcement exemption.
- Nebraska v. Wyoming, April 20, 1993: preserves its concrete acquiescence determination without treating knowledge of wells or passage of time as equivalent conduct here.
- Texas v. New Mexico, 482 U.S. 124 (1987): retains enforcement of past Compact obligations despite good-faith uncertainty, while leaving appropriate remedial considerations for the remedy proceeding.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kansas v. Colorado, June 11, 2001 — compensation and remedy-stage equities, including the distinct interest rules and component accrual judgments.

##### The separate reservoir bargain supplies no independent pumping credit

**Holding:** The 1980 John Martin Operating Plan does not create the asserted independent legal credit against well-pumping liability for benefits Kansas obtained through a separately bargained exchange of release and timing rights. Actual Plan operation and actual imported return flows that reduce the measured loss must remain in the physical accounting; the Court rejects no credit actually created by agreement and awards no final numerical offset.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 482 U.S. 124 (1987): applies enforcement of the actual Compact obligation with remedial flexibility; that flexibility does not create an unbargained credit or establish a present numerical offset.

##### The pleaded Trinidad claim requires the Compact’s material depletion

**Holding:** The 1967 Trinidad Operating Principles do not establish the asserted independent Compact command making every departure a violation without the material depletion required by Article IV-D. Kansas has not established that depletion by comparing actual operation with conditions without the project, so its pleaded Compact claim is dismissed; independent agreement, third-party-beneficiary and other authorized enforcement theories remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies respect for the Compact’s actual institutional authority while forbidding judicial alteration of its allocation; it does not foreclose every valid implementation rule or separate undertaking.

##### The WWSP depletion claim fails on the particular proof

**Holding:** Kansas has not proved even by a preponderance that the reviewed Winter Water Storage Program materially depleted protected usable water, and its depletion exception is overruled. The conclusion rests on the comparison’s operational, nonparallel-error and timing defects, not a finding of no possible depletion, a demand for perfect modeling, or a rule that calibration error larger than the modeled effect always defeats proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., June 28, 1993: preserves reliability and task-specific fit while keeping preliminary admissibility distinct from the persuasive sufficiency of a particular estimate; no mandatory checklist or perfect-model requirement is imposed.

##### The adopted usable-water method fits this liability record

**Holding:** Article IV-D protects usable quantity and availability, including supported timing and recharge, rather than gross annual flow alone. For this liability record the Court accepts Durbin’s approach with Larson’s modifications—72% summer, 25% winter, a 9.9% pre-Compact recharge component, and monthly/seasonal ceilings of 40,000/140,000 acre-feet in summer and 7,500/40,000 in winter—and rejects Kansas’s exception without prescribing a permanent formula or foreclosing reliable daily analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., June 28, 1993: applies the importance of fit to the inference sought, without turning its admission rule into a permanent water-accounting formula or choosing the ultimate Compact proof quantum.

##### The separate WWSP prior-approval ruling is confirmed on its narrow ground

**Holding:** The 1951 Administration policy resolution, the Compact and the cited 1944 and 1962 federal authorizations do not establish the asserted general requirement of prior Administration approval for WWSP. The partial summary judgment is confirmed on that legal ground, while Article IV-D’s material-depletion restriction, Article IV-C’s actual operational qualifications and Article IX’s federal reservation and jointly approved John Martin exceptions remain effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): preserves actual Compact machinery and judicial enforcement despite administrative impasse, without creating either an unexpressed approval veto or a universal exception to administrative procedures.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article IV-C permits authorized Corps flood-control releases at necessary or advisable times and rates regardless of either State's ditch capacities or requirements, conservation-pool releases or suspension of storage needed for maintenance, and temporary use of conservation capacity for flood operations. These are particular operational qualifications, not general reservoir immunity. Article IX-A and consent Act §2 reserve federal sovereignty, rights and jurisdiction; the Chief of Engineers may operate John Martin's conservation features consistently with the Compact, subject to exceptions jointly approved by the Chief and Administration. Article VIII-B procedures involving John Martin operation require District Engineer approval; VIII-D gives each State one vote, requires unanimity and permits arbitration of a divided vote only by subsequent unanimous referral. None establishes the asserted general WWSP approval veto.

##### Lake McKinney is dismissed for failure to prove unauthorized retention

**Holding:** Colorado’s Lake McKinney counterclaim is dismissed because it has not adequately traced protected John Martin releases to unauthorized retention rather than the short retention needed to move water through the pool. Article V-E(2) still requires prompt beneficial use unless the Administration authorizes downstream storage; this record-specific failure of proof supplies no general permission for storage and no universal counterclaim proof rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies enforcement of the enacted obligation with its express qualification; the Court neither deletes authorized downstream storage nor adds a categorical storage permission.

##### The Kansas-well counterclaim fails for want of proved Colorado injury

**Holding:** Colorado’s Kansas-well counterclaim is dismissed because the asserted additional calls, transit or account effects, and lost surplus have not been shown to injure Colorado’s protected supply. The dismissal does not immunize Kansas development, equate all Kansas groundwater with Compact-covered Colorado-origin water, or remove Kansas pumping from the distinct calculation of water Kansas could actually have used.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O’Connor), unanimous on each proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Texas v. New Mexico, 462 U.S. 554 (1983): applies the actual Compact obligation and protected entitlement as the basis for adjudication; failure to prove this claimed injury creates no general immunity for downstream development.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All seven exceptions are overruled, pumping liability and the three unexcepted recommendations are adopted at their stated scope, and the matter is recommitted to the Special Master for remedy evidence and recommendations. Liability and the accepted accounting govern that work; rejected claims receive no automatic new trial. No final quantity, damages, interest, valuation, offset, engineering regime or final decree is entered.

#### Kansas v. Colorado, 533 U.S. 1

**Docket or dockets:** No. 105, Original.
**Decided:** June 11, 2001.

For Kansas's ownership of the claim and the Eleventh Amendment holding in Kansas v. Colorado, June 11, 2001, No. 105, Original, see Federal Courts — Sovereign ownership of interstate compact compensation.

##### Additional compensation for delayed payment is legally available

**Holding:** Unliquidated compact damages are not categorically ineligible for additional prejudgment interest compensating lost investment opportunities. Such compensation is available here beyond the appropriate inflation correction Colorado concedes, subject to the period fixed by the component judgment and a supported calculation that neither duplicates inflation nor awards speculative investment returns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas would deny this additional component on their separate Compact-contract ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Availability of the additional lost-investment component does not establish automatic accrual from every injury, a fixed award or an assumed investment return. Historical-dollar principal and the conceded inflation correction remain distinct from additional interest.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co. — its compensatory reasoning is extended by analogy to this compact remedy; the maritime holding does not already decide interstate accrual.
- Texas v. New Mexico, 482 U.S. 124 — applied to suitable compensation despite good-faith uncertainty; its postjudgment-interest treatment does not resolve this prejudgment component.
- West Virginia v. United States, 479 U.S. 305 — applied to compensation with attention to the governments' actual interests.
- Funkhouser v. J. B. Preston Co. — establishes that unliquidated damages could carry interest before the Compact; it does not make every period or return recoverable.

##### The rate must measure the loss, without duplicate compensation

**Holding:** Kansas's ownership of the recovery does not require replacing the Master's supported year-specific individual-loss rates with the State's lower borrowing rates. The individual-loss method may value the economic injury for periods in which additional interest is allowed, but the calculation must avoid recovering the same inflation twice and must measure supported lost use rather than speculative returns or punitive leverage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas do not reach an additional-interest rate because they would deny that component.

**Material application:** The supported year-specific individual-loss approach values agricultural injury. The State's lower cost of borrowing does not measure that distinct deprivation. Ownership of the award does not dictate the rate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular annual schedule, compounding interval, inflation-subtraction formula or assumed farmer investment is adopted. Historical-dollar loss, inflation correction and additional lost use must remain distinguishable enough to avoid double recovery.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Texas v. New Mexico preserves the distinction between the sovereign's ownership of recovery and evidence measuring the injury; it does not validate any unsupported return.

##### The crop-loss model has affirmative support for the specified exceptions

**Holding:** The specified objections do not defeat the Master's crop-loss calculation where the accepted shortage connection, acreage and price evidence, published crop-specific water/yield studies and 25-percent adjustment for ordinary farming stresses affirmatively support the estimated loss. Kansas retains the burden of proving compensable injury; Colorado's abandonment of a competing model neither supplies that proof nor shifts the burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stevens's opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices, who directly overrule the specified crop-model exceptions.

**Material application:** Colorado challenged the marginal water/yield estimates through the lack of supplemental drilling and comparisons involving new capital expenditures, arguing that the predicted gains implied farmers would have drilled profitable wells. The coefficients rest on published studies, accepted shortage attribution, acreage and prices, with a 25-percent stress adjustment. Capital and credit constraints, debt aversion and limited cost information explain why noninvestment does not itself refute seasonal-water value. Studies requiring new investment may address a different transaction from using additional water without that expenditure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Kansas retains the burden of proof. The ruling rejects the specified objections, not a perfect-model requirement, all future objections or a permanent accounting formula. No final compensable quantity follows from the earlier liability finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Kansas v. Colorado, May 15, 1995, is preserved: its pumping-liability and historical-use determinations did not settle damages. The present evidentiary ruling neither changes the liability proof standard nor fixes a new allocation.

##### The Compact-specific grounds do not require accrual from the earliest losses

**Holding:** Kansas is not entitled to additional lost-investment interest accruing before the Master's 1969 cutoff merely because compensable Compact losses began in 1950. In this remedial setting, the 1949 legal expectations, lack of awareness of a violation during the early years, extraordinary elapsed period and cumulative compounding consequences justify denying that earliest accrual without a finding of culpable Kansas delay; the conceded appropriate inflation correction remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes for these reasons. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas additionally support the denial judgment on their different ground against all additional return. Stone-Zsela dissents from this limitation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earliest-accrual denial concerns additional lost-investment interest, not the conceded appropriate inflation correction. It rests on the collective Compact-specific circumstances rather than unliquidated status or absence of formal demand alone. The five Justices adopting these reasons differ on the later commencement date.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kansas v. Colorado, May 15, 1995 — its rejection of an established inexcusable-delay basis for laches remains intact; the reserved remedy-stage equities are applied without finding culpable Kansas delay.
- City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co. — distinguished on commencement because its maritime setting does not determine the remedial expectations of this old compact.
- West Virginia v. United States — its attention to actual governmental interests is applied, while its ordinary reimbursement setting is distinguished.
- Texas v. New Mexico — preserves past-breach liability without preordaining each prejudgment-interest period.

##### The component judgment requires the Master's 1969 commencement

**Authority of the judgment:** The component judgments require additional interest from the Master's 1969 cutoff, never before the compensable loss. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer allow accrual from that cutoff until December 16, 1985; Kennedy joins them for accrual thereafter. Eight Justices deny pre-1969 accrual, with Stone-Zsela dissenting. Stevens's Part V-B choosing 1969 has only Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's joins. Stone-Zsela's loss-date rationale does not adopt the knowledge cutoff; Kennedy would use the initiating-request date, and O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas reject all additional interest. These rationales are not logical subsets merely because their dates nest, so no controlling Marks rationale chooses the exact cutoff.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The binding component result creates no universal notice prerequisite. #### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

## Standards and Tests selection: Standing to challenge racial composition of an indicting grand jury

### Standing to challenge racial composition of an indicting grand jury

**Governing question:** May a defendant of a different race challenge purposeful exclusion in selection of a voting grand-jury member?

**Current rule:** A defendant may assert excluded jurors’ equal-protection rights when discriminatory composition injures the charging process, the defendant has the required relationship to the excluded citizens, and substantial practical obstacles hinder their own enforcement. The defendant also has personal standing to litigate due process when alleged discrimination affected the composition of the grand jury exercising charging authority over him; different race does not eliminate that injury. Selection of a voting member differs from designation of a ministerial officer from an already constituted grand jury.

**Authority by component:**

- Third-party equal-protection route and independently sufficient personal due-process standing: Campbell v. Louisiana, No. 96-1584 — Holdings I–II, 1998-04-21.

**Present operation effective:** Campbell v. Louisiana, 1998-04-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** Standing proves neither discrimination nor the substantive extent of due-process protection. Applicable proof, preservation and remedial requirements remain; no proportional-representation entitlement, automatic quashing of an indictment or new trial follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Paternal-recognition deadlines in citizenship transmission

### Paternal-recognition deadlines in citizenship transmission

**Governing question:** Whether the male-only recognition deadline may bar citizenship transmission where parentage is independently established.

**Current rule:** A sex-specific citizenship-transmission condition requires a genuine important objective and a substantial relationship to that objective. The additional paternal-recognition deadline in section 1409(a)(4), and the corresponding age limit in the available former-law route, may not bar transmission where reliable parentage is independently established and the prescribed timely formalities do not substantially secure the asserted childhood opportunity. Sever the discriminatory age barrier while enforcing valid remaining transmission conditions; this applies the corrected statutory grant rather than supplying equitable naturalization or citizenship from biology alone.

**Authority by component:**

- Demanding intermediate scrutiny, invalid age barrier and severance: Canonical Decision Record `Tuan_Anh_Nguyen_v_INS_merits_2001-06-11.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The additional male-only recognition deadline fails demanding intermediate scrutiny; II. Sever the discriminatory deadline and apply the remaining citizenship statute, 2001-06-11; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join both holdings.

**Present operation effective:** Tuan Anh Nguyen v. INS, 2001-06-11.

**Controlling construction:** An authorized election of former section 1409(a) retains legitimation and applicable transmission requirements but not the invalid before-twenty-one limit or the amended written-support promise. For a birth governed by the former ten-year requirement, the citizen parent’s prebirth United States or outlying-possession presence must total ten years, five after age fourteen. Statutory credits include honorable United States military service, United States Government or section 288 international-organization employment, and presence abroad as a dependent unmarried child in the household of a person doing that service or employment. The later five-year/two-after-fourteen requirement applies to births beginning November 14, 1986; the maternal one-year rule is not substituted.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding neither requires DNA as the sole proof method nor invalidates separate support or presence classifications. Reliable parentage does not establish every remaining statutory condition. When those conditions establish citizenship at birth, recognition defeats removal as an alien; an independent valid condition’s failure retains its ordinary consequence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Mitigation investigation and prejudice in capital sentencing

### Mitigation investigation and prejudice in capital sentencing

**Governing question:** Counsel’s investigative obligation and the prejudice inquiry for omitted lawful capital mitigation.

**Current rule:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make an informed, reasonable decision that further inquiry is unnecessary. A later claim of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to pursue identified records; competent investigation does not require presenting every item it uncovers. Prejudice asks whether the complete supported aggravating and mitigating record creates a reasonable probability of a different lawful result sufficient to undermine confidence, rather than a more-likely-than-not outcome. Ordinary lawful-mitigation claims require no additional fundamental-unfairness showing under Fretwell. Where death requires a unanimous Virginia penalty jury and disagreement requires life, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death is enough; a merely possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy is not.

**Authority by component:**

- Reasonable-probability prejudice and Virginia’s lawful outcome: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia’s unanimity rule, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by all seven other Associates.
- Informed investigation requirement: Terry Williams — IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** Terry Williams, April 18, 2000.

**Related current rules:** Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice states Fretwell’s separate unavailable-benefit limit. State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254 supplies the independent federal habeas restrictions; a constitutional error finding does not dispense with them.
