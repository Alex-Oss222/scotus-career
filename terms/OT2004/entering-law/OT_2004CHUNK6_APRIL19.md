## Standards and Tests selection: Culpable affirmative assistance to a securities fraud

### Culpable affirmative assistance to a securities fraud

**Governing question:** When the received private §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 action reaches a person assisting another’s fraud.

**Current rule:** A person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct can be privately liable. The defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the activity’s fraudulent character and its conduct’s assisting role. Severe recklessness is an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it. An independent disclosure duty is unnecessary for affirmative assistance. The plaintiff must prove the underlying manipulation or deception, qualifying assistance and culpability, legally sufficient reliance on the underlying fraud, causation and recoverable loss by the ordinary civil preponderance standard. Investors need not have known of or relied on concealed assistance itself; no reliance presumption follows merely from this rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Received action, mental state, underlying-fraud reliance and proof: Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A — The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance; Knowledge or severe recklessness in affirmative substantial assistance; Triable issues require further proceedings, not a liability award, 1994-04-19. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the existence of the confined action and its received-remedy rationale. No fractured-decision synthesis is necessary. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; all five adopt this confined mental-state and affirmative-assistance rule, not a universal recklessness rule for every securities claim. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the record application, preserved proof requirements and limited affirmance and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A, 1994-04-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Negligence, valuation concerns alone, professional status, ordinary services without qualifying culpability and assisting effect, and mere duty-free silence are insufficient. Relabeling inaction does not make it affirmative. No primary misrepresentation by the assistant, general investigation duty, liability from default, universal securities scienter rule or comprehensive SEC-enforcement rule follows.

**Open question:** The full rules for duty-based omissions and consciously intended assistance through duty-free inaction remain open. Contribution separately requires established shared liability under Musick, Peeler.

**Holdings navigation:** The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance; Knowledge or severe recklessness in affirmative substantial assistance; Triable issues require further proceedings, not a liability award.

## Standards and Tests selection: Ordinary pleading and substantive proof requirements

### Ordinary pleading and substantive proof requirements

**Governing question:** Whether substantive liability or evidentiary frameworks justify heightened pleading beyond the Federal Rules in municipal civil-rights and employment-discrimination actions.

**Current rule:** Courts may not impose a judge-created heightened factual-particularity requirement on municipal §1983 complaints beyond the then-operative Federal Rules. Rule 8 governs; Rule 9(b)'s fraud-or-mistake categories do not add municipal liability. Monell and Canton retain their substantive attribution and causation requirements. Rule 11, discovery control and Rule 56 remain distinct. No individual-officer pleading standard is decided.

A Title VII or ADEA plaintiff need not plead each element of the McDonnell Douglas prima facie evidentiary framework. Rule 8 requires a short, plain statement giving fair notice of the challenged employment decision, alleged prohibited basis and grounds of the claim. The proof framework belongs to evidence, not a mandatory complaint checklist. This does not remove substantive elements or the distinct operation of Rule 11, discovery management and summary judgment.

**Authority by component:**

- The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims: Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit — The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims, 1993-03-03. Stone's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous controlling authority.

- Canonical Decision Record `swierkiewicz-v-sorema-na_merits_2002-02-26.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The prima facie proof framework is not a pleading checklist, 2002-02-26; Scalia's unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Swierkiewicz, February 26, 2002, for the employment-discrimination evidentiary-framework distinction; Leatherman's municipal pleading rule remains effective March 3, 1993.

**Holdings navigation:** Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Selecting plant-variety regimes and amended-protection notices

### Selecting plant-variety regimes and amended-protection notices

**Governing question:** The applicable plant-variety regime, its transition and its relation to independently valid utility-patent protection.

**Current rule:** Pub. L. 103-349 and its amendments become effective April 4, 1995, 180 days after enactment. Section 14(a), except as §14 otherwise provides, preserves former law for varieties with certificates issued before that date and applications pending then. Section 14(b) permits refiling on or after that date under amended eligibility and protection terms while retaining the original filing date for §42. Under §14(c), an owner’s §127 notice claiming amended protection must identify that protection, and false or misleading protection claims or labels incur §128 sanctions. Section 10 deletes the former farmer-to-farmer sale proviso for the amended regime without retroactively rewriting Asgrow.

Plant-specific protection does not categorically exclude a qualifying plant invention from utility-patent coverage under §101. Each right requires its own statutory predicates, and certificate-specific saved-seed or research exceptions do not become defenses to a separate valid utility patent. The certificate transition and Asgrow's former-law operation remain unchanged; category eligibility establishes neither the validity of individual claims nor enforceability of particular restrictions.

**Authority by component:**

- Pub. L. 103-349 §§10, 14(a)–(c) and 15: farmer-sales deletion, former-law preservation, optional refiling, notice and effectiveness: Admitted Source Record, 1995-04-04; a nonjudicial source, not a Court holding.

- Canonical Decision Record `j-e-m-ag-supply-inc-v-pioneer-hi-bred-international-inc_merits_2001-12-10.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The plant statutes permit concurrent utility-patent protection; Certificate protections retain their own statutory version and exceptions, 2001-12-10; the Court opinion with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, seven.

**Present operation effective:** J. E. M. Ag Supply, December 10, 2001, for concurrent statutory protection; the certificate amendment and transition remain effective April 4, 1995.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 9’s contract-producer clause independently applies to both former- and amended-law protected varieties of lawn, turf or forage grass, alfalfa or clover, not soybeans. It requires fulfilled contractual terms, the owner’s refusal to take delivery or pay within thirty days of the contract payment date, and notice after that period of intended sale, with paragraph (2)(C)’s further thirty-day payment-and-delivery language preserved in the admitted enacted text. “Owner” includes a licensee and other federal or state rights and remedies remain.

**Open question:** The enrolled §9(b)(2)(C) phrase “unless the owner fails to pay the amounts due under the contract and take delivery of the seed within 30 days of such notification” is awkward and has no controlling judicial construction. This Register preserves it rather than reversing its words or finding any producer entitled to sell.

**Holdings navigation:** Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer — applicable statutory regime.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equipment competition and single-brand aftermarket power

### Equipment competition and single-brand aftermarket power

**Governing question:** Whether equipment-market competition or brand identity categorically defeats Sherman Act claims concerning parts and service aftermarkets.

**Current rule:** Equipment competition is relevant but does not as a matter of law foreclose power in parts or service markets. Summary judgment cannot rest solely on an economic prediction contradicted by evidence supporting a rational inference of power, including material information or switching costs and actual price or exclusion effects; unsupported speculation remains insufficient. One brand's parts or service may constitute a relevant market when actual substitutes and competitive restraints support that definition. Brand identity alone neither establishes nor defeats the market. Sufficient separate consumer demand may distinguish functionally related parts and service; the ordinary tying and monopolization elements and Rule 56 burdens remain independently necessary.

In a supported monopoly-maintenance challenge involving protected parts or service information, patent or copyright exclusion has substantive justificatory force but supplies no conclusive defense. The plaintiff must establish relevant-market power, unlawful acquisition or maintenance, competitive injury and causation beyond the ordinary consequences of lawful exclusivity. Procurement fraud, sham litigation or illegal tying is not an additional prerequisite to the patent claim; unlawful acquisition or extension beyond the copyright grant is not an exhaustive gate for the copyright claim. A separate service-market label, refusal, hostility toward rivals, subjective pretext or injury to one rival alone is insufficient. Lawful exclusion, innovation returns and supported quality, inventory, support and efficiency explanations remain material.

Any remedy must address the proved violation and causal competitive injury while preserving lawful protected returns insofar as compatible with curing the violation. Neither a duty to supply nor an access entitlement follows merely from rejecting categorical immunity. Competent evidence may take the form of documents, testimony and actual conduct; no universal econometric-study requirement is imposed.

For regulated input access, breach of a regulatory obligation neither establishes nor immunizes a Sherman Act §2 offense. The plaintiff must separately prove relevant-market monopoly power, unlawful acquisition or maintenance through an exclusionary competitive mechanism, causally connected antitrust injury and the ordinary elements. Deliberate discriminatory degradation of rival-customer access may qualify if it maintains monopoly power without adequate business justification. A prior voluntary course of dealing is probative rather than an exclusive gateway; statutory access duties, rival injury and exclusionary purpose alone do not substitute for the offense. Supported technical, investment, efficiency and operational explanations remain material. The saving clause preserves existing antitrust duties rather than enlarging them.

**Authority by component:**
- Separate-demand background: Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc., “1. Distinct parts and service demand and conditional sales present a triable tying claim”, June 8, 1992, applying Jefferson Parish.
- No categorical equipment-competition defense: Eastman Kodak, “2. Equipment-market competition is relevant but not a categorical defense,” June 8, 1992.
- Single-brand market possibility: Eastman Kodak, “3. A single brand may define a relevant market when actual substitutes do not constrain it,” June 8, 1992.
- Each proposition: Blackmun, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter (six).

- Patent and copyright defenses, retained monopolization proof, statutory justifications and remedial bounds: Canonical Decision Record `CSU_v_Xerox_merits_2001-06-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Patent exclusion and monopoly maintenance; Copyright justification and the required remand, 2001-06-04; Stevens with Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six direct joins to both holdings.

- Independent exclusion and regulated-input justifications: Verizon Communications v. Trinko, January 13, 2004, Part I; Stevens with Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer [Canonical Decision Record](../terms/OT2003/records/verizon_communications_v_trinko_merits_2004-01-13.md#holdings).

**Present operation effective:** Trinko, January 13, 2004, for independent monopoly maintenance in regulated input access; CSU, June 4, 2001, for patent and copyright defenses; Kodak’s market and proof rules remain effective June 8, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** No presumption of monopoly arises from a patent, proprietary design, brand identity, numerical share alone or imperfect information. Power, exclusionary acquisition or maintenance, antitrust injury and damages remain separate. No general duty to supply competitors is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc., 504 U.S. 451 (1992) — Tying and aftermarket monopolization. [In re Independent Service Organizations Antitrust Litigation: CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation — Patent and copyright exclusion in service-market monopolization](HOLDINGS.md#in-re-independent-service-organizations-antitrust-litigation-csu-llc-v-xerox-corporation).

**Open question:** CSU does not adjudicate patent validity, infringement, copyright ownership, a final misuse claim or particular compulsory-access terms. Professional Real Estate Investors retains its separate sham-petitioning prerequisites; they are not transplanted as commercial-refusal immunity.

#### Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-854.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance

**Holding:** The established private action under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 permits recovery from a person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct, subject to proof of that fraud, the defendant’s qualifying participation, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable loss. This is accessory responsibility within the received investor action, not a finding that the assistant made a primary misrepresentation or a rule imposing liability through professional status or control alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the existence of the confined action and its received-remedy rationale. No fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life & Casualty Co., 404 U.S. 6 — applied as recognition of the received private action; it did not itself decide accessory liability.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — applied for bounded development of the received action and distinguished as to its actual contribution holding; shared liability must still be established before allocation.
- Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Curran, 456 U.S. 353 — used by analogy for the significance of an established judicial remedy; its different statutory setting does not establish congressional enactment of this action.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — applied: the underlying wrong must involve the required manipulation or deception, not unfairness or fiduciary misconduct alone.
- Pinter v. Dahl, 486 U.S. 622 — distinguished: its construction of express seller liability under §12 does not settle this received §10(b) action; its own statutory holding remains intact.
- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Hochfelder itself did not decide that question.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Huddleston itself did not decide that question.

##### Knowledge or severe recklessness in affirmative substantial assistance

**Holding:** For affirmative substantial assistance, the defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the fraudulent character of the activity and the assisting role of its conduct; severe recklessness means an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it. No independent disclosure duty is required for that affirmative participation, but negligence, valuation concerns alone, professional status, ordinary services without qualifying culpability and assistance, or mere silence without a relevant duty do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; all five adopt this confined mental-state and affirmative-assistance rule, not a universal recklessness rule for every securities claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its exclusion of negligence remains controlling; its recklessness reservation is resolved only for this private affirmative-assistance setting.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — its deception-or-manipulation requirement prevents ordinary investment risk and professional error from becoming securities fraud.
- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — its confined affirmative-assistance and extreme-recklessness analysis is adopted for the bank claim; inconsistent knowledge-only or categorical duty requirements for such affirmative assistance cannot govern after this decision. Its separate rulings concerning Pring are outside this review.

##### Triable issues require further proceedings, not a liability award

**Holding:** Viewed favorably to the purchasers at summary judgment, the appraisal warnings, internal concerns and agreement postponing an already demanded independent review until after the offering permit a genuine dispute over severe recklessness and substantial affirmative assistance; the Tenth Circuit’s reinstatement of the pleaded bank claim is affirmed and the case remanded. The purchasers must still prove the underlying violation, the bank’s qualifying assistance and culpability, legally sufficient reliance on the underlying fraud, causation and recoverable loss under the ordinary civil burden; neither the default nor the bonds’ face value establishes fraud damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the record application, preserved proof requirements and limited affirmance and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — affirmed insofar as it reverses summary judgment on the purchasers’ affirmative-assistance claim against Central Bank; triable issues are not findings of fraud or damages.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — the ordinary preponderance standard is preserved; neither institutional status nor information held by the defendant shifts that burden.
- Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485 U.S. 224 — its reliance requirement is preserved. Today’s decision establishes no presumption for these municipal bonds and, as part of the retained accessory action, requires reliance on the underlying fraud rather than on the assistant’s concealed participation.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — its shared-liability prerequisite and contribution limits remain effective; this remand establishes no entitlement or allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the full rules for duty-based omissions, duty-free inaction, agency attribution, a universal securities recklessness rule, other enforcement or statutory settings, or Stone's additional concurrence formulations. The §20(a) defense for a controller acting in good faith without directly or indirectly inducing the violation remains distinct. Reliance on the underlying fraud is required; investors need not personally rely on concealed assistance, and no reliance presumption is established for these bonds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit insofar as it reinstated the pleaded bank claim and remand, 5–4. The bank's summary judgment cannot rest on the rejected categorical grounds or the disputed assistance and culpability record. No liability, damages, automatic indemnity, unpleaded primary claim or adjudication of another defendant's claim is supplied.

#### J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc., 534 U.S. 124

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1996.

**Decided:** December 10, 2001.

##### Whether plant-specific protection excludes utility-patent eligibility

**Holding:** Newly developed plants, including the corn varieties at issue, are not categorically excluded from 35 U.S.C. §101 because the Plant Patent Act or Plant Variety Protection Act also covers the subject. A claimant invoking utility protection must meet that statute's independent requirements; certificate-specific exceptions govern their own statutory right and do not create a general immunity from a valid utility patent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Novelty, nonobviousness, disclosure and other independent patent requirements remain. The holding does not establish validity of each patent claim or enforceability of every sales restriction; administrative practice and legislative silence are not binding constructions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical ineligibility defense is rejected and the Federal Circuit judgment affirmed, 7–2; independent patent and transaction issues retain their own requirements.

##### The surviving certificate exceptions and amendment transition

**Holding:** Concurrent utility eligibility does not rewrite the protection supplied by a plant-variety certificate. Former-law saved-seed protection and the amendments effective April 4, 1995 retain their enacted conditions and transition; no specific certificate, seed transaction or exception entitlement is adjudicated here.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same seven-Justice opinion; the certificate boundaries form part of its coexistence holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under former §2543 as construed in Asgrow, reproductive sale protects seed genuinely saved for the farmer's own planting from authorized seed or its descendants, with seller and purchaser primarily farming for nonreproductive crop sales and compliance with state seed-sale law. Changed planting plans may permit sale of genuinely retained seed, but planned multiplication for marketing is not protected. Bona fide nonreproductive sales through customary channels retain independent protection; diversion by a buyer to planting carries deemed §2567 notice. Section 2544 separately protects plant breeding and bona fide research. None of these certificate provisions is an exception to a separate utility patent.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The amended regime deletes the farmer-sales proviso from April 4, 1995. Section 14(a) generally preserves prior law for certificates issued before that date or applications then pending, subject to §14(b)'s refiling under amended terms while retaining the original §42 filing date and §14(c)'s notice identifying amended protection and sanctions for false or misleading claims or labels. No particular transaction, certificate or transition entitlement is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Diamond v. Chakrabarty, 447 U.S. 303: its broad reading of §101 applies to qualifying human invention rather than a categorical exclusion of living subject matter.
- Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer, No. 92-2038: its former certificate saved-seed construction and transition limits remain; no utility-patent exception is derived from it.
- In re Deuel, January 14, 1997: ordinary supported nonobviousness requirements remain distinct from category eligibility.
- State Street Bank & Trust Co. v. Signature Financial Group, Inc., January 14, 1999: broad category language does not remove genuine statutory limitations or independent proof requirements.

#### Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A., 534 U.S. 506

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1853.

**Decided:** February 26, 2002.

##### The prima facie proof framework is not a pleading checklist

**Holding:** A Title VII or ADEA complaint need not allege facts establishing every element of the McDonnell Douglas prima facie evidentiary framework. A short and plain account identifying the challenged employment decisions, their alleged prohibited basis and the grounds of the claim satisfies Rule 8 when it gives fair notice; this complaint does so.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices, supplies nine direct votes for both the rejection of the added pleading requirement and this complaint's sufficiency.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule applies fair-notice pleading to the actual complaint; it neither validates every conclusory allegation nor relieves the claimant of proving discrimination. Rules 11 and 56 and lawful discovery controls remain applicable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversal sets aside dismissal of the Title VII and intentional-age-discrimination claims under the added prima facie pleading requirement; ordinary proceedings follow.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green remains a conditional framework for circumstantial proof; its elements are not transformed into universal pleading requirements.

Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Thurston supplies the direct-evidence distinction that makes the proposed universal checklist unsound.

Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit is extended in its source-based procedural principle to this employment pleading requirement; its preserved immunity-pleading limits are unchanged.

Crawford-El v. Britton is applied for ordinary procedural control without an invented motive burden or circular discovery barrier.

Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc. remains an evidentiary-sufficiency decision and does not supply an additional complaint requirement.

Conley v. Gibson supplies the governing notice-pleading background.

#### Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc., 504 U.S. 451 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1029.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

##### Whether demand for separate parts and service and conditional parts sales create a triable tying claim

**Holding:** For a Sherman Act §1 tying claim, functionally related products are distinct when sufficient separate consumer demand makes separate provision efficient. Evidence that Kodak parts and service meet that test, and that Kodak conditions parts sales on buying its service or forgoing independent service, creates genuine disputes about distinct products and conditioning; respondents must still prove all remaining tying elements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices.

**Material application:** Independent service providers, customer preference for their services and self-servicing customers permit a finding of efficient separate provision. Restrictions on parts sales and use of independent service permit a finding of conditioning; installation of parts during service does not conclusively merge the products. The tied-commerce volume is undisputed here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final tie, market power or liability finding is made. An independent original-equipment-manufacturer conspiracy claim is not adjudicated; the rule-of-reason theory unpreserved in opposition to summary judgment is outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment on the preserved §1 tying claim; respondents retain proof of its elements and the requirements for recovery.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Jefferson Parish Hospital District No. 2 v. Hyde*, 466 U.S. 2, supplies separate demand, conditioning, tying power and tied commerce. *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, preserves the obligation to produce evidence on challenged elements at summary judgment.

##### Whether equipment-market competition categorically precludes aftermarket power

**Holding:** Competition in an equipment market does not as a matter of law preclude power in its parts or service markets. Rule 56 does not permit summary judgment solely through an economic prediction contradicted by evidence allowing a rational inference of power, including material information and switching costs and actual price or exclusion effects; unsupported speculation remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices reject the categorical defense.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated; equipment competition remains relevant evidence.

**Material application:** Costly equipment replacement and difficulty predicting lifetime service prices permit a finding that installed customers cannot readily escape adverse terms. Evidence of price and exclusion effects supports further proceedings. Kodak may establish that informed new customers and equipment competition adequately constrain its policies, but the conclusion is not inevitable as a matter of law.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court presumes neither market power from imperfect information nor monopoly status for every durable-product seller. Prices, effects and asserted policy changes remain disputed; no universal grandfathering, deception or retroactive-policy-change finding is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The preserved claims survive summary judgment, with actual markets, power and liability remaining to be proved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Zenith Radio Corp.*, 475 U.S. 574, preserves rational inference from evidence but is distinguished because immediate aftermarket gains differ from its prolonged loss-producing conspiracy. *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, preserves the factfinder's role on genuine material disputes. *Jefferson Parish*, 466 U.S. 2, continues to require proof of actual tying power.

##### Whether one brand's parts or service can constitute a relevant market on this record

**Holding:** A relevant product market may consist of one brand's parts or service when actual commercial alternatives and competitive constraints support that definition; brand identity alone neither establishes nor defeats it. Kodak-specific compatibility, very high parts and service shares, and entry and switching barriers create a genuine dispute about monopoly power sufficient to resist summary judgment under §2.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices permit a market and power finding without conclusively establishing either.

**Material application:** Owners cannot necessarily substitute another brand's parts, and replacing the equipment may cost much more than the service at issue. Evidence supports nearly complete control of compatible parts and an eighty-to-ninety-five-percent service share, together with impediments to independent supply. Equipment competitors remain relevant to whether lifetime prices are constrained.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal single-brand-market rule or monopoly presumption follows from patents, proprietary design or numerical share apart from actual competitive constraints. Actual market definition and power remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment on the preserved §2 claims, without a final market or power finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *United States v. Grinnell Corp.*, 384 U.S. 563, requires monopoly power in a relevant market, distinct from exclusion. *United States v. E. I. du Pont de Nemours & Co.*, 351 U.S. 377, supplies practical substitution and competitive-constraint analysis without a categorical bar to one-brand markets.

##### Whether evidence of exclusion and Kodak's business explanations conclusively resolve monopoly maintenance

**Holding:** Monopolization under §2 requires monopoly power plus willful acquisition or maintenance, distinguished from success through a superior product, business acumen or accident. Parts-access restrictions that may exclude service rivals create a genuine issue of unlawful maintenance, and Kodak's quality, inventory and free-riding explanations do not conclusively eliminate that issue here; no general duty to supply rivals or final pretext finding is imposed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices.

**Current-force modification:** CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation, June 4, 2001, resolves the previously open patent and copyright defense to a supported monopoly-maintenance claim. Protected exclusion retains substantial justificatory force but supplies no conclusive defense or exhaustive fraud, sham-litigation, tying, unlawful-acquisition or beyond-grant prerequisite. Kodak's distinct power, unlawful-conduct and injury requirements remain; no duty to supply rivals or finding of liability follows.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for evidentiary sufficiency and the distinct power and conduct requirements.

**Material application:** The quality explanation must be tested against self-service exceptions and independent-service evidence; the inventory explanation against evidence that parts needs do not depend on the service provider; and free-riding claims against the restrictions' operation and alternative-supply obstacles. Kodak may prove legitimate reasons and defeat liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Attempted monopolization remains distinct, with its own intent and dangerous-probability requirements. Ultimate markets, power, unlawful conduct, pretext, antitrust injury, causation, damages and remedial design remain unresolved. Injury to a competitor alone establishes neither antitrust injury nor damages entitlement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of summary judgment on the preserved §2 monopolization and attempt claims. No damages, final injunction or compulsory parts-supply order issues; respondents must prove each claim and recovery requirement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Grinnell*, 384 U.S. 563, distinguishes unlawful maintenance from lawful success. *Aspen Skiing Co. v. Aspen Highlands Skiing Corp.*, 472 U.S. 585, makes exclusion and business justification material without creating a universal supply duty. *Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc.*, 429 U.S. 477, retains the distinct antitrust-injury requirement. *Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp.*, March 24, 1992, retains its RICO directness and SIPA holdings without deciding these Sherman Act elements or injury.

**Later-authority backlinks:** CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation, June 4, 2001 — patent and copyright exclusion as defenses to maintenance of monopoly power in a distinct service market.

#### In re Independent Service Organizations Antitrust Litigation: CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation

**Docket or dockets:** Federal Circuit No. 99-1323; District of Kansas MDL-1021; judgment below, 203 F.3d 1322.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Patent exclusion and monopoly maintenance

**Holding:** A valid patent and unilateral refusal to supply protected parts neither establish monopolization nor conclusively defeat a supported claim that control of those inputs maintains monopoly power in a distinct service market. Without an additional prerequisite of procurement fraud, sham litigation or illegal tying, the plaintiff must prove relevant-market power, unlawful acquisition or maintenance, competitive injury and causation; lawful statutory exclusion and returns on invention retain substantive justificatory force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for this proposition and the bounded remand. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Material application:** The asserted aftermarket must rest on actual substitutes and constraints. End-user sales, selective verification, alternate-supply barriers and service effects require assessment; none establishes a violation. Statutory exclusion is a substantive legal entitlement, and an antitrust market boundary alone does not show conduct outside the patent grant. Quality, inventory, support, innovation returns and supported efficiencies remain relevant.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Refusal, hostility to competitors, injury to CSU and a service-market label are insufficient. Competent documents, testimony and actual conduct can establish or defeat the elements; there is no universal econometric-study prerequisite. No general duty to assist rivals arises. Patent validity and infringement remain outside the accepted question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc.: applies the distinct power, conduct and justification inquiries and its fact-supported aftermarket rule; the patent and copyright defense left open there is now resolved. Its tying holding creates no tying claim here.
- United States v. Grinnell Corp.: preserves monopoly power and unlawful acquisition or maintenance as distinct requirements, with superiority, business acumen and accident outside the offense.
- Aspen Skiing Co. v. Aspen Highlands Skiing Corp.: applies scrutiny of actual exclusion and business justification without a general duty to supply competitors.
- Professional Real Estate Investors, Inc. v. Columbia Pictures Industries, Inc.: preserves its objectively-baseless-litigation prerequisite for the sham petitioning theory it addressed; that rule is not transplanted as immunity for commercial refusal.
- TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc.: its expired-patent functionality and trade-dress holding remains intact and supplies no disposition of valid patent or copyright exclusion here.

##### Copyright justification and the required remand

**Holding:** Copyright's exclusive rights provide a substantial justification for refusing protected manuals or diagnostic-software access, but do not conclusively defeat an objectively supported monopoly-maintenance claim merely because unlawful acquisition or extension beyond the copyright grant is absent. The same ordinary monopolization burdens apply; subjective pretext, rival injury or refusal alone cannot rebut the statutory justification, and every eventual remedy must fit a proved violation and causal injury while preserving lawful protected returns insofar as compatible with curing that violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for this proposition and the bounded remand. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Material application:** The lower court treated copyright exclusion as presumptively justified and limited rebuttal to unlawful acquisition or extension beyond the grant. The Court preserves the justification's substance but removes that conclusive limitation. Protected rights, challenged conduct, competitive mechanism and supporting evidence must be identified separately.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual market definition, durable power, unlawful exclusion, causation, antitrust injury and lawful relief remain to be established. Copyright ownership and infringement are outside review. No final misuse ruling follows; any consequence of removal of the lower court's patent-scope predicate requires its own preserved ground. Temporary settlement access establishes neither liability nor a right to forced access.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The affected patent- and copyright-based antitrust summary judgments are vacated. Renewed summary judgment remains available on a supported failure of proof or lawful-exclusion ground. The mandate orders no discovery, hearing, trial, damages, parts supply or software license.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- California Dental Association v. Federal Trade Commission: applies restraint-specific consideration of effects and substantial explanations; no automatic full-market study or motive-only test follows.
- NYNEX Corp. v. Discon, Inc.: preserves the distinction between injury to one supplier and injury to the competitive process; its section 1 categories are not converted into a new section 2 exemption.
- Brunswick Corp. v. Pueblo Bowl-O-Mat, Inc.: preserves the independent antitrust-injury requirement.

#### Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-2038.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### Saved seed means seed retained for the farmer's own planting

**Holding:** Under former 7 U.S.C. §2543, a farmer satisfying the statutory conditions may sell for reproductive purposes only seed genuinely saved for replanting the farmer's own acreage; intentional multiplication for reproductive marketing does not become exempt merely because the seller primarily grows food or feed crops. Qualifying later disposition of genuinely retained seed remains possible when planting plans change, without a fixed percentage or mechanical ceiling based on the previous year's acreage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices, including the application that these sales fail the own-planting condition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: follows its respect for the enacted condition in statutory context, without importing the veterans statute's protective construction or inventing a numerical PVPA condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Saved seed must be produced by the person from seed obtained, or descended from seed obtained, with the owner's authority for seeding purposes. Seller and purchaser must each have as their primary farming occupation growing crops for sale for nonreproductive purposes; the sale must comply with applicable state seed-sale laws. The retained §2541(4) restriction on using the variety to produce, rather than develop, a hybrid or different variety remains separate. Bona fide nonreproductive sales in customary nonreproductive channels retain their independent statutory protection; a purchaser who diverts that seed to planting is deemed to have notice under §2567 that the diversion infringes. That diversion provision is distinct from §2541(6)'s notice claim. No general ruling on a buyer's later authorized-source lineage is adopted.

**Current-force modification:** Pub. L. 103-349 §10 deletes the former farmer-to-farmer sales proviso for the amended regime from April 4, 1995. Under §14(a), varieties with certificates issued before that date and applications pending then retain former law except as §14 provides. Section 14(b) permits refiling a pending application under amended eligibility and protection terms while retaining its original date for §42; §14(c) requires notice identifying amended protection and retains §128 sanctions for false or misleading claims or labels. Asgrow's former-law holding and adjudicated transactions remain undisturbed. No refiling or transition exception is found, and no judicial construction of the amended saved-seed text is supplied.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Plant Variety Protection Act Amendments, Pub. L. 103-349, 108 Stat. 3136, effective April 4, 1995: §§10, 14–15, amended farmer-sales provision and former-law preservation. Section 9's separate contract-producer clause covers listed lawn, turf, forage-grass, alfalfa and clover crops, not these soybeans; this source creates no judicial holding.

##### Marketing does not require an advertising or distribution apparatus

**Holding:** Former §2541(3)'s multiplication as a step in marketing for growing purposes includes multiplication directed toward holding protected seed forth for sale and the preparatory activities of selling; extensive advertising, intermediaries, or coordinated merchandising are not required. The proviso's primary-farming-occupation condition concerns the farmer's business as a whole, not a judicial rule allowing reproductive sale of less than half of each protected variety's crop, and that occupation condition does not dispense with the distinct saved-seed limitation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices, including the application that these sales fail the own-planting condition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: applies contextual attention to the enacted terms rather than an additional condition; its separate VA-benefits rule remains unchanged.

##### The former Act governs these transactions

**Holding:** The 1994 Plant Variety Protection Act amendments do not govern these transactions or supply the meaning of the former saved-seed provision: Pub. L. 103-349 §15 makes the amendments effective April 4, 1995, after this decision, and §14 preserves former-law treatment for specified older certificates and pending applications subject to its express exceptions. The later deletion of the farmer-sales proviso therefore creates no retrospective liability here and does not establish that every later sale involving an older protected variety loses former-law treatment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices, including the application that these sales fail the own-planting condition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994): applies Congress's express temporal direction as the starting point; does not convert Scalia, Kennedy, or Thomas into joiners of its broader general framework.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298 (1994): preserves the separation of later legislative change from the law governing completed conduct; the new amendment is not a conclusive interpretation of old text.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 14(a), except as otherwise provided in §14, preserves prior law for varieties with certificates issued before April 4, 1995 and applications pending then. Section 14(b) permits refiling a pending application on or after that date under amended eligibility and protection terms, while §42 uses the original filing date. Section 14(c) requires a notice claiming amended protection to identify that protection and applies §128 sanctions to false or misleading claims or labels. No relevant refiling or transition exception is found.

The seven-Justice Court opinion does not decide whether a sale actually authorized by §2543 must nevertheless satisfy §2541(6), because these sales fail the saved-seed condition. It makes no separate notice-infringement finding or damages award. Section 2541 concerns unauthorized acts in the United States or regulated or affected commerce, before protection expires but after certificate issuance or distribution with §2567 notice, subject to the subchapter's exceptions. Section 2541(6) concerns dispensing propagable protected material without the protected-variety notice under which it was received; failure of the saved-seed defense alone does not establish those elements. Former §2567 permits owners to give public notice by associating a label with seed or affixing it to its container or the variety, stating that unauthorized propagation or seed multiplication is prohibited and, after the certificate issues, adding a protected-variety designation. When an owner-authorized distribution reaches the infringer without that marking, the owner cannot recover damages unless the infringer has actual awareness that propagation is prohibited or the variety is protected; damages then extend only to infringement after such notice. For both damages and an injunction, a court may be lenient concerning disposal of material acquired in good faith through acts before such notice. These are reserved remedial conditions, not findings of marking, actual awareness, good faith, or entitlement to leniency in this case. Actual relief and precise lawful injunction implementation remain for the receiving courts. The adjudicated own-planting failure is not reopened by that limited return.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit is reversed, 8–1. The seven-Justice conclusion that these sales fail the saved-seed condition is not reopened on return; unresolved notice elements, damages and lawful injunction implementation remain. No separate notice-liability finding or monetary award is made.
