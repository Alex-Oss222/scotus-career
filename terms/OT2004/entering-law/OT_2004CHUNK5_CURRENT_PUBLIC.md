## bell-v-cone_merits_2005-01-24.md



## Event

Bell v. Cone, No. 04-394; 543 U.S. 447. Merits decision, January 24, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, 359 F.3d 785; submitted for decision on the aggravator-based sentence-relief judgment entered after the prior remand.

The question is whether Tennessee failed to apply its authoritative narrowing construction of the heinousness aggravator and whether an omitted citation could establish that failure. Cone also asserts that the jury's guidance and the subsequent appellate proceeding failed to provide a constitutionally adequate sentencing determination. Tennessee seeks reversal of the aggravator-based writ; Cone seeks to retain sentence relief. The trial occurred in 1982, direct state review in 1984, and the federal application was filed July 1, 1997.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Sixth Circuit's aggravator-based sentence-relief judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse and finally reject the reviewed aggravator claim, returning only independent remaining claims.

The existing aggravator-based writ is vacated. The Court conclusively rejects failure of state judicial narrowing and remands the distinct asserted jury-guidance and weighing theory under the conditions in Part III. Stone-Zsela would retain a broader actual-adjudication and narrowing inquiry on remand. The convictions remain intact, and other properly pending sentence claims remain governed by the 2002 mandate.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

### I. Federal review turns on the state court's legal work, not a case citation

**Holding and operative rule:** A state court's failure to cite its settled narrowing precedent does not itself establish that it applied an unconstitutionally vague capital aggravator. Federal review must examine the authoritative construction and the state court's actual reasoning and result under the applicable review law, without inventing an adjudication or corrective step the record does not establish.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's per curiam opinion, supported by all nine participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, requires meaningful guidance in applying a heinousness aggravator; *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, recognizes the work performed by an authoritative narrowing construction. The constitutional inquiry concerns the rule actually applied. *Sochor v. Florida* permits a sufficiently specific judicial narrowing application without treating citation form as the constitutional act. *Early v. Packer* and *Woodford v. Visciotti* likewise require substantive review of the complete state adjudication. An omitted name cannot replace that inquiry. Conversely, presumed familiarity with state law cannot establish an otherwise unperformed application or correction. The Sixth Circuit's missing-citation inference therefore cannot sustain its sentence-relief judgment.

### II. Tennessee actually applied the narrowed category; exhaustion uncertainty does not prevent rejecting that theory

**Holding and operative rule:** Tennessee's 1984 opinion actually applied its authoritative narrowed heinousness category to Cone's killings, and the asserted failure of judicial narrowing is conclusively rejected. Under 28 U.S.C. §2254(b)(2), a court may deny a habeas claim on a sufficient merits ground despite nonexhaustion; that permission does not require denial, establish a state merits adjudication or dispose of a distinct constitutional theory.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, supported by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela does not join this conclusive application or the §2254(b)(2) rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** *State v. Dicks* confines the category to a conscienceless or pitiless crime unnecessarily torturous to the victim. *State v. Pritchett* requires predeath torture or sufficiently grave depravity and excludes instantaneous death from the torture branch. *State v. Melson* applies that construction through defensive injuries, awareness, fear, resistance and repeated crushing blows. The Tennessee Supreme Court identified the corresponding circumstances here: defensive wounds and resistance, multiple crushing blows, noninstantaneous deaths and terror from the sequence of the two killings. That explanation applies the narrowed category rather than merely repeating the statutory adjectives. The parties' references to the state authorities corroborate the setting; the court's actual reasoning supplies the application.

The claim that the reviewing court failed to narrow the category therefore fails on its substance, without reliance on a disputed presumption that every federal theory was adjudicated. Section 2254(b)(2) permits that sufficient denial despite a dispute about exhaustion. The provision removes the need to settle exhaustion solely to reject this unsuccessful theory; it does not supply the answer to the distinct jury-guidance issue addressed in Part III.

### III. The distinct jury-guidance and weighing claim requires its own lawful disposition

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's aggravator-based sentence-relief judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded to dispose of Cone's existing, distinct jury-guidance and weighing claim under its actual state adjudication, the applicable federal-law cutoff, §2254 and any preserved valid default. Judicial narrowing is settled; if no distinct available theory survives, relief on this ground must be denied, while an available constitutional sentencing error requires an authorized actual correction or the governing State-borne harmlessness determination.

**Authority:** Part III of the per curiam opinion, supported by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six adopt the complete confined mandate and its explanation. Stone-Zsela supports vacatur and remand on the broader inquiry stated in his separate opinion; Scalia and Thomas would finally reject the reviewed aggravator claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The jury found and weighed the heinousness aggravator. Its reported definitions include extreme wickedness, outrageous vileness and cruelty expressed through high pain, indifference, enjoyment of suffering or pitilessness. The state court's later finding that the killings fit its narrower construction does not itself establish that the jury used that construction or that an unconstitutional broader influence, if proved, was corrected. Different formulations alone also do not prove error.

*Sochor* separates judicial narrowing from jury guidance and invalid-factor correction. *Richmond v. Lewis* requires an actual constitutional sentencing determination or adequate harmlessness when a weighing error is established. *Lambrix v. Singletary* preserves the legal significance of a consequential jury contribution despite independent judicial review. Those distinctions answer the argument that valid judicial narrowing necessarily ends this already asserted jury claim.

Relief still requires the correct statutory and temporal predicates. *Bell v. Cone*, 535 U.S. 685, applies amended §2254 to this 1997 application and preserves remaining sentence claims. The 1984 direct adjudication must be assessed against Supreme Court holdings then established; *Godfrey* and *Proffitt* qualify, while later holdings do not become 1984 law merely by explaining it. The 1993 previously-determined description and 1995 affirmance do not themselves establish a new merits date. *Stringer*'s 1985-finality and *Lambrix*'s 1986-finality rulings do not settle that earlier cutoff. *Mitchell v. Esparza* permits final denial when the actual claim and dated law supply it; here judicial category-fit alone leaves a distinct asserted operation unresolved.

An independently sufficient denial may be entered without unnecessary exhaustion litigation or factual proceedings. If an available nonstructural sentencing violation is established, *Brecht v. Abrahamson* places harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on the State when that effect inquiry governs. An authorized actual cure is a separate route. Surviving-factor counts, proportionality or the state harmlessness ruling on the different risk-to-others factor cannot automatically correct a proved heinousness error. The convictions remain intact; other properly pending sentence claims retain the 2002 mandate's protection.

## Precedent Treatment

- *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, and *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242: Applied as date-eligible narrowing authorities for the 1984 judicial application.
- *Early v. Packer*, 537 U.S. 3, and *Woodford v. Visciotti*, 537 U.S. 19: Applied for substantive review of the whole state adjudication instead of citation or terminology tests.
- *Sochor v. Florida*, 504 U.S. 527: Applied for sufficiently specific judicial narrowing and the distinct treatment of jury guidance and invalid-factor correction.
- *Richmond v. Lewis*, 506 U.S. 40: Applied to the conditional need for an actual constitutional correction of an established weighing error; remaining valid factors alone do not supply it.
- *Stringer v. Black*, 503 U.S. 222, and *Lambrix v. Singletary*, 520 U.S. 518: Applied for the distinction between a valid category, an influential jury determination and actual correction. Their respective 1985 and 1986 finality determinations do not establish the §2254(d)(1) rule for a 1984 adjudication.
- *Bell v. Cone*, 535 U.S. 685: Enforced as to the amended review regime, the relevant adjudication-date limit and the continued availability of properly pending sentence claims.
- *Mitchell v. Esparza*, 540 U.S. 12: Preserved for final rejection of a claim when its actual record and dated statutory inquiry establish a sufficient denial; distinguished from treating judicial narrowing as the complete disposition of this distinct asserted jury operation.
- *Brecht v. Abrahamson*, 507 U.S. 619: Applied for the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden when an available nonstructural constitutional violation requires collateral effect review.

## Law After Decision

Actual state judicial application of the narrowed heinousness category is settled in this case. An omitted precedent citation supplies no constitutional failure by itself. A distinct, available jury-guidance and weighing theory requires its own dated statutory and constitutional disposition; adequate judicial narrowing alone does not decide that different operation. Section 2254(b)(2) permits a sufficient merits denial despite nonexhaustion.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and in the judgment:** The omitted citation cannot establish constitutional failure, but the Chief would return the actual state merits treatment and narrowing application for the corrected inquiry rather than conclusively resolve them here. *Sochor* makes substantive legal operation decisive, while *Bell*, 535 U.S. 685, requires the proper statutory gateway and state-adjudication date. He would require the lower court to identify what the state court actually did, apply the dated law and decide any surviving jury-guidance and cure issue without presuming an omitted step. *Richmond*, *Lambrix* and *Brecht* require an actual authorized correction or the State's governing beyond-reasonable-doubt showing once a reviewable violation exists. If relief is warranted and neither route sustains the sentence, a conditional sentence writ must specify a reasonable correction period, require the State to report a lawful replacement determination and require removal of the death sentence if correction does not occur. He joins neither Part II's conclusive application nor its §2254(b)(2) rationale, and does not join Part III's closure of the narrowing inquiry.

**Scalia, concurring in Parts I and II and dissenting in part:** The actual state explanation establishes constitutionally adequate judicial narrowing, so the reviewed aggravator claim should end. *Sochor* recognizes the narrowing work a state court may perform, and *Early* and *Visciotti* reject a citation or curative-label prerequisite where the substantive application is supported. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial despite unresolved nonexhaustion. He objects that the further mandate keeps the reviewed ground open without an established distinct defect. He would reverse and finally deny that ground; a genuinely independent pending claim must establish its own procedural and constitutional basis.

**Thomas, concurring in Parts I and II and dissenting in part:** The state opinion substantively applies the authoritative construction. Different formulations and an omitted citation do not establish an additional constitutional defect requiring continuation of this reviewed claim. His position preserves *Richmond*'s actual-cure requirement when an available weighing violation is established; the present narrowing determination does not establish that predicate. *Early*, *Visciotti* and *Esparza* support final denial on the actual claim and dated law, with §2254(b)(2) available despite nonexhaustion. He would reverse on this ground and return only independent remaining claims, without transferring the separate risk-factor harmlessness ruling to a different error.

## Procedure After Action

The Sixth Circuit must resolve the existing distinct jury-guidance and weighing theory under the mandate in Part III, denying relief if no available ground survives and applying the governing correction or effect requirements if a reviewable violation is established. Actual judicial narrowing may not be reconsidered. Other properly pending sentence claims continue under the 2002 mandate, and the convictions remain intact.

## brosseau-v-haugen_merits_2004-12-13.md



## Event

Brosseau v. Haugen, No. 03-1261. Merits decision, December 13, 2004, after merits submission. On review of the Ninth Circuit's judgment, 339 F.3d 857, reversing summary judgment for Brosseau on Haugen's individual Fourth Amendment damages claim under 42 U.S.C. §1983.

The grant is limited to individual qualified immunity on the plaintiff-favorable summary-judgment record. The force merits are outside that grant. Brosseau seeks restoration of immunity; Haugen seeks continuation of the claim.

The shooting occurred February 21, 1999. After resisting apprehension, Haugen entered his Jeep, locked it, resisted Brosseau's efforts to take his keys and started the engine. For this review, the Court assumes the Jeep had not yet moved when she fired, its escape path was clear and the windshield unobstructed, and no identified person was in the vehicle's path and no objectively supported immediate weapon threat existed. Occupied cars were nearby. Brosseau's concern about possible danger is her reported perception, not a finding of an imminent collision. Haugen's later driving and guilty plea cannot enlarge what she knew before the shot.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the denial of Brosseau's individual qualified immunity and remands for implementation of that disposition.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela and Stevens.

Brosseau is entitled to personal qualified immunity on the accepted summary-judgment assumptions. The mandate resolves this individual federal damages claim. The separate municipal and state-law branches remain at their existing scope.

## Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which O'CONNOR, SCALIA, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., and STONE-ZSELA, C. J., each filed a dissenting opinion.

## Holdings

#### The accepted vehicle-escape setting lacked sufficiently particular preconduct notice

**Holding and operative rule.** Brosseau has individual qualified immunity because law existing before February 21, 1999 did not make the illegality of this prospective-risk response sufficiently apparent on the plaintiff-favorable account: determined resistance followed by imminent vehicle escape in a confined setting near occupied cars, with no already moving Jeep, obstructed escape route, identified person in its path or supported immediate weapon threat. The inquiry requires contextual legal notice, permits an obvious application of an established rule without a factual replica, and does not confer a categorical vehicle or flight exception.

**Authority.** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, adopted in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies seven direct votes for this immunity application and its complete rationale.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the legal clarity of the governing rule is distinct from the factual reasonableness of the force. Tennessee v. Garner supplies the serious-threat requirement for deadly force against a fleeing suspect; Graham v. Connor supplies the contextual merits inquiry. Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton preserve a separate objective defense where preconduct law did not make the particular legal boundary sufficiently clear. Graham's allowance for urgent circumstances and reasonable factual perceptions does not itself answer that legal-notice question.

On the adverse assumptions, Brosseau's concern was a forecast of danger from an imminent departure, not an established collision or an officer standing in the Jeep's path. Smith v. Freland and Cole v. Bone involved substantially greater demonstrated driving danger. They do not establish this shooting's lawfulness, but their differing applications, together with Estate of Starks, show why Garner's general limit did not plainly settle this anticipatory-force boundary. The Court accepts the favorable escape-path and timing account; it does not manufacture legal uncertainty by choosing disputed facts for the officer.

An established principle may give fair warning through an obvious application. Here the combination of determined resistance, imminent vehicular departure and nearby occupied cars makes the precise prospective-risk question insufficiently clear under the preconduct authorities. Saucier v. Katz retains the existing defense without a mandatory merits-first sequence, and Hope v. Pelzer rejects a demand for a factual twin while preserving context. Those later decisions explain the present inquiry, but neither supplies notice for the 1999 conduct. The limited grant permits resolution of immunity while leaving the ultimate force merits outside this review.

## Precedent Treatment

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies the existing objective individual-immunity defense to the conduct-date legal question.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: applies particularized legal clarity without demanding an identical prior case.
- Tennessee v. Garner, 471 U.S. 1: preserves the serious-threat condition for deadly force against a fleeing suspect; its general rule does not give sufficiently particular notice for this prospective-risk application.
- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386: preserves the complete contextual force inquiry and its attention to reasonable factual perceptions; force legality and the clarity inquiry retain separate work.
- Saucier v. Katz, June 18, 2001: applies the retained objective defense and its use of proper factual assumptions; the prior remand established no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- Hope v. Pelzer, June 27, 2002: applies its rejection of a factual-replica requirement and its attention to context; its later date supplies no February 1999 notice.
- Johnson v. Jones, June 12, 1995: preserves the distinction between a separable legal immunity issue on assumed facts and appellate resolution of factual sufficiency.

## Law After Decision

The existing Harlow–Anderson defense resolves this individual claim because the accepted anticipatory vehicle-escape setting lacked sufficiently clear preconduct legal guidance. Lower courts must use the proper plaintiff-favorable facts and assess conduct-date notice in context, including whether established principles make the application obvious. The rule neither requires an identical prior case nor treats vehicle escape as automatic constitutional permission.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens dissents. He would affirm the appellate reversal of summary judgment and return the claim for ordinary proceedings under existing law. Garner already gives the governing rule: escape alone does not justify deadly force without the requisite serious-threat basis. On the assumed clear route, stationary Jeep and absence of an identified immediate threat, the proposed future danger does not make that boundary uncertain. Smith and Cole involved materially more dangerous driving and therefore do not erase Garner's notice here. He regards the officer's reasonable assessment of the possible accident and the surrounding circumstances as matters for lawful adjudication, not a reason to end the claim on this assumed account. His position applies the existing defense; it does not make every historical-fact dispute defeat a separable legal immunity ruling.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela separately dissents. He would expressly replace the broad objective legal-notice defense with a defense of actual reasonable reliance on authoritative law and affirm the removal of summary judgment. An official invoking his proposed defense would have to prove, by a preponderance, identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance on it, and a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable. Legal silence, a department's practice and the absence of a materially identical case would not suffice. A policy or supervisor's instruction would qualify only insofar as it accurately conveyed such identified authority and the official reasonably relied on it.

He reasons that Graham's complete force standard already accommodates urgency, reasonable factual mistakes and lawful discretion. An additional defense that can defeat an established violation without actual reliance on law authorizing the conduct is disconnected from the justification for protecting legal reliance. His proposal would therefore replace the broader Harlow–Anderson rule, preserve Graham and Garner in the force inquiry, and retain Hope's rejection of factual identity. Saucier did not previously adopt that replacement. Brosseau has shown neither directly authorizing authority nor actual reasonable reliance on this record. Further proceedings would apply the complete force rule, resolve genuinely material facts and any supported reliance defense, and require the remaining causation, injury and damages predicates. The proposal concerns the personal defense within the existing cause of action; absolute functional immunity, sovereign immunity and independently sourced institutional liability are outside it. The ultimate force merits remain outside the limited grant.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for implementation of Brosseau's personal qualified-immunity judgment, ending the reviewed individual federal damages claim. The ultimate force merits remain outside the grant, and the separate municipal and state-law branches retain their existing status.

## Source Notes

Fourth Amendment; 42 U.S.C. §1983; No. 03-1261; 339 F.3d 857, 867. The operative assumed facts are those favorable to Haugen at summary judgment, not findings following trial. February 21, 1999 is the date for legal notice. Smith v. Freland, 954 F.2d 343; Cole v. Bone, 993 F.2d 1328; Estate of Starks v. Enyart, 5 F.3d 230, concern their own vehicle and threat settings.


## illinois-v-caballes_merits_2005-01-24.md



## Event

Illinois v. Caballes, No. 03-923; 543 U.S. 405. Merits decision, January 24, 2005. Argued November 10, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Illinois, 207 Ill. 2d 504, 802 N.E.2d 202.

A trooper stopped Caballes for speeding. During the traffic business another officer walked a narcotics dog around the car; its alert led to a trunk search and discovery of marijuana. The trial court found the duration lawful and the alert sufficiently reliable for probable cause. The Illinois Supreme Court reversed the conviction because the canine examination expanded the investigation without reasonable, articulable drug suspicion. The granted question accepts lawful traffic duration and asks whether the Fourth Amendment independently requires that suspicion for this exterior examination. Illinois seeks removal of that requirement; Caballes seeks preservation of suppression.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Illinois Supreme Court's judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Souter and Ginsburg.

The state court must apply the limited nonsearch rule to the accepted lawful-stop premises and resolve genuinely preserved independent grounds. If no independent defect remains, lack of separate drug suspicion does not justify excluding the evidence.

## Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS and BREYER, JJ., joined. SOUTER, J., filed a dissenting opinion. GINSBURG, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SOUTER, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Full support or joins | Relationship to judgment and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer join in full | Complete limited nonsearch rule, application and vacatur-and-remand remedy |
| Search-ground dissent | Souter | No other Justice joins | Would affirm suppression because the examination is a search lacking an independent lawful basis |
| Seizure-scope dissent | Ginsburg | Souter joins in full | Would affirm because the compulsory investigation materially expands the limited traffic seizure |

## Holdings

### The limited exterior canine examination during an independently lawful traffic stop

**Holding and operative rule:** An exterior examination by a properly trained narcotics dog that is actually confined to detecting contraband requires no separate drug suspicion when officers have lawful access and the examination adds no unlawful restraint or other protected intrusion. The State must establish those predicates and ordinary probable cause for any later physical search; the dog's label and the later discovery of drugs do not substitute for them.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court. The common whole-opinion coalition identified in Opinion Topology adopts the rule, its qualifications, this application and the vacatur-and-remand remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** *United States v. Place*, 462 U.S. 696, distinguishes a canine examination confined to contraband from the detention that enables it. *United States v. Jacobsen*, 466 U.S. 109, distinguishes detection of contraband alone from disclosure of lawful private information. Those distinctions govern the exterior examination here.

Authority must exist for each stage. The exterior-examination rule applies only during an independently lawful initial detention: *Whren v. United States* makes the unchallenged speeding stop presumptively reasonable, and nothing rebuts that presumption here. *Ohio v. Robinette* requires independent grounds for restraint after traffic justification ends. *Knowles v. Iowa* requires an independent basis for a physical search following a citation. The later alert must satisfy ordinary probable-cause requirements. *City of Indianapolis v. Edmond* requires antecedent authority for a seizure before a canine examination; here the traffic offense supplies that authority. *Kyllo v. United States* protects concealed domestic information acquired by a targeted examination; the limited method here is confined to contraband.

The grant accepts the lawfulness of the traffic detention's duration. The trial court separately found this alert sufficiently reliable for probable cause. The dissenters object that a dog can err and that a compulsory drug investigation changes the encounter's character. Reliability for probable cause and the information revealed by the examination are distinct questions. A possible erroneous alert does not itself expose lawful private information; the later physical search remains subject to its own justification. On the accepted exterior-examination and lawful-duration premises, an unrelated investigative aim alone supplies neither a protected privacy intrusion nor unlawful added restraint.

The Illinois Supreme Court therefore erred in requiring separate drug suspicion solely because this examination added a drug-investigation purpose to the lawful traffic encounter.

**Operative limits:** The rule requires actual informational confinement, lawful access and lawful restraint. It does not authorize delay after traffic justification ends or entry into a protected area; physical searches retain their independent constitutional predicates. Reliability rests on the trial court's finding about this alert.

## Precedent Treatment

- *United States v. Place*, 462 U.S. 696: Applied to contraband-confined detection while retaining its independent limits on detention.
- *United States v. Jacobsen*, 466 U.S. 109: Applied for the distinction between contraband-only information and lawful private contents; constitutional classification remains separate from a later physical search.
- *Whren v. United States*, 517 U.S. 806: Applied to the independently justified traffic stop, preserving separate manner, scope and duration limits.
- *Ohio v. Robinette*: Preserved for the requirement of independent authority when traffic justification ends; lawful initial detention supplies no continuing authority by itself.
- *Knowles v. Iowa*, 525 U.S. 113: Preserved for the independent justification required for a physical search following a traffic citation.
- *City of Indianapolis v. Edmond*, 531 U.S. 32: Distinguished because the present traffic offense supplies the seizure authority absent from the narcotics checkpoint; the canine-classification question reserved there is resolved here for this defined setting.
- *Kyllo v. United States*, 533 U.S. 27: Distinguished because its examination acquired concealed lawful domestic information. Its protection of that information and its separate warrant requirement remain in force.

## Law After Decision

A contraband-confined exterior canine examination during an independently lawful traffic detention requires no separate drug suspicion when the State establishes the stated access, information and restraint predicates. Existing search and seizure rules independently govern detention duration, protected intrusions and the physical search following an alert.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, dissenting:** The dog was used to investigate concealed effects and to establish grounds for opening the trunk. Souter would treat that investigative examination as a search requiring an independent lawful basis beyond the speeding detention. *Place* supplies a special privacy distinction only when the method is genuinely confined to contraband; sufficient reliability for probable cause does not establish the absolute informational exemption asserted here. *Jacobsen* concerned a test of material already lawfully obtained, whereas this examination began an investigative process directed at a closed enclosure. *Kyllo* supports scrutiny of acquisition of concealed information, and *Edmond* requires authority for the actual intrusion. Souter accepts the individual reliability finding but rejects its use to settle constitutional classification. He would affirm suppression and also joins Ginsburg's separate seizure ground.

**Ginsburg, dissenting, joined by Souter:** A traffic seizure is limited in scope as well as time. The compulsory canine investigation materially expands that seizure from traffic enforcement to a drug inquiry, even though the accepted duration remains lawful. *Robinette* preserves the limited authority conferred by the traffic encounter, *Edmond* distinguishes ordinary crime investigation from an independently justified seizure, and *Knowles* rejects treating a citation as general search authority. Ginsburg agrees that probable-cause reliability does not by itself establish an all-purpose contraband-only exemption, but her sufficient ground concerns the scope of this seizure. She would require articulable drug suspicion for the added investigation and affirm the Illinois judgment. Her agreement with the limited privacy objection is not a whole-opinion join in Souter's separate search rationale.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Illinois courts to apply the corrected federal rule to the accepted lawful-stop premises and resolve genuinely preserved independent grounds. If no independent defect remains, the absence of separate drug suspicion supplies no basis for suppression.

## Source Notes

No. 03-923; 543 U.S. 405. Judgment below: 207 Ill. 2d 504, 802 N.E.2d 202. U.S. Const., amend. IV. The granted question accepts lawful traffic duration and absence of independent drug suspicion. The trial court found this alert sufficiently reliable for probable cause.


## johnson-v-california_merits_2005-02-23.md



## Event

Johnson v. California, No. 03-636. Argued November 2, 2004; decided February 23, 2005. On certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 321 F.3d 791. The question is whether California's express racial assignment of reception-center cellmates is governed by the common governmental racial-classification standard or by Turner's general prison-regulation framework. Johnson repeatedly experienced the policy after transfers; the reception and classification period ordinarily lasted roughly sixty days.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment and remands for application of the common substantial-justification standard and separate adjudication of the damages and prospective-relief branches.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens, who would hold the categorical policy unconstitutional now and remand for remaining relief, and Scalia and Thomas, who would affirm under Turner.

The lower court must evaluate the policy's coverage, duration, evidentiary support, burdens and concrete practicable alternatives under the government's burden. Further evidence is required only for genuinely unresolved material issues. Damages require separate determinations of individual conduct, causation and conduct-date immunity. Prospective relief requires current exposure, proper responsible officials and the statutory remedy findings. If the policy fails, relief must correct its operation for the persons it governs unless California establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation, consistently with §3626.

## Opinion Topology

## Holdings

### The State must substantially justify express racial cell assignments

**Holding and operative rule:** An express racial prison-housing classification must satisfy the common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives that offer comparable effectiveness with materially less racial disadvantage. Custody makes credible security experience, urgency and genuinely incomplete information relevant within that inquiry, while a protective purpose, general racial assumptions or administrative convenience alone cannot satisfy it.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the common government burden for express racial treatment. Oneonta makes deliberate racial selection subject to that burden even when officials assert a benign explanation. Preventing substantiated violence is an important lawful objective, but equal separation of racial groups does not establish the fit of the means chosen. Turner gives operational judgment substantial weight in the prison matters it governs; it does not replace the more specific racial-classification rule. Overton demonstrates why actual security conditions and professional experience matter, without converting them into an exemption. The State must explain both the information deficit for new arrivals and repeated treatment of transferees about whom information already exists. Integration elsewhere and available individualized information are relevant comparisons, not conclusive answers. The standard demands a practical supported assessment, rather than a commissioned study, idealized alternative or requirement to adopt the least restrictive imaginable means. Supported emergency precautions are evaluated within that same rule.

### The policy's validity must be decided under the correct burden

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment sustaining the reception policy under materially more deferential review must be vacated for application of the common standard to the actual policy and record. Additional factfinding serves genuine material disputes about that application; the court must resolve issues the existing record already permits it to decide.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies the six-Justice judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit's Turner analysis did not require California to justify the racial policy's actual coverage and burdens under the governing rule. An ordinary reception period of roughly sixty days does not itself establish either necessity or a universal duration limit. The record permits resolution of the governing legal issue while leaving disputed security fit for its first adjudication under the proper burden. Stevens persuasively identifies the significance of repeated segregation of known transferees and integrated activities, but the Court does not regard those comparisons as resolving every material operational question. Remand calls for a decision under the existing evidence where possible, and focused evidence where a genuine dispute remains. Further proceedings must resolve the supported disputes under that burden. Scalia and Thomas's operational concerns receive weight under the common rule without shifting the burden to the prisoner to disprove every conceivable danger.

### Damages and prospective correction require independent adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Individual damages depend on actionable conduct, causal responsibility and the qualified-immunity law governing when each defendant acted; prospective correction depends on continuing exposure and a responsible current official. Under 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1), a prison decree must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation, use the least intrusive means necessary, and give substantial weight to adverse effects on public safety or operation of the criminal justice system; relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violate state or local law, is permissible only if federal law requires it to correct the violation, it is necessary, and no other relief will correct the violation.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls these independent requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson supply the conduct-date notice inquiry preserved by Saucier and Brosseau. Existing law may make an application obvious without a factual twin; today's merits rule cannot itself supply retrospective notice. Conversely, a damages defense cannot dispose of a properly supported claim for prospective correction. Section 3626 supplies remedy requirements distinct from the common constitutional merits standard. Its least-intrusiveness rule governs the decree, not the State's merits showing. If the common practice is unlawful, correction must reach the persons it governs unless a narrower remedy fully cures the violation, with the statute's necessity and findings independently satisfied. The district court must identify responsible officials and actual continuing exposure before fashioning relief.

## Precedent Treatment

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña is applied to express prison-housing classifications: its common substantial-justification burden governs actual racial treatment.
- Brown v. City of Oneonta is applied: benign purpose does not remove deliberate racial selection from that burden.
- Turner v. Safley is distinguished as the source of general prison-regulation review; its deference does not replace the governing racial-classification rule.
- Overton v. Bazzetta retains force for the prison matters it decides and for the practical relevance of institutional evidence.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton supply the individual conduct-date immunity inquiry.
- Saucier v. Katz and Brosseau v. Haugen preserve separate adjudication of the constitutional merits and the existing personal defense, including obvious applications of existing law.

## Law After Decision

Express racial reception-cell assignments, including assignments after repeated transfers, require the State's substantial justification under the common rule. Courts assess the actual coverage, duration, burdens, security evidence and practicable alternatives while respecting supported urgent conditions. The reception policy's validity remains for adjudication under that burden. Personal damages and prospective correction each require their own factual and legal predicates, and §3626 independently limits a prison decree.

## Separate Writings

Stevens agrees that the common standard governs and that personal immunity and prospective relief are distinct. He would hold the categorical policy invalid on this record because its racial proxy and repeated application to known transferees remain insufficiently justified even under Turner. Integrated activities and available individual information expose the inadequacy of the asserted blanket need. Adarand and Oneonta strengthen that conclusion. A changed review standard does not itself entitle the State to another merits opportunity. He distinguishes genuine emergency precautions and would remand only for personal defenses and appropriate relief, rather than enter an immediate damages award or an unsupported decree.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, would affirm under Turner. Confined reception cells, limited monitoring, changing gang affiliations and incomplete information make this temporary classification an operational security judgment reasonably related to protecting inmates and staff. General racial-classification decisions outside confinement do not silently displace the specific prison line. The integrated activities and existing information about transferees make the application closer but do not negate the particular risk in cells. Their argument does not validate racial allocation for its own sake or permanent system-wide segregation. If the Court imposes the common standard, they would permit genuine factual application rather than invalidate the policy immediately, and would apply the ordinary independent remedy restrictions. That alternative position does not change their primary affirmance vote.

## Procedure After Action

The district court must set a prompt schedule for resolving the relevant evidence and any supported interim-relief request under the applicable statutory conditions. It must identify current responsible officials and continuing exposure, decide any personal damages defenses and causal responsibility separately, and require a compliant housing practice if the common policy fails. Any injunction must specify permitted security exceptions and compliance dates consistently with §3626, so that it corrects the established violation through a workable decree.

## roper-v-simmons_merits_2005-03-01.md



## Event

Roper v. Simmons, No. 03-633. Argued October 13, 2004; decided March 1, 2005. On certiorari to the Supreme Court of Missouri, 112 S.W.3d 397, which granted state collateral relief from Simmons's death sentence. The question is whether the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit execution for a crime committed before the offender's eighteenth birthday and whether Stanford v. Kentucky should continue to govern that question. Simmons committed the murder at seventeen.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the Missouri judgment replacing the death sentence with a noncapital sentence.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–3. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.

Simmons's conviction and sentence of life imprisonment without eligibility for probation, parole or release except by gubernatorial action remain effective. Missouri must maintain that noncapital custody basis and remove authority to execute him for this offense. His established offense-date age supplies the categorical ground for relief.

## Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, joined by THOMAS, J.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports affirmance | Complete categorical rule, precedent treatment and remedy |
| Dissent | O'Connor | None | Would reverse and remand | Accepts evolving standards and independent proportionality; rejects this categorical extension |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Would reverse and remand | Methodological and domestic-evidence grounds for retaining Stanford |

## Holdings

### Offenders under eighteen at the time of the crime are ineligible for death

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit the death penalty for an offense committed before the offender's eighteenth birthday. Age at the offense controls; later adulthood, the crime's severity or planning, and an assessment of exceptional individual maturity do not create an exception.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls every holding and its explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** Atkins supplies the method: domestic enactment and practice inform proportionality, and diminished culpability coupled with unreliable capital selection can require a category even when mitigation is available. Thirty States exclude this punishment—twelve reject capital punishment generally and eighteen exclude under-eighteen offenses by legislation or judicial interpretation. These are not thirty age-specific enactments. Six States have executed juvenile offenders since Stanford, only three in the preceding decade. Five States have changed since Stanford, four legislatively and one judicially. The movement is slower than the change considered in Atkins, but the combined exclusion and concentrated use support the age boundary.

Immaturity and irresponsible judgment, susceptibility to pressure and limited control of circumstances, and a character still developing reduce the class's culpability. Those features weaken retribution and deterrence as grounds for execution. Planned cruelty can establish serious criminal responsibility without reliably identifying an adolescent with the settled culpability claimed to justify death. O'Connor's variability objection therefore identifies a central selection problem rather than a dependable exception. Individual mitigation cannot reliably separate an exceptionally mature, irredeemably culpable juvenile from the protected class. Eighteen is an administrable legal boundary, not a finding that all development ends on a birthday. The domestic evidence and the Court's proportionality judgment together support the rule.

### Stanford is displaced and the substantive restriction governs lawful review

**Holding and operative rule:** Stanford v. Kentucky is overruled insofar as it permits execution for offenses committed at sixteen or seventeen. Because the new rule removes a class from death eligibility, a death sentence within that class must be replaced through lawful noncapital sentencing procedures in nonfinal cases and final cases reached through an available review channel, preserving valid convictions and independent procedural requirements.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice opinion controls both the express precedent change and the application to Simmons's state collateral judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Stanford relied on then-prevailing domestic evidence and the adequacy of individualized mitigation. The developments since that decision, the reduced culpability of youth and the continuing unreliability of identifying an adult-like juvenile for irreversible punishment undermine those premises. Atkins explains why available mitigation can fail to cure a class-wide selection defect; it does not itself decide the age question or equate youth with its distinct disability definition. Retaining a rule that permits execution of this class cannot be justified by an entitlement to continue that punishment. The change leaves convictions and lawful noncapital punishment in place. Thompson's under-sixteen outcome remains consistent with the broader age boundary now adopted. This Court directly reconsiders Stanford in the case presenting that question. The substantive restriction supplies Simmons's relief in his existing collateral proceeding and governs other qualifying judgments when lawfully reached. Offense-date age, rather than an individual maturity hearing, determines its application.

## Precedent Treatment

- Stanford v. Kentucky is overruled to the extent it permits death for offenses committed before eighteen; that permission no longer has force.
- Atkins v. Virginia is extended in its categorical proportionality method, including domestic evidence, culpability, penal purposes and unreliable selection. Its separate disability definition and procedures retain their own scope.
- Thompson v. Oklahoma remains consistent with the result; its under-sixteen protection is encompassed by the present under-eighteen rule.

## Law After Decision

Offense-date age below eighteen categorically excludes a person from capital punishment. Courts applying the rule must reliably resolve a genuinely disputed birth date and, through their lawful review and sentencing procedures, replace a qualifying death sentence with authorized noncapital punishment. The category applies to final and nonfinal judgments reached through those procedures. Serious criminal responsibility, valid convictions and independent lawful punishment retain their force.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor accepts evolving standards and independent proportionality review, as she did in Atkins, but finds this extension insufficiently supported. The changes since Stanford are fewer and slower, continuing authorization remains substantial, and older adolescents vary in maturity. In her view, the evidence does not establish that individualized consideration cannot identify an offender sufficiently culpable for death. Atkins's distinct disability category and its reliable-status inquiry therefore do not decide this age question. She would preserve Stanford's eligibility rule, give youth its substantial mitigating force and return the case for lawful state proceedings. Her position does not reject independent proportionality judgment or treat legislatures as its exclusive source.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, objects both to the Court's method and to its account of domestic evidence. Stanford leaves the disputed eligibility rule in force unless the Constitution supplies a sound basis for change. The twelve States rejecting all capital punishment cannot be counted as twelve age-specific enactments; limited movement and continuing permission among capital States do not establish the claimed rejection. Rare use may reflect careful selection rather than repudiation, and grave planned crimes challenge a universal account of insufficient culpability. They oppose using independent judicial penal judgment to create the category and regard the showing as inadequate even within the prevailing evolving-standards inquiry. Atkins remains binding for its actual disability class, and youth must still receive its lawful mitigating consideration. They would reverse the federal categorical ruling and remand for lawful state proceedings, rather than direct execution.

## Procedure After Action

The affirmed Missouri judgment governs Simmons's custody and bars execution for this offense. His noncapital sentence remains subject to its stated gubernatorial-action qualification. Other qualifying death judgments receive the substantive age rule through their existing lawful review channels and the noncapital correction authorized by governing law.

112 S.W.3d 397; Stanford v. Kentucky, 492 U.S. 361; Thompson v. Oklahoma, 487 U.S. 815; Atkins v. Virginia. The domestic counts distinguish general abolition, age-specific exclusion, legislative change, judicial change and actual executions. The state judgment specifies the qualification for gubernatorial action in the noncapital sentence.


## united-states-v-booker_merits_2005-01-12.md



## Event

United States v. Booker, No. 04-104, consolidated with United States v. Fanfan, No. 04-105. Argued October 4, 2004; decided January 12, 2005. Booker is on writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 375 F.3d 508, which set aside his enhanced sentence. ## Public Action

## Holdings

### I. Facts increasing a binding punishment boundary require element protections

**Controlling proposition:** A fact legally necessary to increase a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must receive applicable grand-jury charging protection and be proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt, validly admitted as an element, or determined beyond a reasonable doubt after a lawful jury waiver. This rule governs present-offense and necessary recidivist facts, including those that increase a binding Federal Sentencing Guidelines range; judicial selection within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions remains permissible.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the established punishment-authorization inquiry. Apprendi protects facts that authorize greater punishment. Angle already applies that rule to binding federal Guidelines ceilings and floors, Harris confirms the mandatory-floor component, Gatewood rejects a categorical recidivism exception, and Blakely identifies the range authorized by a verdict or valid plea admission. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner range discretionary. Mistretta sustains the Commission's institutional arrangement, not an exemption from independent constitutional guarantees; Witte and Watts preserve ordinary assessment only within otherwise authorized punishment. The argument that Commission rules permit ordinary judicial enhancement therefore conflicts with the existing rule rather than distinguishing it. Nor does the dissent's concern about judicial administration justify withdrawing safeguards from facts that legally compel greater punishment. An unchallenged assertion in a sentencing report is not by itself a valid element admission. A jury waiver retains the proof burden and the applicable charge boundary. Booker's additional judicial findings raised a 210-to-262-month calculation to 360 months to life and produced a 360-month sentence. Those findings cannot authorize that increase. Fanfan properly refused to use the comparable unsupported findings. The stated ranges describe the sentencing records; other disputed inputs must satisfy their own applicable requirements.

### II. Constitutional applications preserve the binding statutory system

**Controlling proposition:** The Federal Sentencing Guidelines remain binding to the extent their ranges rest on constitutionally established facts; an unsupported enhancement must be withheld rather than used to replace Congress's mandatory system with an advisory one. Section 3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and the enacted review and remand provisions remain effective in their lawful applications, with judicial discretion exercised within the independently authorized range and lawful statutory departures.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for this holding and the mandate in Holding III. Ginsburg joins Part I only.

**Controlling explanation:** Alaska Airlines v. Brock asks whether the enacted remainder can function consistently with Congress's design. Severability can remove unconstitutional applications as well as invalid provisions; the necessary question is what cannot lawfully operate. Here a binding system can operate on facts established with the required protections. Section 3553(b)(1) commands the applicable kind and range of sentence unless an aggravating or mitigating circumstance of a kind or degree not adequately considered by the Commission warrants a different sentence. Adequate consideration is assessed from the Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. Those departure conditions remain subject to the constitutional authorization rule.

The Government and the remedial dissent emphasize real-conduct sentencing, information available to judges, charging-driven disparities and unequal effects of upward and downward adjustments. Those concerns identify consequences of constitutional safeguards, not an inability of the enacted mandatory command to govern lawful applications. The Court cannot preserve every feature that conflicts with those safeguards, but it can preserve Congress's binding directions and actual appellate-review provisions. Removing both mandatory force and the review structure would substitute a materially different sentencing policy. Angle demonstrated the legal availability of protected factfinding within binding ranges while expressly leaving this system-level remedy open; today's holding answers that reservation. The presumption favoring lawful surviving applications is not overcome by the asserted administrative inconvenience.

### III. The two records require different relief under the retained framework

**Controlling proposition:** Booker's appellate sentence vacatur is affirmed, and his case is remanded for a replacement sentence within a lawfully established binding range, subject to correction of independently established calculation errors. Fanfan's refusal to enhance and resulting 78-month sentence are affirmed against the asserted entitlement to use the excluded findings; any independently preserved calculation issue remains for ordinary disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Booker received the unsupported increased punishment; Fanfan did not. Preserving the binding statute therefore requires correcting Booker's sentence while sustaining Fanfan's refusal to impose the enhancement. Blakely and Gatewood distinguish sentence relief from authority to institute new aggravated proceedings. This mandate supplies no automatic enhancement jury or new charging authority: further proceedings require an independent procedural source and compliance with charging, proof, waiver and finality constraints. Booker's convictions remain intact. Griffith governs application to nonfinal cases on direct review. For a forfeited claim, Olano separately requires error, plainness, an ordinary showing of effect on substantial rights, and a discretionary finding that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. An assumed identical sentence cannot replace those inquiries or make an unauthorized binding range lawful. Existing deadlines, retroactivity rules and collateral-review gateways govern final judgments separately.

- United States v. Gatewood: reaffirmed as rejecting the categorical recidivism exemption and preserving applicable charging and proof requirements and independent authority for further proceedings.

## Law After Decision

Federal sentencing continues under binding Guidelines supported by constitutionally established facts. The existing rule covers any necessary present-offense or recidivist fact increasing a binding ceiling or floor, with applicable charging, jury, admission and waiver requirements. The system-level remedy now preserves §3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and the enacted review and remand structure. Unsupported enhancements must be withheld, and judicial choice proceeds within lawful authority. The rule governs applicable nonfinal cases on direct review; final judgments remain subject to their existing collateral-review regime.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, dissents from the constitutional holding. Their earlier Angle positions distinguish the statutory offense limits from rules regulating judicial selection within them. Mistretta's institutional sentencing arrangement and the established judicial sentencing role support reconsidering the extension of element protections to those internal constraints. Breyer's separate Part I explains his additional constitutional position. He finds no coherent logical distinction between a fact raising a mandatory minimum and one raising a maximum, yet opposes extending the element requirement to the sentencing system in either form. That disagreement concerns the constitutional allocation itself rather than an exception consistent with Angle or Harris.

In Part II, Breyer is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Given the Court's constitutional holding, they would sever §3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and necessarily incompatible cross-references, preserving guided advisory sentencing. Congress's integrated arrangement relied on judicial assessment of actual conduct and sentencing information beyond charging choices; compulsory ranges rebuilt around jury findings would create disparities and unequal upward and downward operation. They view advisory guidance as the closer surviving statutory design. Courts would remain required to consider the Guidelines and policy statements, offense and offender circumstances, lawful alternatives, seriousness, respect for law, just punishment, deterrence, public protection, effective correctional needs, avoidance of unwarranted disparities and restitution. The sentence would have to be sufficient but no greater than necessary for the applicable statutory purposes, accompanied by reasons and reasonableness review. They would affirm the setting aside of Booker's sentence and resentence him under that system, but vacate Fanfan's sentence for advisory reconsideration even though he suffered no constitutional enhancement error. Ginsburg joins the Court's constitutional rule and this remedial position; the other three retain their constitutional objections.
