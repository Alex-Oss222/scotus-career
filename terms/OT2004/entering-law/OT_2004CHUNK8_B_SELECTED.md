#### United States v. Aguilar

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-270.
**Decided:** June 21, 1995.

##### Disclosure of a known application and obstructive purpose

**Holding:** Section 2232(c) requires knowledge of an actual federal application or authorization under chapter 119, purpose to obstruct its interception, and notice or attempted notice of that possible interception to a person. Expiration of the identified authorization does not alone establish a defense; the statute does not require successful interference or that authorization remain formally in force at disclosure.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s §2232(c) portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices support this construction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An actual application or authorization, rather than an imaginary application, is necessary. No knowledge of subsequent orders is imputed; the warning need not reach only a target, and official notice duties do not extend interception authority. Liability decades later and limits following official inventory notice remain open. Gaudin's jury-element allocation is preserved.

#### United States v. Aguilar

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-270.
**Decided:** June 21, 1995.

##### A known nexus to pending judicial work

**Holding:** Section 1503 requires a relationship in time, causation or logic between the endeavor and a pending judicial or grand-jury proceeding, including knowledge that the conduct is likely to affect that proceeding. An unsuccessful endeavor can qualify, but false statements to investigators do not suffice merely because they might later testify.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg’s §1503 portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Breyer; six Justices adopt this statutory nexus and application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The conduct must have the natural and probable relation to interference, with knowledge that it is likely to affect the proceeding. No categorical subpoena condition, wholesale section 1512 repeal of witness obstruction or privilege to lie is adopted. Direct grand-jury lies and knowingly channeled statements remain distinct.

#### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

#### United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative, 532 U.S. 483

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-151.
**Decided:** May 14, 2001.

##### Whether the Cooperative has a medical-necessity defense under the Controlled Substances Act

**Holding:** The Act provides no medical-necessity exception for the Cooperative's prohibited marijuana manufacture, distribution or possession with intent to distribute. Neither medical recommendation nor state permission supplies federal authorization, even if the recipient meets the lower court's medical criteria.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, unanimous among eight participants; Breyer takes no part.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This statutory holding is no finding that cannabis cannot medically benefit a patient. Individual possession, individual-patient necessity and injunctions, and necessity under materially different statutes remain outside the case. Constitutional avoidance supplies no textual exception, but preserved Commerce Clause, substantive-due-process and patient-liberty objections remain unadjudicated on their own requirements.

#### Alexander v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1526.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### Whether this postconviction RICO forfeiture constitutes a traditional prior restraint

**Holding:** A postconviction RICO forfeiture of identified assets linked to adjudicated racketeering does not constitute a traditional prior restraint merely because it impairs the owner's future ability to publish, where it neither forbids future publication nor requires permission to publish. That classification does not dispose of an independent First Amendment challenge to removal of protected works.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; six Justices adopt this proposition. Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy do not join the classification. The independent First Amendment question has a different coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The classification neither licenses a future-publication ban nor permits seizure of protected stock on probable cause alone. It does not resolve the independent challenge to removal of lawful works or immunize publishing assets from neutral sanctions.

##### Whether valid convictions and the absence of a traditional prior restraint suffice to sustain forfeiture of lawful expressive inventory

**Holding:** Where obscenity predicates produce forfeiture of an expressive enterprise and removal of lawful inventory, the judgment sustaining that forfeiture cannot rest solely on the existence of valid convictions and the absence of a traditional prior restraint. The judgment must be vacated for an independent First Amendment examination of the challenged expressive burden, distinguishing the judicial forfeiture from subsequent administrative destruction and confining correction to a reviewable injury and available relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy; five Justices expressly adopt this limited rule and remand. Their separate positions do not establish a majority rule either categorically immunizing expressive inventory or adopting a comprehensive scrutiny framework for every postconviction forfeiture.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court leaves the ultimate constitutional treatment of the distinct assets unresolved. Neither categorical immunity for expressive inventory nor a comprehensive scrutiny framework commands a majority. Judicial forfeiture, subsequent administrative destruction, statutory nexus, causation, reviewable injury and available relief remain distinct. The Court neither adopts a content-selection rationale from R.A.V. nor enlarges its entered overbreadth rule.

#### Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-517, 93-527 and 93-539.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### The State may not select a denomination as the recipient of general governmental authority

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause forbids a State deliberately to allocate general civil authority to a community selected as a religious community, even when the immediate recipients are elected public lay officials rather than clergy. Chapter 748 violates that rule because its design and context use denominational membership as the organizing basis of the special school district; religious homogeneity or coincidence with an existing village line alone would not establish the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II introduction and II-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg: five Justices. This is an independently sufficient ground. O'Connor does not join that portion, and Kennedy's separate judgment rationale is not counted as a join.

**Independent alternative holding:** Chapter 748 independently violates religious neutrality by conferring an anomalously particular benefit of general school-government power on this selected denominational community without neutral terms or dependable assurance of equal treatment for comparable communities. The defect follows from this enactment's design and context, not a rule that every special act is invalid, every general statute is neutral, or an unproved finding that another religious community has already been refused.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This is an independently sufficient alternative ground; Kennedy expressly declines it.

##### Accommodation preserves educational access but does not cure this allocation

**Holding:** A State may accommodate religiously grounded needs beyond what free exercise independently requires, but accommodation does not authorize this denominational allocation of general governmental power. Affirmance leaves the children's statutory special-education rights and lawful neutral local public services intact, without commanding a particular placement, validating the adequacy of earlier services or prescribing a replacement district.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, Part II-C and the implementing limits in Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. This holding rejects the accommodation defense and defines the remedy's limits; Kennedy reaches the judgment on his separate ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling rationale is synthesized under Marks from Kennedy's different religious-boundary theory or other separate writings. The Court does not replace Lemon, overrule Aguilar or Ball, create an aid-merits holding in Zobrest, revise Shaw, decide the state constitutional issue, or find religious voter qualifications, religious instruction, mandatory assimilation, a denied request by another faith, permanent religious demographics or a free-exercise entitlement to a separate district. It neither selects individual placements nor requires elimination of culturally responsive secular instruction.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

#### Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-880.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### The decree as confined is not categorically content based

**Holding:** As authoritatively confined to named responsible parties and actual concert with notice, this injunction addresses established interference with access, traffic, medical care and residential privacy rather than abortion advocacy as such; defendant-specific selection does not make it categorically content or viewpoint based. Each restraint still requires independent constitutional scrutiny, and a materially different viewpoint-based application receives no approval.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A(2), joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this application while accepting the separate actual-concert principle.

##### A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary

**Holding:** A content-neutral injunction burdening protected expression must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve the corresponding significant governmental interest. Its precise terms require a closer fit than ordinary review of generally applicable neutral time, place and manner rules; neither an injunction label nor past misconduct permits suppression of unrelated lawful advocacy.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.

##### The qualified public access arrangement survives

**Holding:** The public portion of the 36-foot clinic clearance, with its retained south shoulder, east shoulder subject to the five-foot setback and no-embankment rule, and prompt-crossing exception, burdens no more speech than necessary on this record to secure driveway access and traffic after narrower obstruction commands failed. It remains enforceable at that qualified scope, without creating a generally permissible clinic-buffer distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five on geographic fit. Stevens adds a vote to leave the arrangement intact only against the categorical challenge; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the expressive exclusion.

##### The private-property extension lacks the necessary remedial connection

**Holding:** The north/west extension of the 36-foot exclusion cannot suppress otherwise lawful expression on adjoining private land without a demonstrated connection between that full exclusion and the access or operational harm to be remedied. Florida's approval of that extension is reversed; removing the government's expressive restriction neither authorizes trespass nor adjudicates owners' property rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on their stricter grounds; Stevens declines distinct geographic review.

##### The qualified medical-noise restriction survives

**Holding:** Paragraph (4)'s noise restriction is valid in its medical-operation context from 7:30 a.m. through noon, Monday–Saturday, during surgical procedures and recovery periods, as to the listed and other sounds within earshot of patients inside the clinic. Its affirmance rests on the treatment interference established here, not disagreement with a message, and adds no decibel, intent or newly drafted volume threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse on strict direct-restraint and adequate-unlawful-predicate grounds, despite accepting fit under the Court's assumed standard; Stevens declines merits.

##### The prohibition on observable images is too broad

**Holding:** The prohibition on all images observable to patients inside the clinic, even within the stated morning surgery-and-recovery periods, burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be removed. Patient vulnerability or distress at a message does not establish a general power to prevent visible advocacy; independently lawful threat or intrusion relief remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens declines merits.

##### Peaceful initial approach cannot depend on advance invitation throughout 300 feet

**Holding:** Paragraph (5)'s requirement of an indication of interest before any physical approach to a person seeking clinic services within 300 feet burdens more speech than necessary and must be removed. This holding protects peaceful initial advocacy, not a right to accompany, encircle, surround, threaten, obstruct or force communication upon a person who declines it, and approves no universal application of disputed harassment or abuse terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-E, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens dissents from reversal of this physical-approach condition.

##### Residential privacy does not justify this territorial exclusion

**Holding:** The 300-foot residential prohibition on approaching, congregating, picketing, patrolling, demonstrating and amplification sweeps beyond the supported protection of a targeted home and must be removed. Its advance-invitation qualification does not restore general neighborhood expression; distinct lawful obstruction, trespass, threat and properly focused residential protections are preserved without selecting a replacement distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-F, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six on this bounded ground. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal under their stricter direct-restraint account; the judgment is unanimous.

#### Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1065.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

##### Fixed access restrictions satisfy the governing injunction standard on this record

**Holding:** A content-neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve its corresponding significant interest. These fixed fifteen-foot demonstration restrictions at identified doorways, driveway entrances and parking-access points of covered facilities—all facilities where abortions are performed in the Western District of New York—with nonthreatening initial counseling by no more than two counselors per approached person or group, satisfy that requirement on the supported obstruction and crowding record; fifteen feet is not a universal safe harbor.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's fixed-access holding is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six Justices. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also join the responsible-party, notice and actual-concert boundary, giving that distinct proposition nine votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decree regulates demonstration, not mere presence; the counseling limit is per approached person or group, not two counselors for an entire zone. Substantial temporary-order compliance does not erase supported access interference, and no requirement to disobey a speech-neutral predecessor or exhaust every narrower possibility is adopted. The temporary order already contained personal-distance and counseling restrictions.

##### The fixed-zone counseling exception may end after a communicated refusal

**Holding:** Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, the allowance for nonthreatening counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates verbally or nonverbally that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; the condition establishes neither silence everywhere nor a mobile personal radius beyond the fixed zone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unexpressed unwillingness does not trigger departure. This condition is an exception to independently valid fixed access relief, not a general unwilling-listener veto or captive-audience rule.

##### The moving-person restriction burdens more speech than the record warrants

**Holding:** The injunction's fifteen-foot demonstration boundary moving with each person seeking access to or leaving a covered facility burdens more protected speech than necessary under Madsen and must be struck, even with its initial counseling exception and communicated-refusal qualification. This record-specific holding does not create a right to threaten, touch, obstruct, crowd or coercively pursue anyone, or invalidate every precisely supported separation rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The initial counseling and refusal qualifications do not cure uncertainty from moving listeners and overlapping zones. The Court invents neither a travel cap nor an overlap rule to sustain the actual restraint. Precisely supported, materially different conduct relief remains open.

##### The moving-vehicle restriction independently fails

**Holding:** The fifteen-foot demonstration radius moving with vehicles seeking access to or leaving covered facilities likewise burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be struck. The decision preserves traffic safety and fixed access protection without deciding the validity of a materially different, precisely supported vehicle restriction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

##### Removal of the moving predicate also removes the dependent retreat distance

**Holding:** Once paragraph 1(b)'s moving-person and moving-vehicle restrictions are invalidated, paragraph 1(c)'s cross-reference cannot recreate an automatic fifteen-foot moving distance outside valid fixed zones. That dependent operation must be removed on remand; independently lawful conduct prohibitions remain, and the Court neither commands a substitute distance nor decides every cessation condition considered apart from the invalidated predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power

**Holding:** RFRA's general application to State and local laws cannot be sustained under §5 because it substitutes its substantial-burden, compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means standard across the field for the constitutional boundary retained in Smith, without the required enforcement relationship. The Fifth Circuit's contrary power ruling is reversed, and the RFRA claim against the City must be dismissed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not reconsider Smith or decide the permit denial's neutrality, motive, substantial burden or justification. Federal RFRA, including its rule concerning later federal enactments, and other possible congressional powers remain outside the disposition.

##### III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments

**Holding:** Church of Scientology and Swanner did not decide the constitutional power question and therefore do not establish §5 authority for RFRA's universal State and local application. Their prospective statutory applications cannot govern future State and local RFRA claims resting only on that power after this decision, but their completed judgments retain finality and their independent grounds and statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket reopening, retrospective recovery or reconsideration of independent constitutional or state-law grounds follows. Particular transactional or remedial reliance claims require their own lawful source.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

#### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 96-5354; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding:** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer also join Part II-B.

##### II. Necessary inclusion in a valid interstate scheme sustains the taking prohibition; an independent externality route remains available

**Holding:** Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Court identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two; Congress may not establish that relationship merely by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute. Where Congress has established an integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, it may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system. The Constitution does not require separate proof that every species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce, or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect if left unprotected. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A's necessary-inclusion rule and its application are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part II-A while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

##### III. The Government identifies the regime, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship; courts decide those boundaries independently

**Holding:** The Government must identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two. Courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. The Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision: the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** For necessary inclusion, the relationship appears from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis. Here the Act's text and structure identify each element: its valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, the taking class section 9(a)(1)(B) actually covers, and that class's function in preserving the populations the trade provisions regulate and keeping those provisions enforceable. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. No universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is imposed. The obligation does not presume that every individual act must cross a state boundary or produce a separately substantial effect.

##### IV. The judgment sustaining the challenged applications is affirmed

**Holding:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9(a)(1)(B) to the challenged habitat modification against this Commerce Clause objection is affirmed. Part II-A resolves the constitutional question presented, so no Commerce Clause evidentiary remand follows. The builders receive no constitutional exemption from section 9 and no injunction against its enforcement on the Commerce Clause theory; the Government may enforce the statute subject to its actual elements and exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sweet Home requires significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability remain required; speculative future harm or bare but-for linkage does not suffice. An omission requires an independently existing legal duty, and this decision creates no general habitat-maintenance duty. No actual injury, liability or permit entitlement is newly found. Otherwise lawful development alone is not an exemption. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permission remains confined to taking incidental to, rather than the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. A conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization and mitigation measures and funding, alternatives considered and reasons rejected, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. The substation's existing permit decides no entitlement concerning the separate intersection/corridor controversy.

#### Gibbs v. Babbitt

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 99-1218; judgment below, 214 F.3d 483. No separate Supreme Court docket is supplied.
**Decided:** March 21, 2001.

##### The regulated class and interstate connection for noncommercial taking of experimental red wolves

**Holding:** The enacted class is protected-wildlife taking without a commercial element. Commercial motives, earlier interstate transport, projected tourism, scientific value and potential trade do not establish the required interstate connection either separately or by accumulating incomplete connections; aggregation measures effects only after an eligible connection is identified.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five direct joins. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this restrictive connection premise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A commercial landowner does not make taking itself commercial. The decision does not categorically immunize all noneconomic conduct or insert a commercial element into the regulation.

##### The experimental-population taking rule is an integral part of the Act's valid interstate scheme

**Holding:** Congress may protect an experimental population established under Section 10(j) of the Endangered Species Act by prohibiting unauthorized taking on private land, where the prohibition is an integral and reasonably adapted component of the Act's valid interstate species-regulation and conservation system. The Constitution does not require proof that each protected animal crossed state lines, that the particular landowner acted commercially, or that the population independently produces substantial interstate economic effects. The red-wolf regulation falls within that permissible federal authority. This conclusion does not enlarge the rule beyond its lawful statutory terms, authorize taking not covered by an exception, eliminate a valid landowner defense, or determine a compensation claim. The challengers have not established that the regulation is unconstitutional on the Commerce Clause ground presented.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins to the holding and the affirmance. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join this application of Home Builders Part II-A while preserving their proposed broader ground for affirmance. No broader substantial-class-effects rationale controls.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Property Clause, treaty and compensation theories are outside the Commerce question. The decision does not alter the existing private-land taking rule. The reported exceptions concern taking neither intentional nor willful, defense of a person's life, a landowner's taking while wolves actually kill livestock or pets with freshly wounded or killed animals evident, an owner's nonlethal and noninjurious harassment, and taking with written approval after abandoned Service capture efforts; the reported 24-hour notification requirement remains. No exception is found to authorize future conduct or taking based merely on feared livestock danger.

#### Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 01-5373; judgment below, 323 F.3d 1062.

**Decided:** February 5, 2004

##### Whether a housing project's commercial character replaces the enacted wildlife-taking class

**Holding:** The commercial character of a housing project does not by itself establish Congress's authority to apply the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition. The Government must identify an eligible interstate mechanism fitted to the taking class Congress enacted, including the habitat-caused injury that class reaches.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stevens and Souter do not join this part.

##### Whether the listed arroyo toad application is integral to the Act's interstate wildlife scheme

**Holding:** Under National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Endangered Species Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of listed wildlife may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system, without separate proof that each species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed arroyo toad within that system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers; Rancho Viejo has not shown that the application is outside the statutory class, that the Act's interstate regulation is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Parts II and III, joined by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Home Builders's independent externality route remains available but need not be satisfied. Statutory liability still requires Sweet Home's actual killing or injury from significant habitat modification impairing essential behavior, proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability; speculative injury and bare but-for causation do not suffice, an omission requires an independent legal duty, and otherwise lawful development supplies no exemption. Section 10 permission remains governed by its own terms.

#### Klinger v. Department of Corrections

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 93-2928; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 31 F.3d 727.
**Decided:** December 4, 1995.

##### Comparison tied to the prison opportunity challenged

**Holding:** Prisoners' similarity for an equal-protection program claim must be assessed in relation to the treatment challenged. Population, security, confinement duration, feasible delivery and actual resource choices may distinguish a particular program; general differences between entire institutions cannot defeat every benefit comparison categorically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous, as are the next two Klinger holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither identity of institutions nor duplication of the larger prison's entire program mix is required. Higher spending or more programs elsewhere does not alone prove constitutional injury.

##### Actual sex classifications and purposeful discrimination in neutral program choices

**Holding:** For a program decision that actually classifies by sex in its terms or administration, the State must establish an important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between the objective and the distinction. For a genuinely sex-neutral choice, plaintiffs must prove a decision made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences for that sex; disparity and awareness alone do not establish purpose, though circumstantial proof may.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sex-separated housing does not itself classify each program choice. No written admission is required to prove discriminatory administration; no impact presumption, least-restrictive-means test or right to a chosen program follows.

##### Correction of both the deficient liability premise and categorical dismissal

**Holding:** Liability cannot stand merely on sex-separated housing and unequal programs without the necessary classification or purpose findings. Inadequate findings under that mistaken approach also do not justify dismissal under an overbroad whole-institution comparator rule; the preserved program claims require adjudication under the proper standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Title IX, independent access-to-courts claims, other male institutions excluded by the denied amendment, unpleaded funding claims, any subsequent final judgment and other-defendant judgments are not reopened. Gunter's individual responsibility and immunity, Clarke's official-capacity responsibility, damages and equitable relief remain distinct and undecided.

#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant; no universal ban on single-sex education.

##### Whether full performance of VWIL’s approved plan cures the denied opportunity

**Holding:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

##### What effective relief and State choices remain

**Holding:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No abandonment of genuine qualifications, named-person admission, closure, quota, identical programs or particular physical standard is ordered. Private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate or sex-conscious opportunity arrangement and independent Title IX claims are not decided.

#### Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall

**Docket or dockets:** District action No. CV-00-0386-RCC; judgment below, 307 F.3d 783.
**Decided:** April 28, 2003.

##### Whether necessary confidential internal handling violates bypass confidentiality

**Holding:** Judges, necessary court personnel and supervised professionals may handle bypass information confidentially for the proceeding and its lawful administration. Reasonable protection against identification is required, not an impossible guarantee against every unauthorized act; fictitious names, confidential records and enforceable nondisclosure duties matter, but a fictitious name alone does not protect every identifying fact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rationale; Scalia and Thomas join only the bounded internal-handling permission and rejection of that claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An illegal-leak possibility without demonstrated inadequacy of lawful safeguards does not justify enjoining necessary processing. Actual administration and burdens remain reviewable; an administrative label supplies no exemption.

##### What connects authorized outside access to intimate bypass information with a lawful governmental function

**Holding:** When government authorizes outside officials to inspect intimate bypass information, the permitted information, recipient audience and governmental function must have a sufficient connection, with safeguards against unnecessary use and redistribution. General eligibility to inspect other closed files or punishment for unauthorized leaks does not itself establish that connection; actual authorizing law and its limits must be determined.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Confidentiality labels and punishment of unlawful leaks do not establish lawful transfer scope. Task limitation, identity redaction where unnecessary and redistribution safeguards may suffice as the function requires. No universal agency list, audit schedule, judicial-preclearance system, least-restrictive-means rule or perfect-containment guarantee is imposed. Independent privacy coverage does not require measurable abortion abandonment.

##### When outside access triggers the complete reproductive-liberty protections

**Holding:** If authorized access causes demonstrated meaningful or severe additional interference with the protected decision or practical use of the bypass, Casey’s corresponding protections apply; absence of a substantial obstacle or completed public leak does not end the inquiry. Challengers must establish the actual duty, affected population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy withholds the residual-interference rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Previability prohibition, effective third-party veto, denial of timely necessary care, proved obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle independently fails. Other severe interference needs compelling justification and narrow tailoring; meaningful interference needs substantiated permissible reasons and fit answering an adequate practicable substantially less burdensome alternative. Reasonable predictions may suffice without a new study or cheapest-policy rule. Below meaningful interference the heightened access inquiry ends, while independent privacy persists. Fear alone does not establish a burden, and no completed leak is prerequisite to examining an actual authorization.

#### Greenville Women's Clinic v. Commissioner — qualifications and patient information

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit Nos. 01-2090 and 01-2235; judgment below, 317 F.3d 357.
**Decided:** October 27, 2003.

##### Whether medical cooperation and secular referrals confer an arbitrary private veto

**Holding:** States may require objective medical qualifications and emergency-care arrangements, but may not make protected care depend on an outsider's arbitrary, legally uncontrolled licensing consent. The separately considered medical and secular-referral provisions, in their established operation here, have not been shown to create that veto.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, unanimous on the qualification and arbitrary-control ground; this agreement does not establish unanimous assent to Casey's complete residual-interference framework.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Existing licenses evidence operation but do not require waiting for closure before challenging a supported prospective veto. A discretionary community-health-and-welfare waiver is no assured timely cure; public-hospital constraints do not automatically bind private institutions. Earlier overall-access and equal-protection judgments are outside review.

##### What permits governmental access to intimate clinical information

**Holding:** Authorized transfer of intimate clinic records to inspectors requires a sufficient connection between the regulatory need and the information, recipients, official function, scope, uses and safeguards. Courts must determine those permissions and connections, restrain established improper access or dissemination and preserve lawful confidential inspection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six reasoning joins. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh vote for vacatur and remand on a separate ground only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 44-7-310's public-identification restriction has facility-licensure, certificate-of-need, employee-licensing and court-order exceptions. Section 44-7-315 requires disclosure on written request while protecting identities of individuals present in a licensed facility. Section 44-41-60 excludes patient and physician names from registrar reports; section 44-41-360 separately requires an explicit constitutional anonymity determination absent the woman's consent. These distinct rules do not establish universal inspection secrecy. Redaction, consent and targeted follow-up are alternatives to assess where adequate, not universal preconditions. Universal compelling need is not the governing rule.

##### When inspection duties also implicate protected reproductive choice

**Holding:** Proved additional interference caused by an actual inspection obligation independently invokes Casey's complete framework. The claimant must identify the duty, affected population, attributable burden and applicable protection; relief must address the violation while preserving lawful requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part IV-B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins. Kennedy's Part IV-A join does not extend to this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A prohibited baseline invasion, effective private veto, denial of timely necessary care, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. Other severe interference requires compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; other meaningful interference requires substantiated justification and fit addressing concrete practicable substantially less burdensome alternatives. Usable exceptions and supported prospective effects matter; success for some patients cannot erase another group's demonstrated burden. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends while independent protections remain. Clinical judgment, truthful communication and timely necessary care retain protection. Identifiers or a privacy label do not presume an access burden, and the earlier overall-access judgment is not reopened.
