#### Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States, 511 U.S. 368

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-445; Jones included under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### The law of the convicting jurisdiction governs the conviction exclusion

**Holding:** Section 921(a)(20)'s direction to use the law of the jurisdiction where the proceedings occurred governs the related exclusion for expungement, set-aside, pardon, or restoration of civil rights, so state restoration alone does not remove a federal conviction as a §922(g) predicate. The enacted exclusion remains subject to its qualification that a conviction is not excluded if the pardon, expungement, or restoration of civil rights expressly provides that the person may not ship, transport, possess, or receive firearms; the Court decides which sovereign's law governs, not whether a particular federal restorative mechanism exists or has been satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous; all nine Justices join the complete rule and both affirmances.

**Material application:** State restoration alone does not remove either federal predicate. Jones's two restored West Virginia convictions remain excluded; the decision does not revive them.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court creates no federal restoration process and does not decide a particular federal pardon, the availability or requirements of §925(c) relief, the civil-rights bundle, automatic versus affirmative restoration, or whether federal habeas qualifies as a set-aside. It enters no guilt finding or independent ruling on the linked false-statement count's elements, materiality or intent.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Fourth Circuit judgments are affirmed, 9–0 each. Jones's indictment-reinstatement and remand disposition remains operative; Beecham receives no new sentencing or restoration order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): applied to reserve lenity for unresolved penal ambiguity after interpretation; competing proposed readings alone do not trigger it.
- United States v. Granderson, decided March 22, 1994: its contextual interpretive approach and residual role for lenity remain intact; its distinct sentencing ambiguity does not establish ambiguity here.
- Jarecki v. G. D. Searle & Co., 367 U.S. 303 (1961): its neighboring-terms principle supports, without mechanically compelling, the shared sovereign reference.
- Dickerson v. New Banner Institute, Inc., 460 U.S. 103 (1983): the intervening statutory choice-of-law text governs; its former federal-law definition is not used to bypass the enacted provision.

The contrary separate-clause reasoning of Geyler and Edwards is rejected on sovereign selection. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.



#### Caron v. United States, 524 U.S. 308

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-6270.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### A continuing state firearms restriction retains the conviction's federal effect

**Holding:** When a State restores civil rights but expressly retains the substantial firearm disability presented here—prohibiting handgun possession outside the home or business—the restoration does not remove the conviction under 18 U.S.C. §921(a)(20). The retained conviction has the federal consequences prescribed by the applicable firearms statutes, including for rifles and shotguns the State permits the person to possess; those statutes' independent offense and enhancement requirements still must be met.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the statutory rule, its explanation, application, limits and affirmance.

**Material application:** The convicting jurisdiction supplies restoration status; federal law determines the resulting consequences. The Massachusetts restriction retains the convictions despite state permission to possess the seized rifles and shotguns, without any individual dangerousness finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Massachusetts convictions remain countable against the restoration objection, and the First Circuit's statutory judgment is affirmed. This is not a complete Massachusetts ban on handguns: possession in the home or business remained permitted. The Court accepts the parties' restoration premise without deciding every automatic restoration mechanism. It does not reconsider original violent-felony classifications, separately adjudicate the California predicate, pronounce every firearms disability constitutional, impose a new sentence or resolve an independently cognizable issue outside the presented restoration question. Section 922(g)(1)'s prohibition and commerce predicates remain distinct from §924(e)(1)'s three qualifying convictions, separate-occasion condition and minimum fifteen-year sentence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit's statutory judgment remains effective. Any further sentencing proceedings must treat the Massachusetts convictions as retained against this §921(a)(20) objection while respecting the independently applicable offense, enhancement and procedural requirements. This Court orders no new sentence or separate constitutional disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States:** Applied to the convicting-jurisdiction selector and connected restoration text; this case decides the residual-restriction question that Beecham left open.
- **Chapman v. United States and United States v. Granderson:** Their contextual interpretation and residual role for lenity remain. The Court finds no unresolved ambiguity after examining this text and structure.
- **Dickerson v. New Banner Institute:** Congress's later state-law selector controls over its former federal conviction-definition rule. A general federal protective purpose cannot create an additional statutory predicate.
- **Love v. Pepersack:** Its personal-right and incorporation holdings remain governing; the validity of a particular firearms restriction requires its own properly presented constitutional question. Lewis's extant-felony disability remains undisturbed.
- **Staples v. United States and Bryan v. United States:** Their distinct, offense-specific knowledge rules do not supply an exception to the restoration provision or a newly adjudicated mental-state issue here.



#### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the qualified actual-verdict inquiry to its omitted statutory death-eligibility finding. The State bears the beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden, with actual-instruction, evidentiary-conflict and reliable-adjudication limits; this extension establishes no universal omitted-element or sentencing-error rule.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:**

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud no-materiality ruling | Reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Materiality is an element of the charged schemes under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 |
| Tax counts 86 and 87: preserved materiality-instruction challenge | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Ginsburg | These convictions survive this challenge; the dissenters would set them aside and permit retrial |
| Affected fraud convictions: further harmlessness review | Affirmance vacated and remanded for initial appellate review, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Count-specific inquiry under the Court's qualified Chapman rule, unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the counts; Stone-Zsela would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now |

The fraud remand decides neither harmlessness nor harmfulness of an individual affected count. A conviction failing the Government's governing showing must be set aside; retrial under correct instructions remains permissible. No insufficiency acquittal, automatic reinstatement of every conviction, release, restitution amount, or sentence calculation is ordered. Separate §1014, conspiracy, and racketeering claims remain subject to their own availability, actual dependencies, and proper proceedings; acquittals are not reopened.



#### Cleveland v. United States, 531 U.S. 12

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-804.
**Decided:** November 7, 2000.

##### Whether sovereign licensing permission is property in the State's hands

**Holding:** Authority held solely as sovereign regulator to issue, withhold, renew, regulate, or revoke a license is not State property under 18 U.S.C. section 1341. Louisiana's video-poker permission has that regulatory character; economic value to its recipient does not supply the charged State-property element, whether deception obtains initial issuance or renewal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's unanimous Opinion of the Court, including the renewal application.

**Material application:** The State transfers regulatory permission, rather than an owned commercial asset. Licensing revenue expectations and continuing power to exclude, limit transfers, or revoke do not change the object charged in the two 1994 and 1995 renewal counts into money or proprietary assets.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ruling does not deny a license's value to its holder or classify every governmental franchise. Schemes directed at actual public money, unpaid revenue, or commercial property require their own analysis. Materiality was outside the grant, and no independent honest-services theory was presented.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The two licensing-based mail-fraud convictions are set aside. On remand, the actual indictment, verdict, and judgment determine which other convictions and sentences depend on those predicates. Independent convictions and surviving independent bases remain intact; no separate tax, laundering, racketeering, or unreviewed-theory acquittal is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McNally v. United States preserves the money-or-property boundary. Carpenter v. United States protects genuine commercial intangible property without converting regulatory permission into ownership. Neder v. United States, June 10, 1999, retains materiality as a separate element and does not replace the missing property object.

##### Whether the acquisition clause independently reaches value created in the recipient's hands

**Holding:** Section 1341's false-pretenses acquisition clause describes a method of property fraud and retains the requirement that the charged scheme target property in the deceived victim's hands. Value acquired by the recipient alone cannot support the alternative license-acquisition theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's unanimous opinion directly answers this separate statutory contention.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The victim-property rule concerns the scheme's object; it does not require completed economic loss or actual reliance. It supplies no alternative ground to sustain either renewal conviction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McNally governs the statute as a whole, including the acquisition clause. Neder's separate rejection of completed-loss and actual-reliance requirements remains unchanged.



#### Artuz v. Bennett, 531 U.S. 4

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1238.
**Decided:** November 7, 2000.

##### Proper filing of a collateral application containing claims subject to mandatory relief bars

**Holding:** Section 2244(d)(2) treats a state collateral application as properly filed when its delivery and acceptance satisfy the requirements for filing that application. A claim-specific rule requiring denial of relief does not defeat proper filing; New York Criminal Procedure Law sections 440.10(2)(a) and (c) are relief rules of that kind.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Paragraph (a) requires denial of an issue previously resolved on its merits on appeal unless controlling law subsequently changes with retroactive effect. Paragraph (c) requires an adequate underlying record for appellate review and an unjustifiable failure to take or perfect a timely appeal or raise the issue on a perfected appeal. These inquiries determine entitlement to claim relief, not delivery or acceptance of the application. No finding applies either bar or its qualifications to a particular Bennett claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Clerical acceptance does not excuse an actual filing defect. Form, delivery deadlines, proper court or office, fees, and genuine filing preconditions remain distinct; the Court does not classify every rule without its terms. Whether exceptions to a filing deadline can preserve proper filing of a late application remains undecided. The absence of a written state denial is Bennett's allegation and the lower court's assumption, not this Court's finding. Actual pendency, transitional grace periods, exhaustion, procedural default, and habeas entitlement remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit is affirmed and its remand remains operative. Timeliness must be calculated without treating the two relief bars as filing defects, with open dates and defenses adjudicated in further proceedings. No writ, retrial, or release is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lindh v. Murphy, June 23, 1997, retains its provision-specific temporal construction; it neither exempts Bennett's post-enactment federal application nor answers proper filing.



#### Duncan v. Walker, 533 U.S. 167

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-121.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### Whether federal habeas pendency qualifies for section 2244(d)(2) exclusion

**Holding:** The word State in 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) modifies both postconviction review and other collateral review. A federal habeas application's pendency therefore is not excluded from the limitation period by that subsection, even when the application was timely and dismissed without prejudice because exhaustion was unestablished; a civil-rights pleading accompanying it does not change that classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct votes for both questions.

**Material application:** State modifies both forms of collateral review. The separate phrase other collateral review accommodates different state procedures and custody outside ordinary criminal convictions. A timely original federal filing, its nonmerits exhaustion dismissal, and a later application's non-successive status do not permanently satisfy the later application's deadline. Qualifying state review still requires proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim, and actual pendency.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rose v. Lundy supplies exhaustion's state/federal distinction without creating compulsory stays. Lindh v. Murphy protects its identified pre-enactment application, not all later filings about the conviction. Slack v. McDaniel preserves the exhaustion-return classification while retaining independent timing requirements. Artuz v. Bennett preserves the difference between filing conditions and claim-relief bars, without changing a federal filing's sovereign category.

##### What timeliness adjudication follows rejection of the federal-pendency exclusion

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's reinstatement cannot rest on excluding Walker's first federal proceeding under §2244(d)(2). On remand the courts must calculate timeliness without that exclusion and adjudicate any independently preserved, legally available commencement or equitable ground under the applicable law and actual record; if none makes the application timely, it must be dismissed as untimely.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct votes for both questions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 24, 1997 transitional deadline is this review's premise, not a universal commencement rule. General equitable-tolling availability and conditions, mixed-petition stays, the constitutional merits, and ultimate habeas entitlement remain undecided. Section 2254(b)(2) permits, but does not compel, merits denial of an unexhausted application. No universal warning duty, election, dismissal deadline, stay-and-abeyance procedure or automatic extension follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit judgment is reversed and remanded. The equitable argument actually raised but not reached below receives its own lawful adjudication; the Court neither finds diligence or extraordinary impediment nor grants tolling, a writ, release or resentencing. Dismissal as untimely is required if no independently available ground establishes timeliness.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs and United States v. Brockamp preserve provision-sensitive analysis of equity but settle neither Walker's entitlement nor categorical unavailability of equitable protection.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Pliler v. Ford, No. 03-221, decided June 21, 2004, resolves the equity reservation for the defined court-created filing loss and supplies a minimum neutral-notice duty for a qualifying court-presented dismissal election. Its causal, diligence, proof and lawful-remedy conditions govern that protection. Federal pendency remains excluded from section 2244(d)(2), and general mixed-petition stay authority remains unresolved.



#### Carey v. Saffold, 536 U.S. 214

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-301.
**Decided:** 2002-06-17.

##### Timely ordinary state review includes its intervals

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a properly filed state collateral application remains pending during timely continuation through the State's ordinary review process, including the interval before a timely filing at the next available level. California's successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function; an abandoned or completed process, an unreasonable gap or an unrelated later attack does not receive interval exclusion merely because another collateral petition is possible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; six direct votes for both holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Proper filing and actual pendency remain separate requirements. The rule covers timely continuation of ordinary state review rather than every extraordinary, repetitive or possible future collateral application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Artuz and Duncan are applied without alteration; Boerckel supplies ordinary-review context. The previously open interval question is resolved at this functional, timely-continuation scope.

##### State timeliness and the actual federal calculation remain necessary

**Holding:** An alternative merits discussion does not establish timely state filing or override a valid independent state determination that the relevant interval was unreasonable. The Ninth Circuit must determine the California order's actual meaning, the applicable state timing standard, the supported reasons for this interval and the exact elapsed time, then recalculate the federal limitation without excluding any interval that fails the governing requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also reject treating alternative merits language as conclusive of timeliness, but do not join the functional-pendency rule or mandate.

**Material application:** The California Supreme Court's order referred both to merits and lack of diligence after an interval of roughly four and one-half months. No nationwide deadline or presumption that this particular interval is reasonable or late replaces the state-law inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Certification or other lawful clarification may be used if needed; it is not automatically required. The decision does not grant equitable tolling, resolve the constitutional claims, or answer Artuz’s separate question whether an exception to an actual filing deadline can preserve proper filing.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for the state-timeliness determination and exact federal calculation. Independently preserved lawful commencement or equitable grounds remain subject to their own requirements. If none makes the application timely, dismissal as untimely is required; other habeas restrictions remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Artuz's filing-deadline-exception reservation remains; Duncan's independent timing rules remain. Lee's adequate-state-ground inquiry and Young's bankruptcy tolling do not determine statutory pendency here.



#### Pliler v. Ford, 542 U.S. 225

**Docket or dockets:** No. 03-221.
**Decided:** 2004-06-21

##### A court-presented dismissal election requires minimum neutral notice

**Holding:** When a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner with a timely mixed petition to choose between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissing to exhaust other claims, and the materials before it disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will defeat timely return, it must give neutral notice that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the original filing date and that a later petition may be time barred. The court must provide a meaningful opportunity to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims, subject to their ordinary requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part I, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The duty is a minimum floor triggered by the court’s election and apparent substantial filing risk. It does not require investigation of missing records, precise computation, advice on strategy or claim strength, or a promised stay; pro se status or complexity alone is insufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The protection governs these pending dismissal consequences and later qualifying elections without supplying a new full limitation period.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Duncan v. Walker: applied to exclude federal pendency from §2244(d)(2); its equity reservation is resolved for the defined court-created impediment while the statutory exclusion remains unchanged.
- Castro v. United States: extended by analogy through a new supervisory holding for this defined court-presented election; its own recharacterization rule retains its scope.
- Rose v. Lundy: preserved in its exhaustion requirement; its operation does not excuse an inaccurate election under the minimum notice rule.

##### A proved court-created loss can receive equitable protection

**Holding:** Section 2244(d) permits equitable tolling for omission of the required notice or another materially misleading judicial direction that causes loss of an otherwise timely federal proceeding or prevents timely return, when the petitioner establishes the required causal connection and reasonable diligence. A notice violation alone does not establish reliance, causation or diligence, and causal loss alone does not establish that tolling is the legally available remedy for the actual timing defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part II, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch, without joining the omitted-notice branch.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Federal pendency alone remains outside statutory tolling. Judicial interference, causation, diligence and a lawful remedy are separate requirements; processing delay, pro se status or an adverse calculation alone do not suffice. The court must distinguish loss of a timely original action from prevention of timely later filing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Duncan v. Walker: applied to exclude federal pendency from §2244(d)(2); its equity reservation is resolved for the defined court-created impediment while the statutory exclusion remains unchanged.
- Carey v. Saffold: applied to qualifying state pendency and intervals in each actual calculation.
- Slack v. McDaniel: applied to keep exhaustion-return classification separate from timeliness and lawful amendment.

##### The petitioner must prove an effective causal remedy

**Holding:** The petitioner must prove the factual predicates for this equitable protection by a preponderance of the evidence, including reasonable diligence during the relevant period and after the obstacle ended; diligence is assessed in the actual circumstances, not as perfect diligence or a duty to anticipate and correct the very misleading direction at issue. The court must identify the precise interval requiring protection, justify its causal and lawful remedial basis, exclude independently avoidable delay, and explain how the remedy cures the whole timing defect, including any expiration before the election.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part III, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins these requirements for the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch.

**Material application:** Both originals were timely with five days remaining, but the period expired before dismissal. Protecting only postdismissal days cannot be assumed to cure the returns. A lawful causal remedy must reach the whole defect; reopening requires its independent predicates, and blanket subtraction of federal pendency is impermissible.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The preponderance rule governs these equitable factual predicates; distinct statutory and remedial predicates retain their applicable standards. Without a lawful effective remedy, the later petition remains untimely.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Duncan v. Walker: applied to exclude federal pendency from §2244(d)(2); its equity reservation is resolved for the defined court-created impediment while the statutory exclusion remains unchanged.
- Grogan v. Garner and Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston: applied to ordinary factual proof and allocation of uncertainty, preserving distinct statutory or remedial proof requirements.

##### Omitted warnings do not automatically join separate actions

**Holding:** A separate later petition does not automatically become an amendment to a closed original action because required warnings were omitted. Relief depending on reopening requires an independently lawful reopening predicate; whether relation back follows an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening remains unresolved because that procedural predicate has not been established here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part IV, directly adopted by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General authority and standards for stays of mixed petitions remain unresolved. The contingent relation-back question after independently lawful reopening also remains open. Neither the warning violation nor first-application classification supplies retained, stayed or amended status to a terminated action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Slack v. McDaniel: applied to keep exhaustion-return classification separate from timeliness and lawful amendment.
- Rose v. Lundy: preserved in its exhaustion requirement; its operation does not excuse an inaccurate election under the minimum notice rule.

##### The repeated claims return for individualized adjudication

**Authority of the judgment:** Seven Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—vacate the repeated-claim disposition and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Souter adopt Part V's affirmative-rule rationale; the other four support vacatur on their no-advisement ground. Ginsburg and Breyer would preserve the lower vacatur of the repeated-claim dismissals by partial affirmance. Parts I–IV supply the controlling rules for further proceedings despite that remedial disagreement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single rationale for repeated-claim vacatur controls under Marks: three Justices adopt the affirmative-rule rationale and four the no-advisement ground. Parts I–IV separately command direct majorities and govern the remand.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Loguercio’s express election and Weed’s nonresponse receive separate preservation, communications, responsive-conduct, causation, diligence and lawful-remedy inquiries. The two new Loguercio assistance claims remain dismissed; development of the five new Weed legal-paper-access claims continues. Automatic new-claim relation back remains denied.



#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.



#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.



#### Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, 530 U.S. 290 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-62.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority

**Holding:** A public secondary school may not organize an invocation as a representative component of its own football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that official component will include religious exercise. The challenged board-created election mechanism violates the Establishment Clause under both its unrestricted and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing versions; relief reaches that mechanism while preserving genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for the rule, explanation and limited affirmance. The particular government-created representative religious choice is essential to all seven joins; the Court adopts no independent religious-pressure holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The District stresses secular purposes, student authorship, no required prayer, optional attendance and the possibility of silence or a secular message. Those features do not remove the existing governmental mechanism. The Court assumes no official textual preapproval and no delivered October prayer; it does not rest on a finding that every secular purpose is a sham. The ability to decline use of the delegated religious authority does not transform it into neutral access. Pinette and Bronx Household protect actually private expression, where government has not adopted the devotion as its own event component. Tanford likewise distinguishes official organization from private composition, without making its adult setting identical to this school policy.

The backup's religious-content limits retain the same official mechanism and do not cure it. The Court does not direct officials to edit private religion into acceptable language. Nor does it prohibit every election, invited student speaker or religious message. The existing mechanism suffices to affirm; Lee and Ingebretsen's separate protection against substantial practical religious pressure remains intact without requiring legal compulsion or compulsory attendance. No additional pressure finding, universal facial-challenge rule, new forum entitlement, graduation ruling or damages award is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment restrains the challenged official representative invocation mechanism. It preserves genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access, and requires neither a new forum nor censorship of otherwise lawful private religious viewpoints. It does not decide graduation, damages or later incidents outside this record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act/function inquiry; purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not independent surviving prongs.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues in the form Agostini adopted; the Court revives neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Its official-organization rule applies to this school-created invocation mechanism. Its independently sufficient substantial-pressure rule remains, without a compulsory-attendance prerequisite; no new pressure holding is made here.
- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Its rejection of automatic insulation by student delivery and its protection of lawful private expression apply. Its provision-sensitive interim ruling is not recast as a universal rule invalidating every future religious message.
- **Tanford v. Brand:** Preserved for its established official devotional program and its distinction between independent wording and private access; the Court does not transplant every feature of university commencement to football.
- **Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Its actual generally available private forum is distinguished from this selective representative event component; religious content and government location remain insufficient by themselves to establish attribution.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its independently organized private use remains protected; the school's own representative ceremony presents a different governmental function.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District:** Its protection of religious viewpoints within genuine comparable private access remains intact; no schoolwide private forum is inferred here.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its independently directed private-expression relationship remains intact; it does not authorize government-organized worship.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio v. City of Cincinnati:** Its contextual attribution inquiry remains; neither a forum label nor location alone supplies the answer.
- **Strout v. Albanese:** Its distinction between genuine private expression and governmental performance or purchase of a religious function is preserved; no football-specific rule is imported from its tuition setting.



#### Good News Club v. Milford Central School, 533 U.S. 98

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-2036.
**Decided:** June 11, 2001.

##### Devotional expression does not remove this moral instruction from the admitted subject

**Holding:** When a public school opens its community forum to comparable private moral and character instruction, it may not exclude an otherwise eligible program because that instruction uses prayer, Bible lessons, worship or invitation to religious adherence. Genuine reasonable and viewpoint-neutral subject, activity and operational limits remain available, but religious form cannot itself reclassify comparable admitted instruction as an excluded subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt Part I of the Opinion of the Court at this level of generality and in its application. Breyer delivers that opinion, and the other seven members of this coalition join. Eight direct votes control; Stevens dissents from the application.

**Material application:** Milford admitted secular moral instruction but excluded the Club's integrated prayer, Bible teaching, songs, games and invitation to Christian adherence. Superintendent testimony about Aesop instruction established the policy comparison, not an actual Aesop meeting. The Club need not remove its devotional components to qualify as comparable instruction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not classify every worship service as instruction or decide uses divorced from the actual admitted subjects. Genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral boundaries survive, including scheduling, supervision, safety, capacity and activity limits. Viewpoint neutrality creates no general resource or financing entitlement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lamb’s Chapel and Ladue are applied to the admitted instructional subject. Bronx Household’s instruction and bounded devotional-access holdings remain controlling. Perry Education and Cornelius retain genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral forum limits. Rosenberger reinforces viewpoint neutrality within the actual private-expression program without creating a financing entitlement.

##### These private-use arrangements defeat the asserted establishment defense and establish equal-access entitlement

**Holding:** Independent adult leadership, parental authorization and after-school operation in this community forum constitute private access where the asserted school arrangements amount to administration of that access rather than governmental religious instruction, adoption, solicitation or substantial school-created religious pressure. On these circumstances the asserted establishment defense fails, and the Club is entitled to equal access under the same genuine neutral rules as comparable groups, with appropriate declaratory and access relief determined below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court, delivered by Breyer, is joined by the other members of the six-Justice coalition comprising Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Those six directly control the establishment conclusion and remedy. Souter and Ginsburg join neither; Stevens dissents.

**Material application:** Private adult leadership, parental authorization and after-school operation, considered with the four-minute dismissal interval, elementary ages, room assignment, permission practices, school activities and audiovisual use, establish administration of private access rather than a governmental devotional function or substantial school-created pressure. The defense's legal insufficiency is determined separately from rejection of Milford's own summary-judgment motion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Agostini's modified Lemon rule asks whether government exercises religious authority or function; purpose, effects and entanglement inform that inquiry rather than operating as independent fallback prongs. Lee and Santa Fe separately prohibit official worship and substantial governmental religious pressure. Parental permission and neutral availability are relevant circumstances, not automatic immunity. Official recruitment, teacher-led devotion, actual compelled participation or materially different governmental adoption require their own inquiry. A possible child's misunderstanding and religious intensity alone do not supply governmental action. Separate equality and RFRA theories need not be reached because the speech holding supplies relief.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit is reversed for implementation of equal access and appropriate declaratory and access relief under genuine neutral rules. The rejected establishment defense is not reopened. Damages and fees require independent determinations; no fixed permit, injunction, resource entitlement, disclaimer or permanent forum-opening obligation is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Agostini’s modified Lemon inquiry is applied unchanged. Bronx Household supplies the private-access attribution rule. Lee and Santa Fe retain their independent official-worship and pressure protections. Marshfield is distinguished because it addresses an actual continuing governmental presentation; its functional inquiry remains operative.



### One-year state-custody habeas filing period

**Governing question:** The limitation period under amended §2244(d).

**Current rule:** A person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment has one year to apply for habeas relief, running from the latest of: finality by completion of direct review or expiration of time to seek it; removal of a State-created filing impediment violating federal law that actually prevented filing; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted constitutional right, if newly recognized and made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could have discovered the claim’s factual predicate. Time during which a properly filed application for state postconviction or other collateral review of the pertinent judgment or claim is pending is excluded.

Proper filing concerns compliance with requirements for delivery and acceptance of the application, rather than the success or relief eligibility of its individual claims. Claim-relief bars therefore do not invalidate an otherwise proper filing. Clerk acceptance cannot cure a genuine filing defect. Form, deadlines, the proper receiving court or office, fees and filing preconditions must be distinguished from rules requiring denial of relief on a claim.

The state-proceeding limitation applies to both postconviction review and other collateral review. Pendency of a federal habeas application is not excluded under section 2244(d)(2), even if that application was timely and dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. Accompanying civil-rights allegations do not convert it into state review. A later application's non-successive status and an earlier application's timely filing do not themselves make the later application timely; proper filing, actual pendency and relation to the pertinent judgment or claim remain independent conditions.

A properly filed state collateral application remains pending through timely continuation of the ordinary state-review process, including qualifying intervals before filing at the next available level. Successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function. Completion, abandonment, an unreasonable interval or an unrelated later attack does not receive exclusion merely because another petition is possible. A state merits discussion does not override an independent valid untimeliness determination. Where the order is ambiguous, the federal court must determine its actual meaning, the applicable state timing rule, the supported explanation for the interval and elapsed time; lawful clarification may be used but is not invariably required. No national fixed deadline or automatic reasonableness presumption applies.

### Official school worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Officially organized school worship, delegated representative religious choices and independently coercive school prayer.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. The official-worship prohibition also reaches a public university’s selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation and benediction prayers into commencement. Mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; independent clergy composition does not make it private forum speech. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

A school may not make an invocation an official representative component of its own ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that component includes religious exercise. Such a mechanism is not transformed into private access by student authorship, optional attendance, possible silence or secular speech, or nonsectarian/nonproselytizing limits. The defect does not require a prayer already delivered or official editing of its words. Genuinely voluntary private prayer, independently chosen student expression, lawful secular messages and neutral private access remain protected; the holding adds no separate religious-pressure test.

**Authority by component:**

- Independently sufficient pressure ground: *Lee*, “The school-directed ceremony also imposes impermissible religious pressure”; Kennedy's concurrence joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. Each rule has five direct supporters; neither depends on Marks or on treating Stone as joining Kennedy.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lee applied existing school-worship principles and Lemon's then-independent primary-effect prong at this recurring graduation setting without creating a replacement establishment test. ### Equal religious eligibility for controlled secular educational services

**Governing question:** Religious-character or religious-location disqualifications within existing programs of independently identifiable secular educational work.

**Current rule:** A State may not exclude a child from consideration for onsite secular special-education service solely because the school is religious where comparable secular locations are permitted and effective public control can preserve the service's secular character. Neutral educational, logistical and fiscal criteria may operate on equal terms. Adequate alternative service does not itself cure that express disqualification.

Religious premises alone do not convert independently controlled, operationally separate public vision service into devotional instruction. Actual safeguards against religious teaching and diversion remain permissible. The establishment permission does not itself require onsite services; the right is to equal consideration within the offered service. Relief must remove the religious criterion while preserving lawful neutral service and site decisions.

For direct institutional grants, an applicant must establish the requested secular work, eligible costs and lawful program compliance under effective use restrictions and reliable accountability. A devotional-financing objection requires a supported operational connection, which may be shown before payment; religious affiliation and hypothetical diversion alone do not establish it. When the work meets the existing program’s lawful requirements and the financing boundary, religious affiliation alone cannot justify denial of equal consideration or payment otherwise required by that program. An express religious disability requires a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, addressing feasible less restrictive means.

Keep the proceedings and legal issues public and assess the actual need for confidential identification in the defense; anonymity does not excuse proving the claim.

### 2005-03-30 — [Rhines v. Weber](../records/rhines-v-weber_merits_2005-03-30.md#public-projection)

**Holdings.**

#### Retaining a timely mixed petition during exhaustion

**Holding and operative rule.** A district court may stay a timely mixed petition while the prisoner exhausts state remedies if the prisoner shows good cause for not exhausting first, the unexhausted claims are not plainly meritless, and the record does not show abusive litigation or intentional delay. Good cause means a reasonable explanation in the actual circumstances, not an extraordinary-circumstances or procedural-default test; the court must set reasonable, definite deadlines for seeking state relief and returning to federal court, accounting for delays outside the prisoner’s control.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete framework. Justice Souter joins the existence of stay power and the merit, anti-abuse and deadline limits, but not the initial good-cause prerequisite.

**Controlling explanation.** Rose v. Lundy gives state courts the first opportunity to decide federal claims. Congress’s exhaustion and limitations rules can serve that purpose while a federal court retains a timely filed action. Landis v. North American Co. supplies ordinary control over pending cases, exercised here within the habeas statutes. Duncan v. Walker excludes time spent in federal court from statutory tolling under §2244(d)(2); retention preserves an existing action rather than supplying a new filing period. Pliler v. Ford left this stay question open while establishing separate protections against particular court-caused filing losses.

The warden correctly argues that routine, open-ended stays would weaken finality and reward premature filing. Cause, a potential-merit screen, exclusion of abuse and definite deadlines answer that objection. Good cause preserves an incentive to exhaust before filing and permits an individualized explanation. Its reasonable-explanation meaning also avoids turning technical uncertainty about exhaustion into a second, rigid procedural-default obstacle. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on its merits; it does not compel denial. Relief remains subject to exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant’s rights, or counsel’s express waiver of exhaustion for the State, as the statute provides.

Rhines filed with more than eleven months remaining before limitations expired during federal processing. The district court identified eight unexhausted claims and imposed sixty-day initiation and return conditions. Those facts establish the practical importance of stay authority. Actual cause, potential merit, delay and the reasonableness of those conditions require findings under the announced standard.

#### Protecting timely review when applying that discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** Where good cause, potential merit and absence of intentional delay are established and dismissal would defeat timely federal review, denying a stay and dismissing the entire petition ordinarily abuses discretion; the court should retain the action, and any refusal requires a reason compatible with the statute. If a stay is inappropriate, the prisoner must be allowed to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims when whole-petition dismissal would unreasonably impair federal relief.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join this sufficient case for retention and the deletion protection. Justice Souter’s agreement does not make good cause a necessary condition in his separate view.

**Controlling explanation.** The exhaustion requirement directs claims first to the state courts; it does not require destroying a timely federal action whenever the two processes overlap. The filing date and continued pendency therefore matter independently of statutory tolling. A refusal must apply the governing predicates and account for its actual effect on federal review. If retention is unsuitable, deletion preserves the exhausted claims without demanding adjudication of the others. Pliler’s neutral-warning duty retains its particular triggers—an unrepresented prisoner and a qualifying court-directed dismissal election. Rhines had appointed counsel for his amended federal petition, so that duty does not automatically govern his election.

**Precedent Treatment.**

Rose v. Lundy’s total-exhaustion requirement remains operative with bounded stay-and-abeyance in the limitations setting. Duncan v. Walker retains its statutory non-tolling rule; this decision answers the distinct stay question. Pliler v. Ford retains its separate notice, causation, diligence and lawful-remedy rules. Landis v. North American Co. supplies ordinary management authority subject to the governing habeas statutes.

**Law After Decision.**

District courts have a uniform standard for retaining timely mixed petitions while state remedies are pursued. The rule preserves the existing filing and requires prompt exhaustion and return; reopening a terminated action still requires an independently valid procedural ground. The actual stay predicates and reasonable deadlines are matters of application under settled standards, including the deletion protection when whole-petition dismissal would unreasonably impair federal review.




### 2005-04-04 — [Johnson v. United States](../records/johnson-v-united-states_merits_2005-04-04.md#public-projection)

**Holdings.**

#### An order removing a particular predicate is a supporting fact

**Holding and operative rule.** An order vacating the particular state conviction used to enhance a federal sentence is a fact supporting a challenge to that sentence under §2255 paragraph 6(4). The movant must establish the order and its material connection to the asserted current-sentence claim; an actual change in that conviction’s status differs from a new judicial interpretation, repeated legal argument or unsuccessful collateral filing.

**Authority.** Part I of the Court’s opinion is joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** The state order changes the legal status of a particular predicate judgment. That event is distinct from the older facts underlying the state challenge. The Government’s argument that only those older facts count would start the period before the state order needed for the present claim exists.

Custis v. United States and Daniels v. United States direct covered attacks on state convictions through their proper review channel and preserve an otherwise authorized later federal application after successful invalidation. Treating the resulting order as fact gives that sequence its actual meaning. Those decisions still require the prisoner to satisfy the independent procedural and substantive requirements for relief from the current sentence.

#### Diligence concerns discovery of the existing order

**Holding and operative rule.** The one-year period under paragraph 6(4) begins when the prisoner could discover the vacatur order through due diligence. The statute requires diligent discovery and filing within one year of that discoverability date; it imposes no additional federal deadline for obtaining a state order that did not yet exist.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg join Part II’s rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress selected discovery of facts supporting the claim. Discovering an existing event and bringing that event into existence are different acts. An order cannot be discovered before it is entered. Clay v. United States preserves attention to the particular statutory timing trigger; the distinct finality and new-right triggers do not supply an unstated procurement condition here.

The Government and dissent argue that a prisoner who initiates state proceedings should not control federal timing through unexplained delay. The concern for finality is substantial, but Congress addressed it through the one-year discovery period and the other restrictions governing collateral relief. State procedural rules govern the state challenge, and the independent federal requirements govern the later sentencing claim. Custis and Daniels establish the proper channel without adding a federal schedule for creating a state judgment. Estimating when a hypothetical earlier state proceeding would have produced relief substitutes a different condition for discovery.

Johnson obtained an order vacating convictions that included a predicate of his career-offender enhancement, then filed federally just over three months later. The categorical timing dismissal therefore cannot stand. Actual discoverability and the order’s material effect on the enhanced sentence require application of the governing rules on remand.

**Precedent Treatment.**

Custis v. United States and Daniels v. United States retain their proper-channel rule and separate prerequisites for a later authorized federal application. This decision determines the previously unresolved vacatur-fact timing question. Clay v. United States preserves the distinct statutory triggers; its ordinary-finality rule remains applicable to the trigger it governs.

**Law After Decision.**

Actual orders changing the status of particular enhancement predicates qualify under the factual-discovery trigger. Diligence is measured by when the order could be learned of, with the movant required to establish discoverability and the order’s material connection to the claim. A later state vacatur thus permits consideration through the enacted timing provision while the independent requirements governing the federal sentence challenge continue to apply.

### 2005-04-04 — [Johnson v. United States](../records/johnson-v-united-states_merits_2005-04-04.md#public-projection)

**Opinion Topology.**

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. All nine Justices joined Part I, recognizing the vacatur as a supporting fact. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg joined Part II, adopting discovery diligence alone and reversal. SOUTER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which O’CONNOR, THOMAS, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg in full; O’Connor, Souter, Thomas and Breyer as to Part I | Reversal and remand | All nine join vacatur as fact; five join the discovery-only rule, application and remedy |
| Dissent | Souter | O’Connor, Thomas and Breyer | Would affirm | Procurement-and-discovery rule and its application; joins Part I |

**Separate Writings.**

Souter, joined by O’Connor, Thomas and Breyer, accepts the vacatur as a supporting fact but would require reasonable diligence in obtaining it after entry of the enhanced federal judgment, as well as diligence in learning of it. The federal judgment gives the prisoner a concrete reason to challenge the predicate. Because the prisoner initiates the process producing the new event, the dissent reads the express diligence condition to include reasonable pursuit; state collateral rules do not replace the federal sentence’s separate finality interest.

That rule includes three qualifications. First, delay in state proceedings not attributable to the prisoner after diligent initiation does not defeat the later trigger. Second, when the underlying grounds for the state challenge could not be discovered through diligence until after federal judgment, pursuit is measured from their discoverability, and diligent pursuit permits time to run from notice of the resulting vacatur. Third, even a prisoner whose pursuit was deficient may qualify if the federal motion was filed within one year of the notice date diligent pursuit would have produced.

The dissent would affirm here. Johnson waited from the November 29, 1994 federal judgment until February 6, 1998 to begin the state challenge. Even crediting all time before AEDPA’s April 24, 1996 enactment leaves more than twenty-one months. Limited education, self-representation and waiting for inmate assistance do not explain reasonable efforts or a specific impediment during that interval; the extension request did not pursue state vacatur. On that sequence the dissent concludes that diligent pursuit would have produced notice more than a year before the eventual federal motion, so the third qualification does not save it. Reasonable diligence permits genuine obstacles and does not demand the fastest conceivable action.
