## Standards and Tests selection: Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification

### Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification

**Governing question:** The new §2255 limitation period and certification for a second or successive motion.

**Current rule:** A §2255 motion has a one-year limitation running from the latest of conviction finality; removal of an unlawful governmental filing impediment that actually prevented the motion; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted newly recognized right made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could discover the supporting facts. A second or successive motion requires certification under §2244 by an appropriate appellate panel of either newly discovered evidence which, if proved and considered with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt, or a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court. The evidence route has its own text and is not silently replaced with §2244(b)(2)(B)’s distinct state-prisoner constitutional-error/diligence formulation.

The certification cross-reference incorporates §2244(b)(3)(E)’s bar on ordinary certiorari review of an appellate authorization decision. An attempted ordinary review of such an order is dismissed for want of jurisdiction, not affirmed on the merits. A distinct independent proceeding must establish its own forum, eligibility and procedural requirements; the All Writs Act creates no jurisdiction.

For a federal conviction affirmed on direct appeal without a certiorari petition, the ordinary finality trigger occurs when the time to seek certiorari expires. Issuance of the appellate mandate does not start the one-year period while that direct-review opportunity remains available.

**Authority by component:**

- Latest-event period and alternative certification predicates: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §105(2), amended 28 U.S.C. §2255 limitation and successive-motion paragraphs, enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Incorporated review-channel prohibition and independent-route limits: Triestman v. United States — The authorization refusal is not subject to ordinary Supreme Court review, 1998-03-12.

- Certiorari-period expiration rather than appellate mandate as ordinary finality: Canonical Decision Record `Clay-v-United-States_merits_2003-03-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Finality includes the available certiorari period, 2003-03-04; unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Present operation effective:** Clay v. United States, March 4, 2003, for the ordinary finality trigger; Triestman’s incorporated review bar and the enacted alternative triggers and certification predicates retain their authority.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** The amendment supplies no conclusion that an earlier dismissal makes a later motion successive, that a particular deadline has expired, or that collateral relief is guaranteed.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application. Triestman v. United States — Successive collateral-review authorization; In re Dorsainvil — Successive collateral-review authorization.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collateral attacks on state predicates at federal sentencing and under section 2255

### Collateral attacks on state predicates at federal sentencing and under section 2255

**Governing question:** When an ACCA sentencing proceeding or a section 2255 challenge to a current federal sentence permits a constitutional attack on a prior state conviction.


**Current rule:** The ACCA sentencing proceeding does not supply a general collateral-review forum for attacks on prior state convictions based on ineffective representation or an involuntary counseled plea. A conviction obtained through an unconstitutional complete denial of counsel, without valid waiver, is the constitutional exception. Statutory objections to whether a conviction meets the enhancement’s predicate requirements remain distinct from such collateral attacks. A defendant may pursue authorized review in the proper forum and, after a predicate is invalidated there, apply for relief from the enhanced sentence; the decision promises neither reopening nor its remedy.

Section 2255 does not reopen an independently expired counseled state predicate for involuntary-plea or ineffective-assistance claims where meaningful original review was available and no external obstacle to using it is identified. An unexcused lost opportunity is not an unavailable remedy. Complete unconstitutional denial of constitutionally required counsel without valid waiver remains distinct and may be challenged under applicable procedure, with an adverse effect on the current sentence established. Procedural default still applies, ordinarily requiring presentation of the complete-deprivation objection at federal sentencing or a legally sufficient excuse. Deficient representation and an involuntary counseled plea do not constitute complete deprivation.

**Authority by component:**

- Forum limitation, complete-denial exception and distinct statutory objections: Custis v. United States — The ACCA supplies no general collateral-review procedure at sentencing; The constitutional counsel exception and the proper review channel, 1994-05-23. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct joins to this statutory ground. The same six Justices join O'Connor's separate constitutional ground and its express remedial reservation; the three dissenters would allow the present attacks under their statutory construction.

- Section 2255 available-original-review limitation and complete-counsel exception: Canonical Decision Record `Daniels_v_United_States_merits_2001-04-25.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Available original review does not become a renewed section 2255 forum; Complete unconstitutional counsel deprivation remains distinct, 2001-04-25; five direct joins to O’Connor’s opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Daniels v. United States, 2001-04-25, for the section 2255 extension; Custis’s sentencing-forum rule remains effective from 1994-05-23.


**Open question:** No controlling rule decides access under section 2255 when meaningful original review was unavailable or frustrated through no fault of the prisoner. Expiration alone does not establish such faultless loss. The materially different section 2254 issue in Coss acquired no controlling access rationale. Independently invalidated predicates require an authorized vehicle and actual relief predicates; neither automatic reopening nor a remedy is promised. Beecham’s conviction-selector rule remains distinct.


**Holdings navigation:** The ACCA supplies no general collateral-review procedure at sentencing; The constitutional counsel exception and the proper review channel. [Daniels v. United States, 532 U.S. 374 — Section 2255 attacks on expired state predicates](HOLDINGS.md#daniels-v-united-states-532-us-374); [Lackawanna County District Attorney v. Coss, 532 U.S. 394 — Indirect review of expired convictions and a conditional appellate mandate](HOLDINGS.md#lackawanna-county-district-attorney-v-coss-532-us-394).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal-law transfer restrictions and exclusion of trust interests

### Federal-law transfer restrictions and exclusion of trust interests

**Governing question:** Exclusion of a debtor's beneficial trust interest from the estate under 11 U.S.C. §541(c)(2).

**Current rule:** Applicable nonbankruptcy law includes federal law. A transfer restriction on a debtor's beneficial interest in a trust is preserved when enforceable under that law; an established ERISA-qualified trust with an ERISA-enforceable antialienation restriction is therefore excluded from the estate. Exclusion is distinct from exemption and does not change exemption deadlines or adjudicate statutory avoidance remedies.

**Authority by component:**
- Federal-law construction and qualifying trust exclusion: Patterson v. Shumate, “Holding 1: An enforceable federal pension restriction excludes this beneficial trust interest”, June 15, 1992; Blackmun, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Patterson v. Shumate, June 15, 1992.

**Open question:** Other plans, nontrust interests, statutory exceptions, distributed benefits, §522(b)(2)(A) and the payment-versus-corpus scope of §522(d)(10)(E) are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** Patterson v. Shumate, 504 U.S. 753 (1992) — Estate exclusions for pension trust interests.

## Standards and Tests selection: Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard

### Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard

**Governing question:** Substantial-evidence review where the agency allegedly applied a more demanding standard than the one it announced.

**Current rule:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the agency finding must have substantial whole-record support under the actual governing substantive standard. The agency cannot silently turn reasonable doubt into near-proof of the contrary fact, or convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule. Review includes evidence fairly detracting from the finding while preserving the agency’s choice between fairly conflicting views. The agency’s §10(c) preponderance burden remains distinct from appellate substantial-evidence review.

**Authority by component:**

- An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof: Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB, 1998-01-26; Scalia Parts III–IV with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lawful expressly adopted presumptions and evidentiary rules remain possible subject to statutory authority and ordinary review. This rule creates no de novo factfinding authority, mandatory treatment of every unverified report, or finding of systematic agency manipulation.

**Holdings navigation:** Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB — Incumbent-union polling and agency evidentiary standards.

#### Custis v. United States, 511 U.S. 485

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5209.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### The ACCA supplies no general collateral-review procedure at sentencing

**Holding:** Under 18 U.S.C. §924(e), a defendant may not use the federal sentencing proceeding to litigate these constitutional attacks on operative state convictions for ineffective assistance and involuntary counseled pleas. The statute does not supply a general collateral-review procedure; the distinct exception for an unconstitutional complete denial of appointed counsel remains, as do objections to whether the enhancement itself satisfies its statutory requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct joins to this statutory ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lewis v. United States, 445 U.S. 55 (1980): applies the distinction between existing conviction status and an unresolved collateral challenge, without extending its civil-disability rationale to every sentencing defect.
- Beecham v. United States, May 16, 1994: preserves the convicting-jurisdiction selector for §921(a)(20), without adding an ACCA collateral-review procedure.

##### The constitutional counsel exception and the proper review channel

**Holding:** The Constitution does not require the ACCA sentencing court to hear Custis's ineffective-assistance and involuntary-counseled-plea attacks, but a conviction obtained through complete denial of appointed counsel in violation of the constitutional entitlement, without valid waiver, may be challenged there. Custis may pursue available review of the state convictions; successful invalidation permits an application concerning the enhancement through an authorized procedure, without a promised reopening or particular result.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six Justices join O'Connor's separate constitutional ground and its express remedial reservation; the three dissenters would allow the present attacks under their statutory construction.

**Current-force modification:** Daniels v. United States, April 25, 2001, resolves part of the expired-custody reservation for section 2255: expired counseled state predicates are not reopened for involuntary-plea or ineffective-assistance claims merely because they enhance federal punishment when meaningful original review was available and no external impediment is identified. Complete unconstitutional deprivation of required counsel remains challengeable subject to applicable procedure and adverse enhancement effect. Faultless loss of meaningful review has no controlling resolution; Custis's sentencing rule and its conditional authorized route following independent invalidation remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gideon v. Wainwright, 372 U.S. 335 (1963): preserves the constitutional appointed-counsel entitlement and valid-waiver boundary.
- Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), and United States v. Tucker, 404 U.S. 443 (1972): preserve the exception for unconstitutional complete counsel deprivation; they do not require the broader sentencing forum sought here.
- Parke v. Raley, December 1, 1992: preserves rebuttable regularity and lawful collateral proof rules without deciding the availability of this forum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 921(a)(20)'s business-regulation exclusions and state-misdemeanor exclusion for offenses punishable by two years or less remain distinct from collateral review. A conviction expunged, set aside, pardoned or followed by civil-rights restoration is excluded under the convicting jurisdiction's law unless the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No removal event is established here. Expired-custody and no-meaningful-review cases remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit is affirmed, 6–3, leaving the 235-month sentence intact against these attacks. Original-conviction review and a possible authorized application after successful invalidation remain available under their own rules. No stay, automatic reopening or fixed sentence reduction is ordered.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Daniels v. United States, No. 99-9136, April 25, 2001: available-original-review section 2255 restriction and complete-counsel exception, with faultless-review question unresolved.

#### Daniels v. United States, 532 U.S. 374

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-9136.
**Decided:** April 25, 2001.

##### Whether a later federal enhancement reopens expired counseled state convictions after available original review

**Holding:** Section 2255 does not reopen an expired counseled state conviction for ineffective-assistance or involuntary-plea claims solely because it enhances a current federal sentence when meaningful original review was available and no external obstacle is identified. Unexcused loss of a usable opportunity does not make review unavailable; this restriction decides access, not the old conviction's constitutional validity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No controlling rule resolves faultless unavailability or frustration of meaningful original review. Stone-Zsela's affirmative exception, Scalia's additional statutory-forum objection, three dissenters' broader current-sentence route and Breyer's sentencing-stage proposal do not yield a common five-Justice exception through Marks. Predicate classification, counting, statutory exclusions and a current sentencing-fact jury claim are not decided. A later independently invalidated predicate requires its own authorized vehicle, prerequisites and demonstrated enhancement effect, with no guaranteed reopening.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of the motion, leaving the 176-month sentence effective against these claims; no predicate invalidation, new hearing, resentencing or reduction is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Custis's sentencing-channel distinction extends to these available-review section 2255 circumstances, without overruling Custis or treating its expired-custody reservation as previously resolved. Parke preserves rebuttable regularity and proof, not conclusive validity. Maleng distinguishes expired custody from consequences; Garlotte's unexpired consecutive series does not encompass these independent expired sentences. Nichols supplies no review forum, and Fiore, Glover and Almendarez-Torres do not remove the distinct access conditions.

##### Whether unconstitutional complete deprivation of required counsel remains reviewable

**Holding:** Section 2255 remains available to challenge a predicate obtained through complete unconstitutional denial of required counsel without valid waiver, subject to applicable procedure and proof that it adversely affected the current sentence. Ineffective representation and involuntary counseled pleas do not qualify; ordinary default rules remain, generally requiring presentation at federal sentencing or a legally sufficient excuse.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion directly preserves this exception and its procedural limits.

**Material application:** Daniels alleges defective counseled proceedings, not complete deprivation, so this exception supplies no route for his motion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gideon supplies the entitlement and waiver boundary; Burgett, Tucker and Custis preserve the distinct rule for unconstitutional complete denial of counsel without converting all deficient representation into that category.

#### Patterson v. Shumate, 504 U.S. 753 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-913.
**Decided:** June 15, 1992.

##### Whether an ERISA-enforceable antialienation restriction excludes this beneficial trust interest under §541(c)(2)

**Holding:** Section 541(c)(2) preserves a restriction on transfer of a debtor's beneficial interest in a trust when that restriction is enforceable under applicable nonbankruptcy law, including federal law. This established ERISA-qualified trust contains an enforceable antialienation restriction, so Shumate's interest is excluded from the bankruptcy estate and cannot be turned over to its trustee as estate property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the statutory exclusion. Scalia's separate concurrence does not limit his full join; Stone concurs in the judgment without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** Plan trust status, ERISA qualification and federal enforceability are established. Shumate's control, which defeated the lower state-spendthrift analysis, cannot impose a state-law limitation absent from §541(c)(2). Section 522 retains work for interests not already excluded; policy observations supply no independent alternative holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The required beneficial trust interest, enforceable transfer restriction and applicable nonbankruptcy law remain essential. Other plans, nontrust interests, statutory exceptions, distributed benefits, avoidance remedies and the plan-fiduciary question preserved in Guidry are not adjudicated or displaced. No general pension immunity is created. The alternative §522(b)(2)(A) exemption and §522(d)(10)(E)'s coverage of presently payable benefits versus undistributed corpus remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 9–0. The qualifying interest is excluded from the estate and cannot be turned over as estate property; no payment amount or new factual inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Guidry v. Sheet Metal Workers National Pension Fund, 493 U.S. 365 (1990): applied for enforceability and the refusal to create an equitable exception; its statutory exceptions and reserved plan-fiduciary issue remain intact.
- Union Bank v. Wolas, simulated December 11, 1991: applied for giving effect to an enacted bankruptcy provision despite a proposed policy limitation; its ordinary-course preference holding remains unchanged.
- Dewsnup v. Timm, simulated January 15, 1992: distinguished as a context-specific construction preserving an allowed real-property lien, not a restriction of §541(c)(2) to state law; no holding is overruled.

#### New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC, 535 U.S. 1

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 00-568 and 00-809.

**Decided:** March 4, 2002.

##### I. The transmission grant includes unbundled interstate service for retail purchasers

**Holding:** The Federal Power Act's separate grant over transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce includes independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. The wholesale limitation governs the separate sales grant; it does not narrow the transmission grant, and FERC may regulate the covered transmission's rates, terms and conditions without ordering a utility to begin retail wheeling.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court. That direct unanimous coalition controls the category and the following limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent interstate transmission already supplied voluntarily or under state direction is distinct from compelled retail wheeling. State siting, local reliability and retail-service functions remain. No particular tariff or facility classification is adjudicated, and no general deference rule is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm No. 00-568 unanimously; the reviewed transmission requirements remain effective within their statutory boundaries.

**Limits and statutory qualifications:** Section 201(b)(1) retains state retail-sale authority and, except as the Act specifically provides otherwise, excludes facilities used for generation, local distribution, intrastate-only transmission or transmission of energy consumed wholly by the transmitter. Its preservation of lawful state authority over hydroelectric exports remains. Section 201(b)(2) applies sections 210, 211 and 212 and their enforcement to the entities those provisions cover; compliance with a section 210 or 211 order does not otherwise subject an entity to general FPA jurisdiction. Section 201(f)'s exclusion of federal, state and local governments, their agencies, authorities and instrumentalities, corporations wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of them, and their officials acting in duty remains unless a provision specifically refers to them.

Section 212(g) prohibits orders inconsistent with state retail-marketing-area law. Section 212(h) prohibits an order conditioned on, or requiring, direct transmission to an ultimate consumer. Its separate rule for transmission to or for a resale entity permits the covered exception only when both conditions hold: the entity is a federal power marketing agency, TVA, a State or political subdivision or its agency, a corporation or association that has ever received a Rural Electrification Administration loan to provide electric service under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, a person with a state- or local-law public electric-service obligation arising independently of contract alone, or a corporation or association wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of those listed entities; and it either served that consumer on October 24, 1992 or would deliver all the energy through transmission or distribution facilities it owns or controls. State and local authority over direct-to-consumer transmission remains preserved. Section 212(e) makes use of sections 210, 211, 213 or 212 unnecessary as a substitute for other lawful authority and, except as those sections provide, prevents them from limiting other Commission authority; it does not eliminate the retail-wheeling restrictions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board: applied to distinguish an affirmative federal statutory assignment from the independent constraints on its exercise and the functions Congress preserves for States.
- National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co.: applied to separate coverage from the validity of a particular regulatory method; its actual formula and classification limits remain.
- United States v. Mead Corp.: its actual-delegation requirement remains; independent statutory construction resolves the transmission category without a new general deference holding.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.: its prohibition on replacing an enacted mandatory obligation remains. This order's sufficient remedy does not dispense with section 206's triggered duty.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala: applied to considered administration within statutory commands and to the requirement of actual agency reasons; counsel's litigation assertions receive no substitute status.
- Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer: applied to distinguish permission to use a method from the independent circumstances in which the statute commands action; no general exemption from a corrective duty follows.

##### II. This proceeding did not compel Enron's additional bundled-service remedy

**Holding:** FERC could remedy the wholesale transmission-access discrimination established in this proceeding through the adopted open-access requirements while declining compulsory unbundling of bundled retail service on its independently sufficient determination that this additional remedy was unnecessary. Section 206 does require a lawful prospective determination and order after a hearing and finding that a jurisdictional rate, charge, classification or affecting rule, regulation, practice or contract is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential; the burden to prove that unlawfulness lies on the Commission or complainant, and the agency may not leave established discrimination uncorrected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those six votes control. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would remand this component for corrected agency analysis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency actually gave both a bundled-retail jurisdictional characterization and an independently sufficient unnecessary-remedy determination. The latter sustains this refusal without adoption of a permanent bundled-transmission exemption. Counsel's litigation reasons cannot replace agency reasons. The universal allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm No. 00-809, 6–3, and deny compulsory additional unbundling in this proceeding without an agency remand. The order supplies no approval of an individual tariff, retail price or facility classification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board: applied to distinguish an affirmative federal statutory assignment from the independent constraints on its exercise and the functions Congress preserves for States.
- National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co.: applied to separate coverage from the validity of a particular regulatory method; its actual formula and classification limits remain.
- United States v. Mead Corp.: its actual-delegation requirement remains; independent statutory construction resolves the transmission category without a new general deference holding.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.: its prohibition on replacing an enacted mandatory obligation remains. This order's sufficient remedy does not dispense with section 206's triggered duty.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala: applied to considered administration within statutory commands and to the requirement of actual agency reasons; counsel's litigation assertions receive no substitute status.
- Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer: applied to distinguish permission to use a method from the independent circumstances in which the statute commands action; no general exemption from a corrective duty follows.

#### California ex rel. Lockyer v. FERC

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket supplied; FERC proceedings EC01-49-002 and EC01-41-002; review of 329 F.3d 700 (9th Cir. 2003).
**Decided:** 2004-03-29.

##### Statutory notice and opportunity for hearing

**Holding:** An expedited approval under Federal Power Act section 203 can satisfy the statutory notice and hearing requirements through informative notice and written proceedings when the agency genuinely considers the material submissions while retaining effective authority to alter its decision, and the issues require no additional fact hearing. The abbreviated periods here, followed by substantive reconsideration, furnished that opportunity; an objector seeking further process must identify a material factual issue or a lost opportunity that the actual process failed to address.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's unanimous opinion of the Court controls all three questions in this entry.

**Material application:** Seven- and four-day comment periods were followed by substantive reconsideration under §313, during which FERC could still alter the approvals. Prescribed mailing, late intervention where allowed, written submissions and responsive orders supplied the relevant opportunity; actual receipt was not an added statutory condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal minimum comment period or rule that postapproval review always cures defective notice follows. Adequacy depends on genuine responsive consideration, effective remedial authority and the absence of an identified factual issue requiring an evidentiary hearing.

##### The creditor's constitutional process claim

**Holding:** Assuming the public power agency had a protected creditor interest, the notice, written submissions and effective reconsideration afforded it constitutionally sufficient process in these proceedings. The nature of the actual dispute required meaningful participation and responsive consideration, rather than a trial-type hearing merely because the proposed transactions might affect creditors.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A protected creditor interest is assumed, not decided. Meaningful written participation was adequate for these legal and policy objections; trial-type process was unnecessary on the identified dispute.

##### Review of the agency's own public-interest determination

**Holding:** A court reviewing these approvals must evaluate FERC's actual reasons and the supporting administrative record. FERC's consideration of the creditor objection together with the financing and generation interests supports the challenged approvals; disagreement with that assessment supplies no basis to replace the agency's supported determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent creditor, fraudulent-transfer and bankruptcy claims retain their own legal requirements and fall outside this procedural review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of review; the challenged transaction approvals stand without another hearing on these objections.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. supplies the requirement of notice suited to the affected interests. Mathews v. Eldridge supplies the functional constitutional inquiry; the statute's independent notice and hearing commands retain their force. National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp. supplies the agency-ground requirement and permits recognition of a discernible actual reason. Arkansas v. Oklahoma supplies whole-record review and bars substitution of a court's preferred policy. Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation v. EPA preserves source-specific statutory authority and reasoned exercise of that authority.

#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.
