#### Hercules, Inc. v. United States, 516 U.S. 417

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-818.

**Decided:** March 4, 1996.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Hercules and Thompson's distinct procurement contracts and consolidated appellate claims; both petitioners' specifications-warranty claims are decided, and Thompson alone has the preserved separate contextual-indemnity claim.

##### The necessary contractual source of a Tucker Act reimbursement claim

**Holding:** A §1491(a)(1) contract claim requires an express or implied-in-fact undertaking the Government was authorized to make. The Tucker Act supplies jurisdiction, not the substantive payment promise, and fairness alone cannot create an obligation implied only in law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices. Stevens does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Uncertain prospective breach damages do not invalidate an otherwise authorized warranty. No actual absence of an appropriation, delegation or required approval is found in these contracts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sutton applies actual-authority limits to implied and express undertakings; Hatzlachh preserves genuine contract remedies despite the absence of tort relief, without supplying a missing promise.

##### Whether the specifications warranty alone covers these settlements and tort-defense expenses

**Holding:** The implication arising from government specifications assures satisfactory performance of the procurement undertaking; without more it does not promise reimbursement of these manufacturers' veterans' tort settlements or expenses defending those claims. Both petitioners' settlement claims and their separately considered defense-expense claims fail on that contractual-scope ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices. Stevens does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is not a rule ending all contractual damages at delivery, requiring express indemnity for every third-party loss or categorically barring such expenses. Different demonstrated authorized duties remain possible. The Federal Circuit's equation of warranty success with a successful government-contractor defense and resulting causation conclusion is unnecessary and undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Spearin retains its actual specifications undertaking and ordinary breach remedies. Ryan Stevedoring and Weyerhaeuser permit foreseeable third-party consequences of proved service duties without an express indemnity; those duties are not established merely by procurement specifications. Boyle's tort defense remains unchanged; Kania's distinct nonprosecution setting is not adopted as a universal expense bar.

##### Whether Thompson establishes indemnity through compulsory procurement and statutory protection

**Holding:** Thompson's combined reliance on DPA compulsion, specifications, alleged government control and superior knowledge and §707 protection does not establish the asserted authorized implied-in-fact promise to pay third-party settlements and defense costs. Section 707 itself supplies no affirmative reimbursement promise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices. Stevens does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 101's production command and §707's specified liability protection are distinct from governmental assent to reimbursement. Genuine authorized indemnities remain enforceable. The full §707 immunity boundary, defective specifications, relative knowledge, actual breach, contract-specific authority, causation and damage allocation are not determined. Hercules's allegations do not revive an unpreserved separate indemnity theory, and no independent superior-knowledge, purchaser-use, good-faith or takings cause is newly decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both petitioners' warranty settlement and separate expense denials and Thompson's contextual indemnity denial, 6–2. Summary judgments remain; no damages inquiry, payment, further merits remand or contract-litigation fees are ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sutton's authority boundary and actual-contract limits govern; X-Citement Video and Lebron permit defense of the existing favorable judgment on an alternative ground without new factual findings or unpresented independent claims.

#### United States v. Winstar Corp., 518 U.S. 839 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-865; consolidated Glendale, Winstar and Statesman disputes.
**Decided:** July 1, 1996.

##### What actual undertaking and jurisdiction the contract claim requires

**Holding:** The Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. §1491(a)(1), supplies contract jurisdiction but creates no substantive promise. A claimant must establish an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact undertaking, not an obligation imposed only for fairness; review of the certified interlocutory partial-liability judgment does not require a final damages award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court of Appeals found Winstar’s agreement express; the implied-in-fact alternative and takings claims are not independently decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hercules supplies the actual-duty requirement; Romein distinguishes regulation from a contractual undertaking.

##### Whether the transaction writings promise the particular accounting and capital treatment

**Holding:** The integrated Glendale, Winstar and Statesman writings establish express promises of the specified accounting and capital treatment and allocate the financial risk of its withdrawal, subject to the actual contractual qualifications. Statesman’s capital-credit undertaking is distinct from its goodwill allowance; neither regulation alone nor general assistance policy creates the promises.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** The operative terms are distinct:

- **Glendale:** The November 19, 1981 Supervisory Action Agreement preserves contemporaneous Board/FSLIC resolutions and letters rather than superseding them. Resolution 81-710 requires a satisfactory independent accountant's opinion describing goodwill, justifying purchase accounting and substantiating amounts and amortization, with compliance with Memorandum R-31b. The approved accountant treatment allocates $18 million over twelve years and $716.666 million over forty years, straight line. The lower judgment found the accounting condition satisfied; the Court does not turn that finding into a concession or treat every dollar as forty-year goodwill.
- **Winstar:** The integrated agreement, Resolution 84-363 and July 13, 1984 letter permit straight-line amortization of purchase-accounting intangibles over a period not exceeding thirty-five years for regulatory reporting. The maximum is permission, not a required period or a promised damages term. Those accounting forbearances are not acceptable in financial statements required to follow GAAP. Ongoing reserve/net-worth records and regulatory reporting remain required. The Net Worth Maintenance obligation concerns the required capital level, while the agreement's accounting terms define what counts toward it; a change in level is not identical to withdrawal of a promised component.
- **Statesman goodwill:** The March 11, 1988 agreement and Resolution 88-169 permit straight-line amortization not exceeding twenty-five years, subject to a satisfactory accountant's analysis within ninety days describing intangibles and substantiating amounts and methods. The lower judgment found the condition satisfied. Approximately $25.8 million of acquisition goodwill is distinct from the capital credit.
- **Statesman capital credit:** The express $26 million credit consists of $21 million of the FSLIC contribution and $5 million debenture principal. It is credited to the regulatory capital account and constitutes regulatory capital under the identified Insurance Regulation §561.13 definition. Its amount and scope do not borrow the goodwill undertaking's twenty-five-year maximum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal insurance against later regulation, perpetual exemption or finding of insolvency causation. The separate maximum periods are permissions, not mandatory amortization or damages periods.

##### How accounting hierarchy, regulatory reservations and termination terms qualify those promises

**Holding:** The incorporated specific benefits retain contractual significance alongside general assistance terms and ordinary regulatory evolution. The whole integrated agreement controls; neither its long accounting permission nor its shorter ordinary assistance term automatically erases the other, and no defined termination event is presumed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** The following actual terms qualify the identified undertakings and subsequent relief:

**Accounting hierarchy and reserved powers.** Winstar §10 begins with GAAP for going-concern savings-and-loan computations except where the agreement, applicable regulations or contemporaneous Board approval requires otherwise. Ambiguity is resolved consistently with the regulations and approval; when those conflict, the Board's transaction resolution or action controls. The section also includes subsequent Board/FASB clarification, interpretation and amendment, with Board requirements controlling conflicts with FASB. Both priority and change language remain operative.

The July 13 letter reserves statutory powers except enforcement of its enumerated waived requirements and their authorizing statutory provisions insofar as waived. The referenced benefits concern direct net-worth credit for the FSLIC cash contribution; the amortization just described; loan-discount recognition by a level-yield method assuming contractual payments for fifteen years and repayment of the balance at that point, or another level-yield method described in R memoranda or other accounting documents acceptable under GAAP; and dividend limits. The dividend waiver permits sixty, seventy and sixty percent of net income for fiscal years beginning July 1, 1985, 1986 and 1987 respectively, while forbidding dividends that reduce net worth below the level §563.13 requires for institutions insured at least twenty years. These ancillary terms identify the reservation's scope and add no separate damages claim. Powers outside those waivers remain reserved; the exception is neither an unconditional cancellation right nor surrender of Congress's authority.

Statesman §17 similarly preserves agreement/approval exceptions to GAAP, resolves ambiguity by the regulations and transaction approval, and gives that approval priority over conflicting regulations. Its changing-accounting language refers to Board changes, amendments, clarifications and interpretations, including the proposal at 52 Fed. Reg. 39112, its staff, FASB and the stated AICPA-successor reference; Board interpretation controls a conflict with FASB or AICPA. Real-estate valuation separately follows the proposed regulations at 52 Fed. Reg. 39070, those regulations as finally adopted by the Board, or any successor regulations then in effect. It is not simply Winstar's clause under another name.

**Compliance, duration and termination.** Glendale's assistance agreement ordinarily terminates after ten years, when FSLIC payment obligations cease. Appointment of a receiver or other official to liquidate Glendale, or termination of Glendale's or its successor's FSLIC-insured status, terminates the agreement and all FSLIC obligations at that event. Contemporaneous resolutions and letters remain preserved; modifications require written agreement by the parties or successors. The agreement requires no unlawful action or inaction. The particular forward accounting benefit is construed with, not equated to, the ordinary assistance/payment term.

Winstar §13(a) ordinarily ends the agreement after two years except as otherwise specifically provided, or at a different date agreed in writing. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the resulting association, or cessation of its insured-account status, separately terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations at that event. Section 15 requires no unlawful action or inaction. Section 13(b) concerns limited continuing indemnity for the liabilities/claims and related or prosecution litigation expenses it identifies, conditioned on litigation at termination and continued observance of the agreement's conditions, covenants, cooperation and best-efforts duties, with recoveries payable over under its referenced limits. It is not a general continuation of every contractual claim; this judgment does not decide an interaction with a receiver event or find a qualifying indemnity claim.

Statesman §20 requires the acquiring association and its subsidiaries, affiliates and service corporations materially to comply with applicable public laws, regulations, orders and restrictions governing property and business, but does not itself require compliance while applicability is contested in good faith. The proviso establishes neither an actual contest nor an exemption from valid public law. Section 19 ordinarily supplies a five-year term or a different written agreed date, with its specific-exception language and separate §16 sale provision. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the acquiring association or cessation of its insured-account status terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations. Its lawful-action term remains. FIRREA's enactment within the five-year period defeats only an assertion that ordinary expiration had already eliminated every undertaking; it decides neither other triggers nor recoverable duration.

Statesman §19(b) directs distribution/payment of the Special Reserve Account's net credit or debit at termination under the agreement. Section 19(c)'s identified liability/claim and related litigation indemnities require litigation at termination and continuing covenants, cooperation, reporting and best efforts; recoveries must be paid over under the applicable limitations, and continuation remains subject to the liquidation/insurance terminating proviso. Section 19(d) requires action on reports, applications, requests and other submissions actually received by termination that require FSLIC approval, consent or action, with cooperation and final-accounting treatment. None is a general survival clause or a finding of an eligible claim or pending submission in this case.

**Statesman's separate sale conditions.** During seven years, a defined Sale by the acquiring association of any of its own assets, or of capital stock of a subsidiary or affiliate formed to conduct its operations or hold its assets, requires prior written approval and prompt payment of an agreed percentage of net proceeds, capped at fifty percent. In calculating net proceeds, an appropriate return of the acquirer's investment is allocated in proportion to the assets sold relative to total assets. Sale aggregates sales consummated or agreed during ninety days with an aggregate sales price of $20 million. An unapproved defined Sale during five years terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations immediately after consummation notwithstanding §19; approval lies in FSLIC's sole discretion. The exclusion concerns transfers to an acquirer affiliate of stock or assets and liabilities of Federated Financial, Combined Mortgage Corporation, Homestead Mortgage Corporation or Perpetual Financial Corporation, not every affiliate transaction. Seven-year approval/proceeds duties and the five-year termination consequence remain distinct. No such Sale, approval, excluded transfer or termination is found.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general indemnity clause preserves every claim. No liquidation, insurance termination, defined Sale, qualifying transfer, contest, indemnity claim, pending submission or damages cutoff is found merely because its clause exists.

##### Whether actual assistance authority permits financial undertakings while preserving legislation

**Holding:** The transaction-effective contracting and assistance powers, exercised through the actual resolutions and approvals, authorize the identified financial undertakings. Separate authority to prevent Congress from changing law is unnecessary for ordinary monetary responsibility because these contracts confer no regulatory veto; general agency mission alone would not cure an unauthorized promise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Former §1725(c)(3) authorized FSLIC contracting and transaction-effective §1729(f) authorized conditional assistance. Glendale authority covered default prevention/restoration and merger or liability assumption; contributions and guarantees were limited to reasonably necessary liquidation savings unless continued operation was found essential to adequate community savings or home-financing services. Actual resolutions supplied the required determinations and approvals. Winstar and Statesman likewise depend on effective powers and approvals.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may change capital law and institutions must obey it. No hypothetical appropriation/approval defect, equitable right to payment, regulatory injunction or inferred general assistance warranty.

##### Whether withdrawal breaches the promises and assumed risk defeats sovereign-acts discharge

**Holding:** Withdrawal of the promised treatment breaches these financial undertakings. Even assuming public and general sovereign action, sovereign-acts protection and ordinary impossibility do not discharge the change-of-law risk the contracts allocated to the Government; this sufficient ground preserves legislative authority and imposes financial responsibility within the actual bargain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** FIRREA’s goodwill limits and OTS’s distinct §567.1(w) capital-credit treatment prevent the promised performance. The latter treatment is regulatory, not statutory wording expressly naming capital credits. Horowitz does not improve the sovereign’s position over a private promisor on assumed risk.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority selects a complete unmistakability framework: Souter/Stevens/O’Connor/Breyer’s limited inapplicability ground has four votes, Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas’s application-and-satisfaction ground three, and Stone-Zsela supports the narrower actual risk allocation. The substantial-contractual-effect sovereign-acts alternative has Souter/Stevens/Breyer only and is not an independent alternative holding. No majority rule excludes all monetary claims from unmistakability or classifies all FIRREA as non-general.

##### What partial liability establishes about causation and relief

**Holding:** Partial contractual liability is affirmed for all three transactions, including Statesman’s separate goodwill and capital credit, with contract-specific causation, compensable loss, mitigation, applicable contractual periods and damages left for ordinary adjudication. Breach does not establish every consequence or a nominal goodwill/capital-credit award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Each institution must prove recoverable loss under its own bargain and lawful defenses. One component cannot borrow another’s amount or duration; any properly proved termination or other qualifier remains material.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic insolvency causation, guaranteed usability of the whole goodwill balance, fixed recovery period, damages amount, takings entitlement or compelled specific performance against valid law.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Partial liability affirmed, 8–1, and cases remanded for genuinely remaining contract proceedings; no mandatory trial or hearing. Ginsburg would vacate present liability affirmance for bounded contract-by-contract reconsideration under unmistakability, not enter Government judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hercules:** Its actual-authorized-duty requirement controls. The Court distinguishes the absent reimbursement undertaking there from the specific integrated promises here, while preserving its limits on fairness-based liability and automatic damages.
- **Sutton and Merrill:** Actual governmental authority remains necessary; neither the Tucker Act nor equitable appeal supplies missing assent or authority. No particular approval defect is found here.
- **Romein:** Regulation governing a relationship is not itself an immutable contractual obligation. These holdings rest on the additional transaction writings and incorporated promises.
- **Cisneros:** The obligation is enforced with its real qualifications, not enlarged into an unconditional payment or regulatory guarantee.
- **United States v. Texas:** An existing obligation is distinct from a mechanism governing its enforcement. It supplies no damages amount or formula in this case.
- **Lynch and Perry:** Genuine government contractual obligations remain legally significant despite sovereign status. This decision preserves legislation and applies the financial undertakings actually made; it does not adopt a general power to enjoin legislative change.
- **Horowitz:** Public/general sovereign treatment does not itself answer ordinary assumed-risk impossibility. The eight-Justice holding rests on allocated risk, not the additional three-Justice substantial-effect theory.
- **Bowen:** Its retained-power and promise-construction principles remain. The Court does not convert the statutory arrangement there into a rule cancelling every express financial-risk undertaking here.
- **Merrion, United States Trust and Home Telephone:** Retained governmental powers and the particular commitments at issue must be distinguished. No majority adopts a universal rule making the sovereign-construction doctrine irrelevant to all money claims.
- **OPM v. Richmond:** Authority and appropriations limits are not erased by monetary relief; no unsupported defect or general equitable right to payment is found.

No earlier decision is overruled. The additional unmistakability and substantial-effect explanations have only the joins stated in the topology; they do not independently alter controlling doctrine.

#### Lincoln v. Vigil, 508 U.S. 182 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1833.
**Decided:** May 24, 1993.

##### Allocation of this unearmarked lump sum is committed to agency discretion

**Holding:** When Congress supplies a lump-sum appropriation without a binding program earmark or other meaningful allocation standard, an agency’s distribution among authorized statutory purposes is committed to its discretion under §701(a)(2). Legislative-history references and the general federal trust relationship do not require continued funding of this particular program; enforceable statutory boundaries and the separately presented constitutional claim remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Heckler v. Chaney, 470 U.S. 821: discretionary resource-allocation rationale applied beyond the enforcement setting within the statutory limits stated.
- ICC v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 482 U.S. 270: recognized discretionary administrative categories applied.
- Citizens to Preserve Overton Park v. Volpe, 401 U.S. 402: distinguished; an actual statutory standard remains enforceable.
- Webster v. Doe, 486 U.S. 592: separate constitutional review preserved.

##### This organizational and policy change does not require notice and comment

**Holding:** Even assuming the program’s termination is a rule, this reallocation and organizational policy change falls within §553(b)(A)’s exceptions for agency organization and general policy statements and does not require notice and comment. The ruling concerns a change in service availability, not a new binding eligibility rule, and leaves the separate §552 publication question unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Enacted program limits remain enforceable. The independent constitutional claim, §552 publication question and unreviewed remedial issue are reserved. The decision relies on §553(b)(A), not the benefits exception in §553(a)(2), which the Department had undertaken not to invoke.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand; continued funding cannot be compelled on the rejected APA grounds. No entitlement to a particular hearing or authority to spend outside the statutory mission.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morton v. Ruiz, 415 U.S. 199: distinguished; compliance with applicable agency procedures does not create a universal notice-and-comment duty.

#### Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp., 531 U.S. 497

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1551.
**Decided:** February 27, 2001.

##### I. What law governs the external claim-preclusive effect of a federal diversity limitations dismissal

**Holding:** Federal common law governs the claim-preclusive effect of a federal diversity judgment on state-law claims and ordinarily incorporates the preclusion law applied by the courts of the State in which the rendering federal court sits, except when that law conflicts with a specifically demonstrated federal interest. No such conflict exists in this California limitations dismissal, so Maryland must apply California's claim-preclusion law rather than an automatic nationwide rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous for both questions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1738 and the Full Faith and Credit Clause do not directly govern this federal judgment. General finality or uniformity does not establish a conflicting federal interest. Federal-question judgments, materially different process-sanction dismissals and the actual content of California preclusion law are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Maryland automatic-preclusion judgment is reversed and remanded to apply California claim-preclusion law through federal common law. The California federal judgment remains final; no refiling there, reopening or survival of the Maryland tort claims is assured.

##### II. Whether Rule 41(b)'s merits designation determines preclusion in every other forum

**Holding:** Rule 41(b)'s default merits operation governs the dismissal's rendering-court effect and does not itself dictate the claim-preclusive consequences in every other forum. The default yields when the dismissal order specifies otherwise and excludes dismissals for lack of jurisdiction, improper venue or failure to join a party under Rule 19; a merits designation under the default does not replace the federal rule stated in Holding I.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Rule retains its rendering-court function without supplying a nationwide preclusion code. The Court does not decide the distinct Rules Enabling Act question whether a federal court may bar refiling where a state court would dismiss without prejudice; direct validity of the California judgment is outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins: applies the allocation protecting state-created rights from a removal-driven change in substantive consequences.
- Hanna v. Plumer: applies the concerns with forum selection and unequal administration while respecting the actual scope of the federal procedural rule.
- O'Melveny & Myers v. FDIC: preserves exceptional federal fields but rejects general uniformity as sufficient reason for displacement; the federal judgment supplies the source here.
- Atherton v. FDIC: applies the need for an identified federal conflict before substituting uniform substantive content.
- Baker v. General Motors Corp.: distinguishes lawful judgment effect from additional forum consequences; its section 1738 holding concerns state judgments and is not extended as a direct command for federal judgments.

#### New Hampshire v. Maine, 532 U.S. 742

**Docket or dockets:** No. 130, Original.
**Decided:** May 29, 2001.

##### Whether a State may repudiate the boundary interpretation accepted as a basis for prior relief

**Holding:** A court may prevent a litigant from repudiating a clearly inconsistent position after judicial acceptance of the earlier position when the change would produce unfair advantage or impair the earlier adjudication's integrity. Inconsistency, actual acceptance and resulting advantage or detriment are nonexhaustive equitable considerations, not inflexible prerequisites; supported genuine mistake or material change in law, policy or essential fact can alter the inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by all seven other participating Justices; Souter does not participate.

**Material application:** New Hampshire obtained judicial acceptance of the middle-main-channel interpretation of the same 1740 language it now invoked for a Maine-shore boundary. The offshore/inland remedial distinction does not reconcile those positions, and materials already available during the earlier proceedings establish no genuine mistake or intervening essential change. A State receives no exemption on these facts; negotiation or compromise alone would not establish judicial acceptance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary governmental policy changes in other cases are not adjudicated. Claim and issue preclusion are unnecessary to decision.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Davis v. Wakelee supplies the successful-position principle; Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. requires actual inconsistency and contextual attention to explanations rather than categorical estoppel.

##### Whether dismissal establishes an enlarged inland boundary decree

**Holding:** Judicial estoppel bars the present shore-boundary complaint, but dismissal neither establishes inland coordinates nor independently adjudicates title. The 1977 marine-boundary decree remains effective only within its existing geographic terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous eight-Justice Kennedy opinion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Maine's motion is granted and the complaint dismissed. No survey, affirmative geographic decree, title award, damages, injunction or further proceeding is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** New Hampshire v. Maine, 426 U.S. 363, supplies the accepted interpretive premise; the decree at 434 U.S. 1 retains its enacted marine geography without enlargement.

#### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1121.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment

**Holding:** Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may amend substantive or procedural law, prescribe expressly retrospective rules for pending litigation, and authorize judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures. It may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation, and later independent judicial determination of eligibility and the merits does not save such a command. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to that extent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and limit stated here. Breyer concurs only in the judgment, on a narrower ground, and joins no proposition; Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg dissent. No combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** Finality is itself an Article III protection; it does not disappear merely because Congress leaves the underlying merits undecided. Petitioners' action was not pending. It was dismissed as time barred on August 13, 1991, and the dismissal became final without appeal before §27A(b)'s December 19, 1991 enactment. Section 27A(b) does not change the law for litigation still pending; it commands a court to vacate that completed judgment and reinstate the same litigation because the law that produced the dismissal has since changed, and the February 11, 1992 motion sought exactly that vacatur and reinstatement. The statute's objective class and retained adjudication answer the contention that Congress selected parties for liability, but not the finality objection: the judicial act Congress sets aside is the completed dismissal itself, and later independent adjudication cannot restore its conclusiveness. Because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, Landgraf's and Rivers's rules of statutory construction cannot avoid the constitutional question. Morgan Stanley's affirmance by an equally divided Court established no precedent, so the question is decided as one of first impression.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: its affirmance by an equally divided Court, 4–4, issued no opinion and established no precedent; it is neither applied nor distinguished, and the Article III question is decided as one of first impression.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: answers, for completed private judgments, the final-judgment question Robertson left open; its rule that Congress may change the law governing pending cases remains intact.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: applies its bar on legislative prescription of judicial results; it continues to forbid legislatively dictated findings or outcomes in any case.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: applies its protection of completed judicial determinations against legislative displacement; it supports the finality rule without being extended to every statute that affects a judgment.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529: its finality limit on judicial retroactivity is consistent with, but does not itself decide, the limit on legislative reopening; its force is unchanged.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes new law governing the future operation of continuing equitable relief within the applicable remedial framework; that prospective setting does not authorize vacating a completed private judgment.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own defense; it does not concern a command to reopen a private defendant's final judgment.
- Chase Securities Corp. v. Donaldson, 325 U.S. 304: remains a due-process revival authority only; it supplies no answer to the Article III question.
- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S. 350: supplies the limitations rule under which the August 13, 1991 dismissal was entered and to which §27A responds; its force is unchanged.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244, and Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298: retained; their presumption against statutory retroactivity does not avoid the question because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, and Congress's power to prescribe expressly retrospective rules for pending litigation remains subject to other constitutional limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is confined to legislative commands of this kind and leaves existing law in place. Congress may change the law while a case remains pending, expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases subject to other constitutional limits, and change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework, as Wheeling Bridge illustrates. It may not require judges to adopt particular factual findings or impose predetermined liability in any case; Klein and McCullough forbid that legislative adjudication. Relief from a final judgment through constitutionally permissible judicial procedures, including reopening on valid Rule 60(b) grounds, remains available. The Court does not decide §27A(a), the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, any due-process question, or severability beyond withholding this application. It does not hold that every statute affecting a final judgment is invalid, decide every form of later judicial reopening, or address the Government's waiver of its own defenses. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses are not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment of the Sixth Circuit is affirmed. The August 13, 1991 dismissal remains final, and the February 11, 1992 reinstatement motion was properly denied. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to the extent it requires the district court to reopen that dismissal. Supreme Court review is complete; no remand is ordered, and no determination of the underlying securities claims is made.

**Material judgment:** Affirmed, 6–3. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance; Breyer concurs in the judgment only, on the ground that §27A(b) reopens final judgments in a closed, identifiable class of cases without general prospective application. Stevens dissents, joined by Souter and Ginsburg; they would reverse and remand for reinstatement. Breyer's closed-class ground and the dissent's statutory-entitlement position are noncontrolling.

#### Christopher v. Harbury, 536 U.S. 403

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-394.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### A retrospective access claim must identify a concrete lost judicial opportunity

**Holding:** A claim seeking relief for past obstruction of court access must identify a nonfrivolous underlying judicial action, intentional obstruction causing loss or material impairment of the opportunity to pursue it, and presently available relief for that loss that cannot be obtained through surviving litigation. Ordinary notice pleading requires identification of these substantive predicates, not proof of certain success or a futile prior filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls for eight Justices. Thomas concurs only in the judgment.

**Material application:** The complaint did not identify the lost underlying cause. The detention, torture and concealment allegations receive dismissal-stage assumptions and are not adjudicated facts.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The asserted constitutional category and Bivens channel are assumed solely to resolve the defects. Their existence, official immunity, foreign-affairs defenses, the truth of the allegations and the surviving torts' elements are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis v. Casey is extended from actual hindrance of current litigation to the necessary injury and remedial connection for an asserted lost past opportunity; Swierkiewicz retains ordinary notice pleading.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even treating counsel's intentional-infliction theory and lost emergency injunction as part of the pleaded case, this access count identifies no presently attainable remedy unavailable through the surviving tort action. That independently sufficient defect requires dismissal of the revived count.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same eight Justices join this alternative holding; Thomas's judgment agreement supplies no additional rationale.

**Material application:** Counsel's clarification identifies intentional infliction of emotional distress and a lost emergency injunction. The injunction could no longer save Harbury's husband when suit began, while counts 18 and 19 still seek relief for the alleged tort's consequences. No additional presently obtainable access remedy is identified.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A predicate claim defeated for a reason independent of the alleged deception does not establish a viable lost action. Ordinary amendment rules remain applicable to a properly sought amendment on its actual allegations; naming the same tort does not cure the adjudicated distinct-remedy defect.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment reviving the access count is reversed and remanded for dismissal of that count. Surviving tort claims retain their ordinary elements and defenses; the mandate orders no amendment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis requires injury-fitted relief, not a duplicative access label. Malesko supplies no automatic new federal damages channel from an allegation of serious injury.

#### Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-948.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. Hialeah’s interlocking scheme targets Santeria sacrifice on that objective showing; adverse impact alone and the words “ritual” or “sacrifice” alone do not establish the rule’s trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fowler v. Rhode Island, 345 U.S. 67: discrimination in the treatment of religious exercise prohibited.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872: targeting falls outside the neutral-law rule; that rule is not reconsidered here.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: special religious disabilities distinguished from neutral regulation.
- Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693: the individualized-exemption discussion supports requiring a compelling reason for denying religious hardship treatment where comparable secular reasons are assessed.

##### Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability

**Holding:** A law is not generally applicable where it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct threatening the interests invoked to burden religious exercise. Each challenged ordinance fails that minimum requirement here, including 87-72’s express state-law exemption for small-number hog or cattle operations; the Court does not define every boundary of general applicability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Smith, 494 U.S. 872: general-applicability requirement applied, without reopening its neutral-law holding.
- Fowler, 345 U.S. 67: unequal treatment of religious exercise applied.
- Hobbie v. Unemployment Appeals Commission, 480 U.S. 136: protection against unequal treatment of religious observers preserved.

##### Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance

**Holding:** A restriction of religious practice that is nonneutral or not generally applicable must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. These ordinances fail narrow tailoring independently of whether the asserted ends could be compelling because comparable harms are left unregulated and neutral, narrower controls on disposal, care, killing methods and location could address the city’s concerns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: rigorous justification of restrictions on religious exercise applied.
- McDaniel, 435 U.S. 618: demanding scrutiny of religious disabilities applied.
- Arkansas Writers’ Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: lack of narrow tailoring independently suffices under strict scrutiny.
- Schneider v. State, 308 U.S. 147: collateral public harms cannot justify unnecessary suppression when direct regulation is available.

##### The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the government burdens protected religious conduct but leaves appreciable comparable harm unregulated despite feasible controls, it has not established that its asserted interest justifies the selective restriction at the highest constitutional level. Hialeah’s substantial underinclusion independently defeats its compelling-interest showing for this scheme; the decision does not declare animal welfare or public health inherently unimportant.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Material application:** Ordinance87-40 incorporates Florida’s unnecessary-or-cruel-killing rule as construed to reject religious necessity while accepting comparable secular reasons. Ordinance87-52 prohibits owning, keeping, possessing, sacrificing or slaughtering enumerated animals or any other animal with intent to use it for food, and limits application to killing in any ritual regardless of whether flesh or blood will actually be consumed; its separate exception allows licensed establishments to slaughter animals specifically raised for food where properly zoned or permitted under state/local law and Agriculture Department rules. Ordinance87-71 defines sacrifice as unnecessary killing, tormenting, torturing or mutilating in a ritual or ceremony not primarily for food consumption and prohibits it citywide. Ordinance87-72 requires food slaughter in a properly zoned slaughterhouse meeting health, safety and sanitation codes, but separately exempts persons or organizations slaughtering or processing for sale small numbers of hogs or cattle weekly in accordance with the state-law exemption. The distinct exclusions, comparable secular harms and available neutral controls support the holdings; 87-72’s independent validity in another setting remains reserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enactment-history discussion lacks a majority. No exemption regime replaces Smith, no universal general-applicability definition is adopted, and exemption from a genuinely evenhanded anti-cruelty law remains undecided. Narrow tailoring and the compelling-interest failure independently sustain invalidation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief against ordinances 87-40, 87-52, 87-71 and 87-72 as presented. No damages or immunity ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Star v. B.J.F., 491 U.S. 524: substantial unregulated comparable harm undermines the asserted exceptional justification; no broader rule is imported from a separate writing.
- Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: the asserted interest must justify the particular restriction at the required level.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

##### II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power

**Holding:** RFRA's general application to State and local laws cannot be sustained under §5 because it substitutes its substantial-burden, compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means standard across the field for the constitutional boundary retained in Smith, without the required enforcement relationship. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Church of Scientology and Swanner are limited prospectively in the invalid §5-based State and local field, preserving completed judgments, independent grounds and statutory reasoning where validly operative. Agostini retains Lemon as the governing Establishment Clause test and updates it into one inquiry with three routes: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives the attribution-and-function inquiry. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test. Plyler's prospective-relief rule does not authorize redefinition of substantive rights.

#### Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats, Inc. v. Weiss

##### Whether government may enforce Orthodox requirements as commercial law

**Holding:** Government may not adopt a religious denomination’s account of orthodoxy as its coercively enforceable civil standard or determine religious compliance as governmental truth. It may enforce objectively verifiable representations about actual preparation or a named private certification, provided officials neither decide theological correctness nor give the certifier compulsory civil control.

No conversion or additional coercion finding is required once the governmental religious function is established. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Agostini prohibits government adoption of doctrine as public truth without reviving independent Lemon prongs. Ballard distinguishes provable factual representations from adjudication of religious truth.

##### Whether Section 201-f’s rabbinic-washing condition confers impermissible civil authority

**Holding:** A civil regulatory condition that requires performance or authorization by a religious office gives religious authority impermissible civil force when that office supplies the governing religious judgment. Section 201-f’s challenged washing condition has that character; private expertise about facts remains permissible without transferring governing civil authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's unanimous opinion of the Court.

Paragraph (1)(b) requires religiously conforming washing within seventy-two hours of slaughter and each subsequent seventy-two-hour period, by a duly ordained Orthodox rabbi or the rabbi’s authorized person. **Limits and questions not reached:** The decision concerns the allocation of civil governing authority, not the religious correctness of washing. Private factual expertise remains permissible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Larkin’s civil-veto rule applies to mandatory religious authorization in commerce. Kiryas Joel’s denominational-authority boundary is not limited to territorial government; its distinct prior coalitions are not combined.

### Actual federal contractual undertakings and allocated regulatory risk

**Governing question:** Formation, authority, scope and regulatory-risk allocation in a Tucker Act contract claim.

**Current rule:** Section 1491(a)(1) supplies jurisdiction, not a substantive promise: an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact undertaking is necessary, and fairness cannot create an obligation implied only in law. The Government’s ordinary specifications warranty assures performance of the prescribed procurement, but does not itself promise payment of a manufacturer’s third-party tort settlements or defense expenses. Compelled procurement and Defense Production Act §707 protection do not themselves create an indemnity or reimbursement promise. A demonstrated authorized financial undertaking may allocate the risk that valid later legislation prevents the promised treatment. Even assuming public and general sovereign action, ordinary impossibility does not discharge that assumed change-of-law risk. The actual bargain and its qualifications govern; general regulatory permission alone creates no immutable promise.

**Authority by component:**

- Hercules — actual authorized undertaking, specifications-warranty scope and §707 limits, 1996-03-04; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).
- Winstar — holdings 1 and 3–5, actual authority, allocated risk and ordinary contract loss, 1996-07-01; all nine support the actual-undertaking requirement; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (eight) support the common financial-risk and assumed-risk holdings.

**Present operation effective:** Winstar, July 1, 1996, for allocated regulatory risk; Hercules’s scope and authorization limits remain.

**Exceptions and limits:** Congress retains its legislative power and the promisee must obey valid law. Contractual responsibility creates no injunction, automatic causation, goodwill-balance award, guaranteed recovery duration or takings entitlement. Causation, recoverable loss, mitigation, actual periods, conditions and termination provisions remain independent. A genuine authorized undertaking may cover third-party losses without an express indemnity; neither uncertain damages nor tort immunity destroys an actual contract.

**Open question:** Winstar supplies no majority comprehensive unmistakability framework or substantial-contractual-effect exception to sovereign acts. Its four-Justice threshold account and three-Justice substantial-effect alternative are noncontrolling; the eight-Justice assumed-risk holding does not depend on either.

**Holdings navigation:** Hercules, Inc. v. United States, 516 U.S. 417; United States v. Winstar Corp., 518 U.S. 839 (1996).

### Claim preclusion after federal diversity judgments

**Governing question:** The source and content of a federal diversity judgment’s claim-preclusive effect in another forum.

**Current rule:** Federal common law governs the external claim-preclusive effect of a federal diversity judgment on state-law claims. It ordinarily adopts the preclusion law used by the courts of the State where the rendering federal court sits, unless that law conflicts with a specifically demonstrated federal interest. General uniformity or finality alone does not establish the exception. Rule 41(b)’s merits designation governs rendering-court effect and does not independently extinguish the claim in every other forum. Its default yields to a contrary dismissal order and excludes lack of jurisdiction, improper venue and failure to join a Rule 19 party.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal source, ordinary incorporation and concrete-conflict exception: Canonical Decision Record `Semtek_International_v_Lockheed_Martin_merits_2001-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Federal law ordinarily adopts rendering-state preclusion for diversity judgments, 2001-02-27; Scalia’s unanimous opinion.
- Rule 41(b) scope, exceptions and separate external effect: the same Record, Holdings / II. Rule 41(b)'s merits designation does not supply universal claim preclusion, 2001-02-27; the same unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Semtek, February 27, 2001.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 1738 and the Full Faith and Credit Clause do not directly govern a federal judgment. The rule does not authorize refiling in the rendering federal court or promise an opportunity to relitigate elsewhere. Federal-question judgments and materially different process-sanction dismissals are outside the decision. Whether a rendering federal court may bar refiling when state procedure would dismiss without prejudice is a separate unresolved Rules Enabling Act question.

### Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments

**Governing question:** Whether Congress may, by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate its final judgment and reinstate the same litigation.

**Current rule:** Article III protects the conclusive disposition of a private case by a federal court once its judgment has become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may amend substantive or procedural law, change the law while a case remains pending, expressly make a new limitations rule applicable to pending cases subject to other constitutional limits, change the law governing the future operation of an injunction within the applicable remedial framework, and authorize judicial relief through constitutionally permissible procedures, including reopening on valid Rule 60(b) grounds. It may not, solely by changing the law previously applicable to a completed private action, require an Article III court to vacate that final judgment and reinstate the same litigation. Finality does not disappear because Congress leaves eligibility and the underlying merits to later independent judicial determination: the judicial act set aside is the completed judgment itself, and an objectively defined class with retained adjudication answers only the separate objection that Congress selected parties for liability. Section 27A(b) is unconstitutional to that extent and cannot require a court to reinstate an action whose private judgment had become final through the ordinary judicial process. Congress may not in any case require judges to adopt particular factual findings or impose predetermined liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Article III finality rule, its qualifications, its application to a final unappealed limitations dismissal and the extent of §27A(b)'s invalidity: Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. — Congress may not, solely by changing the applicable law, require an Article III court to reopen a final private judgment, April 18, 1995. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas; those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and limit. Breyer concurs only in the judgment, on his closed-class ground, and joins no proposition; Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg dissent.

**Present operation effective:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc., April 18, 1995.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule is confined to legislative commands of this kind and leaves existing law in place. It does not hold that every statute affecting a final judgment is invalid or decide every form of later judicial reopening. Section 27A(a), the application of §27A(b) to litigation still pending on direct review, any due-process question, severability beyond withholding this application, and the Government's waiver of its own defenses are not decided. Fraud, reliance, causation, liability, damages, and ordinary merits defenses are not adjudicated. Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994, affirmed by an equally divided Court without opinion, established no precedent on this question, which Plaut decides as one of first impression. Breyer's closed-class ground and the dissent's statutory-entitlement position are noncontrolling.

**Related current rules:** Federal rules applied to parties govern still-open direct review concerns judicial retroactivity alone, which supplies no reopening entitlement; Beam's finality limit on judicial retroactivity is consistent with, but does not itself decide, this limit on legislative reopening. Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct separates express temporal direction from constitutional authority; because §27A(b) expressly directs reinstatement, the ordinary presumption against retroactivity cannot avoid the constitutional question. Robertson's rule that Congress may change the law governing pending cases remains intact; Plaut answers, for completed private judgments, the final-judgment question Robertson left open.

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test. A public-employment restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality/general-applicability inquiry rather than be absorbed into an employee-speech balance. Religious impact or sincerity alone does not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability, 1993-06-11. Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). - Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance: Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah — Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance, 1993-06-11. ### Denominational selection for general governmental authority

**Governing question:** When creating a special public district impermissibly allocates civic power on a denominational basis.

**Current rule:** The State may not select a religious denomination as the recipient of general governmental authority, even when lay officials formally exercise the power. Independently, an anomalous particular grant aligned with a denomination and lacking a neutral basis assuring comparable treatment violates the required governmental neutrality. Religious accommodation may preserve equal educational access but does not cure denominational allocation of civic power. Demographic religious concentration alone does not establish the violation, and neither rationale makes every special statute or every accommodation invalid.

**Authority by component:**

- Five-Justice denominational-allocation ground: Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. O'Connor does not join that portion, and Kennedy's separate judgment rationale is not counted as a join.
- Six-Justice particular-grant ground and accommodation limit: Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet — The particular denominational grant lacks a neutral basis for equal treatment; Accommodation preserves educational access but does not cure this allocation, 1994-06-27. Souter's opinion, Part II-C and the implementing limits in Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg: six Justices. ### Religious nonmedical-care accommodation and public coverage control

**Governing question:** Reimbursement of independently identifiable secular care for religious patients declining medical treatment.

**Current rule:** Congress may accommodate religious refusal of treatment by reimbursing only the independently identifiable nonmedical component of an existing health benefit for patients whose physical condition otherwise qualifies, within the enacted coverage limits and effective public control. This limited accommodation need not include every comparable secular objection. It does not authorize payment for devotional services or make all religion-specific benefits lawful.

An institution may initially recommend coverage when public officials retain independent statutory eligibility responsibility, may demand sufficient objective information and may deny payment for inadequate proof. Exemption from compelled religiously objectionable examinations does not eliminate the physical-condition standard or reliable nonmedical evidence. The separate institutional supervision exemption does not eliminate review of noncovered, excessive or fraudulent billing. Formal public authority must remain meaningful in actual operation.

Supported operational challenges require examination of the financed function and actual public control on the evidence and reasonable supported inferences. A secular label and retained authority do not prove compliance; religious affiliation, private prayer and possible diversion do not prove a violation. Relief must correct the established prohibited function while preserving independently lawful applications.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded accommodation and no requirement to include every secular objection: Canonical Decision Record `Childrens_Healthcare_Is_a_Legal_Duty_v_Min_De_Parle_merits_2001-04-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This limited secular-care accommodation is permissible in its terms, 2001-04-23; five direct joins.
- Initial recommendation versus independent public eligibility and billing control: Canonical Decision Record `Childrens_Healthcare_Is_a_Legal_Duty_v_Min_De_Parle_merits_2001-04-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Initial institutional review does not necessarily transfer final public power, 2001-04-23; all nine.
- Actual operation, fitted relief and the conditions limiting the permission: Canonical Decision Record `Childrens_Healthcare_Is_a_Legal_Duty_v_Min_De_Parle_merits_2001-04-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. The operational judgment requires assessment under the actual-function rules; Statutory conditions preserved by these holdings, 2001-04-23; five direct joins to Part III.

**Present operation effective:** 2001-04-23.

**Statutory conditions:**

Medicare payment requires an effective election and a condition that would qualify for the corresponding inpatient-hospital or extended-care benefit at an ordinary hospital or skilled nursing facility. The signed election states conscientious opposition to nonexcepted medical treatment and inconsistency with sincere religious belief. Excepted treatment means treatment received involuntarily or required by federal, state or local law. An election ordinarily takes effect upon execution and continues until revoked. Written voluntary notice revokes it; nonexcepted treatment reimbursed under Medicare produces statutory deemed revocation. The form separately warns that receipt of nonexcepted medical services revokes the election and may restrict future services. After two elections and revocations, the next election waits one year from the most recent revocation and later elections wait five years.

Constitutional permission depends on independently established statutory institutional eligibility; it supplies neither a subsidy entitlement nor a finding of compliance. Qualifying institutions must be tax exempt and lawfully operated. They may furnish only nonmedical nursing items and services, exclusively to patients who choose sole reliance on religious healing and whose beliefs conflict with accepting medical health services. They must deliver that care through nonmedical nursing staff experienced in those patients' physical needs, on a 24-hour inpatient basis. Because of their religious beliefs, they must provide no medical items or services to their patients, through staff or otherwise; this includes screening, examination, diagnosis, prognosis, treatment and drug administration. An institution may not be owned by, share common ownership with, or hold an ownership interest in a provider of medical treatment or services; interests below five percent are disregarded for this ownership rule. Separately, it may not be affiliated with such a provider or an individual who owns an interest in one. The separate affiliation exceptions cover uncompensated governing-body service, a director, trustee, officer, employee or staff member of the nonmedical institution having a family relationship with an individual affiliated with, or holding an ownership interest in, a medical-treatment provider, and vendors serving both types of providers. Accreditation may substitute only for conditions for which the Secretary finds reasonable assurance of compliance. Utilization committees review admissions, duration, extended stays and services, keep records and satisfy additional necessary review requirements. Institutions must supply required coverage and quality information and meet necessary health-and-safety requirements. The patient examination exemption preserves the sufficient-condition-information requirement; the distinct institutional supervision exemption preserves billing review for noncoverage, excess and fraud.

The Secretary must estimate expenditures before each fiscal year beginning with fiscal 2000 and monitor them beginning with fiscal 1999. If the estimate exceeds the statutory trigger, proportional payment reduction is required, subject to the Secretary's option to impose alternative coverage conditions or limitations sufficient to meet the trigger. The unadjusted trigger starts at $20 million for fiscal 1998 and increases by the prescribed annual consumer-price index; the next year's trigger is reduced or increased by the previous excess or deficit, with a $50 million ceiling on an increase. Permitted patient charges increase by the reduction amount. The review restriction concerns the identified expenditure estimates and reductions, not every coverage determination. After three consecutive excessive-expenditure years beginning no earlier than fiscal 2002, payment from the next January 1 is limited to patients with elections then effective and only for those elections' duration. Medicaid's statutory substitutions and supervision exemptions require their own application; Medicare's election mechanism is not silently made the entire Medicaid regime.


**Effective transition:** The enactment applies to services furnished from August 5, 1997. Its July 1, 1998 initial-regulation deadline and recognition of good-faith elections before those regulations took effect establish no individual compliance. Medicare and Medicaid retain their distinct statutory conditions; constitutional permission supplies no individual subsidy entitlement.
