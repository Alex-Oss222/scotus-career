#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, Wygant and Paradise, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Shaw v. Reno (June 28, 1993): leaves its rejection of the design-only districting claim untouched; the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements also remain unchanged.

##### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Material application:** The lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence. The Tenth Circuit's recorded stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and the concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority keep their ordinary force in their actual scope. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings, and a stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0, for application of the common standard. The remand directions require the court to identify the actual program and preserved challenge; assess the supported objective and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories and scope to it; examine presumptions, certification routes and review in operation; examine burdens and concrete, practicable alternatives; and examine continued justification. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails, and relief addresses the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. No program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract is ordered.

#### Brown v. City of Oneonta

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket stated; reviewed judgment: 221 F.3d 329 (2d Cir. 2000).

**Decided:** November 13, 2001.

##### Whether a victim description exempts deliberate racial selection from equal-protection review

**Holding:** When officials intentionally select people for investigation by race, they use a racial classification even if a victim supplied that feature and no hostility is shown. The government must establish an important lawful investigative objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that objective and the actual selection, its coverage and burdens, including a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives offering comparable investigative value with materially less racial disadvantage.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for all nine Justices controls the classification rule, its standard and the bounded remand.

**Material application:** A reliable description's race, age, sex, injury, location and timing may support a fitted investigation. The actual sweep and burdens, including the alleged encounter with a woman, require examination. Testimony, records and reasonable inferences may establish fit; the rule requires neither a commissioned study nor universally the least restrictive means.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consent to questioning does not itself eliminate an intentional racial burden. Group membership alone does not prove an encounter, injury or a defendant's responsibility. Personal liability, coercion, immunity and relief remain separate inquiries; immunity depends on law at the September 1992 conduct, not retrospective notice from this decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The equal-protection dismissal is reversed insofar as it rests on the categorical victim-description exemption. Independently resolved claims, existing Fourth Amendment dispositions and the university officials' earlier list-release immunity judgment remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña (June 12, 1995): extended to deliberate investigative selection; the common substantial-justification standard governs rather than strict scrutiny.
- Engineering Contractors (1998) and City of Dallas (1999): applied for evidence-supported fit, actual burdens and concrete practicable alternatives.
- Eisenberg (May 30, 2000): its acknowledgment of the binding common standard is preserved; its distinct educational component is not extended to criminal investigation.
- Lesage (November 29, 1999): applied to keep the classification inquiry separate from individual causation, immunity and consequential relief.

#### Saucier v. Katz

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1977; judgment below, 194 F.3d 962.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### The binding remand instruction and its limited authority

**Authority of the judgment:** Souter announces a 5–4 vacatur and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the exact instruction to apply existing objective qualified immunity separately from the force merits on proper interlocutory assumptions, leaving both grant and denial of immunity available. No other Justice joins Souter's full reasoning. His distinct objective-defense rationale, Stone-Zsela's authoritative-reliance replacement and the three-Justice concurrence's force-specific objection are not logical subsets. No controlling Marks rationale establishes a new general immunity rule or mandatory merits-first sequence. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would direct immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harlow, Anderson and Graham retain their existing operation. Agreement between Souter and dissenters creates no new controlling sequence rule. The preserved legal inquiry uses September 24, 1994 law and plaintiff-favorable interlocutory assumptions, including disputed participation; the Parker-only account is not an appellate finding. There is no ultimate force, violation, participation, immunity, causation or injury determination. First Amendment, false-arrest, supervisory and prospective-relief branches are not reopened; the federal-officer damages channel is assumed without a new cause of action or institutional remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must perform the specified existing-law inquiry. Separable legal issues may be decided on the proper assumptions; genuinely material factual disputes remain for the appropriate forum. The mandate requires no actual-authoritative-reliance proof, additional reliance submissions, automatic trial, hearing, liability finding or damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Graham v. Connor:** Its complete, objective, on-scene force inquiry remains the merits rule, including the circumstances and reasonable factual perceptions confronting the officer. No physical-injury prerequisite, least-dangerous-tactic command or categorical security exception is added.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Its existing objective personal-immunity defense remains in force; a separate proposal to replace it is not the Court's law.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Its particularized legal-notice inquiry remains available under the remand; neither an identical-case requirement nor proof of actual authoritative reliance replaces it.
- **Hunter v. Bryant:** Its judicial legal inquiry under proper summary-judgment assumptions governs the return; the court may not choose a more favorable competing factual account for the officer.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its consideration of all relevant preconduct law and ordinary preservation limits remain; later decisions cannot supply September 24, 1994 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its exclusion of evidentiary-sufficiency disputes from immediate immunity review remains applicable to disputed participation.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its distinction between a separable assumed-fact legal defense and a factual-sufficiency appeal remains; the present judgment creates no additional appellate jurisdiction.
- **Sterling v. Borough of Minersville:** Its unanimous participation-review rule applies to the supported two-officer account. Its different confidentiality and April 1997 notice holdings do not answer this force claim or supply earlier notice.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Its contextual force analysis remains; its supported immediate armed attack and absence of a contrary material account do not establish the facts here. Its later decision supplies no September 1994 notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its distinction between constitutional violation and conduct-date personal immunity remains, without requiring a factual twin; its 1999 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap:** Its retention of the existing objective defense remains; its distinct disclosure claims do not settle this force application, and its 2001 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Conn v. Gabbert:** Its reservation of mandatory immunity sequencing is not displaced by a controlling sequence holding here.
- **Marks v. United States:** No common narrower rationale is identified among the materially different judgment-supporting writings; it supplies no basis to combine them into a new rule.

#### Overton v. Bazzetta, 539 U.S. 126 — prison visitation and compatible family association

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-94.
**Decided:** June 16, 2003.

##### Family association compatible with confinement

**Holding:** Incarceration does not extinguish protected family association that is compatible with confinement. Restrictions on the retained interest are governed by Turner's reasonable relationship to legitimate penological objectives, considering the rational connection, actual alternative means, institutional impact of accommodation, and ready alternatives; the standard does not demand the least restrictive means.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No right to unrestricted visits or every preferred visitor follows. Prisoners must establish the burden and missing reasonable relationship; officials must identify legitimate neutral objectives and supported connections, with administrative latitude. Actual communication alternatives and institutional conditions matter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pell preserves compatible communication; Turner supplies the deferential operational inquiry, Shaw rejects a special heightened tier, and Gerber requires actual consideration rather than categorical extinction of the claimed compatible interest.

##### Facial validity of the child-visitor restrictions

**Holding:** The facial showing is insufficient to invalidate the limitation of under-eighteen visitors to the inmate's children, stepchildren, grandchildren, and siblings, the exclusion of a child after parental rights terminate, or the requirement of accompaniment by an adult immediate family member of the child or inmate or the child's legal guardian. These provisions bear a reasonable relationship to child protection and orderly supervision under Turner.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices; Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

**Material application:** Noncontact visits still require child protection and supervision. The record identifies no equally effective ready alternative at negligible institutional cost.

##### Facial validity of the former-prisoner restrictions

**Holding:** Requiring a former prisoner both to be immediate family and to obtain the warden's prior approval survives this facial Turner challenge because it reasonably addresses security and renewed criminal contacts while preserving an approved family exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices; Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

##### Facial validity of the repeated-substance-abuse visitation sanction

**Holding:** The record does not establish facial invalidity of suspending ordinary visits after repeated substance-abuse violations, preserving attorney and clergy visits, and allowing an application for discretionary restoration after two years. Deterrence, remaining communication, and institutional costs establish a sufficient Turner relationship on this facial showing; two years marks eligibility to apply, not guaranteed restoration or a constitutional safe harbor for every later duration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices; Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Letters and calls count according to their actual usefulness. A less severe sanction is not a ready alternative merely because it is less severe. Indefinite suspension, irrational restoration decisions and an actual absence of realistic contact require application of the governing rule to their own facts.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the facial injunction and remand; two years is eligibility to request restoration, not guaranteed restoration or a constitutional safe harbor.

##### The independent facial Eighth Amendment challenge to visitation loss

**Holding:** Loss of ordinary visitation does not by itself establish objectively serious deprivation or a substantial risk of serious harm. The challenged regulatory design and this facial record fail that threshold under Wilson, Farmer, Helling, and Delaney, so the independent facial Eighth Amendment judgment cannot stand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Objective seriousness and culpability remain distinct. A proved severe or dangerous application remains reviewable; actual awareness may be proved circumstantially but obviousness does not compel it, and an objectively reasonable response defeats deliberate indifference. Preventive relief requires current serious danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse likely to continue.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wilson, Farmer, Helling and Delaney retain their objective-deprivation, subjective-fault and preventive-risk requirements; an association injury cannot substitute for those elements.

#### Atkins v. Virginia, 536 U.S. 304

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-8452.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Execution is unavailable for established intellectual disability

**Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit execution of a person with established intellectual disability: significantly impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations and developmental onset. The State must provide a reliable procedure to determine that status; a score, diagnostic label or courtroom impression alone does not establish it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; seven Justices adopt the complete rule and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes no numerical cutoff, proof allocation or universal hearing design. It does not resolve retroactivity in another collateral case or other disability categories. Intellectual functioning, adaptive limitations and developmental onset must be evaluated together; individual mitigation alone does not satisfy the categorical restriction.

**Material application:** Atkins’s defense psychologist reported an IQ of 59 and diagnosed disability, while the State expert disputed that diagnosis. The Court does not resolve this conflict or treat the reported score as conclusive.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the death-sentence judgment and remand for reliable individualized determination. Established intellectual disability requires a lawful noncapital disposition. The convictions remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Trop supplies the evolving-standards inquiry using objective domestic evidence. Coker and Enmund supply categorical proportionality grounded in culpability and legitimate punishment purposes. Ford supports lawful procedures implementing a constitutional death restriction without equating incompetence and intellectual disability. Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989), is overruled only insofar as it permits execution of persons with established intellectual disability. Penry v. Johnson retains its requirement of effective mitigation where death remains lawful.

#### Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-286.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### No in-effect federal standard supplies the express-preemption predicate here

**Holding:** Section 1392(d)'s express restriction requires an in-effect federal motor-vehicle safety standard governing the same aspect of performance as the asserted state duty. The asserted antilock-equipment defect claims are not expressly displaced on this record because that federal predicate is absent; the Court does not decide whether common-law duties themselves are safety standards within that provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), eight Justices; Scalia concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood, April 21, 1993: applies its attention to the aspect of performance actually covered, without importing the separate railroad speed holding or treating every braking rule as coverage of antilock omission.
- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., June 24, 1992: applies clause- and duty-specific analysis; its particular cigarette holdings do not answer all Safety Act questions.
- Paccar, Inc. v. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, 573 F.2d 632: treats its judicial invalidation as the regulatory-history predicate, not as a new federal right to avoid common-law liability.

##### Judicial invalidation alone establishes no actual federal conflict

**Holding:** An express preemption clause does not categorically foreclose inquiry into an independently established actual conflict with operative federal law or a demonstrated federal objective. Here neither impossibility nor interference with such an objective is established by judicial invalidation and the absence of an operative antilock requirement, so the asserted conflict does not bar these common-law claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), eight Justices; Scalia concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., June 24, 1992: rejects the lower court's universal inference that any express provision exhausts every possible actual-conflict inquiry; its enacted-clause holdings remain intact.
- Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, June 18, 1992: preserves the shared rule against automatic displacement from overlap; no physical-impossibility-only theory or broader separate theory is made Court law.
- Paccar, Inc. v. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, 573 F.2d 632: distinguishes inadequate support for a mandatory standard from an affirmative policy protecting noninstallation against state duties.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1397(k)'s compliance saving clause is not held to immunize every common-law action from an actual conflict. The Court does not decide whether tort duties are standards under §1392(d), every effect of the saving clause when a standard exists, or a federal field-wide ceiling. It does not find all air-brake regulation absent, invent an agency policy from an unproduced revision, or decide defect, accident causation, consortium entitlement, wrongful-death recovery, or damages.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The return of the Myrick and Lindsey common-law claims for further proceedings is affirmed. The abandoned Georgia RICO contention, Fruehauf dismissal and stipulated predecessor treatment are undisturbed. Ordinary liability proof and defenses remain.

#### Geier v. American Honda Motor Co., 529 U.S. 861 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1811.
**Decided:** May 22, 2000.

##### I. The express clause does not independently extinguish this saved common-law claim

**Holding:** Former §1392(d), read with §1397(k), does not expressly displace this common-law no-airbag claim merely because the car complied with FMVSS 208. The common-law saving rule defeats categorical compliance immunity without declaring that damages duties can never be state safety standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1392(d) permits enforcement of identical standards and higher performance requirements for vehicles or equipment procured for government use. Section 1397(k) separately saves common-law liability despite compliance; the procurement permission is not transplanted into that provision.

##### II. Preserved common-law claims remain subject to an independently established actual conflict

**Holding:** The presence of the express clause and compliance saving provision does not foreclose an independently demonstrated conflict between a state common-law duty and an operative federal command or supported regulatory objective. Federal compliance, overlapping regulation, agency preference or regulatory silence alone does not establish that conflict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

##### III. The general no-airbag duty defeats this deliberately chosen transition

**Holding:** The presented general duty to install a driver's airbag is preempted because it would displace FMVSS 208's deliberately retained restraint alternatives and phased transition, which were operative means of promoting safety through development and public acceptance. This conflict sustains dismissal only of that general theory; compliance does not immunize every federally permitted design, and nonconflicting federal-minimum, noncompliance, defective-installation, performance, risk-specific and genuinely model-specific duties remain available when otherwise supported.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins. The four partial joiners dissent from this holding and the judgment.

**Material application:** The operative alternatives, phase-in percentages and differentiated incentives supported the chosen development-and-acceptance transition. The conflict requires no fixed technology mix, minimum count of airbag-free vehicles, or command that this Accord lack an airbag. Later safety evidence cannot change the governing historical regulation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general motor-vehicle field immunity, fixed technology allocation, binding deference to litigation advocacy, universal all-tort exemption or impossibility-only test is adopted. No Honda-specific production, credit, attribution, due-care or testing fact, and no completed belt-law trigger, is found. The Court neither invalidates the standard nor decides whether an independently presented different duty would conflict.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal only of the general no-airbag theory. No factual remand, agency proceeding, new claim or determination of ordinary tort elements follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick:** Applies its independent actual-conflict inquiry and distinguishes its regulatory absence from this affirmative, operative transition. Its rejection of compliance immunity and unsupported federal purposes remains intact.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.:** Applies clause- and duty-specific construction. Its distinct cigarette provisions supply neither a universal tort exemption nor a rule excluding every independent conflict inquiry.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Preserves the rejection of automatic displacement from regulatory overlap. No separate physical-impossibility formulation becomes the Court's exclusive rule.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Applies the need to compare actual substantive duties and keep interpretive weight within statutory authority. Damages duties can be requirements without every such duty being preempted.
- **Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Shanklin:** Preserves its specific device-selection holding and its distinction from a universal minimum-standard ceiling; this case rests on its own regulatory method.
- **United States v. Locke / Intertanko v. Locke:** Preserves its subject-specific federal allocation and full enacted saving language. Its national vessel field does not establish a motor-vehicle field immunity.
- **National Mining Association v. Department of the Interior; Public Lands Council v. Babbitt:** Preserve actual delegated authority, distinct statutory conditions and temporal limits. Neither authorizes later advocacy to manufacture an earlier regulatory objective or to disregard common-law saving language.

#### Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc., 518 U.S. 470 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-754 and 95-886.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### What federal predicate is required and whether substantial-equivalence clearance supplies design approval

**Holding:** Section 360k(a) requires an applicable federal requirement and a state requirement respecting a human-use device that differs from or adds to it and concerns safety, effectiveness or another matter included in the federal requirement, subject to subsection (b). This lead's substantial-equivalence clearance does not establish substantive federal approval of its design and therefore does not preempt the restored negligent-design or design-based strict-liability claims on that asserted ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The 1982 marketing letter and §807.97 separate clearance from substantive safety approval. Class III status, grandfathering and manufacturing conformance to the original design do not establish PMA or a substantive FDA design choice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No design defect, breach, causation, actual PMA case or hypothetical device-specific standard is decided. Regulatory overlap alone supplies no independent actual conflict.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Design restoration affirmed in No. 95-754, 9–0; state-law elements remain necessary.

##### Whether a genuinely parallel state conduct duty becomes additional because it supplies damages

**Holding:** Section 360k(a) does not expressly preempt an independently available state duty that genuinely requires the same conduct as the applicable federal duty merely because state law adds damages. Requiring negligence or an unreasonable hazard in addition to violation of that same conduct obligation can narrow recovery without making the substantive duty different; a claim requiring different warnings or production conduct is not parallel merely because its pleading invokes federal law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Actual operation controls: equal conduct plus narrower recovery elements differs from liability despite federal compliance or a requirement of additional warning/production conduct. The appellate court reached the reply-stage contention and assumed, without deciding, Florida recognition and adequate pleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No private FDCA enforcement action is created. Florida recognition, pleading, violation, causation and relief remain undecided; independently established actual conflict remains a separate question.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical parallel-duty barrier in No. 95-886 is reversed and remanded, 9–0; no exemption must first be sought for an otherwise nonpreempted equal duty.

##### Whether the asserted generic manufacturing and warning predicates preempt the presented duties

**Holding:** The general manufacturing and prescription-information requirements invoked here do not establish a federal counterpart that makes the presented ordinary manufacturing and warning duties different or additional within section 360k(a). The challenged preemption barriers are therefore reversed for negligent manufacture, failure to warn and corresponding strict-liability theories, without exempting all tort law or all claims involving generally applicable regulations.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices.

**Material application:** Part 820 and §801.109 impose genuine obligations but their legal force and shared subject do not establish the specific substantive federal choice these duties would alter. Master records and original-design conformance do not substantively approve safety; label submission is not approval of precise contents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General framing does not mean no federal command, and no universal manufacturer- or model-specificity prerequisite applies. Ambiguous duties may be identified in ordinary proceedings; no mandatory preliminary hearing in all products cases.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Manufacturing/warning and corresponding strict-liability barriers reversed, 6–3. O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent as to genuinely divergent duties, while joining the equal-duty remand.

**Independent alternative holding:** Within the statutory comparison, the FDA reasonably interprets section 360k through section 808.1(d) to require specific counterpart regulations or other specific applicable requirements before divergent state requirements are displaced. Giving that formal construction substantial weight independently supports survival of these manufacturing and warning claims, while preserving its general-applicability, equal-duty and divergent substantive-application qualifications.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Breyer’s Part V, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela does not join. The FDA’s formal §808.1 interpretation receives weight within actual delegation and enacted ambiguity, not authority to contradict Congress, universal deference to litigating positions or free-standing preemptive power. Section 808.1(d)(6)(ii) still reaches a generally worded rule whose actual application imposes a divergent specific-device duty.

##### Whether common-law damages obligations can be requirements under §360k(a)

**Holding:** A state obligation enforced through common-law damages can constitute a requirement under section 360k(a); common law has no categorical exemption from the express clause. Inclusion alone does not establish preemption: the applicable federal obligation, actual difference or addition, subject connection and statutory exceptions must still be satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct votes. Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg reserve this general question.

**Material application:** Judicial damages duties can change conduct as effectively as enacted commands. Inclusion does not itself preempt any surviving Lohr claim or supply a categorical preemption outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The following operative qualifications govern the statutory comparison and remand; they do not enlarge the separately stated proposition coalitions.

- **Separate state-requirement exemption:** Section 360k(b) permits the Secretary, on a State or political subdivision's application, after notice and opportunity for an oral hearing, to exempt a requirement by regulation on prescribed conditions if it is more stringent than the otherwise applicable federal requirement, or if compelling local conditions require it and compliance would not violate an applicable federal requirement. The no-federal-violation condition belongs to the second alternative. An exemption does not remove federal obligations. No pertinent exemption is established; an equal, nonpreempted duty does not first require one.
- **Section 808.1's complete scope:** Subsection (b) includes force-of-law court decisions. Subsection (d)(1) preserves general requirements whose purpose reaches other products as well as devices, including general electrical codes and the Uniform Commercial Code's fitness warranty, and unfair-trade rules not limited to devices. Subsection (d)(2) preserves equal or substantially identical requirements. Subsection (d)(6)(ii) preserves general adulteration/misbranding prohibitions but displaces an actual divergent substantive device requirement; interpretation and enforcement, rather than literal wording alone, control that comparison.

State procurement specifications and Medicaid or similar payment criteria are preserved. General enforcement, inspections, registration and licensing remain subject to conflicting federal records, reporting and good-manufacturing-practice rules under sections 519 and 520(f), which prevail. Administrative delegations and related matters, measures solely raising revenue or charging fees, and the identified atomic-energy and radiation measures remain outside preemption on their stated terms; the last category lasts until applicable specific FDA requirements intervene. Subsection (e) assigns the FDA a determination role through prescribed procedures subject to judicial review, without establishing an exhaustion bar here. Subsections (c) and (f) preserve applicable federal duties whether or not a state requirement is exempt or preempted. No unestablished factual exception is applied.
- **Original manufacturing regime:** Part 820 applies to finished human-use devices and defines a finished device to include an accessory suitable for use whether packaged or labeled. It is not generally directed at manufacturers of components or parts; blood and blood components have Part 606's separate treatment. The everyday description of this lead as a component does not settle that classification. Class III status alone is not a substitute finding. Where simultaneous compliance with applicable regulations is impossible, the specific device regulation supersedes the general regulation. ### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The justification required when federal, state or local government deliberately distributes opportunities or investigative burdens by race.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

In K–12 public schooling, supported educational benefits from meaningful integration and avoidance of serious racial isolation may constitute an important lawful objective. An existing desegregation decree is not a prerequisite to that objective, and its absence proves neither a remedial duty nor invalidity. Demographic percentages, annual classifications or a beneficial label do not establish the educational condition, substantial fit or justification of individual burdens; no duty to adopt preferences or presumption validating numerical targets follows.

Intentional selection for police investigation by race is a racial classification even when the identifying feature came from a victim and officials lack racial hostility. A supported description can justify appropriately fitted investigation, but neither its source nor consensual questioning removes the selection from this standard. Courts examine the actual criteria, coverage and burdens; individual injury, responsibility, immunity and relief require their own predicates. A description's age, sex, injury, place and time features may bear on fit, without becoming mandatory factors in every investigation.

**Governing question:** When motor-vehicle safety standards displace state common-law duties, including the interaction of express preemption, compliance savings and actual conflict.

**Current rule:** Section 1392(d)’s express restriction requires an in-effect federal motor-vehicle standard governing the same aspect of performance. The restriction concerns nonidentical standards and preserves enforcement of identical standards and higher performance requirements for vehicles or equipment procured for a federal, state or local government’s own use. Judicial invalidation of a standard and absence of an operative requirement do not themselves establish impossibility or an affirmative federal no-regulation objective. An express clause does not categorically foreclose proof of an independent actual conflict with operative federal law or a demonstrated federal objective, and a savings clause is not immunity from every actual conflict. Read together, former §§1392(d) and 1397(k) preserve common-law liability despite compliance rather than create categorical compliance immunity. ### Medical-device requirements and genuinely parallel state duties

**Governing question:** Express preemption under 21 U.S.C. §360k(a) of state device obligations, including damages duties.

**Current rule:** The inquiry requires an applicable federal requirement, a state requirement respecting a human-use device, an actual difference or addition, and a connection to safety, effectiveness or another matter included in the federal requirement, subject to subsection (b). Common-law damages duties can be requirements; inclusion alone does not establish displacement. An independently available state duty requiring genuinely the same conduct is not additional merely because it adds damages. Extra negligence or unreasonable-hazard elements may narrow recovery without changing that conduct obligation. A theory allowing liability despite compliance with the identified federal obligation cannot become parallel by invoking federal law. Substantial-equivalence clearance does not itself supply substantive FDA design approval. General manufacturing or warning commands require comparison of actual duties rather than displacement from shared subject matter or regulatory force alone.

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

**Governing question:** The §3626 scope, necessity findings and preliminary-relief conditions.

**Current rule:** In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires that federal law require the state-law violation, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

**Controlling construction:** Prospective relief is all relief other than compensatory monetary damages. ### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Governing question:** A restriction affects family association or an asserted no-contact method of conception compatible with confinement.

**Current rule:** Incarceration does not categorically extinguish family association compatible with confinement or a married prisoner’s claim to attempt conception through a privately arranged, no-contact specimen transfer. Apply Turner’s reasonable-relationship inquiry with substantial institutional deference: assess the restriction’s rational connection to a legitimate prison objective, actual alternatives for exercising the interest, the institutional effects of accommodation and ready alternatives. The inquiry does not require the least restrictive means. The actual proposal and justification require adjudication before relief; any prison-conditions injunction separately requires §3626 findings.
