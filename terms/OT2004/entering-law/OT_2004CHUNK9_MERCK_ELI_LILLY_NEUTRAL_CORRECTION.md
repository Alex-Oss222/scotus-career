# Earlier-authority neutral correction for the June 13 patent matter

**Authority:** Eli Lilly & Co. v. Medtronic, Inc., 496 U.S. 661 (1990), No. 89-243, decided June 18, 1990. This is pre-divergence authority.

**Verified writing and participation:** Justice Scalia delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Brennan, Marshall, Blackmun and Stevens. Justice Kennedy dissented, joined by Justice White. Justice O'Connor took no part in consideration or decision.

**Question and existing rule:** Eli Lilly construed §271(e)(1) in the setting of a medical device subject to federal premarketing approval. The Court applied the statutory reference to a federal law regulating drugs to the FDCA's broader regulatory scheme and allowed the statutory exemption to cover qualifying medical-device uses. The decision addresses the statutory class covered by the reference, rather than the specific later question of the regulatory relationship of particular unsuccessful preclinical compound experiments.

**Earlier disagreement:** Kennedy's dissent favored reading the text as referring to particular legal provisions regulating drugs, rather than the entire Act containing them. He disputed the majority's broader device coverage and its inferences about Congress's regulatory compromise. The majority gave effect to the broader statutory reference and the relation between the exemption and patent-term restoration. The separate writings establish earlier interpretive commitments; their effect on the later case requires independent assessment.
