#### Sandin v. Conner

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1911.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty

**Holding:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient: courts must examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and the meaningful restraint, without making state codification necessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is sufficient and nonexclusive. Mandatory procedures, routine transfers and brief inconveniences do not automatically create entitlements; direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration interests remain. No atypical-and-significant-hardship test controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hewitt v. Helms: narrows mechanical reliance on mandatory wording while retaining genuine substantive-predicate liberty protection; no wholesale methodology replacement controls.
- Kentucky Department of Corrections v. Thompson: applies distinction between substantive constraints and procedural directions alone.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves significant prison liberty and context-sensitive procedural protection.
- Meachum v. Fano: distinguishes ordinary sentence-authorized management from a genuine protected constraint.
- Vitek v. Jones; Washington v. Harper: preserve distinct directly protected, qualitatively different restraints; this state-entitlement rule is nonexclusive.
- Farmer v. Brennan; Helling v. McKinney: distinguish Eighth Amendment conditions liability from this procedural-liberty inquiry.

##### The reviewed summary judgment and remand

**Holding:** The summary judgment rejecting the reviewed liberty-and-process claim cannot stand on a mechanical reading of Hawaii’s regulatory words or dismissal of genuine punitive constraints. The district court must determine whether the rules genuinely constrain authority, whether the deprivation is meaningful rather than routine, and what process is due; the Court makes no liability or ultimate protected-interest finding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court and remedial disposition; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg join the bounded rule-and-remand ground. Four concurring Justices separately would find the interest already established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial-evidence language is not rewritten as an express acquittal command; the whole misconduct, impartial-adjudication, evidence and sanction scheme matters. Punitive labels alone do not establish liberty; similarity to separately authorized administrative or protective custody alone does not defeat a punitive constraint. Later expungement supplies no retroactive process and discretionary parole consequences are not inevitable sentence extension. Wolff permits refusal of witnesses for irrelevance, lack of necessity or safety or correctional hazards. Neither the four concurring votes finding an established interest nor the dissent's four-vote replacement framework controls under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff: applies witness qualifications and the separate process inquiry once protected liberty is implicated.
- Hewitt; Thompson: require whole-scheme substantive analysis rather than a one-way mandatory-word inference.
- Meachum: preserves ordinary authorized custody without making every punitive restraint legally unconstrained.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit in the reviewed respect, leaving reversal of officials' summary judgment effective, and remand for genuine constraint, meaningful deprivation and due process determinations. The Court finds neither ultimate protected liberty nor liability; unrelated amended-complaint claims and defenses are not resolved.

#### Connecticut Department of Public Safety v. Doe, 538 U.S. 1

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1231.
**Decided:** March 5, 2003.

##### Whether procedural due process requires a dangerousness hearing when only conviction authorizes inclusion

**Holding:** Procedural due process does not require a hearing on current dangerousness for a registrant in Doe's position when an undisputed qualifying conviction authorizes the challenged inclusion and the State has made no operative individual risk finding. Even assuming a protected liberty interest, a claimant must identify a disputed fact material to the deprivation the governing law authorizes; procedure does not supply a substantive dangerousness exception to the applicable conviction-based rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices adopt the assumed-interest procedural ground. Stevens concurs in the judgment only; reversal is unanimous.

**Material application:** Doe's qualifying conviction is undisputed, and the criminal process allowed him to contest it. The State's website claims no individualized finding that each person is presently dangerous. A hearing on a legally unused fact would not alter the authorized inclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Adequate process remains required for a genuinely disputed conviction or another operative inclusion criterion. The Court assumes rather than establishes the protected liberty interest and does not adopt the separate punitive-classification rationale. Substantive due process was expressly disavowed; independent residential-disclosure safeguards require their own preserved claim and record.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the dangerousness-hearing injunction. The lower court must remove that prerequisite and determine which independently pleaded and preserved claims, if any, remain; it may not create the disavowed substantive claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** W.P. v. Verniero remains governing for legally operative risk grading and notification plans, unlike the conviction criterion here. Paul P. v. Verniero retains its independent protection of compiled nonpublic residential information and fitted disclosure safeguards. Paul v. Davis's reputation distinction remains intact; the Court assumes the asserted interest.

#### Doe v. District of Columbia

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 95-7077; review of 93 F.3d 861.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### CAPTA requires prompt initiation without creating the asserted individual section 1983 right

**Holding:** The conduct-time 42 U.S.C. §5106a(b)(2) obliges a participating jurisdiction to provide for prompt initiation of an investigation after receipt of a report of known or suspected child abuse or neglect, but does not confer the asserted individual federal right to that investigative response enforceable under §1983. The holding concerns that command, not every CAPTA provision or every condition attached to federal funding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's statutory portion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The mandatory investigation obligation remains binding. The different protective-steps clause follows a finding of abuse or neglect and covers other endangered children under the same care; no preinjury finding triggers it here. The 1989 conduct is not governed by later plan amendments. The Court finds no asserted right without adopting a categorical funding or state-plan bar, implied-action prerequisite, or separate section 1983 foreclosure ruling. Breach, responsibility, causation, municipal attribution, immunity and damages remain unadjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the asserted CAPTA section 1983 claim, 5–4, without institutional relief or damages.

##### Mandatory investigative procedures alone create no substantive entitlement protected by procedural due process

**Holding:** Mandatory directions governing an investigation do not, by themselves, create a substantive liberty or property entitlement protected by procedural due process. A claimant must identify a substantive interest and its deprivation, rather than only an official failure to perform an investigative step; the theory presented here does not do so.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's investigative-procedure portion, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding leaves independently created substantive entitlements available in principle. The local emergency-removal provisions require findings that available services are insufficient and time to petition is lacking; the pleaded omission establishes neither those necessary preinjury findings nor an affirmative deprivation. Later custody creates no earlier custodial duty. The certified local notice questions and distinct challenge to loss of the local tort remedy under D.C. Code section 12-309 remain unresolved, including their relationship to infancy, incapacity and District custody.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the investigative-procedure theory unanimously, while preserving local certification and the distinct local-remedy constitutional question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Blessing v. Freestone:** Applied to require an individual entitlement in addition to a binding administrative obligation; its separate §1983 foreclosure inquiry remains intact.
- **Suter v. Artist M.:** Its provision-specific reporting holding supplies the closest analogy; its reasonable-efforts and implied-action holdings remain distinct. It is not enlarged into a categorical state-plan bar, and its reservation of other provisions remains effective.
- **Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment & Housing Authority:** Preserved as authority for §1983 enforcement of a sufficiently defined statutory entitlement; it does not make every funding obligation individually enforceable.
- **Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association:** Preserved for the same provision-specific inquiry into enforceable federal rights, without a general exemption for funded programs.
- **Maine v. Thiboutot:** The statutory-right channel under §1983 remains available when a federal right is established.
- **Livadas v. Bradshaw:** Applied to keep right, enforcement channel, congressional foreclosure, proper defendant, and relief separate.
- **Sandin v. Conner:** Applied for substantive constraints rather than mandatory procedures alone; its prison-specific rule is not converted into a universal child-protection test.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services:** Applied to the absence of a general preinjury custodial protection duty; it does not foreclose independently created noncustodial entitlements.
- **Pinder v. Johnson:** Applied to distinguish the alleged omission from an official's affirmative deprivation. No affirmative-interference theory is decided on these facts.

#### Calderon v. Thompson

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-215.

**Decided:** 1998-04-29.

##### The recall judgment is reviewable through the ordinary channel

**Holding:** Felker’s §2244(b)(3)(E) restriction on review of permission to file a successive district-court application does not bar ordinary review of this separate mandate-recall judgment confined to the first application’s claims and evidence. Under Lindh and Carriger, the identified amended §2254 merits and replacement factual-presumption provisions do not govern this capital application filed in January 1990, outside established chapter 154 qualification; other statutory provisions retain their own temporal and procedural predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of O'Connor’s opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court-initiated label cannot disguise a successive application. Here the recall is confined to the first application’s evidence and excludes Leitch’s later parole account. No blanket AEDPA exemption or hearing-provision holding follows.

##### Completed habeas merits cannot be reopened on ordinary reconsideration alone

**Holding:** When recall reopens the completed merits of a state prisoner’s habeas proceeding, rather than correcting a genuinely different defect in judicial process, exceptional recall requires the applicable miscarriage-of-justice showing: Schlup’s crime-innocence gateway for an attack on a conviction, or Sawyer’s distinct gateway for an eligibility-only attack. A clerical correction, fraud, a mandate that does not express the court’s judgment or another genuinely different process defect remains distinct; capital punishment or disagreement with the prior merits result alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, including the author. These five directly adopt this rule, its application and the remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule extends the distinct innocence gateways to this completed-merits recall category. It does not abolish exceptional recall authority or treat every court-initiated recall as a successive district-court application.

##### The augmented first-application record meets neither distinct gateway

**Holding:** A conviction attack under Schlup requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record showing that, more likely than not, no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict beyond a reasonable doubt. An eligibility-only attack under Sawyer requires clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the necessary rape special circumstance and other state-law eligibility predicates; Thompson’s first-application record satisfies neither showing.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Parts II–III coalition directly adopts both component-specific applications. No fractured-decision synthesis is involved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Added impeachment is assessed with the record, not categorically excluded. No separate ineffective-assistance merits ruling, state finding on every added item, universal judicial awareness or dishonest purpose is found. Evidence may be new relative to trial without postdating the mandate; no AEDPA diligence rule is imported into Schlup.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed 5–4, Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas supporting, with instructions to reinstate the June 11, 1997 denial mandate. Only recall-dependent relief and directions are removed. No new execution date, revival of an expired date, adjudication of another application or displacement of independent protection follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Felker v. Turpin:** Applied channel by channel. The successive-application authorization-review bar does not decide this distinct recall judgment.
- **Lindh v. Murphy; Carriger v. Stewart:** Applied to the identified pre-AEDPA capital-application provisions; no blanket exemption or automatic gateway remand follows.
- **Schlup v. Delo:** Extended to conviction-innocence review for this category of completed-merits recall, retaining reliable new evidence, the whole record and its probability standard.

#### Carlisle v. United States, 517 U.S. 416

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-9247.
**Decided:** April 29, 1996.

##### Authority to grant a new, untimely postverdict acquittal

**Holding:** With the Government's timely objection preserved, Rules 29(c) and 45(b) forbid granting a new acquittal motion filed outside the seven-day period following jury discharge, absent further time fixed within that period under the then-operative rule. Rule 45(b) excludes ordinary excusable-neglect enlargement; this limits the motion remedy, not federal criminal subject-matter jurisdiction. Rule 2, Rule 57, and inherent supervisory authority cannot supply the same prohibited relief under a separate pre-sentence-power label.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), Parts I–II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** No timely trial motion, reserved decision, or valid extension existed. The seven-day computation excluded intervening weekends; the filing was one day late. Attorney error, absence of prejudice, and later reconsideration do not create authority. Rule 29(a)'s acquittal duty cannot nullify the specific postverdict conditions. Rule 2 guides construction, and Rule 57 permits consistent practice in genuine gaps; neither overrides a national rule. Amended Rule 57's nonwillful local-form and actual-notice protections do not excuse this national deadline, and its just-and-practicable pending-proceedings transition does not automatically apply it to the 1993 conduct.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Inherent authority is not abolished generally. Timely court-initiated action, reconsideration of a timely motion, a valid reservation, Government forfeiture, materially different judicial assurances inducing lateness, and hypothetical Article III withdrawal limits remain open. No sufficiency determination follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Smith* supports the restriction against an unrestricted judicial substitute while retaining its different postappeal posture. *Pioneer* permits flexibility only where enlargement is authorized. *Bank of Nova Scotia* requires rule-consistent supervision; *Williams*'s bounded, prejudice-based remedy survives without authorizing a conflicting act. *Fallen* protects timely efforts of an ill, effectively unrepresented prisoner, not ordinary counsel error. *Lonchar* preserves process actually required by law without an additional equitable substitute. *Manypenny*'s timely-motion reconsideration does not resolve the new late motion.

##### Writs, due process, and restoration of the verdict

**Holding:** Neither the All Writs Act nor due process authorizes this untimely acquittal on the presented facts. The Government may obtain restoration of the existing jury verdict without a second factfinding trial. Independently authorized review remains subject to its own preservation, proof, and procedural conditions; the Court neither adjudicates nor guarantees such relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** Section 1651's necessary-or-appropriate, in-aid-of-jurisdiction authority consistent with legal usages supplies no exceptional writ predicate here. No predeadline judicial assurance induced lateness. Substantive protection against insufficient evidence does not create an additional late district-court vehicle; a missed motion is not necessarily an informed waiver of every other claim, and plain-error review is not declared equivalent to ordinary sufficiency review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the no-authority ruling, 7–2; the late acquittal remains unauthorized. The reinstated conviction and sentence already imposed remain undisturbed. No duplicate sentencing, trial, release, custody determination, new collateral proceeding, or reopened claim follows. Any independently available collateral proceeding must satisfy genuinely applicable AEDPA conditions and unresolved transition requirements; none is decided applicable or satisfied here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Morgan*'s extraordinary coram nobis remedy is not another deadline for this ongoing prosecution. *Jackson*'s sufficiency protection survives; *Wilson* permits the Government appeal because restoration requires no new trial. *Harris Truck Lines* and *Thompson* supply no missing judicial-assurance predicate. *Olano* and *Libretti* retain distinct authority, forfeiture, waiver, and lawful-correction inquiries.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

#### Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 99-1639; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below 203 F.3d 487.
**Decided:** February 27, 2001.

##### Whether a genuine private conveyance cures continuing municipal religious presentation

**Holding:** A bona fide sale ends public ownership but does not cure an establishment violation if the municipality continues acts that present the religious message as its own. The City's continued operation of its surrounding park as the undifferentiated setting for its historical religious centerpiece, together with the retained public-park dedication and ineffective separation, preserves that municipal presentation here despite genuine private ownership, maintenance and lighting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct votes for both questions.

**Material application:** The Fund owns, maintains and lights its parcel under a genuine fair-price sale; no covenant enforcement is established. The continuing governmental act is the City's presentation of its surrounding park as an undifferentiated setting for its historical religious centerpiece, with a retained park dedication and ineffective separation. No one feature independently establishes the violation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Agostini supplies the unified governmental-act and function inquiry; governing distinct prohibitions and established permissible relationships retain their scope, with purpose, effect and institutional relationships serving as evidence. Mere location, religious content, offense or possible confusion does not establish governmental attribution. This site is not established as a generally available forum. The challenge to the continuing municipal function remains live after the sale.

##### What lawful corrective relief may end sponsorship while protecting the private parcel

**Holding:** The remand must secure effective cessation of the identified municipal religious presentation through operational and intelligible separation, using clarification or boundary measures on land the City controls or another lawful means. Relief must preserve the genuine conveyance and the Fund's private expression and property, and cannot require destruction, relocation or alteration of private speech merely to prevent possible misunderstanding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct votes for both questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The corrective remand is affirmed. Clarification or boundary measures on municipal land, or an equally effective lawful measure, must end the actual City presentation. A disclaimer succeeds only if the operational arrangement supports it. The lower court must respect private ownership and may not assume authority to change a sign or message on Fund property.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court prescribes no particular fence, covenant cancellation, destruction, relocation or removal and no universal conditions for every monument transfer. Implementation details remain below without reopening the stipulated private maintenance and lighting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Agostini v. Felton: applies the unified governmental-act and function inquiry to an unresolved monument divestiture; its distinct doctrines and established permissible relationships retain their actual scope.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman: remains governing as modified by Agostini; its former independent prongs are not revived.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: preserves genuine private religious access and limits the cure; neither categorical forum immunity nor attribution from proximity follows.
- Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller: preserves contextual private-expression protection; interior or adjacent public location alone is insufficient.
- Robinson v. City of Edmond: applies the distinction between official institutional presentation and private expression without converting its seal holding into a categorical monument rule.
- Brentwood Academy: reinforces examination of actual governmental responsibility; the athletic-association application supplies no automatic rule for adjoining private land.

#### A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Supreme Court docket not supplied; judgment below, 239 F.3d 1004 (9th Cir. 2001).
**Decided:** April 23, 2002.

For Napster's separate §512 protections and fitted preliminary relief, April 23, 2002, judgment below 239 F.3d 1004, see Federal Courts: Immunities and Remedies — Copyright service protections and feasible injunctions.

##### Substantial lawful capability does not immunize specific ongoing assistance

**Holding:** A service can incur contributory liability by knowingly providing material assistance to identified infringement, notwithstanding substantial noninfringing uses of its technology. Sony bars imputing the needed knowledge from capability alone; it does not immunize continuing materially assisting operation after specific knowledge.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous; all nine join Parts I–VII and every qualification.

**Material application:** Notices identified more than 12,000 infringing files, continued availability followed purported account terminations, and the search/index functions materially assisted access. General awareness of copyrighted music is not knowledge of unauthorized copying, and present infringement percentages are not the exclusive lawful-capability measure.

##### Financial benefit and actual supervision independently support preliminary vicarious liability

**Independent alternative holding:** Vicarious liability separately requires a direct financial benefit from infringement and the right and practical ability to supervise it. Attraction of infringing recordings to the user base can satisfy the benefit element without direct transaction fees, while account exclusion and index control support supervision over the service's actual assistance; both elements are supported here at the preliminary stage.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, unanimous; all nine join Parts I–VII and every qualification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The vicarious ground independently suffices at this preliminary stage; it does not replace contributory knowledge. Theoretical shutdown capacity creates no duty to inspect user contents the service cannot access or to guarantee prevention of unknown copying.

#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. #### Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735

**Docket or dockets:** 95-860
**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does interpretive change or a rule's development during litigation categorically defeat deference?

**Holding:** A lawful, adequately explained interpretation within delegated statutory ambiguity does not lose eligibility for deference merely because it changes or addresses an old statute during litigation. Unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious; the holding independently rejects the categorical inconsistency objection without approving every change.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Parts I, II, IV and VI but does not join Parts III-A, III-B or V. Parts III-A and III-B are independently sufficient answers to the asserted prior-policy barrier; they are not combined into a narrower rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Good Samaritan permits reasoned change within lawful authority while requiring statutory fit, adequate explanation and attention to legitimate reliance. Neither statutory age nor litigation impetus supplies a categorical exclusion. The OCC formally proposed its interpretation on March 3, 1995, received 112 comment letters, and explained its response and grounds in the final rule published February 9, 1996. That process differs from a position invented by litigation counsel. The Court relies on the agency's stated clarification of uncertainty, not a judicially invented economic or reliance finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Those procedural facts do not certify compliance with every rulemaking statute. Permission to change an interpretation does not itself authorize retrospective alteration of completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan permits explained interpretive change while retaining authority, statutory-fit and reliance limits. Bowen continues to reject automatic weight for unsupported litigation rationales.

## Selected operative Standards and Tests

### Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

**Governing question:** The distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility, including final policymaking for the challenged function.

**Current rule:** A municipal employee who proves deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and the required municipal responsibility need not prove an additional, independent abuse-of-governmental-power element. The federal deprivation and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries. Employment alone establishes neither municipal liability nor a substantive right; *Canton*'s training analysis supplies attribution and causation, not the underlying constitutional guarantee. For a final-policymaker theory, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, who speaks finally for the particular entity on the challenged subject. Final operational discretion does not alone answer that question; labels, election, territory, financing or lack of commission control is individually insufficient. An Alabama sheriff's independent local office supplies County final law-enforcement authority in the criminal-investigation function addressed in McMillian, even without commission delegation or review. An attributable policy or final decision must still cause the proved federal injury. For a training or supervision omission, the plaintiff must establish a deliberate or conscious municipal choice amounting to deliberate indifference, an identified deficiency closely related to the federal injury, and actual causation. Notice can come from an established pattern or an obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably necessary. Negligence, a single employee’s error, general supervisory responsibility and hindsight that additional training might have helped are insufficient. Responsibility for police conduct requires actual supported authority over that conduct rather than cooperation alone.

Consequential examination injuries caused by a proved municipal removal-policy violation do not invariably require proof of a second examination policy. The underlying wrong, municipal causal connection, injury and recoverable measure must nevertheless be established separately for each consequence. That route does not authorize adjudication of an unpresented independent examination-policy claim or adopt a constitutional rule for such examinations.

Named employees’ exoneration does not itself defeat a separately established policy-caused constitutional deprivation when the employees lacked the personal awareness required for their own liability or the verdicts rejected different constitutional injuries. The municipality’s own deliberate choice, notice, identified policy defect and actual cause still must be proved; pooled employee information cannot manufacture personal culpability. A finding that the relevant constitutional injury did not occur remains different from those exonerations. Transfer to a later custodian does not automatically end the causal effects of an uncorrected municipal policy, but recovery reaches only loss actually caused by that policy.

- Rejection of the additional element and separation of deprivation from attribution: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — No extra governmental-abuse element under §1983, February 26, 1992; Part A of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

- McMillian v. Monroe County — I. Final authority and the government represented are distinct inquiries, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg, all nine
- McMillian v. Monroe County — II. The sheriff's independent local office supplies County law-enforcement authority, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five
- McMillian v. Monroe County — III. The County claim returns for the remaining liability elements, 1997-06-02; the same five

- Deliberate omission, notice, identified deficiency and causation: Carter v. City of Philadelphia — A deliberate municipal omission must actually cause the constitutional injury, March 13, 2000; Ginsburg’s opinion joined by all seven other Associate Justices.

- Consequential injury from an established municipal wrong without an invariably required second policy: Canonical Decision Record `Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. The independent examination theory requires a threshold determination, while consequential injury remains possible, 2001-04-02; all nine.

### Hearings for risk-based and conviction-based notification

**Governing question:** When a notification regime requires advance review of a disputed risk classification, and when conviction alone supplies the operative predicate.

**Current rule:** Where state law substantively limits disclosure to a legitimate and substantial interest with minimized invasion, ordinary Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification requires a meaningful advance opportunity to contest risk classification and the notification plan. The prosecutor bears persuasion on both. The factual basis and risk classification require clear and convincing evidence, rather than preponderance; no general heightened burden for every civil prediction follows. Review must allow factual and calculation challenges, atypical cases and excessive audience challenges. A supported credible indication of likely reoffense requires high-risk treatment regardless of score; a physical condition minimizing risk, including advanced age or debilitating illness, requires low-risk treatment regardless of score.

A different, conviction-based registry does not require a hearing about current dangerousness when an undisputed qualifying conviction authorizes inclusion and government has made no operative individual risk determination. Even assuming protected liberty, the claimant must identify a disputed fact material under the actual governing law; procedural due process does not create a substantive dangerousness exception.

**Exceptions and limits:** Reputation alone and promised procedures alone do not supply this substantive interest. No general informational-privacy right or renewed facial attack on the Scale is established.

### Timely postverdict acquittal and limits on substitute authority

**Governing question:** A new Rule 29(c) acquittal motion filed outside the prescribed period with a timely Government objection.

**Current rule:** Applicable Rule 29(c) permits a postverdict motion within seven days after jury discharge or further time fixed during those seven days, without requiring an earlier trial motion. Rule 45(b) forecloses general excusable-neglect enlargement outside Rule 29’s own conditions. With a timely Government objection, the district court cannot grant a new late motion; one-day lateness, attorney error or lack of Government prejudice does not supply authority. This is a remedy limit, not loss of subject-matter jurisdiction. Rule 29(a)’s after-evidence acquittal authority and Rule 29(b)’s reservation of a timely trial motion do not authorize the same new untimely postverdict relief. Rule 2, Rule 57 and inherent supervision cannot circumvent the specific restriction by relabeling it; the All Writs Act and ordinary counsel error supply no substitute deadline or due-process entitlement to that additional vehicle. Government appeal may restore the existing verdict without a second factfinding trial.

**Authority by component:**

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Government may not pay for worship, religious indoctrination or instructional work in which religious formation is inseparable from the publicly financed service, and parental choice does not alter the constitutional character of what government purchases; neutral eligibility does not cure an unconstitutional governmental expenditure. Courts examine the actual expenditure and the function it finances, treating labels such as voucher, parental choice or secular grant as evidence of a program's structure rather than answers. A religious institution may provide an independently identifiable secular service where the funded work is genuinely secular and enforceable conditions prevent public payment for devotional activity. Religious affiliation alone neither proves a violation nor establishes an entitlement to funding, and genuinely private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of project bonds remain subject to the actual financed-function inquiry even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Demonstrated devotional work and facilities inseparable from it cannot be financed; independently identifiable secular work remains eligible under practical separation and enforceable controls.

Civil regulation may enforce objectively verifiable facts about preparation or a named private certification. Officials may not adjudicate theological correctness, make denominational orthodoxy a coercive civil standard, or confer compulsory civil permission on a religious office supplying the governing religious judgment. Factual expertise or truthful private attribution does not itself confer such authority.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental choice does not alter the constitutional character of what government purchases”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

- Mitchell v. Helms; No. 98-1648. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function; 2. - Public bond financing and confined correction: Canonical Decision Record `Steele-v-Industrial-Development-Board_merits_2003-03-03.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Public bond assistance is governed by actual financed function; III. Both categorical merits endpoints must give way to effective, confined relief, 2003-03-03; O’Connor’s seven-Justice opinion.
- Civil religious standards and the factual-representation boundary: Canonical Decision Record `Commack-Self-Service-Kosher-Meats-v-Weiss_merits_2003-03-24.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Civil enforcement cannot make religious orthodoxy government’s rule; II. The washing condition makes religious authorization a civil requirement, 2003-03-24; unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Commack, March 24, 2003, for the civil-certification boundary, and Steele, March 3, 2003, for public bond financing; earlier unified-inquiry and financed-function components retain their dates.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson's expenditure rule governs voucher arrangements, direct tuition payments and disguised institutional subsidies through the same examination of the function financed; it does not prohibit every benefit involving a religious institution, exclude religious providers by identity or create a constitutional entitlement to subsidy, and its judgment commands no immediate ban on every payment or religious provider. Provider/payment relief and state-law severability turn on the actual record. Monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective as genuinely private benefits: an individual vocational grant and a generally available tax deduction.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location. [Jackson v. Benson — Parental choice does not alter the constitutional character of what government purchases](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#parental-choice-does-not-alter-the-constitutional-character-of-what-government-purchases-1).

**Equipment-aid limits:** A statutory control and its actual observance are different, and not every statutory violation is constitutional. The rule requires appropriate effective separation without a universal failsafe monitor, corporate restructuring, distinct staff or mandatory new audit. It decides no universal voucher rule or entitlement to religious uses of public benefits.

### Municipal divestiture and continuing presentation of a religious display

**Governing question:** Whether transfer of a municipal religious display ends the governmental religious function.

**Current rule:** A genuine conveyance ends public ownership but does not cure continued municipal acts presenting the religious message as the City's own. Assess actual public presentation and intelligible operational separation, accounting for genuine private ownership and operation. A surrounding public park's integrated presentation, retained dedication and ineffective separation can establish the continuing function together; no one feature, religious content, proximity or possible misunderstanding alone establishes it.

Relief must end the proved municipal function by effective lawful separation, including clarification or boundary measures on property the City controls or an equally effective lawful means. It must preserve genuine private ownership and expression. A court may not require destruction, relocation or alteration of private speech merely to avoid possible misunderstanding, or presume authority to put a sign on private land. A disclaimer works only if supported by the actual presentation.

**Authority by component:**

- Continuing governmental presentation despite genuine sale: Canonical Decision Record `Freedom_From_Religion_Foundation_v_City_of_Marshfield_merits_2001-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. A genuine transfer must end the City's continuing religious function, 2001-02-27; six direct joins.
- Operational separation and private-property limits on relief: Canonical Decision Record `Freedom_From_Religion_Foundation_v_City_of_Marshfield_merits_2001-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. **Exceptions and limits:** There is no universal fence, disclaimer, removal, sale or covenant-cancellation requirement. Genuinely private forum access and the unified Lemon actual-function inquiry retain their scopes.

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

### Knowing material assistance and beneficial supervision of infringement

**Governing question:** When an ongoing service incurs contributory or vicarious copyright liability despite substantial lawful uses.

**Current rule:** Contributory liability requires knowing material assistance to identified infringement. Substantial lawful capability prevents imputing knowledge from capability alone but does not immunize continued assistance after specific knowledge. General awareness that copyrighted material circulates does not establish unauthorized copying. Independently, vicarious liability requires a direct financial benefit from infringement and both the right and practical ability to supervise it. Infringement attracting users may supply benefit without per-transaction fees; actual account or index controls may supply bounded supervision. A theoretical power to close an entire service does not create a general obligation to inspect inaccessible files or guarantee prevention of unknown copying.

**Authority by component:**

- Specific knowledge/material contribution and independent benefit/control conditions: Canonical Decision Record `a_and_m_records_v_napster_merits_2002-04-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Substantial lawful capability does not immunize specific ongoing assistance; III. Financial benefit and actual supervision independently support preliminary vicarious liability, 2002-04-23; Stevens for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc., 2002-04-23.

**Consequence or recurring remedy:** Fitted relief must connect usable owner identification of protected works and sufficiently located infringement to feasible action within actual service controls, preserve authorized files and exchange, and satisfy equity and any applicable section 512 limits. Liability theory alone does not defeat a statutory protection. Authority: the same Record, Holdings V and VII, all nine.

**Related current rules:** Function-specific copyright protections and injunction limits supplies the independent section 512 inquiry. Fair use retains its integrated factors; no general preliminary-stage burden allocation is resolved.

### Function-specific copyright protections and injunction limits

**Governing question:** Whether a particular service function qualifies for section 512 protection and which injunctions remain available.

**Current rule:** Test each invoked section 512 service category and its conditions separately before ordering relief beyond its limits. Secondary-liability status alone defeats none of those protections, and failure in one category decides neither another category nor an independent noninfringement defense. A classification left unresolved does not authorize avoidance of statutory relief limits through a general policing command.

**Authority by component:**

- Function-specific eligibility, notice, policy, monitoring and injunction conditions: Canonical Decision Record `a_and_m_records_v_napster_merits_2002-04-23.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / V. Section 512 independently limits liability and relief, 2002-04-23; Stevens for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc., 2002-04-23.

**Operative conditions:** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no content modification. Any intermediate copy must not be maintained in a manner ordinarily accessible to anyone other than anticipated recipients, and must not remain ordinarily accessible to those recipients longer than reasonably necessary for transmission, routing or connection. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption and reasonable implementation of a policy terminating repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances, with subscribers and account holders informed of that policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized to act for the owner of an allegedly infringed exclusive right. Under §512(c)(3)(B), incorporated by (d)(3), a notice that fails substantial compliance may not be considered in determining statutory actual knowledge or awareness of facts making infringement apparent. When a notice delivered to the designated agent substantially supplies the work, material or link location, and contact information but fails other elements, that exclusion applies only if the provider promptly attempts to contact the sender or takes other reasonable steps to assist receipt of a notice substantially satisfying all required elements. 

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect.

### Separate statutory and Guidelines LSD quantities

**Governing question:** Sentencing LSD carried on blotter paper under §841(b)(1) and Amendment 488.

**Current rule:** The statutory mixture-or-substance quantity includes the actual weight of LSD and its blotter-paper carrier under Chapman. Amendment 488’s 0.4-milligram-per-dose calculation governs the Guidelines Drug Quantity Table within its own scope and does not replace the statutory measure. When a statutory minimum exceeds the applicable Guidelines maximum, §5G1.1(b) makes that minimum the Guidelines sentence. Under the amendment, unmarked blotter paper carries a one-dose presumption for each quarter-inch by quarter-inch section; for liquid LSD not on a carrier, pure-drug weight may inadequately reflect seriousness and may warrant an upward departure. - Distinct quantity measures, minimum and amendment qualifications: The statute and Guidelines retain their separate LSD measures, 1996-01-22; Kennedy’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Neal, 1996-01-22.

### 2005-03-30 — [Rhines v. Weber](../records/rhines-v-weber_merits_2005-03-30.md#public-projection)

**Holdings.**

#### Retaining a timely mixed petition during exhaustion

**Holding and operative rule.** A district court may stay a timely mixed petition while the prisoner exhausts state remedies if the prisoner shows good cause for not exhausting first, the unexhausted claims are not plainly meritless, and the record does not show abusive litigation or intentional delay. Good cause means a reasonable explanation in the actual circumstances, not an extraordinary-circumstances or procedural-default test; the court must set reasonable, definite deadlines for seeking state relief and returning to federal court, accounting for delays outside the prisoner’s control.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete framework. Justice Souter joins the existence of stay power and the merit, anti-abuse and deadline limits, but not the initial good-cause prerequisite.

**Controlling explanation.** Rose v. Lundy gives state courts the first opportunity to decide federal claims. Congress’s exhaustion and limitations rules can serve that purpose while a federal court retains a timely filed action. Landis v. North American Co. supplies ordinary control over pending cases, exercised here within the habeas statutes. Duncan v. Walker excludes time spent in federal court from statutory tolling under §2244(d)(2); retention preserves an existing action rather than supplying a new filing period. Pliler v. Ford left this stay question open while establishing separate protections against particular court-caused filing losses.

The warden correctly argues that routine, open-ended stays would weaken finality and reward premature filing. Cause, a potential-merit screen, exclusion of abuse and definite deadlines answer that objection. Good cause preserves an incentive to exhaust before filing and permits an individualized explanation. Its reasonable-explanation meaning also avoids turning technical uncertainty about exhaustion into a second, rigid procedural-default obstacle. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on its merits; it does not compel denial. Relief remains subject to exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant’s rights, or counsel’s express waiver of exhaustion for the State, as the statute provides.

Rhines filed with more than eleven months remaining before limitations expired during federal processing. The district court identified eight unexhausted claims and imposed sixty-day initiation and return conditions. Those facts establish the practical importance of stay authority. Actual cause, potential merit, delay and the reasonableness of those conditions require findings under the announced standard.

#### Protecting timely review when applying that discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** Where good cause, potential merit and absence of intentional delay are established and dismissal would defeat timely federal review, denying a stay and dismissing the entire petition ordinarily abuses discretion; the court should retain the action, and any refusal requires a reason compatible with the statute. If a stay is inappropriate, the prisoner must be allowed to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims when whole-petition dismissal would unreasonably impair federal relief.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join this sufficient case for retention and the deletion protection. Justice Souter’s agreement does not make good cause a necessary condition in his separate view.

**Controlling explanation.** The exhaustion requirement directs claims first to the state courts; it does not require destroying a timely federal action whenever the two processes overlap. The filing date and continued pendency therefore matter independently of statutory tolling. A refusal must apply the governing predicates and account for its actual effect on federal review. If retention is unsuitable, deletion preserves the exhausted claims without demanding adjudication of the others. Pliler’s neutral-warning duty retains its particular triggers—an unrepresented prisoner and a qualifying court-directed dismissal election. Rhines had appointed counsel for his amended federal petition, so that duty does not automatically govern his election.

**Precedent Treatment.**

Rose v. Lundy’s total-exhaustion requirement remains operative with bounded stay-and-abeyance in the limitations setting. Duncan v. Walker retains its statutory non-tolling rule; this decision answers the distinct stay question. Pliler v. Ford retains its separate notice, causation, diligence and lawful-remedy rules. Landis v. North American Co. supplies ordinary management authority subject to the governing habeas statutes.

**Law After Decision.**

District courts have a uniform standard for retaining timely mixed petitions while state remedies are pursued. The rule preserves the existing filing and requires prompt exhaustion and return; reopening a terminated action still requires an independently valid procedural ground. The actual stay predicates and reasonable deadlines are matters of application under settled standards, including the deletion protection when whole-petition dismissal would unreasonably impair federal review.

### 2005-04-26 — [Doe v. Porter; Sixth Circuit Nos. 02-5316 and 02-5823, 370 F.3d 558.](../records/doe-v-porter_merits_2005-04-26.md#public-projection)

#### 2. A public school cannot outsource its own devotional instruction

**Rule and authority.** A public school may not incorporate teaching religious doctrine as truth or prescribed religious observance into its own instructional program by assigning delivery to a private religious provider. Governmental adoption of the instructional function and its actual devotional content must be established under ordinary civil proof; summary judgment requires the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. Part II is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation.** Agostini supplies the inquiry into the actual governmental act and function. McCollum and Schempp establish the public-school instructional prohibition, while Lee and Santa Fe show why private delivery or an opportunity to abstain does not erase official sponsorship. Here the Board supplies regular classroom periods during the elementary-school day, and the actual lessons teach religious commands, divine creation, salvation and devotional conduct. Leaving their composition to college volunteers does not turn the school's instruction into private access. A secular character-education objective cannot justify devotional means, and some secular lessons do not cure the proved program.

Religious affiliation, location and moral vocabulary alone are insufficient. Agostini protects genuinely secular instruction by religious persons, and Good News Club protects independent private access; neither describes this school-adopted devotional curriculum. The supported operation warrants cessation of the program throughout the schools where it operates. A particular redesigned curriculum is outside the record and receives no advance approval.

### 2005-05-23 — [Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association; Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165.](../records/johanns-v-livestock-marketing-association_merits_2005-05-23.md#public-projection)

#### Actual governmental control identifies the speaker

**Holding and operative rule:** A promotional message is governmental for the presented compelled-subsidy objection when government determines its policy and actually selects, supervises and finally approves its content. The Government must establish that operational direction. Appointment power, a governmental label or ordinary compliance review alone is insufficient; the operational direction established here makes the challenged beef campaign the Government's message.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, supplies five direct joins. That same coalition joins Parts II–IV in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Keller requires examination of the particular expressive function rather than a body's general legal status. The Secretary's actual participation in development and final control over content establish more than a dormant veto over an independent private speaker. Producer drafting and the producers' freedom to run their own businesses do not transfer the final message choice from the officials who exercise it. The accountability objection would additionally require public identification of each campaign as governmental. For this funding claim, however, the relevant responsibility follows actual official message selection; a generic identification of the source of funds does not itself turn the controlled campaign into private authorship. United Foods reserved this operational governmental defense because it had not been developed there. The defense is preserved and supported here, so the Court resolves that reservation on the actual control record.

### 2005-05-31 — [Cutter v. Wilkinson, No. 03-9877.](../records/cutter-v-wilkinson_merits_2005-05-31.md#public-projection)

#### Neutral accommodation of private religious exercise in custody

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3's generally available protection against substantial government-imposed burdens on religious exercise in covered institutions is not facially invalid under the Establishment Clause. Government may relieve exceptional constraints its custody imposes on private exercise even when the Free Exercise Clause would not independently require that accommodation or comparable nonreligious interests receive less statutory protection.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this holding and the statutory-administration and remand holdings below.

**Controlling explanation:** Custody limits ordinary means of obtaining materials, associating and observing religious practices. Relieving that government-created dependence restores private exercise; it does not necessarily make the exercise an official religious program. Section 3 selects no denomination, requires no religious adherence and assigns no civil authority to a sect. Kiryas Joel permits accommodation beyond the constitutional minimum while forbidding denominational allocation of government power. Agostini directs attention to the actual governmental act and function, with purpose, effects and entanglement as evidence within its unified inquiry. This provision performs an accommodation function. Doe v. Porter concerns the different act of adopting devotional instruction as a public institution's own program.

The categorical objection that religion receives greater protection than other interests overlooks that distinction. The possibility that accommodation makes private religious practice more attractive does not itself establish coercion or sponsorship. At the same time, accommodation remains subject to neutrality, other persons' constitutional rights and the prohibition on governmental devotion. Those limits and the statute's justification defense require adjudication of concrete demands rather than dismissal of the entire protection.

### 2005-06-13 — [Wilkinson v. Austin, No. 04-495; 545 U.S. 209.](../records/wilkinson-v-austin_merits_2005-06-13.md#public-projection)

State law creates a protected prison liberty interest when it meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Ohio’s genuine substantive placement predicates, combined with OSP’s exceptional isolation, indefinite duration and loss of otherwise available parole eligibility, establish that interest in this confinement.

For the OSP classification at issue, due process is satisfied by usable notice of the actual placement grounds, a meaningful opportunity for oral and written rebuttal, stated reasons and multilevel review in which a negative recommendation terminates placement, objections before final review, review within thirty days after arrival, and genuine annual reconsideration. Those protections make the New Policy adequate without the additional mandatory witness procedures imposed below; security-based restrictions must preserve a meaningful opportunity to contest the actual grounds.

The injunction must conform to the New Policy’s constitutional adequacy while promptly correcting any proved continuing OSP placement that has not received the required process. Each retained command must be necessary to correct the established federal violation, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive necessary means, with substantial weight given to public safety and criminal-justice effects; relief departing from state or local law additionally requires that federal law demand the departure, that it be necessary, and that no other relief correct the violation.

Ohio’s constrained OSP assignment implicates protected liberty under Sandin. The New Policy’s functioning combination of notice, rebuttal, reasons and recurring review supplies due process in this setting. Continuing prison relief must correct proved injury through the specific statutory conditions and workable implementation; the adequacy of the designed policy and the correction of deficient continuing placements require distinct determinations.

### 2005-06-20 — [Rompilla v. Beard, No. 04-5462](../records/rompilla-v-beard_merits_2005-06-20.md#public-projection)

Under the unreasonable-application branch of 28 U.S.C. 2254(d)(1), relief from a state merits decision requires an objectively unreasonable application of the Supreme Court law established at that adjudication, rather than merely an incorrect application. Pennsylvania’s December 10, 1998 approval of this investigation unreasonably applied Strickland because the other inquiries did not supply a reasonable basis for omitting adequate examination of the known accessible source the prosecution intended to use substantially in seeking death.

Capital counsel must examine a readily available prior-case file when counsel knows the prosecution will use its contents as a substantial part of the case for death, unless a reasonable, informed professional judgment makes that examination unnecessary. That duty requires reasonable efforts in the actual circumstances and reasonable pursuit of material leads; counsel’s inadequate timely examination of this source fell below Strickland’s objective standard.

Where deficient investigation would have materially changed the mitigation inquiry and presentation, prejudice exists when the supported cumulative evidence, weighed with the full aggravation case and its own adverse aspects, creates a reasonable probability of a different lawful penalty. That probability undermines confidence in Rompilla’s death sentence; because the state court did not adjudicate prejudice, the Court decides this element independently on the developed record.

Pennsylvania must provide an adequately prepared new penalty proceeding within a definite reasonable period set by the District Court or impose a lawful noncapital sentence. The District Court must require notice of the State’s chosen course, verify completion through the resulting sentencing order, and enforce noncompliance against the invalid death sentence and its execution while preserving the conviction and independently lawful custody.

The established Strickland rule requires reasonable examination of an accessible prior-case source known to supply substantial aggravation, absent a reasonable informed basis for omission. Other investigative work does not by itself answer that obligation. In federal habeas review, the state-adjudication statutory threshold remains separate from actual deficiency and prejudice; an unadjudicated prejudice component is assessed independently after the threshold is satisfied. The whole lawful penalty case, including unfavorable material, governs reasonable probability. The remedy corrects the sentence while preserving the conviction.

### 2005-05-23 — [Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association; Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165.](../records/johanns-v-livestock-marketing-association_merits_2005-05-23.md#public-projection)

**Opinion Topology.**

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion. GINSBURG, J., filed an opinion concurring in the formal vacatur and remand. SOUTER, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which STEVENS and KENNEDY, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Breyer | Government-ground vacatur and remand in both dockets | Parts I–IV: actual control, targeted financing, facial attribution, complete operative remedy |
| Concurrence | Thomas | — | Joins the Court completely | Distinct affirmative protection against objectively established involuntary personal attribution |
| Opinion concurring in the formal vacatur and remand | Ginsburg | — | Judgment-only agreement in that formal act; different remand work | Withholds governmental classification and resolved-claim mandate; proposes independent commercial justification and application below |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Souter | Stevens, Kennedy | Partial affirmance, partial vacatur and remedial remand; opposes the Court's complete vacatur | Accountability ground, current private-support rule, retained protection and actual severability inquiry |

**Separate Writings.**

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court in full and would also recognize a distinct constitutional injury when an unwanted message is objectively and involuntarily attributed to an identified individual or organization. Government control does not answer that personal injury. His position extends beyond the Court's reservation while respecting the absence of an established attribution predicate in this case.

**Justice Ginsburg, concurring in the formal vacatur and remand.** She withholds the governmental-message classification for this producer-presented campaign, without adopting every part of the dissent's affirmative accountability rule. Instead she would permit a commercial subsidy to be justified by a substantial governmental interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit. That approach expressly proposes modifying United Foods's independent-private-support rule; it does not treat economic regulation as outside the First Amendment. Her United Foods dissent supplies the proposed framework, and the consistent presentation of the commercial defense permits its consideration here. Industry welfare identifies the asserted interest, but does not establish all the test's predicates. She would vacate the permanent constitutional decree and return genuinely unresolved application to the actual bench-trial record, supplemented only as ordinary procedure requires, with the Government bearing the burden. Her proposal prescribes neither scientific or quantitative benchmarks nor an automatic new trial. Any interim protection would require ordinary equitable support. She therefore agrees in formal vacatur while withholding the Court's instruction that this constitutional funding question is resolved for the Government.

**Justice Souter, joined by Stevens and Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Actual executive control is accepted, but hidden governmental selection does not provide the publicly identifiable responsibility needed to claim government speech for a campaign presented as the industry's message. Private drafting and targeted financing are not categorically forbidden; accountable governmental authorship must first exist. The writing expressly leaves open whether a discrete group's compelled support could raise another objection even when the Government openly identifies its speech.

Without the governmental justification, Abood, Keller and United Foods require an independently sufficient common function: advertising cannot justify its own compelled association. Producers retain their marketing autonomy and promotion is the principal object. Research spending does not by itself establish an adequate independent function. The preserved commercial defense fails as an independent legal cure even assuming effective promotion and sensible design; the objection is not an empirical finding that advertising failed. Glickman's qualifying collective program and Southworth's separate educational function remain distinct permissible relationships.

The writing would affirm protection against compelled advertising, vacate the categorical nonseverability determination and remand for actual statutory survival and remedial scope. The integrated collection restraint would continue until a conforming order permits a lawful, legally separable operation. Advertising's centrality and the deleted savings clause matter, but the deletion is not an affirmative legislative command resolving every remainder. The lower court would determine whether lawful operations can survive without rewriting the assessment. If they can, relief must permit them while fully protecting against the advertising defect; otherwise the whole-collection restraint may continue on that determination. The research allocation alone authorizes no fractional collection.

### 2005-05-31 — [Cutter v. Wilkinson, No. 03-9877.](../records/cutter-v-wilkinson_merits_2005-05-31.md#public-projection)

**Opinion Topology.**

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Supports the judgment | the whole opinion; the complete coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer |
| Concurrence | Thomas | No other Justice | Joins the Court's opinion and judgment in full | Additional explanation distinguishing federal legislation concerning religion from establishment and from enumerated-power authority |

**Separate Writings.**

**Thomas, concurring.** Thomas joins the opinion of the Court in full and adds that federal legislation concerning religion is not necessarily legislation establishing religion. A general accommodation provision does not necessarily compel a State to impose religious observance or support, or to prefer a sect. A categorical assertion that religion is exclusively a state subject therefore does not establish this facial violation. That distinction does not supply unlimited congressional power: Spending, Commerce and other unadjudicated power objections retain their separate questions. His additional ground is consistent with the controlling accommodation analysis and changes neither the statutory standard nor the remand.

### 2005-06-20 — [Rompilla v. Beard, No. 04-5462](../records/rompilla-v-beard_merits_2005-06-20.md#public-projection)

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent would affirm because §2254(d)(1) does not authorize disturbing Pennsylvania’s performance adjudication. Strickland leaves room for informed professional judgments about how to allocate limited investigative effort, and Visciotti requires more than disagreement with a state court’s application. Counsel conducted client and family interviews, obtained three examinations, and acquired the aggravating testimony. Those efforts and the limited information they produced permit a reasonable state appraisal even if additional file review would have been preferable.

The prosecution’s announced source is a substantial reason for further work, but the dissent does not regard it as establishing that every reasonable application of Strickland had to condemn counsel’s stopping point. Wiggins involved serious mitigation leads already exposed by the materials counsel possessed; the dissent distinguishes this investigation and warns that reliance on later discoveries can obscure the contemporaneous inquiry. The opinion does not assume that counsel examined the entire file. Its sufficient ground is the statutory limitation, so it does not independently resolve actual deficiency or cumulative prejudice. On that route it would leave the conviction and sentence intact.
