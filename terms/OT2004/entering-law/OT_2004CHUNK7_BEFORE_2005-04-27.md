## Event

Doe v. Porter; Sixth Circuit Nos. 02-5316 and 02-5823, 370 F.3d 558. Plenary merits decision, April 26, 2005 (2005-04-26), after full merits submission. The Court reviews the Sixth Circuit's affirmance of an injunction against the Bible Education Ministry program in public elementary schools, the affected families' standing, a protective order and the challenged attorney-fee award. The constitutional question is whether the school may make devotional teaching part of its instruction by assigning delivery to a private religious provider.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The injunction against the devotional program as operated remains effective throughout the affected school system. The family-standing determination, public-pseudonymity order and challenged fee award are sustained. The Board must cease scheduling or authorizing the devotional classes as school instruction and inform the responsible principals. The district court retains authority to enforce the decree and require appropriate compliance reporting.

## Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. All other Justices join Parts I and II. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg also join Parts III and IV; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgments in those parts.

## Holdings

### 1. Actual classroom exposure supports the families' standing

**Rule and authority.** Families whose children are exposed to the challenged devotional teaching in their public-school classes have concrete injury traceable to the program and redressable by an injunction stopping it. Part I has all nine Justices' support.

**Controlling explanation.** The sealed sworn affidavits establish that the children attended the challenged classes. Their protected public identities do not replace evidence of injury; the affidavits supply that evidence and remain available for meaningful defense investigation. The requested cessation directly addresses the children's exposure. The families therefore support the common relief.

### 2. A public school cannot outsource its own devotional instruction

**Rule and authority.** A public school may not incorporate teaching religious doctrine as truth or prescribed religious observance into its own instructional program by assigning delivery to a private religious provider. Governmental adoption of the instructional function and its actual devotional content must be established under ordinary civil proof; summary judgment requires the absence of a genuine dispute of material fact. Part II is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation.** Agostini supplies the inquiry into the actual governmental act and function. McCollum and Schempp establish the public-school instructional prohibition, while Lee and Santa Fe show why private delivery or an opportunity to abstain does not erase official sponsorship. Here the Board supplies regular classroom periods during the elementary-school day, and the actual lessons teach religious commands, divine creation, salvation and devotional conduct. Leaving their composition to college volunteers does not turn the school's instruction into private access. A secular character-education objective cannot justify devotional means, and some secular lessons do not cure the proved program.

Religious affiliation, location and moral vocabulary alone are insufficient. Agostini protects genuinely secular instruction by religious persons, and Good News Club protects independent private access; neither describes this school-adopted devotional curriculum. The supported operation warrants cessation of the program throughout the schools where it operates. A particular redesigned curriculum is outside the record and receives no advance approval.

### 3. The tailored protective order preserves meaningful defense access

**Rule and authority.** Rules 10(a) and 26(c) permit a tailored public-pseudonymity order when substantial intimate privacy outweighs the public-identification interest and meaningful confidential defense access remains. Part III, joined by all eight Associate Justices, applies that rule and sustains this order.

**Controlling explanation.** Roe II supplies the balance between public identification, substantial private injury and the defendant's ability to investigate. The very young children, religious privacy and supported threats justify protection here. Defense counsel's discovery remained unrestricted; the order limited disclosure to others. The Board's claim that anonymity prevented investigation therefore does not describe the order the court entered. The ruling sustains this exercise of discretion on those facts.

### 4. The specific duplication objection does not establish abuse of discretion

**Rule and authority.** An attorney-fee award survives a duplication challenge when the court applies the proper rule, makes supported reductions and the remaining objection does not establish a clearly erroneous calculation or an abuse of discretion. All eight Associates join Part IV.

**Controlling explanation.** The district court deducted $9,500 for unnecessary travel and another five percent for duplication. The Board's remaining $4,600 objection acknowledged that some of the asserted duplication was likely already covered. That overlap supplies no basis to require the same deduction again or set aside the supported award.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act and function inquiry; its specific prohibition of governmental religious instruction controls this program.
- **Illinois ex rel. McCollum v. Board of Education and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp:** Applied for the established boundary against devotional instruction as part of public education.
- **Lee v. Weisman and Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe:** Applied because official adoption remains governmental action despite private composition or delivery.
- **Good News Club v. Milford Central School:** Distinguished because independent private access differs from the school's own instructional program.
- **Roe II v. Aware Woman Center for Choice, Inc.:** Applied to public pseudonymity with substantial privacy grounds and meaningful confidential defense access.

## Law After Decision

The governmental-instruction prohibition governs the recurring outsourcing arrangement as well as this program: a public school remains responsible for the devotional function it makes part of its instruction. Genuine private access and objectively secular study of religion retain their distinct protection. The existing confidentiality rule and ordinary review of fee awards govern their separate procedural components.

## Procedure After Action

The mandate leaves the program injunction, protective order and fee award in force. The district court supervises actual compliance and may address a concrete proposed change through ordinary proceedings. The Board must end the adjudicated devotional program while any such change is considered.

## Event

Pasquantino v. United States, No. 03-725. Argued November 9, 2004; decided April 26, 2005 (2005-04-26). On review of the Fourth Circuit's en banc judgment, 336 F.3d 321. The question is whether the federal wire-fraud statute reaches a scheme using domestic interstate wires to deprive Canada of import-tax revenue, despite the common-law rule against enforcing foreign revenue claims.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The convictions are affirmed on the statutory question presented.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Affirmed, 5–4.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Opposing: Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment sustains the Pasquantino brothers' six wire-fraud convictions and Hilts's Count I conviction against the statutory and revenue-rule challenge. The constitutional sentencing request first made in merits briefing is outside this review. Any independently available sentencing or restitution issue must proceed under its own authority and presentation requirements; affirmance on the question presented does not establish a particular compensable loss or direct a foreign-tax collection award.

## Opinion Topology

Chief Justice STONE-ZSELA delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. Ginsburg filed a dissent joined by Scalia, Souter and Breyer.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas | Actual monetary property; domestic wire-fraud offense; revenue rule; scope and remedy |
| Dissent | Ginsburg | Scalia, Souter, Breyer | Foreign fiscal claims and required congressional authorization |

## Holdings

The five Justices joining the Court's opinion control both holdings and their explanations.

### 1. An accrued foreign tax entitlement can be the money targeted by wire fraud

**Operative rule.** Section 1343 reaches an intentional, material scheme executed through the requisite domestic interstate wires to deprive a foreign sovereign of an existing entitlement to money. The Government must prove that entitlement, material deception, fraudulent intent and the required wire use beyond a reasonable doubt; success of the scheme and completed loss are not elements.

**Controlling explanation.** Cleveland v. United States distinguishes a government's regulatory licensing authority from money or property already belonging to it. The object here is the import revenue due upon the transactions, not Canada's power to decide who may engage in regulated activity. The domestic calls furthered concealment and procurement for the charged scheme. The victim's sovereign nationality does not alter the monetary character of the object. Neder v. United States supplies materiality and the enacted fraud requirements while distinguishing a criminal scheme from every condition of a completed civil fraud. Thus foreign-law noncompliance alone is insufficient, but the charged material scheme against actual money falls within the statute.

### 2. The revenue rule does not bar this domestic criminal prosecution

**Operative rule.** The common-law rule against judicial collection of foreign taxes does not categorically bar the United States from enforcing its own wire-fraud prohibition against a scheme executed through domestic interstate wires. Identifying the foreign victim's property entitlement is distinct from enforcing that sovereign's civil tax claim; a separate collection or restitution remedy requires its own authority.

**Controlling explanation.** This prosecution vindicates the domestic criminal prohibition Congress enacted. Its authority comes from that statute, not from an Executive choice to enlarge federal jurisdiction or foreign-affairs power. The defendants contend that recognition of the Canadian entitlement indirectly enforces Canada's fiscal policy. That objection identifies why the revenue rule must be considered, but its established collection function does not confer immunity from a separate domestic fraud offense. Hartford Fire and Empagran require attention to the statute and the conduct Congress regulates; neither supplies an automatic foreign-victim exception. The actual American execution distinguishes the present application. The Court resolves the criminal question while leaving any specific monetary remedy to its governing statute and proof requirements.

## Precedent Treatment

- Cleveland v. United States: its distinction between governmental regulation and existing property governs; its unpaid-revenue reservation is resolved for this domestic criminal application.
- Neder v. United States: its materiality requirement and distinction between a criminal scheme and completed civil fraud remain controlling.
- Hartford Fire Insurance Co. v. California: its attention to statutory reach and foreign regulatory interests is applied to the domestic conduct actually charged.
- F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A.: its source-specific territorial inquiry is preserved; the domestic execution here supplies a different statutory connection from the independent foreign injury addressed there.

## Law After Decision

A foreign sovereign's accrued right to money may be the property targeted by domestic wire fraud. Courts must establish the actual entitlement and each criminal element, then distinguish punishment for the domestic offense from any separately authorized collection remedy. The revenue rule remains applicable to the collection setting it addresses.

## Separate Writings

**Ginsburg, joined by Scalia, Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** The general fraud language carries the established background limit on implementing foreign revenue claims. Although an accrued tax entitlement is monetary, that classification does not answer whether Congress authorized criminal punishment whose sole property object is the foreign fiscal demand. Domestic telephone calls supply the federal instrument but do not eliminate the need for clearer congressional authorization. Neder's treatment of inherited legal meaning and the statutory attention to foreign sovereign interests reflected in Empagran support that distinction. The four would reverse the convictions based on this theory, with their dependent sentences. Their shared ground concerns foreign fiscal claims, not a general exclusion of foreign private property.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts implement the affirmance of the convictions on the statutory issue. Independently proper sentencing or restitution matters retain their own governing law, procedural prerequisites and evidentiary burdens.

## Source Notes

18 U.S.C. §1343; United States v. Pasquantino, 336 F.3d 321 (4th Cir. 2003) (en banc), for the judgments and charged domestic scheme; Cleveland v. United States, 531 U.S. 12; Neder v. United States, 527 U.S. 1. The monetary entitlement and calculations retain their status in the criminal record; this judgment resolves statutory coverage.

## Event

Small v. United States, No. 03-750. Argued November 3, 2004; decided April 26, 2005 (2005-04-26). On review of the Third Circuit's judgment, 333 F.3d 425. The question is whether a foreign conviction qualifies as a conviction in any court under 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(1), read with §921(a)(20).

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment: Reversed and remanded, 6–3.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The Japanese conviction cannot serve as this prosecution's §922(g)(1) predicate. The lower courts must remove the firearm conviction and dependent sentence resting on that predicate and conduct any remaining proceedings consistently with that ruling.

## Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas dissented, joined by Scalia and Kennedy.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Domestic conviction category and reversal |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia, Kennedy | Inclusive statutory category |

## Holdings

### Foreign convictions fall outside this firearm-disability predicate

**Rule and authority.** A conviction qualifies under §922(g)(1) only if it is domestic and satisfies the definition and qualifications in §921(a)(20). A foreign conviction cannot supply that predicate; the six Justices joining the Court's opinion adopt this rule and its application to Small.

**Controlling explanation.** The word any is broad within the statutory category it describes. Section 921(a)(20) identifies that category through a domestic structure: it excludes the specified federal or state antitrust, unfair-trade and similar business-regulatory offenses, and state misdemeanors punishable by two years or less, and it provides for the effect of pardons, expungement, setting aside and restoration of civil rights. A worldwide general category combined with those domestic exclusions would treat comparable foreign offenses differently without supplying a recognition system to administer that result.

The Government correctly observes that the charged firearm possession occurred in this country. That answers where the federal offense occurs, but not which antecedent convictions Congress included. Beecham v. United States selects the convicting jurisdiction's law for the restoration inquiry once a conviction falls within the statute; it does not define every foreign judgment as a qualifying conviction. Caron v. United States governs the federal effect of a substantial firearm restriction retained after domestic restoration; it likewise concerns the consequence of a qualifying conviction. Reading those separate statutory steps together supports the domestic category.

Small's Japanese conviction is therefore excluded categorically. The Court need not decide whether the individual Japanese proceeding satisfies the lower court's recognition standards. The holding construes this statutory context rather than imposing a general geographical meaning on any wherever Congress uses it.

## Precedent Treatment

- Beecham v. United States: its convicting-jurisdiction selector remains controlling for restoration of a qualifying conviction; it does not resolve the antecedent foreign-predicate question.
- Caron v. United States: its rule for a substantial firearm restriction retained after domestic restoration remains effective. The present holding determines the earlier category question rather than altering Caron's restoration consequence.

## Law After Decision

Courts first determine whether the asserted conviction is domestic and within §921(a)(20)'s definition. They then apply the existing rules for exclusions, pardon, expungement, setting aside and restoration. Foreign judgments are outside the §922(g)(1) predicate category, so that provision requires no individual foreign-fairness screening.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, joined by Scalia and Kennedy, dissenting.** The unqualified phrase any court includes foreign courts, while Congress expressly confined neighboring exceptions to federal or state offenses. The dissent considers that contrast a reason to retain the broader predicate, even if the exceptions produce some asymmetry. The statute regulates later American firearm possession, not foreign conduct or foreign courts. Beecham's selector can operate by reference to the convicting jurisdiction, and Caron's restoration dispute supplies no missing geographical limitation. General concerns about foreign procedures cannot replace the enacted language. These Justices would affirm the statutory ruling; their position does not adopt the lower court's complete recognition test or decide a distinct constitutional objection to an individual predicate.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for implementation of the reversal, removal of the conviction and sentence dependent on the Japanese predicate, and any further proceedings consistent with the domestic-category holding.

## Source Notes

18 U.S.C. §§921(a)(20), 922(g)(1); United States v. Small, 333 F.3d 425 (3d Cir. 2003), for the judgment and Japanese predicate; Beecham v. United States, 511 U.S. 368; Caron v. United States, 524 U.S. 308. The individual foreign-procedure question is unnecessary to the statutory disposition.
