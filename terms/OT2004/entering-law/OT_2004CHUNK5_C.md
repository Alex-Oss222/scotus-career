#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1488.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether the separate b.h. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy (Federal Courts — Mootness and decree scope).

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. The absence of an individual right, the existence of an action, and the scope of relief remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980) — preserves statutory-right enforcement under §1983; a violation of federal law still must invade an enforceable right.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987) — distinguishes its defined rent and utility entitlement; its enforceability holding and separate foreclosure inquiry remain operative.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990) — distinguishes its substantive reimbursement obligation and supplied benchmarks; neither its Medicaid holding nor its rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal is overruled.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981) — applies its clear-notice principle to the asserted funding obligation; no categorical immunity for state-plan provisions is created.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action

**Holding:** The Act does not create an implied private cause of action to enforce §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause because the claimants have not established congressional intent to create that remedy. This conclusion concerns the distinct action asserted directly under the Act and does not make failure to imply an action a bar to an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this independently necessary ground for rejecting the second enforcement route sustained below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to imply a private action does not foreclose an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy; other statutory provisions require their own examination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort v. Ash, 422 U.S. 66 (1975) — applies its inquiry into an implied private remedy, with congressional intent decisive.
- Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis, 444 U.S. 11 (1979) — applies its requirement of congressional intent to create the particular remedy asserted; statutory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992, decision — preserves the distinction between the existence of a cognizable action and appropriate relief within it; no Title IX holding is narrowed.

##### Whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. The reporting ground therefore cannot independently sustain the challenged caseworker injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-A, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this separate statutory holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting obligation and administrative enforcement remain. Section 671(a)(16)’s case-plan and review claim is not decided through this reporting holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wright, 479 U.S. 418 — applies the need to identify a privately enforceable statutory right; its distinct rent holding remains in force.
- Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 — applies provision-specific examination of a binding individual entitlement; no general rule against enforcement of plan provisions replaces it.

##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9) does not create an implied private action for these respondents to obtain the challenged caseworker relief: its reporting requirement and the Act's enforcement structure do not establish congressional intent to authorize that action. Rejection of this distinct statutory vehicle does not decide private enforcement of §671(a)(16).

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-B, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the ground rejecting direct implied enforcement of the reporting claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains open as to enforceable right, implied action, violation and additional lawful relief. No constitutional claim is revived or nationwide three-day deadline imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort, 422 U.S. 66, and Transamerica, 444 U.S. 11 — apply the congressional-intent requirement to the distinct implied reporting action.
- Franklin, February 26, 1992, decision — applies its action/remedy distinction without changing the established Title IX action or relief available within it.

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Barnes v. Gorman, June 17, 2002, resolves punitive-damages availability for the Title VI remedies incorporated in §504 of the Rehabilitation Act and Title II of the ADA. It preserves Franklin’s compensatory holding and does not independently decide punitive availability in every other statutory channel.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.

#### Wright v. MetroHealth Medical Center

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit No. 94-3548.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this anti-nepotism assignment policy directly and substantially burdens marriage

**Holding:** A marriage-triggered employment consequence calls for heightened scrutiny only when its actual operation directly and substantially interferes with marrying. The restriction on spouses working together in this emergency-flight unit, considered with the entire threatened-transfer sequence and the actual same-grade-and-pay hospital alternative, imposes an incidental assignment burden rather than such a marriage barrier.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous; both federal holdings have nine joins.

**Material application:** Paragraph C lets affected employees choose who transfers and gives thirty calendar days after notice before the less-senior hospital employee moves by default; it requires reasonable efforts toward comparable responsibility and pay, without guaranteeing them. Separately, Martha received and declined an actual offer to transfer to any available same-grade-and-pay position in another unit. John worked for contractor PHI; his threatened Louisiana relocation had to be considered, although no move, discharge or proven pay reduction occurred. Later agreed transfers and separate flight schedules did not erase the threat or promise permanent protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual marriage or absence of completed discharge is not dispositive. No unreported vacancy is found, and unavoidable separation, loss of livelihood or materially inferior work is not approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Loving and Zablocki preserve the fundamental decision to marry; Jobst distinguishes substantial barriers from incidental marital-status consequences.

##### Whether the close-interaction restriction has a rational operational basis

**Holding:** Under ordinary rational-basis review, limiting spouses’ simultaneous assignments in a close-interaction emergency-flight team is permissible when reasonably related to safety, supervision, conflicts and effective teamwork. The employer need not select the least restrictive arrangement or prove every married pair unsafe, but bare harm and irrational prejudice cannot justify the imposed disadvantage.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), unanimous.

**Material application:** Shared work in the small emergency unit supports reasonable predictions about loyalty, favoritism and coordination despite different nominal employers. Workable separate schedules during litigation do not compel permanent adoption of that alternative.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general immunity for anti-nepotism rules, suspect marital-status classification, mandatory individual exemption or required safety study is created. Animus, sex-based administration, deterrence of marriage, automatic discharge and materially inferior pay or responsibility are not approved. Additional association, career-liberty and Ohio-law questions remain outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The granted federal judgment is affirmed. Ohio-law Rule 12(b)(6) dismissals and PHI’s settlement remain undisturbed without new merits approval or a new judgment against PHI; no transfer or permanent scheduling arrangement is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heller permits reasonable prediction and imperfect fit within rational review; Romer preserves its prohibition on bare harm and irrational prejudice. Tracy recognizes relevant operational differences at its own scope, without a special rule validating marriage-related restrictions.

#### Blessing v. Freestone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1441.
**Decided:** April 21, 1997.

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

##### What effect state-plan placement has under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10

**Holding:** A Social Security Act provision cannot be denied enforceability merely because it specifies a state plan or its contents. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove that categorical ground without creating an individual right or changing the preserved conclusion that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The respective statutes apply to actions pending on October 20 and October 31, 1994 and those filed thereafter, including this action. Other established grounds governing private actions remain except the state-plan ground the legislation removes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Suter*'s actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights rulings and separate implied-action rulings remain, together with its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim. The statutory reference imports no broader state-plan immunity.

##### Whether a general prayer for relief eliminates Madrid's particular distribution allegation

**Holding:** Madrid's allegation of nondistribution under former §657(b)(1) requires its own inquiry despite the broad programwide prayer. Each actual claim must be matched to its provision, conduct, remedy, governing version, and operative dates; an obsolete or not-yet-effective duty cannot silently justify an injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former distribution language is more specific than the aggregate benchmark, but the Court decides no individual right, eligibility, payment fact, defendant, or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not award a separate $50 for every installment regardless of timing. The rule applies to covered approved-plan collections for fiscal years after September 30, 1976, subject to the distinct foster-care rules in subsection (d).

**Material application:** The foster-care sequence first reimburses current maintenance; qualifying excess up to ordered support goes to the placement agency for the child's interests; the remainder reimburses unrepaid earlier foster-care or family-assistance payments with appropriate federal reimbursement; any balance goes to the agency for the child. The agency may reserve its payments for later needs or make them available to the person providing daily care. No applicable exception or payment fact is established for Madrid.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remand must apply the following distinct statutory dates and qualifications, without inferring an Arizona implementation event or default:

- Pub. L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.
- Under §395(b), the applicable date is the later of the otherwise specified date or the effective date of implementing state legislation, but no later than the first day of the first calendar quarter after the close of the first regular legislative session beginning after August 22, 1996. Each year of a two-year session counts separately. Under §395(c), a State unable to comply without amending its constitution is protected from a noncompliance finding until the earlier of one year after the necessary amendment takes effect or five years after August 22, 1996. Neither rule establishes an Arizona event or compliance fact here.
- The former §654(24) operational-computer deadline was changed from October 1, 1995 to October 1, 1997 by the October 12, 1995 amendment; the old date establishes no present default. The 1996 text separately requires compliance by October 1, 1997 with requirements enacted on or before October 13, 1988, and by October 1, 2000 with requirements enacted on or before August 22, 1996. The latter deadline extends one day for every day the Secretary misses the final-regulations deadline of two years after August 22, 1996. Implementation deadlines remain distinct from enactment and general effective dates.
- Section 654a(g), added by §312(c), takes effect October 1, 1998. Section 312(d)(2) separately permits a State processing receipts through local courts on August 22, 1996 to continue that receipt processing through September 30, 1999, at its option, through the courts already processing those payments by enactment. That narrow exception concerns §654b(b)(1)'s unit receipt-handling requirement; it is not a wholesale extension of every computer-system obligation. No Arizona qualification for it is found, and these future duties are not April 1997 mandates merely because they appear in the compilation.

##### Whether relief may follow general administrative deficiencies without a proved individual violation

**Holding:** Relief requires an actual individual right, an available §1983 channel, a proper defendant, breach, causation, and a fitted lawful remedy. General administrative failure alone supports neither an undifferentiated compliance injunction nor a prescribed statewide staffing or computer redesign; rejection of the broad theory does not require dismissal of every concrete claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for provision-specific adjudication. No specified payment, damages award, structural injunction, or wholesale dismissal is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No directly implied Title IV-D action is decided. The Eleventh Amendment argument and proposed overruling of *Thiboutot*, neither raised below nor included in the questions granted, are not reached; defendant-specific issues remain subject to their own inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Maine v. Thiboutot* remains undisturbed. *Livadas*'s separate remedial inquiries prevent both an automatic structural decree and categorical rejection of particular claims.

#### Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1866.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### Actual notice to a corrective official is not the exclusive attribution route

**Holding:** A funding recipient's responsibility for a teacher's intentional discrimination cannot invariably be defeated solely because no official empowered to correct the harassment had actual notice. Kinman is limited insofar as it made that particular notice route exclusive; employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical regulatory breach, standing alone, still does not establish recipient liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this limited negative proposition. They do not adopt a common complete damages framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Limited insofar as actual notice to a corrective official was the exclusive recipient-attribution threshold. Its actual-notice, deliberate and unreasonable disregard and causal-injury route remains available, together with the other Title IX elements. Its rejection of automatic teacher-status attribution and a technical policy breach as sufficient by themselves remains. No earlier sole-Justice awareness inference becomes Court law merely through this decision.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as allowing appropriate compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and loss. The present attribution holding does not establish a damages award or unintended-effects liability.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** The established private action remains intact; no new regulatory cause of action is recognized.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Its distinction between a directly shared negative proposition and unadopted complete affirmative frameworks supports keeping the exclusivity holding separate from the limited Marks analysis.

##### Authority materially enabling discrimination, combined with the recipient's causal fault, supplies a narrower attribution route

**Holding:** A recipient may be held responsible when school-conferred authority materially enables a teacher's intentional discrimination and the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This is a sufficient route for attributing the discrimination beyond the actual-corrective-official-notice route; it establishes neither the underlying discrimination nor causal loss, damages or the defeat of a supported defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has no other formal joins. Its stated attribution component supplies the narrower controlling ground under Marks v. United States and Cipollone's component-specific application. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer independently support attribution whenever actual delegated educational power materially aids intentional harassment, without Stone-Zsela's additional recipient-fault requirement. School-conferred authority must actually and materially enable the wrong; title or opportunity alone is insufficient under either ground. Every case meeting the narrower conjunction therefore meets that independently sufficient broader attribution route. Those judgment-supporting positions total five, without converting the four Justices into joins in Stone-Zsela's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authority, recipient-fault and causal-continuation conditions are conjunctive. The alternative involving reporting failure requires willful blindness and deliberate indifference, not constructive notice or negligence alone. The Court does not adopt ordinary agency liability as a complete general rule, the four-Justice negligence route or every Title VII attribution principle. Discrimination, institutional fault, causation, compensable injury, damages and defenses remain unresolved. Official-policy cases, prospective enforcement, individual liability and state-law remedies are outside this holding. The separate regulatory duties to adopt and publish prompt, equitable grievance procedures under 34 C.F.R. §106.8(b) and give nondiscrimination notice under §106.9(a) are not converted into strict private damages liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Marks v. United States:** Applied to the particular sufficient attribution ground, without treating every reason or remedy proposed by a judgment supporter as controlling.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group:** Its component-specific logical-subset inquiry supports the limited attribution holding despite the absence of whole-opinion joins; separate proof, defenses and damages questions remain distinct.
- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson, Andrade and Faragher:** Their employment-attribution principles are not transferred wholesale to Title IX. Faragher's unresolved effective-protection/nonuse defense is not resolved here.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman:** Its funding-notice principle remains; neither it nor Title IX's administrative enforcement sequence becomes a literal exhaustion requirement for this private action.

##### The appealed Title IX summary judgment is reversed for ordinary further proceedings

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this formal reversal and ordinary remand. Stone-Zsela alone adopts Part III's application of his recipient-fault reasoning to the disputed warning and reporting evidence; the other four support further proceedings on their separately stated grounds. Their judgment agreement is not a five-Justice finding of actual knowledge, deliberate disregard, willful blindness, negligence, material aid, causation or liability. The Court orders neither compensation nor a compulsory trial. The unappealed constitutional, personal and state-law claims are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it affirms summary judgment for the district on the appealed Title IX claim, and the case is remanded. The lower courts must determine whether school-conferred authority materially enabled the discrimination and whether the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure caused it to continue, while preserving the actual-notice route and the remaining elements, ordinary procedures and defenses.

#### Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-843.
**Decided:** 1999-05-24.

##### A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment

**Holding:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable for its own response to peer harassment because of sex when an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls both the harasser and the relevant educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's attendance does not impute the peer's acts to the recipient; knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination and causal loss are independent requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six Justices).

##### Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard

**Holding:** The harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. Sexually charged conduct or offense alone does not establish discrimination because of sex, the required educational harm or the independent institutional elements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (five Justices). Stone-Zsela does not join the severe-or-pervasive threshold.

##### The pleaded recipient claim warrants ordinary further proceedings

**Holding:** Allegations of repeated school-setting harassment, reports including to the principal, continued conduct, deliberately inadequate protection and resulting educational interference state a claim for examination under the sufficient recipient-fault route. The categorical dismissal must be reversed, but an absent element, reasonable response, inadequate control or notice, missing causation or a supported defense may defeat recovery on the developed record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six Justices).

**Limits and questions not reached:** Notice, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination, educational deprivation and causal compensable injury are independent. Ordinary negligence, policy breach, peer attendance or unsuccessful reasonable correction alone do not establish liability. No general agency/defense code or Stone-Zsela reporting-failure proposal becomes controlling; teacher-attribution routes remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings under the sufficient recipient-own-wrong route. No compulsory trial, damages, particular discipline, individual-capacity recovery or reopening of unrelated claims is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools is applied to available compensation for proved intentional discrimination, with recipient responsibility, causation and loss still distinct.
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District is extended at its sufficient actual-notice/culpable-disregard route to substantially controlled peer conduct. Its severe-or-pervasive contextual educational-harm standard remains; the peer extension is stated expressly.
- Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District retains the limited teacher-authority-plus-recipient-fault conjunction and its rejection of exclusivity in teacher cases. Neither delegated teacher power nor that complete route is imputed to peers.
- NCAA v. Smith preserves the distinct federal-assistance recipient requirement; economic connection or control alone does not establish receipt.
- Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson informs the distinction between participation and welcome through Kinman without importing employment attribution wholesale.
- Harris v. Forklift Systems informs the contextual objective-and-experienced abuse inquiry through Kinman, without mandatory psychiatric injury or any indispensable factor.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman retains its funding-notice principle; the controlling recipient-own-wrong route concerns intentional educational discrimination, not automatic liability for a third party.

#### Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1908.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Title VI's bounded direct prospective action, recognized in Powell v. Ridge, is available to enforce the Department of Justice section 602 effects regulation

**Holding:** Persons actually subjected to a prohibited national-origin effect in a covered federally assisted program may seek prospective compliance with the Department of Justice regulation through Title VI's bounded direct equitable action. Under Powell v. Ridge, a person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects. The action exists within Title VI's particular statutory context and rests on that context, not on the regulation's own force; it does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. The rule applies to every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within Powell's bounded prospective scope; it is not an exception for English-language testing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, directly controls this rule, its explanation, the treatment of Powell, the application, remedy and limits; five direct joins. O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judgment, applying Powell's direct-action holding as controlling statutory precedent without adopting its account of congressional authorization; the four join no part of the Court's opinion. The judgment is unanimous, and there is no dissent.

**Material application:** The Department receives federal assistance. #### Gonzaga University v. Doe, 536 U.S. 273

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-679.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### I. Section 1983 asks whether Congress created the right and whether it excluded the remedy

**Holding:** Section 1983 supplies the cause of action for rights secured by federal statutes. The claimant must show that Congress created the asserted individual right; the defendant may then show that Congress excluded the Section 1983 remedy, expressly or through a comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with individual enforcement. A right so shown is presumptively enforceable. No separate showing that Congress intended to create a private remedy is required, and the standards for implying an action directly from a statute do not govern a Section 1983 claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. Breyer, joined by Souter, also concurs: the decision is statute-specific, and centralized administration informs the content of the duty without displacing Section 1983. Thomas, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, dissents on the ground that FERPA's institution-directed funding condition confers no individual right; that position is noncontrolling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Maine v. Thiboutot, Wright, Wilder, Livadas and Blessing are reaffirmed. Powell v. Ridge's holding that a regulation cannot create an individual right absent from the statute, and South Camden's application of it, stand; this right arises from the statute itself. Sandoval's direct-action holding is neither enlarged nor displaced.

##### II. An individual right rests on identified beneficiaries, a binding obligation and definiteness

**Holding:** A statute creates an individual right when its text, structure and operation show a binding obligation that protects an identifiable person or defined class and gives that protection sufficiently definite content for judicial enforcement. The factors are identified beneficiaries, a binding rather than precatory obligation, and definiteness. Grammatical form is relevant but not controlling: a provision addressed to a funding recipient or to the administering agency, or framed as a condition on or prohibition of federal funding, can secure an individual entitlement when those factors are met. A general benefit, a beneficial purpose, a mandatory directive alone or an aggregate standard of institutional performance does not create a right.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II; the same five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The factors restate Blessing's provision-specific inquiry and adopt no general statutory-benefit test. Suter's provision-specific holdings and Powell's rejection of regulation-created rights mark the limits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Blessing's three factors are restated and applied. Suter's provision-specific holdings, Wright, Wilder and Pennhurst's clear-notice requirement are preserved.

##### III. FERPA secures freedom from unauthorized disclosure through a prohibited policy or practice

**Holding:** 20 U.S.C. §1232g(b) secures to each student, and to the student's parents while the consent rights remain theirs, an individual right to freedom from unauthorized disclosure of the student's education records, or of personally identifiable information from them, through a recipient institution's policy or practice of permitting such disclosure. The policy-or-practice requirement is part of the right, so an isolated employee's mistake does not violate it. Consent, the statutory exceptions, coverage under the maintained-record definition and the nature of the information remain decisive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III; the same five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether a disclosure reflects a policy or practice is an element the claimant must prove; no preserved challenge to the sufficiency of that showing below is decided. Consent rights pass from the parent to the student upon reaching eighteen or attending a postsecondary institution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Owasso's maintained-record definition governs coverage, and the private-enforcement question it reserved is answered for §1232g(b). Blessing is applied.

##### IV. FERPA's administrative scheme is not incompatible with Section 1983

**Holding:** Congress has not excluded Section 1983 enforcement of §1232g(b). FERPA contains no express exclusion, and its administrative complaint process and funding-termination sanction are relevant but not incompatible with individual enforcement: the sanction operates on the institution and gives the injured student no individual relief, and the complaint process supplies no individual remedy that a Section 1983 action would bypass.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV; the same five direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Centralized administration and the Secretary's interpretations continue to inform what FERPA requires; they do not withdraw the Section 1983 remedy. A different statute's comprehensive individual remedy may still show incompatibility.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Blessing and Bruneau are applied. Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association and Smith v. Robinson are distinguished.

##### V. State action and particular remedies are reserved

**Holding:** Receipt of federal funds is not itself state action, and the right recognized here does not make it so. Whether this recipient's employee acted under color of state law is outside the grant, and the Washington courts' determination is undisturbed. Compensatory damages, punitive damages and equitable relief each keep their own conditions; the Court calculates no damages and adopts no punitive-damages rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part V; the same five direct votes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Washington Supreme Court, 5–4, on the reviewed question: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the FERPA-based judgment. The reinstatement of damages on the FERPA-based Section 1983 claim stands; no damages are calculated, the excluded state-action issue is not reopened, no punitive-damages rule is adopted, and the independent state-law judgments are unaffected.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rendell-Baker v. Kohn and Blum v. Yaretsky are preserved. Barnes v. Gorman's incorporated Title VI remedial holding is not extended to Section 1983.

#### County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes and Bands of the Yakima Indian Nation, 502 U.S. 251 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-408 and 90-577.
**Decided:** January 14, 1992.

##### Whether state ad valorem taxation may reach reservation land patented in fee under the General Allotment Act

**Holding:** The General Allotment Act's established fee-land rule and the Burke Act proviso authorize state ad valorem taxation of reservation land validly patented in fee under that Act, including qualifying land later held by the Nation. ##### Whether permission to tax the land also authorizes a sales excise imposed on an Indian owner

**Holding:** Congressional permission to tax qualifying fee land ad valorem does not unmistakably authorize a tax imposed on an Indian owner's sale of that land. Securing the transactional tax with a lien does not change its legal incidence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sales excise remains prohibited, and remand relief must respect that prohibition.

#### Cartwright v. Barnes, 304 F.3d 1138

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 304 F.3d 1138.
**Decided:** February 25, 2003.

For the separate Article I classification in Cartwright, February 25, 2003, judgment below 304 F.3d 1138 (no Supreme Court docket supplied), see Constitutional Structure — Cartwright v. Barnes.

##### Whether the pleaded cumulative burdens distinguish Georgia’s petition system from Jenness

**Holding:** Under Anderson–Burdick, courts weigh the character and magnitude of the actual burden against the State's precise interests and the need for its restrictions, and must explain where the burden falls on that calibrated scale. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to compelling interests; substantial burdens require an important objective and a demonstrated substantial relationship between that objective and the restriction's actual reach, without proof that success is impossible; reasonable nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. Accepting the complaint's facts, the alleged combination of a registered-voter denominator fixed by the preceding election, district identification after reapportionment, surplus-signature and sheet-affidavit verification burdens and the absence of any Libertarian congressional petition qualification since 1943 plausibly states a substantial or severe burden that Jenness does not answer as a matter of law, so the access claim requires factual development.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes control this rule and its application. Thomas, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, would affirm the access dismissal and does not reach Part II's statement of levels.

**Material application:** The requirement is five percent of registered voters eligible to vote in the preceding election in the relevant electorate. The Court accepts the pleaded absence of Libertarian congressional qualification since 1943, district-identification problems after reapportionment, surplus-signature needs and affidavit burdens, which together plausibly allege at least a substantial burden. The six-month circulation period, multiple-petition signing and primary participation remain significant on remand; notarization concerns the circulator’s sheet, not each signature.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The three levels describe the structure of Anderson's calibrated scale, not a new tier. The decision creates no universal five-percent safe harbor, does not independently adjudicate the statewide one-percent rule and does not replace calibrated burden review with rational-basis review. Party-specific failure to qualify is evidence, not proof of severity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of the access claim and remand for factual development, 5–4; the challenged congressional petition requirements remain operative while the claim proceeds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jenness v. Fortson, 403 U.S. 431, is reaffirmed at the scope of the complete Georgia system it sustained; it does not answer as a matter of law the materially different combined burdens pleaded here.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780, Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428, and Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party (April 28, 1997) retain the calibrated burden, precise-interest and fit framework; with Wood v. Meadows (June 11, 2001), they supply the three levels this decision states as that framework's structure, without creating a new tier or substituting rational-basis review.

### Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

**Governing question:** Whether a federal tribal-property claim may proceed against responsible state officers despite disputed title or sovereign interests.

**Current rule:** An alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights may support prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officers actually connected to it. A disputed federal title question or important sovereign interest does not alone bar the claim. Relief must govern proved unlawful future official conduct; it may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or order the State itself to convey title. A general obligation to obey law does not supply the required officer connection.

**Authority by component:**

### Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

**Governing question:** When an asserted statutory obligation creates an individual right enforceable through §1983.

**Current rule:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding obligation and defining the individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than vaguely or amorphously. Text, statutory setting and the asserted entitlement govern; a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish a right. An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable under §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Right creation and remedial foreclosure remain separate inquiries. Section 1983 supplies the cause of action: the claimant must show that Congress created the asserted individual right, the defendant may show that Congress excluded the §1983 remedy, and no separate intent to create a private remedy is required. Text, structure and operation must show identified beneficiaries, a binding obligation and sufficiently definite content; grammatical form is relevant but not controlling, and a funding condition or prohibition can secure an individual entitlement. FERPA §1232g(b) secures a student's individual right to freedom from unauthorized disclosure of education records through a recipient's policy or practice; the policy-or-practice requirement is part of the right, an isolated employee mistake does not violate it, and consent, the statutory exceptions, maintained-record coverage and the nature of the information remain decisive. FERPA's administrative complaint process and institutional funding sanction do not foreclose §1983. Receipt of federal funds is not state action; state action and each remedy keep their own conditions. Title IX’s individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from §1983 enforcement by its administrative funding process or established implied action. The claimant must prove the actual statutory violation, including defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle enlarges no duty. Title IX also does not displace a §1983 Equal Protection claim independently secured by the Constitution merely because the same school harassment supplies both theories; purposeful constitutional discrimination, state action, defendant-specific or municipal responsibility, causation, remedies and actual preclusion remain separate.

### Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX

**Governing question:** Sex-based teacher harassment and compensatory responsibility of the funding recipient.

**Current rule:** Sexual subject matter or offense alone does not show discrimination because of sex. Same-sex harassment can constitute sex discrimination; the claim requires sex-based causation, unwelcome treatment, sufficient educational harm, recipient responsibility and causal injury. Participation or later continuation does not conclusively establish earlier welcome. The environment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to be objectively abusive and actually experienced as such, assessed in the educational context without mandatory grade-loss, psychiatric-injury or factor requirements. One sufficient route to recipient damages is actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial risk and deliberate, unreasonable disregard of that known danger causing injury or continuation. This actual-corrective-official-notice route is not exclusive. A separate sufficient attribution route exists when school-conferred authority actually and materially enables the teacher’s intentional discrimination and the recipient’s own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This route does not itself establish discrimination, causal loss, damages or defeat of an independent defense.

### Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations

**Governing question:** Whether private plaintiffs may enforce the additional disparate-impact prohibitions of Title VI section 602 regulations through a directly implied action or through section 1983.

**Current rule:** A person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects. The action rests on Title VI's particular statutory context, not on the regulation's own force, and does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. It reaches every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within that bounded prospective scope; neither a different agency's regulation, state program or permit nor a finding rather than an allegation of prohibited effects distinguishes it. The plaintiff must establish (1) an applicable section 602 regulation validly issued under the authority Congress conferred, consistent with Title VI's statutory limits; (2) a defendant subject to the regulation through the legally required federal-assistance relationship; (3) a concrete exclusion, denial of benefits or other material discriminatory effect within the regulation's actual terms, attributable to an identified recipient policy or method of administration; (4) an actual or imminent continuing effect that the requested prospective relief can meaningfully correct; and (5) an appropriate defendant and a remedy compatible with applicable immunity, statutory and equitable limitations. The violation is proved on the regulation's own terms, without the intentional discrimination section 601 itself requires. Disproportionate statistics may support proof, but a numerical racial difference alone does not conclusively establish a violation; the claimant must connect the disparity to the challenged administration and the regulation's operative prohibition, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and regulatory coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. No universal Title VII-style business-necessity framework applies, and no prior agency finding is required. Relief is confined to a declaration of an established violation, an injunction requiring the recipient to end or correct the prohibited method, a reasonable compliance period where consistent with preventing ongoing injury, and ordinary equitable supervision proportionate to the violation.

Separately, Powell's section 1983 holding remains operative: section 601's intentional-discrimination protection and section 602's agency directions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted through section 1983. A regulation can elaborate a statutory entitlement but cannot itself supply a missing broader statutory right. That holding rests on the absence of a right, not on congressional foreclosure of an established one.

Section 601's established private action for intentional discrimination remains independently available on its own proof and procedural footing. Discriminatory purpose must be proved; disparate effects may be evidence relevant to purpose, but they do not alone establish intentional discrimination. A substantial federal question supports jurisdiction independently of the merits, and prospective relief on either the direct effects claim or the section 601 claim may issue against a proper state officer only upon an established violation and the officer's actual connection to it. Federal agencies retain the enforcement Congress authorized; section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures govern the agencies' own powers and are not the exclusive route to prospective compliance.

**Governing question:** When a later land cession or state admission extinguishes separately retained treaty use rights.

**Current rule:** A later land cession does not extinguish separately retained treaty use rights unless the agreement, read in its setting and as the tribal parties reasonably understood it, clearly relinquishes those rights.
