### 2005-02-23 — [Johnson v. California; No. 03-636](../records/johnson-v-california_merits_2005-02-23.md#public-projection)

An express racial prison-housing classification must satisfy the common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between the classification and that objective, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives that offer comparable effectiveness with materially less racial disadvantage. Custody makes credible security experience, urgency and genuinely incomplete information relevant within that inquiry, while a protective purpose, general racial assumptions or administrative convenience alone cannot satisfy it.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls.

A judgment sustaining the reception policy under materially more deferential review must be vacated for application of the common standard to the actual policy and record. Additional factfinding serves genuine material disputes about that application; the court must resolve issues the existing record already permits it to decide.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies the six-Justice judgment.

Individual damages depend on actionable conduct, causal responsibility and the qualified-immunity law governing when each defendant acted; prospective correction depends on continuing exposure and a responsible current official. Under 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1), a prison decree must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation, use the least intrusive means necessary, and give substantial weight to adverse effects on public safety or operation of the criminal justice system; relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violate state or local law, is permissible only if federal law requires it to correct the violation, it is necessary, and no other relief will correct the violation.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls these independent requirements.

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens, who would hold the categorical policy unconstitutional now and remand for remaining relief, and Scalia and Thomas, who would affirm under Turner.

The lower court must evaluate the policy's coverage, duration, evidentiary support, burdens and concrete practicable alternatives under the government's burden. Further evidence is required only for genuinely unresolved material issues. Damages require separate determinations of individual conduct, causation and conduct-date immunity. Prospective relief requires current exposure, proper responsible officials and the statutory remedy findings. If the policy fails, relief must correct its operation for the persons it governs unless California establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation, consistently with §3626.

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña is applied to express prison-housing classifications: its common substantial-justification burden governs actual racial treatment.
- Brown v. City of Oneonta is applied: benign purpose does not remove deliberate racial selection from that burden.
- Turner v. Safley is distinguished as the source of general prison-regulation review; its deference does not replace the governing racial-classification rule.
- Overton v. Bazzetta retains force for the prison matters it decides and for the practical relevance of institutional evidence.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton supply the individual conduct-date immunity inquiry.
- Saucier v. Katz and Brosseau v. Haugen preserve separate adjudication of the constitutional merits and the existing personal defense, including obvious applications of existing law.

The district court must set a prompt schedule for resolving the relevant evidence and any supported interim-relief request under the applicable statutory conditions. It must identify current responsible officials and continuing exposure, decide any personal damages defenses and causal responsibility separately, and require a compliant housing practice if the common policy fails. Any injunction must specify permitted security exceptions and compliance dates consistently with §3626, so that it corrects the established violation through a workable decree.


### 2005-03-01 — [Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States; No. 04-190; judgment below, 365 F.3d 385](../records/petro-hunt-v-united-states_merits_2005-03-01.md#public-projection)

Federal common law governs the preclusive effect of the earlier federal-question judgment. A final title judgment binds the claim it adjudicated, including grounds that could have been advanced within that claim, but distinct servitudes with different acquisition and use histories do not become the same claim merely because they share owners, related conveyances or a legal premise; the party invoking preclusion must establish the required identity from the actual instruments and adjudication.

Mutual issue preclusion requires the identical issue to have been actually litigated and necessarily determined. Nebo Oil's adjudication of state-law retroactivity and constitutional objections, while assuming Louisiana law supplied the rule of decision, does not preclude the distinct federal choice-of-law issue now asserted.

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Nebo Oil's adjudication of its original servitude remains effective. The Fifth Circuit's determination that the other 95 servitudes are subject to Louisiana's ten-year nonuse prescription remains effective; the district court must determine actual prescription and resulting title for each servitude.

- Nevada v. United States supplies the inquiry into the property right actually adjudicated and protects final title within that scope.
- Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. supplies the distinction between a judgment's source and actual external effect; its diversity-specific state-law incorporation rule remains confined to that setting.
- Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. retains its protection of completed federal adjudications; applying preclusion to the decree's actual subject matter preserves that protection.
- Commissioner v. Sunnen and Montana v. United States supply the issue-identity and actual-adjudication requirements applied here.
- United States v. Stauffer Chemical Co. retains mutual issue preclusion where its predicates exist; the missing actual litigation distinguishes this case.

The district court must identify each disputed servitude's instrument, acquisition history, qualifying good-faith operations and interruptions, apply the governing prescription rule, and determine title. It must consider the asserted continuing operations on five servitudes, the Government's concessions concerning operations on some servitudes, and the second summary-judgment motion previously dismissed as moot. Any properly supported specific preclusion claim must satisfy the claim-identity or actually-litigated-issue requirements stated here. The fee cross-appeal lacks its former prevailing-party predicate after the blanket preclusion judgment falls; any later fee issue follows the eventual proper judgment.


### 2005-03-30 — [Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Industries Corp.](../records/exxon-mobil-corp-v-saudi-basic-industries-corp_merits_2005-03-30.md#public-projection)

**Holdings.**

#### A parallel state judgment does not extinguish an independent federal action

**Holding and operative rule.** A federal district court does not lose original jurisdiction merely because a parallel state action reaches judgment while the federal action is pending. The Rooker–Feldman prohibition on district-court appellate review applies when a state-court loser brings a federal action after the state judgment, complains of injury caused by that judgment and seeks its review and rejection; independent claims remain subject to the applicable jurisdictional statutes, §1738 preclusion and properly applicable abstention rules.

**Authority.** The opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation.** Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia distinguishes an injury caused by the asserted legal error of a state judicial order from an independently actionable wrong. It therefore bars a damages claim that would require appellate rejection of an earlier state judgment, while preserving an original claim based on underlying conduct. The present case applies that distinction to the sequence of parallel proceedings.

Exxon filed its federal royalty claims before the Delaware judgment, sought relief for SABIC’s conduct and prevailed in the state trial. The later judgment did not turn that original suit into an appeal. Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman preserve the appellate boundary reflected in §1257; that allocation does not make original jurisdiction disappear whenever state litigation reaches judgment first. Congress’s express grants of federal review, including habeas review under §2254, retain their own terms.

SABIC’s finality and duplication concerns call for the doctrines that actually govern those concerns. Section 1738 requires the federal court to give the state judgment the preclusive effect it receives under the applicable state law. Colorado River Water Conservation District v. United States supplies the distinct exceptional-circumstances inquiry for parallel-litigation abstention, and Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. requires an applicable source before surrendering federal adjudication. Overlapping issues, or an independent claim inconsistent with a state court’s legal conclusion, do not alone create an appellate action.

The federal complaint invoked §1330. SABIC’s remaining immunity contentions, the actual effect of the Delaware judgment and any residual mootness issue require adjudication on their own predicates because the court of appeals disposed of the action on the different jurisdictional theory rejected here.

**Precedent Treatment.**

Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia retains its claim-specific distinction between judgment-caused and independent injury; this decision resolves the pending-action timing question it left open. Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman retain their prohibition on district-court appellate reassessment of state judgments. Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. and Colorado River Water Conservation District v. United States preserve the separate sources and conditions of abstention. Marrese v. American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons supplies §1738’s direction to examine the State’s preclusion law.

**Law After Decision.**

The jurisdictional rule governs civil original actions involving parallel state litigation. A state judgment may determine the fate of particular federal claims through preclusion, while the federal action retains its original character. Courts must examine the source of the asserted injury, the relief requested and the commencement sequence instead of treating overlap as jurisdictional extinction. A complaint styled as damages remains subject to the appellate bar when its actual claim seeks rejection of a preexisting state judgment.



### 2005-04-19 — [Monsanto Co. v. McFarling](../records/monsanto-co-v-mcfarling_merits_2005-04-19.md#public-projection)

**Holdings.**

#### Part I: The saved-seed exception does not displace utility-patent coverage

**Holding and operative rule.** A plant-variety saved-seed exception does not authorize reproduction of additional seed within independently valid utility-patent claims. A no-replanting promise directed to that reproduction is not misuse merely because it prevents saving and replanting harvested seed; patent and contractual coverage must be established, and independently applicable defenses retain their force.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join Part I, including its confined contract application.

**Controlling explanation.** J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc. permits utility-patent and plant-variety protection to coexist while keeping each statute’s exceptions within its own right. Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer addresses the certificate-based saved-seed provision and supplies no general immunity from an independently applicable utility patent.

The relevant patent claims are assumed in this appeal to cover the additional generations. McFarling accepted one-season planting permission and conceded saving and replanting the harvest. Ownership of the purchased seed does not itself confer permission to make and use additional patented seed. Its reproductive character and his willingness to pay a technology fee do not compel a different reproduction license. The promise therefore survives the presented categorical defenses, and the conceded conduct establishes its breach.

This holding concerns the additional seed and the presented no-replanting promise, rather than every product made with a patented tool or every contract condition. Patent validity lies outside this appeal because the defense was not preserved in the reviewed partial judgment.

#### Part II: Patent scope does not conclusively defeat an independent tie

**Holding and operative rule.** A restriction within a patent grant does not, for that reason alone, defeat a Sherman Act §1 tying claim. The claimant must establish distinct products through actual separate consumer demand, conditioning, sufficient power in the tying market, a substantial amount of affected tied commerce, and the competitive injury and causal requirements for the relief sought; lawful patent exclusion retains substantial justificatory force.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join this general rule in Part II. Its application requiring further proceedings in this case is the separate six-Justice holding in Part III.

**Controlling explanation.** CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation holds that patent scope does not conclusively immunize independently unlawful monopoly maintenance. The same coexistence of statutory rights and independent antitrust duties supports rejecting conclusive scope immunity for a properly established §1 tie. This extends CSU’s nonimmunity principle, while the offense remains governed by §1’s own elements.

Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc. and Jefferson Parish Hospital District No. 2 v. Hyde supply the demand-based product inquiry and the ordinary tying requirements. Patent ownership does not prove market power, and a patent, trait or single-product label does not conclusively merge products for which separate demand exists. A preferred licensing arrangement or its royalty expense alone does not establish an independent antitrust wrong. Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP likewise requires the particular offense and causal basis for the requested remedy.

#### Part III: The preserved tying claim requires its own adjudication

**Holding and operative rule.** The rejection of McFarling’s tying counterclaim must be vacated because the appellate court relied on conclusive patent-scope immunity and expressly declined to resolve the separate-product inquiry. On remand, the preserved record must be tested under the independent tying elements and ordinary summary-judgment requirements; actual failure of an element may support renewed judgment.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part III and its remedy.

**Controlling explanation.** Monsanto and the partial dissent characterize the requested arrangement as a license to reproduce patented second-generation seed while paying a technology fee, rather than a coerced purchase of something independently demanded. That is a substantial merits defense. But the appellate court acknowledged the separate-demand inquiry and expressly left it unresolved, using patent scope instead. This Court cannot substitute a conclusive finding about product identity for that declined inquiry.

Kodak requires actual demand and market conditions, with the remaining elements proved in their own terms. CSU prevents the scope label from replacing that analysis; it does not transplant §2’s offense into §1. The proper course is therefore an element-based adjudication of the preserved claim. Any resulting effect on contract enforcement depends on a proved violation or defense and its own governing requirements.

#### Part IV: The actual-damages mandate remains operative

**Holding and operative rule.** The vacatur of the $780,000 liquidated sum and the existing remand for actual damages are preserved. Actual damages remain subject to surviving lawful liability, any independently established defense or offset, the proper saved-and-replanted measure and the prohibition on double recovery.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join Part IV’s preservation of the damages disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** The lower judgment applied Missouri’s requirement of a reasonable relation to compensable harm and difficulty estimating the loss. It preserved reasonableness measured against either anticipated harm or actual harm; Monsanto did not defend the grossly disproportionate award through the actual-loss alternative. The uniform 120 multiplier, based on purchased rather than saved and replanted bags, lacked the required relation to this breach’s compensable harm. The existing state-law damages disposition therefore remains in place. The question of an appropriate forecast for supplying or selling saved seed to third parties lies outside this replanting dispute and remains as the lower judgment left it.

**Precedent Treatment.**

J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc. is applied to preserve concurrent protection and the separation of statutory exceptions. Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer remains confined to the plant-variety right it construed. CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation’s rejection of conclusive patent-scope immunity is extended from its §2 setting to the presented §1 tying defense; §1 retains its own offense requirements. Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc. and Jefferson Parish Hospital District No. 2 v. Hyde supply actual separate-demand and tying rules. Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP preserves the distinct requirements for an antitrust offense and its remedy.

**Law After Decision.**

The particular patent-covered reproduction promise survives the categorical saved-seed and scope-based misuse attacks. Patent-related conditions nevertheless remain subject to independently established §1 tying liability, with statutory exclusion receiving substantial justificatory weight. Courts must assess the actual products, conditioning, markets, commerce and injury rather than treating patent scope as a conclusive defense. The general nonimmunity rule commands all nine votes; the conclusion that this record requires further tying adjudication commands six.



### 2005-04-27 — [Gates v. Cook; Fifth Circuit No. 03-60529, 376 F.3d 323.](../records/gates-v-cook_merits_2005-04-27.md#public-projection)

**Opinion authority.**

SOUTER, J., delivered the unanimous opinion of the Court.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | All eight other Justices | Exhaustion, actual-notice constitutional judgment, retained protections and operative remedial limitation |

**Holdings.**

All nine Justices join each holding and its controlling explanation.

#### 1. The emergency process was actually completed

**Operative rule.** Section 1997e(a) requires completion of the administrative process actually available for the prisoner's grievance before suit. A substantive final-level decision on an emergency submission completes that route when the governing procedure prescribes no further review for that disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** Booth v. Churner requires exhaustion even when the process cannot grant the particular form of relief sought; Porter v. Nussle applies the requirement throughout prison life. Here the Commissioner addressed Russell's emergency grievance on its substance before suit. The policy's permission to resubmit a matter through ordinary channels if it is deemed nonemergent does not create a second mandatory sequence after the final substantive response actually given. Officials had the prescribed opportunity to correct the conditions. The statute enforces that opportunity and its real steps, rather than a later invented requirement.

#### 2. A properly exhausted common prospective claim can proceed through a certified class

**Operative rule.** A named prisoner who actually completes the available process for a common condition may represent a properly certified class seeking the same indivisible prospective correction without requiring each unnamed member to duplicate the grievance. The court must establish prefiling completion and identity among the grievance, class condition and requested correction; a different condition or individual claim retains its own exhaustion requirement.

**Controlling explanation.** Booth and Porter supply mandatory exhaustion; Rule 23 supplies representative procedure after that prerequisite is met. Russell completed the process for the systemic conditions for which the class seeks the same correction. Repetition by each absent member would add no distinct administrative claim to the already exhausted common request. This rule rests on an exhausted representative and an identical prospective claim, not administrative futility or substantial compliance. It therefore preserves the statutory requirement while allowing the representative remedy the civil rules provide.

#### 3. Officials must reasonably address known interacting dangers to an identifiable human need

**Operative rule.** Under the governing Eighth Amendment standard, prisoners must prove by the ordinary civil preponderance standard a substantial risk of serious harm and officials' actual awareness followed by unreasonable disregard. Conditions may be considered together when their interaction deprives an identifiable need such as sanitation, health or safety; reasonable protective responses remain a defense even when harm occurs.

**Controlling explanation.** Wilson v. Seiter permits analysis of mutually reinforcing conditions tied to an identified need. Helling v. McKinney permits prevention of serious future harm without awaiting disease. Farmer v. Brennan requires actual knowledge, which persistent obvious risks and repeated notice may support by inference but do not conclusively establish from official position alone. The credited evidence establishes awareness and inadequate responses to fecal contamination, sewage backflow, dangerous heat aggravated by illness and medication, insects and lost sleep, insufficient light, and serious psychiatric-care failures. Repair efforts that left the known serious dangers unreasonably unaddressed do not establish Farmer's reasonable-response defense. These particular findings sustain the constitutional judgment.

#### 4. Each prospective command must correct the proved violation within the statutory limits

**Operative rule.** Section 3626 permits only relief necessary to correct a proved federal violation, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive necessary means, with substantial weight given to public safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting departure from state or local law additionally requires that federal law demand that departure, that it be necessary, and that other relief cannot correct the violation.

**Controlling explanation.** Lewis v. Casey makes the proved injury determine remedial scope. Benjamin v. Fraser supplies the current requirements for supported care and lawful continuing implementation; its pretrial liability standard does not replace Farmer for convicted prisoners. Johnson v. California likewise preserves the separate statutory remedial conditions. The particular cleaning, heat, screening, sewage, lighting and care measures answer the credited Unit 32-C dangers. Their measurements are supported means of correction on this record, not universal numerical constitutional standards. The general unpreserved demand to restart the decree for want of ritual findings does not defeat that support. Paragraph 9's unrestricted code incorporation nevertheless exceeds the required connection because it can reach practices never found to violate federal law. Actual vacatur and an operative limitation cure that defect while retaining necessary care. Officials proposing a narrower means must establish that it fully corrects the violation. The district court must preserve the findings required for continuing implementation and give security evidence its statutory weight.

**Precedent Treatment.**

- Booth v. Churner: mandatory exhaustion of the actually available process is applied to the completed emergency route.
- Porter v. Nussle: prison-life coverage is preserved; the present decision resolves the distinct representative-exhaustion question.
- Wilson v. Seiter: interacting conditions may establish deprivation of an identifiable need; generalized harshness remains insufficient.
- Helling v. McKinney: a proved serious future health risk permits preventive relief.
- Farmer v. Brennan: actual knowledge and reasonable-response protection govern the credited conditions without dilution.
- Lewis v. Casey: the proved injury fixes remedial reach, retaining the Unit 32-C boundary.
- Benjamin v. Fraser: its statutory tailoring and implementation requirements control the care remedy; its pretrial liability rule remains specific to that setting.
- Johnson v. California: its separate application of §3626's remedial requirements is preserved.

**Law After Decision.**

A certified class may pursue the same indivisible prospective prison-condition claim through a named representative who completed the actual available administrative process. Liability for the interacting dangers remains governed by actual knowledge and unreasonable disregard. Each continuing injunction command must independently satisfy §3626 and address the proved classwide violation. Outside professional standards can inform necessary care; they cannot automatically enlarge the decree.


### 2005-05-31 — [Cutter v. Wilkinson, No. 03-9877.](../records/cutter-v-wilkinson_merits_2005-05-31.md#public-projection)

**Holdings.**

#### Neutral accommodation of private religious exercise in custody

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3's generally available protection against substantial government-imposed burdens on religious exercise in covered institutions is not facially invalid under the Establishment Clause. Government may relieve exceptional constraints its custody imposes on private exercise even when the Free Exercise Clause would not independently require that accommodation or comparable nonreligious interests receive less statutory protection.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this holding and the statutory-administration and remand holdings below.

**Controlling explanation:** Custody limits ordinary means of obtaining materials, associating and observing religious practices. Relieving that government-created dependence restores private exercise; it does not necessarily make the exercise an official religious program. Section 3 selects no denomination, requires no religious adherence and assigns no civil authority to a sect. Kiryas Joel permits accommodation beyond the constitutional minimum while forbidding denominational allocation of government power. Agostini directs attention to the actual governmental act and function, with purpose, effects and entanglement as evidence within its unified inquiry. This provision performs an accommodation function. Doe v. Porter concerns the different act of adopting devotional instruction as a public institution's own program.

The categorical objection that religion receives greater protection than other interests overlooks that distinction. The possibility that accommodation makes private religious practice more attractive does not itself establish coercion or sponsorship. At the same time, accommodation remains subject to neutrality, other persons' constitutional rights and the prohibition on governmental devotion. Those limits and the statute's justification defense require adjudication of concrete demands rather than dismissal of the entire protection.

#### The enacted standard accounts for institutional interests

**Holding and operative rule:** A claimant must establish statutory coverage and a substantial burden on sincere religious exercise. Government then must demonstrate, through production and persuasion, that imposing that burden on that person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. Institutional security, order, resources and effects on others receive concrete evidentiary weight within that test.

Section 3 covers a substantial burden imposed in a program or activity receiving federal financial assistance, or one that affects—or whose removal would affect—commerce with foreign nations, among the States or with Indian tribes. When the latter commerce claim is the only jurisdictional basis, section 4(g) makes the provision inapplicable if government demonstrates that all substantial burdens on, or removal of all substantial burdens from, similar religious exercise throughout the Nation would not lead in the aggregate to a substantial effect on that commerce. That conditional coverage limitation is distinct from section 3's compelling-interest defense. Section 4(b)'s separately worded burden provision concerns Free Exercise or section 2 claims; section 3's own language governs the defense here.

Religious exercise need not be compelled by or central to a belief system; sincerity remains a legitimate inquiry. Section 5 permits government to eliminate the burden by changing the policy or practice, retaining it with an exemption for the burdened exercise, exempting applications that substantially burden exercise, or another means that eliminates the burden. The Act neither creates nor precludes a right to religious funding or assistance, although avoiding a substantial burden may require expense in government's own operations. Its broad-construction direction operates within the text and Constitution, its severability provision preserves unaffected provisions and applications, and section 6 leaves the Establishment Clause's independent limits intact.

**Controlling explanation:** A dangerous item, a proposed gathering and harmless literature can present different institutional risks. Officials' experience and supported evidence of security, staffing, expense, coercion and burdens on other persons matter; the court must assess them in the actual setting. Neither a general warning about prison management nor sincerity alone settles the claim. Congress's compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means language cannot be replaced by rationality review, and the accommodation cannot compel unconstitutional religious activity or disregard others' constitutional rights. Courts assess substantial burden and justification without deciding theological truth. The statutory sequence addresses excessive demands while retaining a meaningful protection against unsupported restrictions.

#### The claims and distinct defenses return for ordinary adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** Rejection of the facial Establishment Clause ground requires reversal of that dismissal and adjudication of the statutory claims under the stated prerequisites, defense and constitutional limits. Relief must correspond to a proven violation and the claimant's continuing interest; Article III and the Prison Litigation Reform Act, including applicable exhaustion requirements, retain their force.

**Controlling explanation:** The sincerity stipulation is stage-specific and does not decide a particular practice's burden, risk or remedy. The existence of live claims likewise leaves former inmates' requests for prospective relief subject to their own continuing-interest requirements. The preserved Spending and Commerce objections and distinct federalism defenses fall outside the facial Establishment question decided here. Boerne invalidated RFRA's universal state and local command as an exercise of Fourteenth Amendment section 5 power; that holding neither resolves these different power predicates nor disappears because neutral accommodation is constitutionally permissible. The lower courts must address genuinely preserved defenses in their proper order.

**Precedent Treatment.**

- **Kiryas Joel:** Applied to distinguish permissible accommodation beyond the Free Exercise minimum from denominational allocation of civil authority or particular sectarian privilege.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied through its unified governmental-act/function inquiry. Purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry; the former independent Lemon prongs are not a separate fallback.
- **Doe v. Porter:** Distinguished because official adoption of devotional instruction differs from restoring private religious exercise constrained by custody.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Preserved at its section 5 scope. Its rejection of RFRA's universal state/local command does not determine the distinct Spending or Commerce objections reserved here.

**Law After Decision.**

Section 3 remains available to covered institutionalized persons seeking relief from substantial burdens on sincere religious exercise. Its religious focus is permissible accommodation, while each application remains governed by the enacted defense, constitutional limits, standing and applicable procedural requirements. The facial Establishment ruling supplies no resolution of the separately preserved congressional-power defenses.


### 2005-06-06 — [Gonzales v. Raich, No. 03-1454.](../records/gonzales-v-raich_merits_2005-06-06.md#public-projection)

**Holdings.**

#### Local production and possession are integral to this interstate supply regime

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may include unauthorized local marijuana cultivation, caregiver supply and possession in the Controlled Substances Act's independently valid interstate drug-control regime because that inclusion is reasonably adapted and integral to control of the same fungible substance's supply and distribution. The Government must identify the valid interstate regime, enacted covered class and genuine functional relationship; courts independently determine those boundaries while respecting reasonable congressional enforcement judgments within them.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this necessary-inclusion rule, its application and the following statutory-boundary and remedial holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires an eligible interstate connection before aggregation. Home Builders supplies the necessary-inclusion route, and Gibbs, Rancho Viejo and GDF apply it without demanding border crossing, commercial motive or a separate substantial interstate effect for each local member of a genuinely connected class. Those decisions supply the rule, not facts about drugs.

The CSA controls production, distribution and possession through an integrated supply-and-authorization system for the substance traded interstate. Cultivation creates that substance, caregiver delivery transfers it, and possession retains it. Local unauthorized supply and its substitution for market acquisition bear directly on the regime's objects and enforceability. Section 801's supply and practical-differentiation findings support that concrete mechanism; generalized health costs or the description of the Act as comprehensive cannot supply the constitutional connection by themselves. The accepted local cultivation and possession remain within the mechanism even without a sale or interstate movement by these patients. The rule preserves independent judicial scrutiny of an application outside the enacted class, an invalid interstate regime or a nominal or pretextual relationship. It requires no least-restrictive-means showing or proof that every conceivable exemption would cause measurable enforcement failure.

#### Medical purpose and state permission do not remove the functional connection

**Holding and operative rule:** The accepted local, unpaid, physician-recommended and state-permitted setting does not sever the relationship between these production and possession activities and the federal substance-control system. California permission and a physician's recommendation do not amend federal authorization; the CSA's enacted definitions, authorization provisions and state-law limits retain their actual operation.

Section 841(a) prohibits knowing or intentional manufacture, distribution, dispensing and the related possession-with-intent conduct except as authorized by the subchapter. Section 844(a) governs knowing or intentional possession, preserving substances obtained directly, or under a valid prescription or order, from a practitioner acting professionally, and other authorization under the applicable subchapters. Those are federal statutory conditions, rather than an exemption created by state permission alone.

Section 802 includes planting, cultivation, growing and harvesting in production. Distribution is delivery other than administering or dispensing; delivery includes actual, constructive or attempted transfer regardless of an agency relationship and does not require payment. The manufacture definition excludes preparation, compounding, packaging or labeling in conformity with state or local law by a practitioner as an incident to professional administration or dispensing; that limited exception is not a general cultivation exemption. Marijuana remains governed by Schedule I and the federal authorization, research, registration and scheduling routes in force. Section 903 leaves otherwise valid state law in place unless a positive conflict prevents the federal provision and state law from consistently standing together. Direct federal regulation of private conduct does not require California to criminalize the same conduct or administer the federal program.

**Controlling explanation:** The patients' strongest boundary would define a separately administered local medical class and require proof that excluding it would defeat interstate enforcement. Their medical needs, lack of sales and state supervision are accepted at their proper record status. But medical purpose explains why production or possession occurs; it does not change the substance produced or remove the activity from the authorization and supply controls. A State's ability to administer an exception may inform the functional inquiry without obliging Congress to adopt that State's alternative system. The Court need not find patient-specific diversion or failure of California safeguards to identify this direct relationship. Household substitution alone would be too broad; the holding depends on the actual class and same-substance supply mechanism within an independently valid regime. The statutory operation and accepted activity facts settle that relationship here, so a further Commerce evidentiary proceeding is unnecessary.

#### Independent patient claims retain their own adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The preliminary-injunction direction cannot stand on the rejected Commerce theory. The case returns for genuinely preserved independent patient claims and any request for interim relief under the substantive law, evidence and equitable requirements governing those claims.

**Controlling explanation:** Oakland Cannabis rejected the distributor's statutory medical-necessity exemption and an equitable recreation of it, while leaving individual possession, patient necessity and independent constitutional claims outside that disposition. The present grant resolves federal power, rather than the distinct due-process, Ninth Amendment, patient-necessity or commandeering theories. Those matters remain because they fall outside the question decided and require their own legal and factual adjudication. The physicians' evidence keeps its proper significance for medical risk and any applicable claim or interim-relief requirement; the Commerce decision does not decide those claims' merits or guarantee relief. The lower courts must address the actual preserved requests promptly under their proper standards.

**Precedent Treatment.**

- **United States v. Lopez:** Applied to require an identifiable connection fitted to the enacted class before aggregation; generalized national health, crime or productivity consequences remain insufficient substitutes.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied at its reasonably-adapted and integral necessary-inclusion scope, with independent judicial identification of the valid regime, actual class and genuine relationship.
- **Gibbs v. Babbitt:** Applied for class-based inclusion without individual border crossing, commercial motive or a separate substantial interstate effect.
- **Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton:** Applied for the genuine regime/class/relationship inquiry and its outside-class, invalid-regime and nominal-or-pretextual limits.
- **GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton:** Applied for the same necessary-inclusion rule and rejection of a routine member-specific effects hearing when the actual statutory relationship is established. Its wildlife facts do not establish this drug relationship.
- **United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers' Cooperative:** Preserved at its distributor-specific statutory and equitable scope, including its individual-patient and independent constitutional reservations.
