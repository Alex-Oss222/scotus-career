#### Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-6073.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### These statutory forfeitures are covered fines

**Holding:** Forfeiture to the United States under 21 U.S.C.§§ 881(a)(4) and(a)(7) constitutes payment in kind to the sovereign at least partly as punishment and is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Its civil designation, proceeding against property, and concurrent remedial aims do not remove coverage; coverage does not establish excessiveness or import all criminal-procedure safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S. 257: government prosecution/receipt distinguishes this fine from private punitive award.
- United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435: partly punitive function recognized by analogy; separate Double Jeopardy analysis not imported.
- One 1958 Plymouth Sedan v. Pennsylvania, 380 U.S. 693: lawful property is not contraband merely because used in crime.
- One Lot Emerald Cut Stones v. United States, 409 U.S. 232, and United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S. 354: remedial customs/contraband characterizations distinguished, not abolished.
- Buena Vista, February 24, 1993: statutory owner defenses remain provision-specific; no § 881(a)(7) or constitutional innocent owner holding imported.

##### Historical in rem form does not negate punishment

**Holding:** The historical treatment of statutory in rem forfeiture includes punishment and does not establish categorical exemption from the Excessive Fines Clause for these forfeitures. The traditionally limited common-law innocent-owner defense does not prove that forfeiture was solely remedial; the constitutionality of forfeiting a truly innocent owner’s property remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current-force modification:** Bennis v. Michigan, No. 94-8729, decided March 4, 1996, answers the reserved due-process question for a co-owner’s separately held interest in an ordinarily lawful automobile when she establishes nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention of another’s prohibited use. Ordinary co-ownership and permission for shared lawful use do not conclusively defeat that protection; knowingly tolerated misuse, culpable entrustment and omitted reasonably expected precautions remain outside it. Austin’s punitive-forfeiture holding and Excessive Fines remand remain unchanged. Bennis supplies no comprehensive innocent-owner or instrumentality rule and no state-incorporation holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Peisch v. Ware, 4 Cranch 347: punitive understanding and stranger-removal qualification applied.
- The Palmyra, 12 Wheat 1: no prior personal conviction required, not an absence of punishment holding.
- J.W. Goldsmith, Jr.-Grant Co. v. United States, 254 U.S. 505, and Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht, 416 U.S. 663: punitive/deterrent features recognized; truly innocent owner question reserved.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Bennis v. Michigan, No. 94-8729, March 4, 1996 — qualified due-process owner protection and conditional property relief.

##### Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection

**Holding:** The lower court must evaluate whether Austin’s actual forfeiture is excessive; statutory facilitation and coverage as punishment do not themselves decide that question. The Court adopts no comprehensive excessiveness test and does not limit the remand to instrumentality alone: the property’s connection to the offense may matter, and other relevant considerations may also be examined.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage does not decide excessiveness, incorporate all criminal safeguards or establish state incorporation. Truly innocent ownership, taxation and solely compensatory recovery remain distinct; neither a comprehensive formula nor instrumentality alone is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for an excessiveness inquiry on a developed record. No automatic property return, value finding, ratio or fixed cap; the lower court’s concern was not a completed excessiveness determination.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris, 492 U.S. 257: fine coverage principle applied without inventing a comprehensive proportionality formula.
- Calero-Toledo, 416 U.S. 663: statutory/historical connection not converted into a final excessiveness answer.

#### United States v. Ursery; United States v. $405,089.23 in United States Currency, 518 U.S. 267 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 95-345 and 95-346.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether the reviewed civil forfeitures impose a second criminal punishment

**Holding:** For forfeiture under sections 881(a)(6), 881(a)(7) and 981(a)(1)(A), the court first determines whether Congress intended civil proceedings; that intent can be overcome only by the clearest proof that the scheme is so punitive in purpose or effect as to be criminal. These schemes and the grounds established here do not meet that demanding contrary-proof standard, so the challenged forfeitures supply no double-jeopardy bar to the respective later proceeding; neither a civil label nor an action against property creates an irrebuttable exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. Scalia and Thomas join only the judgments; additional judgment votes do not enlarge the rationale.

**Material application:** Qualified incorporation of customs procedures and the civil statutory mechanisms establish civil intent. Removing facilitating property, discouraging unlawful use and recovering illicit proceeds supply nonpunitive functions beyond reimbursing enforcement costs. Loss, deterrence, a crime connection and an owner defense alone do not satisfy clearest proof.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither each asset’s nexus, provenance or ownership nor excessiveness, attachment of personal jeopardy, offense identity under Blockburger and retained Grady, coordination of proceedings or settlement waiver. Distinct statutory defenses remain: §§881(a)(6)/(7) protect an owner’s interest upon proof that the relevant act or omission occurred without knowledge or consent; §981(a)(2) protects an owner or lienholder upon proof of lack of knowledge. Section 981(a)(1)(A)’s §5313(a) exception bars seizure/forfeiture for that reporting violation by a federally supervised domestic financial institution, an SEC-regulated institution, or their partner, director or employee. Customs procedures apply only insofar as applicable and consistent with the forfeiture statute.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ursery reversed and remanded, 7–2 (Stone-Zsela and Stevens would affirm); Currency reversed and remanded, 8–1 (Stone-Zsela favors vacatur and a narrower remand). All nine reject Currency’s blanket bar, but no unanimous rationale or mandate follows. Properly remaining criminal, probable-cause, excessive-fines and property-authority objections remain; no automatic transfer or new sentence.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kansas v. Hendricks; Hendricks v. Kansas, June 23, 1997, Nos. 95-1649 and 95-9075: extends the civil-intent/clearest-proof method to Ex Post Facto classification of personal confinement; no forfeiture outcome or Double Jeopardy holding is transferred.

##### Whether Excessive Fines coverage itself establishes Double Jeopardy punishment

**Holding:** A forfeiture's coverage by the Excessive Fines Clause does not, without the separate civil-intent and clearest-proof inquiry, establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy purposes. Austin's protection and any properly presented excessiveness objection remain effective; today's classification decides neither excessiveness nor an exclusive test for measuring it.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** Austin’s partly punitive coverage protects against excessive exactions; it does not replace the separate criminal-classification inquiry. Halper’s fixed personal civil-penalty accounting and Kurth Ranch’s combined-operation tax holding retain their distinct fields.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No instrumentality-only fines rule or finding that these forfeitures are nonexcessive. James Daniel Good’s prior-process protection and demonstrated-exigency exception, Bennis’s innocent-owner protection, and Libretti’s statutory and third-party limits remain independently operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Ward, 448 U.S. 242:** Applies legislative-intent and demanding contrary-proof analysis; civil designation is relevant but not conclusive.
- **United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S. 354:** Applies the civil-forfeiture classification test, including the clearest-proof exception, rather than categorical immunity for proceedings against property.
- **Kennedy v. Mendoza-Martinez, 372 U.S. 144:** Preserves consideration of punitive purpose and effect; no individual indicator is conclusive and no new mandatory exhaustive checklist is announced.
- **United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435:** Preserves its exceptional fixed-personal-civil-penalty accounting rule, including reasonable remedial approximation, while distinguishing forfeiture's broader nonpunitive functions. It is not overruled or converted into universal asset-value accounting.
- **Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch, June 6, 1994:** Preserves the combined-operation punishment holding for the extraordinary arrest-linked assessment on confiscated contraband; no universal compensation ceiling follows.
- **Austin v. United States, June 28, 1993:** Preserves Excessive Fines coverage and separate excessiveness review. Its partly punitive coverage rule does not automatically answer the Double Jeopardy question.
- **Witte v. United States, June 14, 1995:** Preserves the distinction between consideration of conduct and an independent punishment, and its separate coordination limits; coordination cannot cure an otherwise established successive-prosecution bar.
- **Blockburger v. United States, 284 U.S. 299; Grady v. Corbin, 495 U.S. 508; United States v. Felix, March 25, 1992; and United States v. Dixon, June 28, 1993:** Preserve necessary-offense and essential-conduct rules and the distinct-agreement limitation. The Court does not reach their application here and does not replace retained Grady with an elements-only rule.
- **United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, December 13, 1993:** Preserves notice and meaningful opportunity before civil seizure of real property; exigency requires showing that less restrictive measures would not prevent sale, destruction or continued unlawful use, with appropriate subsequent process.
- **Bennis v. Michigan, March 4, 1996:** Preserves its stated innocent-owner protection; no qualifying innocent-owner finding is established here.
- **Libretti v. United States, November 7, 1995:** Preserves statutory authority and third-party limits despite agreement; it supplies no settlement waiver in these cases.

No precedent is overruled. The separate writings' broader or contrary theories are not the Court's treatment of precedent.

#### United States v. Bajakajian, 524 U.S. 321

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1487.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### A punitive criminal forfeiture must not be grossly disproportionate to its offense

**Holding:** Section 982(a)(1)’s forfeiture imposed on this convicted offender as punishment is a fine subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. A punitive forfeiture violates that Clause when grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the offense for which it is imposed; statutory property connection alone does not answer excessiveness, and instrumentality is not a categorical prerequisite barring every currency-reporting forfeiture.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial respect for legislative punishment judgments and the imprecision of gravity assessments make gross disproportionality, rather than strict equivalence, the governing boundary. Solem supplies that respect and proportionality analogy without deciding a new imprisonment question. The nature of the offense, culpability, harm, property’s relation to wrongdoing and authorized sanctions inform the comparison where relevant. They are not a compulsory exhaustive checklist, fixed ratio or government-cost formula. Congress may punish a reporting violation through property forfeiture, but its statutory authorization does not foreclose the constitutional inquiry. The Court rejects the circuit’s conclusion that such currency can never be forfeited for want of an instrumentality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Austin v. United States:** Partly punitive coverage and an excessiveness inquiry beyond statutory connection are applied; its holdings are not converted into an instrumentality-only test or a universal historical forfeiture theory.
- **Alexander v. United States:** Its independent offense-related Excessive Fines inquiry for criminal forfeiture is applied and given the gross-disproportionality formulation here.
- **Browning-Ferris Industries v. Kelco Disposal:** Its distinction between governmental punishment and private awards supports fine coverage.
- **Solem v. Helm:** Its legislative-deference and proportionality principles inform this fines rule without a new imprisonment holding.
- **Department of Revenue v. Kurth Ranch, United States v. Ursery and Hudson v. United States:** Their distinct Double Jeopardy classification rules remain intact and supply no automatic Excessive Fines result or universal cost ceiling.
- **One Lot Emerald Cut Stones and One Assortment of 89 Firearms:** Their distinct customs and forfeiture classifications establish no exemption or proportionality answer for this direct criminal reporting fine.

##### The legal proportionality judgment receives independent appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently review the constitutional determination whether this punitive forfeiture is grossly disproportionate, while reviewing supported underlying factual findings for clear error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The facts found and the constitutional evaluation perform different work. Deference to the trial court’s supported findings does not transfer the legal boundary of the Excessive Fines Clause to unrestricted sentencing discretion. Independent review supplies no authority to invent an additional offense or substitute adverse facts for the findings. The Court announces no universal review standard for every mixed question.

##### Full forfeiture is grossly disproportionate on these findings

**Holding:** Forfeiture of the full $357,144 is grossly disproportionate to the willful reporting offense established here, considering the lawful funds and purpose, absence of another-crime connection, limited reporting harm and particular sentencing assessment together. The refusal of full forfeiture is affirmed without reviewing the existing $15,000 amount or setting a constitutional dollar ceiling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Material application:** The reporting duty is serious, and willful failure remains culpable even when transporting the funds would otherwise be lawful. But the proved offense withheld information; the findings establish neither criminal proceeds nor laundering, trafficking, fraud or a separate revenue loss against which the whole fund may be measured. Dismissal of the false-statement count does not establish that no false statement occurred.

The applicable six-month and $5,000 Guidelines maxima reflect the particular offense’s assessed severity. Congress’s broader five-year and $250,000 maxima and the forfeiture authorization deserve weight but do not conclusively make this full exaction proportionate. Treating them as conclusive would deprive independent excessiveness review of work. The lawful source, lawful debt and lack of another-crime connection are relevant together; lawful property is not automatically immune from a fine.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting system’s preventive function and the large unreported transaction supply the strongest contrary argument. They do not overcome the extraordinary mismatch on these supported findings. The Court substitutes no new amount. Because Bajakajian did not cross-appeal, neither $15,000’s validity nor authority to select it is decided; no rule identifies $15,001 as excessive. Wealth, income, livelihood effects, other materially different crime-connected forfeitures and a comprehensive innocent-owner or in-rem rule remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment refusing the Government’s demand for full forfeiture remains effective. No new calculation or further forfeiture hearing is directed. The unappealed partial forfeiture and other sentence components remain undisturbed without affirmative adjudication of their independent validity.

#### Graham v. Collins, 506 U.S. 461 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7580.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality

**Holding:** Teague does not bar Graham's claim that Texas's submitted sentencing questions prevented effective use of his youth and unstable background as material mitigation independent of deliberateness or future danger. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated that functional requirement before his conviction became final on September 10, 1984; no new procedural-rule exception is created.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I–III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this prefinality-law holding, its application to youth and background, and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 — applied; the relevant result must be dictated at finality, not merely supported by a later plausible extension.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — preserved as qualified approval; it does not immunize every application of the special issues.
- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied as the pre-1984 effective-mitigation rule.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied as recognition of that existing rule, not as law inserted retroactively into 1984.
- Stringer v. Black, March 9, 1992 — applied at its existing Teague scope; disagreement alone does not establish novelty.
- Franklin v. Lynaugh, 487 U.S. 164, and Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 — distinguished; evidence whose mitigating use fits the questions, and unrestricted sympathy, differ from material culpability mitigation excluded by them.

##### Youth requires an effective route for its independent mitigating force

**Holding:** When the record supports a material mitigating judgment about culpability that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the submitted mechanism prevents that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Graham's youth at seventeen has that independent significance notwithstanding deliberate conduct or predicted future danger, and the three submitted special issues provide no adequate route for it here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground for sentencing relief and its conditional remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied to youth's independent culpability significance; jurors may assign weight but cannot be legally prevented from giving it effect.
- Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 — its conditional framework remains; this defective application does not establish facial invalidity.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied beyond a permanent-handicap limitation; a mitigating trait need not have only aggravating relevance to future danger to require an effective vehicle.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 — applied as the reasonable-likelihood method of assessing instructional operation, not as the source of a new substantive rule at 1984 finality.

##### The submitted background evidence also requires effective consideration

**Independent alternative holding:** A capital sentencing system may not prevent a jury from acting on the material culpability significance it finds in a defendant's disturbed upbringing merely because that evidence also bears on future dangerousness or lacks a uniquely severe permanent handicap. The evidence of Graham's mother's recurring mental illness and hospitalizations and his shifting family care supports such a mitigating judgment, which the submitted special issues cannot adequately express; this independently requires the same sentencing remedy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Parts III and V of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor; five Justices adopt this independent background-evidence ground and the conditional sentencing remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 — applied to relevant background proffered as a basis for less than death.
- Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 — applied; disturbed upbringing cannot be excluded from mitigating effect as a matter of law.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 — applied; neither a uniquely severe permanent handicap nor complete absence of relevance to the special issues is an exclusive trigger.

##### Positive character as an independent ground

**Authority of the judgment:** The separate contention that Graham's household assistance, religious observance and financial support of his children independently require additional sentencing relief is rejected by all nine Justices, on different grounds. Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter regard its asserted independent culpability significance as insubstantial on this record and its future-behavior use as available under the second issue; White, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would bar the requested mitigation relief under Teague. Stone-Zsela joins the denial of this independent component in the judgment only. These grounds do not yield a common general rule or a Marks subset; the youth and background holdings independently require sentencing relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule about positive character evidence or its value apart from the asserted culpability and future-danger uses is decided. The jury remains free to hear constitutionally relevant evidence at the new sentencing proceeding. Neither denial of this independent ground nor the sentencing remedy disturbs the murder conviction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere admission or some future-danger relevance is insufficient when supported culpability mitigation cannot receive effect. The ruling requires neither favorable weight nor a categorical fourth question, finds no psychiatric disability, and imposes no juvenile-execution prohibition or facial invalidity of the scheme. Texas’s later statute is not adjudicated.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse sentencing relief denial on youth and background and remand for a conditional writ allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital replacement. Preserve the conviction; no automatic acquittal or immediate release.

#### Johnson v. Texas, 509 U.S. 350 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5653.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route

**Holding:** Considering the charge, evidence and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that the special issues prevented Johnson’s jury from giving effect to his youth-related reduced culpability when it truthfully found deliberateness and future danger. Under Graham, Eddings, Penry and Boyde, admission of the evidence and partial relevance to future danger do not supply the missing route; mere possibility of a restrictive reading would be insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993 — independent youth-culpability holding applied as binding merits law.
- Eddings, 455 U.S. 104 — immaturity’s independent culpability significance applied.
- Lockett, 438 U.S. 586 — material mitigation cannot be legally excluded from effect.
- Penry, 492 U.S. 302 — admission distinguished from meaningful sentencing effect.
- Boyde, 494 U.S. 370 — reasonable likelihood, not mere possibility, applied.
- Jurek, 428 U.S. 262 — conditional facial approval preserved; this application fails.

##### Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction

**Holding:** Because this is direct review of the preserved mitigation claim, Teague does not bar relief. The death sentence must be reversed for an effective sentencing vehicle permitting the material youth mitigation to affect punishment without a false answer to a factual special issue; Texas may provide adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, while the guilt judgment remains intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Direct review permits the necessary sentence correction portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood standard, not mere possibility, governs. No categorical age bar, facial invalidation of Texas sentencing, right to favorable weight or unrestricted mercy instruction is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse only the death sentence and remand for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. Guilt remains intact; no exact instruction wording is prescribed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — collateral-review restriction inapplicable to this direct appeal.
- Graham, January 25, 1993 — merits and remedy principles followed; collateral-review limitations do not bar this direct appeal.
- Jurek, 428 U.S. 262 — authority to structure sentencing retained subject to effective mitigation.

#### Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6677.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Whether section 2254(d)(1) permits relief on the use of the earlier defense-requested psychiatric report

**Holding:** The state court reasonably distinguished a defense-requested competency report used to cross-examine a defense mental-condition expert from Estelle v. Smith's compelled, uncounseled capital examination. Its adjudication is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of the federal law established at the February 22, 1995 state-merits cutoff, so section 2254(d)(1) forecloses this psychiatric claim without an actual-effect determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The report's dangerousness conclusion was elicited over objection and repeated in closing; no absence of objection or universal subject-matter waiver is found. The Court decides no general direct-review admissibility or privilege rule. June 7, 1995 rehearing denial does not establish a later substantive-law cutoff.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle v. Smith remains intact in its compelled-examination setting. Buchanan v. Kentucky supplies the defense-initiated evaluation and responsive-use distinction without universal waiver. Williams v. Taylor supplies distinct statutory clauses and objective reasonableness without importing an exclusive allocation proposal lacking a majority.

##### Whether the special issues and mitigation supplement provided a lawful way to choose life

**Holding:** Supported mitigation reducing culpability independently of the factual special issues requires an authorized, coherent means of giving that judgment effect in a life verdict. A supplement allowing a negative answer for mitigation is insufficient when the unchanged form makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt despite the juror's belief that the fact is proved; in the complete charge and trial context here, the reasonable likelihood of restriction makes the state acceptance objectively unreasonable under established law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. No independent contrary-to holding is adopted.

**Material application:** Jurors could find deliberateness, future dangerousness and lack of reasonable provocation while treating intellectual impairment and childhood abuse as mitigating. The form required at least ten jurors' factual reasonable doubt for a negative answer and unanimity beyond reasonable doubt for an affirmative answer. The judge's similar selection-stage explanations and renewed defense closing were considered but did not reconcile that final certification conflict; neither forgotten instructions nor a separate prosecutorial prohibition is invented.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No favored mitigation weight, prescribed fourth question, categorical disability exemption or separate unanimity holding is required. The error is neither classified as structural nor equated with demonstrated effect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma protect meaningful culpability mitigation. Penry v. Lynaugh, Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas supply the pre-1995 lawful effective-vehicle requirement. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood in the complete charge and trial. Buchanan v. Angelone, Weeks v. Angelone and Shafer v. South Carolina retain their later, distinct roles and supply no backdated 1995 substantive benchmark.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Atkins v. Virginia, June 20, 2002, establishes a separate categorical bar to execution for established intellectual disability and requires a reliable status procedure. Penry's effective-mitigation protection remains intact; it did not already establish that categorical bar.

##### Which harmless-error burden governs this preserved original-charge mitigation restriction

**Holding:** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind on supported capital culpability mitigation, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must allow a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence; the guilt judgment is unaffected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins. Stevens and Kennedy support the corrective return but reject this standard; Scalia and Thomas oppose the mitigation disposition. Their distinct effect approaches are not aggregated under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error does not automatically establish contribution, and answers to the factual special issues do not establish what an unconflicted mitigation decision would have been. The entire penalty record, including clarifications, must be considered; no present harmlessness or harmfulness finding is made. Brecht's general rule governs this federal habeas effect inquiry; it does not supply clearly established substantive law at the 1995 cutoff, create a new retroactivity exception or displace its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mitigation certificate disposition is reversed for an issue-specific appeal and remaining full-record effect inquiry, with constitutional error and section 2254(d)(1) eligibility settled. No present writ, sentence vacatur, fixed resentencing deadline, stay, release or execution order is entered; conditional relief follows only if the State fails its burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman v. California is applied through Brecht's general rule to this preserved original-charge capital-mitigation error, allocating the beyond-reasonable-doubt showing to the State. Calderon v. Coleman applied that rule to commutation misinformation and is followed here. Weeks's return of its response error for effect review under Brecht's general rule is undisturbed. Brecht v. Abrahamson supplies the general rule, subject to its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element; O'Neal v. McAninch and California v. Roy applied it to the errors they presented and remain intact.

For reviewability of the certificate denial and the distinction between certification and a present writ, see Federal Courts - Capital habeas certificates and issue-specific appellate return, Penry v. Johnson, No. 00-6677, decided June 4, 2001.

#### United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate, 278 F.3d 1091 (judgment below)

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 00-7059; no Supreme Court docket is supplied.
**Decided:** March 4, 2003.

For displacement of the homestead defense in this case, March 4, 2003, Tenth Circuit No. 00-7059, see Constitutional Structure — Conflict between federal real-property forfeiture and state homestead immunity.

##### What findings and appellate review govern the punitive §881(a)(7) forfeiture

**Holding:** A forfeiture under §881(a)(7) violates the Excessive Fines Clause when grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the wrongdoing for which it is imposed. Lees bears the burden of establishing constitutional excessiveness; the court must make supported findings on the material relationship between the property interest and value, actual offense, owner involvement, culpability, harm, benefit and pertinent penalties, then reach a fresh constitutional conclusion, reviewed independently on appeal with supported subsidiary facts reviewed for clear error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's unanimous opinion of the Court; all nine Justices adopt both holdings and the findings remand.

**Material application:** A facilitation nexus and rejection of the statutory ownership defense do not establish the constitutional amount. The $136,000 appraisal remains evidence, and the grandson's conduct or Lees's earlier separate Valium conviction cannot automatically become her culpability or the gravity of this forfeiture offense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The relevant considerations are not an exhaustive checklist; comparing only the maximum fine is insufficient. The Court decides neither that forfeiture of the whole interest is excessive nor that it is proportionate. Additional proceedings are required only as necessary and procedurally permissible.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the findings remand with statutory liability intact. The District Court must preserve effective relief and resolve gross disproportionality before final enforcement. An excessive forfeiture requires an authorized limitation or denial with its effect on the proved interest, title or proceeds identified; an otherwise permissible forfeiture requires an explanation tying the supported wrongdoing to the amount.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Austin separates Excessive Fines coverage from amount. Bajakajian supplies gross disproportionality, respect for legislative punishment judgments and separate legal/factual review standards. Mackby supports a developed proportionality comparison while liability remains intact. Bennis's protection for a separately held automobile interest retains its proved ownership, nonparticipation, knowledge and reasonable-prevention limits and does not reopen the rejected statutory defense.

#### Tennard v. Dretke, 542 U.S. 274

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-10038.
**Decided:** 2004-06-24

For Tennard v. Dretke, 542 U.S. 274, 2004-06-24, No. 02-10038, see Federal Courts: Habeas and Collateral Review for [Whether the preserved mitigation claim warrants a certificate of appealability](../HOLDINGS.md#whether-the-preserved-mitigation-claim-warrants-a-certificate-of-appealability).

##### Whether culpability mitigation requires a severe permanent impairment or causal connection to the offense

**Holding:** Supported intellectual-functioning and other culpability mitigation cannot be excluded from constitutional consideration merely because the defendant has not proved a uniquely severe permanent impairment or a causal connection between the impairment and the offense. Evidence may bear on culpability independently of deliberateness or future danger; relevance requires neither a completed categorical disability diagnosis nor favorable weight from the sentencer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The IQ score of 67 supports a culpability argument even if it does not establish adaptive limitations, developmental onset or a categorical intellectual-disability diagnosis. Evidence may mitigate culpability while also bearing adversely on future danger.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham v. Collins supplies the rejection of the uniquely severe permanent-handicap screen and protection for independent culpability mitigation. Johnson v. Texas preserves effective consideration beyond the factual special issues. Penry v. Johnson supplies the coherent-verdict requirement and separates the constitutional/statutory inquiry from effect review. Atkins v. Virginia concerns categorical intellectual disability and is distinguished from ordinary mitigation relevance.

#### Burson v. Freeman, 504 U.S. 191 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1056.
**Decided:** May 26, 1992.

##### What scrutiny governs the campaign-specific restriction on ordinary streets and sidewalks within the polling-place boundary

**Holding:** Tennessee's campaign-specific prohibition is a content-based restriction on political speech, and ordinary public sidewalks and streets within the 100-foot boundary remain public forums. The State must show that this restriction is necessary to serve a compelling interest and narrowly drawn to achieve that end; viewpoint neutrality and the availability of speech beyond the boundary do not by themselves lower that scrutiny.

**Proposition-level authority:** Seven participating Justices expressly adopt this governing proposition at the same level of generality: Blackmun's plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, and Stevens's dissent, joined by O'Connor and Souter. It is an independently majority-supported proposition, not a seven-Justice judgment coalition or an Opinion of the Court. Scalia rejects the forum classification and applies a different standard.

**Material application:** Viewpoint neutrality and opportunities to speak beyond the boundary do not make campaign-specific content discrimination neutral. The Justices applying strict scrutiny disagree about necessity and tailoring in this facial challenge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The majority-supported scrutiny rule supplies no shared validity rationale or numerical safe harbor. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated political speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment invalidating the 100-foot restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3, by Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy. The governing scrutiny proposition has a different, seven-Justice coalition; Thomas takes no part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Ass'n v. Perry Local Educators' Ass'n, 460 U.S. 37 (1983) — applies its traditional-public-forum and content-based scrutiny framework to the ordinary streets and sidewalks reached here.
- United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171 (1983) — applies its protection of ordinary public sidewalks; government-building proximity does not alone remove that status.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988) — applies strict scrutiny to political subject discrimination; a claim of regulatory purpose does not automatically make this campaign restriction content neutral.
- Mills v. Alabama, 384 U.S. 214 (1966) — preserves its protection of general election-day advocacy and its reservation concerning conduct in and around polls; it does not automatically settle this boundary's validity.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991, simulated decision — preserves exacting review of content-based burdens; this case's divided application creates no general exception to that rule.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989) — distinguishes its content-neutral time, place and manner standard; it does not replace strict scrutiny here.

##### Whether this 100-foot restriction survives the facial challenge

**Authority of the judgment:** The judgment invalidating the restriction is reversed and remanded, 5–3: Stone, White, Blackmun, Scalia and Kennedy support reversal; Stevens, O’Connor and Souter oppose it. Thomas takes no part. No controlling validity rationale exists under *Marks*: Blackmun’s plurality, joined by Stone, White and Kennedy, treats the place as a public forum and finds strict scrutiny satisfied; Scalia treats the polling environs during voting as a nonpublic forum subject to reasonableness and viewpoint neutrality. The different forum predicates are not established logical subsets, and Scalia does not adopt the plurality’s necessity or tailoring conclusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds this facial challenge to the 100-foot boundary during voting. Neither the four-Justice application nor Scalia’s nonpublic-forum theory becomes a controlling exception. The separately majority-supported scrutiny rule appears above. The separate 300-foot provisions, larger zones, periods outside voting, unrelated speech, discriminatory enforcement, incidental bumper stickers, unusual highway applications and unreviewed state constitutional grounds remain undecided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

### Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation?

**Current rule:** When record-supported mitigation has material culpability significance that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Admission and partial relevance to future danger are insufficient if a truthful finding of deliberateness and danger prevents giving effect to youth's independent mitigating force. Disturbed upbringing need not involve a uniquely severe permanent handicap or lack any relation to future danger. The sentencer need not give favorable weight, and mere possibility of a restrictive reading is insufficient. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated this functional requirement before Graham's September 10, 1984 finality; no new Teague exception follows. Adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence can provide correction without disturbing guilt. No general positive-character rule follows from Graham's separate fractured component.

When a capital jury asks whether proof of an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response considered with the complete charge and trial must resolve a reasonable likelihood that supported mitigation cannot be given effect in choosing life. Referring to an existing instruction may suffice if it unambiguously answers the actual difficulty; no particular words or universal rephrasing duty are required. Evidence, advocacy, further deliberation or a recital that mitigation was considered does not conclusively cure an otherwise unresolved restriction on mitigating use.

A general permission to answer a special issue negatively for mitigation is insufficient where the required verdict form still makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt the juror may not possess. The complete instructions and trial context must provide a coherent, authorized life verdict for supported culpability mitigation outside the factual issues. Earlier explanations and counsel’s advocacy are considered but do not conclusively repair an incompatible final factual certification.

For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind, the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must permit a reasonable time for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, leaving guilt intact. The defect is not structural and does not itself establish actual contribution. This effect inquiry applies Brecht's general rule for preserved constitutional trial error on federal habeas; it supplies no new rule at an earlier state-merits cutoff.

**Authority by component:**

- The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality: Graham v. Collins — The youth and background claims apply law settled before finality, 1993-01-25. ## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

### Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review?

**Current rule:** Forfeitures to the sovereign under §881(a)(4) and (a)(7), and punitive criminal RICO forfeiture, are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Civil labeling, in rem form and concurrent remedial aims do not remove the covered statutory forfeitures' partly punitive character. Coverage and lawful conviction or statutory connection do not decide excessiveness. The court must separately assess the exaction in relation to its offenses; the property-offense connection may matter but review is not limited to instrumentality alone. No fixed ratio, mandatory exhaustive factor list, incorporation holding or automatic return follows. The gross-disproportionality inquiry assesses the offense’s gravity without converting the case’s lawful-funds, limited-harm and sentencing findings into a mandatory checklist or its dollar amounts into constitutional ceilings. The distinct due-process defense for a separately held automobile interest is stated in ‘Reasonable prevention and a separately held automobile interest’; other constitutional innocent-owner questions remain unresolved. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy, which uses the independent civil-intent/clearest-proof inquiry. Alexander’s independent expressive-burden inquiry remains distinct. Punitive criminal forfeiture under §982(a)(1) is a fine and violates the Clause when grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the offense. Statutory connection alone does not answer that question, and instrumentality is no categorical prerequisite excluding all currency-reporting forfeitures. The appellate constitutional proportionality determination is reviewed independently; underlying supported factual findings receive clear-error review.

The combined False Claims Act demand for fixed civil penalties and treble damages is also a covered fine because it is partly punitive. Assess that aggregate demand for gross disproportionality to the actual offense, respecting legislative punishment judgments while considering supported culpability, repeated conduct, harm, compensatory functions and severity. Concurrent compensation does not remove coverage; coverage supplies neither an automatic reduction nor a fixed ratio. This extension does not classify an isolated treble award or materially different recovery.

For §881(a)(7) real-property forfeiture, the claimant bears the burden of proving gross disproportionality. Supported findings must permit comparison of the actual property interest and value with the offense, the owner’s involvement and culpability, harm, benefit and pertinent penalties. These material relationships support the constitutional judgment; they do not create an exhaustive mechanical checklist.

## Standards and Tests selection: Candidate contribution limits and anticorruption fit

### Candidate contribution limits and anticorruption fit

**Governing question:** What justifies direct candidate-contribution limits and differentiated source allowances.

**Current rule:** Candidate contribution limits must be closely drawn to a sufficiently important interest in preventing corruption or its appearance while preserving meaningful political association. No completed bribe is necessary, and equality of political influence is not an independent justification. Amount and inflation comparisons establish no permanent constitutional boundary; actual campaign scale, fundraising burden and cumulative restrictions matter. Under the adjudicated Arkansas classification, a registered committee receiving contributions exclusively from individuals and no more than $25 cumulatively from any individual during a calendar year may receive a $2,500 per-candidate, per-election contribution allowance while ordinary sources face lower ceilings. The actual dispersed-donor structure and its anticorruption fit, rather than a small-donor label, support the distinction.

The same contribution standard permits the presented viewpoint-neutral prohibition of direct business-corporation treasury gifts to federal candidates when the government justifies the direct-recipient and commercial-source safeguard while preserving voluntary human political channels. Neither corporate form, concentrated wealth, ordinary access nor nominal availability of a separate fund alone supplies close fit. Materially different party-financing channels do not require permission for direct treasury transfers where the direct-control risk remains justified; underinclusion must still receive a real fit inquiry. Independent expenditures, materially different organizations and supported operational burdens retain their own analysis.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent political-party campaign expression and candidate connections

### Independent political-party campaign expression and candidate connections

**Governing question:** Application of the Senate party-expenditure ceiling and the alternative candidate-contribution theory to a genuinely independent party advertisement.

**Current rule:** Section 441a(d)(3)'s Senate-election expenditure ceiling cannot be applied to a party's own genuinely independent campaign advertisement merely because the speaker is an official political party. Party identity or a continuing electoral relationship also cannot replace §441a(a)(7)(B)(i)'s required cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion by a candidate, authorized political committee or agent for the asserted contribution theory. Actual independence defeats that theory; realistic corruption and circumvention concerns do not alone establish the statutory candidate connection.

Expenditures actually coordinated with a federal candidate may be limited when the government proves an objectively grounded risk of circumventing valid candidate-contribution limits and a restriction closely drawn to that risk while preserving meaningful party association and effective advocacy. The presented Senate-election party ceiling meets that standard against the categorical facial challenge; party identity, policy agreement, endorsement and ordinary affiliation alone prove neither coordination nor corruption. Ordinary access, equality of influence and dislike of expensive campaigns supply no independent justification. Independent spending cannot automatically cure burdens on joint party expression.

The actual candidate connection remains necessary. Section 441a(a)(7)(B)(i) requires cooperation, consultation or concert with, or a request or suggestion from, the candidate, an authorized committee or an agent. The separate republication and conduit provisions retain their different predicates, including section (a)(8)'s attribution to the original source and source-and-recipient reporting. The same-party transfer exception supplies no categorical constitutional immunity for candidate-connected expenditures. Section (d)(1)'s permission for the identified national and State party committees, including subordinate State committees, is subject to (d)(2) and (3). The Senate and single-Representative-State allowance is the greater of two cents per voting-age resident or $20,000, with section (c)'s annual price-index adjustment; section (d)(3)(B)'s separate indexed $10,000 amount concerns other House, Delegate and Resident Commissioner races. Only the Senate counterclaim's constitutional category is decided.
