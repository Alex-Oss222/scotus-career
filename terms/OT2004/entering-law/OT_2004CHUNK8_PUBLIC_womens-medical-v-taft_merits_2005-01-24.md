## Public Projection

## Event

Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, No. 01-4124, 353 F.3d 436. Submitted on full merits briefing; decided January 24, 2005.

The questions concern the actual reach of Ohio's revised procedure prohibition despite its express dilation-and-evacuation exclusion, and whether its life-and-health provision protects necessary comparative-risk reduction and grave mental-health care. The court of appeals reversed the district court's permanent injunction. Providers seek effective declaratory and injunctive protection; Ohio seeks enforcement of the revised law.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the rejection of categorical ordinary-D&E coverage, vacates the conclusive rejection of the health challenge, and remands for application of the governing standards and effective relief at the proved scope.

## Holdings

### I. The revised prohibition turns on actual acts and distinct exclusions

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio Rev. Code §2919.151 reaches the complete intentional procedure it defines, with each act and mental element satisfied in sequence; its express exclusions preserve ordinary D&E, suction curettage and suction aspiration. The Court rejects the contention that overlapping labels, removal of a fetal part, or an intact delivery alone subjects ordinary D&E to the prohibition, while conduct satisfying the complete defined procedure remains governed by the actual prohibition and its life-and-health terms.

**Authority:** Part I has the eight complete Associate joins shown in Opinion Topology. Stone-Zsela also joins the legal propositions requiring all actual elements and giving each exclusion effect, but withholds the completed coverage application. The eight direct application joins control that disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich requires examination of the actual procedure, fault and alternatives rather than invalidation by nomenclature. The revised definition requires intentional cervical dilation; intentional extraction of an intact fetus beyond the vaginal opening, to at least the lower torso through the navel for breech presentation or the complete head for head-first presentation, while some of the body remains inside; then an intentional partial evacuation of intracranial contents, intentional compression of the head, or another intentional act, in each case known by the performer to cause death; and completion of vaginal delivery. Each condition performs independent work.

Division (F) expressly excludes ordinary D&E and the two suction methods. Division (A)(1) separately distinguishes D&E from dilation and extraction. Division (G) protects performance or attempted performance of a legal abortion when the death-causing act precedes partial birth even if death follows it; that timing exclusion is distinct from the method exclusion. Division (E) separately protects the pregnant woman from criminal responsibility under these prohibitions. The revised text thus supplies protections absent from the different statute considered in Stenberg. A prosecutor's assurance supplies none of this construction's force: the enacted acts, intentions and exclusions do. Actual use of a clinically suitable alternative and the health consequences of substitution remain material to the separate constitutional inquiry below.

### II. Necessary care includes serious comparative risk and grave mental-health danger

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not enforce an abortion-method prohibition when competent individualized medical judgment establishes that substitution creates a material risk of death or serious health harm and the prohibited method is necessary for timely, safe and suitable care. Challengers must establish supported necessary-care applications, including grave medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury; unsupported preference is insufficient, professional disagreement alone does not defeat supported necessity, and usable protection must operate before catastrophic deterioration.

**Authority:** Part II and its necessary-care mandate have seven direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey preserves the necessary-care baseline. Voinovich includes grave medically established mental danger, and Stenberg protects against serious risk imposed by compelled substitution when the prohibited method is necessary for safe and suitable care. Those rules distinguish supported necessary treatment from a marginal advantage or a physician's unsupported preference. They retain viability's separate role while protecting necessary care before and after viability. The civil constitutional inquiry leaves the separate statutory criminal burdens in their proper place.

Ohio's separate previability and postviability provisions prohibit knowing performance when the procedure is unnecessary under their reasonable-medical-judgment life-or-health criterion. Their defined serious-risk category concerns a medically diagnosed condition complicating pregnancy and substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The court of appeals offered a prospective construction addressing future procedural risks, but its explanation's disjunctive wording cannot replace that enacted conjunction. A construction available under Ohio law must actually supply the required protection.

The reported findings identify conditional risks from repeated instrument passes, clotting disorders, blood loss, anesthesia, retained tissue, induction, major surgery and methods of causing death before partial delivery. They support inquiry into actual necessary-care applications rather than universal superiority of one method. The mental-health question likewise concerns grave danger from continued pregnancy combined with the physical method necessary for the indicated abortion, not only distress produced by choosing one technique. Detailed final findings on that question remain below. The new exclusions answer the scope objection but do not conclusively establish that every supported patient retains safe and suitable care. The health validation must therefore be vacated for application of the settled rule.

### III. The complete framework governs remaining previability interference

**Holding and operative rule:** After determining actual coverage and necessary-care protection, courts must apply Casey's complete six-step framework to remaining challenged previability applications. The challengers must establish the operative duty, actual affected population, causal connection and triggering interference; an independent baseline violation, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is invalid, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring, and remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated justification and fit, including an answer to a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective.

**Authority:** Part III and its additional remand command have six direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. The rule rests on those direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey's framework requires six connected inquiries: identify the actual obligation, exceptions and protected baseline; assess its real function, purpose and asserted permissible justification; establish attributable interference for a stable affected population, including supported subgroups and genuinely usable exceptions; apply the independent prohibitions and then the appropriate scrutiny to remaining burdens; protect clinical judgment through competent evidence and patient circumstances; and identify the violation and fit the relief to it. Cumulative effects matter without double counting, and alternatives count only when actually usable for the affected patients.

A substantial obstacle seriously impairs the practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary. Remaining meaningful interference is concrete, nontrivial added burden, not automatic invalidity. Below that threshold the heightened access inquiry ends, while independently prohibited purpose and baseline injury retain their force. Reliable clinical evidence, experience and reasonable predictions can support prospective injury and justification. A professional association has no veto, bare preference supplies no necessity, and neither scientific certainty nor a new study in every case is required.

Kennedy's narrower approach would permit reasonable method regulation outside lost protected choice or necessary care. But the controlling Casey rule also protects against severe or meaningful interference even where some timely care remains available. Treating an alternative's label or the absence of a substantial obstacle as conclusive would erase those distinct protections. The court must therefore assess the remaining actual applications under the full rule, with the State's burdens arising only after the challengers establish the corresponding interference.

### IV. Relief follows the proved violation and the actual procedural scope

**Holding and operative rule:** When an application fails the governing constitutional rule, the decree must protect the affected patients and providers and bar the enforcement consequences dependent on that violation; a proved common-policy violation receives that coverage unless Ohio shows narrower relief fully cures it. The lower court must preserve independently lawful applications under Ohio severability law, promptly supply necessary interim protection when the ordinary equitable predicates are established, and expedite genuinely unresolved material facts while respecting the existing unappealed rulings.

**Authority:** The seven-Justice necessary-care coalition joins these remedial principles and their health application. Six also require their implementation for the complete residual-interference inquiry; Kennedy opposes only that additional command.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich requires lawful construction and relief fitted to the actual violation, including independent operation and Ohio severability limits. A court may apply a construction Ohio law permits; when the text cannot bear the required necessary-care protection, the remedy is protection against incompatible applications and their dependent sanctions. Anderson v. Spear requires relief adequate to correct a proved unlawful policy, while permitting a narrower remedy upon the State's showing of a complete cure. That rule operates after a violation and its scope are proved; it does not shift the providers' burden to establish constitutional injury.

The decree must identify the officials bound and the actual criminal, licensing and civil enforcement consequences within the court's lawful reach. The independent third-party civil-action standing dismissal and the unappealed recklessness construction remain effective. Expedition addresses material factual uncertainty; the current record may itself permit decision where the required predicates are established. Effective interim protection depends on the ordinary equitable findings, and final relief must make the surviving clinical and enforcement duties clear.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey:** Reaffirmed and applied. Its necessary-care baseline and complete six-step previability framework retain their respective controlling coalitions and burdens.
- **Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich:** Applied to actual acts, fault, usable alternatives, grave medically established mental-health danger and Ohio-law severability. The revised enactment receives its own construction rather than the former statute's alternative-method defense and burdens.
- **Stenberg v. Carhart:** Its necessary-care protection and treatment of supported serious comparative risk are reaffirmed. Its ordinary-D&E coverage determination is distinguished because the present sequential definition and express exclusions differ.
- **Anderson v. Spear:** Its corrective-policy remedy is applied to a proved common violation: relief protects the people governed by that violation unless the State establishes a fully effective narrower cure, while lawful provisions retain independent operation.

## Law After Decision

The revised statute's complete sequence and exclusions govern coverage, and the categorical ordinary-D&E objection is resolved against the providers. Seven Justices require usable necessary-care protection for medically supported material risk of death or serious health harm from forced substitution, including the combination of grave mental danger from continued pregnancy and a method necessary for safe treatment. Comparative advantage alone does not establish necessity; a preexisting physical complication cannot be an exclusive constitutional prerequisite. The actual statutory conjunction and a construction permitted by Ohio law must be assessed against the supported clinical applications.

Six Justices directly require the complete previability framework: challengers establish duty, stable affected population, causation and actual interference; baseline injury, obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently fail; remaining severe burdens require compelling justification and narrow tailoring; remaining meaningful burdens require substantiated justification, fit and answers to concrete adequate less burdensome alternatives. Clinical evidence and usable exceptions receive their actual weight. Those are governing rules; clinical application, permissible construction, severability and fitted relief are the work on remand. A proved common-policy violation receives protection for the governed patients and providers unless the State establishes a fully effective narrower remedy. Prompt interim relief and expedited treatment of genuinely unresolved facts implement the rule. The unappealed fault construction and independent civil-standing disposition remain effective.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** The Chief Justice joins the actual-elements rule, necessary-care holding, complete framework and implementing remedy. He would also vacate the conclusive statutory-coverage disposition. Voinovich makes clinically performed acts decisive, and the statutory D&E label supplies an answer only to the extent actual operation preserves the procedure used. He would require focused findings on that operation, usable alternatives and the affected patients instead of conclusively closing the coverage branch. He agrees that Stenberg's different definition cannot be imported and that neither a blanket method entitlement nor automatic invalidation follows. His disagreement concerns the additional coverage inquiry, not the health or full-framework remand he joins.

**Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part:** Kennedy joins the statutory disposition and the necessary-care vacatur and remedy. His Casey baseline position and Voinovich health joins preserve necessary life-and-health care, including grave medically established mental danger; his Stenberg position distinguishes supported comparative risk from an entitlement to a preferred technique. Ohio's new safeguards must receive their actual construction, but the conjunction and proper mental-danger theory require further application before conclusive validation. He would permit reasonable method regulation outside a denial of protected choice or necessary care and therefore opposes the additional command to justify residual meaningful interference under the full framework. His dissent is confined to that operative direction and its rationale; he supports the construction, clinical, severability and prompt interim-protection work required by the necessary-care holding.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas:** Both join the complete-elements interpretation and the rejection of categorical ordinary-D&E coverage. They would affirm the health disposition because they reject the constitutional abortion and necessary-health requirements imposed by Casey, Voinovich and Stenberg, continuing the grounds of their published separate opinions in those cases. Under their view, the defined intentional procedure may receive reasonable regulation for potential-life and medical-ethics purposes. Their disagreement concerns the governing constitutional rule; it does not turn conditional medical evidence into a finding that every substitute is equally safe. They acknowledge ordinary severability and remedial principles but oppose using the Court's constitutional premises to order health and residual-interference remands.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for the construction, clinical application and relief directed above. The lower court must define the surviving obligations and prohibited enforcement in terms usable by patients, clinicians and the officials bound, promptly decide interim protection under the ordinary equitable predicates, and expedite genuinely unresolved material factual work. An injunction against dependent sanctions must match the proved constitutional application and lawful party scope. The independent unappealed civil-standing ruling remains in force.
