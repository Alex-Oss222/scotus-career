### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107

Decision, June 26, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

518 U.S. 515. Argued January 17, 1996; decided June 26, 1996. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit.

The United States sued to challenge VMI's male-only admission policy. The District Court initially rejected liability, 766 F. Supp. 1407; the Fourth Circuit vacated that judgment and required a constitutional remedy, 976 F.2d 890. Virginia challenges that liability ruling in No. 94-2107. After remand, the District Court approved VWIL, 852 F. Supp. 471, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed with monitoring directions, 44 F.3d 1229. The United States challenges the remedy approval in No. 94-1941. The questions are whether the categorical exclusion violates equal protection and whether the separately created program adequately remedies it.

Justice Thomas took no part in the consideration or decision.

#### Judgment

| Docket and component | Disposition and vote | Supporting | Opposing | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No. 94-2107 — constitutional liability | Affirmed, 7–1 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia | The Fourth Circuit's liability judgment stands. |
| No. 94-1941 — approval of VWIL | Reversed and remanded, 7–1 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia | The approved plan cannot serve as an adequate cure; further proceedings must secure prompt effective relief within the Court's stated limits. |

Scalia's primary remedy position is consequential vacatur after reversal of liability, not a vote for the Court's remedy reversal on established liability. His independent conditional position would affirm the VWIL approval if liability remains the governing premise. Virginia retains lawful remedial choices; if it continues the same public VMI without an established adequate alternative, the categorical bar to consideration of qualified women must cease.

#### Opinion Topology

O'Connor delivered the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part I sustains liability under intermediate scrutiny; Part II rejects the actual remedial design; Part III states the operative relief and preserved State choice. Scalia filed a dissent, joined by no other Justice, addressing both dockets and separately stating his conditional remedy position. There is no partial join, judgment-only concurrence or other separate writing.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** The seven-Justice Opinion of the Court identified above controls each holding, its explanation and its qualifications. Scalia joins no part.

##### Part I — Justification for the exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the existing demanding intermediate scrutiny, not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test. Craig v. Boren supplies the important-objective and substantial-relationship requirements; Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan places the burden on the State and requires justification for the discriminatory allocation itself. Hogan's exceedingly persuasive justification describes that burden. J.E.B. rejects sex as a conclusive proxy for individual capacity, while Kelley permits genuinely justified sex-conscious educational choices. Klinger requires comparison of the particular benefit and gives neither immunity nor automatic liability merely because whole institutions differ.

VMI's integrated adversative method has educational value. The accepted findings and predictions that coeducation would change privacy arrangements, physical training and interpersonal methods are not discarded. Their constitutional sufficiency remains a legal question. Preserving an effective program and supplying institutional diversity do not, on this record, adequately explain why its distinctive public opportunity must be reserved to men. Some women can meet the existing physical standards and seek the experience; preferences attributed to most women cannot settle those individuals' entitlement to consideration. The presence of an exceptional individual does not by itself invalidate every sex classification, but these accepted general benefits do not establish sufficient fit for this complete exclusion.

A genuine present educational objective need not have been articulated in 1839 or set out in a contemporaneous written declaration. Litigation timing alone does not establish pretext, and this holding finds neither deceit nor hostility. The failure is the insufficiency of the justification for the actual allocation, not the absence of all pedagogical benefit. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant. The Court does not decide that every single-sex institution or program is unconstitutional.

##### Part II — Adequacy of the proposed alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Controlling explanation:** Sweatt v. Painter supplies the insight that educational opportunity includes the training, resources, standing and connections an institution actually provides. Its racial setting does not establish a universal prohibition on separate education by sex. Klinger directs the inquiry to the benefit denied; neither each isolated disparity nor every institutional difference is an independent constitutional wrong.

VWIL offers real education: leadership courses, seminars, service, an externship, physical education and four years of ROTC at Mary Baldwin. Its cooperative method is a legitimate educational choice. The promised annual State payment equals VMI's per-student appropriation, ordinary student cost is no greater, and a separate endowment supports the program. The VMI Alumni Association promises recruitment assistance and the same access to its placement network, externships and advancement opportunities. The Court credits those promises fully and assumes faithful performance.

That assumption does not supply VMI's integrated military and adversative experience, its degree, its institutional history and standing, or its total resources. VWIL lacks VMI's on-campus engineering option; the alternative requires three years at Mary Baldwin and two years at Washington University, with private tuition and no proposed public discount for the final two years. The cumulative differences in training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources and credential leave the particular opportunity materially unequal. Network access is not absent, but equal access to a network does not erase the other differences or assure identical outcomes.

Missouri v. Jenkins distinguishes installing and funding a program from effectively repairing an established injury. Monitoring faithful implementation cannot make this substantively inadequate design sufficient. The Court finds no breach of a promise or unreported implementation failure. It prescribes no endowment ratio, identical campus, expenditure minimum or guarantee of equal careers, and it does not question Mary Baldwin's independent educational value.

##### Part III — Effective relief and lawful State choice

**Holding and operative rule:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Controlling explanation:** The initial liability remand gave Virginia a choice of lawful responses, not advance approval of any separate program it might propose. Reversal of the present plan does not forfeit the State's responsibility to design an effective cure. Missouri v. Jenkins requires relief connected to the proved injury and distinguishes effective repair from general educational improvement. Lewis v. Casey likewise preserves government choice among effective means and permits necessary implementation without enlarging relief beyond the established wrong; its prison-conditions statutory restrictions supply no additional test here.

Appropriate privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations may preserve legitimate educational needs while providing effective opportunity. The Constitution does not require abandonment of genuine qualifications or admission of a named applicant regardless of them. Nor does this judgment order closure, identical public programs, a quota, equal career outcomes or a particular physical standard. An actual lawful change in State involvement remains different from retaining the same public institution under another label; no proposed withdrawal arrangement is preapproved. The lower courts must secure effective correction and assess any genuinely material remedial proposal consistently with this decision, without a newly required hearing, special master or additional procedural framework.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the general validity of private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate arrangement, every sex-conscious opportunity measure, or any independent Title IX claim. Ordinary respect for underlying factual findings remains intact. No damages, individual admission award, exact admissions date, prescribed training adjustment or finding about unreported 1995–1996 implementation is entered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 26, 1996, the settled intermediate-scrutiny standard governs this public admissions classification, with a controlling application invalidating VMI's complete exclusion of women. An alternative selected to repair that exclusion must provide an effective cure of the material opportunity denied; this VWIL design is inadequate even on full performance of every stated commitment. The decision adds these admissions and remedial applications without raising the scrutiny tier, requiring institutional identity, prohibiting all single-sex education, or displacing supported sex-conscious measures. State design discretion survives within the requirement of prompt and effective correction. The dissent supplies no rule of decision.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting; no joins.** Scalia applies the existing important-objective and substantial-relationship standard but reaches the opposite conclusion on the accepted record. In his view, preserving VMI's integrated method and a real option within a diverse educational system is an important actual objective. The findings about the value of single-sex education and the changes coeducation would cause establish substantial fit. Intermediate scrutiny permits an imperfect fit; some women's ability to meet physical standards does not alone defeat it. He distinguishes Hogan because its permission for men to audit classes undermined the claimed need for exclusion, whereas VMI's residential and educational methods operate together. His position does not treat tradition as immunity, replace heightened scrutiny with rational-basis review, or exempt unlike institutions from comparison.

His primary disposition would reverse liability in No. 94-2107 and, consequently, vacate the compulsory remedy in No. 94-1941 insofar as it rests on that rejected liability, remanding to remove those dependent obligations. That would not prohibit Virginia from voluntarily continuing VWIL.

Separately, assuming liability remains established, he would affirm the existing VWIL approval with its monitoring requirements. He considers the subsidized leadership education, four-year ROTC, endowed support and fully credited alumni assistance meaningfully comparable despite different methods, engineering costs, credentials, resources and prestige. In his view, rejecting the whole alternative gives those differences more constitutional weight than intermediate scrutiny permits. His conditional approval requires faithful funding and alumni performance, the District Court's retained jurisdiction and six-month reports, and the appellate directions for qualified leadership, recruitment and promotion, near-term funding and continuing professional review; promises are not findings of completed performance. This conditional ground neither accepts liability nor replaces his primary cross-docket disposition.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The judgment at 976 F.2d 890 in No. 94-2107 is affirmed. The judgment at 44 F.3d 1229 in No. 94-1941 is reversed, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with Parts I–III. Liability remains established; the existing plan's approval cannot authorize continued unequal exclusion. The lower courts must secure a prompt effective cure while permitting Virginia to design lawful implementation and consider any genuinely material adequate alternative. No fixed admissions date, immediate individual admission, special mandate date or retained Supreme Court proceeding is ordered.

### Hope v. Pelzer, No. 01-309

Merits decision, June 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 17, 2002. On certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment for three prison officers on qualified-immunity grounds while accepting that the alleged punitive restraint violated the Eighth Amendment. The granted issue concerns its materially-similar-precedent requirement and fair warning; ultimate individual responsibility was not taken for review. Hope seeks reversal and continuation of his action under 42 U.S.C. §1983. The officers seek preservation of their personal-immunity judgment.

The favorable summary-judgment account concerns May 11 and June 7, 1995. In May, Hope was attached for two hours after an altercation; the raised position caused pain and wrist discomfort. Water and bathroom breaks were offered every fifteen minutes and recorded. McClaran allegedly ordered attachment, and Pelzer and Gates allegedly attached him. The June account describes approximately seven hours after Hope had been subdued, with scant water, no bathroom break and other aggravations. Gates is the only remaining respondent connected to June, and the alleged act attributed to him is attachment; the account does not itself allege his supervision of the seven hours, removal of clothing, denial of water or taunting. The attribution and even identity of the persons involved remain disputed. These are legal-review assumptions and alleged acts, not liability findings.

The governing assumption for each occasion is extended, painful attachment without continuing danger or work refusal. June's greater severity is distinct from that common premise and is not attributed to May-only officers. The constitutional characterization is accepted for this granted immunity inquiry.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The similarity-based qualified-immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Thomas.

Ordinary further proceedings concerning actual individual responsibility and independent defenses, under the resolved notice rule.

The six Associate Justices supporting reversal enter that decree with ordinary further proceedings preserved. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also expressly directs remand for the remaining responsibility and factual issues. Scalia and Thomas would affirm personal immunity on the conduct attributed to these respondents. The reversal does not reopen the legal notice conclusion on the accepted assumption; materially different actual facts and other lawful defenses retain their ordinary force.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined as to the immunity and attribution grounds.

#### Holdings

##### I. The accepted constitutional premise remains individual and conduct-specific

**Holding and operative rule:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. The same seven directly join each following holding; neither dissenter joins the opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the accepted constitutional premise rather than deciding an ungranted challenge to it. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from unnecessary punitive suffering. Farmer requires actual appreciation of a substantial danger and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits an inference of awareness but does not compel it or aggregate knowledge across guards. Hudson's established prohibition of nontrivial malicious beating does not become a universal rule for every restraint or eliminate the conditions inquiry.

May's regular offers of necessities mitigate the conditions but do not justify the assumed two hours of painful punishment after necessity ceased. June's aggravated conditions strengthen its account without supplying missing acts by McClaran, Pelzer or Gates. The dissent's personal-role objection matters to ultimate responsibility. It cannot change the granted legal assumption into mere initial attachment for a continuing lawful purpose. The Court leaves factual sufficiency and actual attribution to ordinary proceedings because they fall outside the accepted question.

**Precedent treatment:** Farmer's actual-knowledge and reasonable-response requirements are applied. Hudson retains its limited force holding. Rhodes and Whitley supply the distinction between necessary control and gratuitous punishment; Delaney's assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or 1995 notice.

##### II. Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Authority:** Part II has the same seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas's agreement with the possible obvious-violation route does not join the Court's reading or application of the appellate judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow supplies an objective personal defense, and Anderson requires particularized clarity while rejecting immunity merely because the precise act has not been litigated. Lanier explains why civil clearly-established law and criminal fair warning share a notice inquiry. General moral disapproval alone is insufficient, but a settled prohibition can plainly cover new apparatus. A more particular precedent is especially important where earlier law expressly left this application open. Lanier's criminal willfulness element does not become a civil element.

The reviewed judgment rejected Gates and Ort as insufficiently similar despite the accepted after-necessity punishment. The dissent reads circuit law as already allowing obvious applications. That general allowance does not cure this judgment's failure to apply the governing warning to its accepted facts. The inquiry asks what preconduct law communicated, not whether an official actually consulted a favorable precedent. Wilson retains context-sensitive immunity; Saucier establishes no replacement defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Lanier, Wilson and later decisions explain the method without creating notice retroactively. State policy and administrative warnings have contextual roles, not independent authority to create or erase the constitutional right.

**Precedent treatment:** Harlow and Anderson are retained; Lanier's warning method is applied without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve objective conduct-date inquiry. A categorical factual-twin prerequisite is rejected.

##### III. Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding and operative rule:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Authority:** Part III has the same seven direct joins, covering both May and June at the stated legal assumption. It establishes no collective or final personal liability.

**Controlling explanation:** Gates condemned prolonged handcuffing and awkward confinement as corporal punishment. Its place in the binding circuit line, through Bonner, matters more than the name of the apparatus. Ort upheld necessary coercion during work refusal while explaining the different constitutional position of later punishment and measures severe enough to endanger health. That distinction directly addresses the assumed end of necessity here.

Lane, Whitson and the other preconduct district decisions are the strongest contrary evidence. Their accounts involved continued refusal, opportunities to resume work, necessities or shorter duration. May shared some mitigating conditions but, on the accepted premise, lacked the continuing justification. Such lower-court decisions cannot override the superior circuit warning against this gratuitous punishment. The contrary interpretation does not establish immunity by treating the assumed prolonged punitive act as only lawful initial attachment.

The state regulation authorized restraints for refusal or disruption and prescribed return to work when an inmate was ready, quarter-hour necessities and a log. Those safeguards reinforce the coercion/punishment distinction; compliance with some safeguards is not a license for knowingly needless pain, and breach alone is not a federal violation. The preconduct federal report corroborates the warning without proving receipt by any respondent. Later district approval cannot retroactively erase the earlier rule. Each officer's actual knowledge and causal role remain necessary under Part IV.

**Precedent treatment:** Gates and Ort are applied at their specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. The district cases are distinguished, not treated as nonexistent. Later cases and the administrative report are not substitutes for preconduct constitutional authority.

##### IV. Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same seven direct joins in reversal and ordinary further proceedings. Stone-Zsela separately calls for an express remand; the six Associate reversal votes do not depend on that additional formal direction.

**Controlling explanation:** The grant reaches the legal notice issue, not final sufficiency of the pleadings and evidence concerning each named officer. Deciding notice on the court below's accepted premise therefore neither decides the factual dispute nor requires an open-ended repetition of the legal warning inquiry. The dissent would regard the limited attributed acts as enough to affirm immunity now. That inquiry exceeds the issue accepted for review; it remains available in the appropriate proceedings on an actual individual record.

The lower court must separate McClaran's alleged May order, Pelzer's alleged May attachment, and Gates's alleged attachments on the two occasions. It must apply Farmer's personal awareness and reasonable-response limits, ordinary causation requirements and lawful independent defenses. Later supported facts may materially differ from the assumed punitive conduct. This disposition preserves that work rather than ordering collective liability. The withdrawn claims against absent guards remain withdrawn.

**Precedent treatment:** Delaney confirms the distinction between a resolved legal issue and actual responsibility, without making missing allegations into a genuine factual dispute. Farmer and the ordinary summary-judgment rules govern the remaining individual inquiry.

#### Law After Decision

Conduct-specific fair warning can arise from an obvious application of established law without a factual twin. The rule resolves immunity against the accepted knowingly gratuitous restraint on both 1995 occasions, while leaving actual defendant-specific responsibility and lawful independent defenses for ordinary adjudication. Needed control, reasonable responses and materially different circumstances remain subject to the existing constitutional and immunity standards.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's notice rule, both-occasion application and reversal, and expressly directs remand for remaining individual-responsibility and factual issues. The legal fair-warning conclusion is settled on the accepted account; the next court must test each named officer's own participation, knowledge and causation. May's offered necessities do not justify assumed gratuitous punishment, and June's aggravations cannot be imputed across officers. His express remand gives effect to that remaining work without changing the Court's substantive standard.

**Thomas, dissenting, with Scalia joining the immunity and attribution grounds.** They would affirm personal immunity for these respondents. Harlow, Anderson and Malley protect reasonable legal mistakes, and both accept that obvious illegality can defeat immunity without a factual twin. They dispute the Court's reading of the appellate decision: considering factual similarity is not necessarily making it indispensable, and the circuit's own obvious-violation cases belie a categorical rule.

The dispositive question concerns the alleged acts of McClaran, Pelzer and Gates. McClaran allegedly ordered the May attachment; Pelzer and Gates allegedly performed it; Gates alone is linked to June. The seven-hour prolongation, shirt removal, water deprivation and taunting were not attributed to him. May had quarter-hour necessities and a log. Identity, clothing and bathroom accounts are disputed, and a generalized narrative of the worst treatment cannot establish these officers' clearly unlawful acts.

The authorizing regulation, Lane and Whitson, and the other pre-1995 district decisions would permit reasonable disagreement. The policy addressed disruption as well as refusal; the dissent disputes that these officers were alleged to have denied a request to return to work or breached a supervisory duty. The federal report was not shown to have reached them and could not settle a legal disagreement with courts. Ort upheld coercion and only reasoned about later punishment; Gates supplied no precise duration, purpose or necessities rule and preceded Farmer's clarified knowledge requirement. They find no sufficiently clear prohibition of the attributed initial attachment, even though prolonged knowingly gratuitous punishment may be unlawful. Later decisions cannot supply missing notice.

Thomas additionally preserves his view that conditions not imposed as part of a sentence are not punishment in the original sense. He applies the binding contrary conditions law here and remains open to reconsideration in an appropriate case. That reservation does not decide the granted immunity issue or change the Court's existing rule.

### Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs, No. 01-1368

Merits decision, May 27, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 15, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 273 F.3d 844, which reversed the District of Nevada's sovereign-immunity disposition of the FMLA family-care action and vacated the dependent dismissal of state claims. The question is whether the family-care entitlement validly enforces the Fourteenth Amendment and authorizes private State actions despite sovereign immunity; Article I supplies an alternative ground for the same judgment. Nevada seeks categorical dismissal; Hibbs seeks to pursue the statutory action. His intermittent leave to care for his wife was approved for May–December 1997. After his last attendance on August 5, the department asserted exhaustion, instructed him to return by November 12 and discharged him when he did not return. Exhaustion and the alleged statutory violation remain disputed.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The Ninth Circuit's statutory-action remand and dependent vacatur of the state-claim dismissal remain effective. The separate constitutional claim retains its prior procedural disposition.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Congress expressly authorized the family-care action against a State employer

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 2611(4)(A)(iii) and 2617(a)(2) unmistakably authorize an eligible employee's action for the prescribed family-care damages or equitable relief against a covered State employer in a competent federal or state court. Public-agency coverage and express private-action authorization are separate statutory predicates, both satisfied by the enacted text.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2611 expressly includes public agencies, incorporating the definition that reaches States and their agencies. Section 2617 separately permits the employee's action against an employer, expressly including a public agency. Florida Prepaid and Kimel require unmistakable authorization distinct from constitutional power. This direct remedy text answers the clarity inquiry without depending on an inference from employer coverage alone. The statute regulates the employment relationship and authorizes its enforcement; State consent is a different source of amenability. College Savings Bank's rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity remains intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid and Kimel supply the distinct clear-authorization inquiry. College Savings Bank preserves the difference between legislative authority and waiver.

##### II. Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized family-care action

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against a State when both the substantive obligation and the enforcement measure fall within a valid Article I power and the State's exposure to suit is unmistakably expressed. The FMLA family-care employment duty and its enacted employee remedy satisfy that rule under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses and independently defeat Nevada's categorical immunity defense.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor withholds this ground; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from it.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid establishes the affirmative Article I enforcement rule; Kimel applies it to State employment, and Garrett independently sustains the specified Title I employment actions on that basis. Alden applies valid commerce authority to an expressly authorized state-court wage remedy. Family-care leave regulates the terms and continuity of actual economic employment. The employee action is an appropriate means of enforcing that obligation. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming support federal regulation of covered State employment, while the private-remedy question is answered by the later enforcement precedents.

The opposing structural distinction between regulation and a private action against the State's treasury is the objection those precedents rejected. It does not become an existing exception merely because the subject is family-care leave rather than wages, age discrimination or disability discrimination. The Court adheres to its enforcement rule and applies its separate power and authorization requirements here. Congress's commerce designation is consistent with the economic relationship but does not by itself establish power. The action rests on valid legislation, rather than Nevada's inferred consent, and Hibbs need not prove an individual equal-protection violation as an extra element of this statutory claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett are applied to the authorized employment duty and remedy. Alden preserves the independently authorized state-court route. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming supply the substantive employment-regulation foundation; College Savings Bank's no-implied-consent rule remains distinct.

##### III. Section 5 independently sustains the bounded family-care entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** The sex-neutral family-care entitlement in §2612(a)(1)(C), with its enacted coverage, eligibility, medical-need, scheduling and remedial qualifications, is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of constitutional sex equality in State employment. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized private action, including statutory violations that would not themselves establish an individual constitutional tort.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens withholds assent to this ground without adopting an invalidity holding; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention beyond separately provable constitutional violations while forbidding Congress to redefine the constitutional right. United States v. Virginia requires genuine important objectives and a substantial relationship for sex classifications; assumptions about men's and women's proper domestic roles cannot replace that justification. The relevant injury is employment exclusion and unequal leave administration sustained by those roles, rather than the mere absence of any leave benefit.

Congress's evidence included historical employment restrictions, contemporary public-employment accounts and testimony, and private-sector practices. The Massachusetts public-school account and testimony concerning public employers give the inference State-related support. Pregnancy incapacity, child-rearing and care of ill relatives remain different settings; the seven listed women-only regimes are not seven adjudicated unconstitutional family-care denials. Those limits do not erase the connected mechanism: treating women as caregivers and men as continuously available workers can reinforce exclusion in hiring, advancement and leave decisions. Equal family-care opportunity removes that proxy for both sexes.

The principal objection is that a minimum benefit reaches nondiscriminatory employers and decisions, and some evidence concerns neighboring practices. The finite combined twelve-week entitlement, ordinary unpaid status, eligibility and serious-illness requirements, certification, notice, scheduling safeguards and claim-linked remedies keep the preventive means fitted to the identified injury. Boerne allows that preventive reach without a violation in every State or a quota of findings. Garrett accepts that employment prevention may exceed the constitutional minimum when adequately fitted, but found Title I's general requirements, measured against rational-basis review of disability classifications, not so fitted at the full scope presented; Florida Prepaid likewise found undifferentiated patent liability without an adequately supported preventive relationship to unconstitutional deprivation. Heightened scrutiny of sex classifications, the State-related evidence and the bounded sex-role mechanism supply the fit those statutes lacked. Popovich similarly separates fitted statutory protection from an individual constitutional tort; its distinct parental-hearing setting does not substitute for the family-care analysis performed here. Self-care and other leave entitlements lie outside the presented question and require their own constitutional analysis.

**Precedent treatment:** Boerne supplies the prevention/redefinition boundary; Virginia supplies demanding intermediate scrutiny. Florida Prepaid and Garrett supply the requirement of adequately fitted prevention and are distinguished as to the rational-basis disability classification, undifferentiated patent liability and statutory scope they assessed. Popovich is applied for the statutory-duty/constitutional-tort distinction and distinguished as to its court-access subject. Kimel's reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 question remains intact.

**Operative statutory qualifications supporting the family-care holding:** Employee eligibility generally requires twelve months with the employer and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months, calculated under the applicable Fair Labor Standards Act standards. The Act excludes federal officers and employees covered by subchapter V of chapter 63 of Title 5, and a worker at a site with fewer than fifty employees if the employer also has fewer than fifty within seventy-five miles. Express public-agency coverage does not eliminate those employee requirements.

Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to provider certification under §2613, covers care for a spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. Parent includes a biological parent or one who stood in a parental role during the employee's childhood. Son or daughter includes a biological, adopted, foster or stepchild, legal ward or child for whom the employee stands in a parental role, under eighteen or, if older, incapable of self-care because of mental or physical disability. The enacted spouse definition is husband or wife. Serious health condition requires inpatient care in a hospital, hospice or residential medical facility, or continuing treatment by a health-care provider. The entitlement totals twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period across the statutory leave categories, rather than twelve weeks per episode or relative.

Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted, and only leave actually taken reduces the total. Foreseeable planned treatment requires a reasonable scheduling effort to avoid undue operational disruption, subject to provider approval, and thirty days' notice, or practicable notice if treatment must start sooner. For that foreseeable intermittent treatment, the employer may temporarily transfer a qualified employee to an available alternative position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.

Leave may be unpaid. An employee may elect, or an employer require, substitution of accrued vacation, personal or family leave for the specified birth, placement or family-care categories. For family-care or self-care serious-health-condition leave, accrued vacation, personal, medical or sick leave may similarly be substituted; that permission does not require paid medical or sick leave where the employer would not normally provide it. For spouses working for the same employer, combined leave may be limited to twelve weeks for birth, placement or care of a sick parent; that aggregation permission does not cover every family-care category.

An employer may require timely provider certification identifying commencement, probable duration, relevant medical facts, the need and expected time for care, and applicable treatment dates or intermittent-care need and schedule. A supported doubt permits an employer-paid second opinion from a provider not regularly employed by it; a conflict permits an employer-paid third opinion jointly chosen and binding on both sides. Reasonable subsequent recertification is permitted.

Restoration protects the former or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, without creating benefit accrual during leave or a right the employee would have lacked absent leave. The restoration exception requires a salaried employee among the highest-paid ten percent within seventy-five miles, necessity to prevent substantial and grievous economic injury, notice when the employer determines that injury would occur and, if leave has begun, the employee's election not to return after notice. This is a restoration exception, not permission to deny leave generally. Group-health coverage continues on the terms that would have applied during continuous employment. Premiums paid for unpaid leave may be recovered after failure to return when the entitlement expires, except when nonreturn results from continuation, recurrence or onset of a qualifying serious health condition or circumstances beyond the employee's control; timely provider certification may be required for the serious-health-condition exception. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain their enacted force. These qualifications define the scheme; their application to Hibbs requires the ordinary merits record.

##### IV. The statutory action proceeds under its own proof and remedy requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical immunity is affirmed, and the family-care action proceeds under the FMLA's coverage, liability, defense and remedy provisions. Hibbs must prove the required statutory facts and causally recoverable loss by a preponderance; an individual equal-protection violation is not an additional entry requirement under either sufficient enforcement ground.

**Authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The two constitutional grounds establish the State's amenability to the enacted action. They leave the actual statutory dispute to ordinary adjudication, as Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich distinguish valid enforcement from proved liability. The department's assertion that leave was exhausted remains contested rather than an adjudicated fact. The same distinction requires application of the enacted loss measures and defenses to any proved violation. The surviving procedural disposition of the separate constitutional claim remains effective.

**Operative remedy qualifications:** Section 2617 permits wages, salary, benefits or other compensation denied or lost by reason of the violation. When such compensation was not denied or lost, the alternative is actual monetary loss directly resulting from the violation, such as care costs, capped at twelve weeks of wages or salary; that cap applies to the alternative, not all backpay. Prevailing-rate interest is added. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensation or alternative loss plus interest. Only upon the employer's proof of both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the act or omission lawful may the court, in its discretion, reduce liability to principal and interest. Appropriate equitable relief includes employment, reinstatement or promotion; a judgment for the employee also carries reasonable attorney and expert fees and other costs. The limitations period runs two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three years for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint under §2617(d) seeking restraint of delayed payment of the employee's specified amounts, or under §2617(b) seeking recovery of those damages, terminates the private-action right unless that action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich preserve ordinary statutory proof and relief after rejection of immunity; their affirmative enforcement holdings are applied without converting them into merits awards.

#### Law After Decision

Eligible employees may pursue the expressly authorized family-care action against covered State employers under either of two independently sufficient grounds: valid Article I employment enforcement and congruent and proportional Section 5 prevention of sex inequality. Each ground commands five direct joins, although the two coalitions differ. Courts apply the FMLA's actual entitlement, eligibility, defenses, proof and remedy terms. Self-care and other leave provisions remain outside the constitutional question decided here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins statutory authorization, the Article I ground and the statutory remand. His Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett positions establish that Congress may expressly authorize employee enforcement of valid national economic regulation against a State. That ground fully supports affirmance. He remains uncertain whether this particular family-care enactment was needed and sufficiently fitted as Fourteenth Amendment enforcement; his support for preventive power in other settings does not answer that statute-specific issue. He therefore withholds Part III without deciding that Section 5 is inadequate.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment.** She joins the clear statutory authorization, Section 5 holding and statutory remand, while retaining her structural objection to Article I authorization of a private treasury action. The family-care mechanism directly counters assigned sex roles in employment. Virginia supplies the heightened equality standard, and the finite, medically conditioned, generally unpaid entitlement and qualified remedies give the preventive measure sufficient fit. Her agreement with that Section 5 application does not extend to the lead's independent Article I ground.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent accepts the explicit public-agency remedy and valid federal employment regulation but distinguishes that substantive power from authority to subject a nonconsenting State's treasury to private suit. It would reconsider Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett's contrary Article I rule to the extent needed for reversal, rather than treat those holdings as absent.

Independently, the dissent finds the family-care evidence insufficiently connected to the general minimum entitlement and private damages remedy. Historical restrictions, private-sector studies, pregnancy-disability provisions, child-rearing policies and care for ill relatives bear different relationships to unconstitutional State family-care conduct. A State may provide no leave benefit while administering employment equally. Boerne allows genuine prevention, and Virginia supplies heightened scrutiny of actual sex classifications, but those principles do not establish this particular means-to-injury fit. Popovich's duty to secure a meaningful parental hearing is more closely tied to the protected opportunity than a freestanding minimum leave benefit.

The dissent's objection concerns that relationship, not a prescribed incident count, a violation in every State, formal findings, a sunset or an individual constitutional tort in every valid preventive action. State antidiscrimination laws and few reported suits do not establish compliance; official prejudice can arise through indifference as well as express hostility. These limits preserve Kennedy's particular-fit objection, Scalia's rejection of an automatic defendant-State-violation prerequisite and Thomas's acceptance of direct remedy language despite his different textual objection in Kimel.

### United States v. Gatewood, No. 98-5138 below

Merits decision, June 30, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

230 F.3d 186. On review of the Sixth Circuit’s October 10, 2000 en banc judgment affirming three concurrent mandatory-life sentences under 18 U.S.C. §3559(c). The questions are whether the defendant may bear clear-and-convincing persuasion on the nonqualifying-robbery exception, and whether the prior-conviction predicates authorizing mandatory life require federal charging, jury and reasonable-doubt safeguards. The present jury verdict, returned March 6, 1997, convicted Gatewood of Hobbs Act robbery and two kidnappings. The prosecution’s sentencing notice identified four Arkansas convictions from 1966 through 1976. Both sentencing objections were preserved.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed as to the sentences; mandatory-life terms vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** O’Connor, Kennedy, Breyer.

The same coalition resolves both presented sentencing objections.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SOUTER, THOMAS, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which KENNEDY and BREYER, JJ., joined. The six majority Justices join all three parts; the three dissenters would affirm against both presented sentencing objections.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority for the holdings below:** The Court’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct joins to each holding below, its application and remedy.

##### I. Recidivist facts creating greater punishment authority receive element protection

**Holding and operative rule:** A prior-conviction fact legally necessary to increase the authorized maximum or mandatory minimum must receive element protections: applicable federal indictment allegations and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless validly admitted through lawful charging and waiver procedures. The protection covers existence, identity and necessary historical qualification or chronology; a lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder while retaining the same proof standard and charge boundary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that constitutional allocation follows the fact’s work in authorizing punishment. Apprendi protects facts increasing a ceiling; Angle and Harris protect facts increasing a binding floor. Limon now applies element protection to a fact concerning a nonjury juvenile adjudication necessary to increase the adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling. That rule requires an actual valid aggravating-element admission or proper adjudication; the earlier judgment’s original validity and a waiver confined to the current act do not themselves authorize greater adult punishment. Limon addressed that juvenile maximum. The adult-conviction question, the recidivist floor and the applicable federal charge require the further conclusions adopted here.

Almendarez-Torres’s statutory aggravated-element holding supplies the federal charging analogue. The dissent stresses that a prior adult conviction may already have received jury safeguards. Such a judgment is powerful evidence of what it actually adjudicated. But establishing its existence, identity and necessary qualification as a new punishment-authorizing predicate is not a retrial of old guilt. Unusually reliable proof does not eliminate the applicable charge and factfinder requirements. The Court adopts that additional adult-predicate conclusion and extends Angle and Harris’s binding-floor reasoning to the kidnapping enhancements. Judges continue to interpret statutes and classify established facts legally; history-based judgment within an independently authorized range remains distinct. Applicable federal indictment protection operates in federal prosecutions, while the jury and reasonable-doubt allocation follows the fact’s punishment-authorizing function.

##### II. The qualifying-robbery circumstances belong to the prosecution’s case

**Holding and operative rule:** For the necessary robbery predicates under §3559(c), the prosecution must establish qualifying status beyond a reasonable doubt through the required element adjudication. The statutory exclusion requires both absence of firearm or other dangerous-weapon use or threatened use and absence of resulting death or serious bodily injury; defeating that exclusion may rest on the applicable alternative of weapon use, threatened use, death or serious bodily injury, but uncertainty about the needed qualifying facts cannot be assigned to the defendant through clear-and-convincing persuasion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress’s exception label cannot move the factual boundary of mandatory life outside the prosecution’s case. Patterson permits a genuine affirmative defense while preserving element proof; it does not answer the classification by accepting the legislature’s label. The statute actually enacted excludes a robbery satisfying both absence conditions. The government’s hypothetical power to count every robbery cannot replace the qualifying conditions it must satisfy under that statute. Reducing the defendant’s burden to a preponderance would leave that allocation defect intact. Parke concerns regularity and production in a collateral challenge to an earlier judgment, not the present prosecution’s ultimate burden. Cooper illustrates the significance of error allocation in a distinct competency setting; the element rule itself decides this case. An earlier judgment supplies evidence of what it necessarily adjudicated. The label robbery, armed or aggravated does not by itself establish unexamined elements or disputed historical conduct. Gatewood’s missing witnesses and denial of old guilt likewise establish neither the exception nor invalidity of the old convictions.

##### III. The preserved aggravated-charge omission requires vacatur of all three compulsory terms

**Holding and operative rule:** When an indispensable federal enhancement allegation was omitted from the indictment, the defendant preserved the objection and no valid charging waiver supplies authority, sentencing notice and judicial findings do not authorize the aggravated punishment. Gatewood’s three mandatory-life sentences must be set aside and resentencing must begin with the ranges independently authorized by the existing charges and verdicts: the twenty-year Hobbs Act ceiling and the lawful kidnapping ranges without compulsory life.

**Controlling explanation:** Almendarez-Torres’s charge-bound rule makes an admission insufficient to enlarge an indictment omitting its aggravated element. The Court extends that protection to the recidivist elements recognized today. Here sentencing notice and judicial consideration of the old convictions supplied the enhancement procedure, rather than the required aggravated charge and element adjudication. Limon’s distinction between a current-act waiver and a valid aggravating-element admission reinforces the need to examine what was actually authorized; the independently preserved federal charge defect supplies the vacatur here. That preserved charging defect independently prevents retaining the compulsory terms on the strength of sentencing evidence. Cotton’s treatment of an unpreserved indictment error as nonjurisdictional does not turn this preserved objection into forfeiture. Neder and Ring retain their actual-verdict inquiries in their own error settings; this disposition rests on the charge boundary rather than a hypothetical finding from the old records. Mandatory life exceeds the Hobbs Act maximum and removes every lesser kidnapping sentence. The independent availability of discretionary life for kidnapping does not cure that loss of choice. The sentencing court must exercise lawful discretion and give reasons. Any requested new enhancement proceeding requires separate charging and procedural authority and compliance with applicable sufficiency, waiver, notice, finality and double-jeopardy law; the remand itself supplies none.

#### Law After Decision

Building on Limon’s rule for maximum-increasing nonjury juvenile predicates, necessary adult recidivist facts now receive constitutional element protections when they enlarge a binding maximum or minimum. Federal prosecutions also require the applicable aggravated charge, subject to lawful admission and waiver rules. Statutory interpretation and discretionary sentencing within independently authorized ranges remain judicial functions. The rule applies on direct review to nonfinal criminal judgments.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s whole opinion and would apply its new element protection through explicit safeguards against prejudicial use of criminal status. A specific informed and legally adequate admission must establish every fact for which it substitutes; an admission of one status does not establish disputed chronology, conduct or identity and does not cure an omitted indictment allegation. Under Old Chief’s Rule 403 comparison, an actually available unconditional admission proving the complete status element must be weighed against the prejudicial name or nature of the conviction, while a partial admission or a genuine separate evidentiary purpose requires its own analysis. A separately authorized enhancement phase is required if a combined proceeding would create an intolerable risk to determination of present guilt. He preserves Monge and the applicable charging, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules for any independently authorized further proceeding, without borrowing his separate due-process theory in Sattazahn as controlling law. No other Justice joins these additional implementation directions.

**O’Connor, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, dissenting.** They would affirm both sentencing rulings. Their Limon position treats valid adjudicated offender history as usable in structured adult sentencing, even in the juvenile setting; they maintain that distinct ground for adult convictions here. Limon’s controlling juvenile-maximum rule leaves this adult question open. A valid robbery conviction, already obtained through criminal adjudication, supplies a recidivist baseline that Congress may use at sentencing. Section 3559(c)(3)(A) then gives the defendant a genuine exception requiring proof of the complete absence of weapon use or threatened use and of death or serious bodily injury. Patterson preserves the distinction between an element and an affirmative defense; the prior adjudication and the statute’s structure place this exception on the latter side. Parke supports respect for final judgments but is not itself authority for the ultimate clear-and-convincing quantum. Cooper’s competence-to-stand-trial protection does not determine the burden for this recidivist exception. The dissent’s recidivist distinction covers the mandatory floor on the kidnapping counts as well as the robbery maximum. Unavailable old witnesses and denial of participation neither establish the enacted exclusion nor reopen final guilt. Their ground does not require an invented conclusion that the old Arkansas judgments necessarily adjudicated every weapon or injury circumstance.
