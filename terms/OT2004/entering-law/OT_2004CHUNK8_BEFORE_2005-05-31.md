## Clingman v. Beaver; No. 04-37.

Source: [completed Record](../records/clingman-v-beaver_merits_2005-05-23.md#public-projection). Effective date: 2005-05-23.

## Event

Clingman v. Beaver, No. 04-37. Argued January 19, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, which reversed the judgment for Oklahoma and directed relief against its semiclosed-primary restriction. The question is whether Oklahoma may prevent a political party from inviting voters registered with other parties into its primary while permitting invitations to independents. The Libertarian Party sought to invite all registered voters; the State excluded those retaining another party registration. The completed original primary leaves the continuing rule and prospective relief in controversy.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment against the challenged exclusion and remands for prospective relief consistent with its opinion.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer.

The judgment invalidating the continued-registration exclusion is affirmed under contextual review. The mandate confines prospective relief to qualified parties' voluntary invitations and otherwise eligible voters, with implementation as stated in Part III.

## Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, unanimous as to Part I and joined by STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER and GINSBURG, JJ., as to Parts II and III. O'CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which SCALIA, THOMAS and BREYER, JJ., joined.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | All eight Associates in Part I; Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg in Parts II–III | Affirmance and prospective remand | I: protected association and contextual review; II: insufficient justification; III: implementing relief |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | O'Connor | Scalia, Thomas, Breyer | Would reverse | Joins Part I; sustains the limited registration-choice condition |

## Holdings

### Protected association and the governing inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A qualified party's invitation and an otherwise eligible voter's acceptance are protected political association even when the voter retains another party registration. A court must assess the actual burden, the State's precise interests and their fit under Anderson and Burdick: severe burdens require compelling justification and narrow tailoring; lesser burdens still require a sufficient regulatory connection.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Participation in an official nomination is legally effective association. Permission to campaign or endorse outside that process does not replace it. Timmons protects a consenting party's meaningful nomination choice; Tashjian protects invitations to independents while leaving other-party registrants for decision. Jones protects an objecting party against compelled outsiders. These decisions require attention to the parties' actual choices and the restriction's operation. Registration remains an administrable way to define an electorate, but its label does not eliminate the association that it burdens. The State may preserve a single nominating vote and genuine election administration through rules fitted to those purposes.

### The continued-registration exclusion lacks sufficient justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Oklahoma may not exclude an otherwise qualified voter from a consenting party's primary solely because the voter retains another party registration when the State has not shown a sufficient regulatory need for that exclusion. On this record the burden is substantial but not severe, and the categorical disaffiliation condition fails contextual review without automatic strict scrutiny.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Timmons requires a real connection between an electoral burden and the State's asserted stability interests. The trial findings rejected confusion, insuperable administrative difficulty and protection of the inviting Libertarian Party from its own choice. The legitimate interests in identifiable parties, orderly nominations and other parties' continuity remain, but the evidence does not connect this additional exclusion adequately to their preservation. The close-primary statistic shows that invited votes could change results; it does not demonstrate disorganization or corrupted nominations. Other parties may exclude unwanted voters from their own primaries, but that protection does not entitle them to prevent their registrants' willing association elsewhere. The dissent properly recognizes the legal significance of nomination and the ease of a registration choice. Those considerations do not explain why an exclusive primary vote and ordinary verification are insufficient here, or why continuing affiliation itself establishes a threat. The Court therefore affirms the constitutional judgment on the existing record while rejecting the appellate court's severe-burden classification.

### Prospective relief follows the common exclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** The prospective decree must protect qualified inviting parties and their otherwise eligible invited voters against the adjudicated categorical exclusion; Oklahoma may obtain narrower party-specific relief only by showing that it fully cures that same policy defect. The decree must preserve an unwilling party's choice, a voter's single primary vote, lawful deadlines, ordinary verification and independently valid eligibility conditions.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the five Justices supporting Part II, directly controls the remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson v. Spear requires relief that corrects the unlawful operation for those governed by the policy unless the State establishes a fully effective narrower cure. Here the common defect is the demand to surrender an existing registration before accepting a qualified party's invitation. Naming only the plaintiffs does not itself establish that a smaller decree will cure it. Election officials must accept timely invitations and administer a single-primary choice without recreating the forbidden exclusion. The lower court must settle the implementing terms and any supported narrower-cure contention under this standard. The relief operates prospectively.

## Precedent Treatment

- Anderson v. Celebrezze and Burdick v. Takushi supply the contextual burden, precise-interest and fit inquiry; their graduated scrutiny remains controlling.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party is extended from meaningful consensual nomination to reciprocal primary invitations, preserving its substantial-but-not-severe analysis.
- California Democratic Party v. Jones retains its protection against compelled outsider nomination; the party's consent distinguishes this case.
- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut remains controlling for independent-voter invitations; its reserved other-party question is resolved at the consent-based scope stated here.
- Anderson v. Spear supplies the common-policy remedy and the State's opportunity to establish a fully curative narrower decree.

## Law After Decision

Beginning with this decision, voluntary invitations to otherwise eligible voters retaining another affiliation receive the same contextual protection as other legally effective political association. Continued registration alone cannot justify the exclusion on the record adjudicated here. Genuine, distinct electoral threats remain subject to the burden-and-fit inquiry; ordinary one-primary administration and the autonomy of unwilling parties remain operative. The lower court's remaining work is implementation of the settled rule.

## Separate Writings

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, concurs in Part I and dissents from invalidation and its remedy. The writing accepts real reciprocal and overlapping association and applies Anderson and Burdick, but regards the available formal choice of nominating affiliation as a limited burden supported by identifiable parties, campaign planning, nomination-system operation and other-party continuity. Preventive regulation need not await a proved corrupted election. Timmons's protection of a consenting candidate designation does not, in their view, eliminate a distinct affiliation choice for selecting a party's nominee. They accept the lower factual findings and do not rely on the rejected confusion or raiding grounds. Their position depends on reasonable access to the registration choice presented here. They would reverse and sustain the challenged condition.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Tenth Circuit for a fitted prospective decree. Election officials must provide the lawful single-primary means of participation described in Part III. Any supported request for a narrower decree must establish a complete cure of the adjudicated common-policy violation.

## Source Notes

No. 04-37; Oklahoma Statutes, title 26, §1-104; Beaver v. Clingman, 363 F.3d 1048 (10th Cir. 2004). The trial findings and the distinction between changed nomination results and political instability govern the record application. Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut, 479 U.S. 208, addresses invitations to independents and reserves the different-party question.

## Deck v. Missouri; No. 04-5293.

Source: [completed Record](../records/deck-v-missouri_merits_2005-05-23.md#public-projection). Effective date: 2005-05-23.

## Event

Deck v. Missouri, No. 04-5293. Argued March 1, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Missouri, which affirmed a death sentence imposed in a replacement penalty proceeding after the earlier sentence was set aside. Deck appeared before the sentencing jury in handcuffs, leg irons and a belly chain. Counsel repeatedly objected; the trial court relied on the conviction and juror reassurance without identifying a particular current security need. The questions are whether visible shackling throughout capital sentencing requires defendant-specific justification and which prejudice or harmlessness rule governs relief.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the sentencing judgment under review and remands for application of the constitutional harmless-error standard.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The reviewed affirmance of the death sentence is reversed. Missouri must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt on the full penalty record; otherwise the death sentence must be vacated and the State must provide a lawful new penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence. The convictions remain intact.

## Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Parts I–III: individualized necessity, violation, Chapman burden and sentencing remedy |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Antecedent constitutional violation; would affirm |

## Holdings

### Visible restraint during capital punishment selection

**Holding and operative rule:** A court may not require a defendant to appear visibly shackled before a capital sentencing jury unless circumstances particular to the defendant and proceeding establish an essential security or comparable courtroom need. The State must justify the measure; the trial judge retains reasonable discretion to address actual danger, escape risk and courtroom order, considering supported history and reasonable less prejudicial alternatives without a prescribed hearing format or proof quantum.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies seven direct joins. That same coalition controls Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** Allen preserves the court's power to maintain order, while Holbrook distinguishes ordinary security from an inherently prejudicial display. Riggins shows why a compelled courtroom condition requires actual justification and why its effects on participation may resist reconstruction. Their principles extend to the jury's capital punishment decision. Conviction settles guilt; it does not settle punishment. Visible chains can convey the court's own judgment of present dangerousness at that remaining choice, interfere with participation and consultation, and compromise the dignity and reliability of the proceeding. The dissent's distinction between an accused and a convicted defendant therefore does not answer the due-process concern. The Court preserves real security authority: a judge may use the defendant's supported history and need not await a new incident. Necessity, rather than a routine practice tied to conviction, supplies the constitutional justification.

### The restraints in this proceeding lacked the required justification

**Holding and operative rule:** Conviction alone and generalized juror reassurance do not justify deliberately imposed visible shackling throughout a capital penalty proceeding. Deck's preserved challenge establishes unconstitutional restraint because the trial court identified no individualized necessity and this record supplies no exceptional, indisputable need curing that omission.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The objections and the trial court's responses establish that the jury saw Deck's restraints. Estelle's distinction between compelled appearance and an unobjected choice therefore presents no preservation obstacle. The court's reasons addressed the general condition of a convicted defendant and reassurance, rather than circumstances showing why these restraints were needed for Deck. A conclusory reference to security cannot replace the actual judgment Part I requires.

### The State bears the direct-review harmlessness burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Unjustified visible shackling in capital jury sentencing is constitutional trial error. On a preserved direct-review claim, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt from the full penalty record that the error did not contribute to the death sentence; absent that showing, the defendant receives a lawful new sentencing proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition, adopts the rule and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman supplies the governing burden for preserved constitutional trial error. Smith v. Texas applies that burden to a preserved capital sentencing restriction and distinguishes correction of the penalty proceeding from disturbance of guilt. Deck need not reconstruct the jury's unshackled deliberations or prove each juror's actual response before relief becomes available. The inquiry remains a genuine full-record harmlessness review, rather than automatic reversal. Riggins's medication-specific holding supports attention to unreconstructable effects but did not previously decide this restraint error's Chapman treatment. Missouri's contrary demand that Deck establish actual prejudice placed the burden on the wrong party. The reviewing court must apply the proper burden to the actual sentencing record.

## Precedent Treatment

- Illinois v. Allen preserves necessary courtroom control and supplies the individualized security authority applied at capital sentencing.
- Holbrook v. Flynn supplies the distinction between ordinary security arrangements and inherently prejudicial measures; that distinction governs the visible restraints here.
- Riggins v. Nevada is extended by analogy concerning actual justification and effects that cannot reliably be reconstructed; its medication holding remains confined to that subject.
- Estelle v. Williams preserves the distinction between compelled appearance and a measure accepted without timely objection; Deck repeatedly objected.
- Chapman v. California now governs the preserved visible-restraint error on direct review.
- Smith v. Texas supplies the existing capital-error allocation and sentence-specific remedy; its mitigation rule remains distinct from the restraint prohibition adopted here.

## Law After Decision

Due process now expressly protects capital punishment selection against unjustified visible restraints. Particular necessity permits appropriate security; routine reliance on conviction does not. A preserved violation receives State-borne Chapman review, with sentence-specific relief if harmlessness is not established. The rule separates the trial judge's security determination from the reviewing court's inquiry into the error's effect.

## Separate Writings

Justice Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissents. The guilt-stage restraint cases protected the presumption of innocence, and the medication cases addressed distinct liberty and defense effects. A convicted capital defendant stands in a different posture. In the dissent's view, those authorities do not establish that visible restraints at punishment are constitutionally prejudicial without proof of actual unfairness or serious impairment of assistance in the defense. The writing acknowledges that chains can suggest danger but rejects treating that possible incremental effect as a sufficient constitutional violation on this showing. It would affirm. Its disagreement precedes harmlessness: it does not excuse an acknowledged constitutional error under a different Chapman burden.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Missouri courts for full-record harmless-error review under the State's burden. If that showing fails, the State must provide lawful resentencing or the lawful noncapital alternative. At a new penalty proceeding the judge must determine actual security needs before exposing the jury to restraints and reconsider changed circumstances; the prior general rationale cannot substitute for that determination.

## Source Notes

No. 04-5293; State v. Deck, 136 S.W.3d 481 (Mo. 2004). The objections and trial-court responses establish visible restraints and preservation. Fourteenth Amendment; Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337; Holbrook v. Flynn, 475 U.S. 560; Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501; Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18. The proceeding under review selected punishment after the convictions had become settled.

## Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association; Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165.

Source: [completed Record](../records/johanns-v-livestock-marketing-association_merits_2005-05-23.md#public-projection). Effective date: 2005-05-23.

## Event

Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association, Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165. Argued December 8, 2004; decided May 23, 2005. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit. Both dockets concern the certified First Amendment judgment declaring the Beef Act and Order unconstitutional and restraining all further checkoff collection, while other claims remain pending. The questions are whether compulsory promotion assessments violate the First Amendment when the Government actually controls the advertising and whether asserted producer attribution establishes the facial defect.

The Act imposes a dollar-per-head assessment for promotion and other projects. The Secretary appoints the Beef Board, retains removal authority over the relevant committee, approves content, and participates through department representatives in message development. Producers otherwise make their own marketing decisions; some object to generic promotion and messages promoting imported as well as domestic beef. Government-speech and commercial-justification defenses were presented below.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the reviewed First Amendment judgment and remands in both dockets. Five Justices direct rejection of the compulsory-financing claim on the governmental-message ground; Justice Ginsburg supports formal vacatur and remand on a different proposed commercial-justification ground.

## Holdings

### Actual governmental control identifies the speaker

**Holding and operative rule:** A promotional message is governmental for the presented compelled-subsidy objection when government determines its policy and actually selects, supervises and finally approves its content. The Government must establish that operational direction. Appointment power, a governmental label or ordinary compliance review alone is insufficient; the operational direction established here makes the challenged beef campaign the Government's message.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, supplies five direct joins. That same coalition joins Parts II–IV in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Keller requires examination of the particular expressive function rather than a body's general legal status. The Secretary's actual participation in development and final control over content establish more than a dormant veto over an independent private speaker. Producer drafting and the producers' freedom to run their own businesses do not transfer the final message choice from the officials who exercise it. The accountability objection would additionally require public identification of each campaign as governmental. For this funding claim, however, the relevant responsibility follows actual official message selection; a generic identification of the source of funds does not itself turn the controlled campaign into private authorship. United Foods reserved this operational governmental defense because it had not been developed there. The defense is preserved and supported here, so the Court resolves that reservation on the actual control record.

### A lawful targeted assessment may fund that governmental message

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory financing of a message actually selected and controlled by government does not, merely because assessed producers disagree, violate the First Amendment protection against compelled support of private expression. Financing through a lawful industry assessment rather than general revenue does not change the speaker; compelled personal expression and independently applicable constitutional or statutory constraints retain their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Glickman permits germane promotion within its qualifying collective private program, while United Foods forbids selected private advertising from supplying its own compulsory-association justification. These are different funded relationships. Here actual governmental selection supplies the sufficient distinction, rather than industry benefit, majority approval or the opportunity to counteradvertise. The First Amendment does not require the Government to replace a lawful targeted exaction with general taxation whenever an assessed person opposes its message. Ginsburg's commercial proposal addresses an independent justification for private promotion. The government-message ground resolves this challenge before an application of that proposed alternative becomes necessary; the existing private-support rule continues to govern its category.

### Generic funding identification does not establish the facial attribution claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The statute's compulsory financing and generic identification of producers as the funding source do not establish that every objector must personally endorse the advertising. Because the statute does not require a named dissenter's endorsement, this facial attribution objection supplies no independent basis for the reviewed statutory bar; a properly presented individual-attribution claim requires its own facts and adjudication.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the limited facial holding and express reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Governmental authorship, the source of money and a particular person's apparent endorsement are distinct questions. Barnette and Wooley protect against compelled personal expression, but compulsory payment and a broad funding label do not themselves establish the necessary personal attribution. The Court's ruling therefore disposes of the presented facial theory while preserving the distinct claim of an identified person or organization whose unwanted message is attributed to it. That claim's governing application remains unadjudicated because its factual predicate has not been established here.

### The dependent whole-program decree loses its constitutional premise

**Holding and operative rule:** Both dockets require vacatur of the reviewed constitutional judgment, its dependent declaration against the Act and Order, and the whole-checkoff collection injunction resting on the rejected funding theory. The lower court must remove those dependent restraints while adjudicating the separately pending producer-communications, referendum, statutory-power, administration and accounting claims under their own law, preserving independently supported relief.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the operative mandate; Ginsburg supports the formal vacatur and remand but not this resolution of the constitutional claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual decree restrained all further collection, not merely advertising expenditures. Once the adjudicated compulsory-financing ground fails, it cannot sustain either the advertising or nonadvertising collection restraints. A severability ruling is unnecessary to remove a remedy whose constitutional premise has been rejected. The three opposing Justices' proposed continued restraint depends on sustaining that advertising defect. Their concern about lawful survival of other functions does not preserve the defect after the Court's governmental-message ruling. The lower court must identify the restrictions resting solely on the rejected ground and remove them, while addressing the actual remaining claims.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. United Foods, Inc. retains its independently sufficient private-support rule; its reserved operational government-speech question is resolved for an actually controlled governmental message. Its treatment of the preserved commercial alternative remains distinct from its refusal to reach an undeveloped governmental defense.
- Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott retains its permission for germane promotion within the qualifying collective private program and its conditions.
- Keller v. State Bar of California supplies the functional inquiry; a body's general governmental status alone does not classify each financed message.
- West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette and Wooley v. Maynard retain protection against compelled personal expression; the generic funding identification does not establish personal endorsement here.

## Law After Decision

Actual governmental selection and control defeat the presented private-subsidy objection to financing that message, including through a lawful industry assessment. Nominal supervision of independent private speech is insufficient. The existing rule for compulsory private advertising remains controlling; governmental authorship, financial support and personal attribution remain distinct. The reviewed funding claim is resolved, while the independent pending claims proceed under their own requirements.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court in full and would also recognize a distinct constitutional injury when an unwanted message is objectively and involuntarily attributed to an identified individual or organization. Government control does not answer that personal injury. His position extends beyond the Court's reservation while respecting the absence of an established attribution predicate in this case.

**Justice Ginsburg, concurring in the formal vacatur and remand.** She withholds the governmental-message classification for this producer-presented campaign, without adopting every part of the dissent's affirmative accountability rule. Instead she would permit a commercial subsidy to be justified by a substantial governmental interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit. That approach expressly proposes modifying United Foods's independent-private-support rule; it does not treat economic regulation as outside the First Amendment. Her United Foods dissent supplies the proposed framework, and the consistent presentation of the commercial defense permits its consideration here. Industry welfare identifies the asserted interest, but does not establish all the test's predicates. She would vacate the permanent constitutional decree and return genuinely unresolved application to the actual bench-trial record, supplemented only as ordinary procedure requires, with the Government bearing the burden. Her proposal prescribes neither scientific or quantitative benchmarks nor an automatic new trial. Any interim protection would require ordinary equitable support. She therefore agrees in formal vacatur while withholding the Court's instruction that this constitutional funding question is resolved for the Government.

**Justice Souter, joined by Stevens and Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Actual executive control is accepted, but hidden governmental selection does not provide the publicly identifiable responsibility needed to claim government speech for a campaign presented as the industry's message. Private drafting and targeted financing are not categorically forbidden; accountable governmental authorship must first exist. The writing expressly leaves open whether a discrete group's compelled support could raise another objection even when the Government openly identifies its speech.

Without the governmental justification, Abood, Keller and United Foods require an independently sufficient common function: advertising cannot justify its own compelled association. Producers retain their marketing autonomy and promotion is the principal object. Research spending does not by itself establish an adequate independent function. The preserved commercial defense fails as an independent legal cure even assuming effective promotion and sensible design; the objection is not an empirical finding that advertising failed. Glickman's qualifying collective program and Southworth's separate educational function remain distinct permissible relationships.

The writing would affirm protection against compelled advertising, vacate the categorical nonseverability determination and remand for actual statutory survival and remedial scope. The integrated collection restraint would continue until a conforming order permits a lawful, legally separable operation. Advertising's centrality and the deleted savings clause matter, but the deletion is not an affirmative legislative command resolving every remainder. The lower court would determine whether lawful operations can survive without rewriting the assessment. If they can, relief must permit them while fully protecting against the advertising defect; otherwise the whole-collection restraint may continue on that determination. The research allocation alone authorizes no fractional collection.

## Procedure After Action

Both dockets return through the Eighth Circuit. The lower court must dissolve the declaration and collection restraints resting solely on the rejected compulsory-financing ground, and proceed with the actual remaining claims and independently supported relief. Any separately established attribution injury receives its own appropriate adjudication and remedy.

## Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.; No. 04-163.

Source: [completed Record](../records/lingle-v-chevron-usa-inc_merits_2005-05-23.md#public-projection). Effective date: 2005-05-23.

## Event

Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc., No. 04-163. Argued February 22, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed relief against Hawaii's limit on oil-company rents charged to dealer-operated service stations. Chevron's surviving facial theory alleged that the cap failed substantially to advance the State's objective. The lower courts evaluated competing economic predictions about the cap's effects. The question is whether that efficacy inquiry independently identifies a taking and supports the judgment.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment resting on the substantial-advancement taking theory and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower court must remove the declaration or injunction resting solely on the rejected taking theory and enter judgment consistent with its rejection. Any independently preserved matter must satisfy its own substantive and procedural predicates.

## Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the unanimous opinion of the Court.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | All eight other Justices | Parts I–III: independent efficacy predicate, precise precedent change and surviving rules, claim-specific judgment |

## Holdings

### Regulatory efficacy does not independently establish a taking

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure substantially to advance a legitimate governmental objective is not, by itself, a taking requiring compensation under the Fifth Amendment. A claimant must establish the relevant property burden or demanded dedication under the applicable taking rule; an economic-efficacy trial cannot substitute for that showing, whether the challenged regulation is general or an individual permit denial.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion supplies nine direct joins; the same coalition joins Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** The compensation command addresses the nature and burden of governmental interference with property. An ineffective regulation may impose little loss, while an effective one may take property and require payment. Efficacy cannot distinguish those cases. Loretto identifies a compelled permanent occupation; Lucas addresses complete loss of beneficial use; Penn Central and Tahoe-Sierra examine the continuing property interest and the actual regulatory burden. Nollan and Dolan address the distinct demand to surrender identified property as a condition of permission. None makes ordinary economic disagreement the constitutional event. Chevron's strongest objection is reliance on the real purpose-relationship holding in Del Monte Dunes. Restricting that holding to individual denials would retain the same source error: ineffective means do not become a taking merely because they concern one parcel. Independently unlawful or arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable constitutional protections, including due process, with their own elements and remedies. Compensation is not a license to undertake action forbidden by those independent protections.

### The earlier efficacy holding is overruled at its precise scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Agins and Del Monte Dunes are overruled insofar as they recognize lack of substantial advancement as an independent taking predicate; Del Monte Dunes's allocation of that displaced substantive issue likewise ceases to govern. Its final judgment and award, independent complete-deprivation ground, municipal compensation rules, finality rulings, and constitutional jury entitlement and task allocation for surviving legal claims remain in force.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion expressly changes the earlier controlling rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Del Monte Dunes sustained an undifferentiated award on sufficient evidence supporting both submitted alternatives. Its purpose-relationship ground was an actual holding. The Court therefore confronts and withdraws that ground instead of dismissing it as dictum. Stare decisis carries particular weight where property and transactions are arranged under judicial rules, but it does not justify retaining a liability predicate unrelated to the burden that the compensation command identifies. Preserving final judgments and independent valid grounds respects those interests without perpetuating the mismatch in pending and future cases. Rejecting the substantive efficacy issue also removes the occasion for assigning that issue to a jury; it does not abolish a jury right for an otherwise valid legal damages claim. The replacement rule governs this case and other cases still open on direct review where the issue is properly presented. The completed Del Monte Dunes compensation judgment remains final.

### The surviving property rules govern distinct interferences

**Holding and operative rule:** Compelled permanent physical occupation follows Loretto; total elimination of beneficial use follows Lucas, subject to substantiated background property or nuisance limits already inherent in ownership; other regulatory burdens follow Penn Central's inquiry into economic impact, interference with distinct investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action. For an individualized development-permit condition demanding identified property interests, the government must establish Nollan's essential nexus and demonstrate Dolan's rough proportionality between the dedication's nature and extent and the development's effects, allowing reasonable estimates without exact mathematical equivalence or unique attribution to the project.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion preserves these rules as the operative alternatives to the rejected predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The court must classify the actual interference before choosing its test. Tahoe-Sierra preserves examination of a continuing property interest rather than manufacture of total loss by temporal segmentation. Lucas requires the State to substantiate an antecedent ownership limitation rather than rely on a new legislative harm label. Dolan requires a connection between the nature and extent of demanded property and the development's effects, a distinct burden that ordinary scrutiny of regulatory effectiveness cannot replace. These rules retain their own triggers, proof and consequences.

### Chevron's selected theory cannot sustain the judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment resting solely on failure of substantial advancement must be reversed, and relief based on that predicate must be removed. Chevron's surviving efficacy theory therefore fails; independently preserved claims require their own legal and procedural basis.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion supplies the judgment and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The economic testimony addressed whether Hawaii's cap would achieve its objectives. That inquiry establishes neither the nature nor the magnitude of a taking. Chevron's reviewed theory does not invoke a physical occupation, total deprivation or demanded conveyance. The lower court must reject the chosen predicate and identify any independently preserved matter actually remaining. A distinct due-process or compensation claim is not presented for decision here, and a remand cannot create one by relabeling the failed theory.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agins v. City of Tiburon is overruled only insofar as lack of substantial advancement supplies an independent taking ground.
- Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey is overruled at its accepted purpose-relationship alternative and the assignment of that displaced issue. Its final award, independent complete-deprivation holding, municipal compensation route, damages and finality rules, and jury entitlement and allocation for surviving claims remain controlling.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp. retains the categorical compelled-permanent-occupation rule.
- Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council retains the total-beneficial-use rule and substantiated background-title limitation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City retains the contextual property-burden inquiry.
- Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency retains its continuing-property treatment and rejection of automatic temporal segmentation.
- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission and Dolan v. City of Tigard retain their distinct nexus and rough-proportionality requirements for demanded property dedications.

## Law After Decision

Regulatory-taking liability can no longer rest solely on a mismatch between a regulation and its asserted objective. That change reaches general rules and particular denials, while the recognized occupation, deprivation, regulatory-burden and dedication rules remain available on their own predicates. Independently arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable due-process law. The new rule applies in this case and properly presented cases still open on direct review; final compensation judgments retain their finality.

## Lofton v. Secretary of the Department of Children & Family Services; Eleventh Circuit, 358 F.3d 804.

Source: [completed Record](../records/lofton-v-secretary-of-the-department-of-children-and-family-services_merits_2005-05-23.md#public-projection). Effective date: 2005-05-23.

## Event

Lofton v. Secretary of the Department of Children & Family Services, review of 358 F.3d 804. Submitted on the merits; decided May 23, 2005. On review of the Eleventh Circuit's January 28, 2004 judgment sustaining Florida's categorical adoption exclusion, with rehearing denied July 21, 2004. The questions concern equal protection and the alternative claim that the bar impermissibly burdens established foster and guardianship families. The plaintiffs seek removal of the statutory barrier and genuine consideration for adoption, rather than a general entitlement to adopt.

Florida permits gay adults to foster children or serve as guardians but excludes them from adoption, while unmarried heterosexual applicants remain eligible. Lofton raised Doe from infancy. Houghton cared for Roe from age four and became co-guardian after the biological father's placement; the father later consented to termination of parental rights. Lofton declined guardianship unless it led toward adoption because it would sacrifice foster-care payments and threaten Medicaid coverage. These circumstances explain the claimed importance of permanency without settling any particular adoption's merits.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at merits submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment sustaining the categorical exclusion and remands for declaratory and injunctive relief under the Equal Protection Clause.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

Florida must accept and evaluate applications without the categorical orientation bar in §63.042(3), under otherwise lawful child-specific eligibility and best-interest criteria. Relief corrects the statewide eligibility policy for all applicants governed by it unless Florida establishes that a narrower order fully cures the same defect. Existing placements and guardianship rights retain their present legal scope.

## Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Parts I–III: existing equality rule, categorical exclusion, fitted relief and alternative-claim nonreach |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Proposed rejection of intermediate scrutiny and separate rejection of the existing-family claim |

## Holdings

### The actual classification must satisfy existing intermediate scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not categorically exclude gay and lesbian adults from adoption unless it demonstrates a substantial relationship between that classification and a genuine important child-welfare objective. Intermediate scrutiny governs sexual-orientation classifications, including deliberate conduct proxies; ordinary civil evidence and supported professional judgment may carry the State's burden, while stereotype, private bias, moral disapproval and merely conceivable post hoc reasons cannot.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, applies the binding Shahar and Equality Foundation standard. The same seven join Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** Shahar requires genuine justification for the actual distinction and extends its protection to deliberate status proxies. Equality Foundation applies that rule outside employment. Describing adoption as a statutory benefit or construing the bar in terms of same-sex conduct does not remove the classification from that law. Child welfare is an important responsibility, and predictive professional judgments deserve consideration. The standard requires neither scientific certainty nor least restrictive means or an individualized exception in every rule. It does require substantial fit. The dissent would replace this existing standard with ordinary rationality. Its conceivable future-household rationale does not answer the burden under the governing rule, and the particular adoption setting supplies no sufficient reason to abandon that rule.

### Florida's categorical bar fails on the developed record

**Holding and operative rule:** Florida's categorical orientation exclusion lacks the substantial relationship to child welfare that intermediate scrutiny requires and is unconstitutional. The State must evaluate applicants using supported parenting-capacity, safety, stability and child-specific welfare criteria, without treating orientation or a deliberate status proxy as categorical incapacity.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition, resolves the classification on the developed record.

**Controlling explanation:** A preference for a presently two-parent, mother-and-father household does not describe a statute that permits unmarried heterosexual applicants but excludes every gay applicant. The possibility that a heterosexual single applicant may later marry does not substantiate why each excluded applicant is unsuitable for consideration. Florida's actual reliance on gay foster parents and guardians also prevents treating orientation itself as demonstrated incapacity, while leaving each adoption to its own evidence. Foster care and adoption differ, especially in permanence; that distinction does not turn an unsupported orientation inference into substantial fit. Longstanding care relationships sharpen the mismatch without proving any applicant entitled to a particular placement. The developed justifications can be resolved under the governing standard, so the remand implements the settled equality ruling. Lawrence's reservation of civil status does not suspend independently established equal-protection law.

### Equal consideration and effective policy relief complete the decision

**Holding and operative rule:** Prospective relief must remove the categorical bar for applicants governed by the policy and provide genuine consideration under lawful individual adoption criteria; Florida bears the burden of showing that narrower relief fully cures the policy defect. Because that equality relief affords the complete requested eligibility opportunity, the alternative substantive-due-process claim concerning existing families is unnecessary to the judgment and is neither accepted nor rejected.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the seven-Justice Court coalition, supplies the remedy and the ground for alternative-claim nonreach.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson v. Spear requires a remedy that corrects the policy's unlawful operation rather than only grants a named plaintiff an exception that leaves the defect intact. The Department must remove the disqualifying criterion from its instructions and decisions, notify responsible personnel and provide prompt lawful reconsideration of operative rejections resting only on that basis within ordinary applicable procedures. The district court must set and enforce a compliance schedule. Officials retain responsibility for actual fitness, existing parental rights and the particular child's interests, but cannot restore the invalid categorical assumption as an unexamined fitness rule. Troxel and Smith v. Organization of Foster Families address the legal sources, expectations and interference relevant to family liberty. Here equality affords the requested opportunity in full, making an independent family-liberty ruling unnecessary.

## Precedent Treatment

- Shahar v. Bowers is applied: existing intermediate scrutiny and its deliberate-status-proxy rule govern the adoption classification.
- Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati v. City of Cincinnati is applied: the same equality protection extends beyond employment and rejects unsupported categorical disability.
- Romer v. Evans remains an independent ordinary-review floor, not a replacement for the governing tier.
- Lawrence v. Texas retains its private-intimacy protection and civil-status reservation; that reservation does not curtail the independently applicable equality rule.
- Anderson v. Spear supplies effective common-policy relief and the State's opportunity to establish a fully curative narrower decree.
- Troxel v. Granville and Smith v. Organization of Foster Families retain their distinct family-relationship principles; complete equality relief makes the alternative liberty ground unnecessary here.

## Law After Decision

The categorical adoption disqualification and deliberate proxies for it cannot govern eligibility. Florida must offer real, prompt consideration under supported child-specific criteria. The ruling applies the existing intermediate-scrutiny rule to the statewide exclusion; it preserves professional evaluation, relevant individual differences and ordinary adoption procedures. The established-family due-process theory remains undecided because the equality judgment supplies the requested eligibility relief.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents on both claims. The writing acknowledges that Shahar makes intermediate scrutiny the current rule and expressly proposes rejecting that tier to apply its maintained ordinary-rationality position. Under that proposed standard, responsibility for permanent placements and the possibility that single heterosexual applicants may later form mother-and-father households sufficiently justify the distinction despite imperfect coverage. The dissent does not represent conceivable reasons as satisfying intermediate scrutiny.

On the independent family-liberty claim, the dissent distinguishes protection of an existing family's care and custody from a constitutional command to create adoptive status. Troxel protects a fit parent's established authority; Smith v. Organization of Foster Families requires attention to the statutory origins and expectations of foster relationships. The challenged law does not itself remove the children or dissolve current care arrangements. The biological father's consent in Houghton's case removes an obstacle but does not establish every legal adoption condition. Longstanding bonds and the practical financial and permanence interests are substantial, yet in the dissent's view they do not require conversion to a new status. It would affirm both claims on those grounds.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for a declaration and an injunction enforcing equal eligibility. The district court must establish and enforce the compliance schedule, including corrected instructions, notice to responsible officials and prompt lawful reconsideration within ordinary procedures. Actual applications remain subject to child-specific determinations, existing parental rights and lawful adoption requirements.

## Medellín v. Dretke; No. 04-5928.

## Event

Medellín v. Dretke, No. 04-5928. Argued March 28, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, which denied a certificate of appealability from denial of federal habeas relief. The granted questions concern whether the International Court of Justice's Avena judgment binds a federal court to provide review and reconsideration of the Vienna Convention claim, or should receive effect through comity and uniform treaty interpretation.

Avena was delivered March 31, 2004; the Fifth Circuit denied certification May 20, 2004. After the grant, the President issued a February 28, 2005 memorandum concerning state-court compliance. ## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The writ of certiorari is dismissed as improvidently granted. The Court issues a per curiam explanation. The dismissal rests on the materially changed vehicle, not approval of the Fifth Circuit's substantive treaty reasoning. The Court does not publish an overall tally or a full disposition lineup; the separate writings state their authors' public positions.

## Judgment & Remedy

This grant terminates. The Fifth Circuit's certificate denial remains in place, and the separate Texas proceeding retains its pending status. Dismissal does not affirm the lower court's categorical treaty rationale. A later request for review must arise from an actual judgment and satisfy its own timing and jurisdictional requirements.

## Opinion Topology

PER CURIAM. GINSBURG, J., filed an opinion concurring in the dismissal. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion. BREYER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS, J., joined. SOUTER, J., filed a dissenting opinion.

The per curiam explanation addresses the changed vehicle and the retained boundaries of certificate review. The concurrence and dissents state distinct preferences concerning retention and a limited certificate-review remand.

## Holdings

### The changed vehicle warrants discretionary dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court dismisses this writ because the new, unadjudicated domestic implementation ground and pending state proceeding materially change the vehicle for deciding the granted international-judgment questions, while the federal gateways remain independently unresolved. This discretionary disposition determines this grant and does not establish a substantive bar to the pending state claim or a properly presented later petition.

**Authority:** The Court's per curiam explanation supplies the reason for its procedural disposition; it decides no treaty or presidential-power merits question.

**Controlling explanation:** Slack permits review of a judicial certificate denial while separating that review from issuance of a certificate and underlying habeas relief. Miller-El requires a threshold assessment under the applicable restrictions, not completed merits proof, and retains the statutory constitutional component. Reed likewise distinguishes federal-law character from collateral entitlement. The older certificate judgment did not adjudicate the President's newly asserted source of implementation, now before the Texas court. Deciding that source here first would combine different records and different legal gateways. The dissents reasonably seek to preserve review by a definite hold or correct the lower court's erroneous use of Breard. But a remand of the older certificate dispute would retain the independent gateways and create renewed parallel proceedings while the new domestic source develops; a hold would retain the submitted international questions in the same altered vehicle. The Court instead ends this grant. That choice neither predicts state relief nor treats the federal controversy as moot.

### The unresolved substantive questions retain their proper channels

**Holding and operative rule:** The dismissal leaves Avena's domestic binding effect, the asserted comity ground and the memorandum's authority undecided; the memorandum was not adjudicated below, and the treaty merits are not reached in this vehicle disposition. Existing certificate, exhaustion and habeas requirements retain their separate operation in proceedings where they apply.

**Authority:** The limits are express parts of the per curiam disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Current Breard and Paraguay decided confined interim requests, preserving final questions of enforceability, default, prejudice and remedy. They cannot supply the categorical merits bar attributed to them below. Section 2253(c) still requires the actual constitutional showing; following a procedural dismissal, Slack requires reasonable debate on both the constitutional claim and the procedural ruling. A debatable treaty question alone does not replace that requirement. The other habeas restrictions and the domestic source of an eventual remedy remain distinct issues for a proper adjudicated record. Dismissal selects a vehicle without resolving those issues against either party.

- Reed v. Farley retains its distinction between federal-law jurisdiction and the collateral standard for the nonconstitutional claim it decided; it supplies no categorical treaty exclusion.
- Breard v. Greene and Republic of Paraguay v. Gilmore retain their confined interim force and reservations concerning treaty enforceability, default, prejudice and remedy.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Ginsburg, concurring in the dismissal.** She would prefer retaining the submitted case through June 20, with a status submission by June 13 or promptly after an earlier state disposition, so that the Court could reassess the vehicle by a further actual order. **Justice O'Connor, dissenting.** She would vacate the certificate denial and remand for application of the actual certificate framework.
