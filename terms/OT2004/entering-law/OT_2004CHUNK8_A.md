#### Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-453.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For reviewability of the original categorical-prohibition period, see Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, June 29, 1992, No. 91-453, under Federal Courts — Finality and earlier-period takings claims.

##### Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use

**Holding:** A land-use regulation that removes all economically beneficial use of the relevant property effects a taking requiring just compensation unless the prohibited use was already outside the owner's title under background state property or nuisance law. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate an applicable background limitation that would independently prohibit the claimed use in the property's circumstances; a new legislative harm declaration alone does not suffice, and ordinary adjudication supplies the proof requirements without a special heightened quantum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor and Thomas, supplies five votes for the complete rule and its explanation. Kennedy supports reversal through an expectations-sensitive analysis, without joining this categorical formulation. No Marks combination is required or used.

**Material application:** Total loss is the trial finding assumed for the legal question, not a Council concession or appellate affirmance of value. A new legislative declaration of harm cannot replace substantiated background law; changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance principles.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the trial court's total-loss finding to decide the presented legal issue; it makes no new appraisal and does not resolve the Council's preserved challenge to that finding. No unconditional entitlement to the trial award, final application of state nuisance law, right to anticipated profits, new parcel rule, or disposition of post-amendment permission is adopted. Legitimate environmental purposes remain sufficient to justify regulation; compensation is a distinct consequence. The Court overrules no earlier decision.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The no-compensation judgment is reversed and remanded for background property and nuisance law and the remaining defenses, including the sustained extent and duration of loss. Any compensation covers the actual taking period; the $1,232,387.50 trial award is not reinstated automatically.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pennsylvania Coal Co. v. Mahon, 260 U.S. 393 (1922): applies its recognition that regulation can go too far despite valid public purposes; does not attribute today's exact categorical rule to that decision.
- Agins v. City of Tiburon, 447 U.S. 255 (1980): specifies and extends its complete-economic-use formulation into the rule and necessary exception stated here; does not adjudicate its distinct legitimate-interest formulation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104 (1978): preserves contextual analysis for partial deprivation, including economic impact, expectations and the character of governmental action; no percentage automatically defeats or establishes compensation.
- Mugler v. Kansas, 123 U.S. 623 (1887), Hadacheck v. Sebastian, 239 U.S. 394 (1915), Miller v. Schoene, 276 U.S. 272 (1928), and Goldblatt v. Hempstead, 369 U.S. 590 (1962): limits a broad noxious-use reading that would make any legislative harm characterization conclusive despite complete beneficial-use deprivation; preserves their actual outcomes and lawful protective regulation.
- Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470 (1987): distinguishes its productive-enterprise and partial-interest setting; no reversal of its actual holding or adoption of a new denominator follows.
- Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): applies existing rules and understandings to identify the protected property interest; does not decide a procedural due-process claim.
- Scranton v. Wheeler, 179 U.S. 141 (1900): applies its antecedent-title-limit principle; a previously existing servitude is distinct from a newly imposed public burden.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419 (1982), and Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164 (1979): preserve physical-occupation and exclusion protections and illuminate the difference between existing limitations and a new imposed servitude; this decision concerns regulatory loss.
- Webb's Fabulous Pharmacies, Inc. v. Beckwith, 449 U.S. 155 (1980): applies the prohibition on converting private property into public property by governmental assertion alone.

#### Dolan v. City of Tigard, 512 U.S. 374

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-518.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### The City must justify the nature and extent of each demanded dedication

**Holding:** When an individualized development permit requires conveyance of identified property interests, the government must first establish an essential nexus to the legitimate interest supporting the restriction and then demonstrate rough proportionality between the dedication's nature and extent and the development's impacts. Some individualized determination is required; reasonable estimates suffice, and neither exact mathematical equivalence nor proof that the condition is uniquely attributable to the project is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt this rule and its government justification burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission, 483 U.S. 825: followed as to essential nexus and extended by the nature-and-extent inquiry and government justification burden; purpose connection remains necessary but is not sufficient.
- Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164: applied to the distinct property interest in excluding the public; an asserted public benefit does not erase that interest.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp., 458 U.S. 419: applied for the distinction between compelled physical occupation and ordinary use regulation; no completed occupation is found here.
- Armstrong v. United States, 364 U.S. 40: applied to the allocation of public burdens; it does not establish a numerical exaction formula.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104: distinguished as ordinary regulatory-taking analysis; its force for use restrictions remains unchanged.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey, June 16, 1997, Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-16248 and 94-16313: the special dedication burden does not govern an ordinary permit-denial claim.

##### The public-greenway dedication is insufficiently justified

**Holding:** Although the increased runoff supplies an essential nexus to flood control, a requirement to keep floodplain land open does not by itself justify surrendering the right to exclude the public. Because the City has not made the required individualized showing connecting that additional public-access interest to the development's impacts, the judgment sustaining the public-greenway dedication must be reversed and the condition returned for lawful treatment on remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; all five support both the rule and this application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser Aetna v. United States, 444 U.S. 164: applied to preserve the difference between leaving land open and compelling access by the public.
- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission, 483 U.S. 825: applied to the actual interest demanded, with the further proportionality rule stated above; lawful regulation does not itself authorize an insufficiently justified conveyance.

##### The pathway dedication requires more than a possible traffic benefit

**Holding:** Increased traffic and the usefulness of alternative transportation establish the purpose nexus for a pedestrian and bicycle route, but the finding that this route could offset some traffic does not adequately connect the extent of the demanded strip to the development's impacts. The City must make an individualized effort to relate the dedication's nature and extent to the mitigation it can reasonably provide, without exact arithmetic; the judgment sustaining this dedication is reversed and remanded.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices adopt the application and its estimation allowance.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission, 483 U.S. 825: applied to distinguish the conceded transportation purpose from the insufficient showing supporting this particular dedication.
- Armstrong v. United States, 364 U.S. 40: applied to the burden imposed for a public improvement; no exact valuation or compensation amount is decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No conveyance occurred and no completed taking or compensation amount is determined. The separate 15-percent open-space rule and the constitutionality and unadjudicated consequences of the variance procedure remain outside decision; failure to propose an alternative is not made a forfeiture rule. Ordinary zoning, monetary exactions and generally applicable legislative conditions remain reserved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Oregon Supreme Court's judgment sustaining both challenged dedications is reversed and the case remanded. If the City retains a dedication demand, it must supply the required constitutional relationship. Lawful regulation, alternatives, a justified dedication and compensated acquisition remain available. No unconditional permit, completed-taking finding or compensation is ordered; the separate open-space rule and variance-procedure constitutionality are not adjudicated.

#### Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-16248 and 94-16313; district court No. CV-86-05042-CAL.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### Dolan’s demanded-conveyance burden does not govern an ordinary permit denial

**Holding:** The government must establish nexus and individualized rough proportionality when it demands conveyance of identified property interests as a permit condition. That special burden does not govern this ordinary permit-denial claim; the Ninth Circuit's contrary proportionality discussion supplies no independent ground to disturb the judgment because it was not part of the jury instructions and was unnecessary to verdict review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Parts I–III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual jury alternatives were the received Agins reasonable-relationship formulation and complete economic deprivation. Lucas requires total loss and permits a government-substantiated background property or nuisance limitation; a newly declared environmental harm alone is insufficient. Penn Central continues to govern partial deprivation. Repeated denials, legitimate environmental purposes and a later sale price are relevant but not conclusive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Dolan is confined to compelled conveyance of identified property interests, without its special government burden extending to ordinary denial. Lucas and Penn Central retain their separate fields. Agins is applied at the actual instruction’s scope without comprehensive approval or replacement of its formulations.

##### An established municipal section 1983 action permits compensation for the proved taking

**Holding:** Section 1983 permits compensatory damages for a proved taking attributable to a municipality's own qualifying policy or final decision, subject to the action's constitutional and procedural predicates. Recovery is limited to the legally attributable extent and duration of the deprivation, accounting for sale proceeds or other recovery to prevent duplication; prospective relief alone does not extinguish a completed compensable injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Parts I–III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No anticipated profits are guaranteed, ordinary permit processing is not made compensable, and municipal policy attribution, causation and proof of loss remain necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Monell requires the municipality’s own policy or final decision; McMillian preserves function-specific institutional attribution without identifying a new Monterey policymaker. First English permits compensation for the actual temporary-taking period; Franklin distinguishes relief in an established action from creating the action or proving loss.

##### Sufficient evidence on both submitted taking grounds sustains the single award

**Holding:** The City is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law or a new trial on the reviewed grounds because sufficient evidence supports both undifferentiated taking alternatives under the instructions actually given. The taking verdict therefore sustains the existing single $1,450,000 compensatory award without decision of the separate equal-protection theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Parts V–VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices.

**Material application:** Evidence supported the combined conditions’ elimination of feasible development and the unreasonable relationship of the fixed denial to the specified purposes, including compliance with fifteen conditions and the habitat and access evidence. The later sale for $800,000 above purchase price did not conclusively prove productive use during the earlier period or payment for its loss. Neither denial of the preferred housing proposal alone nor disagreement among experts would suffice.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No substantiated antecedent title limitation independently required the entire deprivation found. Equal-protection objections remained preserved but need not be decided because both taking alternatives support the award; the unappealed substantive-due-process ruling is untouched.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the $1,450,000 award and denial of a new trial, 5–4. Affirmance activates the owners’ conditional waiver of additional damages; no second award, permit or construction order is directed.

For this case’s finality, statutory-jury and Seventh Amendment holdings, see Federal Courts, “Land-use finality and the civil jury,” decided June 16, 1997, Ninth Circuit Nos. 94-16248 and 94-16313.

#### Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, 535 U.S. 302

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1167.
**Decided:** April 23, 2002.

##### A regulatory moratorium is not a categorical taking merely through temporal segmentation

**Holding:** For a temporary development restriction without physical appropriation, an owner cannot establish Lucas's total-deprivation category merely by defining the relevant property as the exact time during which the restriction bars development. The continuing property interest must be assessed as such; economic impact, expectations, duration and governmental character remain subject to Penn Central when that claim is properly presented, and a temporary label supplies no immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly support the rule, preservation limit and affirmance.

**Material application:** The claim concerns Ordinance 81-5 from August 24, 1981 and Resolution 83-21 from August 27, 1983 through April 25, 1984. The former allowed case-by-case approval for some single-family homes on Nevada-side Class 1–3 lots, but not stream-environment-zone lands or California parcels. The restrictions therefore did not identically bar every parcel.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No thirty-two-month safe harbor, environmental exception or numerical duration ceiling follows. Physical occupation and appropriation of a leasehold remain distinct. The owners abandoned their rejected Penn Central theory; later plans and judicial restraints have separate causal and procedural predicates outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The rejection of the categorical claim is affirmed, with no compensation on temporal segmentation and no remand reviving the abandoned contextual claim or adjudicating separate later periods.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council:** Its complete-deprivation category and genuine background-law exception remain. The Court rejects using the exact moratorium period alone as the relevant property for this regulatory claim; Lucas did not settle every temporal denominator.
- **Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104:** Its combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character inquiry remains available for properly preserved claims, but this abandoned theory is not revived.
- **First English Evangelical Lutheran Church of Glendale v. County of Los Angeles, 482 U.S. 304:** Compensation is required for a temporary taking once independently established; it does not make every temporary development bar a taking.
- **Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470, and Andrus v. Allard, 444 U.S. 51:** Applied to the prohibition on manufacturing complete deprivation from a selected burdened strand of a continuing interest.
- **Palazzolo v. Rhode Island:** Its distinction between meaningful remaining ownership and the separate contextual inquiry remains. Neither regulatory notice nor a valid environmental purpose independently defeats compensation, and its unresolved general parcel question is not converted into a contrary temporal rule.

#### Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1184.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### Germane generic promotion may be funded as part of this collective marketing program

**Holding:** A comprehensive agricultural marketing program may require regulated producers to finance generic, nonideological product promotion germane to its substantially collective economic functions when the message is not attributed to dissenting producers and they remain free to advertise and criticize independently. On this combination of facts, Central Hudson's framework for restrictions on a speaker's commercial message does not require invalidation of the assessment; common benefit or majority approval alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt this program-specific ground, its qualifications and reversal with remand.

**Current-force modification:** United States v. United Foods, Inc., June 25, 2001, resolves the reserved advertising-centered independent-market setting: selected private promotion cannot itself justify compulsory association when no independently sufficient collective function supports it. Industry benefit, free-riding concerns, majority approval and freedom to counteradvertise do not cure that defect. Glickman's integrated-program permission remains intact, as do separately justified programs such as Southworth's neutral educational forum; United Foods does not decide the unpresented operational government-speech theory or require comprehensive agricultural regulation for every valid subsidy.

**Material application:** The orders already replace substantial independent price, quality, supply and marketing decisions with collective regulation. Promotion is a germane expense of that system; the decision does not find generic advertising superior to individual advertising.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Compelled funding can implicate speech without ideology or attribution. Advertising-only arrangements in otherwise independent markets, ideological or false content, viewpoint penalties, personal attribution and interference with independent expression remain outside the holding; there is no universal commercial-speech or economic-regulation exception.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed First Amendment judgment is reversed, 6–3, and remanded. Unreviewed or resolved statutory and administrative matters are not reopened, and no collection award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abood, Keller and Lehnert supply germane common-function principles, while Gibson preserves safeguards for disputed nongermane dues. Hurley is distinguished because no private expressive product or authorship is compelled. Rosenberger reserved compulsory-fee questions. Central Hudson, 44 Liquormart and Turner retain their speech-restriction rules without requiring an efficacy trial for this germane collective expense.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. United Foods, Inc., June 25, 2001 — compelled private advertising in an otherwise independent market.

#### United States v. United Foods, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-276.
**Decided:** June 25, 2001.

##### Whether independent mushroom producers may be compelled to finance generic private advertising

**Holding:** Government may not compel otherwise independent producers to finance the selected private generic promotion here merely because the campaign benefits the industry, overcomes free riding, receives majority approval or leaves producers free to advertise separately. Where no independently sufficient collective function justifies the required association, the advertising cannot supply its own justification; the challenged mushroom advertising obligation therefore violates the First Amendment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; six directly adopt Parts I–III.

**Material application:** Generic promotion is the principal activity for which these independent competitors must associate. The program lacks the comprehensive collective marketing arrangements that supplied the independent function in Glickman.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No finding of failed advertising or empirical Central Hudson violation is necessary. The Court does not treat the commercial-speech argument as waived. Genuine government speech, factual commercial disclosures, separate health and safety purposes, and independently lawful research or information assessments remain outside this advertising holding. The operational government-speech review question is recorded under Federal Courts — Review discretion over a new operational defense.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit's judgment rejecting the challenged advertising assessment is affirmed; the consolidated enforcement proceeding must conform to that constitutional holding. Under §6106(b)(3), a contrary-to-law administrative ruling must be remanded to the Secretary either for a lawful ruling directed by the reviewing court or for the further action the law requires; unresolved accounting, severability and monetary relief remain governed by their own legal and factual predicates. Mere pendency of an administrative petition does not impede enforcement under §6106(b)(4), but that provision does not authorize collection of an adjudged unconstitutional obligation or settle independent interim-relief requirements. No automatic refund, fixed monetary award or invalidation of other Council activities follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Glickman is distinguished at its expressly reserved independent-market boundary; its integrated-program permission survives. Abood and Keller supply the independently justified common function and germaneness requirements. Southworth preserves its distinct, neutral educational function and shows that collective agricultural regulation is not the only possible justification. Central Hudson remains effective in its field, but commercial advancement and fit do not answer this separate private-subsidy defect.

#### Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1608.
**Decided:** 1997-04-28.

For the separate questions in Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party, 1997-04-28, see Federal Courts — Mootness and conditional reenactment.

##### The categorical ban is not justified as applied to this consenting nomination

**Holding:** Minnesota may not apply its categorical prohibition to prevent this qualified minor party from timely nominating a consenting, eligible candidate, and having that nomination officially recognized on the ballot, merely because he also seeks or holds another party's nomination. In this application the burden on the Party's candidate selection and on its members' ability to vote for their nominee under its name is substantial but not severe, and the interests in ballot integrity, defined nomination choices and a stable electoral system, though legitimate, are not shown to require the ban under Anderson and Burdick. When a qualified party genuinely nominates an eligible, consenting candidate, the State must provide a meaningful official means of recognizing that nomination; it may regulate ballot presentation through separate party lines or a single consolidated listing, require consent and prevent any ballot from being counted more than once.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The challenged 1994 §204B.06 subdivision 1(b) barred an affidavit when another affidavit was on file for any office at the same primary or next general election. Section 204B.04 subdivision 2 barred petition nomination for the same office by a primary candidate, subject to two distinct exceptions. Under §204D.10 subdivision 2, if any major-party primary candidate received ten percent of the average vote for the party's state officers at the last state general election in the relevant district, its highest-vote candidates became nominees; if none met that threshold, none were nominated and all could seek petition nomination under §§204B.07–.09. The relevant state officers were governor, lieutenant governor, secretary of state, state auditor, state treasurer and attorney general. The §204B.13 subdivision 4 nonpartisan-vacancy exception allowed a petition within one week of the vacancy and no later than four calendar days before election; an eligible voter could sign despite voting in the primary or signing another petition. Neither exception applied to Dawkins. The distinct ban on nomination by two major parties is not decided.

The conditional replacement permitted a major-party and one-or-more-minor-party nomination for the same partisan office with written consent of the parties' state chairs; petition nomination also required the candidate's written consent. Losing the major-party primary forfeited the simultaneous minor-party nominations, and a party candidate could not also file as an independent. It retained the nonpartisan-vacancy exception, barred a defeated major-party primary candidate except to fill a §204B.13 vacancy, and repealed the old primary-turnout provision. Its affidavit rule referred to any other office, required petition acceptance and the consent forms, and contained a distinct dual-office exception for a soil-and-water district supervisor outside Anoka, Hennepin, Ramsey and Washington counties and a mayor/council or town-supervisor office in a city or town of no more than 2,500 people wholly or partly within the district. That different-office exception is not fusion. A multiply nominated candidate's name appeared once with party names; major/minor nomination used the major-party position and multiple-minor nomination the first filing party's position. Votes for multiply nominated candidates were excluded from the specified party-status qualification or retention counts. These features neither establish compliance in this case nor receive independent constitutional approval.

The Court recognizes no right to duplicate votes, separate party vote totals or a particular ballot design, decides no party-specific counting rule, and adjudicates no independent challenge to consent, counting, format, support, deadline or sore-loser rules. Whether a demonstrated threat to effective governing, connected to fusion, could justify a narrower limit is reserved; a required other-party consent, the independent validity of chapter 419's conditions and the distinct two-major-party prohibition are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Anderson and Burdick retain contextual burden, precise-interest and fit review; Burdick's protection of a genuine choice of an actual candidate and its rejection of arbitrary-message tallies confirm that this nomination is not a fictional vote. Norman's protection of authorized party identity against categorical bars broader than the State's concern applies. Colorado Republican confirms that shared candidate support does not erase a party's independent identity, without imposing campaign-expenditure scrutiny on ballot designations. Tashjian and Eu protect the party's choice of its standard bearer; Storer, Jenness and Munro permit orderly selection, support and preventive rules but do not dispense with a connection between a substantial burden and the interests invoked. No strict-scrutiny requirement, new scrutiny tier, voter-incapacity premise, incumbent-protection rationale or universal fusion right is adopted.

##### Affirmance does not engage the enacted expiration condition, and no election is reopened

**Holding:** Chapter 419 §10 makes its amendments effective for the 1996 state primary and thereafter, suspends them while the McKenna judgment is stayed or its mandate recalled, and provides that reversal causes the amendments to expire and prior law to revive. Affirmance does not engage that expiration-and-revival condition: the amendments remain effective according to their terms, and the old prohibition may not be applied to this nomination. Further proceedings under the affirmed judgment assume no unproved stay, recall or continuous intervening statutory regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Parts I and III, joined by all eight Associates; nine direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 5–4. The old prohibition may not be applied to this consenting nomination, and relief is prospective. Chapter 419's amendments remain effective according to their terms; their independent validity is not decided. The completed 1994 election is not reopened, and no intervening filing is adjudicated.

#### California Democratic Party v. Jones, 530 U.S. 567 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-401.

**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### 1. Compulsory outsider selection of an official party nominee imposes a severe burden

**Holding:** A State severely burdens political association when it requires an objecting party to give voters unaffiliated with that party equal participation in choosing its official nominee through this blanket-primary mechanism. The requirement therefore must be narrowly tailored to serve a compelling state interest; ordinary authority to administer elections does not alone supply that justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Material application:** The burden concerns the legally designated nominee: retaining endorsement and advocacy does not restore the party's selection choice. Sincere crossover can matter without organized raiding; neither organizational collapse nor a universal numerical threshold is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not decide conventional open primaries, every registration condition, national-delegate arrangements or an independent Elections Clause claim. Constitutional voting protections continue to limit party exclusion.

##### 2. The asserted interests do not justify this compulsory nomination mechanism

**Holding:** The interests advanced for Proposition 198 do not justify its severe interference with the objecting parties' nomination choices. Participation, choice, competition, privacy and stable elections may support appropriate election rules, but the State has not established a compelling justification and necessary fit for imposing equal outsider participation in the party's own official selection.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nonpartisan candidate winnowing and a party's voluntary invitation illustrate less intrusive possibilities without validating every implementation. The Court does not invalidate all efforts to increase turnout, competition or choice.

##### 3. Relief follows the nomination defect and does not decide other election systems

**Holding:** The defense judgment must be reversed and the case remanded for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief against compulsory blanket selection of these objecting parties' official nominees. The ruling leaves ordinary election administration and constitutional voting protections intact and reserves the validity of unpresented primary designs and any remaining severability or implementation questions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief confined to the unconstitutional compulsory nomination mechanism and any ordinary implementation questions properly remaining. No new election, damages award or statutory replacement is ordered. State and congressional nominations receive the same disposition; national-delegate arrangements and the amended presidential reporting provisions are not adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut supplies protection for meaningful party associational choices within a regulated electoral system; this decision does not adopt an unlimited power of party leaders to override members.
- Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee supplies the protection against state control of party governance and political choice; ordinary election administration remains available.
- Democratic Party v. Wisconsin ex rel. La Follette supplies protection against forced participation inconsistent with protected party selection rules; the present decision applies that principle to the challenged official nominations without deciding national-delegate questions.
- Burdick v. Takushi supplies burden-sensitive review of election regulations. The severe burden here triggers compelling-interest and narrow-tailoring review rather than the lesser justification sufficient for ordinary mechanics.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party remains controlling in requiring meaningful official recognition of a qualified party's voluntary nomination of a consenting candidate. That protection of the party's own choice distinguishes it from compelled outsider participation in the actual choice.
- Reform Party of Allegheny County v. Allegheny County Department of Elections retains its equal-access holding concerning a timely consensual cross-nomination and its limits on the independent association issue. It does not itself decide the present public-primary question.
- Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston supplies protection for the composition of private expression, with its actual limits; this decision neither exempts ordinary services from neutral regulation nor classifies every regulated association as private expression.
- The white-primary decisions, including Smith v. Allwright and Terry v. Adams, retain their prohibitions on unconstitutional exclusion from electoral processes. Associational protection supplies no exemption from those constitutional commands.

#### Reed v. Farley, 512 U.S. 339

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5418.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

##### The compact supplies federal law and a federal habeas question

**Holding:** The congressionally sanctioned Interstate Agreement on Detainers is federal law, and a state prisoner's claim that custody violates its trial-period provisions presents a federal statutory question within 28 U.S.C. §2254(a) as in force at decision. Jurisdiction to consider that question does not itself establish entitlement to collateral relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. These five Justices directly adopt the jurisdictional conclusion; the dissent also accepts the compact's federal character but does not join the opinion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cuyler v. Adams, 449 U.S. 433 (1981): applied to the congressionally sanctioned compact's federal-law character; voluntary state participation does not remove that character.
- Fex v. Michigan, February 23, 1993: its application of the compact as federal law remains intact; its Article III actual-receipt trigger does not replace Article IV's arrival trigger or decide collateral entitlement.
- Stone v. Powell, 428 U.S. 465 (1976): its restriction concerning the Fourth Amendment exclusionary remedy is not extended to decide this statutory claim.

#### Slack v. McDaniel, 529 U.S. 473 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-6322.
**Decided:** April 26, 2000.

##### I. The judicial certificate denial is reviewable

**Holding:** A court of appeals' judicial denial of appellate certification is a case reviewable by certiorari under section 1254(1), whether the certificate was correctly or incorrectly required. That review does not itself issue a certificate or authorize the underlying merits appeal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial certificate review is distinct from review of a circuit authorization determination concerning a successive district application. The latter restriction is unchanged.

##### II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate

**Holding:** Amended section 2253(c) governs an applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, even when the identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier habeas application. Slack's April 29, 1998 notice therefore invokes the certificate-of-appealability requirement; the prior CPC label and the State's earlier contrary legal position do not dispense with that required authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas with its author, Ginsburg: five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with applying that regime but joins no Part II rationale. Stevens, Souter and Breyer would retain former CPC treatment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The appellate-date rule does not apply amended section 2254(d) to the pre-enactment district application or establish a universal claim-by-claim transition rule. A mislabeled CPC request or notice may be treated as a request under Rule 22; neither is an issued certificate. The State's legal concession cannot supply the required authorization.

##### III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings

**Holding:** When a district court dismisses on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a certificate of appealability requires reasonable debate both about whether the petition states a valid claim of denial of a constitutional right and about whether the procedural ruling was correct. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent component first, and any certificate must identify the qualifying issue or issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has nine direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens, Souter and Breyer adopt this interpretation conditionally while maintaining that former CPC governs this appeal; their Part III agreement does not enlarge the Part II coalition.

**Material application:** Reasonable debate requires more than a nonfrivolous assertion but not final proof of success. Where the district court has actually rejected constitutional merits, reasonable jurists must be able to debate that assessment or find it wrong. A plain correctly applied procedural bar defeats certification without an unnecessary constitutional inquiry; the constitutional component may instead be considered first when more apparent.

##### IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds

**Holding:** When an initial federal application is dismissed without prejudice solely to permit completion of state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, the renewed application is not second or successive merely because it follows that filing or adds grounds. Rule 9(b)'s new-ground abuse branch does not supply the missing second-or-successive predicate in this exhaustion-return setting; the contrary classification here is rejected, while actual independent default, exhaustion, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, making seven direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal with remand. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this classification and disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 9(a)'s distinct delay provision permits dismissal where filing delay prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before that prejudice arose. No such prejudice or diligence finding is made here. Rule 9(b)'s dismissal permission is not an automatic command, and former section 2244(b)'s exception to its prior-merits permission requires both an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse; that prior-merits predicate is absent here. No free-floating equitable sanction replaces an applicable source's conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not cover every nonmerits dismissal, immunize deliberate withholding under an actually applicable rule, authorize unlimited amendment or excuse independent state default, exhaustion or timing requirements. Amended section 2244 retains its own applicability and classification conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit certificate denial is reversed for certification review with the successive classification rejected. The abuse dismissals of grounds 2, 4(c), 5, 6 and 7 cannot stand on that classification; their separate exhaustion objections remain unresolved. Ground 3's whole-action nonexhaustion dismissal and the merits of grounds 1, 4(a) and 4(b) are not adjudicated. No certificate, writ, hearing, automatic reopening or limitations reset issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hohn is applied to judicial review of the certificate denial and the distinction between review, certification and relief. Its reserved transition issue is resolved here only at the scope of Part II.
- Felker retains its bar on review of actual successive-application authorization and its distinct original-habeas rules; this judicial certificate denial is outside that authorization determination.
- Lindh retains pre-enactment treatment of its identified district applications. The present appellate requirement does not apply amended section 2254(d) merely because an appeal is later.
- Carriger retains its specified capital district-law extension; it establishes no universal timing rule for every habeas provision.
- Barefoot supplies the substantial-showing and reasonable-debate background; amended section 2253 requires the constitutional component and issue identification stated here.
- Peguero is distinguished because an authorized judge had actually issued a certificate there; neither its existing-authorization rule nor this judgment creates one here.
- Rose is applied to the first federal merits opportunity following complete-exhaustion procedure; exhaustion itself remains required on its actual terms.
- Stewart is used as a limited first-review analogy, preserving its presented, premature and expressly reserved Ford-claim boundary. The additional-claim exhaustion-return rule is adopted here, not attributed to that earlier holding.
- Lonchar is applied to require actual statutory or Rule predicates for dismissal; no general equitable disapproval or automatic entitlement to a hearing replaces them.
- Sanders and McCleskey retain their structured abuse inquiries. Their pleading and excuse principles do not supply the missing successive category for this exhaustion return.
- Weeks retains independent statutory eligibility, constitutional claim and relief inquiries; a successful procedural challenge alone grants no writ.
- Michael Wayne Williams retains applicant-attributed failure and actual hearing prerequisites; an undeveloped record here does not itself trigger a hearing bar or compel a hearing.
- Terry Williams retains only its adopted statutory propositions, separate constitutional entitlement and remedy. Neither four-Justice exclusive clause construction is imported, and its post-enactment application does not change Slack's district-law temporal premise.

#### Romer v. Evans, 517 U.S. 620

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1039.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Withdrawal and constitutional foreclosure of ordinary protection for an identified class

**Holding:** Colorado Amendment 2's combined withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary state and local government violates equal protection under ordinary rational-basis review. Its established class-specific disability lacks a rational relationship to the asserted legitimate interests, without reliance on suspect status, political-process strict scrutiny, elimination of generally applicable protection, or a finding about every voter's motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Current force:** Romer remains valid as an earlier rational-basis decision and an independent floor for the actual disadvantage. Shahar v. Bowers, March 2, 1998, Part III-B, subjects sexual-orientation classifications to intermediate scrutiny, a tier Romer did not itself supply.

**Material application:** The authoritative state construction covers legislative, executive, and judicial channels of the State, agencies, schools, and local government, including public and private settings; it is not merely a quota prohibition or local preemption leaving the legislature free to act. Uniformity, regulatory restraint, finite resources, religious and intimate association, parental teaching, and conduct-based morality do not explain this blanket ineligibility across public and impersonal commercial life. Ordinary review permits supported predictions, imperfect categories, and conceivable grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone is insufficient. The defect concerns the relationship to the actual disadvantage, rather than an independent hostility finding or mandatory least-restrictive-means inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that generally applicable criminal, tort, contract, and public-service protections remain. It creates no suspect or quasi-suspect tier, general duty to enact nondiscrimination laws, nationwide code, quota entitlement, universal access to every lawmaking level, or presumption invalidating every group-affecting repeal, referendum, or constitutional policy reservation. Particular religious, intimate-association, and expressive defenses retain their own predicates; no municipal exemption is approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Beach* preserves conceivable legitimate grounds and reasonable generalizations together with the *Moreno/Cleburne* limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice. *Heller*'s controlling proof-burden decision requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage; its separate family-participation holding preserves relevant generalizations and rejects invalidity based on narrower alternatives alone. *Hurley* protects concrete private expression without making ordinary service categorically expressive association; *Swanner* and *American Life League* retain their particular religious-liberty inquiries. *Bowers* remains effective on its specified-conduct question but does not justify this broader disability. *Adarand* adds no orientation tier, and *Klinger*'s treatment-specific comparison does not import *Armstrong*'s charging-discovery threshold into an express classification.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Lawrence v. Texas, June 26, 2003, overrules Bowers insofar as it permits criminal punishment of private consensual adult intimacy based solely on moral disapproval. Romer's ordinary-equality holding remains effective, but its former preservation of Bowers no longer supports punishment within that category.

##### Resolution of the preserved alternative and complete injunctive relief

**Holding:** The fully presented rational-basis alternative permits affirmance without adoption of the lower court's strict-scrutiny rationale. The defect permeates Amendment 2's inseparable common operation, warranting the complete existing injunction without a new merits remand or federal rewriting of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** State severability law treats orientation, conduct, practices, and relationships as descriptions of the same class; deletion of a description leaves the same disability. No independently lawful remainder is established. Failure under strict scrutiny is not itself rational-basis failure, and no unconditional State concession supplies the result. The ability to seek another constitutional amendment does not cure the unequal barrier to ordinary protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 7–2; the permanent injunction remains effective in full. No enactment, damages, factual inquiry, severance proceeding, or new hearing is ordered. Unappealed suspect-status treatment is not reopened, and independently preserved First Amendment claims are neither decided nor presumed abandoned.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hunter* and *Seattle* retain their racial political-restructuring settings without a new fundamental-right extension. *Valtierra*, *Gordon*, and *Crawford* prevent treating every referendum, neutral voting rule, or policy revision as exclusion. *Dalton* requires relief matched to the violation and preservation of independently lawful operation; no general facial formula, *Miller* abortion method, or *44 Liquormart* empirical burden replaces this analysis.

#### Shahar v. Bowers

**Decided:** 1998-03-02.

##### Conduct and hiring labels do not remove a sexual-orientation classification from equality review

**Holding:** A conduct or hiring label does not remove an adverse civilian employment classification from equal-protection review. Government that disadvantages an individual because of sexual orientation, including through a nominal conduct rule operating as a deliberate status proxy, classifies by sexual orientation. Government that regulates actual conduct because of independently relevant professional obligations is reviewed under the applicable constitutional protections.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-A: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Material application:** Conduct operates as a status proxy when it is penalized because it reveals or expresses orientation, as when comparable conduct is treated differently or the asserted concern attaches to a relationship’s same-sex character rather than to a professional obligation the conduct implicates. The lower court’s conduct/employment characterization ended its equality analysis without asking which kind of action this withdrawal was. On the plaintiff-favorable record, the asserted concerns could be found to attach to what a same-sex religious commitment ceremony disclosed; no refusal of duties or lawbreaking is established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Conduct that genuinely conflicts with confidentiality, representation or assigned duties may be regulated on that ground, subject to whatever separate protections it independently carries under other constitutional provisions. The office’s professional responsibilities and reasonable predictions remain legitimate considerations. Bowers v. Hardwick concerns its specified conduct; orientation or the ceremony alone proves no violation, and no military inference governs this civilian ceremony. Which kind of action this withdrawal was returns on remand.

##### Sexual-orientation classifications receive intermediate scrutiny

**Holding:** Sexual-orientation classifications receive intermediate scrutiny. Once government is shown to have disadvantaged an individual because of sexual orientation, including through a deliberate status proxy, it must demonstrate a genuine, important objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the actual classification; it bears that burden, and moral disapproval, private bias, stereotype and post hoc or merely conceivable reasons do not carry it. Supported professional judgment and reasonable contemporaneous predictions receive substantial but nonconclusive weight, and the standard requires neither least restrictive means, an individual exception in every case nor an entitlement to a particular position. The standard adopted here does not itself establish fair notice in July 1991; prospective constitutional validity and personal damages liability remain separate on remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-B: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices. Kennedy joins Part III-A and the equality vacatur but not Part III-B. O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas reject Part III-B and would sustain the equality judgment under ordinary rationality.

**Material application:** Romer’s ordinary review excludes bare harm but accepts conceivable reasons, reasonable generalizations and imperfect fit, leaving the challenger to negate them; in Walmer, Thomasson, Richenberg and this withdrawal, conduct, statement, relationship or ceremony labels can let an orientation classification stand on grounds the government need not establish. Adarand’s common standard supplies the method: an important objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship to the classification actually used. Confidentiality, professional competence, credible representation and workplace administration remain available interests, and prevention need not await disruption, but generalized predictions about gay employees do not show that a particular exclusion substantially serves them, and an assertion of deference is not a showing. The summary judgment required no such showing.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The standard is demanding, not an absolute prohibition; experience may suffice without scientific certainty or formal findings. Neither Romer nor Virginia already supplied a tier for this classification. Bowers v. Hardwick’s specified-conduct holding is not reopened. The military decisions sustained under ordinary review are not reopened; their particular rules are assessed under this standard when it materially affects them.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Five Justices vacate the equality judgment and remand for application of Parts III-A and III-B; Stone-Zsela joins Part III but would formally reverse that claim and remand under intermediate scrutiny on a developed record, and O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas would affirm. No liability, reinstatement or damages is ordered.

##### Causation, conduct-date immunity and relief remain independent

**Holding:** Remand requires claim-specific constitutional predicates, personal responsibility, causation and lawful relief. Where retaliation analysis applies, protected activity must be a substantial or motivating factor and a same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance; ordinary rationality’s conceivable basis is not that proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part IV: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O’Connor also joins its application to the free-exercise remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Any procedurally available damages immunity concerns notice in July 1991; the Court neither awards that defense nor decides waiver or automatic revival. Later decisions supply no retrospective notice. Personal damages, prospective relief and successor or official-capacity relief retain separate predicates. Later evidence may lawfully affect remedy without becoming contemporaneous motive. Elapsed time alone establishes neither mootness nor a continuing job entitlement.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All four preserved claims are remanded under their distinct rules. No liability, reinstatement, backpay, damages, trial or discovery plan is ordered; the separate speech and substantive-due-process claims are not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Umbehr distinguishes benefit entitlement from unconstitutional retaliation and preserves actual causation and the preponderance same-decision defense.
- Romer’s ordinary rationality remains valid as a floor for the actual disadvantage; it did not itself supply the intermediate tier Part III-B adopts. Valot keeps equality review distinct from a protected-right penalty and a same-decision defense.
- Walmer, Thomasson and Richenberg retain their military and conduct-related scope and establish no civilian exclusion. Under Part III-B, intermediate scrutiny, not the ordinary review they applied, governs sexual-orientation classifications; their judgments are not reopened, and their particular rules are assessed under that standard when it materially affects them. Bowers v. Hardwick retains its specified-conduct holding and is not extended to orientation or a ceremony.
- Danahy supplies no office-wide merits exception and no retroactive July 1991 notice. NTEU’s broad prospective-restraint burden is not transferred wholesale to this single hiring decision.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Lawrence v. Texas, June 26, 2003, overrules Bowers insofar as it permits criminal punishment of private consensual adult intimacy based solely on moral disapproval. Within that category the statements above preserving Bowers v. Hardwick’s specified-conduct holding do not apply; this decision’s equality and remand holdings remain unchanged.

For this case’s other questions, see First Amendment (Private association requires its own protection and employment inquiry; Free exercise cannot be absorbed into a general employee-speech balance).

#### Lawrence v. Texas, 539 U.S. 558

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-102.
**Decided:** June 26, 2003.

##### Constitutional liberty against criminal punishment of private consensual adult intimacy

**Holding:** Fourteenth Amendment liberty protects private consensual sexual intimacy between adults against criminal punishment justified solely by moral disapproval of that intimacy. Bowers v. Hardwick is overruled to the extent it permits that punishment. Convictions and penalties imposed on that basis must be removed; an evenhanded prohibition of the same protected conduct cannot cure the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, supported directly by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The protected category excludes coercion, conduct involving minors, public conduct and commercial sex. Civil relationship status is not decided. No express fundamental-right classification, compelling-necessity test or collateral-review rule has controlling support here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both convictions and their dependent penalties must be vacated, and these prosecutions terminated. The rule applies in covered nonfinal prosecutions and direct appeals through the lawful procedures governing each case.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bowers loses authority for punishment within the defined category. Griswold and Eisenstadt supply individual intimate-choice principles; Casey supplies protected choice and a justification-based approach to overruling without exporting its abortion framework. Glucksberg is distinguished by its concrete protective reasons concerning assisted death and is not read as prescribing an exclusive historical-description method. Romer's former preservation of Bowers yields to this express overruling. The inadequacy of Bowers's description of the liberty, continued criminal intrusion and conflict with individual-choice principles support departure; comparable settled transactional reliance is absent.

##### Independent equal-protection defect in the selective same-sex criminal penalty

**Independent alternative holding:** Ordinary rational-basis review forbids this additional criminal penalty for same-sex participants when otherwise comparable private consensual adult conduct is unpunished and the distinction bears no rational relationship to a legitimate reason for the added disadvantage. The challenger retains the burden of negating a reasonably conceivable legitimate explanation; it is met because disapproval of the class does not establish a separate injury from the matched conduct.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part II of Ginsburg's Court opinion, supported directly by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices independently require the same conviction remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Because the selective penalty fails even ordinary review, the Court need not decide whether this same-sex distinction is a sexual-orientation classification subject to Shahar's intermediate scrutiny, and the comparison creates no general rule excluding moral interests from legislation. Ordinary review still permits imperfect classifications, reasonable generalizations and conceivable grounds without formal findings, empirical certainty or a least-restrictive-means inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Romer requires a rational connection to the particular unequal disadvantage. Heller preserves the challenger's burden and ordinary legislative latitude. Moreno and Cleburne supply the limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice. Authority to regulate conduct generally does not answer the independent discriminatory-penalty question. Shahar v. Bowers's intermediate scrutiny is not reached because the selective penalty fails even ordinary review.

#### Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8466.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### What justification is required to continue antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee’s objection

**Holding:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Material application:** Continued medication became involuntary after objection. Medical appropriateness was accepted, but the unexplained order established neither essential safety need nor the insufficiency of less intrusive means; testimony indicated that competence might continue without Mellaril.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

**Later-authority backlinks:** Sell v. **Holding:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. **Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

#### Sell v. United States, 539 U.S. 166 — prosecution-only medication for trial competence

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-5664.
**Decided:** June 16, 2003.

##### Conditions for medication solely to prosecute serious nonviolent charges

**Holding:** The Government may medicate an objecting, incompetent defendant solely to permit trial of serious nonviolent criminal charges, including serious property offenses, only if it establishes an important interest in this prosecution; a regimen substantially likely to restore competence and substantially unlikely to impair significantly effective assistance to counsel or the fairness of the defense; necessity after reasonable less intrusive treatments and methods of administration are considered; and medical appropriateness for this defendant. The court must assess defense effects before treatment and reassess actual effects before trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual prosecution interest includes confinement already endured and realistic further civil confinement; the latter can reduce the interest without automatically eliminating it. Minimal competence cannot suffice if treatment materially impairs comprehension, consultation, testimony, reactions or presentation. Clinical evidence, alternatives and workable safeguards must support advance findings; perfect prediction is unnecessary. Less intrusive treatments must be unlikely to achieve substantially the same result, and reasonably available less intrusive administration methods must be considered. The treatment must serve this defendant’s medical interest. Ordinarily the court examines whether an independent danger or medical basis has been sought and why not if absent; the sequence neither assumes danger nor compels an unavailable basis. Diagnosis, benefit and serious charges alone are insufficient.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Riggins’s refusal, need, alternatives and defense-risk protections and Weston’s advance and pretrial assessment requirements remain intact; this holding resolves their reserved prosecution-only question. Harper’s distinct danger-and-treatment authority requires its own predicates. Benson’s collateral-review ruling supplies no prospective treatment authority.

##### The existing authorization cannot support treatment

**Holding:** A prosecution-only medication order must connect the proposed treatment to a fair prosecution through advance findings about defense effects and the actual governmental interest. The present authorization fails that requirement and is vacated; a renewed request may proceed on current circumstances under Parts II and III or on a separately established lawful treatment basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part IV, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg: five direct joins.

**Material application:** The orders failed to connect treatment to fair prosecution through advance defense-effects findings and the actual prosecution interest after Sell’s confinement; rejected dangerousness could not supply the missing basis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the treatment authorization. A renewed request requires current findings meeting every prosecution-only condition or a separately lawful treatment basis. Continued custody requires independent statutory authority and review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Meaningful official recognition of a qualified party's consenting nomination

### Meaningful official recognition of a qualified party's consenting nomination

**Governing question:** Whether a State may categorically prevent a qualified political party from nominating, and having recognized on the official ballot, a consenting eligible candidate who also seeks or holds another party's nomination.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, denying a qualified party's legally operative nomination of its chosen, consenting candidate solely because another party also nominated him imposes a substantial, though not severe, burden on the party's candidate selection and on its members' ability to vote for that nominee under the party's name. Endorsement and campaigning outside the ballot do not replace the nomination the State has made legally operative. No new scrutiny tier follows; the State's precise interests must be weighed and genuinely connected to the burden. Ballot integrity, defined nomination choices and political stability are legitimate interests, but stability cannot mean preserving established parties' competitive position, and conjecture does not justify a categorical ban where consent, recognized-party status, clear labeling and single counting address the asserted concerns and the record shows no confusion, fraud, duplicate counting or splintered vote. When a qualified party genuinely nominates an eligible, consenting candidate, the State must provide a meaningful official means of recognizing that nomination. It may choose separate party lines or a single consolidated listing with the applicable party names, require the candidate's consent and prevent any ballot from being counted more than once.

**Authority by component:**

- Burden, fit and meaningful official recognition: Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party — II. The categorical ban is not justified as applied to this consenting nomination, 1997-04-28; Stone-Zsela, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (five). Thomas, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Breyer, dissents.
- Live controversy and the statutory consequence of affirmance: Timmons — I. The conditional enactment leaves a live controversy; III. Affirmance does not engage the enacted expiration condition, and no election is reopened, 1997-04-28; all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Timmons, 1997-04-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** No right to a separate ballot line, multiple vote credit, a particular ballot format or separate party vote totals is recognized, and no party-specific counting rule is decided. No independent challenge to consent, counting, format, support or deadline rules is adjudicated. The holding concerns a timely nomination of a candidate who was not defeated in another party's primary; sore-loser rules, a required other-party consent, the distinct two-major-party prohibition and the independent validity of Minnesota's chapter 419 conditions are not decided. Whether a demonstrated threat to effective governing, connected to fusion, could justify a narrower limit is reserved. Completed elections are not reopened.

**Related current rules:** “Authorized party expansion and independently qualified slate components” protects authorized party identity; “Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system” supplies the calibrated framework, and “Effective alternative-candidate route after early closing of nomination” protects a genuine choice of an actual candidate.

### Compulsory outsider selection of official party nominees

**Governing question:** The burden imposed by a blanket primary requiring outsider participation in an objecting party’s nominee selection.

**Current rule:** Giving voters unaffiliated with an objecting political party equal participation in choosing its official nominee through a compulsory blanket primary severely burdens association. The State must establish a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. The party need not prove organizational collapse or organized raiding; retained endorsement and advocacy do not restore control of nominee selection. A desire to moderate party choices or make nominees responsive to other associations does not itself justify that interference, and ordinary election-administration authority is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- California Democratic Party v. Jones; No. 99-401. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Compulsory outsider selection of an official party nominee imposes a severe burden; 2. The asserted interests do not justify this compulsory nomination mechanism, 2000-06-26; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-26; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Conventional open primaries, nonpartisan or top-two designs, voluntary party invitations and registration conditions are not categorically resolved. The decision creates no exemption from constitutional voting protections, no unlimited party-leader authority over members and no holding on national-delegate or separately presented Elections Clause questions. Fusion recognition and equal cross-nomination remain governed by their distinct rules; a party's voluntary choice of a consenting nominee is not compelled outsider participation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Involuntary antipsychotic medication of an objecting trial detainee

### Involuntary antipsychotic medication of an objecting trial detainee

**Governing question:** Justification for continuing antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee's objection and the consequence of unsupported treatment through trial.

**Current rule:** Upon the detainee's objection, the State must establish medical appropriateness and an overriding need sufficient to overcome the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. Clinical acceptability alone is insufficient. Medically appropriate treatment essential to safety remains possible after alternatives are considered. When unjustified compelled treatment creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not establish exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed; explanatory testimony about demeanor does not cure unmeasurable effects on testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel. The combination of unjustified treatment and that supported, unreconstructable trial risk requires a new trial; renewed compelled treatment requires adequate contemporaneous justification.

Where restoration of competency is an actual treatment purpose and no independently adequate safety justification exists, the judge must assess likely effects of the proposed regimen on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor and defense presentation before authorizing medication. The Government must support the preliminary inquiry with available clinical evidence, reasonable alternatives and workable safeguards. Perfect prediction is unnecessary, but postponing the entire inquiry until treatment has occurred is insufficient; actual effects require renewed assessment before trial. Existing effective containment of immediate danger must be addressed as an actual alternative in any claimed safety justification, without making indefinite isolation constitutionally required.

For prosecution alone to justify involuntary medication of an objecting, incompetent defendant facing serious nonviolent charges, including serious property charges, the Government must establish an important interest in this actual prosecution; substantial likelihood of restoring competence and substantial unlikelihood of significantly impairing counsel assistance or a fair defense; necessity after considering reasonable less intrusive treatments and methods of administration; and medical appropriateness for this defendant. Assess the actual prosecution interest in light of detention already served and realistic further civil confinement, which can reduce but does not automatically eliminate that interest. Reasonable less intrusive treatments must be unlikely to achieve substantially the same legitimate result. Clinical evidence and workable safeguards must support the judge’s advance decision, followed by reassessment of actual defense effects before trial.

Ordinarily determine whether a separately supported danger or medical treatment ground has been sought, and if not, why not. An adequate independent ground may avoid the prosecution-only inquiry; this sequence does not require asserting an unavailable ground. No categorical freedom-of-thought prohibition defeats restoration satisfying all these protections, but cognitive risks receive full weight in the necessity, medical-interest and fair-defense inquiries.


**Authority by component:**

- Justification and alternatives: Riggins v. Nevada, “1. The State must justify antipsychotic medication continued over objection”, May 18, 1992; O'Connor Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter (six).
- Trial-risk consequence at the stated scope: Riggins, “2. This unsupported medication order requires a new trial,” May 18, 1992; O'Connor Part III, same six Justices.

- Safety justification and consideration of actual containment: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Weston_merits_2001-04-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The present safety justification does not support unwanted medication, 2001-04-17; all nine join O’Connor’s Part I.
- Preliminary defense-effects inquiry without independent sufficient safety justification: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Weston_merits_2001-04-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The restoration purpose requires a preliminary assessment of likely defense effects on this record, 2001-04-17; seven direct joins to Part II.

- Canonical Decision Record `Sell-v-United-States_merits_2003-06-16.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Prosecution alone can justify medication only on an individualized showing protecting a fair trial; III. The asserted freedom-of-thought interest does not categorically bar justified restoration, 2003-06-16; Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.


**Present operation effective:** Sell, June 16, 2003, for prosecution-only authorization and its complete conditions; Weston and Riggins retain their distinct earlier components.


**Exceptions and limits:** No universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula, documentary ritual or structural-error category is established. Harper's convicted-prisoner procedures are not automatically imported. Kennedy's extraordinary-showing proposal and Stone-Zsela's Chapman formulation are not Court rules.

**Open question:** Sell resolves the prosecution-only question through the conditions above. A materially different order resting on an independently sufficient safety ground remains governed by that ground’s own requirements. A competent defendant’s refusal that would produce incompetence and the unpreserved Eighth Amendment sentencing-display theory remain undecided. Immediate emergency treatment and treatment solely for execution are outside Weston’s holding.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. After district refusal, the applicant may request a circuit judge’s certificate; a request addressed to the court is addressed to its judges, and a notice of appeal without an express request serves as that request. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative. An appellate court’s judicial denial of a certificate of appealability is a case in that court reviewable by certiorari under §1254(1), although it prevents the merits appeal from proceeding. Review or vacatur of a certificate denial does not itself issue a certificate or satisfy its substantial-showing and issue-identification conditions.

An authorized judge’s actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal even when the judge allegedly erred in assessing §2253(c)(2)’s constitutional showing. That alleged assessment error does not itself nullify the existing appellate authorization. The substantial-constitutional-showing condition remains necessary for proper issuance; this rule neither finds a bare rules claim sufficient nor authorizes future certificates without the statutory showing. When dismissal rests on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, certification requires reasonable debate both over whether the petition states a valid constitutional claim and over the correctness of the procedural ruling. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent one first and must identify any certified issues. Mere nonfrivolousness, a debatable procedural question alone or a bare constitutional label is insufficient; ultimate merits success is not required. For an actual merits rejection, reasonable jurists must be able to debate the assessment or find it wrong.

At the certificate stage after rejection of constitutional merits, reasonable debate concerns the district court’s assessment under the actual governing habeas restrictions, not the abstract constitutional claim alone. The applicant need not conclusively prove factual or legal unreasonableness by clear and convincing evidence before obtaining the appeal. Sections 2254(d)(2) and (e)(1) retain their distinct functions for merits adjudication; an indisputably applicable habeas bar cannot be bypassed by an arguable abstract claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Appeal, certificate, district/circuit application and transfer procedures: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §§102–103, 28 U.S.C. §2253 and Fed. R. App. P. 22, enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

- Hohn Holdings I–II: certiorari review and distinct certificate requirements: Hohn v. United States — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-15.

- Issued authorization distinguished from correctness of the constitutional-showing assessment: Canonical Decision Record `peguero_v_united_states_merits_1999-03-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The issued certificate supplied the appellate authorization in this case, 1999-03-02; eight Associate joins.

- Post-enactment appeal transition: Slack v. McDaniel — II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate, April 26, 2000; Ginsburg with O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
- Constitutional and procedural debate, no fixed order, and issue identification: Slack — III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings, April 26, 2000; all nine directly adopt the construction.

- Debate under the actual habeas framework without completed merits proof: Canonical Decision Record `Miller-El-v-Cockrell_merits_2003-02-25.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Certification tests reasonable debate under the actual habeas framework, 2003-02-25; Ginsburg’s opinion joined by all Justices except Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** Miller-El v. Cockrell, February 25, 2003, for the certificate-stage use of actual habeas restrictions; the earlier appellate transition, issued-certificate, procedural-dismissal and reviewability rules retain their authority.

**Effective transition:** Amended §2253(c) governs an applicant’s appeal initiated after April 24, 1996 even when identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier application. A CPC label or an earlier contrary legal position does not supply missing authorization. ## Standards and Tests selection: Federal habeas relief for an IAD statutory timing defect

### Federal habeas relief for an IAD statutory timing defect

**Governing question:** When a state prisoner’s Interstate Agreement on Detainers claim warrants federal collateral relief.

**Current rule:** The IAD is federal law and can present a federal habeas question, but an ordinary nonconstitutional statutory violation warrants collateral relief only when it is a fundamental defect resulting in a complete miscarriage of justice or inconsistent with rudimentary fair procedure. An assumed Article IV(c) timing violation, where predeadline written invocations exist but no specific contemporaneous objection was made to the scheduled dates and no demonstrated prejudice was shown in the posture decided, does not itself meet that standard. This is not a required oral formula, automatic waiver rule or conclusion that federal jurisdiction guarantees relief. Article IV(c) ordinarily requires trial within 120 days after arrival, allowing necessary or reasonable continuances for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present; Article VI’s inability-to-stand-trial tolling is not automatically established by filing motions. Where Article V’s dismissal requirement legally applies, it is mandatory rather than a discretionary remedy.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal question and distinct collateral-relief threshold: Reed v. Farley — The compact supplies federal law and a federal habeas question; the assumed timing violation does not establish the required collateral defect on this record, 1994-06-20. Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. These five Justices directly adopt the jurisdictional conclusion; the dissent also accepts the compact's federal character but does not join the opinion. Parts II and III of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly join the standard and this record's denial. The stronger-demand discussion in Part IV has three votes and supplies no additional Court holding; no combination of separate opinions is required.

**Present operation effective:** Reed v. Farley, 1994-06-20.

**Open question:** A materially stronger timely-demand or prejudice record remains open, as does an independently established constitutional speedy-trial claim. The separate Article III delivery rule is unchanged.

## Standards and Tests selection: Intermediate scrutiny for sexual-orientation classifications

### Intermediate scrutiny for sexual-orientation classifications

**Governing question:** When government action classifies by sexual orientation, and the justification such a classification requires.

**Current rule:** A conduct or hiring label does not remove an adverse civilian employment classification from equal-protection review. Government that disadvantages an individual because of sexual orientation, including through a nominal conduct rule operating as a deliberate status proxy, classifies by sexual orientation. Conduct operates as such a proxy when it is penalized because it reveals or expresses orientation, as when comparable conduct is treated differently or the asserted concern attaches to a relationship’s same-sex character rather than to a professional obligation the conduct implicates. Government that regulates actual conduct because of independently relevant professional obligations is reviewed under the applicable constitutional protections. Sexual-orientation classifications receive intermediate scrutiny: government must demonstrate a genuine, important objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the actual classification. It bears that burden, and moral disapproval, private bias, stereotype and post hoc or merely conceivable reasons do not carry it. Confidentiality, professional competence, credible representation and workplace administration remain available interests, and prevention need not await disruption, but generalized predictions about gay employees do not show that a particular exclusion substantially serves them, and an assertion of deference is not a showing. Supported professional judgment and reasonable contemporaneous predictions receive substantial but nonconclusive weight; the standard requires neither least restrictive means, an individual exception in every case nor an entitlement to a particular position.

**Authority by component:**

- Conduct and hiring labels, deliberate status proxies and the professional-obligation distinction: Shahar v. Bowers — III-A. Conduct and hiring labels do not remove a sexual-orientation classification from equality review, 1998-03-02; Souter Part III-A with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six.
- Intermediate scrutiny, the government’s burden, excluded reasons and the weight of professional judgment: Shahar v. Bowers — III-B. Sexual-orientation classifications receive intermediate scrutiny, 1998-03-02; Souter Part III-B with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five. Kennedy joins Part III-A but not Part III-B; O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas reject Part III-B.

**Present operation effective:** Shahar, March 2, 1998.

**Exceptions and limits:** The standard is demanding, not an absolute prohibition; experience may suffice without scientific certainty or formal findings. Romer’s ordinary rationality remains valid as a floor for the actual disadvantage. Military decisions sustained under ordinary review stand and are not reopened; their particular rules are assessed under this standard when it materially affects them, and no military inference governs civilian employment. The standard does not itself reopen Bowers v. Hardwick’s specified-conduct holding; Lawrence v. Texas, June 26, 2003, overrules that holding insofar as it permits criminal punishment of private consensual adult intimacy resting solely on moral disapproval. The standard, announced in 1998, does not itself establish fair notice in July 1991; prospective constitutional validity and personal damages liability remain separate.

**Related current rules:** Military statement-based conduct inference and evidentiary use; Class-specific foreclosure of ordinary municipal protection; Equal protection against same-sex criminal disadvantage.

## Standards and Tests selection: Fit-parent visitation choices and judicial preference

### Fit-parent visitation choices and judicial preference

**Governing question:** The constitutional limit on overriding a fit parent’s visitation decision.

**Current rule:** A fit parent’s protected direction of the child’s care and upbringing cannot be displaced solely because a judge prefers a different visitation arrangement. Ordinary judicial preference is insufficient constitutional justification; significant benefits of another relationship alone do not erase the parental interest.

**Authority by component:**

- Troxel v. Granville; No. 99-138. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, Parental liberty forbids displacement based only on a judge's preferred arrangement, 2000-06-05; Breyer Part I with Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-05; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** No complete special-weight test, universal serious-harm requirement, unfitness prerequisite, scrutiny level, proof burden or general facial invalidity is controlling. Legitimate child protection, the custody-transition rule and the distinct complete-contact-cutoff rule retain their scopes; the judgment yields no Marks synthesis.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private consensual adult intimacy and criminal prohibition

### Private consensual adult intimacy and criminal prohibition

**Governing question:** A State punishes private consensual sexual intimacy between adults solely to enforce moral disapproval.

**Current rule:** Fourteenth Amendment liberty protects private consensual adult sexual intimacy from criminal punishment resting solely on moral disapproval. Bowers no longer permits that punishment. Convictions and penalties resting on the invalid prohibition must be set aside.

**Authority by component:**

- Canonical Decision Record `Lawrence-v-Texas_merits_2003-06-26.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Private consensual adult intimacy is protected against this criminal prohibition, 2003-06-26; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Lawrence, June 26, 2003.

**Exceptions and limits:** The protected category is private, consensual adult intimacy. The holding supplies no universal scrutiny tier, marriage entitlement or rule for minors, coercion, public conduct or commercial sexual activity.
