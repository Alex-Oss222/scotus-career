## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Government may not pay for worship, religious indoctrination or instructional work in which religious formation is inseparable from the publicly financed service, and parental choice does not alter the constitutional character of what government purchases; neutral eligibility does not cure an unconstitutional governmental expenditure. Courts examine the actual expenditure and the function it finances, treating labels such as voucher, parental choice or secular grant as evidence of a program's structure rather than answers. A religious institution may provide an independently identifiable secular service where the funded work is genuinely secular and enforceable conditions prevent public payment for devotional activity. Religious affiliation alone neither proves a violation nor establishes an entitlement to funding, and genuinely private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

Affirmative governmental issuance and tax qualification of project bonds remain subject to the actual financed-function inquiry even when private investors provide the capital and the borrower alone repays it. Demonstrated devotional work and facilities inseparable from it cannot be financed; independently identifiable secular work remains eligible under practical separation and enforceable controls.

Civil regulation may enforce objectively verifiable facts about preparation or a named private certification. Officials may not adjudicate theological correctness, make denominational orthodoxy a coercive civil standard, or confer compulsory civil permission on a religious office supplying the governing religious judgment. Factual expertise or truthful private attribution does not itself confer such authority.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson's expenditure rule governs voucher arrangements, direct tuition payments and disguised institutional subsidies through the same examination of the function financed; it does not prohibit every benefit involving a religious institution, exclude religious providers by identity or create a constitutional entitlement to subsidy, and its judgment commands no immediate ban on every payment or religious provider. Provider/payment relief and state-law severability turn on the actual record. Monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective as genuinely private benefits: an individual vocational grant and a generally available tax deduction.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Equipment-aid limits:** A statutory control and its actual observance are different, and not every statutory violation is constitutional. The rule requires appropriate effective separation without a universal failsafe monitor, corporate restructuring, distinct staff or mandatory new audit. It decides no universal voucher rule or entitlement to religious uses of public benefits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal religious eligibility for controlled secular educational services

### Equal religious eligibility for controlled secular educational services

**Governing question:** Religious-character or religious-location disqualifications within existing programs of independently identifiable secular educational work.

**Current rule:** A State may not exclude a child from consideration for onsite secular special-education service solely because the school is religious where comparable secular locations are permitted and effective public control can preserve the service's secular character. Neutral educational, logistical and fiscal criteria may operate on equal terms. Adequate alternative service does not itself cure that express disqualification.

Religious premises alone do not convert independently controlled, operationally separate public vision service into devotional instruction. Actual safeguards against religious teaching and diversion remain permissible. The establishment permission does not itself require onsite services; the right is to equal consideration within the offered service. Relief must remove the religious criterion while preserving lawful neutral service and site decisions.

For direct institutional grants, an applicant must establish the requested secular work, eligible costs and lawful program compliance under effective use restrictions and reliable accountability. A devotional-financing objection requires a supported operational connection, which may be shown before payment; religious affiliation and hypothetical diversion alone do not establish it. When the work meets the existing program’s lawful requirements and the financing boundary, religious affiliation alone cannot justify denial of equal consideration or payment otherwise required by that program. An express religious disability requires a compelling interest and narrow tailoring, addressing feasible less restrictive means.

## Standards and Tests selection: Devotional study conditions on individual scholarships

### Devotional study conditions on individual scholarships

**Governing question:** May a State exclude a devotional ministry degree from an otherwise available individual educational scholarship?

**Current rule:** The Free Exercise Clause permits the confined exclusion of a degree devotional in nature or designed to induce religious faith and preparing the student for ministry, while religious students, religious institutions, individual theology courses and secular courses of study remain eligible. Constitutional permission to fund such training does not compel its inclusion in this individual-benefit arrangement.

**Authority by component:** Locke v. Davey, February 25, 2004, sole controlling holding; Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** February 25, 2004.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is a permission, not an Establishment Clause command. It authorizes no exclusion based merely on belief, institutional affiliation or an isolated theology course. Independently identifiable secular services and work, integrated tuition and restricted provider purchases retain their distinct rules; a business double major does not convert the individual scholarship into a restricted secular-department grant.

## Standards and Tests selection: Procedural correction and successive habeas applications

### Procedural correction and successive habeas applications

**Governing question:** When a postjudgment Rule 60 motion seeks correction of federal habeas procedure rather than renewed adjudication of custody merits.

**Current rule:** A request confined to correcting an asserted federal procedural defect that prevented adjudication of an already pleaded habeas claim is not a second or successive merits application merely because reopening could later permit relief. A new custody claim, new factual merits supplement or renewed constitutional-merits adjudication remains subject to the applicable successive-application restrictions. Separate a mixed motion’s genuine procedural request from those merits demands before allowing it to proceed as a process-only Rule 60 motion.

Classification does not establish entitlement to reopening. A Rule 60(b)(6) request must independently satisfy reasonable timing and extraordinary circumstances; the court must assess the failure to appeal the original procedural ruling and the legal effect of the asserted intervening change. Applicable certificate requirements remain independent, including both constitutional and procedural reasonable-debate showings after a procedural dismissal.

## Standards and Tests selection: One-year state-custody habeas filing period

### One-year state-custody habeas filing period

**Governing question:** The limitation period under amended §2244(d).

**Current rule:** A person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment has one year to apply for habeas relief, running from the latest of: finality by completion of direct review or expiration of time to seek it; removal of a State-created filing impediment violating federal law that actually prevented filing; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted constitutional right, if newly recognized and made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could have discovered the claim’s factual predicate. Time during which a properly filed application for state postconviction or other collateral review of the pertinent judgment or claim is pending is excluded.

Proper filing concerns compliance with requirements for delivery and acceptance of the application, rather than the success or relief eligibility of its individual claims. Claim-relief bars therefore do not invalidate an otherwise proper filing. Clerk acceptance cannot cure a genuine filing defect. Form, deadlines, the proper receiving court or office, fees and filing preconditions must be distinguished from rules requiring denial of relief on a claim.

The state-proceeding limitation applies to both postconviction review and other collateral review. Pendency of a federal habeas application is not excluded under section 2244(d)(2), even if that application was timely and dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. Accompanying civil-rights allegations do not convert it into state review. A later application's non-successive status and an earlier application's timely filing do not themselves make the later application timely; proper filing, actual pendency and relation to the pertinent judgment or claim remain independent conditions.

A properly filed state collateral application remains pending through timely continuation of the ordinary state-review process, including qualifying intervals before filing at the next available level. Successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function. Completion, abandonment, an unreasonable interval or an unrelated later attack does not receive exclusion merely because another petition is possible. A state merits discussion does not override an independent valid untimeliness determination. Where the order is ambiguous, the federal court must determine its actual meaning, the applicable state timing rule, the supported explanation for the interval and elapsed time; lawful clarification may be used but is not invariably required. No national fixed deadline or automatic reasonableness presumption applies.

## Standards and Tests selection: Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

### Second or successive state-custody habeas applications

**Governing question:** Repeated/new claims, appellate authorization and district verification under amended §2244(b).

**Current rule:** A claim in a second or successive §2254 application already presented in a prior application must be dismissed. A new claim must also be dismissed unless it relies on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court, or both its factual predicate was not previously discoverable through due diligence and its facts, if proved and viewed with all evidence, would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense. Before district filing, the applicant must seek appropriate appellate authorization; a three-judge panel may authorize only on a prima facie showing of these requirements and must act within thirty days. A grant or denial is not appealable and cannot be the subject of rehearing or certiorari. Authorization is not final entitlement: the district court must dismiss unless the applicant establishes the statutory conditions.

A Ford claim actually presented in the initial merits application, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved for later consideration is not second or successive when renewed after prematurity ends. Its first merits consideration requires no circuit authorization, subject to other habeas requirements. Section 2244(b)(3)(E) does not bar ordinary review of a circuit’s distinct determination that authorization is unnecessary; the classification itself remains reviewable and cannot evade the statute through a label.

For section 2244(b)(2)(A), Supreme Court retroactivity may be established by an express holding or by necessary propositions of multiple Supreme Court holdings that jointly compel the result. A lower court’s persuasive extension, prediction or independent choice is insufficient. Teague’s preserved watershed-procedure exception, together with Sullivan’s necessary no-valid-verdict holding, establishes that status for the confined Cage rule invalidating a verdict-wide reasonable-doubt standard. Structural-error labeling, importance or absence of harmless review alone does not establish retroactivity. The authorization-review bar does not foreclose review of a later final judgment, and advance authorization never replaces district verification of statutory conditions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Supplemental jurisdiction over related state administrative review

### Supplemental jurisdiction over related state administrative review

**Governing question:** Federal power and retention of state administrative-review claims accompanying substantial federal claims.

**Current rule:** Substantial federal claims on a complaint’s face may supply §1331 original jurisdiction and §1441(a) removal. Section 1367(a) then covers related state administrative-review claims forming part of the same Article III case, notwithstanding confinement to an administrative record. The governing state review standards remain intact. Power does not compel retention: §1367(c) permits nonretention for novel or complex state law, substantial state predominance, dismissal of all original claims, or exceptional circumstances with other compelling reasons. Economy, convenience, fairness and comity inform lawful discretion without replacing those conditions.

**Authority by component:**

**Exceptions and limits:** Established abstention retains its own predicates and remedy limits. Burford surrender requires timely adequate state review and either difficult state law of transcendent public importance or disruption of coherent policy on a matter of substantial public concern; independent legal damages cannot simply be relinquished on that ground, although a justified stay may be available. A state-administrative-only action and a diversity route for it remain unresolved.

**Related current rules:** Burford abstention and independent legal-damages claims states the separate remedy boundary.

## Standards and Tests selection: Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

### Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

**Governing question:** Effect of a preserved removal error when complete diversity actually exists before trial and through judgment.

**Current rule:** Absent complete diversity at removal, a timely remand motion requires remand under the applicable statutes. A later lawful dismissal of the nondiverse party does not validate the earlier refusal, waive the objection or require extraordinary pretrial review. If actual §1332 diversity arises before trial and continues through judgment, that earlier defect alone does not require vacating the completed federal judgment. Actual jurisdiction at trial and judgment is indispensable; consent, efficiency and finality cannot supply it.

A continuing party’s post-filing change of citizenship does not cure diversity absent when suit began. A partnership retains every partner’s citizenship for that commencement inquiry; later membership withdrawal changes the continuing party’s citizenship rather than dismissing an actual party. Section 1653 corrects defective allegations, not the underlying jurisdictional facts. The party invoking jurisdiction must establish the required citizenship at the controlling time. Actual dismissal of a dispensable party remains available subject to dispensability and prejudice review, but waiver, consent and litigation expense cannot create jurisdiction.

## Standards and Tests selection: Acquisition for Amtrak service and the presumption of need

### Acquisition for Amtrak service and the presumption of need

**Governing question:** The acquisition, negotiation and need requirements of 45 U.S.C. §562(d), including service-promoting reconveyance.

**Current rule:** The ICC may construe property required for passenger service to include property usefully and appropriately acquired for Amtrak's rail operations, without indispensable permanent Amtrak ownership; express service-promoting reconveyance authority remains available. The independent inability-to-agree condition requires a supported finding from the parties' actual positions that further sale negotiations will not produce agreement, without an additional mandatory bargaining ritual. An unaccepted unilateral offer alone is not invariably sufficient, and the need presumption does not replace that antecedent finding. To rebut presumed need, the railroad must establish both significant impairment of its common-carrier duties and an adequate available alternative. The ICC may assess impairment from the transaction as a whole, including preserved trackage rights and compensation, and may construe alternative property as an adequate alternative route rather than a lesser interest in the same segment. Each failure to prove a conjunctive condition independently leaves the presumption unrebutted. Passenger-service connection, just compensation and separate regulatory requirements continue to constrain acquisition.

- Service-connected acquisition and reconveyance: National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992, holding 1.
- Independent negotiation prerequisite and its factual showing: the same record, holding 3, “Inability to agree on sale terms.”
- Conjunctive rebuttal and whole-transaction impairment: the same record, holding 4.
- Route-based alternative-property construction: the same record, holding 5. Kennedy for Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter supplies six controlling joins throughout.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation. United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority.


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

#### Strout v. Albanese

**Docket or dockets:** No Supreme Court docket supplied; review of First Circuit No. 98-1986, 178 F.3d 57.

**Decided:** February 28, 2000.

##### Maine may refuse the integrated devotional tuition presented here

**Holding:** The Religion Clauses permit Maine to refuse public payment for the integrated devotional instructional function presented by this ordinary-tuition application. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Holding:** Refusing this public purchase of integrated devotional instruction does not, in this application, violate the Free Exercise Clause. The conclusion does not depend on whether attending the school is a central religious tenet and does not authorize religious hostility, exclusion from a neutral private-expression forum, or categorical rejection of genuinely severable secular work solely because its provider is religious.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court rejects the lower court's central-tenet premise and independent-prong Lemon approach. Reliably separated secular services and genuinely private expression retain their existing protections; no distinct service or forum application is decided.

**Holding:** The judgment sustaining the challenged refusal of integrated tuition is affirmed within the granted Establishment and Free Exercise questions. The decision does not reopen the ungranted speech, equal-protection or parental-liberty claims, the unchallenged dismissals of local defendants, or distinct applications for reliably separated secular services.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory eligibility conditions remain distinct: basic approval; First-Amendment-compliant nonsectarian status; Maine or United States incorporation; required reporting, auditing and adopted requirements; statewide assessment at sixty-percent public enrollment measured by the previous October/April average; and requested transfer of records for transferring students. Section 5204(4) applies only when the resident unit neither maintains a secondary school nor contracts for secondary-school privileges. Receiving-school permission remains necessary, and payment is required up to the legal rate for the allowed approved private, adjoining public, or out-of-state or foreign schools. School self-identification is an administrative finding, not statutory text; the repealed fourth condition is not restored.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed as applied, 6-3, within the granted Religion Clause questions. The tuition refusal stands; no tuition, damages, recoupment, transfer, closure, statewide cutoff or new factfinding is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing unified Lemon inquiry and to the distinction between integrated devotional work and separately controlled secular instruction; no categorical inference follows from religious location.

#### Zelman v. Simmons-Harris, 536 U.S. 639

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 00-1751, 00-1777 and 00-1779.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

## Selected opening holding — federal-courts-habeas


#### Abdur'Rahman v. Bell, 537 U.S. 88

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-9094; original appellate Nos. 98-6568/6569, transfer appeal No. 01-6504 and authorization No. 01-6487.
**Decided:** December 10, 2002.

##### Judicial review and actual authorization remain distinct

**Holding:** A circuit's judicial certificate determination and dismissal of an asserted appeal are cases reviewable under §1254(1), including review of whether the circuit had jurisdiction; that authority does not itself establish jurisdiction over the underlying appeal or issue a certificate. Section 2244(b)(3)(E) separately bars review of the actual authorization denial in No. 01-6487, which remains untouched.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 23, 1996 original application retains its applicable pre-AEDPA merits law; that status does not exempt the separate later authorization proceeding from section 2244(b)(3)(E). Review of a judicial certificate proceeding does not itself issue a certificate or establish lower appellate jurisdiction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismiss review of the actual authorization denial in No. 01-6487, unanimously; the denial remains untouched.

##### Authority for corrective lower-court processing

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices require corrective processing of the separable default-only request, but no lower-appellate-channel rationale commands five votes. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on the circuit's authority in its original appellate proceedings and its responsibility to correct improper routing of the transferred request. Stone-Zsela instead relies on the final, conclusive refusal to exercise Rule 60 jurisdiction as an appealable disposition. Neither route is a logical subset of the other; no Marks rule or universal transfer-appealability holding follows. The corrective mandate controls this case.

**Material judgment:** Eight Justices leave the distinct No. 01-6504 dismissal and certificate rulings undisturbed. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer resolve the procedural request through the original appellate proceedings and therefore do not set aside the redundant separate appeal; they do not decide that the transfer was nonfinal. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas sustain dismissal because the transfer was nonfinal and reject the original-appellate remand as well. Stone-Zsela would set aside the separate dismissal and certificate refusal on his final-order view. Those positions establish no five-Justice rule about transfer finality.

##### A genuine federal procedural request is distinct from renewed merits litigation

**Holding:** A motion confined to correcting an asserted defect in federal habeas procedure, including a barrier that prevented adjudication of an already pleaded claim, is not a second or successive merits application merely because reopening could later permit relief. A request for a new custody claim, a new factual merits supplement or renewed adjudication of constitutional merits remains subject to the applicable successive-application restrictions; a mixed motion must have its procedural request separated from those merits demands before it can proceed as a process-only Rule 60 motion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III's procedural rule and its application to the separable default request have five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt its distinct exclusion of renewed merits adjudication from this Rule 60 remand.

**Material application:** The separable request concerns the federal default barrier to ten already pleaded misconduct claims. Four misconduct claims previously adjudicated on their merits—the 1972 transcript, blood evidence, publication of inadmissible information and sentencing argument about the ministry—and cumulative merits reconsideration with assistance and instruction claims are excluded from the procedural remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The motion alleges no newly discovered evidence. Procedural classification establishes neither the alleged misconduct nor timeliness, an excuse for the omitted original appeal, extraordinary circumstances or entitlement to a writ. All nine Justices adopt the separate renewed-merits exclusion.

##### Rule 60 requirements and Rule 39's effect must receive their own adjudication

**Holding:** After separation, the default-reopening request may receive ordinary Rule 60(b)(6) consideration; the court must assess a reasonable time, extraordinary circumstances, the failure to appeal the original default ruling, and the legal effect of Tennessee Rule 39. Correcting categorical classification neither establishes those conditions nor authorizes reconsideration of the surviving constitutional merits judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion has five direct supporters: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** If later proceedings require a certificate, reasonable debate must concern both a valid constitutional claim and the procedural ruling, and the qualifying issues must be identified. Reclassification supplies neither showing. Allegations of state-trial misconduct are not established fraud on the federal tribunal.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the categorical refusal of corrective processing in original appellate Nos. 98-6568/6569 and return the separated procedural request for ordinary Rule 60 consideration. The No. 01-6504 transfer-appeal dismissal and certificate rulings survive. No writ, automatic reopening or stay issues; the constitutional merits judgment remains effective.

**Material application:** Tennessee's June 28, 2001 rule declares that, in criminal and postconviction appeals from July 1, 1967 onward, rehearing and discretionary Supreme Court review are unnecessary for exhaustion after adverse intermediate review; a claim presented to either appellate court and denied is exhausted. For automatic capital review, an intermediate-court claim is deemed exhausted without renewal in the Supreme Court. Discretionary Rule 11 review still exists. O'Sullivan left the federal effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review unresolved; the state declaration does not by itself decide that federal issue or extraordinary circumstances.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hohn v. United States: applies reviewability of the actual judicial certificate proceeding; review creates no certificate or lower appellate jurisdiction.
- Slack v. McDaniel: applies the separation of review, certification, classification and relief; its exhaustion-return holding does not itself decide a final-default Rule 60 request.
- Felker v. Turpin: preserves the bar on actual authorization review and its provision-specific temporal application.
- Stewart v. Martinez-Villareal: applies inquiry into the actual threshold category, while retaining its earlier premature-and-reserved-claim limits.
- Lindh v. Murphy and Carriger v. Stewart: preserve the identified pre-AEDPA merits treatment of the April 23, 1996 application without exempting a later authorization proceeding.
- Calderon v. Thompson: preserves the distinct ordinary review channel and exceptional requirements for reopening completed habeas merits; it does not equate every genuine process correction with merits reconsideration.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel: preserves the unresolved effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review; Rule 39's effect must be decided in the relevant Rule 60 inquiry.
- Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson: preserves the requirement for an actual source of judicial authority; auxiliary power supplies no missing appellate jurisdiction.


## Selected opening holding — criminal-procedure


#### Artuz v. Bennett, 531 U.S. 4

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1238.
**Decided:** November 7, 2000.

##### Proper filing of a collateral application containing claims subject to mandatory relief bars

**Holding:** Section 2244(d)(2) treats a state collateral application as properly filed when its delivery and acceptance satisfy the requirements for filing that application. The absence of a written state denial is Bennett's allegation and the lower court's assumption, not this Court's finding. Actual pendency, transitional grace periods, exhaustion, procedural default, and habeas entitlement remain unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices.

**Material application:** Atlas's Mexican partners made it nondiverse from Grupo when it sued. Their pretrial departure changed Atlas's attributed citizenship, not the identity of the plaintiff. Their appearance on a counterclaim does not transform membership withdrawal into a court-ordered dismissal curing the original plaintiff's citizenship.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1653 cures defective allegations, not the missing jurisdictional facts. Lawful dismissal of an actual dispensable party, with dispensability and prejudice review, remains a distinct cure. Expense, consent and delayed objection cannot supply jurisdiction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for dismissal without prejudice. The verdict supports no federal merits judgment, and any dependent judgment must be vacated; a new action must independently establish jurisdiction and satisfy applicable limitations.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Carden attributes all partners' citizenship, and Mollan fixes the commencement inquiry. Newman-Green preserves actual-party dismissal under its conditions. Caterpillar's cure by lawful dismissal producing complete diversity before trial and through judgment does not extend to changed citizenship of the same party. Kontrick distinguishes subject-matter jurisdiction from forfeitable processing rules.


## Current-term public law or published positions


### 2005-03-30 — Rhines v. Weber

**Holdings.**

#### Retaining a timely mixed petition during exhaustion

**Holding and operative rule.** A district court may stay a timely mixed petition while the prisoner exhausts state remedies if the prisoner shows good cause for not exhausting first, the unexhausted claims are not plainly meritless, and the record does not show abusive litigation or intentional delay. Good cause means a reasonable explanation in the actual circumstances, not an extraordinary-circumstances or procedural-default test; the court must set reasonable, definite deadlines for seeking state relief and returning to federal court, accounting for delays outside the prisoner’s control.

**Authority.** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete framework. Justice Souter joins the existence of stay power and the merit, anti-abuse and deadline limits, but not the initial good-cause prerequisite.

**Controlling explanation.** Rose v. Lundy gives state courts the first opportunity to decide federal claims. Congress’s exhaustion and limitations rules can serve that purpose while a federal court retains a timely filed action. Landis v. North American Co. supplies ordinary control over pending cases, exercised here within the habeas statutes. Duncan v. Walker excludes time spent in federal court from statutory tolling under §2244(d)(2); retention preserves an existing action rather than supplying a new filing period. Pliler v. Ford left this stay question open while establishing separate protections against particular court-caused filing losses.

The warden correctly argues that routine, open-ended stays would weaken finality and reward premature filing. Cause, a potential-merit screen, exclusion of abuse and definite deadlines answer that objection. Good cause preserves an incentive to exhaust before filing and permits an individualized explanation. Its reasonable-explanation meaning also avoids turning technical uncertainty about exhaustion into a second, rigid procedural-default obstacle. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on its merits; it does not compel denial. Relief remains subject to exhaustion, absence of an available state corrective process, circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant’s rights, or counsel’s express waiver of exhaustion for the State, as the statute provides.

Rhines filed with more than eleven months remaining before limitations expired during federal processing. The district court identified eight unexhausted claims and imposed sixty-day initiation and return conditions. Those facts establish the practical importance of stay authority. Actual cause, potential merit, delay and the reasonableness of those conditions require findings under the announced standard.

#### Protecting timely review when applying that discretion

**Holding and operative rule.** Where good cause, potential merit and absence of intentional delay are established and dismissal would defeat timely federal review, denying a stay and dismissing the entire petition ordinarily abuses discretion; the court should retain the action, and any refusal requires a reason compatible with the statute. If a stay is inappropriate, the prisoner must be allowed to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims when whole-petition dismissal would unreasonably impair federal relief.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join this sufficient case for retention and the deletion protection. Justice Souter’s agreement does not make good cause a necessary condition in his separate view.

**Controlling explanation.** The exhaustion requirement directs claims first to the state courts; it does not require destroying a timely federal action whenever the two processes overlap. The filing date and continued pendency therefore matter independently of statutory tolling. A refusal must apply the governing predicates and account for its actual effect on federal review. If retention is unsuitable, deletion preserves the exhausted claims without demanding adjudication of the others. Pliler’s neutral-warning duty retains its particular triggers—an unrepresented prisoner and a qualifying court-directed dismissal election. Rhines had appointed counsel for his amended federal petition, so that duty does not automatically govern his election.

**Precedent Treatment.**

Rose v. Lundy’s total-exhaustion requirement remains operative with bounded stay-and-abeyance in the limitations setting. Duncan v. Walker retains its statutory non-tolling rule; this decision answers the distinct stay question. Pliler v. Ford retains its separate notice, causation, diligence and lawful-remedy rules. Landis v. North American Co. supplies ordinary management authority subject to the governing habeas statutes.

**Law After Decision.**

District courts have a uniform standard for retaining timely mixed petitions while state remedies are pursued. The rule preserves the existing filing and requires prompt exhaustion and return; reopening a terminated action still requires an independently valid procedural ground. The actual stay predicates and reasonable deadlines are matters of application under settled standards, including the deletion protection when whole-petition dismissal would unreasonably impair federal review.

### 2005-04-27 — Pace v. DiGuglielmo; No. 03-9627; 544 U.S. 408.

**Opinion authority.**

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas. STEVENS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

The Court's five-Justice coalition—Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas—joins Part I on statutory filing and Part II on assumed equitable authority and diligence. The dissent addresses statutory exclusion and does not reach independent equity.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas | Parts I and II: statutory filing and separate assumed-equity diligence |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Statutory tolling; independent equity not reached |

**Holdings.**

The five Justices joining the Court's opinion adopt both holdings and their controlling explanations.

#### 1. An exception-bearing deadline remains a genuine filing condition

**Rule and authority.** A state collateral application rejected under an applicable application-filing deadline is not properly filed under §2244(d)(2) merely because that deadline contains exceptions the application does not satisfy. Federal courts identify the operative state rule and its authoritative application, distinguish rules directed solely at claim relief, and require the applicant to establish the dates and qualifying state pendency supporting the claimed exclusion; Part I controls that inquiry.

**Controlling explanation.** Artuz separates conditions governing an application's delivery and acceptance from rules governing relief on its claims. A filing condition can require judicial evaluation; its function, rather than a clerk's acceptance, controls. Exceptions supply additional permissible filing routes.

Pennsylvania's §9545(b)(1) requires any petition, including a second or later petition, within one year of finality unless the petition alleges and the petitioner proves one of three grounds: prior failure to present the claim resulted from governmental interference violating Pennsylvania or federal constitutional or statutory law; predicate facts were unknown and could not have been ascertained through due diligence; or a constitutional right was recognized after the ordinary period by this Court or Pennsylvania's Supreme Court and held retroactive by that recognizing court. Appointed and retained defense counsel are excluded from government officials. An exception petition must also be filed within sixty days after the claim could have been presented. Finality follows completion of direct review, including discretionary review in either Supreme Court, or expiration of review time. For judgments already final on January 16, 1996, the amendments' transition permits a first petition within one year of that effective date. Pace's petition was his second.

These provisions govern filing, separately from §9543's relief eligibility. The state appellate court held that Pace satisfied neither the deadline nor an exception. Carey gives effect to that determination despite the trial court's earlier merits discussion. The November 27, 1996–July 29, 1999 state-review interval therefore supplies no statutory exclusion.

Pace answers that claim-dependent exceptions resemble Artuz's relief bars and that exhaustion can consume federal time before the State decides timeliness. But legal and factual adjudication can enforce a filing prerequisite. Congress requires both proper filing and pendency; the latter cannot replace the former. The applicant must establish the dates and qualifying state pendency supporting the claimed exclusion.

#### 2. The asserted equitable protection fails on reasonable diligence

**Rule and authority.** Assuming the broader equitable authority invoked here exists, the prisoner must establish an extraordinary impediment and reasonable diligence in pursuing the rights for which protection is sought. Part II holds that Pace's supported course of pursuit fails the required diligence showing for this asserted interval and therefore denies that protection, while preserving Pliler's existing rule for proved court-caused loss.

**Controlling explanation.** Pace attributes the lost federal time to the combination of uncertain Pennsylvania timing law and circuit law requiring exhaustion. That explanation concerns pursuit of the second state petition. It does not establish reasonable pursuit across the preceding years or the five months after state review ended.

The sentencing and plea grounds concerned facts available in 1986; Pace raised a version of the plea claim that year. The assistance ground concerned events in or before 1991. His first collateral proceeding ended in September 1992; the second began November 27, 1996. The family affidavits concerned a meeting with Pace and counsel in 1985 or 1986. Their old subject matter does not itself establish when those affidavits were signed or received. State review ended July 29, 1999, and federal filing followed December 24. The asserted trap does not account for the material gaps in pursuing the available grounds. The established chronology and asserted obstacle permit resolution of diligence without a further factual remand.

Diligence means reasonable pursuit in the circumstances, rather than the fastest conceivable action. The earlier years inform that inquiry; they are not charged against a federal limitation before its enactment. Pliler protects proved court-caused loss through the required causation, diligence and lawful remedy. Pace identifies no corresponding court-directed dismissal election or materially misleading federal direction. Rhines permits retention of a timely mixed petition under its reasonable-explanation, potential-merit, anti-abuse and deadline conditions. Diligence here is assessed against the procedures available when Pace acted, without imposing a later protective-filing duty retroactively.

The Court rests on the unmet diligence requirement, rather than the Third Circuit's capital/noncapital distinction. This record-specific conclusion neither makes five months invariably excessive nor resolves whether the asserted legal uncertainty was an extraordinary impediment. The full range of equitable authority beyond Pliler's established protection remains outside the grounds and facts necessary to resolve this application.

**Precedent Treatment.**

- **Artuz v. Bennett:** Its distinction between application-filing conditions and claim-relief bars governs; the reserved question concerning deadline exceptions is resolved for genuine filing deadlines that the application does not satisfy.
- **Carey v. Saffold:** Reaffirmed in giving effect to the actual state untimeliness determination despite alternative merits reasoning; qualifying state-review pendency still depends on the operative state rules.
- **Duncan v. Walker:** Reaffirmed in its separate statutory treatment of state collateral pendency and federal pendency; equitable protection retains its own predicates.
- **Pliler v. Ford:** Its protection for omission of required notice in a qualifying court-directed dismissal election or materially misleading judicial direction remains effective when the petitioner proves causal loss, reasonable diligence and a lawful effective remedy. The present asserted state-exhaustion obstacle has different predicates.
- **Rhines v. Weber:** Its authority to retain a timely mixed petition remains subject to a reasonable explanation for nonexhaustion, claims not plainly meritless, absence of abuse or intentional delay, and reasonable definite deadlines. Its later clarification supplies no retroactive protective-filing duty here.

**Law After Decision.**

Genuine state application-filing deadlines remain conditions of §2244(d)(2) even when exceptions require judicial findings. Courts must distinguish those conditions from claim-relief bars and calculate qualifying state pendency from the actual dates and authoritative state-law determinations. They must separately address supported equitable grounds under their own predicates. Pliler's established protection for court-caused loss remains available; the broader theory assumed here requires reasonable diligence in the actual circumstances.



## Current-term public law or published positions


### 2005-05-23 — Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.; No. 04-163.

**Holdings.**

#### Regulatory efficacy does not independently establish a taking

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure substantially to advance a legitimate governmental objective is not, by itself, a taking requiring compensation under the Fifth Amendment. A claimant must establish the relevant property burden or demanded dedication under the applicable taking rule; an economic-efficacy trial cannot substitute for that showing, whether the challenged regulation is general or an individual permit denial.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion supplies nine direct joins; the same coalition joins Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** The compensation command addresses the nature and burden of governmental interference with property. An ineffective regulation may impose little loss, while an effective one may take property and require payment. Efficacy cannot distinguish those cases. Loretto identifies a compelled permanent occupation; Lucas addresses complete loss of beneficial use; Penn Central and Tahoe-Sierra examine the continuing property interest and the actual regulatory burden. Nollan and Dolan address the distinct demand to surrender identified property as a condition of permission. None makes ordinary economic disagreement the constitutional event. Chevron's strongest objection is reliance on the real purpose-relationship holding in Del Monte Dunes. Restricting that holding to individual denials would retain the same source error: ineffective means do not become a taking merely because they concern one parcel. Independently unlawful or arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable constitutional protections, including due process, with their own elements and remedies. Compensation is not a license to undertake action forbidden by those independent protections.

#### The earlier efficacy holding is overruled at its precise scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Agins and Del Monte Dunes are overruled insofar as they recognize lack of substantial advancement as an independent taking predicate; Del Monte Dunes's allocation of that displaced substantive issue likewise ceases to govern. Its final judgment and award, independent complete-deprivation ground, municipal compensation rules, finality rulings, and constitutional jury entitlement and task allocation for surviving legal claims remain in force.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion expressly changes the earlier controlling rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Del Monte Dunes sustained an undifferentiated award on sufficient evidence supporting both submitted alternatives. Its purpose-relationship ground was an actual holding. The Court therefore confronts and withdraws that ground instead of dismissing it as dictum. Stare decisis carries particular weight where property and transactions are arranged under judicial rules, but it does not justify retaining a liability predicate unrelated to the burden that the compensation command identifies. Preserving final judgments and independent valid grounds respects those interests without perpetuating the mismatch in pending and future cases. Rejecting the substantive efficacy issue also removes the occasion for assigning that issue to a jury; it does not abolish a jury right for an otherwise valid legal damages claim. The replacement rule governs this case and other cases still open on direct review where the issue is properly presented. The completed Del Monte Dunes compensation judgment remains final.

#### The surviving property rules govern distinct interferences

**Holding and operative rule:** Compelled permanent physical occupation follows Loretto; total elimination of beneficial use follows Lucas, subject to substantiated background property or nuisance limits already inherent in ownership; other regulatory burdens follow Penn Central's inquiry into economic impact, interference with distinct investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action. For an individualized development-permit condition demanding identified property interests, the government must establish Nollan's essential nexus and demonstrate Dolan's rough proportionality between the dedication's nature and extent and the development's effects, allowing reasonable estimates without exact mathematical equivalence or unique attribution to the project.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion preserves these rules as the operative alternatives to the rejected predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The court must classify the actual interference before choosing its test. Tahoe-Sierra preserves examination of a continuing property interest rather than manufacture of total loss by temporal segmentation. Lucas requires the State to substantiate an antecedent ownership limitation rather than rely on a new legislative harm label. Dolan requires a connection between the nature and extent of demanded property and the development's effects, a distinct burden that ordinary scrutiny of regulatory effectiveness cannot replace. These rules retain their own triggers, proof and consequences.

#### Chevron's selected theory cannot sustain the judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment resting solely on failure of substantial advancement must be reversed, and relief based on that predicate must be removed. Chevron's surviving efficacy theory therefore fails; independently preserved claims require their own legal and procedural basis.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion supplies the judgment and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The economic testimony addressed whether Hawaii's cap would achieve its objectives. That inquiry establishes neither the nature nor the magnitude of a taking. Chevron's reviewed theory does not invoke a physical occupation, total deprivation or demanded conveyance. The lower court must reject the chosen predicate and identify any independently preserved matter actually remaining. A distinct due-process or compensation claim is not presented for decision here, and a remand cannot create one by relabeling the failed theory.

**Precedent Treatment.**

- Agins v. City of Tiburon is overruled only insofar as lack of substantial advancement supplies an independent taking ground.
- Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey is overruled at its accepted purpose-relationship alternative and the assignment of that displaced issue. Its final award, independent complete-deprivation holding, municipal compensation route, damages and finality rules, and jury entitlement and allocation for surviving claims remain controlling.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp. retains the categorical compelled-permanent-occupation rule.
- Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council retains the total-beneficial-use rule and substantiated background-title limitation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City retains the contextual property-burden inquiry.
- Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency retains its continuing-property treatment and rejection of automatic temporal segmentation.
- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission and Dolan v. City of Tigard retain their distinct nexus and rough-proportionality requirements for demanded property dedications.

**Law After Decision.**

Regulatory-taking liability can no longer rest solely on a mismatch between a regulation and its asserted objective. That change reaches general rules and particular denials, while the recognized occupation, deprivation, regulatory-burden and dedication rules remain available on their own predicates. Independently arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable due-process law. The new rule applies in this case and properly presented cases still open on direct review; final compensation judgments retain their finality.

## Published public-use explanation and separate agency objection

#### Public use despite private reconveyance

A compensated condemnation serving a rationally supported public passenger-service purpose satisfies the Fifth Amendment’s public-use requirement even when title is reconveyed to another private railroad. The ICC’s determination that this transaction facilitates Amtrak service meets that requirement.

This holding and explanation control in Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia and Souter: six Justices support them.

The Court holds that transfer to a private railroad does not by itself negate public use. Midkiff sustains a condemnation rationally related to a conceivable public purpose, including a subsequent private transfer; Berman likewise recognizes that private participation can implement a public project. Here the conveyance supports restoration and maintenance of passenger service over the Montrealer route while securing Amtrak’s continuing use. The ICC’s public-purpose determination is rational, and the record supplies no basis for treating the arrangement as naked private favoritism. Carmack’s concern about limited delegations to private entities does not alter the result: the condemnation power here belongs to the ICC, which must apply the statute and provide compensation. The Court need not predict whether every operational objective will be achieved. It decides the public-use objection, leaving the amount of just compensation and the remaining statutory review issues unresolved.

Hawaii Housing Authority v. Midkiff, 467 U.S. 229, is applied: rational public purpose can support condemnation followed by private transfer.

Berman v. Parker, 348 U.S. 26, is applied: private implementation does not defeat an otherwise public purpose.

United States v. Carmack, 329 U.S. 230, is distinguished: the condemnation power here is exercised by a government commission.

Section 562(d) allows the approved service-connected acquisition and reconveyance. Judicial deference remains tied to an interpretation actually discernible in the agency’s decision. The conjunctive rebuttal, inability-to-agree finding, compensation obligation and passenger-service boundary retain distinct work; no condemnation power for naked private favoritism follows.

White, joined by Blackmun and Thomas, accepts that the statute permits acquisition followed by reconveyance. They would require the ICC to explain and apply the service-need condition itself, treating counsel’s useful-or-appropriate construction as a later rationalization. They rely on State Farm, Chenery and Burlington Truck Lines to require the agency itself to supply the explanation, and do not endorse the circuit’s categorical permanent-ownership rule. Their agreement about possible statutory power is not a join in the Court’s approval of this order.

Both cases return to the D.C. Circuit for the compensation challenges and other outstanding review issues. The Court does not itself fix a new valuation, resolve distinct regulatory claims, or schedule further Supreme Court proceedings. The decision resolves Supreme Court review on the stated terms; lower-court proceedings do not imply retained Supreme Court supervision.
