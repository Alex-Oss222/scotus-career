## Event

Black Political Task Force v. Galvin; lower-court No. 02-11190, 300 F. Supp. 2d 291. Decided March 22, 2005, after full merits submission, on direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from the three-judge District of Massachusetts's June 21, 2004 final judgment. The questions concern section 2 liability for the challenged Boston-area district arrangement and the continuing effect of the injunction after enactment of Plan C.

## Holdings

### I. The continuing injunction leaves a live appeal

**Holding and authority — Part I, joined by all nine Justices.** Enactment of a replacement districting plan does not end an appeal seeking relief from a still-operative permanent injunction and its statutory liability predicate. The State remains subject to the June 21, 2004 restraint, which this Court can affirm or remove; the separate candidate-remedy issues for the completed 2004 election cycle remain moot.

**Controlling explanation.** The question is the practical effect of the judgment under review. Laidlaw distinguishes a change in conduct from disappearance of a controversy. Here the declaration and permanent prohibition continue to bind the election official even though the legislature has enacted Plan C. Review therefore can alter an existing legal obligation. Growe requires legal effect for a superseding state disposition, but does not require dismissal of an appeal from this continuing federal restraint. Treating the completed candidate requests separately respects the final judgment's actual scope.

### II. Actual electoral opportunity and independent totality govern the statutory claim

**Holding and authority — Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** A districting-based section 2 claimant must establish lawful compact district capacity, political cohesion and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the group's preferred candidates, subject to supported special circumstances, and must independently prove unequal electoral opportunity by a preponderance under the totality. District capacity may be shown through a lawful compact majority district or Valdespino's rigorous single-group dependable-crossover route; neither another feasible district nor satisfaction of the preliminary conditions creates a presumption of liability.

**Controlling explanation.** Gingles supplies the causal conditions. De Grandy separates those conditions from the ultimate injury and rejects maximization. Valdespino permits a single cohesive group to prove dependable ability to elect with crossover support, while preserving lawful geography, population, causal proof and independent totality; speculative alliances, isolated success and mere influence do not suffice. Martinez requires actual local nomination and election evidence rather than racial labels or a projected vote threshold.

Proportionality is relevant evidence, neither a safe harbor nor an affirmative defense shifting the burden. Demonstrated crossover opportunity belongs in that assessment, and one district may afford opportunity to more than one group. Candidate race is not voter preference. Incumbency and unopposed contests may be relevant special circumstances, but success cannot be discarded merely because a candidate is an incumbent or is white. Opportunity elsewhere cannot offset a proved local impairment, and discriminatory purpose is not an additional element of this results claim.

The supported compact alternative here satisfies the majority-district route under the material demographic measures. The trial findings establish cohesion and the required bloc voting. Its election-specific treatment of exogenous contests, incumbency and cross-racial preferences supports those findings. The lower court nevertheless erred by excluding crossover opportunity from its proportionality numerator and suggesting that these preliminary conditions presumptively establish the ultimate violation.

### III. The supported local packing findings establish unequal opportunity independently of those errors

**Holding and authority — Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** The Count I judgment stands because the supported precinct-transfer, population, voting and feasible-alternative findings independently establish that the challenged configuration concentrated Black voters so as to deny equal electoral opportunity. Protecting incumbents does not excuse that proved injury; racial concentration, racial purpose or the possibility of another majority district alone would not establish it.

**Controlling explanation.** De Grandy forbids using aggregate opportunity to excuse actual local packing. Voinovich requires proof of unequal opportunity rather than condemnation of a heavily minority district by its composition. Martinez likewise asks whether the actual arrangement and a supported lawful alternative establish the local impairment.

The legislature removed six predominantly Black precincts from the 11th and 12th districts, which needed population, and placed them in the already heavily Black 6th, which did not need them. The findings connect those transfers and the Fitzgerald Amendment to protection of white incumbents and impaired electoral opportunity. Supported polarized-voting findings and the workable compact alternative establish the practical effect of that concentration. This is proof of an electoral injury, not merely an unfilled district target.

The reassessment opinion rightly insists that the lower court's numerical and presumptive errors be rejected. But the trial court separately examined actual crossover successes, including minority-preferred white and Hispanic candidates, and explained the relevant election circumstances. Those findings, together with the concrete precinct changes, establish the denial without excluding crossover opportunity from consideration or shifting the voters' burden. Affirmance thus rests on the independent lawful findings, not on treating erroneous arithmetic as harmless wording. Section 2's established districting coverage supplies the legal basis; the access-only construction urged by the other dissent is inconsistent with Gingles and the governing De Grandy, Valdespino and Martinez line.

### IV. The existing seventeen-district remedy remains fitted to the violation

**Holding and authority — Part III, joined by the same five Justices.** The Count I declaration, permanent prohibition on elections under the 2001 configuration in Suffolk House districts 1–15 and 17–18, and reasonable fees and costs as provided by law are affirmed. Plan C, approved by the district court and enacted unchanged as chapter 74 of the Acts of 2004, remains operative; modification of the continuing injunction requires an ordinary equitable showing that the proposed change fully cures the proved arrangement.

**Controlling explanation.** Growe preserves the State's first opportunity to choose a timely lawful correction. The district court afforded that opportunity, and the legislature exercised it. The violation operates through interrelated boundaries binding all affected voters; relief confined to the named plaintiffs would not cure it. The seventeen-district scope corresponds to the arrangement adjudicated below, and the State has established no narrower cure. The enacted replacement supplies the current implementation rather than a new judicial map. A party seeking a narrower injunction must establish that it remains sufficient to remedy the proved violation. The affirmed fee entitlement retains its statutory qualifications, with calculation left to the proper fee proceeding. Counts II and III remain dismissed. The district court retains ordinary implementation and modification authority consistent with the judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30:** applies the causal voting conditions and contextual totality inquiry; preliminary conditions remain necessary rather than presumptively sufficient, subject to Valdespino's existing modification of the capacity condition.
- **Johnson v. De Grandy, 512 U.S. 997, June 30, 1994:** applies relevant but nonconclusive proportional opportunity, the claimant's independent burden and the prohibition on maximization or geographic offsets. The concrete independent packing findings distinguish this record from a demand for every feasible additional district.
- **Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District, June 17, 1999:** preserves its rigorous single-group crossover-capacity route and ordinary demographic and disclosure rules. This case meets the retained majority-district route and does not extend the functional alternative.
- **Martinez v. Bush, April 29, 2004:** applies actual local electoral performance, distinct proof quantities, preponderance and the requirement of a supported improving alternative; preserves the separation between racial statutory claims and partisan constitutional claims.
- **Voinovich v. Quilter, 507 U.S. 146, March 2, 1993:** applies the difference between a State's lawful districting choice and a court's remedy for established unequal opportunity; a district's racial composition alone is insufficient.
- **Growe v. Emison, 507 U.S. 25, February 23, 1993:** applies local causal proof and legislative remedial priority. Its superseding-state-judgment rule does not eliminate review of the permanent federal restraint that remains here.
- **Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw, January 12, 2000:** applies the distinction between changed conduct and disappearance of effective judicial relief; the injunction itself supplies the continuing controversy here.
- **Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630, June 28, 1993:** preserves the separate constitutional injury inquiry; it adds no constitutional-injury or discriminatory-purpose element to the section 2 results claim.

## Law After Decision

Section 2 requires independent proof of practical unequal opportunity, with dependable crossover opportunity included in the evidence and the claimant retaining the ultimate burden. Supported local packing can establish the violation despite electoral successes elsewhere, but neither racial population figures nor another feasible majority district creates an entitlement. Here the independently sufficient precinct and electoral findings sustain the final injunction notwithstanding rejection of the lower court's erroneous numerical comparison and presumption. The existing single-group capacity routes, separate constitutional limits and ordinary evidence rules retain their force.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, concurring in Parts I and II and dissenting from Part III and the judgment.** They accept the live appeal, governing section 2 coverage, supported causal findings and the Court's corrections to the legal test. De Grandy makes actual opportunity relevant without establishing either a quota or a safe harbor; Martinez requires applying that rule to the local proof. In their view, expressly excluding crossover opportunity from a principal proportionality comparison and treating the preliminary conditions presumptively could have affected the trial court's ultimate totality assessment. The concrete packing evidence could sustain liability, but the district court must weigh it under the corrected standard. They would vacate Count I liability and its dependent declaration, injunction and fee basis and remand for reassessment, preserving supported subsidiary findings. Plan C remains enacted, and any interim federal restraint must rest on ordinary equitable grounds. Neither apparent aggregate proportionality nor an additional feasible majority district dictates the outcome.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurring in Part I and dissenting from the statutory holdings and judgment.** They would reverse and direct dismissal of Count I, with dissolution of its dependent federal injunction and fee basis. Their continuing construction, stated in their De Grandy, Valdespino and Martinez positions, confines section 2 to equal voting access, casting and counting rather than judicial allocation of electoral strength through district boundaries. They retain a results rule for practices within that coverage; the objection is not a demand for discriminatory intent. The statutory text, in their view, supplies no benchmark for the districting allocation required by the Court's dilution framework. If that framework is applied, the lower court's majority-only comparison and presumption furnish a further reason to reject its analysis, but that conditional objection is not a join in the reassessment mandate or the Court's findings of unequal opportunity. Reversal of the federal statutory judgment would leave enacted Plan C and dismissal of Counts II and III in place.

## Procedure After Action

The affirmed Count I declaration and permanent injunction remain effective. Election officials continue under enacted Plan C, subject to ordinary implementation and lawful modification of the existing judgment. The statutory fee proceeding determines reasonable fees and costs. Counts II and III remain dismissed, and the conceded mootness of the remaining 2004 candidate-remedy issues stands.

## Holdings

### I. The writ application and state merits date determine the applicable review

**Holding and authority — Part I, joined by all nine Justices.** The ordinary AEDPA provisions governing adjudication apply to Payton's post-April 24, 1996 application for the writ, although counsel and interim-protection proceedings began earlier. Section 2254(d)(1) tests the state court's November 23, 1992 merits decision against this Court's holdings established by that date; relief requires a contrary governing rule or an objectively unreasonable application, not merely an independently incorrect result.

**Controlling explanation.** Garceau distinguishes an application requesting adjudication from proceedings preparing for it, with substance rather than caption controlling. Payton's earlier proceedings sought counsel and interim protection; the writ application followed enactment. Williams gives independent work to the statute's contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses and permits application of clearly established general rules without a factual twin. Lockyer preserves the state-decision-date substantive cutoff and separates statutory eligibility from constitutional entitlement. Early and Visciotti require examination of the state's actual reasoning and complete context, rather than a citation checklist, a count of judges or a factual clear-and-convincing burden imported into the legal question. Later decisions explaining federal review do not turn their later substantive holdings into law established in 1992.

### II. The state court's contextual conclusion is not objectively unreasonable

**Holding and authority — Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.** On this complete instructional and advocacy context, the California Supreme Court could reasonably find no reasonable likelihood that jurors understood themselves forbidden to give Payton's postcrime mitigation lawful effect. Its rejection of the claim is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of the governing 1992 law, so section 2254(d)(1) bars the habeas relief awarded on this claim.

**Controlling explanation.** Boyde requires the charge and trial to be considered together. Lockett and Eddings distinguish legal exclusion from a sentencer's choice of weight, and Skipper establishes the relevance of postcrime adjustment. Applying those governing protections, the Court acknowledges the substantial objection here: the prosecutor repeatedly argued that the postcrime evidence was legally unavailable; the judge's response did not expressly correct that proposition. The judge's private broad understanding of factor (k) never reached the jury. The direction to consider all evidence was expressly subject to later instructions, which listed the statutory factors, including the crime-centered factor (k), and directed weighing of applicable factors. It was not an unqualified corrective instruction.

The entire presentation nevertheless permits the state court's conclusion. Eight witnesses supplied the substantial conversion and prison-conduct case; the defense forcefully explained why it could support life; the charge identified the court as the source of law; and the prosecutor also contested sincerity and argued that the conversion was outweighed. Admission, defense advocacy or an alternative weight argument is not independently a cure. Together with the full charge, however, these circumstances permit a reasonable adjudicator to conclude that the evidence remained available for the sentencing choice.

The dissent reasonably stresses the risk that the qualified charge reinforced legal exclusion. The statutory question is whether that reading is compelled, not whether it is the reviewing court's preferred constitutional answer. The state decision remains within the objective range allowed by Boyde. The Court therefore resolves the statutory issue without deciding the independent constitutional-error or harmlessness questions; the statutory bar is sufficient to dispose of the claim.

## Law After Decision

Section 2254(d)(1) requires objective review of the state court's actual application of law established at the state merits date. A possible independent constitutional error does not itself satisfy that restriction. The specific combination of the qualified charge, repeated exclusion argument, extensive mitigation presentation and competing advocacy here permits the state court's conclusion within that statutory range. The holding establishes neither a general cure by admission or defense argument nor categorical approval of factor (k). The independent mitigation and actual-effect rules retain their existing force for claims that lawfully reach those questions.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Part II and the judgment; Stone-Zsela joins Part I of this separate opinion.** Part I finds both constitutional error and an objectively unreasonable state application. Skipper made relevant postcrime adjustment legally available, while Eddings distinguished exclusion from weight. The prosecutor repeatedly told this jury that the defense's principal mitigating case could not count. The court declined to correct that legal statement, and the instruction to consider all evidence was qualified by later directions that included factor (k)'s crime-centered wording. Defense advocacy was not authoritative legal authorization. The alternative attack on sincerity did not withdraw the exclusion claim. These features distinguish Boyde and, in the dissent's view, exceed the range of reasonable application rather than merely warrant a different independent answer.

Parts II and III, joined only by Stevens and Ginsburg, separately determine actual effect and would affirm sentence-only habeas relief. Under Brecht, O'Neal, Coleman and Penry II, the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. The dissent considers the murder, sexual violence, other attacks, earlier violence and incriminating statements alongside the eight witnesses' conversion and conduct evidence and both sides' arguments. That aggravation could justify death in a lawful proceeding, but it does not establish what this jury did with its principal reason for choosing life after being told that reason was legally unavailable. Neither the admission of evidence nor the alternative weight argument establishes that the jurors instead weighed it. The State therefore fails its burden on the existing record. This is a separate actual-effect determination, not a structural-error rule or automatic relief from every Boyde violation. The dissent would preserve conditional sentence relief allowing a reasonable court-fixed opportunity for a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Part II and the judgment.** He joins Souter's Part I because the uncorrected legal exclusion of the only affirmative mitigation makes the state application objectively unreasonable and creates a reasonable likelihood that jurors treated the evidence as legally unavailable. The qualified general direction to consider evidence and the alternative prosecution argument about weight do not resolve that legal-use problem. He withholds Souter's completed harmlessness determination and proposed affirmance of final relief. He would affirm the statutory-gateway and constitutional-error determinations, vacate the final remedial component, and require a reasoned whole-record harmlessness adjudication with the State bearing the beyond-reasonable-doubt burden. The reviewing court must obtain and examine the full existing sentencing record and permit focused submissions; strong aggravation does not shift the burden or replace inquiry into the actual sentence. If the State fails, a conditional sentence writ must set and enforce a reasonable court-fixed period for a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. The convictions and unrelated habeas claims remain outside that relief.

The case returns for implementation of the reversal of penalty habeas relief on this claim. The convictions remain intact, and unrelated habeas claims proceed according to their own lawful posture.

## Event

**Muehler v. Mena**, No. 03-1423. Argued December 8, 2004; decided March 22, 2005. On review of the Ninth Circuit, 332 F.3d 1255. ## Holdings

**1. A proper premises search permits detention of occupants and reasonable initial safety measures.** Part I controls with all nine Justices. Officers executing this weapons warrant could detain the occupants and initially use handcuffs to secure the premises against the anticipated armed-gang danger. Individual suspicion that each occupant committed an offense is not a prerequisite to that search-related detention.

Summers supplies authority to control occupants while a lawful search is executed, protecting officer safety and orderly completion. Graham separately measures the force used by objective reasonableness. The anticipated weapons risk justified securing four occupants at the outset. Mena's status as an occupant who was not suspected of crime therefore does not itself invalidate the initial control. That detention authority and the reasonableness of its manner remain distinct inquiries.

**2. Continued handcuffing requires continuing objective justification under the actual conditions.** Part II controls with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Courts must assess the risks, degree of control, duration, restraint-related harm and available supervision as conditions develop, preserving the historical facts supported by the verdict. The lawful start of restraint cannot conclusively establish its reasonableness throughout the search; the Ninth Circuit must review the continued-force ground on that basis.

Graham supplies objective review of the force actually applied. Holland explains why a reasonable initial measure can become excessive as its justification changes. The weapons search, four adults and limited guards carry substantial weight, as the partial dissent emphasizes. They must be considered together with the supported account of compliance, control, discomfort, requests for release and the course of the search. A general force verdict does not establish every subsidiary fact, but the reviewing court must preserve the historical facts the evidence supports in its favor. Neither an appellate preference for a different tactic nor a categorical approval of all search-period cuffs supplies that review. The Constitution requires reasonable conduct, not the least intrusive feasible arrangement or a fixed handcuff time limit.

**3. The subject of an ordinary question does not alone create an additional seizure.** Part I controls with all nine Justices. Immigration questions during an otherwise lawful detention require no separate reasonable suspicion solely because they concern immigration, provided they add neither detention nor a distinct coercive intrusion. Actual added restraint must have its own adequate justification.

Bostick distinguishes a request for information from a seizure. When a person is already lawfully detained, the relevant inquiry concerns what the questioning adds to that restraint. Caballes preserves the need for independently lawful custody and attention to the protected interest actually invaded; it supplies no general permission for every investigative technique that takes no extra time. The Ninth Circuit's topic-based ground therefore fails. Whether these questions actually extended custody remains part of the factual duration inquiry.

**4. Detention must remain tied to an adequate lawful basis, and the verdict must be reviewed by its surviving grounds.** Part III controls with all nine Justices. The lower court must separately decide the preserved claim of post-search detention, identifying the actual interval, reasonable completion tasks and any independent lawful basis. It must then determine what the legally valid force and duration grounds, supported findings and instructions establish about liability and damages.

Summers links its detention authority to execution of the search; a valid initial detention cannot indefinitely sustain later custody. The separate duration ground was not resolved below. Because compensatory damages were not allocated between force and duration, correction of one theory does not by itself settle the whole award. The court must decide whether supported findings sustain an amount, require reduction or require a limited new trial; punitive damages retain their separate predicates and individual causation remains necessary.

The present questions concern constitutional detention, force and questioning. The officers' requested independent immunity ruling is outside that scope in this case: factual overlap and alternative briefing do not make the distinct conduct-date notice defense necessary to decide those questions. Any defense still properly available below remains subject to preservation, finality and the mandate. Its notice inquiry concerns the particular conduct on February 3, 1998; later decisions cannot supply earlier fair warning.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Michigan v. Summers:** Applied to occupant detention and initial control during this lawful search; its execution-related authority must remain tied to the detention's actual basis.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied to the objective reasonableness of the force used, separately from the authority to detain.
- **Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington:** Applied to continuing force as circumstances change; its current merits rule does not itself establish notice before its decision.
- **Florida v. Bostick:** Applied to distinguish ordinary questions from an added seizure, with attention to the restraint added when custody already exists.
- **Illinois v. Caballes:** Applied at its lawful-custody and protected-interest limits; absence of extra time alone does not authorize every distinct intrusion.
- **Brosseau v. Haugen** and **Back v. Hastings-on-Hudson:** Preserve the separate, particular conduct-date notice inquiry for any defense properly remaining below.

## Law After Decision

Search-related detention, the force used to maintain it, and any additional investigative restraint remain separate Fourth Amendment inquiries. Occupant status permits reasonable initial control; continued force depends on the actual changing conditions. An unrelated conversational subject alone supplies no additional seizure, while an extension of custody requires a lawful basis. These rules govern the remand; evaluation of the supported verdict facts and the award is ordinary application and remedial work.

## Separate Writings

Justice Kennedy, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, concurs in Parts I and III and dissents from Part II's continued-force disposition. They would hold the identified two-to-three-hour search-period restraint reasonable given the weapons search, four adults and one or two guards, even accepting the supported unfavorable factual account. Summers permits practical control during execution, and Graham does not require the best possible alternative staffing or restraint arrangement. Those circumstances, in their view, resolve this force ground for the officers.

Their conclusion carries concrete limits. Real pain or serious discomfort from prolonged cuffs requires officers to alter the conditions sufficiently to attend to the detainee's needs. Cuffs must be removed when an objectively reasonable officer would readily recognize that removal would neither compromise safety nor interfere with the search or cause substantial delay. Two to three hours calls for serious attention to continuing justification; it is not a categorical safe period. They join review of the separate post-search custody and damages issues, and the Court's present question-scope limit.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit to perform the force, duration and verdict review stated in the holdings and to order the further district-court proceedings that review requires. The mandate permits a limited new trial only where a material issue cannot be settled through the existing lawful verdict and appropriate appellate review.

## Event

**Rancho Palos Verdes v. Abrams**, No. 03-1601. Argued January 19, 2005; decided March 22, 2005. On review of the Ninth Circuit, 354 F.3d 1094. The question is whether 42 U.S.C. §1983 supplies an additional action enforcing the Telecommunications Act's wireless-siting restrictions alongside the Act's express expedited judicial remedy. The District Court granted statutory permit relief but rejected the additional action; the Court of Appeals allowed that action.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment insofar as it permits the additional statutory section 1983 action and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed in the reviewed part and remanded, 9-0. Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Abrams's additional action enforcing the same siting restrictions under section 1983 is foreclosed. Damages and section 1988 fees resting solely on that action must be denied. The direct statutory permit judgment remains in force; remaining properly preserved relief must be considered under its actual legal source.

## Holdings

**1. The specific thirty-day expedited siting action forecloses a parallel section 1983 action enforcing the same statutory restrictions.** The unanimous opinion controls. Assuming a qualifying individual right, the City bears and satisfies the burden of establishing incompatibility between the additional action and the personal judicial mechanism Congress supplied in 47 U.S.C. §332(c)(7)(B)(v). The holding governs enforcement of the restrictions in §332(c)(7)(B).

Blessing separates the existence of a right from Congress's displacement of the ordinary section 1983 route. Here Congress gave the person injured by final action or failure to act a direct action within thirty days and required expedited hearing and decision. An ordinary section 1983 action enforcing the same violation would not inherit those conditions and would make the specified timetable optional. Abrams's proposal to borrow the short period identifies the conflict but supplies no legal basis for creating a hybrid cause of action. This personal judicial arrangement differs from the funding administration that Bruneau found compatible with section 1983. Sea Clammers and Smith v. Robinson support exclusion when the actual statutory remedies conflict. The holding rests on this demonstrated incompatibility, not a general presumption that the existence of any express remedy defeats section 1983.

**2. The saving clause preserves independently existing law without supplying a second, inconsistent route for the new siting entitlement.** The unanimous opinion controls. Section 601(c)(1) preserves other law subject to the Act's express provisions, but it does not require the newly created siting rights to acquire an additional action bypassing their prescribed judicial procedure. Independently established rights remain enforceable subject to their own requirements.

Abrams reads the saving clause to preserve section 1983 and therefore his additional action. Section 1983 remains operative, but its application still includes the statutory-foreclosure inquiry. Giving the new right an incompatible second timetable would defeat the particular enforcement arrangement rather than preserve preexisting law. The separate authority to petition the FCC for the radio-frequency violation specified in clause (iv) retains its own subject; it does not replace the competent-court route applicable to the written-decision and substantial-evidence obligation in clause (iii).

**3. Relief must follow its surviving statutory basis.** The unanimous opinion controls. The failure of this section 1983 predicate defeats damages and section 1988 fees dependent exclusively upon it. The direct TCA permit relief survives, and the lower court must separate that judgment from the requests dependent on the foreclosed action.

The additional remedies Abrams seeks cannot cure the conflict in enforcement procedures. Conversely, denial of that route does not invalidate the direct statutory judgment already obtained. The absence of an express fee provision and the request for additional damages reinforce the statutory context but do not themselves establish universal remedial exclusivity. The complete direct-TCA damages question lies outside the enforcement-channel issue presented and remains open.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Blessing v. Freestone:** Applied. Identification of an individual right and foreclosure of section 1983 remain distinct inquiries; the defendant must establish the incompatible scheme.
- **Middlesex County Sewerage Authority v. National Sea Clammers Association:** Applied to the demonstrated conflict between enforcement routes, rather than to remedial silence alone.
- **Smith v. Robinson:** Applied for the consequence of an incompatible statutory remedial structure.
- **Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District:** Distinguished. General funding administration and an implied action there supplied no comparable incompatible personal filing and adjudication procedure.
- **Gonzaga University v. Doe:** Its controlling rejection of the particular FERPA claim remains intact; its broader nonmajority formulation supplies no universal rule here.
- **Alexander v. Sandoval** and **Powell v. Ridge:** Their prospective-enforcement holdings remain in force. The present decision concerns the conflicting mechanism of this specific statute.

## Law After Decision

The direct action in §332(c)(7)(B)(v) is the judicial enforcement route for the siting restrictions at issue. Its short filing period and mandatory expedition cannot be bypassed through a parallel statutory section 1983 claim. In other statutory settings, courts continue to identify the particular individual right and then test actual remedial incompatibility; general funding oversight alone does not establish the conflict proved here. The existing permit remedy remains operative.
