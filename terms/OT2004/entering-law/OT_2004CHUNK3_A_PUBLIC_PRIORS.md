### Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich, Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-3157 and 96-3159

Plenary merits decision, April 20, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Sixth Circuit’s final judgment, 130 F.3d 187, entered November 18, 1997.

The questions are whether Casey’s abortion-review standard or Salerno’s no-valid-applications formulation governs this facial challenge; whether Ohio’s D&X prohibition fails because of its definition, clinical reach, notice or criminal and civil culpability provisions; and whether postviability law must permit necessary abortion to avert severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. The Court addresses the complete Casey standard rather than adopting an independent numerical facial threshold.

Ohio’s H.B.135 created an all-stage method prohibition, a separate postviability prohibition, viability-testing requirements and related clinical duties. The district court’s preliminary injunction became permanent on January 12, 1996 after consolidation with trial by stipulation. The Sixth Circuit sustained an injunction against the whole Act, relying on the method definition’s overlap with D&E, clinical-fault concerns, serious mental-health exclusion and inseverability. Those legal conclusions remain subject to review. The lower standing ruling and the State’s failure to contest it in the court of appeals supply no new standing holding here.

Under §2919.17(A), purposeful performance, inducement or attempted performance or inducement of an abortion on a viable fetus is prohibited unless a physician, acting in good faith and using reasonable medical judgment, determines either that abortion is necessary to prevent maternal death or a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function, or that the fetus is not viable after the assessment required by §2919.18(A). The latter provision requires, beginning with the twenty-second week, a good-faith determination of nonviability using reasonable medical judgment, after examining the patient and performing or arranging the viability tests a reasonable physician would use. Its medical-emergency exception excuses that testing obligation. The separate twenty-four-week viability presumption is rebuttable.

Section 2919.16(F) defines the emergency through the physician’s good-faith, reasonable medical assessment of a pregnancy complication: immediate abortion must be needed to prevent the patient’s death or to avoid a serious risk, created by delay, of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Section 2919.16(J) separately defines that serious-risk category by a medically diagnosed condition complicating the pregnancy that directly or indirectly causes such impairment. Its nonexclusive examples are preeclampsia, inevitable abortion, prematurely ruptured membranes, diabetes and multiple sclerosis. These enacted categories remain distinct from the constitutional protection and the method-prohibition defenses addressed below.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at submission and decision. All nine participated in each component.

#### Holdings

##### 1. The complete Casey framework governs the facial and access claims

**Holding and operative rule.** Casey’s complete six-step standard, as applied in Fargo, governs the challenged previability regulation. Neither Salerno’s no-valid-applications barrier nor an independent numerical large-fraction threshold displaces that inquiry, and failure to establish a substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Authority.** Part I; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the governing standard, including its proof allocation and qualifications:

Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Burdens and proof.** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Controlling explanation.** Casey supplies the complete sequence and distinguishes protected choice from permissible regulation. Fargo applies it to an abortion-access challenge while separating clinical notice, actual fault and substantive care. The Sixth Circuit’s facial formula cannot substitute for this inquiry. Ohio’s law must be tested through its actual commands, exceptions, sanctions and practical operation. Overlap with a commonly used procedure can establish a real interference question without alone proving every application unconstitutional. Conversely, patients need not show that no one can comply before obtaining protection for a supported violation. The Court changes neither Casey’s framework nor its allocation of proof. Salerno supplies no independent barrier in this setting; its operation elsewhere is not decided. The final method applications and corresponding breadth of relief remain for the separate inquiry below.

##### 2. An act-defined method and professional clinical criteria are not categorically unintelligible

**Holding and operative rule.** Ohio’s method definition and specified good-faith, reasonable-medical-judgment criteria cannot be invalidated on the categorical ground that statutory and medical procedure labels overlap or that every subsidiary clinical judgment lacks an express culpability term. Courts must examine notice and enforcement guidance, actual fault for the relevant element, criminal and civil liability, and substantive access as distinct questions; this holding neither supplies a universal fault rule nor validates every enforcement.

**Authority.** Part II; Souter and all seven other Associates. Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 2919.15 defines the prohibited act through purposeful insertion of a suction device into the fetal skull to remove the brain, expressly excluding suction curettage and suction aspiration; subsection (B) addresses knowingly performing or attempting that act. Purposeful skull suction can occur during a procedure medically called D&E without making the statutory description unintelligible. The published testimony concerns purposeful conduct in some D&E procedures, not accidental contact or every D&E. Its substantive significance requires the separate analysis in Part IV.

The postviability provisions identify outcomes, good faith and reasonable medical judgment. Fargo separates an intelligible prospective clinical standard from unsettled fault concerning error. Vuitch supports professional judgment without diagnostic certainty; Colautti requires separate scrutiny of the actual trigger and culpability. X-Citement Video supplies contextual interpretation, not knowledge of illegality as a universal element. Reasonable later medical disagreement alone does not prove every element, but neither professional immunity nor a bad-faith-only safe harbor follows. No Ohio default culpability rule, concrete prosecution, civil award or materially different authoritative construction is decided. An intelligible exception may still fail to protect necessary care.

##### 3. Postviability prohibition must preserve the presented necessary mental-health care

**Holding and operative rule.** A State may prohibit abortion after viability, but it must permit abortion necessary in competent individualized medical judgment to avert grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. The patient need not await catastrophe; transient distress, unsupported assertion or mere preference does not establish the protected necessity.

**Authority.** Part III.A; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins. Part III.B’s particular construction and relief directions receive the same joins except Stone-Zsela’s: six direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Casey expressly retains physical-and-mental-health protection at the viability boundary. Doe supports individualized clinical judgment, while Fargo distinguishes a term’s intelligibility from the adequacy of the care it permits. The necessary-care protection therefore does not turn on whether Ohio’s emergency language is understandable. Pennsylvania’s emergency-delay construction does not automatically validate Ohio’s broader postviability prohibition, and Pennsylvania’s separate harmful-disclosure defense supplies no Ohio exception. The injunction is affirmed insofar as it prevents exclusion of the serious necessary care presented here.

Under Part III.B, the lower court must examine whether the actual words of §§2919.17(A) and 2919.16(F)/(J), and genuinely dependent commands, permit a protective construction fairly supported by Ohio law. If they do not, the restriction remains unenforceable to the extent of the established constitutional violation. The court may neither insert an unlimited new exception nor presume that every protective construction is impossible. The holding preserves competent medical support, individualized circumstances and the distinction between necessary care and a physician’s unsupported preference. It does not invalidate every postviability duty.

##### 4. The method prohibition requires application to actual conduct, care and alternatives

**Holding and operative rule.** The final method-ban disposition must be vacated where categorical notice reasoning or the displaced facial formula substituted for Casey’s substantive inquiry. On remand, the lower court must assess actual affected applications, attributable interference, usable alternative care and the distinct statutory defenses before deciding invalidity and matching relief; previability and postviability applications remain distinct.

**Authority.** Part IV; Souter, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** Casey and Fargo require a supported assessment of what the enacted duty does. The evidence that purposeful skull suction occurs during some D&E establishes a real scope and clinical-interference issue. It does not alone determine the magnitude of interference, affected applications, practical alternatives or final remedy. The prohibition’s criminal defense first requires the physician to make a prima facie showing that every available alternative would pose greater health risk; the prosecutor must then prove beyond a reasonable doubt that at least one available alternative would not. The civil affirmative defense is separate and receives no imported criminal burden shift.

The lower court must determine whether these protections permit necessary care in practice and whether demonstrated additional interference survives the applicable substantive protection. Alternatives count only if usable for the patient and clinical circumstances. Failure to prove D&X universally superior does not settle necessity for a supported subgroup, while bare clinical preference or an assumption that every D&E is prohibited cannot establish invalidity. The remand requires the identified substantive application; it orders no new evidentiary hearing or trial absent a demonstrated need. No final determination of every method-ban application or element-specific Ohio liability follows.

##### 5. Relief must follow Ohio severability law and the component judgments

**Holding and operative rule.** Independently operable provisions and applications must receive Ohio’s state-law severability analysis; invalidity of one command does not itself eliminate the Act. The lower court must conform relief to the affirmed necessary-care protection, determine the unresolved applications and dependent duties separately, and promptly decide interim protection under ordinary equitable standards without automatic blanket enforcement or permanent blanket invalidation.

**Authority.** Part V.A’s ordinary severability principles receive all eight Associates’ direct joins. Part V.B’s full remaining mandate receives Souter, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part III.B separately supplies the six-Justice health-linked construction directions. Stone’s decision to leave precise relief and severability below does not add a join in these particular analyses.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 1.50 preserves provisions and applications that can operate without the invalid part. Geiger requires attention to independent operation, legislative intention and whether separation requires inserting terms; Maurer bars severance that fundamentally disrupts the scheme. Leavitt requires this state-law inquiry while preserving independently justified constitutional protection. Its distinctive Utah independent-enactment clause cannot settle Ohio’s statute.

The lower court must keep these commands separate: viability testing begins at the twenty-second week, subject to its medical-emergency exception; viability is rebuttably presumed at twenty-four weeks, not constitutionally fixed then. Section 2919.17(A) permits the necessity or nonviability determination under the actual terms described above. The five postviability duties are the attending physician’s written necessity certification; a second physician’s written certification after reviewing records and tests; a facility with access to neonatal services; a survival-favoring method unless it creates significantly greater maternal death risk or serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function; and a second physician present to care for the fetus. Their emergency exemption permits noncompliance when its terms are satisfied; it does not require omission. The method-choice exception is distinct from the alternative-method affirmative defense.

Sections 2919.16(F)/(J)’s immediate-or-delay risk and medically diagnosed substantial irreversible impairment remain subject to the necessary-care holding. Felony, misdemeanor and civil channels, dependent certifications and other duties require separate treatment; the patient is not criminally liable under the method and postviability prohibitions. No reporting, testing, certification or damages provision is automatically sustained or eliminated. No liability award, fixed constitutional week or new criminal element follows. Interim protection for unresolved applications requires prompt lower-court consideration, not automatic dissolution before that determination, an unnecessary new hearing or permanent approval of the whole injunction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992):** Applied without changing its complete six-step rule, proof allocation, individualized viability, physical-and-mental-health baseline or tailored-relief requirement. Pennsylvania’s emergency-delay construction and distinct information-omission defense do not resolve Ohio’s separate commands.
- **Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, February 13, 1995:** Applied to preserve complete access review, separate intelligibility from actual fault and substantive necessary-care protection, and require supported application and ordinary interim-relief consideration. No categorical express-scienter requirement is derived from it.
- **United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987):** Its all-applications formulation does not independently bar the Casey challenge; its operation outside this setting remains unaddressed.
- **Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health, 497 U.S. 502 (1990):** Its facial-review formulation remains subject to Casey’s tailored-relief qualification; it supplies no independent numerical or no-valid-applications threshold here.
- **United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971):** Applied for the intelligibility of professional clinical judgment, without importing the D.C. statute’s construction or prosecution burden into Ohio law.
- **Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979):** Applied to require independent attention to the actual statutory trigger and culpability for clinical error; it supplies no universal express fault level or automatic invalidity.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994):** Applied for contextual attention to definition and culpability; its federal construction does not create an Ohio knowledge-of-illegality rule or cure every notice defect.
- **Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 (1973):** Applied for individualized clinical judgment and relevant physical and mental health; Casey’s retained constitutional baseline, rather than an unlimited reading of Doe’s statutory discussion, supplies the necessary-care rule.
- **Leavitt v. Jane L., June 17, 1996:** Applied to require actual state-law severability and preservation of independently supported federal protection; its Utah independent-enactment provision is distinguished and supplies no conclusion about Ohio’s entire Act.
- **Geiger v. Geiger, 117 Ohio St. 451, 160 N.E. 28 (1927), and State ex rel. Maurer v. Sheward, 71 Ohio St.3d 513, 644 N.E.2d 369 (1994):** Applied as Ohio’s severability authorities, requiring separability, fidelity to legislative intent and avoidance of judicial insertion or fundamental disruption. No final state-law severance is decided.

#### Law After Decision

The complete Casey framework continues unchanged, with no independent numerical facial threshold or Salerno barrier. The Court applies the existing necessary-care baseline to severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury after viability. That controlling protection is distinct from a claim that every distress, preference or procedure choice is protected necessity.

Overlap between a statutory act and a medical procedure label does not itself establish unconstitutional vagueness. Clinical intelligibility, actual culpability and substantive access remain separate inquiries; no universal Ohio fault rule or final liability determination is adopted. The method prohibition’s ultimate applications and the breadth of final relief remain unresolved. Ohio severability and the specified interim directions govern further proceedings. No separate opinion changes these rules, and no later case or event is decided.

#### Separate Positions

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Stone joins the complete Casey/Fargo standard and the required protection for grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. He rejects both Salerno’s independent barrier and an independent numerical facial threshold. He would affirm invalidity of the D&X prohibition and deficient penal medical exceptions now. In his application, the definition reaches the predominant D&E method, and courts cannot cure that reach by rewriting the definition. Intentional performance supplies no adequate culpability concerning an erroneous clinical-necessity determination; an affirmative defense does not cure the initial criminal and civil exposure. He therefore opposes the categorical-notice vacatur and the Court’s decision to leave method invalidity for further substantive application. His application does not command the Court.

Stone leaves severability and precise relief to the lower court without reopening his invalidity holdings. Ohio may regulate a genuinely distinct procedure, prohibit postviability abortion outside necessary life-and-health circumstances, punish bad-faith or sufficiently culpable violations, and enforce evidence-based safety rules. His health protection excludes transient distress and unsupported assertion and creates no unrestricted physician veto. His agreement that relief belongs below does not join the Court’s particular saving-construction analysis or its interim-only treatment of unresolved method applications.

**Justice Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Kennedy joins the actual-wording notice holding, serious necessary mental-health protection, its lawful protective-construction inquiry and ordinary Ohio severability principles. His retained Casey protected-choice baseline and Fargo separate position distinguish the substantive adequacy of care from the intelligibility of a rule. A method-specific health defense cannot cure a separate prohibition that excludes necessary mental-health care.

He would reverse the previability method injunction under his reasonable-regulation approach. The act-defined restriction, actual alternative procedures and enacted health defense defeat this categorical challenge absent an established denial of protected choice or necessary care. He rejects the general residual lesser-burden substantiation rule and therefore does not join the six-step method remand or continued unresolved-method protection under that mandate. He neither requires failure in every application nor finds every alternative equally safe. Supported necessary-care applications remain protected; he does not approve the entire Act. His narrower regulatory approach supplies no substitute for the Court’s controlling framework.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Thomas.** Scalia and Thomas join the bounded notice/fault holding and ordinary Ohio severability principles. Their Fargo position supports attention to the purposeful act and specified professional criteria, without a universal scienter cure, a bad-faith-only safe harbor or invented criminal and civil elements.

They would reverse the constitutional injunction under their continuing objection to Casey’s heightened abortion framework and their ordinary reasonable-regulation approach. They dissent from the required constitutional mental-health protection as well as the method remand. They acknowledge that Casey’s actual necessary-care rule is the controlling premise from which they dissent; they do not redescribe it as merely Doe’s statutory construction or a numerical test. Their position neither supplies a different statutory fact nor establishes professional immunity. Agreement on notice and severability adds no vote to their proposed reversal or to the Court’s health and method-remand rationales.

### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, D.C. Circuit No. 96-5354

Plenary merits decision, June 8, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the D.C. Circuit's December 5, 1997 judgment, 130 F.3d 1041, affirming summary judgment sustaining application of ESA section 9(a)(1)(B) to the challenged development projects affecting the Delhi Sands flower-loving fly. The question is whether Congress may prohibit the intrastate taking of an endangered species confined to one State and not traded, based on asserted aggregate effects on interstate commerce. The reviewed applications involve hospital, road and commercial development. The builders seek reversal and declaratory relief against those applications. No separate Supreme Court docket or argument date is established.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9(a)(1)(B) to the challenged habitat modification against this Commerce Clause objection stands, and no evidentiary remand follows. The builders receive no constitutional exemption from section 9 and no injunction against its enforcement on that theory; the Government may enforce the statute subject to its actual elements and exceptions. The disposition determines no actual taking, grants or denies no section 10 permit, resolves no independent property claim and does not enlarge the substation's existing permit. Scalia and Thomas would reverse and remand for relief confined to the reviewed applications.

#### Holdings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the Opinion of the Court. Part I is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; Parts II-A, III and IV by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part II-B by O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each coalition supplies a majority for the proposition attributed to it below. No narrowest-ground analysis or combination of incompatible rationales supplies authority.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding and operative rule.** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Authority.** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment; Ginsburg and Breyer also join Part II-B.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the relevant class is the prohibition Congress enacted. Lopez requires a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that class before aggregation measures magnitude. Section 9 makes injury to listed wildlife its predicate whether the actor undertakes an exchange, construction or private noneconomic conduct. Sweet Home's habitat construction identifies an injury-causing act but supplies no commercial element. A court cannot treat the actual expenditures or development function of these projects as though Congress had defined an economic land-conversion class.

The contrary as-applied argument has force because the construction itself alters the habitat, rather than merely financing some unrelated act. But that factual identity does not answer the legal fit question. Hinds requires the enacted predicate to perform its own work; an owner's economic relations do not substitute for it. Here the injury predicate likewise remains distinct from an economic-development classification. Wickard and Perez preserve aggregation of properly defined economic classes, and Hodel preserves regulation of economic activity for environmental ends. Those authorities do not establish that this taking class acquires the necessary commerce connection from the commercial features of particular projects. The Court adds no statutory element, makes no categorical rule against regulation of intrastate species, and leaves supported alternative interstate mechanisms to the following parts.

##### II. Necessary inclusion in a valid interstate scheme sustains the taking prohibition; an independent externality route remains available

**Holding and operative rule.** Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Court identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two; Congress may not establish that relationship merely by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute. Where Congress has established an integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of protected wildlife, it may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system. The Constitution does not require separate proof that every species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce, or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect if left unprotected. A defendant may show that a purported application is outside the relevant statutory class, that the supposed interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the asserted integral relationship is merely nominal or pretextual.

The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed Delhi Sands flower-loving fly within the Act's integrated system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers. This constitutional conclusion neither establishes an actual taking nor eliminates the statutory conditions for liability, permits or relief.

Congress may also regulate noneconomic intrastate conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. That route is an independent alternative. It need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes congressional power, and it is not applied to this fly.

The second route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

**Authority.** Part II-A's necessary-inclusion rule and its application are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part II-A while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that local conduct is not beyond congressional power merely because it is noneconomic, but national importance does not establish an interstate mechanism. The Commerce Clause, with the Necessary and Proper Clause, lets Congress select means reasonably adapted to the effective execution of a valid regulation of commerce among the States; it does not require every included intrastate act to be independently regulable. Lopez recognized inclusion genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime and reserved its fuller definition for a case presenting an actual integrated regime.

The unit of analysis is the regulated class within the federal scheme, not each species or each local controversy. The Act's conservation provisions protect the populations whose exploitation and interstate commercial movement the Act also regulates. Congress can reasonably determine that protecting listed populations against unauthorized depletion is a necessary component of that system. The question is whether the intrastate prohibition performs a reasonable, identifiable function within an independently valid scheme, not whether removing protection from one local species would independently produce a substantial interstate economic consequence. The builders' challenge does not show that these applications fall outside section 9's class, that the Act's interstate regulation is invalid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual. The fly's absence from trade does not defeat an application whose relationship to commerce lies in the scheme it serves.

The dissent correctly insists that calling a statute comprehensive does not establish congressional power and that Congress cannot turn every local activity into interstate commerce by enacting a national statute that includes it. The rule keeps that limit. A law criminalizing ordinary local misconduct solely because the misconduct has long-term economic consequences would not qualify; neither would a statute whose interstate provision is a decorative addition to an otherwise general police regulation. But requiring a separate species-specific interstate economic relationship would make the evidentiary demands of the externality route govern necessary inclusion as well, and the Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision.

**Controlling explanation, Part II-B.** The second route addresses a different connection left open by Lopez. A concrete burden crossing jurisdictional lines, or operating through an inherently interstate system beyond reasonable territorial control, can itself connect the regulated class to commerce among the States. Substantiality, territorial limitation and statutory fit distinguish that mechanism from generalized downstream effects through health, productivity or consumption. No balancing of national desirability against local preference replaces those requirements. The same problem occurring in several States does not show that its effects cross their authority.

The dissent also correctly demands an actual mechanism but would deny independent constitutional force to a demonstrated interstate externality. The Court concludes that requiring an independently established market-regime relationship in every such case is too narrow: a regulated class may itself produce a concrete interstate burden beyond reasonable territorial control, supplying a distinct connection. The Court requires the Government to support each part of that mechanism and its fit; regulatory need alone does not establish it. Because Part II-A establishes power here, the route is not applied to this fly. The Court makes neither every ecosystem national nor every species a commodity. Actual interstate sale, transport, trafficking, migratory movement and another independently sufficient Commerce mechanism remain distinct.

##### III. The Government identifies the regime, the covered class and a genuine functional relationship; courts decide those boundaries independently

**Holding and operative rule.** The Government must identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the intrastate class actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship between the two. Courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. The Necessary and Proper Clause is not a least-restrictive-means provision: the Government need not prove that every conceivable exemption would cause a quantitatively demonstrated failure of enforcement. A court may not invent a regime or relationship that the statute and its supporting legislative or adjudicative basis do not supply.

**Authority.** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a constitutional label is not proof of the connection it names. Lopez separates the judicial identification of an eligible mechanism from legislative judgment within the resulting power. The first task is not discharged by accumulating national benefits. The second does not require judges to redo environmental science or choose the best policy. A reasoned legislative or adjudicative basis may support predictive conclusions even though scientific uncertainty remains; deference does not turn an unsupported assertion into a demonstrated interstate relationship.

For necessary inclusion, the relationship appears from the statute's operation and any supporting legislative or adjudicative basis. Here the Act's text and structure identify each element: its valid regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife, the taking class section 9(a)(1)(B) actually covers, and that class's function in preserving the populations the trade provisions regulate and keeping those provisions enforceable. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. No universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is imposed. The obligation does not presume that every individual act must cross a state boundary or produce a separately substantial effect.

##### IV. The judgment sustaining the challenged applications is affirmed

**Holding and operative rule.** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9(a)(1)(B) to the challenged habitat modification against this Commerce Clause objection is affirmed. Part II-A resolves the constitutional question presented, so no Commerce Clause evidentiary remand follows. The builders receive no constitutional exemption from section 9 and no injunction against its enforcement on the Commerce Clause theory; the Government may enforce the statute subject to its actual elements and exceptions.

**Authority.** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the existing judgment is sustained by the necessary-inclusion ground, without substituting the projects' economic importance, biodiversity value or the breadth of the ESA for the required relationship. The published record establishes development-related habitat modification and the absence of trade in this fly. Neither fact defeats the application, because the relationship that sustains it lies in the taking prohibition's function within the Act's valid interstate regulation. A further hearing on the local fly would leave the constitutional question unsettled without bearing on that relationship.

The disposition does not grant or deny the particular section 10 permit, resolve an independent property claim, prescribe the final intersection design or authorize damages; whether the proposed intersection construction will cause a prohibited taking remains governed by the statutory limits below. Any independently pending statutory or permitting issues retain their ordinary procedural posture. Sweet Home's statutory construction remains binding: a permissible reading of the harm prohibition does not itself settle Congress's constitutional power in every case.

**Statutory and remedial limits.** Sweet Home requires significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability remain required; speculative future harm or bare but-for linkage does not suffice. An omission requires an independently existing legal duty, and this decision creates no general habitat-maintenance duty. No actual injury, liability or permit entitlement is newly found. Otherwise lawful development alone is not an exemption. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permission remains confined to taking incidental to, rather than the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. A conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization and mitigation measures and funding, alternatives considered and reasons rejected, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. The substation's existing permit decides no entitlement concerning the separate intersection/corridor controversy.

The distinct threatened-species protections, section 7 agency and incidental-take provisions, scientific permissions, experimental-population provisions and enforcement channels receive no new application here. Sweet Home's reserved section 6(g)(2) question remains reserved; deciding its terms or application would require the operative official text and applicable State-agreement record. The Court inserts no commercial element into section 9 and announces no categorical immunity for traditional State subjects.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied and clarified. Its connection-before-magnitude sequence, enacted-class fit, independent judicial eligibility inquiry, no-insertion rule and rule against manufacturing a commerce connection from aggregate downstream consequences remain controlling. The Court gives fuller definition to the necessary-inclusion route Lopez recognized and reserved, and resolves its open question concerning a materially supported substantial interstate externality; generalized downstream effects remain insufficient.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Preserved. Its machinegun-regulation holding supplies a different example of including local possession within a valid interstate transfer-and-stock regime; no machinegun-specific premise is imported into conservation law. The anti-evasion and material-impairment relationship it applied is assessed for the regulated class within the federal scheme, not species by species, and statutory comprehensiveness alone does not establish it.
- **United States v. Rybar, November 24, 1997:** Preserved as an application of Hale to the demonstrated relationship between possession and prohibited machinegun stock; that relationship is not inferred for the ESA.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Preserved for the same fitted regime relationship and the absence of any judicially added interstate element; no machinegun premise is transferred to species protection without support.
- **McCulloch v. Maryland:** Applied: Congress may select means reasonably adapted to the effective execution of an enumerated power, and the Necessary and Proper Clause does not require every included intrastate act to be independently regulable under the Commerce Clause.
- **Wickard v. Filburn:** Applied for examination of a valid regulatory class and the practical relation of included conduct to the federal scheme, with the Necessary and Proper Clause supplying the decisive relationship. Aggregation measures effects after a sufficient connection; it does not manufacture the connection missing from a noneconomic taking class.
- **Perez v. United States:** Applied for class-wide assessment of conduct included in a valid federal regulatory class without requiring every act independently to cross a state boundary.
- **Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association:** Applied for regulation of an integrated class for environmental purposes within a valid interstate scheme; distinguished from treating economic features of particular projects as the enacted taking class.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Applied and left intact as statutory law governing habitat-caused actual injury, ordinary causation and conditional permissions. It decided no universal Commerce authority over intrastate takings.
- **American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995:** Preserved for intentional conduct whose object and operation directly interfere with an actual interstate service market; that distinct mechanism does not establish all environmental applications.
- **United States v. Robinson, March 23, 1998:** Preserved for direct interference with operating commercial assets; the relevant market relationship remains necessary, rather than generalized consequences of local conduct.

#### Law After Decision

Congress may include intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Government identifies the valid interstate regulation, the covered intrastate class and a genuine functional relationship between them; courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments, without a least-restrictive-means test, species-by-species or crossing proof, a numerical threshold or a universal formal-findings requirement. A defendant may show that an application is outside the statutory class, that the interstate regime is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual. Noneconomic intrastate conduct may also fall within federal Commerce authority through a materially supported substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, an independent route that need not be satisfied where necessary inclusion establishes power. The enacted class comes first; economic features of these projects, national biodiversity value and comprehensiveness alone do not establish the required connection.

This decision gives fuller definition to the necessary-inclusion route Lopez reserved, develops the externality question Lopez left open and preserves Hale's regime holding. ESA section 9(a)(1)(B)'s taking prohibition, as applied to the listed fly within the Act's integrated system, is within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers. Sweet Home's statutory injury, causation and permission requirements remain intact.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring, joined by O'Connor.** They join both routes, the identification-and-fit rule and the affirmance in full. Lopez requires a meaningful distinction between commerce among the States and a general police power. Hale demonstrates one way a local act may be necessary to interstate regulation; American Life League, Robinson and Bird identify other concrete interstate relationships from a prohibition's actual operation. No one mechanism is an exhaustive taxonomy. The necessary-inclusion rule keeps that distinction because a court independently identifies a valid interstate regulation and the actual function the local prohibition performs within it; a declared national objective or a decorative interstate provision does not suffice. The Act's regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and its protection of the populations those provisions govern supply that function from the statute's own operation, not from a hospital label, construction expenditure or the national importance of biodiversity, so no further record about this fly is needed. A substantial burden that escapes ordinary territorial control may also have the interstate character the Constitution requires even though the source activity is local and noneconomic.

The territorial limit does real work. A problem's existence in many States, uneven State policy or the desirability of national uniformity does not alone show that States cannot reasonably contain or internalize the asserted effect. The Court must identify the effect that crosses their authority and test its fit to the regulated class. Because Part II-A establishes power here, that route is not applied to this fly. They do not demand categorical invalidation or an added commercial-transaction element.

**Breyer, concurring in Parts II, III and IV and the judgment, and dissenting from Part I, joined by Ginsburg.** They would regard the actual habitat-altering construction and conversion as a sufficient economic-application ground under Sweet Home, Hinds, Wickard, Perez and Hodel. They therefore do not join Part I's rejection of that ground. The construction is the injury-causing activity, not simply the owner's collateral purchases.

Part I nevertheless has five votes independently of theirs and supplies the Court's governing classification. Part II-A sustains these applications on that classification as well, and they join it, its application, the identification-and-fit rule in Part III and the affirmance in Part IV. The ESA's integrated regulation of interstate trade in listed wildlife and its protection of the populations that trade concerns form one practical regulatory system, and Congress may reasonably judge that protecting those populations against unauthorized depletion is a necessary part of it. Their judgment vote therefore rests on two sufficient grounds: their own economic-application view and the Court's necessary-inclusion rule.

The two routes recognize practical interstate relationships without categorically immunizing noneconomic local conduct. The Government must identify a genuine relationship, but that showing does not require de novo judicial environmental science, universal formal findings, a fixed quantity of interstate effects, clear-and-convincing proof or proof that every exemption would defeat the regime. Congress retains substantial latitude over regulatory necessity and practical enforcement. They join Part II-B's independent externality route as an available alternative that this case does not require. Their economic-application view remains a disagreement with Part I, not a competing holding.

**Stevens, concurring in Parts II-A, III and IV and the judgment, and dissenting from Part I, joined by Souter.** They join the Court's necessary-inclusion rule, its application to the fly, the identification-and-fit rule and the affirmance. They would also affirm on a further ground. The habitat-altering construction and conversion are themselves economic activities operating in actual land, construction, financing, labor and materials markets. Sweet Home identifies that modification as the injury-causing conduct, while Hinds directs attention to actual function rather than an owner's unrelated economic relationships. Their as-applied inquiry therefore identifies the activity section 9 actually reaches here; it does not insert a new element or declare every taking economic. Wickard and Perez permit class-wide aggregation after this economic connection, and Hodel confirms that environmental purpose does not disqualify regulation of economic activity.

The Court's enacted-class approach, in their view, treats the breadth of the taking prohibition as a reason to disregard the narrower economic application before the Court. Lopez's separate payment fact and Hinds's owner purchases differ from the physical development that itself causes the habitat injury here. They need no speculative biodiversity benefits. The necessary-inclusion ground independently sustains the judgment, because the taking prohibition protects the populations whose interstate exploitation the Act regulates and requires no showing that this fly is traded. Because Part II-A suffices, they do not reach the independent externality route, reserving it for a case that requires it.

**Scalia, concurring in Part I, and dissenting from Parts II-A, II-B, III and IV and the judgment, joined by Thomas.** They would reverse and remand for relief confined to the challenged applications. They join the Court's ruling that the taking class cannot be reconstructed from these projects' economic attributes. Lopez's no-insertion rule and the Hale line require a connection grounded in the enacted prohibition, not the actor's expenditures, motive or the national importance of the subject. They accept necessary inclusion of local conduct when a demonstrated relationship to a valid interstate regime exists, as in the machinegun cases, where excluding possession would have defeated the transfer-and-stock restriction. They find no such relationship here. Calling a statute comprehensive does not establish congressional power, and the record shows no trade in this fly and no evasion or material impairment of the Act's trade provisions from its local taking. A functional relationship identified at the level of the whole statute, without that showing, makes the national statute that includes a local prohibition its own constitutional justification.

They also reject the independent externality route. A substantial ecological burden that States cannot reasonably confine does not, for them, establish commerce or necessary protection of an existing commerce regime. The territorial-control inquiry risks substituting the need for national regulation for the enumerated power to undertake it. The Court's evidentiary requirements narrow that concern but do not resolve it. They would grant confined relief against the reviewed applications on the grounds offered. They obey Sweet Home's statutory construction, do not overrule the entering Lopez/Hale architecture, and propose no facial invalidation of the ESA, categorical intrastate-species immunity or blanket development permit.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The complete Casey standard governs the enacted duty and proved applications

**Holding and operative rule:** The complete six-step Casey standard, as applied in Fargo and Voinovich, governs Nebraska's method prohibition; an established substantial obstacle and denial of timely necessary life-and-health care are independently invalid, and the absence of an obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference. **Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined in full by the six Justices identified above, controls all four holdings at the same level of generality. Kennedy's agreement with the necessary-care baseline does not add a join in this complete framework or the Court's completed applications.

**Governing rule retained from Casey:**

Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Burdens and proof:** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey preserves the patient's previability decision and necessary care while replacing the former trimester timetable with the complete ordered inquiry above. Fargo applies that framework without confusing notice, culpability and substantive access. Voinovich requires examination of the actual prohibited acts, clinically usable alternatives, statutory defenses and matching relief; it does not make overlap between medical and statutory labels enough to decide every issue. Here the enacted substantial-portion language and developed clinical findings permit substantive application. The Court identifies the affected nonviable procedures and patients, the prohibition's added interference and the actual safeguards before reaching the independent obstacle and necessary-care bars. Neither bar can be overcome by balancing after the violation is established. No separate obstructive-purpose finding or new residual-interference application is needed to sustain this judgment. The remaining steps and their proof allocation remain governing law, not abandoned requirements.

##### 2. The enacted definition reaches ordinary D&E and creates a substantial obstacle

**Holding and operative rule:** Nebraska's definition reaches the supported ordinary dilation-and-evacuation sequence when the physician deliberately and intentionally brings a living fetus or a substantial portion into the vagina to perform a procedure the physician knows will cause death and that does cause death before delivery is completed. Prohibiting those ordinary D&E applications removes practical access to the predominant second-trimester method for the affected previability patients and creates a substantial obstacle, independently sustaining the existing bounded declaration and injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory elaboration includes a substantial portion, not only intact delivery. The described ordinary procedure can deliberately bring a living fetus's limb through the cervix before dismemberment; the act, intent, knowledge, life and sequence elements can therefore be satisfied in ordinary D&E. The Court does not hold that every D&E is prohibited, that a label establishes all elements, or that removal after fetal death satisfies the living-fetus condition.

Nebraska's strongest answer invokes the entire delivery-and-death sequence, the enactment's purpose and constitutional avoidance to confine the prohibition to the intact method. Those considerations cannot erase the enacted substantial-portion alternative or insert an exclusion the text does not contain. The Attorney General's nonenforcement position is not an authoritative Nebraska Supreme Court construction or established control over every county prosecutor. Constitutional avoidance permits a legally available construction; it does not authorize rewriting this statute.

Casey requires concrete interference rather than a presumption against all method regulation. The operative comparison is care with and without this prohibition, with other circumstances held constant, for patients whose timely clinically appropriate nonviable care uses the ordinary D&E acts described. Penal and dependent licensing exposure constrains that usable provider capacity. The availability of other procedures in some circumstances does not establish adequate care for that affected population. Voinovich's rejection of categorical notice reasoning does not cure a demonstrated substantive obstacle; Ohio's distinct alternative-method defenses are absent here. The law's absence of a viability limit does not enlarge the present injunction beyond its nonviable scope, and no categorical vagueness holding is necessary.

##### 3. The supported necessary-health applications independently require protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Even if the prohibition could lawfully be confined to an intact procedure, it cannot be enforced in supported circumstances where compelling substitution for that method creates a material risk of death or serious health harm and the method is medically necessary for timely safe and suitable care. Nebraska's specified life-only exception and post-charge life-necessity evidence do not supply a usable exception for those necessary serious-health applications; this independent defect supports the existing protection at its proved nonviable scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey's necessary-care baseline requires protection before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration, and Fargo and Voinovich separate substantive adequacy from an intelligible emergency definition. The District Court credited explained clinical advantages for Carhart's intact method in recurring circumstances involving roughly ten to twenty annual cases: reduced instrumentation, retained parts, trauma, blood loss, operating time and infection risk. The findings support clinically consequential risk reduction in the identified applications, not universal superiority or a right to every preferred method.

The State properly invokes competent medical disagreement, the absence of controlled comparative studies, available alternatives and professional ethics. Those considerations receive weight; no physician or professional association has a constitutional veto. But the developed, credited evidence establishes the necessary-care applications here, and general availability of another method does not establish that it is timely, safe and suitable for those patients. The Court adopts no new numerical risk threshold or presumption that every comparative advantage is necessary.

Section 3(1) authorizes the defined life-saving care; the defect is not an assertion that it protects no life. Its terms do not independently authorize care needed to avoid serious nonfatal harm. The broader emergency-situation definition is not incorporated into that exception. A hearing after charges permits evidence on the same life criterion, not permission to provide the otherwise forbidden necessary-health treatment. Neither admissibility of the Director's findings nor a possible motion to delay trial supplies the missing substantive defense. Voinovich's Ohio alternative-method protections cannot be imported. This conclusion adds no general medical immunity and does not decide every future, precisely drawn method restriction with genuinely usable care safeguards.

##### 4. Affirmance preserves the actual nonviable injunction and bars its evasion through dependent sanctions

**Holding and operative rule:** The existing declaration and injunction remain in force for Carhart, his patients and similarly situated persons regarding his D&E and D&X methods on nonviable fetuses, against the defendants and persons bound under Rule 65(d), with the existing county-attorney service direction. Criminal and licensing consequences cannot enforce those same unconstitutional applications, while independently lawful requirements and applications remain subject to Nebraska's section 8 severability direction and the judgment's actual limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey's sixth step requires relief corresponding to the proved violation; Voinovich preserves independently lawful provisions and dependent-duty analysis without automatic wholesale invalidation. The District Court expressly declined whole-statute facial relief. Affirming its bounded judgment does not create a nationwide or all-gestation injunction, decide fetal personhood, award damages or independently adjudicate the separate fee appeal. It also does not permit officials to reimpose the invalid prohibition through its dependent license sanctions.

Nebraska section 8 preserves the validity of remaining portions when a section or part is unconstitutional. It supplies no power to insert an intact-only definition or health exception and does not erase the sanction provisions for every lawful application. The enacted Director, petition, hearing and evidentiary procedures remain material to what the law does; the Court neither converts the life-only hearing into a health defense nor decides the unnecessary relationship between section 3(4)'s mandatory wording and the general sanction menu. Patient immunity remains intact. No new hearing, administrative veto, prosecution, damages award or statewide medical-policy revision is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey retains protected choice, individualized viability, timely necessary life-and-health care and the complete six-step rule. Both independent invalidity grounds are applications of that rule; residual severe- and meaningful-interference protections remain, and no obstacle-only substitute is adopted.
- Fargo Women's Health Organization v. Schafer supplies the distinction between intelligibility, applicable fault and substantive access under the complete framework. An understandable life exception does not itself supply adequate necessary-health protection, and fault words do not automatically cure an exclusion of care.
- Women's Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich requires attention to actual prohibited acts, usable alternatives, distinct statutory defenses and violation-matched relief. Its rejection of categorical notice reasoning remains; its remand on the Ohio method record does not require remand on every method law. Ohio's enacted alternative-method defenses and Ohio severability rules do not become Nebraska law.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton retain the protected decision and clinical safeguards preserved in Casey; the independent trimester timetable remains displaced. No broader reconsideration or new fetal-personhood ruling is undertaken.

#### Law After Decision

Nebraska cannot enforce LB23 in the nonviable Carhart-type applications covered by the existing judgment, including through criminal or licensing consequences dependent on the same invalid prohibition. Ordinary-D&E access and supported necessary-health applications each independently support that protection. A defined method, intentional conduct and a life-only exception do not end the substantive inquiry. The complete Casey framework remains controlling, and a materially different precise procedure regulation with clinically usable care protections would require its own full analysis. Patient immunity, independently lawful regulation and ordinary professional discipline remain; no general professional veto or whole-statute invalidity is created.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He would vacate and remand rather than affirm or authorize immediate blanket enforcement. Reading the complete delivery-and-death sequence in context, he would confine the law to the intact method and rejects the Court's ordinary-D&E access ground. Potential-life and professional-ethics interests permit meaningful regulation; the physician's preferred technique or every asserted comparative advantage does not control the legislature. He also rejects the residual lesser-burden portion of the complete Casey framework.

He retains the necessary-life-and-health baseline he joined in Casey, Fargo and Voinovich. Voinovich's method analysis materially included Ohio's alternative-method health defense and preserved supported necessary-care applications. Nebraska has no corresponding defense: the exception and later licensing evidence concern only the stated life-saving condition. He therefore would require identification of the circumstances in which available alternatives are clinically inadequate for timely safe and suitable care under that retained baseline. He accepts the credited comparative reductions in the described surgical risks without finding every intact-method use necessary or joining the Court's completed health application.

The lower court may make the needed legal application on the existing record if adequate. His remand orders no automatic new hearing, filing or invented condition. It permits only a Nebraska-law-supported protective construction, preserves supported necessary-care and dependent-sanction protection, and requires ordinary consideration of interim relief. It neither inserts a new exception nor automatically dissolves existing protection. The serious necessary mental-health protection in Voinovich supports his adherence to necessary-care protection, not an imported medical test for every physical-risk claim here.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissenting.** They would reverse the constitutional judgment at the granted scope and remand to remove the corresponding injunction. Their principal disagreement is with the constitutional abortion and necessary-health requirements themselves. They acknowledge Casey's complete framework and the actual Fargo and Voinovich holdings as the governing law from which they dissent; they do not replace that law with an obstacle-only description or a different facial-review threshold.

They favor the contextual intact-method construction and ordinary reasonable regulation for potential-life and medical-ethics purposes. The Attorney General's representation is not made binding on every prosecutor, and the credited clinical findings are not declared nonexistent or every alternative equally safe. Their constitutional objection supplies the reason for rejecting the Court's protective conclusion notwithstanding those facts. Agreement in earlier notice and severability propositions does not establish substantive validity, universal knowledge of illegality, good-faith immunity or a cure for every enforcement problem. They seek no new prosecution, damages, administrative sanction, fetal-personhood adjudication or injunction governing viable procedures. Their constitutional objection remains independent of their statutory-construction argument.

### Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton, D.C. Circuit No. 01-5373

Merits decision, February 5, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

On merits submission for review of the D.C. Circuit's April 1, 2003 judgment, rehearing denied July 22, 2003. The question is whether the Commerce Clause authorizes application of the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition to a commercial housing development threatening a species found entirely within one State. The proposed 202-acre San Diego County development threatens the arroyo toad. The circuit sustained the application by treating the commercial development as the regulated activity. Rancho Viejo seeks reversal and relief against the application; the federal officials seek affirmance.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0, against the Commerce Clause challenge.** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application of the taking prohibition stands on the necessary-inclusion ground, not on the circuit's commercial-project ground.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The judgment sustaining the application against Rancho Viejo's Commerce Clause objection remains in effect, and Rancho Viejo's requested relief against the application is denied. The Government may enforce the taking prohibition in this application subject to its actual elements and exceptions. Whether the proposed development will cause a prohibited taking remains governed by the statute's injury and causation requirements, and any section 10 permission or other independently pending statutory issue retains its ordinary procedural posture.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined, and in which STEVENS and SOUTER, JJ., joined as to Parts II and III. STEVENS, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, in which SOUTER, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; Stevens and Souter as to Parts II and III | Affirms | Part I: enacted taking class and rejection of the commercial-project ground (seven Justices); Part II: necessary inclusion under Home Builders sustains the taking prohibition as applied to the arroyo toad (nine); Part III: affirmance, with the statutory questions left to their own rules (nine) |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stevens | Souter | Joins Parts II and III and the judgment | Additional economic-application ground for affirmance and proposed reconsideration of Home Builders's contrary class ruling at that scope; does not join Part I |

#### Holdings

##### I. A commercial project does not replace the statutory taking class

**Controlling proposition:** The commercial character of a housing project does not by itself establish Congress's authority to apply the Endangered Species Act's wildlife-taking prohibition. The Government must identify an eligible interstate mechanism fitted to the taking class Congress enacted, including the habitat-caused injury that class reaches.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court. The seven Justices identified above as joining the full opinion directly join this holding and its explanation; Stevens and Souter join Parts II and III and the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt governs this question. Section 9 prohibits taking protected wildlife without making a project's commercial character the defining condition of the prohibition. United States v. Lopez requires a constitutionally sufficient connection for that enacted class before aggregation can measure its effects.

The D.C. Circuit relied on Rancho Viejo's commercial housing development as the regulated activity. The development is commercial, and the contrary view correctly identifies habitat conversion itself, rather than an incidental purchase, as the injury-producing conduct. But Home Builders already considered and rejected that feature as an independently sufficient connection for the enacted taking class. Describing the challenge as applied to this project does not remove the need to identify the mechanism that connects the prohibition to interstate commerce. The Court applies that existing rule.

Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon supplies the statutory treatment of habitat-caused injury. It does not supply the separate constitutional connection. Wickard v. Filburn and Perez v. United States permit class-wide assessment within properly connected economic classes; under Home Builders they do not make this project's commercial character a substitute for the required connection.

##### II. The taking prohibition, as applied to the arroyo toad, is an integral part of the Act's valid interstate scheme

**Controlling proposition:** Under National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, Congress may regulate intrastate conduct that is neither itself interstate commerce nor independently shown to have substantial interstate effects when inclusion of the regulated class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of executing an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. The Endangered Species Act's integrated system regulating interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition of listed wildlife may include the unauthorized taking of listed wildlife as a means of preserving the populations and enforceability of that system, without separate proof that each species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or would independently produce a substantial interstate commercial effect. The section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition, as applied to the listed arroyo toad within that system, falls within Congress's Commerce and Necessary and Proper powers; Rancho Viejo has not shown that the application is outside the statutory class, that the Act's interstate regulation is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual.

**Authority:** Parts II and III of Kennedy's opinion of the Court. All nine Justices identified above directly join this holding, its explanation and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Home Builders supplies the rule and its unit of analysis. The Commerce Clause, with the Necessary and Proper Clause, lets Congress select means reasonably adapted to the effective execution of a valid regulation of commerce among the States; it does not require every included intrastate act to be independently regulable. The unit is the taking prohibition within the federal scheme, not each species or local controversy.

The Act regulates the importation, exportation, interstate transport, sale and commercial handling of listed wildlife, the possession and shipment of unlawfully taken wildlife, the taking of listed wildlife and permissible incidental taking. The taking prohibition protects the populations whose exploitation and interstate movement those provisions govern and keeps the provisions enforceable. Home Builders sustained it as applied to the Delhi Sands flower-loving fly, a species found in one State and absent from trade.

That holding governs this application. The challenged application concerns habitat modification that section 9(a)(1)(B) reaches, as Sweet Home construes it, when the modification actually kills or injures listed wildlife. The Act's valid interstate regulation, the covered class and the protective function connecting them appear from the statute's operation, so the toad's intrastate range and the absence of an established market in it do not defeat the application. Rancho Viejo identifies no feature placing this application outside the class, no defect in the Act's interstate regulation and nothing nominal or pretextual in the relationship. United States v. Holston applied necessary inclusion to the reproducible supply of a prohibited interstate image market; this application rests on the Act's own wildlife scheme, not on a transferred market analogy.

The rule keeps an enforceable distinction between the commerce power and a general police power. Congress cannot establish the relationship by declaring a national objective or assembling unrelated provisions into one statute, and biodiversity's general importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism and development pressure do not supply it. Aggregation measures magnitude only after a constitutionally eligible connection exists. Because necessary inclusion establishes congressional power here, Home Builders's independent externality route need not be satisfied.

##### III. The judgment sustaining the challenged application is affirmed

**Controlling proposition:** When a judgment sustaining an application of the taking prohibition rests on the insufficient commercial-project ground but Home Builders's necessary-inclusion rule sustains the same application on the statute's text and structure, the judgment is affirmed on that ground, and no Commerce Clause evidentiary remand is required. The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining application of section 9 to the arroyo toad against Rancho Viejo's Commerce Clause objection is affirmed.

**Authority:** The same nine-Justice Parts II and III control the disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reviews the judgment, not only its reasoning. The commercial ground below cannot sustain the application, but the necessary-inclusion ground does, and it rests on the Act's text and structure and the record already before the Court. A further hearing on the local toad would leave the constitutional question unsettled without bearing on the relationship that sustains the application. Rancho Viejo's requested relief against the application is therefore denied.

Whether the proposed development will cause a prohibited taking remains governed by Sweet Home's statutory injury, proximate-cause and foreseeability requirements. Any section 10 permission and any independently pending statutory or permitting issue retain their own statutory conditions and ordinary procedural posture.

**Application and remedy:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application is affirmed. The Government may enforce the taking prohibition in this application subject to its actual elements and exceptions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998:** Reaffirmed and applied. Its enacted taking class and rejection of project commerce as independently sufficient govern Part I. Its necessary-inclusion rule sustains the taking prohibition as applied to the listed toad within the Act's integrated system, without species-by-species, border-crossing or independent-effects proof, subject to its outside-class, invalid-regime and pretext showings, its identification rule, independent judicial determination of the legal boundaries and respect for reasonable congressional judgments. Its independent externality route remains available but need not be satisfied.
- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied. The constitutional connection precedes magnitude, must fit the enacted class and must leave an enforceable boundary; neither project commerce nor insertion of an unexpressed commerce element supplies the required inquiry.
- **United States v. Holston, October 30, 2003:** Applied for the necessary-inclusion method and distinguished on its actual image-supply relationship. Its demonstrated mechanism supplies no presumed wildlife market or ecological spillover; this application rests on the Act's integrated wildlife scheme.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Preserved. Its machinegun holding supplies a different example of necessary inclusion within an identified valid interstate regime; no machinegun-specific premise is imported into conservation law.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Preserved for a fitted prohibited-stock relationship without a universal individual-sale requirement. Its specific stock relationship is not imported into conservation law; the relationship here lies in the Act's own scheme.
- **Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111:** Preserved for aggregation within a properly connected economic class; it does not displace Home Builders's enacted-class rule.
- **Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146:** Preserved for class-wide assessment of connected economic conduct without a separately substantial effect from each act; it does not create a missing connection through this project's commerce.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Preserved as the separate statutory rule. Significant habitat modification must actually kill or injure protected wildlife through significant impairment of essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering, with proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability. Speculative harm and bare but-for linkage are insufficient; an omission requires an independent legal duty, and otherwise lawful development alone is no exemption. Those statutory conditions remain distinct from constitutional authority and from any section 10 permission.

#### Law After Decision

Effective February 5, 2004, Home Builders's necessary-inclusion rule sustains the Endangered Species Act's section 9(a)(1)(B) taking prohibition as applied to listed wildlife within the Act's integrated system, including an application to a commercial project threatening a species found only in one State, without separate proof that the species crosses state boundaries, participates in interstate commerce or independently produces a substantial interstate commercial effect. A challenger may show that an application is outside the statutory class, that the Act's interstate regulation is not independently valid, or that the relationship is nominal or pretextual. A project's commercial character does not itself supply the connection for the enacted taking class, and the independent externality route remains available on its own requirements.

The commercial-project rationale cannot alone sustain this application; the necessary-inclusion ground sustains it, and the judgment below stands. This decision applies the existing doctrine without creating a new Commerce category.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Souter.** They join Parts II and III and the judgment. The Court's necessary-inclusion ground sustains this application of the taking prohibition, as they agreed in Home Builders. They would also affirm on a further ground: the activity directly producing the alleged habitat injury is commercial housing construction and land conversion. Sweet Home identifies that physical habitat alteration as the conduct reached by the taking prohibition. Their additional ground therefore looks to the actual economic application, rather than to the developer's collateral equipment purchases or a generalized claim that species preservation benefits the national economy.

Wickard and Perez permit class-wide regulation of connected economic activity, and Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association permits regulation of economic land use for environmental ends. On that reasoning, the actual construction supplies a sufficient economic connection without a market in the toad or a newly found ecological spillover. They accept the continuing statutory requirements of actual habitat-caused injury and ordinary causation; their additional ground concerns the Commerce Clause challenge.

Home Builders's contrary enacted-class holding directly obstructs this ground, and they do not join Part I. Stevens and Souter would reconsider that holding to the extent it excludes reliance on the actual injury-producing economic activity in this application. They regard treating the prohibition's broader coverage as a reason to disregard this commercial application as a mistaken class inquiry. They neither claim that their earlier dissent already controls nor distinguish Home Builders through a new factual premise. Their ground is an additional reason for the same judgment; it would not make every noneconomic taking an economic activity.

The threatened project's commercial character does not itself establish a cross-border ecological effect or a mechanism of market evasion.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the challenged application is affirmed, and the taking prohibition remains enforceable in that application subject to its actual elements and exceptions. Any section 10 permission and any independently pending statutory or permitting issue retain their ordinary procedural posture. Supreme Court merits review of the Commerce Clause challenge is complete.
