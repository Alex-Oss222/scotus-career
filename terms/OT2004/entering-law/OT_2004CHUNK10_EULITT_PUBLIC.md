## Event

Eulitt v. Maine Department of Education; review of 386 F.3d 344 (First Circuit).

Merits decision, June 20, 2005. The First Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Maine on October 22, 2004. The questions are whether equal protection requires payment of the ordinary St. Dominic's tuition requested by these parents and whether they may assert the school's separate constitutional rights without an obstacle to its own suit.

Minot contracts for at least ninety percent of its high-school students to attend Poland Regional High School. Up to ten percent may attend another approved nonsectarian school upon showing educational needs the public school cannot meet. The parents seek ordinary tuition at St. Dominic's specifically for Catholic instruction and viewpoint. They did not submit a formal application because the statutory sectarian exclusion would foreclose it. Their own challenge is adjudicated separately from the attempted assertion of the school's rights.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Court of Appeals for the First Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting both components:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The parents' equal-protection claim concerning this integrated-tuition purchase fails, and their requested tuition and injunction are denied. Their attempted assertion of St. Dominic's independent rights is rejected for lack of a demonstrated obstacle to the school's own suit; that disposition does not adjudicate the merits of the school's claim.

## Holdings

The opinion of the Court commands all nine votes on both holdings. Its author and complete joins are stated in Opinion Topology; each holding is directly supported at the stated level of generality.

### 1. Equal treatment depends on the educational work publicly purchased

**Holding and operative rule:** Equal protection does not require a State to purchase integrated devotional instruction when its educational program purchases eligible secular work. Religious identity alone cannot disqualify an independently identifiable, reliably separated secular service that meets lawful neutral program requirements and effective use controls; the parents' request for ordinary St. Dominic's tuition to obtain integrated Catholic instruction falls on the devotional-purchase side of that distinction.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini requires the Court to identify the government's actual function. Strout applies that inquiry to ordinary integrated tuition at St. Dominic's, while Zelman and McCallum establish that parental choice and payment routing do not change the function a restricted payment buys. The parents identify Catholic instruction and viewpoint as the need the public school does not meet. Their requested tuition purchases that integrated instructional package.

The equality objection is substantial when religious affiliation excludes otherwise eligible secular work. Columbia Union and K.D.M. protect participation on equal terms in such work, and the present rule preserves that protection. Here the financed services differ in the constitutionally material respect established by the governing cases. The program's support for private secular alternatives therefore does not require the requested devotional purchase. The educational-needs condition, school-approval requirements and sectarian exclusion remain distinct; the Court decides the actual funding claim rather than presuming an additional denial under an unapplied neutral criterion.

Strout resolved its granted Religion Clause questions. Its rule supplies substantive authority here; it did not previously adjudicate these different parents' equality claim. Locke's permission concerning an individual ministry-degree scholarship likewise cannot become a general license to exclude religious institutions. These distinctions answer the parents' equal-access argument while retaining equal eligibility for genuinely controlled secular services.

### 2. A close relationship does not replace a genuine obstacle to the school's own suit

**Holding and operative rule:** Parents seeking to assert a school's separate constitutional rights must establish a genuine hindrance to the school's own assertion, in addition to their own injury and a sufficiently close relationship. These parents have an actual relationship with St. Dominic's, but have not established the independent obstacle required to litigate its institutional claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Kowalski requires examination of the relationship and obstacle as separate prerequisites. Its hypothetical future clients differ from the identified school here. That difference satisfies neither the missing obstacle nor a general entitlement to litigate another legal person's claims. The parents' purchase of the school's services and the financial consequences of the exclusion explain their personal controversy, which the Court has decided. They do not show why the school cannot present its own rights. The institutional claim is therefore rejected at the representative-standing threshold.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton supplies the unified governmental-act-and-function inquiry; the decision applies that inquiry to the public purchase actually requested.
- Strout v. Albanese supplies the integrated-tuition rule, applied to a distinct equal-protection claim rather than treated as an earlier adjudication of that claim.
- Zelman v. Simmons-Harris and Freedom From Religion Foundation v. McCallum establish that neutral selection and private routing do not alter the devotional function bought by a restricted public payment; both retain their stated boundaries.
- Columbia Union College v. Oliver and K.D.M. v. Reorganized School District No. 7 preserve equal eligibility for genuine secular services under their separation, accountability and service-specific requirements.
- Locke v. Davey retains its bounded ministry-training holding in the individual-scholarship setting.
- Kowalski v. Tesmer is applied to the independent requirements for third-party assertion, with its absent-client circumstance distinguished from the existing relationship here.

## Law After Decision

The existing financed-function framework now supplies the direct answer to the equality theory presented by an ordinary integrated devotional-tuition request. Eligible controlled secular work remains open to religious providers on equal terms. The third-party-standing rule continues to require a supported institutional hindrance even when the claimant has a close existing relationship with the rights holder.
