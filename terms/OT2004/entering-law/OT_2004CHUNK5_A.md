#### Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-6188.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### Necessary invalidity on the present in-custody record

**Holding:** A state prisoner in Heck's position, in custody with a lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, has no presently cognizable §1983 damages claim when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that outstanding conviction or sentence, unless the judgment has undergone qualifying invalidation. The rule decides that setting; it does not make present habeas availability a universal element or decide plaintiffs who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity or whose custody has ended.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this bounded rule; the distinct sufficient rationales are not added together to obtain its support.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, resolves the reserved former-prisoner category to this extent: a necessarily contradictory damages claim needs no prior invalidation when the claimant never had realistic lawful custody review or lost it beyond his control despite timely pursuit. Unexcused loss of a genuinely usable opportunity retains the prerequisite; release or current federal habeas unavailability alone establishes neither the exception nor invalidation. Heck's original in-custody rule, qualifying invalidation routes and deferred accrual for claims still covered remain; independent law governs accrual outside the prerequisite.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475 (1973): its release-channel distinction informs the new, bounded damages prerequisite; its holding is not misdescribed as already deciding damages-only claims.
- Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974): preserves civil relief compatible with the operative conviction and custody judgment.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982): remains the rule against general §1983 exhaustion; this decision addresses present cognizability instead.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: former-prisoner realistic-opportunity exception, unexcused-loss boundary and conditional cognizability remedy.

##### Statutory coordination supports this limited prerequisite

**Holding:** For the in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim described in Part I, coordination of §1983 with the available habeas channel supports favorable invalidation as a prerequisite, with the common-law malicious-prosecution analogy implementing that coordination. This is a new, limited restriction on §1983, not conversion of the damages complaint into habeas, a general exhaustion rule, or adoption of every malicious-prosecution element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join Part II.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez: extends statutory coordination to the particular damages claim while preserving the distinct relief and conditions of habeas.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves its permissible civil-remedy category; factual overlap with prosecution does not remove a claim from that category.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: distinguishes general exhaustion from the newly adopted prerequisite; exhaustion alone cannot satisfy it.
- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 (1978): applies common-law remedial guidance as an aid to construing the federal remedy, not a wholesale importation of a state tort.

##### Qualifying invalidation and deferred accrual

**Holding:** The claim barred under Part I becomes cognizable only when the conviction or sentence has been reversed on direct review, expunged by executive order, declared invalid by a state tribunal authorized to do so, or called into question by a federal court's issuance of a writ of habeas corpus. That claim accrues only upon such invalidation; exhaustion, unsuccessful review, or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation, and neither immediate accrual with equitable tolling nor a preservation stay is required for this unaccrued claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt the four invalidation routes and the accrual consequence for the barred claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus: applies its common-law-informed approach to the federal remedy without imposing additional constitutional-liability elements.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: preserves the distinction between exhaustion and existence of a cognizable damages claim.

##### Independent injuries, claim-specific analysis and this dismissal

**Holding:** A claim whose success does not necessarily establish invalidity falls outside this prerequisite even if it concerns the same investigation or trial; courts must compare the civil claim's elements with the basis of the conviction, and compensatory recovery still requires an actual compensable injury. On the accepted characterization of Heck's presented action, dismissal without prejudice stands on lack of present cognizability, without deciding misconduct, immunity, personal responsibility, preclusion, damages, or entitlement to release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt this boundary, application and remedy.

**Material application:** The lower courts' characterization that Heck's action necessarily attacked the conviction was not disputed in the petition or principal merits brief; the late reply established no separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand. An unlawful-search claim can remain compatible with a conviction through independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error, but those possibilities establish neither automatic admissibility of every search claim nor compensation for conviction injury without invalidation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves the distinction between compatible civil relief and relief dependent on invalidating custody.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: distinguishes its opportunity-only claim; the existing §1983 holding remains intact.
- Carey v. Piphus: applies the requirement of actual compensable injury without adjudicating the amount or merits here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Former prisoners and persons who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity remain outside the question resolved. No universal constitutional malicious-prosecution action, automatic state-law preclusion, automatic habeas conversion or pending-prosecution abstention rule is adopted. Custis's distinct no-meaningful-review reservation in the ACCA channel remains intact. Recognition of a conviction's invalidation by an authorized state tribunal identifies one circumstance in which the present §1983 prerequisite is satisfied. It does not itself create jurisdiction, confer authority to reopen the underlying conviction, or prescribe grounds upon which a state tribunal must grant collateral relief. Those questions remain governed by the independently applicable law authorizing that tribunal's action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal without prejudice is affirmed unanimously for lack of present cognizability. The barred claim has not accrued, so its limitations period is not consumed while the prerequisite remains unmet. A later otherwise proper action may follow qualifying invalidation, subject to remaining requirements; no stay, release, automatic conversion to habeas, liability, immunity or damages determination is ordered.

#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** Accepted benefits of adversative education and predicted effects of coeducation remain findings; their legal sufficiency is independently examined. General preferences attributed to most women do not justify denying consideration to capable willing women. A genuine present purpose need not have been stated in 1839 or contemporaneous writing; litigation timing alone proves no pretext.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant; no universal ban on single-sex education.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Liability judgment in No. 94-2107 affirmed, 7–1.

##### Whether full performance of VWIL’s approved plan cures the denied opportunity

**Holding:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** The Court credits equal annual public subsidy, ordinary student cost, separate endowment and full promised alumni recruitment, placement, externship and advancement access. Even so, cumulative differences in integrated adversative/military training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources, degree and institutional standing leave the opportunity materially unequal. The engineering alternative requires three Mary Baldwin years plus two at Washington University at private tuition without the proposed final-two-year public discount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 reversed, 7–1; monitoring cannot cure a substantively inadequate design.

##### What effective relief and State choices remain

**Holding:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** State design responsibility survives within prompt effective correction; genuine privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations remain possible. A lawful change in State involvement differs from relabeling the same public institution, but no withdrawal proposal is preapproved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No abandonment of genuine qualifications, named-person admission, closure, quota, identical programs or particular physical standard is ordered. Private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate or sex-conscious opportunity arrangement and independent Title IX claims are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand for prompt effective cure without indefinite exclusion under the rejected plan; no precise admissions date, damages, special master or newly required hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### Edwards v. Balisok

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1352.
**Decided:** 1997-05-19.

##### Procedural labels do not avoid necessary invalidity

**Holding:** In the in-custody review setting presented, damages and retrospective declaratory claims that a disciplinary adjudicator deliberately concealed exculpatory evidence and acted dishonestly or with disabling bias are not presently cognizable under §1983 when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of the outstanding deprivation of earned good-time credits, absent qualifying invalidation. The inquiry concerns the legal consequence of the actual claim, not its procedural label or omission of a request to restore credits; the reserved position of former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity is not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, resolves the shared Heck/Edwards reservation for former prisoners who never had realistic lawful custody review or lost it beyond their control despite timely pursuit: their necessarily contradictory damages claims need no favorable invalidation. Unexcused loss of a usable opportunity retains the prerequisite; present habeas unavailability or release alone does not suffice. Edwards's in-custody holding and claim-specific legal-consequence inquiry remain, as do invalidation, deferred accrual and without-prejudice dismissal for claims still covered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A later lawful hearing's possibility of the same result does not validate the existing adjudication. Present habeas availability is not made a universal element; former prisoners and persons without a realistic lawful custody-review opportunity remain reserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck's necessary-implication rule applies at its bounded in-custody scope; Preiser reserves credit restoration to habeas. Wolff and Carey preserve compatible process injuries, not categorical permission for every procedural challenge. Sandin and Young v. Harper preserve meaningful liberty and process without removing remedy-channel limits.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: bounded resolution of the former-prisoner and realistic-review reservation.

##### Genuinely independent procedural injuries require their own analysis

**Holding:** A claim falls outside Heck's invalidation prerequisite when its success does not necessarily establish that the credit adjudication was invalid, even if it arises from the same disciplinary hearing. A genuinely separable failure to state supporting facts can meet that boundary, and questioning or other procedural allegations must be classified individually; separability establishes neither a due-process violation nor entitlement to damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A nominal-damages label does not save an invalidity-dependent claim. Refusal of questions is neither invariably unconstitutional nor necessarily invalidating; Wolff permits refusal for irrelevance, lack of necessity, safety or correctional hazards. Ordinary pleading, personal responsibility, immunity, liability and damages requirements remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck and Wolff preserve genuinely independent injuries. Carey allows appropriate nominal relief for an established independent process denial without presuming compensable loss.

##### Qualifying invalidation precedes accrual; an exhaustion stay is not the remedy

**Holding:** For an invalidity-dependent claim within Heck's decided category, qualifying invalidation means reversal on direct review, executive expungement, invalidation by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling the custody judgment into question, and the claim accrues only then. Without that event the claim is dismissed without prejudice rather than stayed to impose state-remedy exhaustion; failed review, exhaustion or expiration or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The barred unaccrued claim consumes no limitations period while the prerequisite is unmet. No conversion to habeas or release follows. Independently required statutory administrative exhaustion and an independently authorized stay remain separate questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck supplies all four invalidation routes, accrual and dismissal; Patsy bars general judicial state-remedy exhaustion, which differs from cognizability.

##### Prospective date stamping remains for ordinary equitable adjudication

**Holding:** A genuinely prospective request governing future date stamping of received witness statements ordinarily need not invalidate a completed credit adjudication and is not automatically barred by Heck. Because this request was not adjudicated below, remand requires examination of live standing, ordinary equitable requirements and the actual claim; no prospective label saves relief whose substance necessarily invalidates the past judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous. This direct agreement does not change Heck's earlier rationale alignments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical procedural exemption and remand unanimously. Dismiss invalidity-dependent damages and retrospective declarations without prejudice absent qualifying invalidation; separately classify genuinely independent reasons, questioning or other theories and adjudicate the unaddressed prospective request. No credit, damages, injunction or misconduct finding follows.

#### Saucier v. Katz

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1977; judgment below, 194 F.3d 962.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### The binding remand instruction and its limited authority

**Authority of the judgment:** Souter announces a 5–4 vacatur and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the exact instruction to apply existing objective qualified immunity separately from the force merits on proper interlocutory assumptions, leaving both grant and denial of immunity available. No other Justice joins Souter's full reasoning. His distinct objective-defense rationale, Stone-Zsela's authoritative-reliance replacement and the three-Justice concurrence's force-specific objection are not logical subsets. No controlling Marks rationale establishes a new general immunity rule or mandatory merits-first sequence. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would direct immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harlow, Anderson and Graham retain their existing operation. Agreement between Souter and dissenters creates no new controlling sequence rule. The preserved legal inquiry uses September 24, 1994 law and plaintiff-favorable interlocutory assumptions, including disputed participation; the Parker-only account is not an appellate finding. There is no ultimate force, violation, participation, immunity, causation or injury determination. First Amendment, false-arrest, supervisory and prospective-relief branches are not reopened; the federal-officer damages channel is assumed without a new cause of action or institutional remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must perform the specified existing-law inquiry. Separable legal issues may be decided on the proper assumptions; genuinely material factual disputes remain for the appropriate forum. The mandate requires no actual-authoritative-reliance proof, additional reliance submissions, automatic trial, hearing, liability finding or damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Its existing objective personal-immunity defense remains in force; a separate proposal to replace it is not the Court's law.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Its particularized legal-notice inquiry remains available under the remand; neither an identical-case requirement nor proof of actual authoritative reliance replaces it.
- **Hunter v. Bryant:** Its judicial legal inquiry under proper summary-judgment assumptions governs the return; the court may not choose a more favorable competing factual account for the officer.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its consideration of all relevant preconduct law and ordinary preservation limits remain; later decisions cannot supply September 24, 1994 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its exclusion of evidentiary-sufficiency disputes from immediate immunity review remains applicable to disputed participation.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its distinction between a separable assumed-fact legal defense and a factual-sufficiency appeal remains; the present judgment creates no additional appellate jurisdiction.
- **Sterling v. Borough of Minersville:** Its unanimous participation-review rule applies to the supported two-officer account. Its different confidentiality and April 1997 notice holdings do not answer this force claim or supply earlier notice.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Its contextual force analysis remains; its supported immediate armed attack and absence of a contrary material account do not establish the facts here. Its later decision supplies no September 1994 notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its distinction between constitutional violation and conduct-date personal immunity remains, without requiring a factual twin; its 1999 decision supplies no 1994 notice.

#### Hopkins v. Reeves

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1693.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### Beck does not require creation of a nonincluded offense

**Holding:** Beck does not require a capital trial court to instruct on second-degree murder or manslaughter when those offenses are not lesser included offenses of the charged felony murder under Nebraska's governing elements. Its protection against withholding a legally available, evidence-supported lesser included option remains intact, as does independent constitutional review of a genuinely arbitrary exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for this rule and explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not approve withholding a genuine, supported lesser included instruction. An unpresented manslaughter-arbitrariness claim, the Teague question, any separate challenge to a presumption of intent, and the independent validity of judge sentencing remain undecided. Judicial selection of the sentence is an additional factual distinction, not a categorical cure for an unreliable guilt choice. The death-eligibility culpability requirement does not itself change the elements of the guilt offense.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas judgment is reversed on the Beck instruction ground and remanded; the separate rejected due-process ground is not reinstated, and no unrelated claim or execution date is determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Beck v. Alabama retains its protection against withdrawing legally and factually available lesser included instructions in capital cases; it does not require redesign of generally applicable offense elements.
- Spaziano v. Florida is applied for legal availability of the proposed alternative, without extending its separate judge-sentencing holding.
- United States v. Gaudin is applied to actual offense elements and jury determination; it supplies no power to create a different offense.
- Bowersox v. Williams remains limited to genuine supported noncapital options and does not decide their complete absence.
- Enmund v. Florida and Tison v. Arizona preserve the culpability limits on death; they do not themselves alter the guilt-phase elements of felony murder.
- Cabana v. Bullock supplies the distinction between the capital-culpability determination and the elements the guilt jury must decide.
- Buchanan v. Angelone remains a sentencing-mitigation decision and supplies no authority to withhold a genuine lesser included guilt instruction.

#### Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-478.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### Present-offense facts necessary for a higher maximum require element protections

**Holding:** Under the Sixth Amendment's jury guarantee, applied to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, and the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause, a fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to authorize punishment above the maximum supported by the verdict or plea is an element. It must be admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt; a sentencing label does not remove those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. Six direct rationale votes control the rule, both procedural guarantees, the Count 18 application and the bounded remedy. This is a new constitutional holding; the earlier statutory decisions did not establish it.

**Material application:** The plea's express reservation permits this claim; knowledge of possible exposure and admission of an unlawful purpose to frighten or harass do not admit the additional racial purpose. The lower court's view of strong evidence supplies neither an admission nor a jury verdict. The judgment sustaining the enhanced term is reversed and the term vacated, with the limited remand stated below. This application adopts no universal structural-error classification and does not automatically extend Neder's qualified materiality-error inquiry to every sentencing aggravator or plea defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Facts of prior conviction are not decided; there is no constitutional prior-conviction exception in this holding. Whether, or in what form, a State must charge a covered fact is not decided, and the Fifth Amendment's grand-jury clause is not applied to the States. The federal charging consequences of the earlier statutory decisions remain intact. Judicial factfinding within punishment already authorized by the conviction remains permissible. Unchanged-maximum mandatory minima, general consecutive-sentence rules, capital eligibility or selection, and materially different sentencing systems, including general Guidelines classifications, remain outside this decision. McMillan and Walton are not overruled. No valid waiver of the reserved claim occurred here; any distinct lawful-waiver question retains its own requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The New Jersey judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains the twelve-year Count 18 sentence. The constitutional rule adopted here requires vacatur of that enhanced sentence. On the existing admitted offense, a sentence within the five-to-ten-year range is permissible. Any further proceeding must have a basis in state law and comply with the governing plea, notice, waiver, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules; the Court neither orders nor authorizes a new aggravated prosecution or enhancement jury merely by remanding.

The pleas and convictions remain intact. The Court orders no acquittal, withdrawal of the pleas, specific new sentence, consecutive restructuring, aggregate sentence or release. The separate Graves Act question is not decided; any parole consequence must follow the lawful sentence and that provision's own terms rather than automatic retention of the former four-year figure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Duncan v. Louisiana:** Supplies application of the criminal jury guarantee to state prosecutions; the present rule identifies the additional fact that receives that guarantee.
- **In re Winship:** Supplies the reasonable-doubt protection for offense facts; that burden now governs the covered maximum-increasing present-offense fact.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies once the constitutional element is identified; the jury must determine that element, while Gaudin alone did not decide this antecedent classification.
- **Mullaney v. Wilbur:** Supports examination of the fact's legal role rather than its label when necessary facts determine aggravated punishment.
- **Patterson v. New York:** Preserves legislative definition of substantive offenses without permitting a label alone to transfer a covered aggravating element away from the jury.
- **Almendarez-Torres v. United States:** Preserves its aggravated-reentry statutory construction and federal charging consequences; neither a general constitutional maximum rule nor a constitutional recidivism exception is attributed to it.
- **Jones v. United States**, No. 97-6203: Preserves its construction of aggravated carjacking grades and associated federal element protections; the constitutional issue reserved there is decided here only at the present-offense scope.
- **Castillo v. United States:** Preserves the statutory element treatment of the former firearm-type aggravators; it is an analogue, not an earlier constitutional holding.
- **Carter v. United States:** Preserves its statutory value-element and distinct offense-comparison holdings; it does not supply a prior constitutional answer or a state charging rule.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Distinguished because its minimum left the maximum unchanged; that holding is not overruled.
- **Witte v. United States:** Preserves ordinary sentencing consideration within punishment authorized by the conviction; it supplies no additional maximum here.
- **United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Preserves its within-authority sentencing operation; the Court does not extend it to the fact that first authorizes a higher maximum.
- **Monge v. California:** Preserves its bounded noncapital prior-conviction double-jeopardy classification and reservations of independent charging, jury and proof questions; it supplies no rule for this nonrecidivist fact or automatic authority for a further proceeding.
- **Neder v. United States:** Preserves its qualified actual-verdict inquiry for the materiality errors it decided; strong evidence does not supply an actual admission here, and no universal sentencing-error rule is adopted.
- **Walton v. Arizona:** Leaves its capital rule undisturbed; the present noncapital decision does not resolve its relation to the broader constitutional theories in separate writings.

**Current-force modification:** United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, extends the present-offense element rule to facts raising either binding end of a punishment range, including Guidelines ceilings and floors below an outer statutory maximum, and overrules McMillan's contrary unchanged-maximum minimum rule. Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the rule to the statutory aggravator necessary for death eligibility and overrules Walton to that extent. Limon v. Kansas, June 26, 2003, then requires element protections for nonjury juvenile-adjudication facts necessary to raise an adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling. United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, extends the rule to necessary adult recidivist facts raising a binding maximum or mandatory minimum, with applicable federal charging protection. The covered allocations are therefore no longer reserved. Judicial assessment within independently authorized punishment remains lawful; the distinct state-charging question and the difference between death eligibility and final sentence selection retain their earlier scope.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, binding punishment ranges and federal charging; Harris v. United States, June 24, 2002, mandatory brandishing minimum and its separate plain-error remedy; Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, death-eligibility factfinding and qualified actual-verdict review. Limon v. Kansas, June 26, 2003, maximum-increasing nonjury juvenile predicates; United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, necessary adult recidivist predicates raising a binding maximum or minimum and applicable federal charging.

#### United States v. Angle, 254 F.3d 514 (judgment below)

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187; consolidated appeals of Corey Angle and James Edward Phifer.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections

**Holding:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to a jury, unless a lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner Guidelines range discretionary; ordinary judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Guidelines remain binding subject to the constitutional conditions. Prior-conviction questions, a general advisory-system remedy and consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Angle's independent findings-based sentence vacatur is affirmed unanimously; six Justices add the constitutional resentencing directions. The lawful range and supported findings within it must be determined without a judicially supplied replacement range.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi is extended to present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ceilings or floors. McMillan is overruled insofar as ordinary present-offense sentencing findings may raise a mandatory minimum merely because the maximum is unchanged. Mistretta retains institutional approval of the Commission. Witte and Watts/Putra retain ordinary assessment within independently authorized punishment, subject to the binding-range rule.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Limon v. Kansas, June 26, 2003, extends element protection to necessary nonjury juvenile predicates raising the adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling; United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, further extends protection to necessary adult recidivist predicates raising a binding maximum or mandatory minimum, resolving the prior-conviction reservation at those scopes.

##### Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact

**Holding:** In a federal prosecution, a present-offense fact performing the aggravating legal work identified in Part I-A must receive the applicable grand-jury charging protection as an element. A quantity-free charge and verdict, ordinary sentencing findings, or notice of possible exposure do not themselves establish that element; a valid element admission or lawful waiver must satisfy its own governing requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the disposition on this component without adopting the separate charging proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A valid admission or waiver must satisfy its own requirements. The mandate supplies no new charging or jury procedure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jones and Castillo supply federal aggravated-element charging consequences while retaining their distinct statutory holdings. Cotton confirms that the constitutional charging omission does not withdraw subject-matter jurisdiction.

##### Phifer's actual drug-count term is erroneous and plain

**Holding:** Phifer's 292-month drug-count sentence exceeds the 240 months authorized by his quantity-free verdict and is constitutional error. Applying the established review-time rule in the settled-law-then-changed setting, the count excess is plain; substantial-rights effect and discretionary correction remain separate requirements.

**Holding:** A demonstrated lawful and inevitable identical aggregate may bear on substantial-rights effect under Rule 52(b), but unused cumulative statutory exposure or a supposed mandatory total derived from the constitutionally disputed quantity findings does not itself establish that result. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano preserves all four forfeiture conditions and the ordinary defendant burden. Vonn permits consideration of the relevant existing record. #### Harris v. United States, 536 U.S. 545

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-10666.
**Decided:** 2002-06-24.

##### The amended statute uses a mandatory sentencing condition

**Holding:** Amended §924(c)(1)(A) defines the basic firearm offense and assigns graduated mandatory minimums; brandishing is a sentencing condition under that structure, a classification that does not dispense with constitutional element protections. The opening exception applies to the extent a greater minimum is otherwise provided by §924(c) or another provision of law; brandishing requires displaying all or part of the firearm, or otherwise making its presence known to another person, for the purpose of intimidating that person, whether or not the firearm is directly visible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jones supplies the interpretive method and retains its carjacking construction. Castillo is distinguished because its former firearm-type text differs materially; its statutory-element holding remains intact.

##### Compulsory additional punishment requires element protections

**Holding:** A present-offense fact legally necessary to raise a mandatory minimum must receive the charging protections applicable to the prosecution and must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder. **Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary judicial selection remains lawful within an independently established range. Prior-conviction facts, collateral retroactivity and general sentencing redesign are outside this decision; no constitutional prior-conviction exception is created.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Angle is applied, including its prior overruling of McMillan to the stated extent. Apprendi supplies the punishment-authorization principle; Gaudin and Winship supply proper factfinding and reasonable doubt. Jones and Castillo retain their independent federal charging consequences.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Gatewood, June 30, 2003, extends mandatory-floor element protections and applicable federal charging to necessary adult recidivist predicates; Harris's distinct plain-error disposition and other limits remain effective.

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that Harris forfeited the constitutional theory, Rule 52(b) requires error, clarity at review in this settled-law-then-changed setting, the defendant's ordinary showing of outcome-affecting prejudice, and a separate determination that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction is warranted. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's four conditions and ordinary burden are applied; Johnson supplies limited review-time plainness but its refusal of correction is distinguished. Cotton preserves nonjurisdictional treatment and record-specific discretion. ##### Recidivist facts increasing a binding maximum or minimum require element protections

**Holding:** A prior-conviction fact necessary to authorize a higher maximum or mandatory minimum must receive applicable federal charging protection and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless established by a valid admission under lawful charging and waiver procedures. **Holding:** When the indictment omits an indispensable federal enhancement allegation, the defendant preserves the objection and no valid charging waiver exists, sentencing notice and judicial findings cannot authorize the aggravated punishment. **Holding:** A cognizable §1983 claim creates no automatic stay of execution. #### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

##### Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

#### Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs, 538 U.S. 721

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1368.
**Decided:** 2003-05-27.

##### The FMLA expressly authorizes this family-care action against a covered State employer

**Holding:** Sections 2611(4)(A)(iii) and 2617(a)(2) unmistakably authorize eligible employees to seek prescribed family-care damages or equitable relief against covered State employers in competent federal or state courts. Public-agency coverage and explicit private-action authorization are separate requirements satisfied by the text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid and Kimel separate clear authorization from constitutional power. College Savings Bank preserves the distinct waiver inquiry and rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity.

##### Article I and Section 5 independently sustain the family-care action despite State immunity

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private suits against a State when both the substantive duty and its enforcement measure lie within a valid Article I power and suit is unmistakably authorized. The FMLA family-care employment duty and employee remedy satisfy the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses and independently defeat Nevada's categorical immunity defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), supported at this scope by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor does not join this ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett supply the Article I enforcement rule for valid legislation; Alden preserves the authorized state-court employment remedy. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming support substantive federal regulation of covered State employment. Legislative authorization is distinct from inferred State consent under College Savings Bank.

**Independent alternative holding:** Section 2612(a)(1)(C)'s sex-neutral, bounded family-care entitlement is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of constitutional sex equality in State employment. Section 5 independently supports the authorized private action even when the statutory violation would not itself prove an individual constitutional tort.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), supported at this scope by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens withholds this ground without deciding it invalid. Each sufficient ground has five direct joins; no Marks combination is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employee eligibility generally requires twelve months with the employer and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months, calculated under the applicable Fair Labor Standards Act standards. The Act excludes federal officers and employees covered by subchapter V of chapter 63 of Title 5, and a worker at a site with fewer than fifty employees if the employer also has fewer than fifty within seventy-five miles. Express public-agency coverage does not eliminate those employee requirements.

Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to provider certification under §2613, covers care for a spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. Parent includes a biological parent or one who stood in a parental role during the employee's childhood. Son or daughter includes a biological, adopted, foster or stepchild, legal ward or child for whom the employee stands in a parental role, under eighteen or, if older, incapable of self-care because of mental or physical disability. The enacted spouse definition is husband or wife. Serious health condition requires inpatient care in a hospital, hospice or residential medical facility, or continuing treatment by a health-care provider. The entitlement totals twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period across the statutory leave categories, rather than twelve weeks per episode or relative.

Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted, and only leave actually taken reduces the total. Foreseeable planned treatment requires a reasonable scheduling effort to avoid undue operational disruption, subject to provider approval, and thirty days' notice, or practicable notice if treatment must start sooner. For that foreseeable intermittent treatment, the employer may temporarily transfer a qualified employee to an available alternative position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.

Leave may be unpaid. An employee may elect, or an employer require, substitution of accrued vacation, personal or family leave for the specified birth, placement or family-care categories. For family-care or self-care serious-health-condition leave, accrued vacation, personal, medical or sick leave may similarly be substituted; that permission does not require paid medical or sick leave where the employer would not normally provide it. For spouses working for the same employer, combined leave may be limited to twelve weeks for birth, placement or care of a sick parent; that aggregation permission does not cover every family-care category.

An employer may require timely provider certification identifying commencement, probable duration, relevant medical facts, the need and expected time for care, and applicable treatment dates or intermittent-care need and schedule. A supported doubt permits an employer-paid second opinion from a provider not regularly employed by it; a conflict permits an employer-paid third opinion jointly chosen and binding on both sides. Reasonable subsequent recertification is permitted.

Restoration protects the former or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, without creating benefit accrual during leave or a right the employee would have lacked absent leave. The restoration exception requires a salaried employee among the highest-paid ten percent within seventy-five miles, necessity to prevent substantial and grievous economic injury, notice when the employer determines that injury would occur and, if leave has begun, the employee's election not to return after notice. This is a restoration exception, not permission to deny leave generally. Group-health coverage continues on the terms that would have applied during continuous employment. Premiums paid for unpaid leave may be recovered after failure to return when the entitlement expires, except when nonreturn results from continuation, recurrence or onset of a qualifying serious health condition or circumstances beyond the employee's control; timely provider certification may be required for the serious-health-condition exception. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain their enacted force. These qualifications define the scheme; their application to Hibbs requires the ordinary merits record.

**Treatment of earlier authority for alternative holding:** Boerne permits fitted prevention beyond separately provable violations but forbids redefinition of constitutional rights. Virginia supplies genuine important objectives and substantial fit for sex classifications, excluding role stereotypes as justification. Florida Prepaid and Garrett, which held Section 5 insufficient for undifferentiated patent liability and for Title I's general requirements measured against rational-basis review at the scope presented, are distinguished: sex classifications receive demanding intermediate scrutiny, and public-employment accounts and testimony give State-related support to the inference of employment exclusion and unequal leave administration sustained by sex-role assumptions, the injury this bounded entitlement targets. Popovich preserves the statutory-duty/constitutional-tort distinction without importing its parental-hearing setting. Kimel's reserved ADEA Section 5 question remains reserved. Self-care and other leave entitlements are outside this constitutional holding.

##### Rejection of immunity leaves ordinary FMLA liability, loss and remedial requirements

**Holding:** The family-care action proceeds under the FMLA's coverage, eligibility, liability, defenses and relief provisions, with Hibbs bearing the preponderance burden on required statutory facts and causally recoverable loss. Neither independently sufficient enforcement ground requires an additional individual equal-protection violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), supported at this scope by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The employer's exhaustion assertion remains contested; immunity rejection determines no statutory violation or award. The separate constitutional claim retains its existing procedural disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Section 2617 permits wages, salary, benefits or other compensation denied or lost by reason of the violation. When such compensation was not denied or lost, the alternative is actual monetary loss directly resulting from the violation, such as care costs, capped at twelve weeks of wages or salary; that cap applies to the alternative, not all backpay. Prevailing-rate interest is added. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensation or alternative loss plus interest. Only upon the employer's proof of both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the act or omission lawful may the court, in its discretion, reduce liability to principal and interest. Appropriate equitable relief includes employment, reinstatement or promotion; a judgment for the employee also carries reasonable attorney and expert fees and other costs. The limitations period runs two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three years for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint under §2617(d) seeking restraint of delayed payment of the employee's specified amounts, or under §2617(b) seeking recovery of those damages, terminates the private-action right unless that action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion. The statutory-action remand and dependent vacatur of the state-claim dismissal are affirmed, 6–3.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich distinguish valid statutory enforcement from proved liability and an individual award.

#### Nelson v. Campbell, 541 U.S. 637 (2004) — Federal Courts: Immunities and Remedies, OT2003

**Docket or dockets:** No. 03-6821.
**Decided:** May 24, 2004.

##### What prison-litigation conditions remain after recognizing the procedure-specific civil claim

**Holding:** The cognizable execution-procedure claim remains subject to exhaustion of actually available administrative remedies under §1997e(a) when applicable and to independent screening and prospective-relief restrictions. ### Whole-record review of a jury verdict under Rule 50

**Governing question:** How a court assesses whether the record can support a jury verdict.

### Conviction-negating damages claims and favorable invalidation

**Governing question:** When custody-related §1983 claims require prior favorable invalidation and when an execution-procedure challenge is compatible with the operative sentence.

**Current rule:** For an in-custody claimant with a real lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, a §1983 damages claim whose success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that conviction or sentence cannot proceed before favorable invalidation. Qualifying invalidation is reversal on direct appeal, executive expungement, a declaration of invalidity by an authorized state tribunal, or a federal writ of habeas corpus calling it into question. The court compares the actual civil theory and criminal basis, not labels. Independent injuries whose success is compatible with the criminal judgment remain separately cognizable. The barred claim accrues upon qualifying invalidation; the rule is not a direction to file now and seek a stay or an invented tolling regime. Within the in-custody category, damages or retrospective declarations alleging dishonest concealment of exculpatory evidence or disabling adjudicator bias in an outstanding earned-credit deprivation likewise require invalidation when success necessarily defeats that adjudication. Classify each alleged procedural injury by its legal consequence: a genuinely independent failure to state supporting facts and compatible prospective relief may proceed under ordinary requirements. An invalidity-dependent claim is dismissed without prejudice, not stayed for exhaustion; failed review or loss of a review opportunity is not qualifying invalidation.

For a former prisoner whose damages theory necessarily contradicts an operative custody determination, prior favorable invalidation is unnecessary if no realistic lawful opportunity ever existed to contest the confinement before custody review became unavailable, or if timely pursuit lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond the claimant's control. An unexcused failure to use a genuinely usable opportunity leaves the prerequisite in force. Release and present federal habeas unavailability alone establish neither the exception nor qualifying invalidation. This rule protects meaningful first review, not repeated review after an unsuccessful challenge.

Identify the actual judgment, administrative calculation or custody adjudication and compare it with the civil theory before applying the prerequisite. A claim that officials executed a valid sentence contrary to its terms may be compatible with that determination; an administration label alone decides nothing. Realistic availability and excused loss depend on supported custody, notice, access, timing and ability to pursue the particular challenge. Neither a theoretically imaginable procedure nor the absence of a successful writ answers the inquiry. Requiring exhaustion of every conceivable state remedy is not permitted. A compatible claim or one within the exception proceeds under ordinary civil rules; accrual and limitations outside the invalidation prerequisite follow their independently applicable law.

A challenge to a severable execution-related procedure may proceed under §1983 when success leaves the State a lawful means to carry out the sentence. At the pleading stage, a claim alleging an unnecessary procedure and a feasible alternative permitting lawful execution receives that claim-specific channel analysis; the allegations are not established medical facts. Temporary litigation delay does not by itself make compatible civil relief an attack on the sentence. If factual development shows the procedure is necessary for lawful execution, the court must reconsider the channel on that premise. A demand to forbid execution irrespective of the challenged procedure requires its own scope and channel inquiry. Civil cognizability establishes neither the medical merits nor entitlement to a stay.

### Equitable delay independently of a habeas procedural bar

**Governing question:** Whether an execution stay may lack equitable support even assuming the challenge is cognizable under §1983 and not barred as abuse of the writ.

**Current rule:** A court considering an execution stay may weigh the State's interest in enforcing its judgment and unjustified delay in presenting a long-available claim independently of a habeas procedural bar. A supported serious constitutional claim and the irreversible consequence of execution require consideration but do not alone establish entitlement to last-minute relief; later evidence strengthening the claim does not necessarily explain withholding the underlying challenge. Equitable vacatur on that ground may proceed while assuming an open §1983 channel and without adjudicating every McCleskey abuse element. The rule supplies a contextual equitable inquiry, not an automatic bar for late claims or an automatic capital stay.

The presumption remains contextual: the reasons for delay and irreversible harm must be considered, and nearness to execution alone does not establish avoidable delay. A properly presented interim request must receive meaningful prompt adjudication where the actual schedule permits; that scheduling duty supplies no automatic restraint. Any warranted restraint requires its own lawful basis, precise scope and duration justified by the record, with reconsideration as its premises change.

### Facts changing binding punishment ranges

**Governing question:** Which present-offense and recidivist facts must receive constitutional element protections.

**Current rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the jury. A lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder but retains that proof standard. This rule reaches mandatory Guidelines constraints below a larger outer statutory maximum: an enforceable inner range is not discretionary merely because a higher sentence is statutorily possible. Judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. A sentencing label, ordinary sentencing admission, notice of exposure or unused aggregate maxima on other counts supplies neither the missing element nor an inevitable lawful aggregate.

In a federal prosecution the covered aggravating fact also receives applicable grand-jury charging protection. A lawful jury waiver does not enlarge the charge or convert ordinary sentencing findings into element adjudication.

A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available likewise receives jury and reasonable-doubt protection in a jury-tried prosecution, subject to valid element admission and existing lawful waiver requirements. Eligibility is distinct from mitigation, weighing and ultimate selection among already authorized punishments.

Necessary recidivist facts receive element protection as well: existence, identity, and historical qualification or chronology that increases the authorized maximum or mandatory minimum must receive the applicable federal aggravated indictment allegation and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless lawfully admitted under valid charging and waiver procedures. A lawful jury waiver retains the proof burden and charge boundary. A nonjury juvenile adjudication needed to raise an adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling, is not categorically exempt; a waiver confined to the current act or an ordinary sentencing stipulation does not admit the aggravating element. Judges retain statutory interpretation, legal classification of established facts and discretion within independently authorized ranges.

For necessary §3559(c) robbery predicates, the prosecution must establish qualifying status through that element adjudication. The exclusion requires both no firearm or dangerous-weapon use or threatened use and no resulting death or serious bodily injury; the prosecution may defeat it by establishing the applicable alternative of weapon use, threatened use, death or serious injury. It may not shift uncertainty about necessary qualifying facts to a defendant’s clear-and-convincing burden. A prior judgment proves what it actually adjudicated, not every disputed historical qualification.

A preserved omission of the indispensable federal aggravated allegation, without a valid charging waiver, prevents enhanced punishment despite sentencing notice or judicial findings. Sentencing begins with ranges independently authorized by the charges and verdicts. Any new enhancement proceeding requires its own charging and procedural authority; remand grants none. Forfeited claims retain every Rule 52(b) requirement. Section 5G1.2(d) cannot make an identical aggregate inevitable by relying on a mandatory total-punishment range that itself depends on quantity lacking the required element adjudication.

### Effective correction of sex-based educational exclusion

**Governing question:** The adequacy of a proposed alternative remedy for an unconstitutional exclusion from a public educational opportunity.

**Current rule:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, considering material tangible and intangible benefits. A shared general purpose or equal annual public subsidy alone does not establish adequacy. Government must promptly eliminate the denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, retaining initial responsibility for lawful design under judicial supervision. Continued provision of the same public opportunity without an adequate lawful alternative requires consideration of qualified applicants without the unconstitutional categorical sex bar; promises of improvement do not permit indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive remedial adequacy and lawful government choice: Part II — Adequacy of the proposed alternative and Part III — Effective relief and lawful State choice, 1996-06-26; O’Connor, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven); Thomas did not participate.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Virginia / Virginia v. United States, 1996-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Legitimate qualifications, privacy, physical training and operational accommodations remain available. No identical campus, numerical resource ratio, expenditure minimum, guaranteed career outcome, quota or categorical prohibition of all separate education is prescribed. Admission scrutiny remains demanding intermediate review: a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means; neither litigation timing alone nor lack of an original written rationale establishes pretext.
