#### Blessing v. Freestone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1441.
**Decided:** April 21, 1997.

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

##### What effect state-plan placement has under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10

**Holding:** A Social Security Act provision cannot be denied enforceability merely because it specifies a state plan or its contents. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove that categorical ground without creating an individual right or changing the preserved conclusion that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The respective statutes apply to actions pending on October 20 and October 31, 1994 and those filed thereafter, including this action. Other established grounds governing private actions remain except the state-plan ground the legislation removes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Suter*'s actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights rulings and separate implied-action rulings remain, together with its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim. The statutory reference imports no broader state-plan immunity.

##### Whether a general prayer for relief eliminates Madrid's particular distribution allegation

**Holding:** Madrid's allegation of nondistribution under former §657(b)(1) requires its own inquiry despite the broad programwide prayer. Each actual claim must be matched to its provision, conduct, remedy, governing version, and operative dates; an obsolete or not-yet-effective duty cannot silently justify an injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former distribution language is more specific than the aggregate benchmark, but the Court decides no individual right, eligibility, payment fact, defendant, or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not award a separate $50 for every installment regardless of timing. The rule applies to covered approved-plan collections for fiscal years after September 30, 1976, subject to the distinct foster-care rules in subsection (d).

**Material application:** The foster-care sequence first reimburses current maintenance; qualifying excess up to ordered support goes to the placement agency for the child's interests; the remainder reimburses unrepaid earlier foster-care or family-assistance payments with appropriate federal reimbursement; any balance goes to the agency for the child. The agency may reserve its payments for later needs or make them available to the person providing daily care. No applicable exception or payment fact is established for Madrid.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remand must apply the following distinct statutory dates and qualifications, without inferring an Arizona implementation event or default:

- Pub. L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.
- Under §395(b), the applicable date is the later of the otherwise specified date or the effective date of implementing state legislation, but no later than the first day of the first calendar quarter after the close of the first regular legislative session beginning after August 22, 1996. Each year of a two-year session counts separately. Under §395(c), a State unable to comply without amending its constitution is protected from a noncompliance finding until the earlier of one year after the necessary amendment takes effect or five years after August 22, 1996. Neither rule establishes an Arizona event or compliance fact here.
- The former §654(24) operational-computer deadline was changed from October 1, 1995 to October 1, 1997 by the October 12, 1995 amendment; the old date establishes no present default. The 1996 text separately requires compliance by October 1, 1997 with requirements enacted on or before October 13, 1988, and by October 1, 2000 with requirements enacted on or before August 22, 1996. The latter deadline extends one day for every day the Secretary misses the final-regulations deadline of two years after August 22, 1996. Implementation deadlines remain distinct from enactment and general effective dates.
- Section 654a(g), added by §312(c), takes effect October 1, 1998. Section 312(d)(2) separately permits a State processing receipts through local courts on August 22, 1996 to continue that receipt processing through September 30, 1999, at its option, through the courts already processing those payments by enactment. That narrow exception concerns §654b(b)(1)'s unit receipt-handling requirement; it is not a wholesale extension of every computer-system obligation. No Arizona qualification for it is found, and these future duties are not April 1997 mandates merely because they appear in the compilation.

##### Whether relief may follow general administrative deficiencies without a proved individual violation

**Holding:** Relief requires an actual individual right, an available §1983 channel, a proper defendant, breach, causation, and a fitted lawful remedy. General administrative failure alone supports neither an undifferentiated compliance injunction nor a prescribed statewide staffing or computer redesign; rejection of the broad theory does not require dismissal of every concrete claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for provision-specific adjudication. No specified payment, damages award, structural injunction, or wholesale dismissal is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No directly implied Title IV-D action is decided. The Eleventh Amendment argument and proposed overruling of *Thiboutot*, neither raised below nor included in the questions granted, are not reached; defendant-specific issues remain subject to their own inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Maine v. Thiboutot* remains undisturbed. *Livadas*'s separate remedial inquiries prevent both an automatic structural decree and categorical rejection of particular claims.

#### Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1908.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Title VI's bounded direct prospective action, recognized in Powell v. Ridge, is available to enforce the Department of Justice section 602 effects regulation

**Holding:** Persons actually subjected to a prohibited national-origin effect in a covered federally assisted program may seek prospective compliance with the Department of Justice regulation through Title VI's bounded direct equitable action. Under Powell v. Ridge, a person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects. The action exists within Title VI's particular statutory context and rests on that context, not on the regulation's own force; it does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. The rule applies to every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within Powell's bounded prospective scope; it is not an exception for English-language testing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Souter's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, directly controls this rule, its explanation, the treatment of Powell, the application, remedy and limits; five direct joins. O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judgment, applying Powell's direct-action holding as controlling statutory precedent without adopting its account of congressional authorization; the four join no part of the Court's opinion. The judgment is unanimous, and there is no dissent.

**Material application:** The Department receives federal assistance. The regulation's validity and the lower courts' findings of prohibited national-origin effects were not independently presented and are not adjudicated anew. The Eleventh Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the granted private-action question, and its underlying prospective judgment against the Alabama Department of Public Safety's English-only driver's-license examination policy is left undisturbed: the injunction requiring accommodation of non-English-speaking applicants is not vacated for absence of a private cause of action.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 601 identifies the protected beneficiaries and section 602 authorizes implementation of their protection; Congress legislated, in the 1986 remedies provision and the Civil Rights Restoration Act, where private Title VI enforcement and effects regulations were familiar. Section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures govern the agencies' own powers; they are not the exclusive route to prospective compliance, and federal agencies retain the enforcement Congress authorized. The claimant must establish what Powell requires: statistics alone are not conclusive, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. Remedies are confined to declaration, an injunction ending or correcting the prohibited method, a reasonable compliance period where consistent with preventing ongoing injury, and proportionate supervision; the action supplies no damages for unintended disparate impact, retrospective compensation without an independent legal source or private order terminating or withholding federal grants. The section 601 intentional-discrimination action keeps its intent requirement, and the authority and validity limits on section 602 regulations remain. The Court finds no discriminatory purpose and infers none from the effects finding. Section 1983 enforcement is not presented. No new liability fact, procedural exhaustion duty or statistical presumption follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 9–0. The injunction remains subject to ordinary lawful modification or independent review of issues outside the grant. No new factual inquiry, damages award, remand to relitigate the private action, discriminatory-purpose finding, new section 1983 claim or funding order follows, and the Court retains no return condition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cannon is preserved at the scope Powell states, applied in its Title VI-derived context and not as universal implied-action authority. Guardians is preserved as fractured precedent at the scope Powell states; its distinction between prospective compliance and retrospective compensation informs the remedial bounds, and no disputed plurality proposition is treated as an earlier majority holding. Choate keeps the statutory prohibition and the effects regulations distinct standards. Franklin governs relief within the Powell action and supplies no damages for effects alone. McClellan stands as decided: its contextual, statute-specific authorization inquiry is the one Powell applied, and its rejection of a new Cable Act damages action is distinguished, not displaced. Central Bank's confined received securities action is intact. Blessing, Wright and Wilder are preserved; section 1983 enforcement is not presented, and Powell's section 1983 holding is consistent with them. Gebser and Davis are preserved at the scope Powell states, without importing their mental-state requirements into effects regulations.

##### Powell v. Ridge controls and is applied

**Holding:** Powell's direct-action holding decides this case and is applied without change. Nothing distinguishes it: not a different agency's regulation, not a different state policy, and not a district-court finding rather than a pleaded allegation of prohibited effects. Powell's separate section 1983 holding is outside the grant and remains controlling at its own scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five Justices join this treatment of Powell and its statutory stare-decisis explanation in Souter's opinion of the Court. The four Justices concurring in the judgment apply Powell's direct-action holding as controlling statutory precedent in O'Connor's opinion; they keep as their own view their disagreement with Powell's account of congressional authorization but do not ask the Court to reconsider Powell.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Petitioners argue that section 602 addresses only agencies, that a valid implementing obligation does not show congressional authorization of a private action, and that an agency's wording cannot create one; Powell considered and answered that argument on prospective relief against state officials. Statutory precedent carries special force because Congress can alter a construction it disagrees with; Congress has not amended Title VI, and no intervening decision undermines Powell. The Court applies Powell at its actual scope and extends it to no statute with a materially different enforcement scheme.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Powell v. Ridge, April 3, 2000, is applied and controlling. Its direct-action holding decides this case at its actual scope, reaching every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation, and is not distinguished by a different regulation, policy or proved effects; its separate section 1983 holding is outside the grant and remains controlling at its own scope.

**Later-authority backlinks:** South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection, review of 274 F.3d 771, May 28, 2002: applies Sandoval with Powell's direct-action holding to valid EPA section 602 effects regulations, 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b) and (c), and a state permitting program, all nine Justices holding that concretely affected plaintiffs may bring the Powell prospective action and that it is not a program-specific exception; in a separate 5–4 Part, Powell's section 1983 holding, which Sandoval left undisturbed, is reaffirmed for the EPA regulations; all nine also hold a preserved section 601 intentional-discrimination claim cognizable and remand it on its own proof.

#### Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-5843.
**Decided:** June 8, 1992.

For the preservation bar on the jury-heinousness instruction claim in *Sochor v. Florida*, June 8, 1992, No. 91-5843, see Federal Courts — Adequate and independent state grounds.

##### Whether the judge's conscious-victim strangulation application supplies a sufficiently narrowed heinousness aggravator

**Holding:** A sentencing judge may apply an otherwise vague heinousness aggravator through an authoritative and sufficiently specific state narrowing construction. Florida's consistently recognized conscious-victim strangulation construction supplies adequate guidance here; inconsistent applications in other factual settings do not establish that this judge lacked guidance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; seven Justices. Blackmun and Stevens do not join this disposition because they would require correction of the separate jury defect.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at the stated judicial application and record.

**Material application:** Florida's identified narrowing decisions address the fear and conscious suffering of strangulation. The victim's resistance, fear and strangulation support that concrete construction; the judge did not merely repeat the statutory adjectives.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves neither every Florida heinousness decision nor the bare instruction given to this jury, and it does not decide a different victim's condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject the preserved constitutional challenge to the judge's narrowed heinousness application; that factor supplies no basis for relief.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Godfrey v. Georgia*, 446 U.S. 420, and *Maynard v. Cartwright*, 486 U.S. 356, require meaningful narrowing. *Walton v. Arizona*, 497 U.S. 639, permits reliance on authoritative state constructions and presumes the judge's familiarity with them. *Proffitt v. Florida*, 428 U.S. 242, preserves a narrowed heinousness category without approving every later application.

##### Whether submission of a lawful but unsupported coldness aggravator establishes that a general advisory jury recommendation relied on it

**Holding:** Submitting a legally available aggravating circumstance without evidentiary support does not itself establish that a Florida advisory jury relied on it in a general death recommendation that identifies no aggravator findings. The asserted inference of jury-coldness error is rejected without a finding that the jury actually rejected the factor or resolution of a legally invalid instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Souter's opinion, joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five Justices. Stone reserves this independent question, Blackmun and Stevens would include the submission in correction, and Scalia does not join this holding.

**Current force:** Remains controlling at this narrow factual-insufficiency inference and general advisory recommendation.

**Material application:** Cold, calculated and premeditated murder was a lawful aggravator, but proof of its heightened premeditation requirement failed. The jury identified no aggravator findings, so submission alone cannot establish its reliance. The judge's express coldness finding establishes a separate error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding makes no factual jury finding, does not assimilate every capital recommendation to a guilt verdict, does not decide that all instructional errors are harmless, and leaves broader capital-jury doctrine open. Legally invalid alternatives remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Griffin v. United States*, December 3, 1991, is extended narrowly through its factual-versus-legal distinction to this asserted inference from a general advisory recommendation. *Stromberg v. California*, 283 U.S. 359, and *Yates v. United States*, 354 U.S. 298, retain their rules for legally invalid alternatives. *Estelle v. McGuire*, December 4, 1991, retains whole-charge reasonable-likelihood review, which neither removes the preservation bar nor supplies an automatic inference of factual reliance here.

##### Whether the judge's express weighing of the evidentially unsupported coldness aggravator creates constitutional error

**Holding:** When a Florida capital sentencing judge expressly weighs an aggravator that the state supreme court holds unsupported by the evidence, that invalid factor creates Eighth Amendment error in the weighing decision. Lawful reweighing, constitutional harmless-error review or resentencing is required; other valid aggravators do not themselves cure the error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B-1 of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices. Scalia rejects the constitutional weighing-error premise.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for documented reliance on an unsupported aggravator.

**Material application:** The judge expressly found and weighed heightened coldness, which Florida held unproved. Proof of ordinary murder premeditation does not establish this additional aggravating requirement. Surviving death eligibility therefore does not eliminate the actual weighing error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The convictions and mitigation findings are not disturbed. Removal of an aggravator does not itself require a life sentence or a new penalty jury. The no-mitigation finding does not mean no mitigating evidence was presented.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The sentence requires a supported determination under *Chapman*, lawful reweighing if Florida law authorizes it, or resentencing. The separate holding below identifies why the existing appellate treatment fails to provide that correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, requires adequate correction of capital weighing error. *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, applies that principle to unsupported Florida aggravators and the actual sentencing record. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, supplies the correction principle for this documented reliance; its habeas retroactivity and default reservations do not bar direct review. *Zant v. Stephens*, 462 U.S. 862, is distinguished because its nonweighing setting does not make surviving eligibility an automatic cure. *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, supplies constitutional harmlessness with the State bearing the burden.

##### Whether Florida's proportionality discussion and surviving-factor count constitutionally cure the weighing error

**Holding:** A state appellate court affirming this sentence must substantively establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the invalid aggravator did not contribute to the sentence, or provide another authorized constitutional cure. Florida's existing proportionality discussion, count of surviving factors and mixed citations do not establish that inquiry; no verbal formula is either required or sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts III-B-2 and IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Kennedy; six Justices. White and Thomas accept the error framework but find the existing review sufficient; Scalia would affirm on a separate ground.

**Material application:** Proportionality compares sentences across cases; it does not itself determine whether this invalid factor influenced this sentencing balance. Three valid factors and no found mitigation may bear on harmlessness but do not establish the missing inquiry. The State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither undertakes reweighing nor determines the outcome of lawful correction. It requires no prescribed phrase and rejects a bare harmless-error label as sufficient. No automatic life sentence, mandatory new penalty jury, new mitigation finding or new habeas standard is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the death sentence and remand to Florida for supported *Chapman* review, lawful reweighing if Florida law permits, or resentencing. The convictions remain intact; the state courts must choose and perform an adequate lawful correction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Chapman v. California*, 386 U.S. 18, retains the State's burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute. *Clemons v. Mississippi*, 494 U.S. 738, and *Parker v. Dugger*, 498 U.S. 308, require corrective analysis of the sentence and actual record rather than proportionality alone. *Stringer v. Black*, March 9, 1992, preserves the inadequacy of factor counting and proportionality as standalone cures.

#### Penry v. Johnson, 532 U.S. 782

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-6677.
**Decided:** June 4, 2001.

##### Whether section 2254(d)(1) permits relief on the use of the earlier defense-requested psychiatric report

**Holding:** The state court reasonably distinguished a defense-requested competency report used to cross-examine a defense mental-condition expert from Estelle v. Smith's compelled, uncounseled capital examination. Its adjudication is neither contrary to nor an objectively unreasonable application of the federal law established at the February 22, 1995 state-merits cutoff, so section 2254(d)(1) forecloses this psychiatric claim without an actual-effect determination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The report's dangerousness conclusion was elicited over objection and repeated in closing; no absence of objection or universal subject-matter waiver is found. The Court decides no general direct-review admissibility or privilege rule. June 7, 1995 rehearing denial does not establish a later substantive-law cutoff.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Estelle v. Smith remains intact in its compelled-examination setting. Buchanan v. Kentucky supplies the defense-initiated evaluation and responsive-use distinction without universal waiver. Williams v. Taylor supplies distinct statutory clauses and objective reasonableness without importing an exclusive allocation proposal lacking a majority.

##### Whether the special issues and mitigation supplement provided a lawful way to choose life

**Holding:** Supported mitigation reducing culpability independently of the factual special issues requires an authorized, coherent means of giving that judgment effect in a life verdict. A supplement allowing a negative answer for mitigation is insufficient when the unchanged form makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt despite the juror's belief that the fact is proved; in the complete charge and trial context here, the reasonable likelihood of restriction makes the state acceptance objectively unreasonable under established law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. No independent contrary-to holding is adopted.

**Material application:** Jurors could find deliberateness, future dangerousness and lack of reasonable provocation while treating intellectual impairment and childhood abuse as mitigating. The form required at least ten jurors' factual reasonable doubt for a negative answer and unanimity beyond reasonable doubt for an affirmative answer. The judge's similar selection-stage explanations and renewed defense closing were considered but did not reconcile that final certification conflict; neither forgotten instructions nor a separate prosecutorial prohibition is invented.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No favored mitigation weight, prescribed fourth question, categorical disability exemption or separate unanimity holding is required. The error is neither classified as structural nor equated with demonstrated effect.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lockett v. Ohio and Eddings v. Oklahoma protect meaningful culpability mitigation. Penry v. Lynaugh, Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas supply the pre-1995 lawful effective-vehicle requirement. Boyde v. California requires reasonable likelihood in the complete charge and trial. Buchanan v. Angelone, Weeks v. Angelone and Shafer v. South Carolina retain their later, distinct roles and supply no backdated 1995 substantive benchmark.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Atkins v. Virginia, June 20, 2002, establishes a separate categorical bar to execution for established intellectual disability and requires a reliable status procedure. Penry's effective-mitigation protection remains intact; it did not already establish that categorical bar.

##### Which harmless-error burden governs this preserved original-charge mitigation restriction

**Holding:** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind on supported capital culpability mitigation, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must allow a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence; the guilt judgment is unaffected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins. Stevens and Kennedy support the corrective return but reject this standard; Scalia and Thomas oppose the mitigation disposition. Their distinct effect approaches are not aggregated under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error does not automatically establish contribution, and answers to the factual special issues do not establish what an unconflicted mitigation decision would have been. The entire penalty record, including clarifications, must be considered; no present harmlessness or harmfulness finding is made. Brecht's general rule governs this federal habeas effect inquiry; it does not supply clearly established substantive law at the 1995 cutoff, create a new retroactivity exception or displace its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The mitigation certificate disposition is reversed for an issue-specific appeal and remaining full-record effect inquiry, with constitutional error and section 2254(d)(1) eligibility settled. No present writ, sentence vacatur, fixed resentencing deadline, stay, release or execution order is entered; conditional relief follows only if the State fails its burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chapman v. California is applied through Brecht's general rule to this preserved original-charge capital-mitigation error, allocating the beyond-reasonable-doubt showing to the State. Calderon v. Coleman applied that rule to commutation misinformation and is followed here. Weeks's return of its response error for effect review under Brecht's general rule is undisturbed. Brecht v. Abrahamson supplies the general rule, subject to its exceptions for structural defects and rights carrying their own prejudice element; O'Neal v. McAninch and California v. Roy applied it to the errors they presented and remain intact.

For reviewability of the certificate denial and the distinction between certification and a present writ, see Federal Courts - Capital habeas certificates and issue-specific appellate return, Penry v. Johnson, No. 00-6677, decided June 4, 2001.

#### Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1127.
**Decided:** March 5, 2003.

For §2254(d)(1), the 1997 substantive-law cutoff and the conditional sentence-only writ in this case, March 5, 2003, No. 01-1127, see Federal Courts: Habeas and Collateral Review — Objective habeas review of the aggregate California theft punishment.

##### Whether the actual fifty-year minimum independently violates the Eighth Amendment

**Holding:** The actual fifty-year minimum for these two low-value nonviolent thefts is grossly disproportionate even with the serious priors and separate occasions fully considered.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes establish the constitutional violation separately from the §2254(d)(1) defect.

**Material application:** The punishment must be evaluated as the imposed fifty-year aggregate, not isolated into two terms. The two thefts total approximately $150; serious prior burglaries and separate occasions retain substantial weight but leave the disparity constitutionally extreme. Eligibility for parole after the minimum is not assured release.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both convictions remain valid, and California retains responsibility for choosing a lawful replacement sentence under otherwise valid recidivist law. Passing the federal statutory threshold alone would not establish this constitutional result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Solem supplies actual-offense proportionality; Rummel supplies the relevance of priors and real parole conditions. Harmelin's narrow proportionality principle retains force at its actual supporting level. These authorities govern the independent merits inquiry as well as the distinct habeas threshold.

#### Tennard v. Dretke, 542 U.S. 274

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-10038.
**Decided:** 2004-06-24

For Tennard v. Dretke, 542 U.S. 274, 2004-06-24, No. 02-10038, see Federal Courts: Habeas and Collateral Review for [Whether the preserved mitigation claim warrants a certificate of appealability](../HOLDINGS.md#whether-the-preserved-mitigation-claim-warrants-a-certificate-of-appealability).

##### Whether culpability mitigation requires a severe permanent impairment or causal connection to the offense

**Holding:** Supported intellectual-functioning and other culpability mitigation cannot be excluded from constitutional consideration merely because the defendant has not proved a uniquely severe permanent impairment or a causal connection between the impairment and the offense. Evidence may bear on culpability independently of deliberateness or future danger; relevance requires neither a completed categorical disability diagnosis nor favorable weight from the sentencer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The IQ score of 67 supports a culpability argument even if it does not establish adaptive limitations, developmental onset or a categorical intellectual-disability diagnosis. Evidence may mitigate culpability while also bearing adversely on future danger.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham v. Collins supplies the rejection of the uniquely severe permanent-handicap screen and protection for independent culpability mitigation. Johnson v. Texas preserves effective consideration beyond the factual special issues. Penry v. Johnson supplies the coherent-verdict requirement and separates the constitutional/statutory inquiry from effect review. Atkins v. Virginia concerns categorical intellectual disability and is distinguished from ordinary mitigation relevance.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-6615.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure

**Holding:** Section 2254(e)(2) is triggered by deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the claim's state-court factual basis; an incomplete record alone is insufficient. When triggered, the applicant must show either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence, and must also show by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Material application:** Reasonable effort is measured against known leads and available procedures. A generic development request is neither categorically sufficient nor categorically deficient; the public existence of a document is not an informative lead by itself. This post-enactment application falls within section 2254(e)(2).

##### II. The known psychiatric report triggers the statutory bar

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to pursue an identified psychiatric report through reasonably available means, after receiving a transcript repeatedly referring to it, constitutes deficient diligence here. Williams's conceded inability to satisfy section 2254(e)(2)(B) therefore forbids the requested hearing on that report claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices and controls with eight votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to pursue the identified report is not a new Brady merits determination or a finding of bad faith, and it is not attributed to the two relationship claims. The conceded failure to satisfy the cumulative innocence condition makes other exception questions unnecessary.

##### III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead

**Holding:** The applicant did not fail to develop the juror-relationship and prosecutor-nondisclosure claims where counsel lacked a lead to the concealed relationships, reasonably sought investigative assistance, and the State opposed and the court denied that assistance. The public existence of a divorce record does not by itself establish deficient diligence, so section 2254(e)(2) does not bar factual development of these claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory hearing eligibility does not establish bias, dishonesty, constitutional misconduct or entitlement to a writ. Independent lawful grounds may resolve either claim without immaterial factfinding; remaining hearing and merits predicates still apply.

##### IV. Cause is established only for the specified concealed-fact nonpresentation

**Holding:** The concealed relationships, lack of an informative lead, reasonable unsuccessful investigative efforts and later unavailability of state relief establish cause insofar as default consists of failing to present these two relationship-based claims in the Virginia proceeding. That conclusion does not excuse a different default or establish prejudice; the appropriate prejudice standard and its application remain for the lower courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Ginsburg's opinion has the support of all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the lower no-cause ground without joining this reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exhaustion, actual independent and adequate state default, section 2254(d)'s state-merits predicate, section 2254(e)(1)'s factual presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden, and ordinary hearing requirements remain distinct. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on the merits but does not require it; unavailable or ineffective state process and express State-counsel waiver have their own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The report-hearing denial is affirmed, 8–1. The eight-Associate Court sets aside the relationship claims' diligence barriers; all nine support removing those barriers and the specified no-cause ground, although Stone-Zsela seeks further diligence inquiry and joins no cause rationale. Remaining default, prejudice and hearing issues return for adjudication. The separate plea-agreement disposition and identified unreviewed rulings stand; no writ or universal hearing command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Keeney and Townsend retain their deliberate-bypass former-law hearing rule. The later statute changes the consequence of deficient diligence in applications it governs; the Court does not rewrite Keeney's prior holding.
- Lindh is applied for provision-specific temporal reach; this post-enactment application is governed by section 2254(e)(2).
- Coleman remains controlling for an actual adequate and independent state claim default; hearing eligibility does not eliminate it.
- Vasquez retains the distinction between supplementing an exhausted claim and fundamentally altering it into a new claim.
- Weeks preserves the actual state-merits predicate and independent statutory conditions for relief; no general construction of section 2254(d)(1) beyond that holding is supplied here.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

#### Early v. Packer, 537 U.S. 3

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1765.
**Decided:** November 4, 2002.

##### Whether omitted Supreme Court citations or federal supervisory instructions establish a state-trial habeas violation

**Holding:** A state court need not cite Supreme Court decisions for its adjudication to comply with clearly established federal law; its actual rule, reasoning and result control. Jury coercion is assessed from all surrounding circumstances, and federal supervisory rules do not independently establish a broader constitutional command for state trials.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

##### Whether the state court reasonably rejected coercion on the complete deliberation sequence

**Holding:** On this complete sequence, the state court’s rejection of coercion is a reasonable application of the constitutional rule. Section 2254(d)(1) therefore precludes the writ direction on this claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and disposition.

#### Woodford v. Visciotti, 537 U.S. 19

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-137.
**Decided:** November 4, 2002.

##### Whether occasional prejudice shorthand establishes use of an impermissible burden

**Holding:** Occasional shorthand does not establish a higher prejudice burden when the state opinion as a whole states and uses Strickland’s reasonable-probability standard. Courts must nevertheless examine the actual assessment of the complete supported mitigation, aggravation and counsel’s presentation; reciting the evidence or the correct formula alone cannot establish a reasonable application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable probability means sufficient to undermine confidence, not more likely than not. Deficiency remains assumed. Strickland supplied the constitutional rule at the 1996 state adjudication; Williams interprets Section 2254. Factual clear-and-convincing review does not govern the legal inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Strickland supplies complete-record prejudice review. Williams preserves independent statutory inquiries and substantive mitigation assessment; neither a correct quotation nor imperfect shorthand determines reasonableness. Fretwell does not add an unfairness requirement to ordinary prejudice from impaired presentation of lawful mitigation.

##### Whether this state no-prejudice assessment falls outside the permissible statutory range

**Holding:** The state court’s substantive no-prejudice determination on this developed record falls within Strickland’s reasonable range. Neither a contrary governing rule nor an objectively unreasonable application is established under §2254(d)(1), so the sentence writ on this claim must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela dissents from this application and remedy.

**Material application:** The state assessment substantively considered the omitted abusive childhood, neurological difficulties and counsel’s damaging presentation against the killing, attempted killing and earlier violence; some brain-injury evidence had reached the jury. The actual assessment remains reasonably supportable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither aggravated murder nor omitted childhood evidence dictates prejudice categorically. Jury questions and deliberation length do not themselves establish the required probability. Williams’s truncated mitigation and added Fretwell condition are distinguished; its Virginia-specific unanimity and remedy rules do not govern California.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the penalty-phase writ on this ineffective-assistance claim, 8–1. The convictions remain unaffected.

#### Lockyer v. Andrade, 538 U.S. 63

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-1127.

##### How §2254(d)(1) review and its substantive-law date differ from constitutional entitlement

**Holding:** For a claim decided on the merits in state court, §2254(d)(1) requires a decision contrary to this Court's clearly established governing holdings or an objectively unreasonable application of them; an independently incorrect result alone is insufficient. The relevant substantive holdings are those established at the state merits adjudication, a general principle may clearly establish law without a factual twin, and entitlement to a writ additionally requires the underlying constitutional violation and appropriate relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court is unanimous; all nine Justices adopt the review framework without unanimous agreement on its application or relief.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The relevant substantive proportionality holdings predate the 1997 state merits adjudication. **Treatment of earlier authority:** Terry Williams supplies the distinct contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses and objective review. Bell preserves the separate constitutional merits requirement. Early rejects citation formalism and a factual-twin prerequisite. Norris's later pre-AEDPA sentencing decision supplies no new substantive law for the earlier state adjudication.

##### Whether the state decision sustaining the actual fifty-year minimum unreasonably applied established law

**Holding:** The state court's decision sustaining two consecutive twenty-five-years-to-life terms for these two nonviolent thefts totaling approximately $150, with the established serious prior burglaries fully considered, objectively unreasonably applied the preexisting gross-disproportionality principle. **Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt the statutory application. The same five join Part III's independent constitutional finding and conditional remedy.

The District Court must hear the parties and fix a reasonable compliance period requiring California to impose a lawful proportionate sentence or terminate custody attributable to the invalid punishment. Compliance requires a new lawful judgment or proof that the affected custody ended; on noncompliance the writ must operate against that custody. Both convictions survive, and any independent valid custody basis must be separately identified.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rummel's meaningful earlier parole distinguishes its recidivist result. Solem supplies an enforceable property-offense proportionality limit. #### Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington — planned force and continued gunpoint restraint

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 99-1373; no Supreme Court docket supplied. Review of 268 F.3d 1179 (10th Cir. 2001).

##### Whether the deliberate plan immediately shaping a seizure receives Fourth Amendment force review

**Holding:** The Fourth Amendment reaches a deliberate force decision that immediately and materially shapes an ensuing seizure as well as force used during execution. Objective reasonableness depends on the force actually organized, circumstances reasonably known to officials and each defendant's own causal conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

Garner supplies the need to justify the degree and manner of force. Michigan v. Summers permits reasonable execution-related control; Wilson v. Layne distinguishes authority to execute a warrant from lawful means of execution.

##### Whether continued direct firearm threats to controlled, nonthreatening children are unreasonable

**Holding:** Intentionally aiming firearms directly at compliant, nonthreatening children after submission and complete control, without a supported safety need, is unreasonable force. The intentional gunpoint restraint is a seizure even without a shot or physical wound; an initial need to secure the premises does not itself justify continuing the direct threats.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Maintaining readiness for an actual safety need is distinct from sustained direct aiming on the assumed after-control facts. Individual participation and causation remain subject to ordinary proof.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham requires reassessment as justification changes and control is obtained. Brower v. County of Inyo supplies intentional control as the relevant seizure characteristic; Summers does not authorize unjustified continuing force.

#### Gisbrecht v. Barnhart, 535 U.S. 789

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-131.
**Decided:** May 28, 2002.

##### Whether section 406(b) requires an exclusive lodestar starting point

**Holding:** When §406(b)'s favorable-judgment and attorney-representation conditions are satisfied, the court begins with the lawful contingent-fee agreement and independently determines a reasonable fee for the actual court representation, no more than 25 percent of the statutorily defined past-due benefits. The agreement carries no presumption of reasonableness, the attorney bears the burden of substantiating the requested award, and an exclusive lodestar cannot replace the agreement at the outset merely because it is customary in adversary fee-shifting cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes. Scalia rejects the agreement-centered method.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** City of Burlington v. Dague, 505 U.S. 557 (1992), and Blanchard v. Bergeron, 489 U.S. 87 (1989), are distinguished by the statutory obligation and payor; their fee-shifting holdings remain in force.

##### How the court reviews a contingent fee for reasonableness

**Holding:** The court must assess the actual services and results and explain the fee allowed. It may reduce the contractual request for substandard representation, attorney-caused delay inflating accumulated benefits, or compensation disproportionate to the representation; time records and rate information may assist that inquiry, but a high effective hourly rate alone does not establish unreasonableness and no universal multiplier or hourly limit governs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part II has the same eight-Justice coalition. Scalia separately agrees with mandatory review, substantiation and the ceiling, without joining the agreement-centered implementation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Hours and rates are evidence, not mandatory replacement arithmetic. Contingency gives no separate entitlement to a risk premium, unrelated-loss reimbursement or a fee justified merely by generic Social Security litigation risk. Each representation requires its own determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the affirmance of Gisbrecht’s, Miller’s and Sandine’s awards and remand for the three determinations to be set aside and redetermined. No maximum or other amount is directed; Anderson is outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Buckhannon Board & Care Home, Inc. v. West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources, May 29, 2001, supplies only the distinction between eligibility and reasonable amount; its statute-specific eligibility holding is not transferred to §406(b).

##### Which statutory payment conditions and EAJA overlap rules remain distinct

**Holding:** Section 406(b) permits a judicially allowed fee for successful court representation from, and not in addition to, the prescribed past-due benefits, subject to its 25 percent ceiling and applicable statutory payment conditions. EAJA separately permits government-paid fees and other expenses under its own conditions; when counsel receives fees under both provisions for the same work, counsel must refund the smaller fee to the claimant under Public Law 99-80's saving provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III is adopted by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela additionally joins favorable judgment, court representation, the cap, independent allowance, separate EAJA operation and the overlapping-fee refund, without a position on individual certification, assessment, benefit-base or criminal-liability applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 406(a) administrative representation is separate, and the aggregate administrative-and-court ceiling is not decided. EAJA rates do not determine the §406(b) award; the smaller-fee comparison concerns fees for the same work, not every expense. A contract alone is not an award. Apart from the EAJA saving provision, no additional fee may be payable or certified for the same court representation outside §406(b).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The applicable benefit-base provision excludes continued benefits under §423(g) or (h) and calculates benefits before an applicable §1320a-6(a) reduction. Its 1994 addition applies to favorable judgments made after 180 days after August 15, 1994. The assessment amendment applies when certification is required after January 31, 2000. Subsection (d) assesses the attorney, permits offset collection, prohibits direct or indirect claimant reimbursement, and permits collection and availability for obligation only to the extent and in the amount provided in advance by appropriations Acts. The pre-2001 rate is 6.3 percent; after 2000 the Commissioner sets the cost-recovery rate, capped at 6.3 percent. Actual dates and inputs govern those applications on remand. Section 406(b)(2) forbids charging, demanding, receiving or collecting more than the court allows, with a maximum $500 fine, one year of imprisonment, or both, subject to the specific EAJA saving rule. The methodology dispute establishes no violation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shalala v. Schaefer, 509 U.S. 292 (1993), and Forney v. Apfel, 524 U.S. 266 (1998), retain their distinct remand, fee and review functions. Martin v. Hadix, June 21, 1999, does not replace these express judgment and certification transitions with its service-date rule.

#### Gonzaga University v. Doe, 536 U.S. 273

**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Whether FERPA nondisclosure provisions establish the asserted Section 1983 entitlement

**Authority of the judgment:** Reverse and remand the FERPA-based federal judgment, 6–3: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support; Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg oppose. The four-Justice lead opinion supplies no controlling general requirement of congressional intent to create a private remedy. No additional damages ruling follows this threshold disposition; the excluded state-action issue and independent state-law judgments remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Blessing and Bruneau remain distinct provision-specific right and enforcement inquiries; Owasso's reservation of enforcement is resolved for these nondisclosure provisions. No general rule supported only by the four-Justice lead is elevated into controlling law.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the FERPA-based federal judgment and remand consistently with rejection of that claim, without reopening state action or the independent state-law judgments.

#### Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington — individual immunity and appellate scope

#### Voinovich v. Quilter, 507 U.S. 146 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1618.

**Decided:** March 2, 1993.

##### Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts

**Holding:** Section 2 does not prohibit a State from choosing majority-minority districts merely because no federal violation compels their creation; challengers bear at least the initial burden of showing, under the totality of circumstances, unequal electoral opportunity caused by the challenged arrangement. A federal court may order such districts only when their creation is necessary to remedy an established federal-law violation, but that limit does not become a categorical restriction on state redistricting authority; independently applicable federal constitutional and statutory requirements remain enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II.B, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt the complete proposition; it directly controls the rejection of the lower court's statutory prohibition and burden allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): followed for the challengers' burden and the totality-of-circumstances inquiry; no district form is automatically unlawful under Section 2.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: applied to distinguish the State's primary districting authority from federal judicial remedial authority; its actual majority holdings remain controlling.

##### The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting

**Holding:** Assuming that Section 2 reaches the asserted loss of minority influence districts, the claim fails because challengers did not establish sufficiently cohesive white majority voting usually to defeat the minority's preferred candidates. The Gingles prerequisites apply with attention to the claim's nature: an influence claim would require modification or elimination of the first prerequisite that the minority could constitute a district majority, but the Court need not select that adaptation or decide whether influence claims are cognizable because the independently necessary bloc-voting showing is absent.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts II.A and II.C, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this independently dispositive rejection of the presented Section 2 claim and each stated reservation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): applied to the necessity of majority bloc voting and the causal mechanism of dilution; its reservations about influence claims remain unresolved.
- Growe v. Emison, decision of February 23, 1993: followed for application of the threshold framework to single-member district challenges, with its influence-claim reservation preserved rather than silently decided.

##### The particular finding of intentional discrimination is clearly erroneous

**Holding:** Assuming that the Fifteenth Amendment reaches the asserted vote-dilution claim, the finding that Ohio intentionally injured black voters cannot stand on this record: attention to perceived federal-law requirements and possession of an opponent's speculative documents, without evidence of reliance, do not establish the prohibited purpose the District Court inferred. The Court reverses that finding as clearly erroneous without deciding the Amendment's coverage of dilution or its relationship to race-conscious districting generally.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this record-specific constitutional disposition; none adopts a general immunity for political objectives or racial awareness.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Beer v. United States, 425 U.S. 130 (1976): followed in leaving unresolved whether the Fifteenth Amendment reaches vote-dilution claims; the assumption made here creates no new coverage holding.
- Mobile v. Bolden, 446 U.S. 55 (1980): its plurality and separate writings are background to the alleged intentional-discrimination claim, not treated as a single majority rationale or a new categorical rule governing every race-conscious map.

##### Population deviations above ten percent require a genuine justification inquiry

**Holding:** A state legislative plan whose maximum total population deviation exceeds ten percent establishes a prima facie case requiring the State to justify the disparity; crossing that threshold does not make preservation of county boundaries legally unavailable as a justification. The District Court must determine whether the actual plan reasonably advances that rational state policy, including its substantiated connection to the deviations and consistency of implementation, and whether the resulting disparities nevertheless exceed constitutional limits; the Court does not decide that Ohio has met its burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by the other eight Justices. All nine adopt this standard and the population-only remand; neither the State's success nor a particular remedial map receives a controlling vote.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brown v. Thomson, 462 U.S. 835 (1983): followed for the prima facie threshold, the required connection to a rational state policy, and the separate constitutional limit; departures below ten percent remain generally minor, not categorically immune.
- Mahan v. Howell, 410 U.S. 315 (1973): followed in allowing a substantiated political-subdivision policy to justify departures above ten percent; it does not automatically validate Ohio's particular departures.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State remains subject to independent federal constitutional and statutory limits. Influence-claim cognizability and adaptation of the first Gingles prerequisite, Fifteenth Amendment dilution coverage and the distinct unpresented racial-classification claim remain unresolved. Neither political aims nor racial awareness receives categorical immunity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand only the population-equality claim for substantiated county-preservation justification and independent constitutional limits. The statutory and intent grounds do not return for retrial; no map or election date is ordered.

#### Growe v. Emison, 507 U.S. 25 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1420.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Timely state redistricting includes the State's courts

**Holding:** When a State's legislature or judiciary is timely undertaking redistricting, a federal court must defer its own redistricting action unless the record shows that the State will not produce a lawful plan in time for the election. The federal court may set a reasonable deadline and retain jurisdiction as a safeguard, but may neither obstruct timely state work nor displace it merely because it prefers another plan; completion of every state appeal before the election is not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion on state priority, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt this rule. The separate eight-Justice dismissal direction concerns this completed record, not the general availability of retained jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Scott v. Germano, 381 U.S. 407 (1965): applied to both legislative and congressional redistricting through state courts; its timely-action condition and reserve federal jurisdiction remain.
- Reynolds v. Sims, 377 U.S. 533 (1964): applied for primary state responsibility and the obligation to remedy unconstitutional apportionment; population-equality requirements are unchanged.

##### A superseding state judgment must receive legal effect

**Holding:** Once a state court's final judgment replaces the challenged legislative plan, the federal court must give the replacement legal effect rather than treat it as one optional proposal. A challenge to the old plan becomes moot unless it also reaches the superseding plan; allegations against the old plan alone do not establish a violation in the replacement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion on the live-plan issue, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the stated rule. No party-preclusion rule beyond that necessary legal effect is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Atlantic Coast Line Railroad Co. v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 398 U.S. 281 (1970): applied for respect for state judgments and the federal-state judicial relationship; no enlarged preclusion rule adopted.
- Wise v. Lipscomb, 437 U.S. 535 (1978): applied for the need to evaluate the legally operative replacement before federal remedial displacement.

##### Fragmentation claims require proof of the causal voting conditions

**Holding:** A §2 claim that single-member districts fragment a minority group's potential electoral majority requires proof that the group is sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a majority in a single-member district, is politically cohesive, and faces majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate. Without the required cohesion and majority-bloc evidence, the court may not order a minority district by adding distinct groups' populations; the Court assumes without deciding that a supported coalition claim is permissible and leaves influence-only claims and the population denominator unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's statutory opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; all nine adopt the stated single-member extension and record application. No fractured-opinion or Marks inference is required.

**Current-force modification:** Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District, June 17, 1999, modifies the exclusivity of the district-majority condition for one protected group. The group may instead prove a lawful, geographically compact district in which cohesive voting and dependable crossover support give it actual ability to elect its candidates of choice. The numerical-majority route survives; speculation, isolated electoral success, near-majority arithmetic and influence alone do not establish the alternative. Cohesion, the relevant majority-bloc showing with its special-circumstances qualification, causation, independent totality and constitutional limits remain necessary. The distinct-group coalition and influence-only reservations are not resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): extended from multimember dilution to the single-member fragmentation claim at issue; its causal prerequisites and totality inquiry remain distinct, and its influence-only reservation remains open.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gingles prerequisites are necessary, not sufficient; the totality inquiry remains. No proportional representation, mandatory statistical method, filing-date priority or enlarged party-preclusion rule is adopted. Coalition permissibility is assumed; influence claims and population denominator remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the federal redistricting judgment. Eight Justices—White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—direct dismissal of this action; Stone-Zsela supports reversal but would allow conditional retention. That case-specific mandate does not abolish the unanimous general rule permitting reserve jurisdiction.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District, lower docket 98-50227, June 17, 1999: first-condition functional ability-to-elect alternative for a single protected group.

#### Shaw v. Reno, 509 U.S. 630 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-357.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993, No. 92-357, see Federal Courts — Review of federal preclearance determinations.

##### Territorial racial design alone does not state the asserted deprivation

**Holding:** Alleged deliberate or predominant consideration of race in drawing territorial constituencies, even with departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not by itself state this equal-protection claim where no racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or other independently cognizable constitutional deprivation is alleged. This is a substantive disposition of the design-only theory, not heightened pleading, a universal requirement of additional downstream loss, or an added constitutional-injury prerequisite for an independent statutory claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary notice pleading applies; no expert proof or replacement map is required at pleading. Independent exclusion, dilution, unequal participation, civic-status and statutory claims retain their own elements. No statewide racial-strength defense, general replacement of Croson, predominance test or added constitutional-injury gate for §2 is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the design-only state claim (5–4); no injunction, new map or trial on that theory. Other properly presented claims and any properly sought amendment remain subject to ordinary procedure and preclusion. Leave federal-official dismissal undisturbed (9–0) on its separate channel and nonreviewability grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United Jewish Organizations v. Carey, 430 U.S. 144: its fractured reasoning supports a limited distinction, not a comprehensive controlling test or a statewide-white-representation defense.
- Wright v. Rockefeller, 376 U.S. 52: distinguished; racial purpose was not established there, so it did not decide the present assumed-purpose question.
- City of Richmond v. Croson, 488 U.S. 469, and Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1: personal equality retained; the Court declines to equate territorial design alone with Croson’s racial allocation of opportunities.
- Anderson v. Martin, 375 U.S. 399: official racial ballot labeling remains prohibited without proof that an election winner changed.
- Gomillion v. Lightfoot, 364 U.S. 339: racial municipal disenfranchisement remains prohibited; Whittaker’s broader concurrence is not adopted as a rule covering every racial district adjustment.
- White v. Regester, 412 U.S. 755, and McLaurin v. Oklahoma State Regents, 339 U.S. 637: impaired participation may be unconstitutional despite formal access; no universal downstream-loss requirement.
- Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41; Leatherman, March 3, 1993: ordinary notice pleading retained, no demand for expert proof or a replacement map.

#### Johnson v. De Grandy, 512 U.S. 997

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 92-519, 92-593 and 92-767.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Johnson v. De Grandy, No. 92-519; De Grandy v. Johnson, No. 92-593; United States v. Florida, No. 92-767. Holder v. Hall, decided June 30, 1994, precedes this decision in the expressly coordinated same-day sequence.

For the other questions in Johnson v. De Grandy, 1994-06-30, Nos. 92-519, 92-593 and 92-767, see Federal Courts — The state validation does not preclude the reserved factual claims; Federal Courts — The United States brings an original enforcement suit.

##### Necessary voting conditions do not require maximization

**Holding:** This Section 2 fragmentation claim requires a minority sufficiently large and geographically compact to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate; the claimant must also establish unequal opportunity to participate and elect chosen representatives under the totality of circumstances, without proving discriminatory purpose. Even when those necessary conditions are satisfied, Section 2 does not require every feasible additional majority-minority district; the disputed first condition is assumed here, leaving citizenship, population-denominator and coalition or influence-claim questions unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg; seven Justices directly adopt the complete necessary-versus-sufficient distinction and rejection of maximization. Thomas and Scalia's statutory noncoverage position is a different rationale and supplies no join.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows its causal conditions, special-circumstances qualification and independent totality inquiry; the conditions do not establish liability by themselves.
- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: preserves local cohesion and majority-bloc proof for single-member fragmentation and the unresolved coalition, influence and population questions.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: follows the challengers' burden and the distinction between a State's authority to choose districts and a court's power to remedy a proved violation.
- Holder v. Hall, June 30, 1994: applies its separation of coverage, comparison, proof and remedy; a permissible comparison creates no maximization command.
- Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993: preserves the independent statutory elements; its constitutional injury rule adds no further element to Section 2.

##### Proportional opportunity is relevant evidence, not a safe harbor

**Holding:** The relationship between a group's share of the relevant population and the share of districts in which it has an effective opportunity to elect its preferred candidates is relevant evidence within Section 2's totality inquiry, whose weight depends on the other evidence; it is neither a conclusive safe harbor nor a burden-shifting affirmative defense, and disproportionality alone likewise does not establish liability. The inquiry concerns demonstrated electoral opportunity, not nominal district labels or the race of officeholders; it guarantees no proportional election, racial floor or ceiling, statewide offset, or universal geographic or population denominator, and cannot excuse proved local packing, fragmentation or another barrier merely because opportunities exist elsewhere.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this contextual rule and its explanation. Kennedy separately accepts the exact limited propositions that proportionality is relevant, that the necessary conditions alone do not establish liability, and that proportionality supplies no safe harbor; his concurrence does not join Part III's full rationale or Parts IV and V's affirmative applications. No combined rationale under Marks is needed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows its contextual inquiry and treatment of electoral success; adopts no conclusive proportional-success presumption.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: preserves the need for proof of actual unequal opportunity rather than automatic condemnation or approval of a district form.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: preserves independent totality proof after an adequate comparison; its local institutional comparison supplies neither a quota nor an evidentiary safe harbor.

##### The House liability and dependent remedy cannot stand on this record

**Holding:** Assuming the disputed first Gingles condition in the challengers' favor and retaining the supported findings of discrimination and polarized voting, the tried House record does not establish unequal opportunity under the totality beyond the erroneous inference that every feasible additional Hispanic district must be created. The liability judgment and dependent substitution of Modified De Grandy Plan 268 are reversed; no new liability trial, Supreme Court replacement map or numerical district minimum is ordered, and implementation must follow lawful election administration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this full contextual application. Kennedy supports reversal and the mandate under Parts II and VI on the narrower rejection of maximization, without joining Part IV; the House disposition therefore has seven votes, against Thomas and Scalia's vacatur-and-dismissal alternative.

**Material application:** The Court considers the distinct eighteen- and twenty-district House frames, actual effective opportunities, accepted discrimination and polarization evidence, and testimony about neighborhood splitting and concentration. No finding of inconsistent treatment of comparable minority and other neighborhoods is made or imposed as a new liability element. Supported findings retain Rule 52(a) protection; the legal error lies in making feasible additional districts obligatory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows the totality standard and Rule 52(a)'s protection of supported findings; corrects a legally infected liability determination without creating a ratio presumption or new intent requirement.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: applies the need for an established federal violation before judicial imposition of remedial districts.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: distinguishes a sufficient comparison from the separate proof of unequal opportunity; its established liability and remedy remand do not supply missing liability here.

##### The Senate judgment stands because no violation was proved

**Holding:** On the tried Senate record, the asserted unequal opportunity is not established under the totality merely because an additional Hispanic district and a different additional Black district are each feasible; the judgment leaving the State's Senate plan operative is affirmed for lack of a proved violation, not because a proved violation may remain when proposed remedies conflict. The existing Black electoral opportunity with crossover support is relevant evidence, not a second Black-majority district, proof of general cohesion between Hispanic and Black voters, or a legal entitlement to a crossover district.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part V, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt this full contextual application. Kennedy supports affirmance under Parts II and VI on the narrower no-maximization ground without joining Part V; the Senate disposition has seven votes, against Thomas and Scalia's vacatur-and-dismissal alternative.

**Material application:** The operative July 2 Senate judgment found no violation; the July 17 explanation's competing-remedies account cannot excuse a proved wrong. The seven-district, five-wholly-Dade and broader South Florida frames remain distinct, and cohesion within each group does not establish a combined Hispanic–Black coalition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: applies contextual unequal-opportunity proof without inferring liability from separately feasible district increases.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves the need for proved common cohesion before distinct groups can be treated as one electoral coalition.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: preserves the unresolved influence-claim question and ties compelled relief to an established violation.
- Holder, June 30, 1994: preserves the obligation to formulate lawful relief for an established wrong; no competing-remedies exception to liability or relief is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The House liability judgment and dependent Modified De Grandy Plan 268 remedy are reversed in No. 92-519; the Senate judgment is affirmed in Nos. 92-593 and 92-767, each 7–2. #### Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District

**Docket or dockets:** lower docket 98-50227.
**Decided:** 1999-06-17.

##### The first condition includes a proved functional ability-to-elect route

**Holding:** For a claim by one protected group, the first Gingles condition may be met either through the retained district-majority route or through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would provide the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority population arithmetic or mere influence does not suffice; the relevant majority-bloc showing, causal connection and independent totality remain necessary.

##### The population figures do not resolve functional opportunity

**Holding:** The accepted 47.9% and 48.3% Hispanic citizen voting-age shares neither prove nor foreclose dependable ability to elect. **Proposition-level authority:** The same five Justices join Stone-Zsela's Part I and this application and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The proposed district must remain lawful in population and geography; the 8.1% underpopulation is not approved. ##### The independent demographic and disclosure rulings stand

**Holding:** The credited proof of concrete post-census housing, occupancy and growth changes supports the present demographic findings under clear-error review. Excluding the survey disclosed on the eve of trial for surprise under the applicable disclosure rule was within the trial court's discretion; changing the capacity rule does not itself make either independent ruling erroneous.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia and Thomas do not join it unconditionally.

- Thornburg v. Gingles: expressly modifies numerical exclusivity in the first condition for the defined single-group functional route; preserves compactness, cohesion, relevant majority-bloc proof, causation and independent totality.
- Growe v. Emison: modifies the same exclusivity while preserving its causal framework and the distinct-group reservation.
- Voinovich v. Quilter: preserves the claimant's burden, relevant bloc-voting requirement and distinction between lawful state districting choice and a court-compelled remedy; the present rule resolves only the defined functional-capacity question.

A majority-population route remains available, but neither route establishes every other condition or liability. Cohesion, relevant bloc-voting causation with applicable special circumstances, totality, independent constitutional limits and ordinary evidence procedures retain their force. Vacatur concerns the categorical sub-fifty bar. The lower courts must assess the functional route on the preserved admissible record and any supplementation independently permitted under ordinary procedure. They must then consider the remaining statutory conditions and totality if necessary. No liability, district, election system, new trial or automatic survey admission follows. The claim may still fail.

#### Martinez v. Bush

**Docket or dockets:** Lower-court Nos. 02-20244-CIV-JORDAN and 02-10028-CIV-JORDAN; review of 234 F. Supp. 2d 1275 (S.D. Fla. 2002); no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** 2004-04-29.

##### The intentional-racial claims fail on supported findings

**Holding:** A claimant alleging intentional racial exclusion through redistricting procedures or plans under equal protection or Title VI § 601 must prove by a preponderance that racial discrimination was a motivating factor. Supported findings receive clear-error review; the trial court's findings sustain judgment for the defendants on the preserved intentional claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer and controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Title VI claim is the preserved §601 intentional-discrimination action. The bounded direct prospective route for an ongoing violation of a valid applicable §602 effects regulation is not presented; Powell, Sandoval and South Camden preserve that distinction, reject a regulation-created effects right under §1983 and retain agency enforcement. The supported absence of racial purpose does not dispose of partisan injury.

**Material application:** The trial court considered access, notice, hearings, participation and the plan's purposes. Its findings of partisan advantage without a purpose to injure Black or Hispanic opportunity were not clearly erroneous.

##### Section 2 requires demonstrated local electoral opportunity

**Holding:** Section 2 does not require replacement of districts affording reliably demonstrated equal opportunity to elect protected voters' preferred candidates merely to increase racial percentages or seat totals. Plaintiffs bear the preponderance burden on unequal opportunity under the applicable voting predicates and complete circumstances; proportionality is relevant evidence, not a conclusive defense, and opportunity elsewhere cannot offset a proved local barrier.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven rationale votes. Scalia and Thomas join only the statutory judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gingles capacity, cohesion and ordinary opposing-bloc defeat remain distinct from the independent totality inquiry, with unopposed races and incumbency considered as possible special circumstances. Valdespino permits one protected group to show capacity through rigorous proof of dependable crossover in a lawful compact district without an absolute population-majority gate; other predicates remain required. Coalition and influence-only theories are not decided. Population, electorate share, candidate vote share and probability of victory are different quantities; no projected 50-percent line establishes opportunity. Section 2 does not acquire Vieth's purpose requirement or partisan causes the racial statutory prerequisites.

##### The challenged congressional districts retain supported opportunity

**Holding:** The supported findings concerning CDs 3, 17 and 23 sustain rejection of the presented Section 2 claims. The assessment concerns dependable opportunity under their actual electoral conditions, including the conditional primary structure, rather than a racial headcount or a guarantee of every future result.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–IV of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven rationale votes. Scalia and Thomas join only the statutory judgment.

**Material application:** CDs 3 and 17 had supported expected performance and no evidenced lawful alternative materially improving it. CD 23 had separate local evidence supporting opportunity, including an open-seat finding. The forecast of primary closure rested on actual election history and write-in qualification, not a guaranteed filing; coalescence and withdrawal remained evidence-based predictions.

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

Supported curtailment of an identified person's local recreational or aesthetic use because of reasonable concern about ongoing discharges can establish personal injury without separate proof of environmental damage. An unexplained subjective fear or interest in statutory compliance alone is insufficient; a permit violation does not injure everyone downstream.

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

A state opinion need not cite this Court’s decisions. Courts examine its actual rule, reasoning and result in the complete constitutional context. Occasional shorthand does not impose a different prejudice burden when the whole opinion states and uses the reasonable-probability standard. Conversely, correct terminology or recital of the evidence does not shield an objectively unreasonable assessment of the supported record.

### Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

**Governing question:** When an asserted statutory obligation creates an individual right enforceable through §1983.

**Current rule:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding obligation and defining the individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than vaguely or amorphously. Text, statutory setting and the asserted entitlement govern; a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish a right. An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable under §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Right creation and remedial foreclosure remain separate inquiries. Title IX’s individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from §1983 enforcement by its administrative funding process or established implied action. The claimant must prove the actual statutory violation, including defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle enlarges no duty. Title IX also does not displace a §1983 Equal Protection claim independently secured by the Constitution merely because the same school harassment supplies both theories; purposeful constitutional discrimination, state action, defendant-specific or municipal responsibility, causation, remedies and actual preclusion remain separate.

### Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations

**Governing question:** Whether private plaintiffs may enforce the additional disparate-impact prohibitions of Title VI section 602 regulations through a directly implied action or through section 1983.

**Current rule:** A person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may bring a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued Title VI section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects. The action rests on Title VI's particular statutory context, not on the regulation's own force, and does not mean every federal regulation creates a private cause of action. It reaches every valid Title VI section 602 effects regulation within that bounded prospective scope; neither a different agency's regulation, state program or permit nor a finding rather than an allegation of prohibited effects distinguishes it. The plaintiff must establish (1) an applicable section 602 regulation validly issued under the authority Congress conferred, consistent with Title VI's statutory limits; (2) a defendant subject to the regulation through the legally required federal-assistance relationship; (3) a concrete exclusion, denial of benefits or other material discriminatory effect within the regulation's actual terms, attributable to an identified recipient policy or method of administration; (4) an actual or imminent continuing effect that the requested prospective relief can meaningfully correct; and (5) an appropriate defendant and a remedy compatible with applicable immunity, statutory and equitable limitations. The violation is proved on the regulation's own terms, without the intentional discrimination section 601 itself requires. Disproportionate statistics may support proof, but a numerical racial difference alone does not conclusively establish a violation; the claimant must connect the disparity to the challenged administration and the regulation's operative prohibition, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and regulatory coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. No universal Title VII-style business-necessity framework applies, and no prior agency finding is required. Relief is confined to a declaration of an established violation, an injunction requiring the recipient to end or correct the prohibited method, a reasonable compliance period where consistent with preventing ongoing injury, and ordinary equitable supervision proportionate to the violation.

Separately, Powell's section 1983 holding remains operative: section 601's intentional-discrimination protection and section 602's agency directions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted through section 1983. A regulation can elaborate a statutory entitlement but cannot itself supply a missing broader statutory right. That holding rests on the absence of a right, not on congressional foreclosure of an established one.

Section 601's established private action for intentional discrimination remains independently available on its own proof and procedural footing. Discriminatory purpose must be proved; disparate effects may be evidence relevant to purpose, but they do not alone establish intentional discrimination. A substantial federal question supports jurisdiction independently of the merits, and prospective relief on either the direct effects claim or the section 601 claim may issue against a proper state officer only upon an established violation and the officer's actual connection to it. Federal agencies retain the enforcement Congress authorized; section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination procedures govern the agencies' own powers and are not the exclusive route to prospective compliance.

### Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations

**Governing question:** The effect of an evidentially unsupported but legally available ground when a general decision does not identify which ground was used.

**Current rule:** A general multiple-object federal conspiracy verdict need not be set aside merely because one lawful object lacks sufficient evidence if another is sufficiently proved under the unchanged beyond-a-reasonable-doubt standard. Factual insufficiency is distinct from legal invalidity; this rule does not preserve a verdict resting on a legally forbidden theory. In the separate Florida advisory-capital-jury setting, submission of a legally available but evidentially unsupported aggravator does not alone establish reliance when the general recommendation identifies no aggravator findings. That inference boundary neither finds actual rejection of the factor nor resolves a legally invalid instruction. Express judicial reliance remains distinct and subject to capital-weighing correction.

**Authority by component:**

- General multiple-object conspiracy verdict and factual/legal distinction: Griffin v. United States, “General verdict containing a factually unsupported lawful object”, December 3, 1991; Scalia, Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- Limited extension to an unparticularized advisory capital recommendation: Sochor v. Florida, “The general jury recommendation does not establish coldness reliance”, June 8, 1992; Souter's Part III-A joined by White, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Sochor*, June 8, 1992, for the advisory-recommendation component; *Griffin*, December 3, 1991, remains the operative authority for the conspiracy-verdict component.

**Exceptions and limits:** Failure of proof on every route, an element-diluting instruction, an independently presented unanimity defect and broader capital-jury error rules are not resolved. No fact-specific jury finding may be supplied from a general recommendation.

**Related current rules:** “Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error” governs constitutionally defective weighing and the influence of an inadequately narrowed jury instruction; the factual-insufficiency rule does not displace it.

**Holdings navigation:** Griffin v. United States, 502 U.S. 46 (1991) — General verdicts; Sochor v. Florida, 504 U.S. 527 (1992) — Capital sentencing.

### Invalid aggravators in capital weighing and correction of sentencing error

**Governing question:** Constitutionally invalid aggravation in a weighing State, whether used directly by the judge or transmitted through a jury recommendation carrying legally required great weight.

**Current rule:** Remaining valid aggravators do not alone cure a constitutional weighing error, although an invalid weighing factor does not automatically negate death eligibility if valid eligibility findings remain. The state judicial system must provide genuine reweighing without the invalid factor where state law authorizes it, or constitutional harmless-error review proving beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not affect the sentence. Bare factor counting, proportionality review or a harmless-error label supplies neither cure; no particular verbal formula is indispensable if the substantive finding is actually made. A jury's inadequately narrowed aggravating instruction can infect the final death sentence when the judge must give the resulting recommendation great weight, even if the judge directly finds only valid aggravators. Correction must address that transmitted influence. If no lawful cure is available, a new sentencing proceeding is required. Neither automatic life imprisonment nor disturbance of guilt follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Distinct weighing error and eligibility, with lawful correction routes: Stringer v. Black, “A weighing error needs a constitutionally adequate cure”, March 9, 1992; Kennedy for Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and O'Connor.
- Actual judicial reliance, substantive cure and no magic-words requirement: Sochor v. Florida, “The judge's documented coldness reliance requires correction” and “The existing appellate explanation does not cure the error”, June 8, 1992; respectively the eight-Justice Part III-B-1 and six-Justice Parts III-B-2/IV, applying Stringer, Clemons, Parker and Chapman without changing their correction standard.

**Present operation effective:** *Espinosa*, June 29, 1992, extending the rule to the recommendation's legally required influence. The earlier direct-weighing rule remains supplied by *Stringer*, March 9, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** On the harmless-error route the State bears Chapman's beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. That requirement is distinct from authority to perform a genuine reweighing and from collateral-review gateways.

**Exceptions and limits:** States retain latitude in assigning sentencing roles, but must give constitutional guidance to an influential recommendation. No factor-specific juror finding is presumed, and constitutional permission for a kind of appellate cure does not establish that state law authorizes it. **Related current rules:** “Factually unsupported lawful grounds in general verdicts and capital recommendations” preserves the distinct inference boundary for a legally available but evidentially unsupported jury factor. “Punishment-eligibility innocence as a gateway to barred habeas claims” governs access to review of otherwise barred claims, not correction of an available claim's weighing error.

### Effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern effective capital consideration of independent culpability mitigation?

**Current rule:** When record-supported mitigation has material culpability significance that the authorized sentencing answers cannot express, a reasonable likelihood that the charge, evidence and trial as a whole prevent that use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. Admission and partial relevance to future danger are insufficient if a truthful finding of deliberateness and danger prevents giving effect to youth's independent mitigating force. Disturbed upbringing need not involve a uniquely severe permanent handicap or lack any relation to future danger. The sentencer need not give favorable weight, and mere possibility of a restrictive reading is insufficient. Jurek's conditional approval, Lockett and Eddings dictated this functional requirement before Graham's September 10, 1984 finality; no new Teague exception follows. Adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence can provide correction without disturbing guilt. No general positive-character rule follows from Graham's separate fractured component.

When a capital jury asks whether proof of an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response considered with the complete charge and trial must resolve a reasonable likelihood that supported mitigation cannot be given effect in choosing life. Referring to an existing instruction may suffice if it unambiguously answers the actual difficulty; no particular words or universal rephrasing duty are required. Evidence, advocacy, further deliberation or a recital that mitigation was considered does not conclusively cure an otherwise unresolved restriction on mitigating use.

A general permission to answer a special issue negatively for mitigation is insufficient where the required verdict form still makes that answer certify factual reasonable doubt the juror may not possess. The complete instructions and trial context must provide a coherent, authorized life verdict for supported culpability mitigation outside the factual issues. Earlier explanations and counsel’s advocacy are considered but do not conclusively repair an incompatible final factual certification.

For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind, the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to death. If it cannot, conditional sentence relief must permit a reasonable time for constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence, leaving guilt intact. The defect is not structural and does not itself establish actual contribution. This effect inquiry applies Brecht's general rule for preserved constitutional trial error on federal habeas; it supplies no new rule at an earlier state-merits cutoff.

### Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** Harmlessness for preserved constitutional trial error properly available on federal habeas, including preserved Doyle error, the preserved personal-intent instructional claims and the capital-selection commutation-misinformation claim.

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. When a federal habeas court properly reaches a preserved constitutional trial-error claim, including preserved Doyle error, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the verdict or sentence, considering the whole record, unless another controlling rule treats the defect as structural or the right carries its own prejudice element. The reviewing court examines the actual verdict or sentence and may not substitute the conclusion that a properly instructed hypothetical jury could have convicted. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure. O’Neal applied the same State-borne Chapman inquiry to a preserved constitutional trial violation in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor’s statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant’s own intent required by the offense. Kotteakos’s substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt inquiry does not govern that claim. If an available violation is established and the State fails to prove that it did not contribute to the verdict, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected conviction; identifying the wrong review standard alone does not establish a violation or command a writ. Roy applied the same rule to omission of the defendant’s required aiding purpose without an accompanying prosecutorial statement. Such omission is trial error, not automatically structural error. Necessary jury findings embracing the omitted purpose are sufficient but not the exclusive route to harmlessness: supported whole-record inferences may show no contribution to the actual verdict. The reviewing court must ask whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting that inference. If the omission prevented reliable consideration of the personal-purpose question, strong evidence alone is insufficient. Knowledge plus assistance, general mental capacity, or evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction cannot substitute for the required actual-verdict inquiry.

Calderon v. Coleman applied the same State-borne Chapman rule to a properly available constitutional trial error in a commutation instruction affecting a capital jury’s choice between death and life without parole: the State must show beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. Consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests, rather than what a hypothetical properly instructed jury could have imposed. A corrective direction or strong aggravation is not independently conclusive. Boyde’s error-identification inquiry does not itself establish the effect required for relief. The actual reasoning must perform that causal inquiry under the governing burden, but no prescribed words, citation or separate heading is indispensable. An available violation failing the State’s showing requires conditional sentence relief allowing a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a method error alone does not establish entitlement to a writ.

### Facts changing binding punishment ranges

**Governing question:** Which present-offense and recidivist facts must receive constitutional element protections.

**Current rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the jury. A lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder but retains that proof standard. This rule reaches mandatory Guidelines constraints below a larger outer statutory maximum: an enforceable inner range is not discretionary merely because a higher sentence is statutorily possible. Judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. A sentencing label, ordinary sentencing admission, notice of exposure or unused aggregate maxima on other counts supplies neither the missing element nor an inevitable lawful aggregate.

In a federal prosecution the covered aggravating fact also receives applicable grand-jury charging protection. A lawful jury waiver does not enlarge the charge or convert ordinary sentencing findings into element adjudication.

A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available likewise receives jury and reasonable-doubt protection in a jury-tried prosecution, subject to valid element admission and existing lawful waiver requirements. Eligibility is distinct from mitigation, weighing and ultimate selection among already authorized punishments.

Necessary recidivist facts receive element protection as well: existence, identity, and historical qualification or chronology that increases the authorized maximum or mandatory minimum must receive the applicable federal aggravated indictment allegation and proof beyond a reasonable doubt to a unanimous jury, unless lawfully admitted under valid charging and waiver procedures. A lawful jury waiver retains the proof burden and charge boundary. A nonjury juvenile adjudication needed to raise an adult maximum, including a binding grid ceiling, is not categorically exempt; a waiver confined to the current act or an ordinary sentencing stipulation does not admit the aggravating element. Judges retain statutory interpretation, legal classification of established facts and discretion within independently authorized ranges.

For necessary §3559(c) robbery predicates, the prosecution must establish qualifying status through that element adjudication. The exclusion requires both no firearm or dangerous-weapon use or threatened use and no resulting death or serious bodily injury; the prosecution may defeat it by establishing the applicable alternative of weapon use, threatened use, death or serious injury. It may not shift uncertainty about necessary qualifying facts to a defendant’s clear-and-convincing burden. A prior judgment proves what it actually adjudicated, not every disputed historical qualification.

A preserved omission of the indispensable federal aggravated allegation, without a valid charging waiver, prevents enhanced punishment despite sentencing notice or judicial findings. Sentencing begins with ranges independently authorized by the charges and verdicts. Any new enhancement proceeding requires its own charging and procedural authority; remand grants none. Forfeited claims retain every Rule 52(b) requirement. Section 5G1.2(d) cannot make an identical aggregate inevitable by relying on a mandatory total-punishment range that itself depends on quantity lacking the required element adjudication.

### Execution of persons with intellectual disability

**Governing question:** Whether established intellectual disability removes execution as a permissible punishment and how that status must be determined.

**Current rule:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit execution of a person with established intellectual disability: significantly impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations and developmental onset. ### Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims?

**Current rule:** A §2 single-member fragmentation claim requires the district-capacity condition described below, political cohesion and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the group’s preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate. These are necessary, not alone sufficient; totality remains. Distinct groups' numbers cannot replace missing cohesion or bloc-voting proof. Coalition cognizability and the population denominator remain open. A State need not prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts; challengers initially must show unequal opportunity caused by the arrangement under the totality, while a federal court may order such districts only as necessary to remedy an established violation. Independent constitutional limits remain. Assuming an influence-only claim exists, majority bloc voting remains necessary; influence-only cognizability remains unresolved. The necessary voting conditions do not require maximization of majority-minority districts. Unequal electoral opportunity under the totality does not require discriminatory purpose. Relevant proportionality compares the group’s share of the relevant population with the share of districts in which it has an effective opportunity to elect preferred candidates; its weight depends on the other evidence. It is neither a conclusive safe harbor nor a burden-shifting affirmative defense, and disproportionality alone does not establish liability. Nominal district labels and officeholders’ race do not replace demonstrated opportunity. No proportional-election entitlement, racial floor or ceiling, universal geographic scale or population denominator follows. Opportunity elsewhere cannot excuse proved local packing, fragmentation or another electoral barrier. For one protected group, the first condition may also be met through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would give the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Speculation, isolated favorable elections, near-majority arithmetic and mere influence do not suffice. Plaintiffs must prove actual voting behavior and cohesion within that proposed district and the relevant majority-bloc defeat and causal connection in the challenged arrangement; success in one setting neither negates nor establishes defeat in another. Both the retained district-majority route and this functional route remain subject to lawful population, geography, special-circumstance qualifications, independent totality and constitutional limits.

### Justification of state legislative population deviations

**Governing question:** What instructions govern justification of state legislative population deviations?

**Current rule:** A maximum total deviation exceeding ten percent establishes a prima facie case requiring the State to justify its legislative plan. County preservation remains a possible rational policy, not automatic justification. Courts must assess whether the plan reasonably advances it, with substantiated connection to the deviations and consistency of implementation, and whether resulting disparities nevertheless exceed constitutional limits. Crossing ten percent supplies neither automatic invalidity nor approval of the offered policy.

A maximum total deviation below ten percent does not itself establish a prima facie violation, but a challenger may prove arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. When the asserted constitutional wrong depends on prohibited purpose, the claimant need only establish that purpose as a substantial or motivating factor; the government must then prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

### Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury

**Governing question:** What instructions govern territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury?

**Current rule:** Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in territorial district design, including departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not alone state the design-only equal-protection claim without racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or another independently cognizable constitutional deprivation. This substantive boundary is not heightened pleading, a universal downstream-loss requirement or an additional injury element for independent statutory claims. Other claims retain their own elements; no predominance scrutiny rule or general replacement for Adarand's common standard follows. This remains so when race actually predominates and traditional criteria are subordinated. A cognizable status or participation injury can be complete in government’s own unequal treatment without a second governmental act, monetary loss or later electoral defeat; shared-electorate composition alone does not establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race. Absence of a statutory command or prior adjudication requiring a particular district does not itself prohibit otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions. Shape is evidence, not a necessary gateway or sufficient proof. Purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review; their legal consequences remain separate. An intentional dilution or participation claim requires the actual impairment protected by that claim and a causal connection to the challenged action; proof of racial purpose alone does not establish either. ### Partisan districting: equal electoral treatment, comparison, proof and remedy

**Governing question:** When do electoral boundaries unconstitutionally impair voters' political capacity because of their political affiliation or activity?

**Current rule:** The Equal Protection Clause prohibits district assignments selected to impose a material electoral disadvantage on identified voters because of political affiliation or activity. The claimant must prove: (1) an identified, existing or demonstrably attainable practical capacity to organize, participate, nominate or elect; (2) a concrete, practically significant impairment of that capacity; (3) affiliation-based disadvantage as a substantial motivating purpose, neither necessarily exclusive nor predominant; and (4) the boundaries' material contribution to the impairment. Materiality is a demonstrated, nontrivial reduction in effective ability to organize, nominate or elect under ordinary electoral conditions; proof and severity are distinct, and a predicted vote-share change without reliable evidence of practical consequence does not independently establish it. Political design, awareness, partisan benefit, preferred constituents and unfavorable outcomes alone are insufficient. Political awareness and independently legitimate representative choices remain permissible; ordinary partisan advantage, incumbent protection and bipartisan agreement do not independently justify the defined discrimination. Total exclusion, inevitable defeat, statewide entrenchment, a lost election and a fixed numerical threshold are unnecessary.

The claimant bears persuasion by a preponderance on each element using an independently justified lawful comparison. Specify legal constraints and genuine permissible priorities; examine pretext; establish a feasible alternative, including consequential neighboring changes, when the claim depends on different boundaries. No full statewide map, number of alternatives, statistical ensemble or named expert method is invariably required. Evidence must explain the practical effect under actual electoral conditions and address material assumptions and uncertainty. A better map alone proves neither discrimination nor entitlement. The initial causal showing concerns the boundaries' contribution to injury and does not require the claimant to disprove every lawful justification.

After that showing, the State bears persuasion by a preponderance that materially the same lawful decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose. Actual independent reasons may satisfy the defense; conceivable explanations and repetition of the prohibited objective may not. A proved violation cannot be balanced away by partisan benefits. The comparison establishes capacity, impairment and causation, not an automatic right to its map. Personal standing and ordinary preservation rules apply. After liability, the State receives the first opportunity to cure; judicial relief must fully correct the injury, comply with independent requirements and extend no further than necessary.
