## Standards and Tests selection: Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

### Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

**Governing question:** What instructions govern age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness?

**Current rule:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate and have a determinative influence on the employment decision. Service-based pension interference alone does not establish it; deliberate use of pension status as an age proxy or mixed age and pension motivation may. Age-triggered vesting and disparate impact remain unresolved. A disparate-treatment violation is willful if the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether conduct violated the Act, for individual decisions as well as formal policies. Once shown, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive. A good-faith nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willful. Within the assumed McDonnell Douglas framework, the initial showing must support an inference of age discrimination; replacement by someone at least forty does not defeat it. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference, whereas an insignificant difference standing alone does not. No numerical threshold or indispensable replacement element is imposed when other evidence supplies the inference. Employer production of a lawful reason remains distinct from the employee’s ultimate persuasion burden; rejection of the explanation does not compel liability.

The ADEA protects against disadvantage because of advancing age. Favoring older workers over younger workers does not violate that prohibition merely because both groups fall within the protected class aged forty or older. Coverage of the asserted disadvantage must be established before the statute’s proof and remedial rules are invoked; the relative-age inference does not make the statutory protection bilateral.

- Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- O’Connor — Parts I–II, replacement status and relative-age inference, 1996-04-01; all nine support protected-group nonexclusion, the positive relative-age proposition and retained burdens; the eight Associates alone adopt insignificant-gap-alone insufficiency.

- Directional statutory coverage: General Dynamics Land Systems v. Cline, February 24, 2004; Breyer with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg [Canonical Decision Record](../terms/OT2003/records/general_dynamics_land_systems_v_cline_merits_2004-02-24.md#holdings).

**Present operation effective:** Cline, February 24, 2004, for directional coverage; O’Connor, April 1, 1996, for the replacement inference; Hazen Paper’s April 20, 1993 causation and willfulness components remain.

**Holdings navigation:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993); O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308.

**Open question:** Wholesale application of McDonnell Douglas to every ADEA claim, alternative reduction-in-force formulations, particular small-gap records and the force of additional evidence remain unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: One-year state-custody habeas filing period

### One-year state-custody habeas filing period

**Governing question:** The limitation period under amended §2244(d).

**Current rule:** A person in custody pursuant to a state-court judgment has one year to apply for habeas relief, running from the latest of: finality by completion of direct review or expiration of time to seek it; removal of a State-created filing impediment violating federal law that actually prevented filing; initial Supreme Court recognition of the asserted constitutional right, if newly recognized and made retroactively applicable on collateral review; or when due diligence could have discovered the claim’s factual predicate. Time during which a properly filed application for state postconviction or other collateral review of the pertinent judgment or claim is pending is excluded.

Proper filing concerns compliance with requirements for delivery and acceptance of the application, rather than the success or relief eligibility of its individual claims. Claim-relief bars therefore do not invalidate an otherwise proper filing. Clerk acceptance cannot cure a genuine filing defect. Form, deadlines, the proper receiving court or office, fees and filing preconditions must be distinguished from rules requiring denial of relief on a claim.

The state-proceeding limitation applies to both postconviction review and other collateral review. Pendency of a federal habeas application is not excluded under section 2244(d)(2), even if that application was timely and dismissed without prejudice for unestablished exhaustion. Accompanying civil-rights allegations do not convert it into state review. A later application's non-successive status and an earlier application's timely filing do not themselves make the later application timely; proper filing, actual pendency and relation to the pertinent judgment or claim remain independent conditions.

A properly filed state collateral application remains pending through timely continuation of the ordinary state-review process, including qualifying intervals before filing at the next available level. Successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function. Completion, abandonment, an unreasonable interval or an unrelated later attack does not receive exclusion merely because another petition is possible. A state merits discussion does not override an independent valid untimeliness determination. Where the order is ambiguous, the federal court must determine its actual meaning, the applicable state timing rule, the supported explanation for the interval and elapsed time; lawful clarification may be used but is not invariably required. No national fixed deadline or automatic reasonableness presumption applies.

- State-only collateral pendency, independent timing and preservation of actual alternative grounds: Canonical Decision Record `Duncan_v_Walker_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / 1. Federal habeas pendency is outside section 2244(d)(2); 2. The statutory error requires reversal, with actual independent alternatives preserved, 2001-06-18; seven direct joins to O’Connor’s opinion.

- Canonical Decision Record `carey-v-saffold_merits_2002-06-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Timely ordinary state review includes its intervals; State timeliness and the actual federal calculation remain necessary; June 17, 2002; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six direct joins).

## Standards and Tests selection: Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254

### Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254

**Governing question:** State-remedy exhaustion and express waiver in §2254(b).

**Current rule:** Relief for a person in custody under a state judgment may not be granted unless state remedies have been exhausted, no available state corrective process exists, or circumstances render that process ineffective to protect the applicant’s rights. The court may deny on the merits despite nonexhaustion; it is not required to do so. A State is neither deemed to waive exhaustion nor estopped from relying on it unless its counsel expressly waives it.

**Authority by component:**

- Exhaustion alternatives, permissive merits denial and express counsel waiver: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(1), 28 U.S.C. §2254(b), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** AEDPA enactment, April 24, 1996; Lindh, June 23, 1997, for the chapter 153 amendments at issue in preenactment noncapital §2254 applications. The separate §2255 and capital questions are not thereby decided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Court-directed mixed-petition elections and loss of timely review

### Court-directed mixed-petition elections and loss of timely review

**Governing question:** What protections apply when a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner to choose how to dispose of a timely mixed habeas petition?

**Current rule:** When the court directs a choice between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissal to exhaust the others, and its materials disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will prevent timely return, it must neutrally explain that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the original filing date and that a later petition may be time barred. It must give a meaningful opportunity to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims under their ordinary requirements. The trigger is the court-directed election and apparent filing risk, not pro se status or complexity alone. The duty requires neither investigation of missing materials, exact deadline computation, claim evaluation, strategic advice nor a promised stay, and states a minimum rather than an exhaustive judicial duty.

Under §2244(d), failure to give required notice or another materially misleading judicial direction may support equitable tolling when it causes loss of an otherwise timely proceeding or prevents timely return and the petitioner proves reasonable diligence. The petitioner bears preponderance on the factual predicates, including causation and diligence during the relevant period and after the obstacle ends. Diligence is reasonable in the actual circumstances, not perfect diligence or anticipation and correction of the very misleading direction. Notice error alone establishes neither causation nor diligence.

The court must identify the interval needing protection and a lawful remedy that cures the entire timing defect, including any pre-election expiration, while excluding independently avoidable delay. Loss of a timely original and prevention of timely later filing are distinct remedial problems. A later omission need not physically cause earlier elapsed days, but the supported causal chain and lawful remedy must reach the actual expiration. Federal pendency cannot automatically be subtracted. Separate later and closed original actions do not automatically merge; any reopening requires an independently lawful predicate.

**Authority by component:** [Pliler v. Ford — Parts I–IV](../terms/OT2003/records/pliler_v_ford_merits_2004-06-21.md#holdings), June 21, 2004. Stevens, Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join Parts I–III; O’Connor also joins Parts II–III for actual materially misleading judicial directions, not omitted notice. All nine join Part IV’s no-automatic-relation-back rule.

**Present operation effective:** June 21, 2004; governs the pending dismissal consequences before the Court and later qualifying elections, without a new full limitation period.

**Exceptions and limits:** A lawful remedy must be legally available for the actual defect; factual causal loss alone does not establish availability of tolling. General mixed-petition stay authority and relation back after an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening remain unresolved. First-application classification does not establish timeliness, and the statutory state-pendency rules retain their separate conditions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal damages claims seeking review of state injunction judgments

### Federal damages claims seeking review of state injunction judgments

**Governing question:** When Rooker and Feldman bar a district-court damages claim concerning a state injunction.

**Current rule:** A district court cannot adjudicate a claim whose alleged federal wrong is the legal error of a particular state injunction and whose requested damages compensate the order’s direct effects. State litigation’s having caused, preceded or related to an injury is not itself enough to bar an independently actionable claim. Where the federal pleadings supply no original jurisdiction, §1367(a) supplies no supplemental jurisdiction over related state claims.

**Authority by component:**

- Jordahl Holdings 2 and 4: bounded appellate-reassessment bar and absence of original jurisdiction: Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia — controlling Holdings, 1998-09-21.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-09-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** Heck governs present §1983 cognizability within its custody setting, not lower-federal appellate jurisdiction, and creates no universal favorable-termination requirement for every civil injunction.

**Holdings navigation:** Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia — Federal damages and appellate reassessment of state injunctions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Burford abstention and independent legal-damages claims

### Burford abstention and independent legal-damages claims

**Governing question:** Whether abstention permits surrender of an action seeking coercive contract damages.

**Current rule:** Outright dismissal or remand under the governing Burford abstention precedents requires equitable or otherwise discretionary relief; the court may not relinquish independent coercive contract-damages claims. An otherwise justified stay may postpone litigation while preserving federal jurisdiction, but its conditions must separately be satisfied. A declaratory count does not make the entire action declaration-only. Subject to timely and adequate state review, NOPSI's alternative grounds concern difficult state-law questions of substantial public importance transcending the immediate result, or disruption of state efforts to establish coherent policy on a matter of substantial public concern; they are not cumulative requirements, and complexity or possible conflict alone is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- Remedy boundary and distinct alternative abstention predicates: Quackenbush — Burford does not authorize relinquishing these independent damages claims; Limits and questions not reached, June 3, 1996; unanimous Court opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Quackenbush, June 3, 1996.

**Open question:** No general exception for extraordinary federalism interests is adopted. The treatment of a genuinely separable declaratory claim and the source-specific state-tax comity, Thibodaux and other abstention questions reserved in the Record remain open; forum non conveniens is distinct.

**Related current rules:** Discretion to stay a declaration-only coverage action supplies Wilton's separate §2201 authority.

**Holdings navigation:** Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., 517 U.S. 706.

## Standards and Tests selection: Covered removal grounds and the bar on appellate remand review

### Covered removal grounds and the bar on appellate remand review

**Governing question:** When §§1447(c)–(d) bar review, including a removal also invoking bankruptcy jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** A remand to the originating state court for a timely raised removal-procedure defect or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction is not reviewable on appeal or otherwise under §1447(d), even when removal also invokes §1452; a procedural-remand motion must be made within thirty days after removal. The then-applicable exception permits review of a case removed pursuant to §1443. Courts may identify the stated ground but may not review a covered ground's correctness merely because it is alleged erroneous. A Burford abstention remand is outside this bar, but absence of the bar does not itself create an appellate route. Section 1452(b) coexists with the ordinary bar rather than displacing it.

**Authority by component:**

- Covered grounds, §1443 exception and bankruptcy coexistence: Things Remembered — The ordinary covered-remand bar applies in bankruptcy removals, December 5, 1995; Thomas for all nine.
- Abstention-ground classification: Quackenbush — The statutory remand-review bar does not cover this abstention ground, June 3, 1996; unanimous Court opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Things Remembered, December 5, 1995; Quackenbush, June 3, 1996, clarifies the abstention boundary.

**Open question:** No universal rule for all bankruptcy or discretionary state-law remands is adopted; reviewability of Cohill remands and the complete §1452(b) equitable-remand scope remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, 516 U.S. 124; Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., 517 U.S. 706.

## Standards and Tests selection: Appeal of an abstention order surrendering the federal forum

### Appeal of an abstention order surrendering the federal forum

**Governing question:** A remand that completely disassociates the district court from the action.

**Current rule:** Absent a statutory review bar, an abstention remand conclusively surrendering federal adjudication is appealable under §1291 on two independently sufficient grounds: Moses H. Cone's limited effectively-out-of-court finality rule and Cohen's collateral-order rule. The latter requires a conclusive, important federal-forum question separate from the merits and effectively unreviewable after a later federal final judgment; this surrender satisfies each condition. Mere delay, litigation expense or an asserted right to avoid trial does not suffice. Thermtron's mandamus-only language yields to this application of Cone, while its construction of the covered-remand bar survives.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent finality and collateral-order grounds, with limited disavowal: Quackenbush — Complete surrender of the federal forum supplies an appealable decision; The collateral-order doctrine independently permits this appeal, June 3, 1996; unanimous Court opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Quackenbush, June 3, 1996.

**Holdings navigation:** Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., 517 U.S. 706.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

#### Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1600.

**Decided:** April 20, 1993.

##### Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation

**Holding:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate the employment decision and have a determinative influence; pension interference based only on years of service does not by itself establish that requirement. Liability remains possible when pension status is deliberately used as an age proxy or age and pension status both motivate the decision; age-triggered vesting and disparate impact are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Aikens, 460 U.S. 711, and Burdine, 450 U.S. 248 — applied: actual motivation may be proved circumstantially; no automatic inference from any unlawful motive.
- Ingersoll-Rand, 498 U.S. 133 — distinguished: ERISA pension interference and age discrimination remain distinct.
- McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. 792 — preserved; its legitimate-reason terminology does not make every improper motive an ADEA violation.

##### Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies

**Holding:** An ADEA disparate-treatment violation is willful when the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether its conduct violated the Act, whether the decision was individual or a formal policy. Once that showing is made, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive; a good-faith, nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willfulness.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thurston, 469 U.S. 111 — reaffirmed and applied to individual disparate-treatment decisions.
- Richland Shoe, 486 U.S. 128 — applied: negligence and mere awareness of statutory relevance are not willfulness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Pension status is no safe harbor when used as an age proxy. Age-based vesting and disparate impact are not decided. A nonreckless good-faith mistake about a statutory defense or exemption can defeat willfulness without defeating ordinary liability. No direct-evidence, outrageousness or predominant-age requirement is added.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for reconsideration of ADEA sufficiency and, if needed, willfulness. The younger replacement, confidentiality demands and comments are not held sufficient or insufficient here. Independent ERISA and state-law judgments retain their own effect; no liquidated-damages award is made.

#### General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline, 540 U.S. 581

**Docket or dockets:** No. 02-1080.
**Decided:** 2004-02-24.

##### Whether the ADEA forbids favoring older workers over younger workers who are also at least forty — General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline, 2004-02-24

**Holding:** The ADEA protects against disadvantage because of advancing age and does not prohibit an employer's preference for older workers over younger workers solely because both groups are within the age-forty-and-over class. Statutory coverage of the challenged disadvantage must precede use of the Act's proof and remedial rules.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg: six direct joins.

**Material application:** Preserving retirement health benefits for workers fifty and older while withdrawing the future benefit from younger workers does not state the reviewed ADEA claim. The operative language, findings and structure give the prohibition a directional meaning; the EEOC's contrary construction cannot override it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court settles this statute's meaning without choosing a degree of deference to an otherwise permissible agency interpretation. It does not replace coverage with a general constitutional anti-classification rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit's coverage judgment is reversed and remanded for dismissal of the reviewed directional-discrimination theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins retains the distinction between age motivation and correlated pension or tenure factors. O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp. retains its proof rule for substantially younger replacements within the protected class. Neither made ADEA coverage bilateral.

#### Duncan v. Walker, 533 U.S. 167

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-121.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### Whether federal habeas pendency qualifies for section 2244(d)(2) exclusion

**Holding:** The word State in 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) modifies both postconviction review and other collateral review. A federal habeas application's pendency therefore is not excluded from the limitation period by that subsection, even when the application was timely and dismissed without prejudice because exhaustion was unestablished; a civil-rights pleading accompanying it does not change that classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct votes for both questions.

**Material application:** State modifies both forms of collateral review. The separate phrase other collateral review accommodates different state procedures and custody outside ordinary criminal convictions. A timely original federal filing, its nonmerits exhaustion dismissal, and a later application's non-successive status do not permanently satisfy the later application's deadline. Qualifying state review still requires proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim, and actual pendency.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rose v. Lundy supplies exhaustion's state/federal distinction without creating compulsory stays. Lindh v. Murphy protects its identified pre-enactment application, not all later filings about the conviction. Slack v. McDaniel preserves the exhaustion-return classification while retaining independent timing requirements. Artuz v. Bennett preserves the difference between filing conditions and claim-relief bars, without changing a federal filing's sovereign category.

##### What timeliness adjudication follows rejection of the federal-pendency exclusion

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's reinstatement cannot rest on excluding Walker's first federal proceeding under §2244(d)(2). On remand the courts must calculate timeliness without that exclusion and adjudicate any independently preserved, legally available commencement or equitable ground under the applicable law and actual record; if none makes the application timely, it must be dismissed as untimely.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct votes for both questions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 24, 1997 transitional deadline is this review's premise, not a universal commencement rule. General equitable-tolling availability and conditions, mixed-petition stays, the constitutional merits, and ultimate habeas entitlement remain undecided. Section 2254(b)(2) permits, but does not compel, merits denial of an unexhausted application. No universal warning duty, election, dismissal deadline, stay-and-abeyance procedure or automatic extension follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit judgment is reversed and remanded. The equitable argument actually raised but not reached below receives its own lawful adjudication; the Court neither finds diligence or extraordinary impediment nor grants tolling, a writ, release or resentencing. Dismissal as untimely is required if no independently available ground establishes timeliness.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs and United States v. Brockamp preserve provision-sensitive analysis of equity but settle neither Walker's entitlement nor categorical unavailability of equitable protection.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Pliler v. #### Pliler v. Ford, 542 U.S. 225

**Docket or dockets:** No. 03-221.
**Decided:** 2004-06-21

##### A court-presented dismissal election requires minimum neutral notice

**Holding:** When a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner with a timely mixed petition to choose between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissing to exhaust other claims, and the materials before it disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will defeat timely return, it must give neutral notice that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the original filing date and that a later petition may be time barred. The court must provide a meaningful opportunity to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims, subject to their ordinary requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part I, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The duty is a minimum floor triggered by the court’s election and apparent substantial filing risk. It does not require investigation of missing records, precise computation, advice on strategy or claim strength, or a promised stay; pro se status or complexity alone is insufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The protection governs these pending dismissal consequences and later qualifying elections without supplying a new full limitation period.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Duncan v. Walker: applied to exclude federal pendency from §2244(d)(2); its equity reservation is resolved for the defined court-created impediment while the statutory exclusion remains unchanged.
- Castro v. United States: extended by analogy through a new supervisory holding for this defined court-presented election; its own recharacterization rule retains its scope.
- Rose v. Lundy: preserved in its exhaustion requirement; its operation does not excuse an inaccurate election under the minimum notice rule.

##### A proved court-created loss can receive equitable protection

**Holding:** Section 2244(d) permits equitable tolling for omission of the required notice or another materially misleading judicial direction that causes loss of an otherwise timely federal proceeding or prevents timely return, when the petitioner establishes the required causal connection and reasonable diligence. A notice violation alone does not establish reliance, causation or diligence, and causal loss alone does not establish that tolling is the legally available remedy for the actual timing defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part II, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch, without joining the omitted-notice branch.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Federal pendency alone remains outside statutory tolling. Judicial interference, causation, diligence and a lawful remedy are separate requirements; processing delay, pro se status or an adverse calculation alone do not suffice. The court must distinguish loss of a timely original action from prevention of timely later filing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Duncan v. Walker: applied to exclude federal pendency from §2244(d)(2); its equity reservation is resolved for the defined court-created impediment while the statutory exclusion remains unchanged.
- Carey v. Saffold: applied to qualifying state pendency and intervals in each actual calculation.
- Slack v. McDaniel: applied to keep exhaustion-return classification separate from timeliness and lawful amendment.

##### The petitioner must prove an effective causal remedy

**Holding:** The petitioner must prove the factual predicates for this equitable protection by a preponderance of the evidence, including reasonable diligence during the relevant period and after the obstacle ended; diligence is assessed in the actual circumstances, not as perfect diligence or a duty to anticipate and correct the very misleading direction at issue. The court must identify the precise interval requiring protection, justify its causal and lawful remedial basis, exclude independently avoidable delay, and explain how the remedy cures the whole timing defect, including any expiration before the election.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part III, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins these requirements for the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch.

**Material application:** Both originals were timely with five days remaining, but the period expired before dismissal. Protecting only postdismissal days cannot be assumed to cure the returns. A lawful causal remedy must reach the whole defect; reopening requires its independent predicates, and blanket subtraction of federal pendency is impermissible.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The preponderance rule governs these equitable factual predicates; distinct statutory and remedial predicates retain their applicable standards. Without a lawful effective remedy, the later petition remains untimely.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Duncan v. Walker: applied to exclude federal pendency from §2244(d)(2); its equity reservation is resolved for the defined court-created impediment while the statutory exclusion remains unchanged.
- Grogan v. Garner and Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston: applied to ordinary factual proof and allocation of uncertainty, preserving distinct statutory or remedial proof requirements.

##### Omitted warnings do not automatically join separate actions

**Holding:** A separate later petition does not automatically become an amendment to a closed original action because required warnings were omitted. Relief depending on reopening requires an independently lawful reopening predicate; whether relation back follows an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening remains unresolved because that procedural predicate has not been established here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s opinion, part IV, directly adopted by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General authority and standards for stays of mixed petitions remain unresolved. The contingent relation-back question after independently lawful reopening also remains open. Neither the warning violation nor first-application classification supplies retained, stayed or amended status to a terminated action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** - Slack v. McDaniel: applied to keep exhaustion-return classification separate from timeliness and lawful amendment.
- Rose v. Lundy: preserved in its exhaustion requirement; its operation does not excuse an inaccurate election under the minimum notice rule.

##### The repeated claims return for individualized adjudication

**Authority of the judgment:** Seven Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—vacate the repeated-claim disposition and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Souter adopt Part V's affirmative-rule rationale; the other four support vacatur on their no-advisement ground. Ginsburg and Breyer would preserve the lower vacatur of the repeated-claim dismissals by partial affirmance. Parts I–IV supply the controlling rules for further proceedings despite that remedial disagreement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single rationale for repeated-claim vacatur controls under Marks: three Justices adopt the affirmative-rule rationale and four the no-advisement ground. Parts I–IV separately command direct majorities and govern the remand.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Loguercio’s express election and Weed’s nonresponse receive separate preservation, communications, responsive-conduct, causation, diligence and lawful-remedy inquiries. The two new Loguercio assistance claims remain dismissed; development of the five new Weed legal-paper-access claims continues. Automatic new-claim relation back remains denied.

#### Things Remembered, Inc. v. Petrarca, 516 U.S. 124

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1530.
**Decided:** December 5, 1995.

##### Review of a covered remand when removal also invokes bankruptcy jurisdiction

**Holding:** When a removed case is remanded to the State court from which it came because of a timely raised removal-procedure defect or lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, §1447(d), read with §1447(c), bars appellate review even if removal also invoked §1452. The then-applicable exception allows review on appeal or otherwise of an order remanding a case removed under §1443; that exception is not implicated here.

#### Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., 517 U.S. 706

**Docket or dockets:** 95-244
**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does section 1447(d) bar review of a remand entered on Burford abstention grounds?

**Holding:** Section 1447(d), read with section 1447(c), bars appellate review of remands actually resting on the covered subject-matter-jurisdiction grounds or timely raised removal-procedure defects; a procedural-defect motion must be made within thirty days. A remand on Burford abstention grounds is outside that bar, although escaping the bar does not itself establish an affirmative appellate channel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I-II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; unanimous controlling rationale for all questions in this entry.

**Controlling explanation:** The reviewing court identifies the remand's actual stated ground; it does not reconsider the correctness of a covered ground as a way around the bar. Burford abstention does not assert defective federal jurisdiction or removal procedure. The statutory section 1443 exception does not govern, and the reserved Cohill question about discretionary remand of state-law claims is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Thermtron's covered-ground construction remains controlling; Rice and Gravitt retain the bar on review of covered remands even when error is alleged. Cohill's distinct remand-review question remains open.

##### Is this conclusive surrender of the federal forum reviewable under section 1291?

**Holding:** A remand that conclusively surrenders the entire federal action and leaves no further federal adjudicative work is reviewable under section 1291 because it effectively puts the litigant out of federal court. Independently, this order satisfies the collateral-order doctrine: it conclusively determines an important public question of federal jurisdiction, separate from the merits, that would be effectively unreviewable after a later federal judgment that will never occur.

**Controlling explanation:** The first, independently sufficient ground applies Cone's practical treatment of complete federal-forum surrender; it does not pretend that this remand is a conventional Catlin final merits judgment. The second, independently sufficient ground satisfies Cohen and Digital Equipment because it concerns the public allocation of judicial responsibility and forecloses later review. Litigation expense, a private settlement right or a bare wish to avoid trial would not meet that test. Each ground independently supports jurisdiction; the order's particular conclusive effect matters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Not every stay, remand or interlocutory order is appealable. Section 1292(b) certification is unnecessary on these grounds; that distinct route still requires a written finding of a controlling legal question, substantial ground for disagreement and material advancement of termination, a timely application within ten days, appellate permission, and no automatic stay.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Cone supplies the independently sufficient effectively-out-of-court route for complete surrender of the federal forum.
- Catlin retains its conventional final-decision rule, which is not stretched into a fictional final merits disposition here.
- Cohen supplies the independent collateral-order requirements.
- Digital Equipment requires public importance and effective unreviewability; a private right to avoid litigation is insufficient.
- Thermtron's mandamus-only language is disavowed insofar as it conflicts with the later Cone application. Its construction of section 1447(d)'s covered grounds remains intact.

##### May Burford abstention support outright remand of independent legal damages claims?

**Holding:** Burford abstention permits outright dismissal or remand only when the relief sought is equitable or otherwise discretionary; an independent legal damages claim cannot be surrendered on that basis. A properly justified stay may preserve federal jurisdiction over such a claim, but this decision neither requires nor automatically authorizes one.

**Controlling explanation:** These independent contract claims seek legal damages. State liquidation interests, setoff, participation in liquidation, procedural merger of law and equity, and the presence of a genuine declaratory claim do not make all the requested relief discretionary. Wilton's discretion over an action seeking only declaratory relief does not convert this mixed action into such a case. The actual state asset orders permit proceedings elsewhere; the Court does not assume that every insurance action disposes of assets under exclusive state custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether Burford's predicates otherwise exist. They require timely and adequate state review and either difficult state-law questions bearing on public policy transcending the case's result, or disruption of coherent state policy on a matter of substantial public concern; those alternatives are not cumulative, and complexity or potential conflict alone is insufficient. The Court rejects neither all abstention-related stays in damages actions nor the distinct doctrines involving state-tax comity, Thibodaux, Colorado River or foreign forum non conveniens.

The Court decides no arbitration entitlement or sequencing priority. Any Federal Arbitration Act section 3 application still requires a written covered agreement and referable issue, the court's satisfaction, an application by a party, a stay according to the agreement, and absence of applicant default. Ordinary evenhanded formation, fraud, duress, unconscionability, assent and preservation rules remain; coverage, scope, delegation, nonsignatory enforcement and McCarran-Ferguson issues are undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment vacating the abstention remand is affirmed, 9-0. Federal proceedings resume without an ordered stay, arbitration, damages award, distribution of assets or new evidentiary hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Burford is confined as to outright dismissal or remand to equitable or otherwise discretionary relief, while leaving a properly justified stay available for damages claims.
- Wilton's discretion over solely declaratory relief does not authorize surrender of an independent legal damages action.
- Fair Assessment's state-tax comity doctrine remains distinct.
- Thibodaux and Colorado River retain their separate grounds and limits; neither supports a universal ban on stays in legal actions.
- The separate law of foreign forum non conveniens remains undisturbed.

#### Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** 97-859.

**Decided:** 1998-09-21.

##### Past restraints do not alone establish a present controversy under changed law

**Holding:** A prospective challenge requires identified intended conduct facing a realistic current enforcement threat; ended injunctions under replaced or materially amended provisions do not alone supply it. Count One must be dismissed on this record, without requiring an actual prosecution or deciding whether any publication or disclosure rule is constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Kennedy's Part I, joined by all nine.

**Material application:** Pic-A-State Pennsylvania, Inc. v. Reno and Virginia v. American Booksellers Association permit review before enforcement where the threatened constraint is concrete. Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona requires a continuing personal controversy. Here all injunctions against these plaintiffs have ended, the statutes have materially changed, and no identified intended activity faces a supported current threat. The Party's failure to disavow every future suit does not fill that gap. Caldwell's authoritative exclusion of solely issue advocacy from the current Virginia provisions further weakens a threat resting on issue-only speech, without treating the express vote-against pamphlet as issue-only or deciding any past order. Amendment does not automatically moot every claim, and the importance of timely political speech cannot replace the absent current controversy. No rule limits proper defendants universally to the Commonwealth, bars every suit against a private enforcer, or disposes of accrued damages merely because prospective relief ended.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Pic-A-State applies the concrete-threat requirement and preserves review without prosecution.
- American Booksellers preserves credible pre-enforcement review; no such threat is established here.
- Arizonans applies the continuing-controversy requirement without extending its unavailable-damages holding to these private damages defendants.
- Caldwell supplies current Virginia construction only; it does not adjudicate the past injunctions.

##### These pleaded damages counts seek appellate reassessment of particular state judgments

**Holding:** A federal district court lacks authority under Rooker and Feldman to adjudicate these counts insofar as their asserted federal wrong is the legal error of the particular state injunctions and their requested damages compensate those orders' direct effects. The rule is confined to that pleaded theory; it does not bar a claim merely because state litigation caused, preceded or bears on an independently actionable injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Kennedy's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Material application:** Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman reserve appellate reassessment of state judicial decisions to the proper appellate channel. The orders here applied law after an opportunity to object; prospective terms did not make those adjudications legislative acts. The published descriptions of the four counts demand a determination that the specific restraints were legally erroneous, without identifying a separate procurement wrong. Their later expiration or dissolution and the demand for damages against the procuring party do not change that pleaded wrong. Dennis v. Sparks preserves independent corrupt-procurement claims, and Johnson v. De Grandy preserves original federal enforcement without appellate review of a judgment against the claimant. Neither supplies a missing independent allegation here. The Court rejects a universal rule covering every consequence of a state order and a simple but-for causation test. It makes no finding that the Party's conduct was lawful and decides no preclusion, state action, immunity, limitations, personal responsibility or damages question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rooker applies the lower-federal appellate boundary to the actual wrong and relief asserted.
- Feldman applies the original-action versus individual-judgment distinction; prospective relief can remain judicial adjudication.
- Dennis preserves genuinely independent procurement claims; judicial immunity does not immunize private corrupt conspirators.
- De Grandy preserves its independent-enforcement holding and is distinguished from these pleaded attacks on judgments against the claimants.
- Lugar v. Edmondson Oil Co. remains the separate state-attribution inquiry; merely winning or filing private litigation does not establish it.

##### Heck supplies no universal civil-injunction jurisdiction bar

**Holding:** Heck's favorable-invalidation requirement concerns present §1983 cognizability in its bounded custody setting, not lower-federal appellate jurisdiction. It supplies no universal rule requiring favorable termination of every civil injunction before an independent damages action may proceed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Kennedy's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes on this explanation. The separate writings likewise reject a categorical bar but do not add joins to the Court's opinion.

**Material application:** Heck v. Humphrey coordinates §1983 with a real lawful opportunity to challenge an outstanding conviction in custody, while preserving independently cognizable injuries and its unresolved populations. Edwards v. Balisok applies that rule to invalidity-dependent disciplinary-credit claims. Spencer v. Kemna keeps Article III injury, statutory access and possible civil relief distinct and preserves the reservations for former prisoners and those without a realistic habeas opportunity. Those decisions neither convert cognizability into subject-matter jurisdiction nor authorize appellate reassessment of these orders. The lower court's assimilation of Heck to Rooker and Feldman is rejected. Affirmance rests on the actual claim-specific jurisdictional analysis, not a new civil-injunction favorable-termination rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Heck retains its bounded statutory prerequisite and independent-injury limits; it is not a Rooker-Feldman decision.
- Edwards retains the invalidity-dependent disciplinary-credit extension, without governing these civil orders.
- Spencer preserves the distinct inquiries and unresolved populations; no exemption or universal bar is created here.

##### The state claims lack the required supplemental-jurisdiction foundation

**Holding:** Where the pleaded federal claims supply no original jurisdiction, §1367(a) supplies no supplemental jurisdiction over their related state claims. Counts Four, Five, Six and Nine therefore remain dismissed without prejudice, without an adjudication of tort merits or a discretionary retention decision under §1367(c)(3).

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Kennedy's Part IV, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Material application:** Section 1367(a) requires a civil action within the district court's original jurisdiction before its supplemental grant operates. Count One lacks a current controversy and the pleaded damages counts demand appellate authority the district court does not possess. The district court's actual no-original-jurisdiction ground therefore supports dismissal. Section 1367(c)(3), which permits declining supplemental jurisdiction after claims within original jurisdiction have been dismissed, is not an interchangeable description of that defect. Nothing here requires a court possessing original jurisdiction to retain every related state claim. State tort elements, timeliness and recovery remain for a competent forum.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The Court corrects the Fourth Circuit's discretionary description while preserving the district court's §1367(a) ground; no earlier Supreme Court decision is displaced.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. #### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.

Intentional punishment for complaint activity is a distinct prohibition, even when the complaint alleges sex discrimination against someone else. Jackson's alleged loss of coaching duties and pay establishes personal injury; it does not establish that the enacted right protects the activity that allegedly caused that injury. The dissent's beneficiary and statutory-causation objections therefore remain distinct from standing.

Title VII's express retaliation provision illustrates that Congress can separately protect complaint activity. Sullivan construed section 1982's property-right protection, and neither its standing analysis nor its substantive setting compels the asserted Title IX funding obligation. Cannon supplies an action for rights Congress enacted; Franklin permits remedies after a statutory wrong is established. Neither decides this disputed expansion of coverage. Pennhurst adds a notice objection to imposing the asserted condition on recipients without a sufficiently clear statutory command. The dissent accepts the actual scopes of the existing Title IX and Title VI enforcement precedents but finds no enacted retaliation right here. It would conclude the appealed Title IX theory.

### 2005-03-01 — [Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States; No. 04-190; judgment below, 365 F.3d 385](../records/petro-hunt-v-united-states_merits_2005-03-01.md#public-projection)

Federal common law governs the preclusive effect of the earlier federal-question judgment. A final title judgment binds the claim it adjudicated, including grounds that could have been advanced within that claim, but distinct servitudes with different acquisition and use histories do not become the same claim merely because they share owners, related conveyances or a legal premise; the party invoking preclusion must establish the required identity from the actual instruments and adjudication.

Mutual issue preclusion requires the identical issue to have been actually litigated and necessarily determined. Nebo Oil's adjudication of state-law retroactivity and constitutional objections, while assuming Louisiana law supplied the rule of decision, does not preclude the distinct federal choice-of-law issue now asserted.

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Nebo Oil's adjudication of its original servitude remains effective. The Fifth Circuit's determination that the other 95 servitudes are subject to Louisiana's ten-year nonuse prescription remains effective; the district court must determine actual prescription and resulting title for each servitude.

- Nevada v. United States supplies the inquiry into the property right actually adjudicated and protects final title within that scope.
- Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. supplies the distinction between a judgment's source and actual external effect; its diversity-specific state-law incorporation rule remains confined to that setting.
- Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. retains its protection of completed federal adjudications; applying preclusion to the decree's actual subject matter preserves that protection.
- Commissioner v. Sunnen and Montana v. United States supply the issue-identity and actual-adjudication requirements applied here.
- United States v. Stauffer Chemical Co. retains mutual issue preclusion where its predicates exist; the missing actual litigation distinguishes this case.

The district court must identify each disputed servitude's instrument, acquisition history, qualifying good-faith operations and interruptions, apply the governing prescription rule, and determine title. It must consider the asserted continuing operations on five servitudes, the Government's concessions concerning operations on some servitudes, and the second summary-judgment motion previously dismissed as moot. Any properly supported specific preclusion claim must satisfy the claim-identity or actually-litigated-issue requirements stated here. The fee cross-appeal lacks its former prevailing-party predicate after the blanket preclusion judgment falls; any later fee issue follows the eventual proper judgment.
