# OT2004 chunk 2: prior public Anderson authority

Derived reading copy of the bounded Public Projection in [Anderson v. Spear](../records/anderson-v-spear_merits_2004-11-30.md), effective November 30, 2004. The Record controls. Exact public text follows; no private Record section is included.

## Event

Anderson v. Spear, Sixth Circuit No. 02-5529, 356 F.3d 651. Merits decision, November 30, 2004, following merits submission. The Court reviews the reversal of summary judgment for Kentucky officials in a challenge to the 500-foot electioneering prohibition, the $50,000 personal campaign-loan ceiling and the complete post-election contribution ban. The candidate seeks declaratory and injunctive relief; officials defend the restrictions as safeguards for voting and against corruption.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The judgment is affirmed in part, vacated in part and remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer—support each disposition below.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Challenged 500-foot public-forum speech restriction | Invalidation affirmed, 9–0 | Declaratory and injunctive relief against its adjudicated unlawful operation |
| $50,000 personal-loan ceiling | Invalidation affirmed, 9–0 | Judgment against the unsupported personal-funding limit |
| Complete post-election contribution prohibition | Invalidation affirmed, 9–0 | Judgment against the unsupported categorical debt-retirement ban |
| Universal express-advocacy-only construction | Vacated, 9–0 | Remand for lawful state-law severability and precise relief |

The district court must define prohibited enforcement and afford relief protecting the candidates and speakers governed by these policies unless the State shows that narrower relief fully cures the proved violation. Officials must conform written enforcement directions before renewed application to protected activity. The decree must preserve separately lawful anti-intimidation and contribution rules and remain enforceable if the invalid restrictions continue.

## Holdings

**Ordinary public forums retain protection near polling places.** A campaign-specific restriction on ordinary public streets and sidewalks must be necessary to serve a compelling voting-protection interest and narrowly drawn to achieve it. Kentucky has not justified the actual 500-foot reach and suppression of nonintimidating write-in instructions and advocacy. Part I controls through the seven Justices identified above.

The statute operates on election day at polling places and within 500 feet of the county clerk's office or an entrance to a building containing a voting machine. It covers signs, literature, cards, handbills, petition signatures and vote solicitation. Exit polling is excluded. The private-property exception concerns activity inside a private residence or establishment other than the polling premises by a person with an ownership interest; it does not restore ordinary public-sidewalk speech. Burson v. Freeman supplies the controlling scrutiny proposition, while its divided 100-foot validity judgment cannot justify a different distance and record automatically. Mills v. Alabama protects election-day advocacy, and United States v. Grace preserves ordinary sidewalk status near government premises. Preventing intimidation is compelling, but the State has not shown why nonintimidating communication throughout this broad area must be suppressed. Administrability alone does not establish that fit.

**Initial personal funding and later donor repayment are different transactions.** The State may regulate actual third-party contributions used to repay campaign debt under the governing contribution standard, but the possibility of later repayment does not justify this $50,000 ceiling on a candidate's initial use of personal campaign resources. Part II is unanimous.

Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes the candidate's own spending from a donor's transfer. A later contribution retiring personal debt can confer a personal benefit and carry corruption risk. That concern concerns the later donor transaction; it does not transform all initial personal funding into a contribution or justify suppressing the candidate's own campaign expression at this ceiling.

**The complete post-election contribution ban lacks the required fit.** A State restricting debt-retirement contributions must support its anticorruption justification and show close fit while preserving meaningful political association. Kentucky's categorical prohibition on candidates or agents soliciting or accepting contributions after the general election fails that standard on this record. Part III is unanimous.

Russell v. Burris and Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC require actual fit rather than an assumed immunity or categorical ban. Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont preserves source-specific circumvention analysis. Winner identification and personal debt retirement can heighten the risk of donor influence, but those interests do not justify closing every lawful post-election contribution channel. Ordinary donor amount, source, true-source and disclosure restrictions retain their independent operation under the governing law.

**Relief must correct the unlawful policies without rewriting state law.** The district court must enter precise declaratory and injunctive relief against the restrictions' adjudicated unlawful operation and determine lawful state-law severability. Agreement that a speech restriction fails does not authorize a universal federal insertion of an express-advocacy-only definition. Part IV's construction ground has the eight direct joins shown above; all nine support its vacatur and the general implementing remedy.

The lower court's universal narrowing construction is therefore vacated. The remedial inquiry must apply permissible construction and severability principles to the enacted provisions while carrying out the invalidations and scope of relief stated in the judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

Burson v. Freeman is applied for its independently majority-supported scrutiny rule; its fractured judgment retains its bounded force without becoming a numerical safe harbor.

Mills v. Alabama and United States v. Grace are applied for election-day advocacy and the continuing public-forum character of ordinary sidewalks.

Buckley v. Valeo is applied to distinguish initial personal campaign financing from later donor contributions.

Russell v. Burris and Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC are reaffirmed and applied for justified, closely fitted contribution restrictions preserving meaningful advocacy.

Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont is applied for actual source and circumvention analysis in assessing contribution regulation.

## Law After Decision

The challenged public-forum perimeter, personal-loan ceiling and complete post-election contribution prohibition are unenforceable at their adjudicated reach. Protection of voting remains a compelling objective requiring fit to the actual speech restriction. Candidate financing and later debt-repayment contributions retain their distinct constitutional treatment, with lawful donor safeguards operating independently. Precise relief and permissible state-law severability implement these rules.

## Separate Writings

Scalia agrees with the judgment and joins the financing and remedial parts. His polling-environs position distinguishes the historically administered immediate environs during voting from a 500-foot reach across ordinary neighborhood streets. The record supplies insufficient historical or functional justification for extending his Burson approach that far, so he reaches invalidity without joining the Court's full forum-and-strict-scrutiny analysis.

Thomas likewise joins the financial and remedial parts and agrees with the speech judgment. His own historical and strict-scrutiny approach protects direct political communication against this broad exclusion, drawing on his positions in McIntyre and Watchtower. That sufficient ground does not adopt Part I's complete forum methodology.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for entry of a conforming judgment, precise declaratory and injunctive terms, and lawful severability. The district court must preserve separately lawful voting and contribution safeguards and enforce the decree's prohibitions against renewed unlawful application.
