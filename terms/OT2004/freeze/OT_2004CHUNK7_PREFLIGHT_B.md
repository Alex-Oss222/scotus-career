## Bates — fresh neutral corrections and decisional boundaries

The live problem is claim-specific FIFRA preemption following summary judgment. The farmers alleged design/manufacture defects, negligent testing, fraud, warranties and DTPA theories; the appellate court characterized the design/manufacture/testing theories as disguised warnings. **They do not independently press an actual failure-to-warn claim in this Court.** That clarification is authenticated by the control-supplied objective recitation and consistent with the lower opinion's exact characterization. The lower opinion does not quote that pleading, so its exact words should be copied from the objective handoff or appendix rather than expanded from inference. A parallel-duty route must confront the disclaimer and ordinary pleading posture; it cannot deem an unasserted federal violation proved.

The lower opinion resolves diversity amount and discretionary declaratory jurisdiction; the scheduled review treats that posture as established. The challenged judgment establishes neither defect nor falsity, reliance, causation or damages. Reversal of a preemption ground does not restore claims already lost on another unchallenged basis. The source identifies two precise limits: footnote 14 treats the breach-of-contract preemption objection as waived for lack of appellate briefing; footnote 15 preserves the unappealed application of the label's limitation-of-remedies provision to one materially different presale representation. Do not add an unidentified implied-warranty dismissal. Express warranty, contract and independently rejected representation theories must retain their actual procedural distinctions.

Section 136v(a) permits state sale/use regulation only insofar as it does not permit federally prohibited sale/use; §136v(b) separately bars additional or different labeling/packaging requirements. Section 136(q)(1)(A) reaches false or misleading labeling; (F) and (G) separately address adequate directions and necessary warnings. Section 136a(c)(5) permits efficacy-data waiver and, then, registration without determining that composition warrants efficacy claims; this is not a finding that no federal misbranding duty exists. Section 136a(f)(2) makes registration no defense to an offense, while providing prima facie evidence of registration compliance when no cancellation proceeding is in effect. Keep both halves. No EPA conduct-date approval, required efficacy statement or protected federal choice beyond the supported sources is presumed.

## Heller — complete statute and differentiated disclosure analysis

The entire challenged statutory text was mechanically copied to `OT_2004CHUNK7_B_sources/nevada-operative-statute.txt`. The compressed packet is supplemented as follows. Section 294A.320(1) requires the name and **mailing or street address** of each person paying or responsible for paying for publication, plus a statement that the person paid or is responsible. It does not invariably demand a home address. Subsection (2)(a) exempts candidate/party-funded advertising referring only to that candidate with the candidate's name prominently displayed. Subsection (2)(b) separately exempts material expressly approved and paid for by the candidate when preparation/publication cost has been reported as a campaign contribution under §294A.120. Subsection (2)(c) exempts an independently acting natural person who is not cooperating with or directed by a business/social organization, nongovernmental legal entity or government entity. Subsection (3) deems identification complying with the Communications Act and its regulations sufficient. “Material” includes printed/written matter or a photograph; “publish” includes causing distribution and the enumerated methods. The case concerns the printed communications described in the record.

Concrete alleged intended publications concern local ballot questions, public-comment rules and redistricting information; affidavits and prior prosecution supply the supported self-censorship setting. No general ideological objection substitutes for this record. The statute's breadth reaches neutral election information, very small pooled spending, and speech remote from the campaign period. A narrowing construction cannot convert “information relating to” an election into express advocacy without a permissible textual basis. Absence of a threshold, election window or magic words is relevant to actual fit, not a universal constitutional defect by itself.

The Ninth Circuit's categorical on-publication-versus-reporting distinction and broad extension of McIntyre require reassessment against actual entering law. Stengel sustained organizational paid candidate-identification, including justified payer address, while reserving materially different individual applications. McConnell sustained the specified sponsor-identification extension beyond express advocacy upon a material electoral-information/source-tracing relation. Its on-message duty does **not** import the separate financial-reporting threshold. The prior holdings do not establish that every small cooperative speaker, every address demand or every item of neutral information is validly covered. A model must compare the supported legitimate reach and burdens under the actual standard before selecting facial invalidation, bounded sustaining treatment or a legal-standard remand. A supported protective claim retains its own factual showing and government justification of remaining exposure; no threat or reprisal is invented.

**Law navigation:** “Anonymous individual political leaflets” (McIntyre April 19, 1995); Stengel September 14, 1998 Part IV; “Election-finance reporting, sponsor identification and political-request records” (McConnell December 10, 2003); “Issue advocacy and definite campaign-finance coverage” (Bartlett May 1, 2000, with McConnell's separately bounded coverage updates). Reporting, sponsor identification, suppression and full committee status are different duties. Their triggers do not migrate between provisions.

The lower opinion reports that Nevada added its natural-person exception in 1997 in response to McIntyre. The underlying individual-pamphlet protection exists in this timeline, so that predicate is intact. The broader organizational issue was expressly reserved and subsequently addressed by Stengel and McConnell. No new statutory existence choice is required on the present evidence.
