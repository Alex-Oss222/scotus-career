## Reconciled rules and remedy

**R1 — Similar plan.** These tax-qualified rollover IRAs provide rights to retirement-income payments under a similar plan or contract within section 522(d)(10)(E). Their objective structure supplies income replacing wages: restricted early access, tax-supported retirement saving, required later-life distributions and the pension funds they hold. Similarity requires the relevant common function and legal characteristics, not identical sponsorship, financing or every payment term. A future right to payment qualifies; a retirement label or subjective intent alone does not.

**R2 — Age connection.** These accounts provide payment on account of age because the substantial ten-percent early-distribution tax restricts full access before age 59½ and ceases to impose that restriction at that age. Possible earlier withdrawal at a substantial tax cost does not make the right to the entire balance age-independent. Age need not be the only permissible distribution trigger. The minimum-distribution provisions begin by the specified April 1 after the year of age 70½, with the then-effective fifty-percent deficiency tax; later statutory ages do not enter this ruling.

The narrow early-distribution alternatives remain intact. Death, defined disability, qualifying substantially equal life-expectancy payments, qualifying medical expenses, unemployment-related health premiums, higher education, first-home acquisition and a section 6331 levy have their own statutory predicates. Health-premium distributions may occur in the unemployment-compensation year or the succeeding taxable year, subject to twelve consecutive weeks of qualifying benefits, the qualifying insurance-expense ceiling, the sixty-day reemployment cutoff and the defined self-employed treatment. Education and first-home amounts retain their expense, beneficiary, institution, coordination, lifetime-dollar and timing limitations. Modifying substantially equal payments before 59½ or within five years ordinarily recaptures avoided tax and interest, with death/disability modifications excepted. IRA-inapplicable age-fifty-five separation and domestic-relations exceptions remain excluded. These alternatives do not convert the supported accounts into ordinary savings; no particular exception's application to the Rouseys is necessary. Exact retained definitions and terms are in `OT_2004CHUNK6_ROUSEY_OBJECTIVE_SUPPLEMENT.md` and its copied statutory text.

The strongest contrary argument treats the tax as merely a price attached to unrestricted access. The substantial proportional loss of the distribution answers that characterization; full access changes materially with age. The catchall's wage-replacement function and express section 408 reference answer the related contention that an individually controlled account cannot resemble the enumerated plans. The holding concerns the supplied ten-percent structure. Lower or fixed penalties present different facts outside this question; they receive no invented categorical rule.

**R3 — Judgment and remaining administration.** Reverse the categorical denial and remand. The age and similar-plan requirements are satisfied; the lower courts apply the reasonably-necessary-for-support ceiling and any genuinely unresolved account-specific objection under the governing rules. Rule 4003(c) retains the objector's burden to show the claimed exemption improper, while identification of the account and statutory claim remains necessary. The turnover direction cannot rest on the rejected taxed-access ground. Eligibility does not by itself exempt the entire balance.
