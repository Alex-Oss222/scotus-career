## Reconciled propositions and application

**J1 — Vacatur as fact: all eight.** A state order vacating the particular conviction used to enhance a federal sentence is a fact supporting the prisoner's challenge under event-date section 2255 paragraph 6(4). Its issuance changes the predicate's status; it is not merely a later judicial opinion stating general law. The Government's old-facts-only approach would start time before the needed state order exists and undermine the proper-channel route Custis and Daniels preserve. A timely motion proceeds under the otherwise applicable requirements for relief from the current sentence.

**J2P — Procurement and discovery: O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.** Notice of the state vacatur starts the one-year paragraph 6(4) period when the prisoner acted with reasonable diligence to obtain it after entry of the enhanced federal judgment and to learn of the order. Entry of that judgment establishes the federal stake; reasonable diligence does not demand the fastest conceivable action. The express diligence condition has work to do where the prisoner initiates the process producing the new fact. State collateral rules protect state judgments, but do not replace the distinct federal interest in timely challenges to federal sentences.

The adopted rule includes all three qualifications: (1) after diligent initiation, delay in state proceedings not attributable to the prisoner does not defeat the later trigger; (2) if facts underlying the state challenge were not discoverable through diligence until after federal judgment, diligence is measured from their discoverability, and diligent pursuit then permits time to run from notice of the resulting vacatur; and (3) even a prisoner who did not act diligently may use paragraph 6(4) by showing that the federal motion was filed within one year of the notice date diligent pursuit would have produced. These are parts of the rule, not optional equitable exceptions.
