## 3. The common substantive determination is now complete

Dicks confines the aggravator to an unnecessarily torturous, conscienceless or pitiless killing. Pritchett and Melson give the construction concrete content through predeath torture, sufficiently grave depravity, conscious suffering and opportunity to resist. The state opinion in Cone described multiple crushing blows, defensive wounds and resistance, noninstantaneous deaths and the terror of the sequential killings. That is an actual application of the narrowed category, not simply repetition of the statutory adjectives. The parties' references to Pritchett and Melson corroborate the setting; they do not substitute for what the state court itself did.

All eight therefore reject the Sixth Circuit's contrary inference. The result rests on the actual state explanation, not a categorical presumption that every court always applies every relevant precedent. Early and Visciotti require substantive review of the whole adjudication, and Sochor permits a specific judicial narrowing application without a prescribed citation. Proffitt and Godfrey are eligible federal authorities at the direct-review cutoff. All eight accept that the state construction and its demonstrated judicial application satisfy the narrowing inquiry presented here.

Section 2254(b)(2) separately permits denial notwithstanding nonexhaustion. It removes any suggestion that a court must first decide the competing state previously-determined and federal-default accounts before rejecting a theory that fails on a sufficient merits ground. It does not compel denial, prove that the state decided every asserted federal issue, or enlarge the merits proposition proved by the record. Those distinct propositions remain separate in the final common ground.

## 4. What actually differs from the historical decision

The historical Court reversed and remanded without a recorded dissent. It did more than reject a citation requirement. There is a concrete changed doctrinal premise. The operative simulated Lambrix does not supply that proposition. Its binding mandate rejects automatic cure from the judicial-independence label. The unchanged historical record makes that changed premise material. The jury itself found the heinousness aggravator and used the aggravators in choosing death. The reported definitions include extreme wickedness, outrageous vileness and cruelty expressed through high pain, indifference, enjoyment of suffering or pitilessness. The later state court's concrete discussion establishes the crime's fit within the authoritative narrower construction. Its discussion of what the jury could consider is not, by itself, a finding that the jury used that narrower rule or a separate determination that a broader unconstitutional branch had no effect. Cone's asserted objection concerns the jury's guidance and the adequacy of appellate correction; the primary opinion describes that claim. It is not a new Ring theory or a hypothetical concern invented after the frozen modeling.

The six need not—and do not—infer an actual constitutional defect merely because the formulations differ. Nor do they decide that the quoted definitions exhaust every sentence of the charge.
