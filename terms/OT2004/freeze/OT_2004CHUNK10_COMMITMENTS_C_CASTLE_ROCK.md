# Preserved baseline section

## 101. Town of Castle Rock v. Gonzales — No. 04-278

### Components and adversarial comparison

Component A is whether Colorado's actual scheme and individualized order secure a substantive service entitlement protected by due process. Component B is whether the municipal complaint may proceed on a deprivation-without-process theory, with policy, causation and relief separately proved. The individual-officer and substantive-due-process dispositions are outside this review.

The statutory duties concern every reasonable enforcement means and arrest, or pursuit of a warrant when arrest is impractical, on probable cause of violation and service or actual notice. The permanent order permits alternate-weekend and summer parenting time, a midweek dinner arranged with reasonable notice, and pickup for parenting time. The allegation of taking without notice must be assessed against those permissions; contact is not itself a forbidden act. Printed reverse-side notice is not a decree directed to nonparty police. The statute supplies any police obligation.

The service-entitlement path distinguishes discretion over means from discretion to refuse the legally prescribed response to a named beneficiary. Its strongest objection is Doe v. District of Columbia's unanimous separation of mandatory enforcement steps from substantive property: the commanded act governs officials' treatment of another person rather than necessarily conferring a privately held benefit. The no-entitlement path therefore cannot rest on the word reasonable alone, or on a general right-to-protection label. It must examine the specific arrest-or-warrant direction and explain why those duties do not establish constitutional property.

The United States' separate process path assumes an interest and argues that repeated opportunities to supply the material facts were enough. Its strongest support is Connecticut Doe: process need not supply a substantive exception or an irrelevant hearing. Its strongest objection is that an alleged policy preventing genuine consideration can make repeated speaking opportunities empty. Mathews concerns the useful process actually required, not the correct tactical decision or successful protection. The complaint cannot be treated as proof either that municipal refusal occurred or that an eligibility determination actually took place. Zinermon and the municipal-responsibility rules preserve proof of foreseeable policy-caused deprivation without converting every unauthorized error into municipal liability.

### Resulting scope and genuine differences

Five Associates reverse (O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas); three affirm survival (Stevens, Ginsburg, Breyer). The reversal ground is absence of the claimed substantive property interest; the mandatory duties are not denied. Souter's ground can join an opinion centered on the substantive-interest distinction. A separate concurrence is warranted only if a proposed opinion instead treats retained implementation discretion as independently disproving any possible entitlement. The three affirming Justices can share the bounded service and complaint-survival ground; their prior CAPTA position alone would not suffice without the present order and prescribed substantive response.

Affirmance permits proof of the qualifying circumstances, actual deprivation and usable process, attributable policy or custom, fault where required, causal injury and appropriate remedy. Nominal relief for proved denial of process differs from compensation for deaths; neither follows at this stage. Reversal terminates this federal municipal procedural theory without changing the separate dispositions outside review. The supported conduct-time statutory core suffices; the later-labeled compilation is not certified wholesale as the 1999 code. No dispositive unresolved Colorado construction has been identified that requires certification on these grounds.

## 101. Castle Rock

Green Party's constitutional protection of actual political association does not establish property whenever the government supplies a useful service. Its protected interest, concrete enrollment burden and electoral standard are independent of whether Colorado conferred the asserted police-enforcement entitlement. Halbert likewise supplies a constitutional advocacy right in initial appellate merits review; it does not convert a mandatory enforcement step into property or decide adequate emergency police process. Eulitt distinguishes the actual service purchased and representative standing, rather than prescribing the due-process classification of every statutory service.

Orff unanimously keeps an asserted substantive entitlement separate from consent to the suit. That distinction is consistent with requiring a genuine due-process interest before municipal attribution or remedy, but its sovereign-consent holding neither negates state-created property nor decides Colorado's particular obligations. Allapattah, Kelo, Mid-Con and Mayle alter no statutory trigger, individualized-order qualification, procedural-interest boundary or municipal-liability predicate here.

The five Associates denying the asserted property interest retain that sufficient ground; the three recognizing a bounded service interest retain their complaint-survival and practical-process positions. No Justice shifts because another constitutional doctrine protects a different government service. The alternative adequate-process argument and the material distinction between pleaded nonconsideration and a merely incorrect outcome remain as modeled.
