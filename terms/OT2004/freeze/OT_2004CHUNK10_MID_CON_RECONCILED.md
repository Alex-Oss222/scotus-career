## M1 — Affirmance rule, application and limits

Section 14504 preempts state registration obligations that concern the federal system's specified subjects and exceed the federal standards for those subjects. Those subjects are registration of evidence of federal operating authority, proof of insurance, the local service agent and the associated allocated fee system. The statute does not displace an independent state registration program on another subject merely because the carrier operates interstate, the State gathers vehicle information or requires a decal.

Both sentences of subsection (b) refer to registration measured against the Secretary's standards. Subsection (a) defines those standards by their forms-and-procedures function, and subsection (c) identifies the particular federal registration system. Reading the same registration terminology in that setting gives the excess-obligation sentence real work without treating it as a separate prohibition on every registration demand. The restrictions are substantive: a State cannot use another label to impose excess obligations within the covered subject.

The narrower opposing M2 argument is addressed directly. It reads the carrier's interstate description as limiting the reason for registration and then treats the standards as the ceiling on all obligations within that class. M1 instead reads that description as identifying the transportation regulated by the federal system. The statute does not state that a demand outside the defined subjects becomes covered whenever interstate operation is its trigger. M2's allocation of functions between subsections (b) and (c) does not overcome the common subject identified by the definition, standards and repeated language. Rejection of M2 does not depend on pretending that it is the broader M3 rule.

The former system and its replacement confirm the defined-subject reading: the change centralizes the relevant filings and fee collection and ties the fee to insurance proof rather than federal-permit filing. *Yellow* supplies the fee-system context and enforces the benchmark within the actual delegated system. Statutory history corroborates the reading of the enacted text; the charge's age and a general preference for state regulation do not supply an exemption by themselves. The national system's burden-reduction objective does not become a universal carrier-fee limit.

Michigan's section 478.2(2) requires information identifying the vehicle and payment for its separate program, not the listed federal permit, insurance or agent filings. The record does not establish that the carrier must pay the $100 to complete federal registration. The apparent waiver of Michigan's otherwise available $10 fee creates a subsidiary administrative connection but does not convert the different obligation into a federal-registration charge. This is not a stipulated finding that every aspect of the systems is unconnected; it is the application supported by the actual requirements and the absence of demonstrated conditioning. An unsupported legal linkage cannot be supplied by the waiver alone.

The State's disputed claim that its two $100 provisions form a neutral general scheme need not be resolved under M1. Neutrality is not the adopted test for subsection (b)'s reach, and no finding of equal actual coverage or treatment is made. The lower court's categorical reasoning that regulatory purpose necessarily makes a fee nonregistration is not adopted: registration may serve regulatory purposes. The judgment is sustained because the actual obligation concerns a different subject.

**Remedy:** Affirm the judgment rejecting this federal statutory claim. Section 478.2(2)'s charge remains enforceable against this challenge. No refund amount, exact regulatory cost, dormant-Commerce-Clause ruling or disposition of the distinct local-haul challenge follows. No additional factfinding is ordered merely to search for a legal linkage the supplied record does not establish.

## M2 — Vacatur-and-remand rule and substantive separate position

O'Connor and Kennedy directly join the following position. Subsection (b) reaches registration obligations imposed on a motor carrier because it provides interstate transportation in the State. The first sentence recognizes compliance with the federal standards; the second prohibits additional registration obligations in that interstate-specific category. A general neutral registration rule incidentally affecting interstate carriers is not brought within subsection (b) by their mere inclusion, but applicable subsection (c) restrictions still govern the listed federal-system demands.

On this reading, subsection (a) defines what the federal standards require, not every additional obligation that subsection (b) forbids a State to impose. The first subsection (b) sentence identifies the class of interstate-registration requirements and the allowed subset; the second makes the standards a ceiling. A State cannot escape that ceiling by seeking different information or using the proceeds for safety regulation. Michigan's information, fee and decal amount to registration in the ordinary sense even if they serve a regulatory purpose. The lower court's regulatory-purpose explanation therefore cannot decide the federal question.

M1's strongest objection is that the statute describes a carrier and its transportation without expressly saying that registration must be imposed because of that status. M2 answers that the description naturally identifies the category of registration the provision governs, while the generally phrased excess-obligation prohibition cannot be confined to the already listed federal proofs without adding another subject-matter exemption. The general-neutral qualification avoids turning the Act into universal immunity from ordinary tax and licensing registration. Separate subsection (c) commands protect the listed federal registration process, so neutrality under subsection (b) does not authorize excessive covered fees or multiple demands for the federal proofs.

The older regulatory system cannot manufacture an ambiguity that defeats the replacement's operative wording. The lack of confirmatory legislative statements does not add a subject-matter limitation absent from the enacted command. *Yellow*'s enforcement of the federal benchmark is compatible with this reading and its preservation of unrelated regulation does not decide relatedness. These are independent statutory grounds for withholding assent to M1, not a demand to treat every carrier charge as part of the $10 fee system.

The proper outcome under M2 is not immediate invalidation of the $100 charge. Section 478.2(2) expressly refers to wholly interstate vehicles, but Michigan contends that its operation with section 478.2(1) creates a common neutral scheme for Michigan-plated for-hire vehicles. The carriers dispute that characterization. Neither equal dollar amounts nor subsection (2)'s wording alone resolves the legally relevant combined operation. Because the lower court used a different, insufficient categorical ground, the case should return for the disputed statutory characterization and any application needed under the proper rule.

**Remedy:** Vacate and remand to determine whether the combined provisions actually impose an additional registration requirement because of interstate-carrier status, keeping subsection (c)'s applicable independent commands distinct. The remand does not seek an invented condition tying the $100 payment to federal registration, does not presume the scheme either neutral or discriminatory, and does not order invalidation, repayment or any particular sum. The existing state provision relevant to the combination may be examined without importing the same-day companion's judgment or constitutional analysis.

**Separate-writing function:** A dissent must explain this statutory construction, its objection to both the lower regulatory-label rationale and M1, the general-neutral qualification, the continued role of subsection (c), and the exact characterization remand. The identity of its author is for assembly; historical authorship is not imported. Both O'Connor and Kennedy join the complete foregoing position and remedy.

## Distinct subsection (c) commands and scope of assent

All eight agree that Michigan cannot evade the commands governing the listed federal-registration demands by calling a program regulatory or neutral. For this participating-State case, the designated registration State's authority over the listed proofs, direct allocated fees and service-agent information must be respected. Subsection (c)(2)(B)(iii) limits what the Secretary's standards may require concerning identification of particular vehicles; it is not an independent declaration that every state decal is unlawful. Subsection (B)(iv)'s insurance-proof fee system retains its November 15, 1991 benchmark and $10 ceiling. Subsection (B)(v) separately forbids authorizing charges for filing and maintaining evidence of federal registration. Subsection (C) addresses fees under this section inconsistent with that fee system. These commands are neither interchangeable nor a universal $10 cap.

The refreshed model stated some subsection (c) propositions across all States. Final common assent is confined to the demands and participating-State setting necessary here; it does not use an ancillary rule about States excluded from participation to manufacture a common subsection (b) rationale. The historical dissent discussed a participation-sensitive account of subsection (c), while the historical majority rejected its division of statutory work. That background is retained as comparator evidence, not silently converted into a new shared holding. The disagreement over subsection (b)'s category remains explicit. This scope refinement changes no neutral fact or remedy alternative and requires no new source premise.
