## What the earlier wording may and may not establish

The earlier statement that Gonzalez did not pursue further review after the April 2000 denial must not be used to find that he made no attempt. The accurate record distinguishes the alleged, unaccepted rehearing submission; the disputed reason for its return; his asserted resulting misunderstanding; and the absence of an established certiorari filing. The neutral phrase that no external impediment is established remains true only as a description of the present proof status. It cannot imply that no allegation of such an impediment exists.

Nor may the attempted-rehearing allegation be converted into a finding that ordinary review was diligently exhausted, that the clerk committed an error, that Gonzalez used the correct appeal number, that a timely corrected filing was impossible, or that further review legally remained available. The material unknowns include the actual submission and return, the correctness and content of the information provided, its effect on subsequent efforts, and any lawful opportunity to correct the problem. Neither account resolves those points by assertion alone.

The earlier appellate sequence and the later post-Artuz interval are separate periods. Proof explaining a returned April 2000 rehearing submission would not automatically explain the eight-to-nine-month interval following Artuz. Conversely, a later delay does not retrospectively prove that no earlier effort occurred. The model should identify the actual importance assigned to each period and avoid relying on a single undifferentiated label of abandonment.

## Existing law and alternative applications

Abdur'Rahman v. Bell already supplies the process-only classification rule and separately requires reasonable timing, extraordinary circumstances, assessment of omitted ordinary review and the effect of an intervening legal development. That rule remains unchanged. The supplied motion targets the federal limitations barrier rather than a merits rejection of the plea claim. The attempted-rehearing dispute affects the Rule 60 inquiry, not that classification, unless some independently supported new merits demand is identified.

Pace requires attention to reasonable diligence in the actual circumstances and distinguishes true state filing conditions from claim-relief bars. It does not establish a fixed monthly bar to Rule 60 relief or decide Gonzalez's original tolling question. Rhines concerns retention of a pending timely petition and does not replace Rule 60(b)(6)'s exceptional-relief requirements or impose a later protective-filing obligation retroactively. Lovasz is evidence of the tolling argument Gonzalez presented; its citation alone supplies no Supreme Court holding or entitlement to a certificate.

Two disposition paths remain supported for independent assessment:

1. **Deny extraordinary relief on the showing even while considering the alleged attempt.** The model can determine that the procedural legal change, the asserted rehearing episode, the later interval and the circumstances shown do not together establish Rule 60(b)(6) relief. It must state whether it assumes the disputed account for this analysis or regards the unresolved fact as immaterial to the sufficient ground. It cannot silently resolve the credibility dispute against Gonzalez or premise denial on a finding that he never sought rehearing. If later delay is decisive, its actual significance requires explanation rather than a categorical elapsed-time rule.
2. **Return the motion for ordinary discretionary adjudication.** If the prior categorical successive-application approach prevented a proper Rule 60 assessment and the disputed review episode could materially affect that assessment, a remand may permit evaluation of the complete circumstances and resolution of the specific material factual dispute. Such a remand recognizes neither extraordinary circumstances nor a valid excuse as established. It need not order a particular factfinding procedure absent a source requiring one and grants no writ or automatic reopening.

The strongest objection to the first path is that a legally mistaken threshold classification may have prevented consideration of a concrete asserted attempt to use ordinary review, making a categorical lack-of-diligence premise unsafe. The strongest objection to the second is that a factual dispute does not require remand if the motion fails on an independent sufficient Rule 60 ground even accepting the movant's version. The model must identify the actual relation between the dispute and its chosen sufficient ground.

An eventual grant of reopening would require its own complete Rule 60 showing; the allegation alone does not support automatic relief. Even lawful reopening would decide neither the first petition's timeliness under the governing filing rules nor the validity of the guilty plea. A remand must preserve those independent questions and applicable certificate requirements.
