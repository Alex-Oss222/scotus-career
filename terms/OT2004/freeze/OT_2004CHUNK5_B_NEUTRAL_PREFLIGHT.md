## Muehler: validated predicates

The warrant concerned weapons and gang evidence at a multi-occupant residence; officers expected a potentially armed gang member. Mena and three other occupants were restrained in a garage with one or two guards during the search. These facts support initial detention and substantial initial safety measures, but do not resolve every later interval of force.

Plaintiff's evidence includes prompt securing of the premises, a calm operational signal, compliance, requests to remove uncomfortable cuffs, and continued restraint despite a tactical plan contemplating release of nonsuspects after control. Departmental practice is evidence of circumstances, not the constitutional standard. The defense relies on the continuing weapons search, multiple adult detainees, limited guards, and risks that do not disappear simply because a person is compliant. Objective reasonableness remains distinct from whose expert preferred a tactic.

The special verdict found both excessive-force seizure and excessive-duration seizure against Muehler and Brill. It separately rejected challenged premises-search claims against each and identified entry/property claims against other officers. The compensatory questions awarded damages if either seizure violation was proved; they did not allocate an amount between force and duration. The overall judgment was $60,000, including punitive damages. These general findings are not subsidiary jury findings on every disputed minute, risk, or individual direction. Neither the rejected search claims nor claims against other officers are revived by correcting the appellate questioning rule.

The record supports a preserved contention that detention continued after search completion, including the ending video sequence and Brill's testimony. Precise duration and lawful basis remain disputed. The Ninth Circuit did not dispose of that alternative ground. Immigration questions were not a separate affirmative jury verdict. The statement that no added detention occurred is an advocate's characterization, not an established oral concession here. No new purse-search ruling may replace the presented questioning question.

Summers gives execution-related authority to detain; Graham and Holland separately govern force. Holland permits neither an automatic inference that every risk ended on submission nor an inference that initial justification lasts until the final search task. Its direct firearm threats to controlled children without a safety reason are not factually identical to cuffs on adult occupants during a weapons search. Brosseau retains the particular conduct-date immunity inquiry and obvious applications. Caballes is limited to a contraband-only examination under lawful access and restraint. It does not make every unrelated investigative intrusion permissible if it consumes no time. Ordinary information requests without additional detention or compelled intrusion require no separate suspicion solely because of their subject. Added restraint requires its own justification.

Remaining work is judicial application, not a source-access blocker: review the verdict under the correct force rule; resolve or remand the supported post-search ground; apply immunity to exact surviving conduct under February 1998 law; determine the effect of valid and invalid theories on compensatory and punitive relief. No automatic new trial or cancellation of all damages follows from correcting the appellate legal rule.

## Rancho: validated predicates

The additional claim seeks section 1983 damages and section 1988 fees for violation of the Telecommunications Act's local siting restrictions. The direct statutory action already produced permit relief. The supported violation concerned the written-decision/substantial-evidence requirement of 47 U.S.C. section 332(c)(7)(B)(iii). The lower court rejected the effective-prohibition theory and did not decide the unreasonable-discrimination theory; no new merits ruling on those theories is needed to decide the added remedial route.

Section 332(c)(7)(B)(v) expressly permits an adversely affected person to commence a competent-court action within thirty days of a final action or failure inconsistent with the subparagraph and directs expedited decision. Its separate radio-frequency clause permits an FCC petition for the violation described in clause (iv); these provisions are not conflated. Section 601(c)(1)'s preservation of other law does not itself answer whether a newly created TCA entitlement carries an additional inconsistent enforcement route. Loss of the asserted section 1983 predicate defeats section 1988 fees resting only on that predicate, without deciding every other possible fee authority.

Blessing separates a particular right from actual remedial foreclosure. Bruneau, joined by all eight Associates on the statutory point, rejects foreclosure based merely on administrative oversight plus an implied action. Gonzaga rejects a remedy-intent formula and places the exclusion showing on the defendant. The available narrow ground is this enactment's express, personal, time-limited and expedited process and the conflict a parallel ordinary section 1983 action would create. An express remedy is not a universal dispositive presumption. An independent constitutional right is distinct and no such merits claim is resolved here. Precise availability of damages under the direct TCA action need not be decided to identify the incompatible additional route.

## Brown: validated predicates

The original penalty charge used factor (k)'s crime-centered language. Payton offered extensive postcrime conversion and prison-adjustment evidence. Defense counsel proposed broader language and a rehabilitation factor. The judge privately explained that the existing catchall was broad, declined modifications, and let counsel argue competing readings.

Before the jury, the prosecutor repeatedly said the enumerated mitigation factors did not cover postcrime events, including conversion. This was more than an argument about weight. The defense objected, sought correction, and forcefully argued that the evidence could be weighed. The court gave a generic reminder about arguments and evidence rather than an express legal correction, then repeated the all-evidence instruction and statutory factors. The prosecutor also attacked sincerity and argued the balance even if conversion were considered. Both the legal exclusion and the alternative weight argument belong in the analysis.

The constitutional question is whether the whole charge and trial created a reasonable likelihood that jurors could not give effect to relevant mitigation. Boyde's actual context included no comparable clear prosecutorial legal exclusion; its whole-context method and the nonconclusive significance of admitted evidence remain central. Skipper establishes postcrime prison adjustment's relevance without making every assertion of religion automatically mitigating or directing weight. State-decision reasonableness under section 2254(d)(1) is separate from this Court's best constitutional reading. Later Penry II or Bell cannot supply nonexistent substantive law at the 1992 cutoff. Their current procedural rules do not alter that cutoff.

The merits application followed AEDPA's effective date; Garceau governs despite earlier preliminary proceedings. Under governing Brecht, O'Neal, and Penry II in the authorized law, a proved preserved constitutional error places on the State the burden to show beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the sentence. The historical substantial-and-injurious-effect rule is not imported. Aggravation and sincerity belong in actual harmlessness review, not an invented default or an assumption that no mitigation was offered. A Justice rejecting the statutory gateway need not decide harmlessness. A Justice crossing it can require correct full-record harmlessness review without asserting that trial materials are unavailable.

## Black Political Task Force: validated predicates and discrepancy

The three-judge court's final June 21, 2004 judgment adjudicated Count I under section 2 and enjoined elections under the challenged 2001 plan in the seventeen Suffolk House districts. Counts II and III were dismissed. Replacement Plan C had already been approved for section 2 and equal-protection compliance and enacted unchanged; remaining 2004 candidate-relief questions were moot. Review of the continuing injunction is live. No new six-week legislative-redrawing command is necessary in March 2005. Removing a federal predicate would not repeal the enacted replacement map.

The full lower merits opinion and final judgment were reviewed. The court found race-based movement of six predominantly Black precincts out of the 11th and 12th districts into the already heavily Black 6th district, despite contrary population needs. It found a feasible compact alternative, cohesion, and sufficiently persistent majority bloc voting. It evaluated cross-racial success and incumbency, discounted examples for identified reasons, and considered exogenous and same-race contests. Candidate race is not automatically voter preference. Findings receive proper review; they do not bind this Court to an erroneous legal yardstick.

A material discrepancy exists between the totality discussion and current De Grandy, Valdespino, and Martinez law. The lower court expressly excluded crossover opportunity districts from its numerical proportionality comparison, relied on a majority-district count, and described totality in terms tending toward a presumption after Gingles. Current law requires actual opportunity, gives proportionality relevant but nonconclusive weight, forbids maximization and racial seat entitlements, and does not convert necessary conditions into a sufficient presumption. White officeholders may be minority-preferred. Aggregate opportunity cannot automatically offset a proved local injury.

Two genuine paths remain. Affirmance can rest only on independently supported actual packing and unequal-opportunity findings after rejecting the erroneous ratio and presumptive language; feasibility and percentages alone cannot do that work. Vacatur can require totality reassessment with proved crossover opportunities counted and without a maximization presumption, preserving supported subsidiary findings. Remedy must track the ground. Thomas and Scalia's sustained section 2 noncoverage position supplies a distinct reversal/dismissal ground, not a statutory-totality finding. Discarded constitutional claims are not restored without a separately presented basis.
