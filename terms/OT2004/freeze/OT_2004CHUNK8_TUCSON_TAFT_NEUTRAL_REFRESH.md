**Necessary care.** Taft's Part II directly holds that a State may not enforce an abortion-method prohibition when competent individualized medical judgment establishes that substitution creates a material risk of death or serious health harm and the prohibited method is necessary for timely, safe and suitable care. Supported necessary-care applications include grave medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. Unsupported preference and marginal comparative advantage are insufficient; professional disagreement alone does not defeat supported necessity; usable protection must operate before catastrophic deterioration. The rule protects necessary care before and after viability and keeps the separate criminal burdens distinct from civil constitutional proof.

**Complete previability framework.** Part III and its additional mandate expressly reaffirm all six Casey steps after actual coverage and necessary-care protection are addressed. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Meaningful interference is a concrete nontrivial additional burden, not automatic invalidity; a substantial obstacle need not make care absolutely impossible. Below the interference threshold, independent baseline and purpose protections remain.
