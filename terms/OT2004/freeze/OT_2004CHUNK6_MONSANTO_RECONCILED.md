## Common rules and judgment components

**M1 — Patent scope and the particular contract breach: all eight.** A one-season license of seed does not, merely through the farmer's ownership of that seed or the plant-variety saved-seed tradition, authorize production and use of additional seed embodying the assumed valid utility-patent claims. The presented no-replanting promise concerns that additional protected seed and does not on this ground enlarge the patent to unpatented subject matter. J. E. M. preserves concurrent utility protection and confines certificate-specific exceptions to their own statutory right; Asgrow supplies no general utility-patent saved-seed defense. The conceded saving and replanting violate the one-season agreement, and the breach determination and rejection of these categorical defenses stand, subject to any legally established consequence of a separately preserved enforcement defense.

The strongest contrary argument derives a replanting liberty from purchased seed's reproductive nature or willingness to pay the technology fee. Assumed coverage of later-generation seed distinguishes ownership of the purchased articles from a license to produce protected additional articles. The fee offer itself supplies no reproduction authorization. This is a confined holding concerning the presented promise; the agreement's research, breeding and distribution terms have not all been adjudicated. The partial contract judgment does not become a final judgment on unfinished patent causes. The validity issue was not preserved for this appeal, and assumed coverage is not an affirmative merits determination of validity.

**M2L — General independent-tying rule: all eight at this level.** A restriction's relation to a patent does not conclusively defeat an independently unlawful Sherman Act section 1 tie. A claimant must establish distinct products under actual separate demand, conditioning, sufficient tying-market power, the requisite commerce, competitive injury and the causal prerequisites for the relief sought. Patent ownership or a product label neither establishes the necessary elements nor automatically merges separately demanded products. Lawful statutory exclusion and returns to invention retain substantial justificatory force. A mere request for a differently priced reproduction license does not establish an independent tie.

All eight permit that limited common proposition. They do not all join the conclusion that this record requires further inquiry. Kodak and Jefferson Parish supply the actual-demand and ordinary tying elements. CSU demonstrates the coexistence of intellectual-property rights and an independent antitrust prohibition; its section 2 holding is extended only to the legal rejection of conclusive scope immunity in section 1. The distinction between that shared legal rule and its disputed application must remain visible.

**M3 — Existing damages remand: all eight.** Preserve the vacatur of the $780,000 liquidated sum and the existing remand for actual damages. The lower judgment's Missouri rule permits reasonableness through anticipated harm or actual harm; Monsanto did not defend this grossly disproportionate sum through the actual-loss alternative. The uniform 120 multiplier based on purchased rather than saved/replanted bags lacked the required relationship to this breach's compensable harm. This mandate retains the lower damages ruling; it does not create a generalized federal liquidated-damages test.

Actual damages proceed under the adjudicated liability, any independently established defense or offset, the proper saved/replanted measure and the prohibition on double recovery. Patent damages and requested injunctive relief remain in their unfinished proceedings under their own prerequisites. The question concerning a forecast for supplying or selling saved seed to third parties is outside this replanting disposition and remains as the lower judgment left it.

## Divided application of the independent-tying rule

**M2R — Vacatur and remand: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** The appellate patent-scope ground does not decide whether the presented tying claim satisfies its independent elements. The court acknowledged the demand inquiry and expressly declined to resolve it; this Court should not replace that missing inquiry with an invented conclusive finding about product identity. Vacate the antitrust rejection and remand under the correct rule, leaving ordinary evidentiary and summary-judgment burdens in place. A supported failure of proof may yield renewed summary judgment. Any effect of a proven wrong on contract enforcement requires its own preserved legal ground.

These five answer the reproduction-license objection by accepting it as a substantial merits defense rather than a categorical substitute for the declined demand inquiry. The express step from CSU is that a patent-scope label cannot conclusively immunize a condition meeting the established section 1 offense, just as it cannot conclusively immunize independently unlawful monopoly maintenance. Kodak's section 1 rule supplies the relevant elements; section 2's elements are not transplanted. Trinko confirms separate offense and remedy predicates. Neither a compulsory license nor a present tie follows merely from removal of the legal immunity.

**M2A — Affirmance on a confined alternative ground: O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.** The actual theory seeks authorization to use patented second-generation seed while paying a technology fee rather than buying newly licensed seed. In these Justices' assessment, refusal to offer that reproduction transaction does not identify a distinct coercive acquisition or independently unlawful tie. The presented demand is for an ungranted right in the additional patented item, not a demonstrated requirement to buy an unpatented product as the condition of an otherwise licensed use. Affirm the antitrust rejection on that confined absence of an independent wrong.

They preserve liability for a genuine independent tie and do not adopt the lower court's universal scope immunity. They answer the remand group's strongest objection at the conduct level: reconsidering the product label or the claimant's preferred transaction does not cure the failure of the presented theory to identify the required wrong. Their Kodak concerns about proprietary-product dependence, CSU refusal analysis and Trinko independent-offense objection support that result. They do not join M2R's conclusion that the record requires the further demand inquiry.
