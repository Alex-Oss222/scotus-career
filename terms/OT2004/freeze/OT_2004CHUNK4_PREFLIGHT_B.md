**Verified record and corrections:** There are 96 distinct servitudes, six mineral conveyances and eleven federal land acquisitions. Nebo Oil concerned one servitude associated with about 800 acres, within a larger 24,943.93-acre acquisition. The earlier decree contains broad findings, but its operative decree vests the minerals in the lands described in that complaint. The United States acknowledges preclusion as to that adjudicated servitude. The present dispute concerns the other 95 distinct servitudes and their individual histories. The district court extended preclusion to roughly 180,000 acres without deciding the separate prescription evidence. The Fifth Circuit rejected claim identity, rejected actual prior litigation of the choice-of-law issue, and alternatively relied on changed controlling principles. Its conclusion also holds the other 95 servitudes subject to the ten-year nonuse prescription provisions, while remanding which have actually prescribed. This distinction must remain explicit: the judgment settles applicability of prescription but does not find universal nonuse or award every disputed mineral right to the Government.

The second summary-judgment motion claimed continuing drilling on five servitudes covering nearly seventy percent of the acreage; the Government conceded sufficient operations on some servitudes. The district court dismissed that motion as moot because of its preclusion ruling. The Fifth Circuit returned that factual work. It did not reach the fee cross-appeal because the preclusion reversal removed prevailing-party status. No new stand-alone takings question is supplied by the stated grant; the Government's objection that such a claim was not presented remains a party procedural assertion, not an additional adjudicated issue.

**Entering doctrine:** Semtek's unanimous Scalia opinion distinguishes a federal judgment's source, rendering-court effect and external preclusion; its ordinary state-law incorporation holding addresses diversity judgments, not this federal-question decree. Plaut protects the earlier completed judgment, with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas joining its full finality ground, Breyer accepting a narrower judgment ground, and Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg dissenting on the statutory-entitlement theory. None of those published positions makes an unadjudicated parcel part of a final judgment. New Hampshire v. Maine addresses judicial estoppel and does not collapse it into claim or issue preclusion. Surviving pre-divergence authorities identified and applied in the official lower opinion include Nevada v. United States (same property right required before finality controls), Montana and Sunnen (actual issue identity and materially changed legal setting), Little Lake Misere (federal choice of law in federal acquisition), and Stauffer (distinguishing unchanged-law mutual issue preclusion).
