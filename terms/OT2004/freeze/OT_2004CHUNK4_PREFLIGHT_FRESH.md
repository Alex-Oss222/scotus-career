## Roper v. Simmons

### Validated neutral frame

The question is categorical capital eligibility for an offender who was seventeen at the offense. It concerns neither innocence nor a general proportionality rule for noncapital sentences. The supplied neutral packet establishes the Missouri state-postconviction posture, its replacement of the death sentence with life imprisonment without release eligibility, the underlying aggravated murder and youth mitigation, and the competing domestic-consensus and individualized-sentencing positions. This is not a federal habeas gateway case.

Atkins is controlling only for established intellectual disability. Its exact entering rule requires significantly impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations, and developmental onset, together with a reliable status determination. It does not supply an age rule, numerical intellectual cutoff, proof allocation, or automatic collateral remedy. Its reliance on objective domestic evidence, culpability, and legitimate punishment purposes makes the analogy relevant without silently overruling Stanford's age-specific rule. The lower court's obligation to obey controlling precedent and this Court's own authority to reconsider it are distinct questions; one does not predetermine the other.

The material paths are retention of the surviving age-specific eligibility rule or an express categorical change on the presented constitutional question. An individualized mitigation defect is not an alternate escape route unless independently supported and preserved. Any reversal must identify the state sentencing consequence it actually orders, without assuming that execution follows without further lawful process.

## Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma v. Leavitt

### Required neutral supplementation

The two proceedings and the annual claims must remain distinct:

| Docket | Claims identified in the federal merits submission | Judgment below |
|---|---|---|
| No. 02-1472 | Cherokee Nation, FY1997; Shoshone-Paiute Tribes, FY1996–1997 | Tenth Circuit affirmed judgment for the Government. |
| No. 03-853 | Cherokee Nation, FY1994–1996 | Federal Circuit affirmed the Board of Contract Appeals' ruling for the Nation. |

The source reports both initial or expanded program costs and Cherokee's ongoing-program costs. It does not permit treating every claim as an undifferentiated lump-sum funding dispute. The material enacted provision for initial or expanded contracts states that, of the funds provided, $7,500,000 shall remain available until expended for the Indian Self-Determination Fund, available for transitional costs of initial or expanded tribal contracts. The legal question whether this wording is a ceiling is separate from the effect of committee-report allocations for total support costs. The source reports an explicit overall “not to exceed” cap beginning in FY1998; these claims concern FY1994–1997, so that later text cannot silently become their original annual funding term.

Both clauses of then-§450j-1(b) must be addressed independently: provision of funds is subject to availability of appropriations, and the Secretary is not required to reduce funding for programs, projects, or activities serving a tribe to make funds available to another tribe or tribal organization. The second is permission not to reduce other tribal funding, not a command never to reduce it, and it cannot be omitted merely because a lump sum exceeded one claimant's demand. The Government's characterization of funds as unavailable and the Tribes' claim of sufficient available funds are competing legal positions, not agreed findings resolving statutory availability.

The compacts and annual funding agreements contain the availability qualification; the source also identifies adjustment language concerning congressional action in appropriations Acts or other laws affecting availability. The model agreement's promise is likewise expressly subject to appropriations availability. The statutory duty to fund reasonable and allowable support costs, nonduplication limit, actual bargain, authority, and these qualifications must be considered together.

Section 314 supplies another distinct dispute. Its operative language makes the amounts appropriated or earmarked in committee reports for the identified support costs the total amounts available for FY1994–1998. The Government asserts a retrospective limitation or ratification defeating additional recovery; the Tribes and Federal Circuit understand it as limiting future use of unspent appropriations rather than extinguishing accrued contractual obligations. This issue is separate from both the original annual text and the measure of a contract judgment. The submission contains an apparent public-law-number inconsistency; use the verified citation Pub. L. 105-277, §314, 112 Stat. 2681-288, rather than copying its inconsistent “105-227” reference.

### Law and alternative paths

Hercules requires an actual authorized undertaking; the jurisdictional statute supplies no payment promise. Winstar enforces the particular financial risk allocation and preserves actual conditions, causation and loss. Its four-Justice unmistakability account and three-Justice substantial-contractual-effect theory are not controlling holdings. Lincoln preserves discretion over an unearmarked lump sum absent an enforceable boundary; whether the additional contract/statutory undertaking supplies that boundary is the present question, not already answered by Lincoln.

The competing paths therefore require actual construction of the annual allocation text, both §450j-1(b) clauses, the agreements, and §314. A liability determination does not calculate damages, award every future support-cost shortfall, or direct the agency's entire program allocation. The judgments and remaining proceedings must be specified by docket and claim category.

## Tenet v. Doe

### Required neutral supplementation

The Does' espionage relationship, promised lifetime security, service, defection, citizenship, and interrupted support are allegations. The Government has neither confirmed nor denied the relationship. The pleaded relief includes interim monthly support pending CIA review, a declaration prescribing administrative review, and mandamus requiring basic support and regulations. The question is thus not confined to ordinary breach damages.

The district court dismissed the equal-protection count and denied a preliminary injunction while allowing estoppel and constitutional claims to continue; the Government's later summary-judgment request was also denied. The appealed orders were certified under §1292(b), the court of appeals accepted the Government's appeal and refused the Does' cross-petition. No neutral basis is supplied to reopen the dismissed equal-protection branch as an additional surviving merits claim.

The appellate majority's material grounds are stronger and more specific than a general preference for confidentiality: it regarded Totten as a contract-law rule; distinguished the requested fair internal process; relied on pseudonyms and minimal identifying disclosure; and treated Reynolds as requiring a formally invoked evidentiary privilege and judicial inquiry before dismissal. It noted the absence of a formal personal invocation by the Director. The lower dissent understood Totten as a categorical bar, separate from the evidentiary privilege, protecting secrecy of the engagement itself. These are opposing lower-court analyses, not authoritative resolution by this Court.

### Law, alternatives and limits

The neutral alternatives are a categorical rule for the agreement-dependent undertaking, without resolving its truth, or allowing the asserted process/estoppel claims to proceed under the narrower reading of Totten and the actual prerequisites of Reynolds. The latter must confront whether requested internal process can be adjudicated without establishing the alleged undertaking. The former must confront the precise distinction between a secret-agreement claim and an independently sourced constitutional or statutory claim. Neither route authorizes a universal bar for intelligence-agency defendants.

Harbury's actual-injury and distinct-remedy rules do not decide the covert-agreement or state-secrets question, recognize a new cause of action, or supply a waiver. An administrative complaint opportunity mentioned by the Government is not an established enforceable benefits remedy. The Government's use of the label “jurisdictional” for Totten does not itself settle the Court's characterization or priority over ordinary jurisdiction and waiver questions.

## Catholic Charities of Sacramento, Inc. v. Superior Court

### Adopted posture correction

Treat the constitutional merits as properly presented on March 2, 2005 under the binding Run instruction. Remove the packet's finality stop and its conditional “if jurisdiction is established” framing. The underlying dispute still arises from denial of preliminary relief, so the provisional record and relief requested remain material. Proper arrival supplies no finding of liability or entitlement to a permanent injunction.

### Statutory and factual corrections

The record describes insured group health coverage for 183 employees through Blue Shield and Kaiser. Section 1367.25 reaches covered contracts on its stated January 1, 2000 issuance/amendment/renewal/delivery conditions and excepts specialized health-service contracts. It requires a plan offering outpatient prescription benefits to cover a variety of approved prescription contraceptive methods; when none designated is medically appropriate to the patient's medical or personal history, another approved medically appropriate prescribed method must be covered. Covered spouses and nonspouse dependents receive the same outpatient prescription benefits.

The religious-employer exception requires all four conditions, not merely institutional religious affiliation: purpose of inculcating religious values; primarily employing adherents; primarily serving adherents; and the specified nonprofit tax-return status under §6033(a)(2)(A)(i) or (iii). An eligible employer may request omission of methods contrary to its tenets; a plan must supply the requested exclusion. The employer invoking it must give prospective enrollees written notice of excluded contraceptive services before enrollment. The separate preservation of coverage for noncontraceptive uses and contraception necessary to preserve an enrollee's life or health remains operative. The law does not require experimental or investigational treatment and preserves regulatory compliance authority.

Catholic Charities conceded that it satisfied none of the four exemption conditions. Its claim that dropping all prescription coverage conflicts with its faith is a claimed religious obligation to provide just wages and adequate benefits; it is not an independently established legal duty requiring this particular coverage. Its sincerity is not resolved by reclassifying charitable service as inherently secular.

The lower opinion describes Insurance Code §10123.196 as the corresponding rule for disability policies. Its complete text is not independently reproduced in this validation. Do not turn the opinion's statement that the two provisions are essentially the same into a finding that all their clauses and timing rules are identical; apply the actual insured-plan provision to the established facts and obtain separate exact text for a ruling dependent on a distinct insurance-policy clause.

### Material lower-court grounds and neutral forks

The majority sustained the coverage requirement as neutral employee-benefit regulation and the religious exception as a permissible accommodation. It rejected religious targeting, distinguished the denominational classification in Larson, and separately rejected the asserted speech/hybrid-rights route. It also found the law sufficient under strict scrutiny when addressing the state constitutional claim, without deciding that state law always required that standard. That is an alternative lower-court reason to examine, not a federal factual finding that ends scrutiny. Its justification focused on equal benefits for employees; absence of insurance for other populations does not necessarily involve the same regulated harm.

The separate concurrence questioned the inculcation criterion but regarded the unfulfilled fourth, tax-status criterion as independently dispositive for this applicant. This is a material remedial and independent-ground issue: invalidating only the first criterion does not automatically establish Catholic Charities' statutory eligibility. A proposed remedy must address which criteria are challenged, why the applicant obtains relief, and what severable coverage or exemption remains.

The dissent contested government classification of which functions of a religious organization count as religious, questioned whether the exemption and asserted secular exclusions meet neutrality/general-applicability requirements, and disputed the proof of necessity for denying a broader exemption. It noted the possibility of self-insured plans outside state insurance regulation. That claimed federal preemption boundary is not automatically a discretionary state exception or proof that Catholic Charities can readily self-insure; legal source, comparability, and actual feasibility require separate attention. The dissent also invoked distinct state constitutional protection, which this Court does not convert into a federal rule.

Under the controlling simulated law, objective targeting and appreciable comparable secular underinclusion remain distinct Lukumi grounds. Agostini applies one actual-governmental-function inquiry; independent Lemon prongs cannot be restored from the lower opinion. Larson's specific denominational rule and Amos's accommodation setting require their own predicates. Commack bars governmental theological choice without barring ordinary proof of objective statutory qualifications. Locke permits its confined ministry-degree funding exclusion, not a universal power to classify religious activity for all regulation.

The neutral paths must therefore compare sustaining the insured-benefit rule and bounded accommodation; finding a forbidden religious classification or governmental theological function; and finding a supported general-applicability defect requiring strict scrutiny. They must confront the strongest opposing statutory-function and employee-benefit arguments, the independent criteria, and the actual requested provisional relief. Neither the mere existence of an accommodation nor the mere presence of religious words supplies the answer.
