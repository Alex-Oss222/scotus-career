## Brosseau v. Haugen

Source: factual account at 543 U.S. 194, 195–197, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/543/194. The account expressly uses the plaintiff-favorable summary-judgment perspective. The shooting occurred February 21, 1999. Haugen had a felony no-bail warrant on drug and other offenses. Officers responded to a fight, and Haugen escaped into the neighborhood during their arrival. The search lasted approximately 30–45 minutes.

The officer directed two men to remain in their pickup on the street in front of the driveway. Haugen's girlfriend and her three-year-old daughter remained in a small car in the driveway. That car faced Haugen's Jeep. The Jeep was angled somewhat left, approximately four feet from the small car and twenty to thirty feet from the pickup. Haugen returned, entered the Jeep and locked its door. Brosseau thought he was retrieving a weapon. He was looking for keys. She ordered him out, broke the driver's window with her handgun, attempted to take the keys and struck his head with the handgun. He started the engine. As the Jeep started or shortly after it began moving, she moved back and left and fired through the rear driver's-side window at a forward angle, striking his back.

Brosseau described concern for officers she believed were nearby, the occupied vehicles and other possible citizens. That account is her stated perception, not a finding that an officer or occupant faced an imminent collision. Haugen accelerated, maneuvered through the small space without striking the occupied vehicles, crossed a neighboring lawn and stopped about half a block away after realizing he had been shot. He later pleaded guilty to felony eluding. His post-shot driving and that conviction must be distinguished from what Brosseau knew when she fired. State claims and claims against the municipality are distinct from the individual federal immunity question supplied for this event.

## Kansas v. Colorado

Source: published recitations of the Fourth Report and the parties' exceptions, 543 U.S. 86, 98–106, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/543/86. The historical Court's conclusions about these recitations are excluded.

The States agreed to use the H-I Model. It compares modeled flows with and without post-1949 well pumping and adjusts the resulting figures for usable rather than total flow. The Master proposed a rolling ten-year calculation: the first ten years determine the obligation in year eleven; years two through eleven determine the obligation in year twelve. Kansas requested annual calculation and invoked the Compact's restrictions on accumulated credits and debits and on depletion of usable availability.

The Master found that the model's annual projections of diversions could differ from observed diversions by as much as 22 percent, with error in both directions. Annual flow projections also showed substantial discrepancies. Over the longer historical period of 540 months, projected and observed diversions nearly matched. The Master found the model insufficiently accurate to determine compliance monthly or annually. Those are measurement findings, not an amendment of the Compact's substantive allocation.

Colorado's replacement program required junior users, especially post-1949 well users, to acquire replacement water or retire irrigated land with pre-1949 rights. Associations administered the transactions, reported monthly to the State Engineer and apportioned replacement credits. Kansas challenged parts of the program. The Master found compliance during 1997–1999 using Kansas's own figures. Kansas's distinct exception to use of that aggregate period depended on its objection to any measurement period longer than one year. The separate technical objections included model calibration, acreage, particular water credits and future monitoring.

The recommendation initially used Colorado Water Court determinations for replacement-plan credits, but expressly preserved Kansas's ability to invoke this Court's original jurisdiction on every matter essential to compact compliance at the state line. The Master explained that initial state proceedings could reduce inconsistent credit determinations affecting both senior Colorado users and Kansas. The remaining technical disputes were proposed for deferral where additional information was needed. These recitations supply the proposals and their factual basis; the neutral context must assess whether they justify acceptance, modification or further fact development.
