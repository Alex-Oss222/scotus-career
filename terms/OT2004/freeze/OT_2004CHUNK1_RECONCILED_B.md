## 6. San Diego v. Roe — December 6, 2004

Roe deliberately marketed the sexually explicit performance through police-associated presentation and his claimed law-enforcement status. Public sale, compensation and an off-duty setting do not themselves make that performance commentary on a matter of public concern. Nor does the lawful status of adult expression alone answer the distinct public-employment question. Connick supplies the contextual threshold; Pickering supplies balancing when that threshold is met. This expression does not require balancing merely because it was available to the public.

## 7. Theofel v. Farey-Jones — December 6, 2004

### Shared constructions and remand

Section 2510(17)(B) independently covers storage by an electronic communication service for backup protection of the communication. Delivery or recipient access does not terminate that coverage when the retained copy actually serves that function. The reference to the communication in clause (B) does not import clause (A)'s temporary-intermediate-storage condition. The competing remote-computing provisions retain work because not every remote computing service is an electronic communication service and not every retained message is stored for backup. Actual function must be shown; potential usefulness, permanent retention or a lone storage repository does not automatically establish it.

Section 2701(c)(1)'s provider-authorization exception does not automatically follow from physical disclosure when the requesting party consciously procures it through a materially deceptive claim of compulsory authority going to the essential nature of the access. Mere negligent misunderstanding, an ordinary process irregularity or collateral deception is insufficient under this rule. The February 17 amendment expressly rejects negligence-based constructive knowledge. The provider's ability to object does not turn deliberately induced permission into genuine authorization.

Konop's actual-user exception is a different provision, and its assumption of electronic storage did not decide the present definition. Its contemporaneous-acquisition holding does not eliminate independent stored-communication protection. Hartford Underwriters supports giving each enacted actor, access and remedy condition its own effect. The Court affirms rejection of the two categorical SCA dismissal grounds. Ordinary proceedings determine the qualifying storage, each access, the relevant mental state, remaining section 2707 predicates, applicable good-faith defenses and authorized relief. Separate Wiretap, computer-fraud, state-law and petitioning-immunity dispositions remain outside this grant.

## 9. KP Permanent Make-Up v. Lasting Impression — December 8, 2004

### Shared rule, application and remedy

The infringement claimant bears the burden of proving likely consumer confusion, including when the registration is incontestable. A defendant invoking section 1115(b)(4) must establish the defense's own conditions: use otherwise than as a mark, fairly and in good faith, only to describe the user's goods or services or their geographic origin. The defendant need not negate every likelihood of confusion. Some likely confusion can coexist with descriptive fair use; otherwise the affirmative defense would disappear exactly when an infringement case had been established.

The statutory allocation controls. Russello supports giving effect to Congress's inclusion of confusion in the infringement provision and omission of a no-confusion element from this defense. Park 'N Fly preserves incontestability subject to express defenses. Qualitex and TrafFix preserve source-identifying protection with independent statutory limits, without themselves deciding this defense. The common-law decisions concerning truthful descriptive use reinforce the distinction between some confusion and an unqualified monopoly on descriptive language. The rule rejects Lasting's categorical position while retaining fairness, good faith and nonmark use as real requirements.

The degree of confusion is not declared irrelevant to the actual statutory fairness inquiry, but no new factor catalogue, quantitative ceiling or accuracy-alone rule is adopted. Vacate and remand for application of the correct burdens to the actual presentation under ordinary summary-judgment rules. This does not restore KP's judgment merely because it asserted earlier use.

## 10. Kowalski v. Tesmer — December 13, 2004

### Majority ground and competing third-party route

## 11. Devenpeck v. Alford — December 13, 2004

### Shared rule and lawful remand

An arrest's objective probable-cause basis may rest on an offense different from, and not closely related to, the officer's announced offense, when facts actually known at arrest satisfy that offense's elements and the constitutional probable-cause standard. The legal label does not erase contemporaneously known facts; later-acquired evidence cannot create an earlier justification. Whren rejects subjective motive alone as a ground for defeating objective initial authority; Pringle requires a supported contextual inference. An offense-relationship test would make the same facts authorize one officer's seizure and invalidate another's solely because they named different statutes.

That correction does not itself validate the actual impersonation or obstruction theories, which the Ninth Circuit excluded rather than resolved. Reverse and remand for those applications and preserved defenses. The in-world Ricci and Atwater limits remain operative for any properly presented added station transport and confinement for a known fine-only offense. Initial probable cause is not blanket permission for every later restraint. Conduct-date immunity remains tied to November 22, 1997; later decisions cannot supply earlier notice.

## 12. Florida v. Nixon — December 13, 2004

### Shared constitutional rule and remand

When capital defense counsel adequately discusses a concession strategy with the accused, the accused neither agrees nor objects, and an actual trial with prosecution proof, jury adjudication and substantive defense advocacy remains, absence of express consent alone establishes neither deficient performance nor presumed prejudice. A courtroom concession does not itself enter a guilty plea. Boykin protects the rights surrendered by an actual plea; Brookhart concerns a materially abbreviated proceeding imposed contrary to the accused's trial choice. Nixon retained the trial's adjudicative protections, and counsel continued evidence and instruction advocacy and substantial penalty representation.

Strickland governs the actual investigation, consultation, professional choice and reasonable-probability prejudice. Bell v. Cone distinguishes particular choices within substantive advocacy from Cronic's complete failure to act as an adversary. Neither the strategy label nor strong guilt evidence establishes competent representation automatically. Williams rejects an extra fundamental-unfairness hurdle for ordinary prejudice. Reverse the automatic-ineffectiveness judgment and remand for the remaining ordinary claims. The trial-era professional standard controls the representation; section 2254's federal-habeas gate does not govern this section 1257 review. The case's express silent-client condition does not resolve a concession over an expressed contrary trial objective.
