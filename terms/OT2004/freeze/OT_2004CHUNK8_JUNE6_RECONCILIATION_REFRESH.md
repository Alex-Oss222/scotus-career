## Retained operative grounds, statutory boundaries and remedy

**R1 remains the necessary-inclusion ground.** The Government must identify the independently valid interstate controlled-substance regulation, the enacted cultivation/manufacture, distribution and possession class, and a genuine functional relationship. Courts determine those legal boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments within them. Inclusion must be reasonably adapted and integral to the interstate system. The CSA application remains supported by its actual control of the same substance's production, possession and authorized supply, with fungibility, substitution for regulated acquisition and practical differentiation supporting that direct mechanism. General health costs, comprehensiveness or demand substitution alone do not supply authority. An invalid underlying regime, outside-class application or nominal or pretextual relationship remains challengeable.

Lopez's connection-before-aggregation requirement and the actual Home Builders, Rancho and GDF necessary-inclusion holdings remain the controlling premises. GDF's unanimous current rule and application do not decide a different statute automatically: the separate CSA mechanism must still be established. That independent application was performed in the original reconciliation and is not weakened by any new decision. Cutter's reserved power questions provide no substitute route in either direction.

**R2 remains bounded by the actual class and enacted authorizations.** Locality, nonpayment, physician recommendation and state permission do not alone sever the direct substance-supply relationship or place the accepted cultivation and possession outside the federal class. No patient-specific sale, border crossing or diversion is found. The current rule does not demand separate substantial-effects proof for every purpose-defined subclass, require that every conceivable medical exemption would collapse the system, or impose least restrictive means as the constitutional inclusion test.

The strongest historical medical-subclass objection remains real: the patients can contend that an identifiable, medically supervised local class is administrable without materially impairing interstate controls. Neither the original nor this refresh finds California's safeguards ineffective. The answer remains the actual production and authorization relationship, assessed under the current rule each Associate has joined, rather than an assumption of failed state enforcement or a general assertion that Congress controls all local conduct. Legislative permission for accommodation, as illustrated by Cutter, does not establish a constitutionally compelled exemption from this otherwise sufficient inclusion.

The CSA's date-effective definitions, preparation/compounding and practitioner limitations, research/registration/scheduling channels and actual exceptions retain their own work. Cultivation is within production; delivery does not require payment. State permission and medical recommendation do not amend federal authorization. Section 903's positive-conflict condition is not general displacement of state law. Sustaining direct federal regulation of private conduct does not require California to criminalize it or administer the federal system. Andersen supplies no new mens rea or statutory-liability ruling for the patients.

**R3 remains correction of the Commerce ruling and its preliminary-relief direction, followed by remand.** The appellate judgment and its direction to grant relief on that ground cannot stand. There is no additional Commerce-evidence hearing requirement where the accepted production/possession facts and actual statutory operation establish the relationship; the new decisions supply no material factual defect in that analysis. Remaining genuinely preserved claims and requests for relief receive their own legal, factual and equitable adjudication.

Oakland Cannabis remains confined to the distributor's asserted statutory medical-necessity exemption and impermissible equitable recreation of it. Its individual-possession, patient-necessity and independent-constitutional reservations remain intact. The power ruling does not decide the separately preserved due-process, Ninth Amendment, necessity or other distinct theories outside that question. The medical affidavits retain their supplied procedural status and possible relevance to those claims, irreparable injury and appropriate relief. Reservation does not guarantee relief, and the pleadings alone cannot preserve the rejected Commerce-based direction.

Tucson and Taft do not decide those patient claims. Their necessary-care protection has an independently established reproductive-liberty source, not a general rule that serious need creates entitlement to any chosen treatment. That limit is equally not an adjudication that Raich's independent patient claims fail. No new search, privacy, notice or equality claim is invented from Tucson. No replacement injunction, reclassification order, criminal adjudication or general drug entitlement follows from this handoff.
