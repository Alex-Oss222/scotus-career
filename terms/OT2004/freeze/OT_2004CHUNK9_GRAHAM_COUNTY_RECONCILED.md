## Corrections to provisional grounds

The six-to-two Associate disposition split is confirmed, but two provisional formulations overstate agreement or change an expressly supported rule.

First, Ginsburg and Breyer do not replace the statutory false-claim trigger with retaliation accrual. Their historical position applies six years from the alleged or suspected §3729 violation. They expressly accept that the trigger can precede retaliation; the relatively long and uniform period ordinarily protects claimants and permits related fraud and retaliation litigation together. Their textual answer is that limitations provisions routinely identify alleged events without requiring the alleged violation ultimately to be proved. This is different from making actual fraud an element of retaliation.

The current McKenzie rule establishes independent retaliation conditions, but the historical dissent already recognized that the employee need not prove actual fraud. Thus McKenzie supplies no new premise answering their historical willingness to use the suspected-fraud date. North Star Steel begins with whether an express federal period applies and does not authorize replacing the specified event. Donnelley's concern for uniformity likewise supplies no replacement retaliation date. No Justice-specific changed premise supports the frozen G2 substitution. It is corrected here while preserving their six-year disposition.

Second, Stevens does not supply an undifferentiated full join to the principal textual analysis. He finds the broad civil-action cross-reference naturally inclusive and values federal uniformity, but regards the possibility that time expires before retaliation as the decisive contrary reason. His sufficient anti-premature-expiration ground can support state borrowing without accepting that textual ambiguity is a necessary precondition. His historical concurrence and current contextual statutory approach confirm this narrower support. Count his actual sufficient ground, not every proposition offered by the other five.

Scalia's frozen North Star Steel reservation does not require withholding assent from the ordinary state-borrowing disposition here. The historical principal opinion cites his borrowing work, notes the exceptional practice without extending it, and finds no basis for a federal substitute in this case. He can join that confined application. A new expansive endorsement of federal borrowing would exceed his recorded scope and is unnecessary.

## Law, counterarguments and disposition scope

The principal five read §3731(b)(1)'s broad action reference with its explicit violation-of-§3729 event. McKenzie already establishes that qualifying retaliation, employer knowledge and causation do not require successful fraud recovery. An actual §3729 violation therefore cannot be assumed as every retaliation claim's clock. Reading suspected into the trigger is the two dissenters' actual answer and must be answered on its own terms: fraud plaintiffs ultimately must allege and establish a violation, while a valid retaliation claim need never establish one. The five use the neighboring §3731(c) government-burden context as evidence that an unqualified action-under-§3730 phrase can be narrower in context. They also address §3731(d)'s express (a)/(b) language: drafting precision elsewhere does not override the present sentence's incompatible trigger and adjacent contextual usage.

The normal accrual principle favors a period beginning when retaliation occurs rather than before the protected injury exists. It supports state borrowing; it does not authorize grafting an unprovided retaliation trigger onto the federal six-year provision. Stevens considers that anomaly sufficient even without the five's initial ambiguity analysis. Souter joins the necessary resolution without joining the methodological rebuke of Stevens.

The two dissenters accept the independent retaliation elements but treat the alleged or suspected underlying false claim as the legislatively selected event. Their rule can produce a period beginning before retaliation, and potentially expiring first. They consider that possibility uncommon compared with the concrete burden of short, varied state limits. Their position is neither an ordinary-accrual six-year rule nor an actual-fraud prerequisite. Nothing here supplies a separate holding under §3731(b)(2), which has its own federal-official knowledge requirement, three-year period and ten-year outer boundary; those terms must not be collapsed into (b)(1).

North Star Steel provides ordinary state borrowing when no federal period governs, with its confined federal-analogue exception; no supported basis invokes that exception here. Donnelley's §1658 rule supplies no period for the cause created in 1986. The correct North Carolina analogue is outside the granted issue and was not decided by the Court of Appeals. Reversal therefore does not reinstate the district court's dismissal automatically or certify its three-year selection. Proper selection and any necessary accrual/tolling application return to the lower court. The independent qui tam case and the retaliation merits remain outside this limitations judgment.
