## Common premises and precise options

**M0 — Review and the certificate boundary.** All eight Associates accept this Court's section 1254(1) authority to review the judicial denial of a certificate under Slack. Reviewing or vacating that denial does not itself issue a certificate or authorize an appellate habeas merits decision. Section 2253(c) requires the actual constitutional showing; after a procedural dismissal Slack requires reasonable debate about both that constitutional claim and the applicable procedural ground. Miller-El requires a threshold assessment under the real habeas restrictions, not completed merits proof. The mere importance or debatability of Avena does not satisfy the constitutional component. No Associate in this reconciliation directs issuance of a certificate on the supplied record.

Reed's ordinary nonconstitutional collateral-relief standard is distinct from federal-law custody jurisdiction and does not establish a categorical rule against all treaties. Current Breard and Paraguay are confined interim decisions, reserving final enforceability, default, prejudice and remedy. They cannot be treated as a Supreme Court merits holding that individuals can never enforce this treaty or that every procedural default necessarily defeats the presented claim. The certificate inquiry, any section 2254 limitation, collateral cognizability, nonretroactivity, default, actual prejudice, exhaustion, domestic source of law and eventual remedy remain distinct. None of the procedural options below decides those unresolved questions.

**MH — Preferred bounded retention.** Retain the submitted writ through June 20, 2005, requiring a status submission by June 13 or promptly on an earlier state disposition, and reconsider the vehicle by June 20 through a further actual order. These timing terms come from the independent commitments as prospective case management; they are not purported historical filings or a prediction that Texas will act by then. Retention neither vacates the certificate denial nor grants a certificate, merits relief or an execution stay. It preserves this Court's pending review while the new state route invoking the memorandum develops. Landis supplies ordinary pending-case management; Rhines supplies no automatic Supreme Court hold rule and its mixed-petition district conditions are not mechanically transferred here. If MH is adopted, the matter remains open until an actual later dated action.

**MD — Vehicle dismissal.** Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because the new asserted domestic implementation source and pending state proceeding materially change the vehicle for the submitted international-judgment questions. The dismissal leaves the Fifth Circuit judgment in place without affirming its substantive treaty rationale. It decides no presidential authority, Avena enforceability, constitutional certificate entitlement or other habeas barrier. Any later actual state judgment would require its own lawful review proceeding; its outcome or eventual review is not guaranteed.

**MR — Certificate-review remand, without certification.** Vacate the judicial denial of a certificate and remand for a fresh application of the actual certificate framework, removing reliance on a supposed categorical Supreme Court merits bar supplied by Breard. The court of appeals must independently enforce section 2253(c) and the applicable habeas restrictions before authorizing an appeal. It may again deny certification if the required constitutional showing or another applicable threshold is absent. No constitutional injury is invented, no treaty issue is renamed constitutional, and no certifying command follows from the correction of the erroneous precedent premise. Any merits work must wait for lawful certification.

MR is narrower than a remand directing a certificate or directing adjudication of the treaty merits. The independent commitments correctly rejected those latter directions but incorrectly treated their rejection as excluding all correction and remand of the judicial denial. Slack itself separates review of a certificate refusal, correction of an erroneous threshold ground, and the still-required certificate conditions. O'Connor joined its reversal and remand while preserving independent gates. The current record supplies the lower court's broader use of historical Breard; correcting that legal premise does not adjudicate every alternative basis for denial. Ordinary allocation of unsettled certificate questions to the lower court is not a finding that those questions will be answered in the applicant's favor.

## Historical reconciliation and changed premises

O'Connor's provisional independent dismissal is corrected. The actual writing is direct evidence against the premise that uncertainty about the new state route and federal gateways would lead her to MD. Current Slack does change part of the historical analysis: it expressly makes a qualifying constitutional showing necessary, whereas her historical opinion treated the significance of the statutory change as unsettled and relied on waiver and an analogy to Peguero. The current Peguero position concerns an actually issued certificate; it does not erase conditions for a certificate that has never issued. Consequently she supports a limited review remand preserving those conditions, not an instruction that the Vienna Convention claim necessarily deserves certification. The material source-supported reason for retaining a remand is correction of a distinct erroneous legal premise, not an invented constitutional claim.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor retains her historical choice of certificate-denial vacatur and remand but withholds the historical suggestion that treaty debatability and a waived objection suffice for certification. Her own current Slack join makes the constitutional component obligatory, and current Peguero concerns an issued certificate; her remand therefore permits renewed denial under those actual requirements while removing the false premise that this Court's confined Breard orders resolved the merits.

**Historical departure:** Ginsburg's preferred present hold differs from her published historical dismissal because her actual writing identified retention as her first choice and dismissed only after retention lacked support. The current commitment preserves that expressly stated preference and the conditional MD fallback; it does not attribute a newly invented change of treaty doctrine or a new state factual development to her.

**Historical departure:** Stevens, Souter and Breyer presently prefer the bounded hold that their historical writings expressly favored before falling back to vacatur and remand. The difference from their published remedial alternative concerns which supported case-management choice is available, not an inferred abandonment of the certificate statute. Their fallback remands retain the current constitutional gate, and Souter alone conditions his fallback on a lower-court pause.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain MD and its nonmerits limits. Ginsburg's conditional MD matches her actual fallback. No Justice receives the historical per curiam's list of possible obstacles as adjudicated holdings. The analysis neither reproduces an actual historical vote count nor manufactures a two-way present poll.
