## 96. Orff v. United States

### Components, proposed operative rule, and remedy

**O1 — Consent:** Section 390uu consents to adding the United States as a necessary defendant in a qualifying suit adjudicating a contracting entity's and the United States' contractual rights under federal reclamation law. It does not authorize this surviving direct action against the United States and its agents merely because the farmers claim to benefit from the contract. The first sentence's joinder condition governs the second sentence's amenability and judgment provisions; the venue sentence identifies a forum for a suit within that consent rather than creating a separate direct-action waiver.

**O2 — Entitlement and disposition:** Affirm dismissal on sovereign-immunity grounds. Beneficiary status cannot create consent and is not adjudicated: entitlement is downstream of the unsatisfied jurisdictional condition. The districts' earlier participation does not supply the continuing independent nonfederal dispute that the farmers' surviving action lacks. The proper result is a jurisdictional disposition of this invoked route, not a merits rejection of the contract or a judgment establishing all beneficiaries' inability to proceed under another law.

**Controlling explanation available to the shared coalition:** Meyer requires respecting the breadth of the clause Congress actually wrote; its broad sue-and-be-sued clause is not the language enacted here. Blue Fox separates the alleged substantive obligation from consent to the requested relief. Idaho likewise treats the scope of a consent as distinct from an additional obligation asserted against the Government. Giving effect to the term necessary party defendant leaves meaningful work for adjudication of a reclamation contract when an independent dispute calls for federal joinder. Reading any suit and the ensuing amenability language as new grants detached from the opening condition would erase that procedural limitation.

## 97. Halbert v. Michigan

### Components and supported rules

**H1 — Initial appellate assistance:** The Fourteenth Amendment requires appointed counsel for an indigent defendant seeking this first-tier Michigan appellate merits review after a guilty or no-contest plea. A State may use a genuinely equivalent advocate-assisted screening process and need not require advocacy of frivolous claims. Its designation of this first error-correcting opportunity as discretionary, and the reduced number of issues surviving a plea, do not replace the advocate's role where no earlier lawyer-assisted appellate presentation exists.

**H2 — Waiver:** Entry of a plea does not itself relinquish the distinct assistance required to seek initial appellate review. An actual knowing, intelligent, and voluntary waiver remains effective when established from the full record. The qualified appointment discussion here, together with Halbert's repeated requests for counsel, does not establish an intentional informed surrender of that appellate assistance. No prescribed formula is required; the missing element is demonstrated understanding and surrender, not missing ritual words.

**H3 — Remedy:** Vacate the judgment denying the uncounseled leave application and remand for constitutionally adequate first-tier consideration with appointed assistance. The deprivation is of the required screening and presentation function. Under Robbins's restoration principle, Halbert need not first prove that an unpresented issue probably would have changed the appellate result. The conviction remains in place while the restored review proceeds under its ordinary preserved-issue rules.

**Competing sufficient disposition:** Scalia and Thomas would affirm because Douglas's entitlement attaches to an appeal as of right, while the State may make plea review a selective leave stage under Ross. On that ground they do not reach an affirmative waiver holding. Their votes do not establish either a categorical plea waiver or knowing surrender on this record.

## 98. Mayle v. Felix

### Preserved record clarification

The timely May 8, 1998 petition contained confrontation, instructional-error, and insufficient-evidence grounds. The original pleading did not attempt the interrogation factual core. Appointment of counsel occurred May 29, 1998. Both make the January amendment late by months, and this handoff does not adjudicate that one-day calculation.

The amicus's footnote 1 characterizes the Ninth Circuit as apparently combining appellate assistance with coercion because the amendment combined those theories. It expressly says that the court did not separately rule on assistance timeliness. That advocacy description is retained as such; it supplies no separate appellate disposition. The Confrontation Clause merits affirmance remains outside the granted Rule 15 question.

### Components, alternatives, and remedy

**M1 — Common factual core:** Under Rule 15(c)(2), an amended habeas ground asserted after expiration relates back when it arises from the same core operative facts set forth or attempted in the timely pleading. A different legal theory, more particular facts, or fair elaboration of an imperfect pro se account can qualify. A common trial, conviction, or requested release alone does not establish the required connection.

**M2 — Application:** The alleged coercion of Felix's own pretrial statements and the circumstances of Williams's videotaped testimony are distinct episodes. On the supplied description, none of the timely grounds attempted the interrogation factual core. The amended coercion ground therefore does not relate back. Reverse the relation-back portion of the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with that conclusion; leave its separate Confrontation Clause merits disposition undisturbed. The lower court must handle the related assistance theory under the same rule within its actual procedural scope, without a fictitious separate appellate merits judgment.

**M3 — Relation to other procedure:** Section 2242 permits civil-rule amendment; Rule 15(a)'s permission to amend is distinct from Rule 15(c)(2)'s effect on an expired limitation. The proposed rule leaves ordinary timely amendments and genuinely related later amendments available. Rule 2(c)'s factual specificity supplies context for identifying what the original pleading attempted, rather than an additional formal-wording requirement. The existing statutory triggers, qualifying state pendency, Rhines retention authority, and Pliler relief for proved judicially caused loss retain their own predicates; none is established as an alternative basis for this amendment.

**Alternative M4 — Trial occurrence:** Stevens and Souter would affirm the relation-back ruling. They treat the challenged criminal trial's use of evidence as a sufficiently connected occurrence for the added coercion ground in this pending attack on that conviction. Rule 15(a)'s ordinary controls address prejudice, abuse, and delay; Rule 15(c) remains an enacted accommodation to limitation rather than a judicially invented extra year. They would return for the actual remaining proceedings, without deciding the coercion merits or treating the unrelated original-claim merits disposition as before the Court.

## 99. Green Party of New York State v. New York State Board of Elections

### Components and proposed governing propositions

**G1 — Protected burden and likelihood:** In a system that maintains and supplies official affiliation information for organizing, contact, mobilization, and fundraising as well as primary administration, erasing and refusing the affiliations of organizations that have demonstrated the required statewide ballot support imposes a substantial associational burden. On this preliminary record it is substantial rather than severe: the organizations retain political and ballot-access activity, but lose a useful continuing channel selectively available to established parties. Under Cartwright's calibrated Anderson–Burdick scale, important state interests must have a demonstrated substantial relationship to that exclusion. Primary eligibility can remain separate from recorded affiliation, and an accurate explanation on the form addresses the identified confusion concern. The supplied findings show a substantial likelihood that the categorical exclusion fails this standard. The overlapping First and Fourteenth Amendment claims receive this same actual-burden-and-fit inquiry; unequal treatment does not automatically substitute strict scrutiny without examining its operation.

**G2 — Interim protection:** Preserve the preliminary injunction. The ongoing loss of identification and communication with willing affiliates establishes the required irreparable associational injury on this record. The plaintiffs meet the lower court's applied substantial-likelihood standard for this affirmative change to the system; an alternative serious-questions formula is not the ground. Review does not convert the factual showing into a final trial judgment. Preserving the record and admitting future affiliation entries are both necessary to stop the ongoing exclusion; keeping only obsolete copies does not provide equivalent interim relief.

**G3 — Functional implementation:** Preserve the Other write-in mechanism with accurate instructions about primary eligibility; continued maintenance and updating for qualifying organizations; best efforts to notify already disenrolled Liberal and Right to Life voters that they may reenroll using the new form; and the Green Party's equal-form access to enrollment data. These are the actual distinct existing directions. The form's ability to name any organization does not itself require officials to maintain every organization's continuing data. Maintenance protection retains the demonstrated-support condition: sufficient support to place a statewide candidate on the ballot at the most recent gubernatorial election. Officials and the supervising court may implement an equally effective arrangement without elevating particular database design or exact phrasing into an independent entitlement. Existing protection continues during that work.

**G4 — Reach and further proceedings:** Affirm interim protection and remand for continued merits proceedings and application of Clingman's common-policy remedy rule. For persons and organizations governed by the same exclusion who satisfy the support condition, the decree must cure that policy's operation unless the State establishes a narrower fully effective remedy. The existing plaintiff-only limitation is not sufficient merely because only the plaintiffs were named; neither does the record establish any unlisted group's qualification automatically. The lower court must apply the governing support criterion and settle an administrable effective scope while maintaining the current injunction. No primary eligibility or full statutory party status follows from affiliation recording; those distinctions are integral to the remedy and the answer to the State's objection.
