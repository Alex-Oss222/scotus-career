# Brown — reconciliation remedy clarification

This supplement clarifies the remedial explanation in `OT_2004CHUNK5_BROWN_RECONCILED.md` after a separate read-only review. It changes no vote, gateway conclusion, factual premise or original frozen commitment. It supplies no current Chief position or author choice.

Calderon v. Coleman Part III is an additional direct common authority: all eight Associates joined its requirement of a substantive effect inquiry distinct from identifying Boyde error. That holding also rejects a required citation, verbal formula or separate heading. Thus neither the historical dissent's brevity nor its failure to reproduce a separate checklist would justify rejecting its effect conclusion. The dissent's central-mitigation premise and its distinction between legal exclusion and weight are substantial and remain part of the present assessment.

Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's current Coleman and Penry II commitments require distinguishing the reasonable likelihood of forbidden exclusion from contribution to the actual sentence. They decline here to treat the historical dissent's stated inference from that likelihood and the possibility of execution as a completed whole-record contribution determination, and leave H for application of the governing State burden. This is their present case-specific remedial judgment. Penry II does not categorically require remand whenever mitigation error is identified, and the present record does not establish harmlessness merely because a return is ordered.

Souter authored, and Ginsburg joined, Penry II's distinct effective-mitigation and State-burden effect holdings. Stevens joined effective mitigation and supported the corrective return while withholding the governing effect standard; he nevertheless directly joined Coleman's independent substantive-inquiry requirement. Those distinct positions must remain explicit. The stronger current State burden alone would not justify postponing otherwise demonstrated relief. The return instead rests on these Justices' present assessment that the actual contribution question remains to be completed under the governing rule, with the importance of the conversion evidence, legal-exclusion argument, alternative weighting arguments and full aggravating record all considered. No missing-document assumption or shifted prisoner burden supports it.

Read this clarification with the original Brown reconciliation. Its express historical-departure discussion remains operative subject to the qualifications above; no silent revision of the freeze is made.
