## 2. Smith v. Texas — November 15, 2004

### Reach, rule and operative remedy

All eight reach the two presented federal questions through section 1257 on the supplied state-collateral posture. No federal section 2254 gateway or deference standard applies. None invents an independently adequate procedural ground or a finding of harmlessness. All preserve the distinction between the evidence's mitigating relevance and a categorical intellectual-disability diagnosis.

The reversal path applies the settled *Graham–Johnson–Penry–Tennard* rule: supported intellectual-functioning and educational evidence may reduce culpability apart from deliberateness and future danger; it needs no uniquely severe permanent impairment or causal link to the offense. A mitigation-based negative special-issue answer must be an authorized coherent verdict, not an answer that the instructions simultaneously require the juror to certify as factual reasonable doubt. The described unchanged factual certification leaves a reasonable likelihood of restricted mitigating use.

The proposed disposition reverses the state court's merits rejection and remands for genuinely remaining procedural and whole-record effect adjudication. For a preserved restriction of the described kind, the State must establish beyond reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to death. If that burden is not met, Texas must provide constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence. No current finding of harmlessness, automatic sentence vacatur, release, or disturbance of guilt is supplied.

## 3. United States v. $100,348.00 in U.S. Currency — November 15, 2004

### Common reach and rules

All eight reach the presented questions. The pre-CAFRA regime controls. They recognize Mayzel's first-party interest in the entrusted fund on the legally substantive gratuitous-bailment relationship established in the neutral preflight: lawful possession and surviving duties concerning delivery and care. The claim protects that interest against the punitive forfeiture; it is not an assertion of the defaulting owner's rights or a universal right of unexplained possessors. State law establishes the interest; federal constitutional law determines its protection. A final judgment that Mayzel is liable to the owner for the full amount is unnecessary to recognize the existing possessory interest. The owner's late claim remains closed.

All eight agree that this partly punitive civil reporting forfeiture is a fine; its connection to an offense and the civil label do not settle excessiveness. The claimant bears the burden of establishing gross disproportionality in this reporting-forfeiture setting, extending the stated burden from *Wagoner County*. Constitutional application is reviewed independently, supported facts for clear error. There is no compulsory factor checklist, dollar ceiling, fixed ratio, automatic lawful-funds exemption, or rule that the broader statutory maximum is conclusive. The forfeiture is for the reporting violation; the false-statement conviction can inform supported culpability but cannot silently enlarge the forfeiture's statutory predicate.

### Enhanced alternatives and compatibility

**Standing path C1 — own protected interest.** All eight select the narrow first-party route. The strongest objection is that loss of another person's title punishes the owner alone, and that the owner's procedural failure cannot create third-party standing. The response is the legally substantive bailment, covering the fund itself and surviving its unsupported purported disclaimer. The present holding does not extend to a thief, unexplained custodian, stranger or person proved to have no protected interest. No holding that a bailee necessarily owes $100,000 in damages is required.

**Standing path C2 — third-party-only or no personal fine.** This would remove protection beyond any proved personally owned funds and leave the owner's default intact. Its decisive barrier here is that it disregards the independently established possessory interest and duties. The Court need not loosen the separate close-relationship/hindrance requirements of third-party standing; they do no work on C1. The speculative possibility that a concealed owner may benefit does not eliminate this claimant's own injury.

**Amount path C3 — affirm the actual reduction.** Stevens, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer find full forfeiture grossly disproportionate but sustain $10,000. The strongest counterargument combines a large reporting evasion, preventive information value, deception, possible $30,000 ordinary fine and Congress's $250,000 maximum. Their conclusion gives those matters weight but measures the actual civil reporting offense against the credited lawful source, no further proved criminal connection and limited harm. They do not infer innocence from acquittal or treat zero financial loss as zero regulatory harm. The $10,000 judgment survives even if the lower $5,000 benchmark applies; a twofold comparison alone neither decides this case nor licenses another.

**Amount path C4 — permit full forfeiture.** O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy retain their *Bajakajian* application position: Congress may impose a serious forfeiture for withholding a large transaction from a preventive reporting system. The strongest objection is the majority's binding application to lawful funds with limited harm. Their distinction is that the constitutional boundary is gross disproportionality, not the fixed dollar result from *Bajakajian*, with this materially smaller amount, supported deceptive conduct, and no settled entitlement to the lowest hypothetical fine. They need not displace the general rule or find another offense. Their ground does not command the other five's joins.

**Findings/remedy alternative C5.** An additional-findings remand would be necessary if the unresolved Guidelines facts changed the legal conclusion. C3 deliberately tests the actual exaction against both possible fine benchmarks, and C4 finds the claimant's showing insufficient without relying on the higher one as a proved fact. No Justice uses an invented guideline finding. The lower court's arithmetic compromise language is not endorsed; review concerns constitutional permissibility of its actual $10,000 judgment, not a judicial formula for selecting the best penalty. Fee and owner-default rulings are not reopened.

## 5. Anderson v. Spear — November 30, 2004

### Common reach, components and remedy

All eight reach the three presented restrictions on the scheduled live controversy. The 2003 appropriation suspension does not repeal them, and section 1257 finality is inapplicable to this federal appellate channel. The six other historical claims are not adjudicated.

**A — 500-foot zone and write-in instructions.** All eight reject the challenged zone's unsupported breadth across ordinary streets and sidewalks, including its application to nonintimidating write-in instructions. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the controlling *Burson* strict-scrutiny rule and apply it. Scalia and Thomas join the invalidity conclusion on the scope stated below without being counted as adherents to a universal majority methodology. No replacement distance is selected and no categorical express-advocacy-only definition is enacted. The protection of polling interiors, valid entrance safeguards, vote-buying and intimidation rules remains distinct.

**B — personal-loan ceiling.** All eight invalidate the $50,000 restriction on the candidate's initial use of his own funds for campaign expression. A loan used for campaign spending is not a donor contribution merely because repayment may occur later. The State's concern about later donor influence must be addressed through a fitted restriction on that later transaction; it cannot justify this broad restriction on the initial protected spending. No blanket immunity for sham loans, unlawful sources, contribution evasion, or later donor repayment is supplied.

**C — post-election debt-retirement contributions.** All eight invalidate the complete timing prohibition at issue. Later contributions can create genuine corruption and circumvention risks, especially when repaying the officeholder personally, but the State has not justified excluding the entire lawful contribution channel, including ordinary limited gifts and support for unsuccessful candidates, when appropriately fitted amount, source and disclosure rules remain available. This is not a holding that a losing candidate can never be corruptly influenced or that existing donor ceilings conclusively eliminate every risk.

**Formal judgment.** Affirm the appellate judgment invalidating the three challenged restrictions on these grounds; vacate only any direction that the federal court must impose a universal express-advocacy-only rewriting of Kentucky's electioneering definition. Remand for precise declaratory and injunctive relief and lawful severability consistent with state law. Federal constitutional relief requires no invented replacement number, new state statutory words, treatment of the other six claims, damages award or refund.

### Enhanced two-path analysis by component

**A1 — sustain an administrable voting safeguard.** Kentucky has a compelling interest in preventing intimidation, vote buying and disruption. A rule need not wait for consummated coercion or prove that every single foot is indispensable. That is the strongest path to sustaining a line beyond the polling-room door. Its failure here is the actual breadth of the 500-foot rule across ordinary public forums, the substantial ordinary speech it excludes, and the inadequate relationship between that full reach and the supported voting harms. Its narrow private-interior exception and exit-polling exception do not restore the suppressed public campaign communication.

**A2 — invalidate this reach.** All eight select this judgment. Six share the exact *Burson* strict-scrutiny proposition; Scalia's historically administered polling-environs analysis and Thomas's protected direct-expression approach do not create a new majority alternative rule. All reject a replacement number. As to speech coverage, the present record supports relief for nonintimidating write-in instructions, but not a categorical rule that only express electoral advocacy can ever be controlled near polls. The appellate court's universal limiting construction is therefore not carried into the mandate without a permissible state-law construction.

**B1 — treat the loan cap as a repayment safeguard.** The strongest supporting argument is that a candidate retaining a personal receivable may later solicit contributions to replenish his own wealth. Unlike a final gift, that loan creates a personal repayment incentive. The decisive problem is that the statute restricts the initial amount of the candidate's own campaign funding, including money that may never be repaid by a donor, rather than fitting the asserted risk in the subsequent contribution transaction. The option to surrender all repayment rights does not supply a justification for the speech burden. No candidate resources-equalization rationale is valid under the entering law.

**B2 — protect initial personal funding while preserving later regulation.** All eight select this route. The loan's actual campaign expenditure is protected under *Buckley*; *Russell*, *Shrink Missouri* and *Beaumont* preserve objectively supported, closely fitted treatment of later contributions. The holding does not prevent regulation of sham or conduit transactions and does not declare donor repayment indistinguishable from candidate spending.

**C1 — sustain a categorical post-election cutoff.** The State can identify a distinctive risk when the winner is known and a donor's payment personally retires that official's loan. Disclosure after voting cannot recreate information voters lacked beforehand. Those arguments support meaningful review of a fitted repayment rule. They do not establish that the entire post-election contribution channel must close for every candidate and every lawful amount, purpose and recipient.

**C2 — invalidate the total ban.** All eight select this route under the current close-fit rule. *McConnell*'s complete minor-contribution ban supplies a pertinent shared example: legitimate evasion concerns do not excuse excluding genuine voluntary contributions as a class when fitted rules remain available. Existing donor ceilings and disclosure are relevant alternatives, not conclusive proof that every contribution is safe. The Court does not write a new deadline, donation ceiling, winner-only rule or special debt-payment code.

**Remedy compatibility.** All eight support precise prospective relief and a state-law severability remand. No majority for a statutory rewrite is inferred from agreement that the existing application is unconstitutional. No public claim that all election speech, all loans, or all debt retirement is categorically immune is supportable.
