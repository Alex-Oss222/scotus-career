## Distinct questions and governing grounds

**G1a — actual exhaustion.** Russell exhausted the responsive process before suit. The emergency route expedited review at the Commissioner's level. The Commissioner received the grievance, considered its substance and rejected the material concerns on April 15, 2002, without requiring ordinary resubmission or rejecting delivery on technical grounds. The rule permitting resubmission as a regular grievance when an emergency is deemed nonemergent creates no additional mandatory stage after this substantive final-level rejection. This holding does not adopt a general rule that every missed response deadline excuses remaining administrative review, and it does not rely on a new certificate requirement.

**G1b — the certified common prospective claim.** A named representative who completes the available administrative process for the same institutional conditions satisfies section 1997e(a) for a properly certified class seeking the same indivisible prospective correction. Rule 23 supplies representative procedure; it does not waive a statutory prerequisite. The action is brought by an exhausted representative, and unnamed members need not repeat an identical request for the same correction. Different incidents, unpresented conditions and individual damages remain distinct claims requiring their own statutory treatment. Booth establishes mandatory exhaustion of an actually responsive available process even if it cannot award requested damages; Porter establishes coverage of prison-life claims, including individual episodes. Neither earlier decision already resolved the class question.

**G2 — constitutional liability.** The findings of prolonged fecal contamination, sewage backflow, dangerous heat aggravated by illness and medication, the insect/ventilation tradeoff and sleep disruption, grossly inadequate light affecting sanitation and mental health, and seriously inadequate psychiatric care establish identified health and safety deprivations. Wilson permits interacting conditions to be considered together when they deprive an identifiable human need. Helling permits prevention of a serious future risk without waiting for disease. Farmer requires actual awareness and unreasonable disregard; obviousness and repeated notice support an inference of actual knowledge but do not compel it or establish collective institutional knowledge without the required proof. Reasonable protective measures remain a defense even if unsuccessful. The trial findings and record defeat the claim that mere repair efforts, absence of a diagnosed illness or accreditation conclusively preclude relief.

**G3 — statutory relief.** Section 3626 requires relief necessary to correct the proved federal violation, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive necessary means, with substantial weight to public safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting departure from state or local law additionally requires that federal law require that departure, that it be necessary and that no other relief correct the federal violation. Existing findings may establish the predicates at their actual scope; ritual language is not independently dispositive. Benjamin supplies the current implementation requirements; its pretrial constitutional standard does not replace Farmer for these convicted prisoners. Johnson v. California's remedial part confirms the separate statutory conditions without importing its race-classification dispute.

The general request to restart the decree for absent particularized PLRA findings was not preserved below. That does not authorize an independently overbroad command. The court's continuing implementation must retain the current statutory findings required by Benjamin. Paragraph 9's reference to private professional standards is therefore limited or replaced as stated below; those standards do not establish constitutional rights or automatically expand the decree when a professional organization changes them.
