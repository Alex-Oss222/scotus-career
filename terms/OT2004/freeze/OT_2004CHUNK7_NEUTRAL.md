## 57. Pasquantino v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Pasquantino v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-725; judgment below, 336 F.3d 321 (en banc).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-26 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a scheme using domestic interstate wires to evade Canadian liquor taxes is punishable under 18 U.S.C. §1343 despite the common-law rule against enforcement of foreign revenue laws.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit, sitting en banc, affirmed the wire-fraud convictions.
* **Relief Requested:** The defendants seek reversal of their convictions on statutory and revenue-rule grounds. The United States seeks affirmance as enforcement of a domestic fraud offense.
* **Material Record:** The defendants arranged United States liquor purchases by interstate telephone, concealed the liquor in vehicles and transported it into Canada without declaring it or paying the import charges. The alleged property was Canada’s entitlement to money due, not its abstract authority to regulate imports. The criminal prosecution was brought by the United States. No civil tax judgment from Canada is being registered for collection in this proceeding.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The preserved question concerns the charged property-fraud theory and the revenue rule. Independent sentencing objections retain their actual preservation and event-date law; a possible sentencing defect does not decide guilt. Restitution requires its own statutory and judgment basis rather than an assumed order.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date above controls date eligibility; later proceedings and authorities are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Small v. United States concerns whether foreign convictions fall within a domestic firearm predicate. Its statutory question is distinct and same-day order supplies no borrowed rule. Booker may affect a preserved sentencing question only if an actual effective decision exists.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Government must prove every element of §1343 beyond a reasonable doubt. Foreign law is determined through the applicable criminal procedural rule, while the existence of the scheme and domestic wire use require the proper evidentiary showing.

##### Issue 1: Property fraud and the revenue-rule boundary

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Cleveland v. United States excludes sovereign licensing authority from the mail-fraud property category but expressly reserves schemes targeting actual public money or unpaid revenue. Neder v. United States preserves materiality as an element of the federal fraud offenses.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing fraud rule requires property in the victim’s hands and does not convert every regulatory deception into property fraud. Its reservation for unpaid revenue must be answered rather than treated as already resolved.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §1343; the received common-law revenue rule; Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 26.1; separately applicable restitution statutes. No foreign tax law itself supplies a United States criminal offense.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory reference to interstate or foreign wires and the domestic execution of the charged scheme support the United States’ construction. The revenue rule’s protection against judicial collection of foreign taxes supports the defendants’ narrower reading.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Entering law settles neither this foreign-revenue application nor every extraterritorial question. Actual entitlement to money differs from permission held solely as sovereign regulator.
* **Open Point:** Whether a domestic fraud prosecution targeting an accrued foreign entitlement falls within the revenue rule or the enacted wire-fraud prohibition.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The defendants argue that identifying a Canadian tax debt and punishing its evasion indirectly enforces the foreign revenue policy; they invoke the common-law background, territorial restraint and lenity. The United States answers that it prosecutes domestic use of wires to execute property fraud, not Canada’s tax cause of action, and that §1343 contains no exclusion based on the victim’s nationality. The court below accepted the property and domestic-enforcement distinction. The restitution issue illustrates a separate remedial question; it cannot be assumed resolved by the existence of the offense.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance on the domestic fraud theory leaves the convictions standing while preserving any independently available sentencing question. Reversal could follow if the statute excludes the asserted foreign-revenue scheme; that would require removal of convictions resting only on it. A narrow ruling can distinguish domestic criminal enforcement from a direct civil collection action without deciding every foreign-law-based fraud or restitution award.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The record supports a rule about domestic execution of a scheme aimed at an actual foreign entitlement to money. It does not support criminalizing all breaches of foreign law, dispensing with materiality or authorizing the direct collection of any foreign tax judgment.

## 58. Small v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Small v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-750; judgment below, 333 F.3d 425.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-26 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the phrase “convicted in any court” in 18 U.S.C. §922(g)(1) includes a conviction entered by a foreign court.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit affirmed Small’s conviction based on a Japanese predicate judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Small seeks reversal because his foreign conviction is outside the statutory predicate category. The United States seeks affirmance under the breadth of “any court.”
* **Material Record:** Small was convicted in Japan for attempting to smuggle firearms and ammunition and later possessed a firearm in the United States. He entered a conditional guilty plea preserving the foreign-predicate question. The statute’s text, exclusions and restoration provisions matter independently of whether the particular Japanese proceeding was fair or its offense serious.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The conditional plea preserves this statutory issue. The Court need not decide a constitutional right to firearm possession, relitigate Japanese guilt or invent an individualized foreign-fairness review procedure. A domestic conviction supplying an independent predicate is not established by the record.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date above controls date eligibility; later proceedings and authorities are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Nordyke v. King presents constitutional firearms and expression questions, not the statutory meaning of foreign convictions. Pasquantino v. United States concerns a domestic fraud statute’s foreign-money object and is not controlling merely because it shares an event date.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Statutory construction is independent. A conviction cannot stand without a qualifying predicate; lenity operates only after ordinary textual and contextual interpretation leaves a genuine penal ambiguity.

##### Issue 1: The domestic statutory category

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States assigns the restoration inquiry to the law of the convicting jurisdiction. Caron v. United States addresses continuing firearm restrictions after domestic rights restoration. Neither decides which foreign judgments initially enter the category.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rule separates predicate eligibility, the convicting-sovereign selector, restoration and independent offense elements. A selector within a statutory category does not itself define that category’s territorial reach.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§922(g)(1) and 921(a)(20), including federal-or-state business-regulation exclusions and the state-misdemeanor provision. Congress’s domestic statutory context and ordinary territorial interpretive principles must be examined together.
* **Material Other Authority:** The word “any” supports breadth within the noun’s relevant statutory universe. The scheme’s treatment of convictions and civil-rights restoration supplies evidence about the universe Congress selected.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Entering law governs domestic conviction exclusions and does not adopt either categorical foreign inclusion or a discretionary test of foreign procedural fairness.
* **Open Point:** Whether foreign convictions are included at all and whether statutory context limits the apparent breadth of “any.”

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Government emphasizes that Congress said any court and that excluding foreign convictions can leave serious offenders outside the prohibition. Small responds that the surrounding exclusions and restoration provisions are organized around domestic law, while foreign offenses and procedures may differ fundamentally. The Third Circuit’s approach accepted foreign predicates and considered fairness limitations; Small argues that no such judicial screening procedure appears in the statute. Both sides must distinguish a desirable firearms policy from the category Congress enacted.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A domestic-only construction requires reversal of a conviction resting on the Japanese judgment. A foreign-inclusive construction could affirm if all other preserved conditions are satisfied, but must identify a lawful source for any fairness limitation rather than add an undefined judicial exception. The narrow statutory route leaves constitutional firearms questions untouched.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule defining the predicate’s territorial category can apply uniformly without reviewing foreign criminal justice case by case. It does not create immunity from domestic firearm offenses based on qualifying domestic convictions or other disqualifying categories.

[Original opinion and record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/385/).

## 59. Doe v. Porter

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Doe v. Porter
* **Citation or Docket:** 370 F.3d 558; Sixth Circuit Nos. 02-5316 and 02-5823
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-26 (scheduled merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a public elementary school violates the Establishment Clause by permitting instructors from a religious college to teach the Christian Bible as religious truth during the regular school day?
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, affirming summary judgment, a prospective injunction against the challenged Bible Education Ministry program, and related protective-order and fee rulings.
* **Relief Requested:** The school defendants seek reversal of the constitutional judgment and injunction; alternatively they seek judicial guidelines and an opportunity to restructure the program. The parents seek preservation of effective relief against the school program.
* **Material Record:** Bryan College volunteers conduct weekly thirty-minute Bible classes during the regular elementary-school day in three Rhea County schools. Lesson plans present Christian doctrine as truth and prescribe religious conduct; the Board supplies the classroom opportunity while leaving content to the college. Sealed affidavits establish the named parents’ children attend affected classes. The college’s religious identity alone is not the asserted violation; actual instructional content and the school’s adoption of that instruction matter.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The children’s actual classroom exposure supplies concrete, redressable injury without depending on taxpayer standing. The protective order permits defense counsel to investigate standing and the merits while protecting young children against supported retaliation risks. An independent organizational-standing ruling is unnecessary once affected families obtain the same injunction. No separate constitutional claim about every religious volunteer or all teaching about the Bible is presented.
* **Relevant Dates:** Appellate judgment: June 7, 2004. A timely petition is assumed for the scheduled April 26, 2005 merits event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 1983 supplies the federal constitutional vehicle; summary judgment requires no genuine material factual dispute. Rule 10(a), protective-order discretion, Article III, and the established equitable limits on advisory relief remain applicable. The constitutional inquiry is de novo, not deference to the local board’s curriculum label.

##### Issue 1: Official religious instruction in elementary classrooms

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Agostini v. Felton modified Lemon into one inquiry identifying the governmental act and function, with controlling specific prohibitions, established permissible relationships, and residual attribution analysis. Government may not adopt doctrine as truth or solicit adherence through public authority. Lee v. Weisman and Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe establish that official sponsorship is independently sufficient; private delivery or optional participation does not automatically make an official program private.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing doctrine treats purpose, effect and entanglement as evidence, not independent surviving prongs. Actual governmental organization or purchase of a religious function controls; religious identity and physical location alone do not. Genuine private access and reliably separated secular instruction remain protected.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; 42 U.S.C. §§1983 and 1988. Tennessee’s character-education requirement supplies an asserted secular objective but cannot authorize a forbidden means.
* **Material Other Authority:** School District of Abington Township v. Schempp and Illinois ex rel. McCollum v. Board of Education identify the established public-school instructional boundary. Rule 56 governs whether the operational record requires a trial.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law already forbids school adoption of religious truth. Whether this particular program performs that function is an ordinary application, not an unresolved demand for a new universal establishment test.
* **Open Point:** The judgment must distinguish elimination of the demonstrated devotional program from exclusion of private religious speakers or objective academic study. No replacement curriculum is before the Court.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Sixth Circuit relied on separate purpose, endorsement and entanglement analyses. Those formulations cannot simply be imported as entering governing law: Agostini requires the governmental-function inquiry. The result below can nevertheless be defended on the actual lessons and the school’s regular curricular arrangement.

The Board argues that volunteers assist legally required character instruction, that some lessons concern secular values, and that the college rather than public employees writes the material. Its strongest limiting principle would preserve genuinely secular instruction supplied by religious people. That does not answer the undisputed devotional lessons delivered as the school’s own instructional opportunity. The Board also requests directions for redesign rather than total exclusion.

The families answer that public-school adoption of Christian truth cannot be made permissible by outsourcing or a secular objective. They seek cessation of the program as operated, not a ban on Christianity in genuinely private speech or historical study. The supported threats and young children justify anonymity without preventing counsel’s discovery.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance can rest on existing official-instruction law while replacing the lower court’s obsolete independent-prong reasoning. It preserves the injunction and ordinary prevailing-party fees. Reversal would require identifying a genuine private-access or severable secular instructional relationship inconsistent with the documented program. A limited remand could clarify injunction wording if it also prohibits lawful academic study or private expression; it need not leave the proved program operating while officials request an advisory curriculum. Threshold dismissal would require an actual loss of all affected plaintiffs’ exposure, not the mere use of pseudonyms.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The supported application reaches public-school adoption of devotional instruction during its own school day. It neither condemns volunteers by religious identity nor supplies a general ban on instruction concerning religious history. A court may explain the constitutional boundary necessary to its injunction without preapproving an unsubmitted curriculum.

#### E. SOURCES

[Sixth Circuit opinion and reproduced record](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/370/558/522158/).

## 60. Pace v. DiGuglielmo

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Pace v. DiGuglielmo
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-9627; 544 U.S. 408
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-27 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a state postconviction petition rejected as untimely is nevertheless properly filed for tolling under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), particularly when the state time bar contains exceptions, and whether equitable tolling preserves this federal petition.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Third Circuit, reversing the district court’s recognition of statutory and equitable tolling and finding the federal habeas application untimely.
* **Relief Requested:** Pace seeks restoration of federal consideration of his habeas claims. Pennsylvania seeks affirmance of the limitations disposition, not a Supreme Court adjudication of the underlying conviction claims.
* **Material Record:** Pace’s second Pennsylvania collateral petition was filed November 27, 1996. The trial court denied relief on other grounds; the Superior Court expressly held the petition untimely and no statutory exception established. State review ended July 29, 1999. The federal petition followed December 24, 1999. His underlying sentencing, plea and ineffective-assistance claims concerned events available years earlier. Actual application dates and the independent state untimeliness ruling, rather than clerical acceptance, determine the tolling question.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal petition is late absent tolling; no independent later commencement date is established. A federal court must respect the actual state-law timeliness determination while independently interpreting the federal phrase properly filed. The merits of the constitutional claims and a new state-law exception are not before the Court.
* **Relevant Dates:** The November 1996 collateral petition postdates enactment of the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA); the grace-period calculation does not change the conceded need for tolling. Five months elapsed after state proceedings ended before federal filing. The February 28, 2005 argument and April 27 event are the historical review calendar.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Rhines concerns retention of a timely federal mixed petition while claims are exhausted; Pace concerns whether an untimely state filing stops the federal clock. Johnson concerns a different §2255 factual-predicate trigger after state vacatur.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Section 2244(d)(1) supplies commencement; subsection (d)(2) supplies state collateral pendency exclusion. Statutory tolling and equitable protection must be analyzed separately. An asserted equitable interval requires a supported causal justification and diligence rather than a general dislike of the deadline.

##### Issue 1: State filing requirements and independent equity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Artuz v. Bennett distinguishes application-delivery requirements from claim-specific relief bars and expressly reserves the effect of exceptions to filing deadlines. Carey v. Saffold makes valid state untimeliness decisive despite alternative merits discussion. Duncan v. Walker denies statutory exclusion for federal pendency. Pliler v. Ford protects only proved loss caused by the specified court-directed mixed-petition election and reasonable diligence.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The current standard preserves genuine filing prerequisites, actual pendency, state timeliness, and separately supported alternative commencement or equity. The exception-bearing-deadline question remains open; no prior holding establishes a universal equitable-tolling rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2244(d), including all four possible commencement triggers, and Pennsylvania’s then-applicable collateral filing deadlines and exceptions. The application must satisfy the actual state rule; adjudication of an exception does not itself establish satisfaction.
* **Material Other Authority:** Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs supplies traditional equity background rather than a universal guarantee of habeas tolling. The exact Pennsylvania orders supply the state-law classification and disposition.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Artuz already rejects treating every unsuccessful claim as an improperly filed application. Carey already rejects equating a merits discussion with timeliness. Whether an actual filing deadline changes character simply because it has judicially determined exceptions remains unresolved.
* **Open Point:** The federal consequence of an exception-bearing deadline and the reach of equity outside Pliler’s court-created impairment require separate answers or a lawful assumption sufficient to dispose of this record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court reasoned that exceptions requiring judicial examination made Pennsylvania’s deadline a rule of entitlement to relief rather than filing; it also found extraordinary circumstances. The Third Circuit treated the deadline as a genuine filing requirement and rejected the equitable excuse.

Pace argues that a petition accepted, litigated and initially adjudicated on other grounds should count as properly filed. State exhaustion and a later appellate timeliness ruling otherwise create a trap for a prisoner trying to use the state process first. His strongest rule distinguishes a deadline whose applicability depends on claim-specific exceptions from an unconditional delivery bar.

Pennsylvania answers that exceptions define when late delivery is permissible; they do not erase the delivery requirement for applications satisfying none. Clerical acceptance cannot let a prisoner extend a federal limitations period unilaterally through untimely filings. Even if extraordinary impediments may justify equity, Pace’s years of unexplained delay and the additional five-month federal delay defeat diligence here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A statutory-tolling holding for Pace would reverse the limitations barrier and require recalculation, with exhaustion, default and merits restrictions still intact. A state-deadline holding against him permits affirmance only after addressing preserved independent equity. The Court may assume equitable authority without deciding its full scope if the actual unexplained delays defeat a necessary diligence condition. It could remand if material diligence facts remained unresolved, but may not invent an obstacle or automatic protection from good-faith exhaustion.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The statutory rule can cover real filing deadlines with exceptions without recasting ordinary claim-relief bars as filing defects. Equity can be resolved on this record without extinguishing Pliler or adjudicating every attorney error, state-court delay or innocence claim.

[Judicial record and opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/408/); [original opinion text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/03-9627).

## 61. Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-388; 544 U.S. 431
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-27 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act preempts the farmers’ state-law claims that Dow’s Strongarm herbicide damaged their peanut crops.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, affirming judgment that the challenged claims are preempted when liability would induce a change in the federally registered label.
* **Relief Requested:** The farmers seek restoration of their state claims. Dow seeks affirmance of the preemption judgment, not a finding after trial that the product was safe or its representations true.
* **Material Record:** Twenty-nine Texas farmers allege crop damage from Strongarm used in alkaline soils and challenge design, manufacture, testing, warnings, representations and warranties. Dow brought the federal declaratory action; the farmers counterclaimed. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) registration does not establish a finding resolving these asserted tort facts. The claims’ actual state-law elements, rather than their descriptive titles, matter to preemption. Ordinary defect, falsity, reliance, causation and damages remain unadjudicated.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Federal jurisdiction over the underlying declaratory action is the established posture and is not expanded by the preemption defense alone. The record contains no preserved basis to decide every state-law element or all independent conflicts. One independently rejected state-law theory must not be revived solely because other claims survive preemption.
* **Relevant Dates:** Strongarm’s 2000 use and the applicable registration and labeling duties control the alleged conduct. Later label changes may illuminate the record but cannot be substituted for the then-operative federal requirement. Argument occurred January 10, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Dowhal concerns a Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-specific conflict over nicotine warnings and a different saving provision; Bates concerns FIFRA’s label-comparison clause and several nonlabel duties.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Preemption is reviewed as a legal question after identifying the actual state duty and applicable federal command. The procedural posture requires respecting the farmers’ supported allegations without finding them proved. Nonpreemption is not a federal cause of action or an award of damages.

##### Issue 1: Label requirements, parallel duties and actual conflict

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Cipollone v. Liggett Group requires clause- and duty-specific analysis. Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr holds that damages duties can be requirements, but genuinely equal substantive obligations are not additional merely because state damages enforce them. Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. preserves independent actual-conflict analysis while rejecting compliance, regulatory overlap or litigation preference as automatic immunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Current standards compare the actual state conduct obligation with the operative federal source. Three bounded rules are material: identify the covered duty; compare substantive commands; separately test a supported actual conflict. They are compatible, not a presumption that all product suits survive or all labels are immune.
* **Other Governing Law:** FIFRA §136v(a) preserves state authority over sale or use subject to the Act, and §136v(b) prohibits labeling or packaging requirements different from or additional to federal requirements. Section 136(q) defines misbranding; §136a(f)(2) prevents registration from conclusively establishing compliance. No retrospective amendment is used.
* **Material Other Authority:** The applicable FIFRA regulations and actual EPA registration supply federal obligations. The United States’ merits brief supports Dow’s preemption construction; agency litigation advocacy is not itself an enacted label requirement.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law already rejects categorical tort immunity and recognizes parallel obligations. Whether these Texas duties regulate labeling, match federal misbranding duties, or actually conflict requires claim-specific examination under this statute.
* **Open Point:** The precise equivalence of Texas fraud and warning duties to FIFRA and any properly supported independent conflict remain for identification and application, not an assumption that incentive to change a label establishes displacement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit treated likely inducement to change a label as sufficient and characterized design and testing claims as disguised warnings. Dow defends national labeling uniformity and argues that damages can pressure manufacturers to contradict EPA-approved instructions. Its valid limiting point is that a plaintiff cannot avoid §136v(b) by relabeling a demand for different warnings as negligence.

The farmers answer that design, manufacture and testing duties can require safer conduct without requiring a label change; voluntary commercial responses to a verdict are not themselves legal commands. An express warranty enforces the manufacturer’s undertaking. Actual labeling claims survive only to the extent their substantive obligations match federal duties. They do not claim that every tort theory automatically escapes preemption.

The United States supports respondent and national label uniformity, arguing that the actual claims impose additional labeling obligations. That position supports affirmance rather than an independent federal remedy. It must be evaluated against the statutory comparison and the agency’s earlier contrary litigation position, not given controlling force simply because it is the latest filing.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject the inducement theory, restore genuinely nonlabel claims and remand labeling claims for equivalence analysis. It can affirm preemption for a specific duty that actually demands different label content. An independently demonstrated conflict remains available on its preserved record, but a general desire for uniformity cannot replace the express comparison. A remand does not award damages or excuse pleading and proof of state-law elements.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A duty-based rule applies to statutory and common-law obligations alike. Parallel state remedies remain possible even though FIFRA itself supplies no private damages action; a demand for different labeling is preempted regardless of its state-law caption. The rule does not convert federal registration into a finding that no warning or design defect can exist.

#### E. SOURCES

[Original judicial opinion and record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/431/); [United States merits brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/bates-v-agrosciences-amicus-curiae-merits); [official docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/Search.aspx?FileName=/docketfiles/03-388.htm).

## 62. Gates v. Cook

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gates v. Cook
* **Citation or Docket:** 376 F.3d 323 (Fifth Circuit 2004)
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-27 (scheduled merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether prison officials’ deliberate indifference to the combined health risks of unsanitary cells, excessive heat, insect infestations, and related confinement conditions violates the Eighth Amendment and supports targeted injunctive relief? Whether exhaustion of available administrative remedies by a named prisoner satisfies the Prison Litigation Reform Act for a class seeking prospective relief from the same conditions?
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, affirming part of the Unit 32-C injunction, vacating three provisions, and confining four others to that death-row unit.
* **Relief Requested:** Corrections officials seek dismissal for nonexhaustion or reversal of the constitutional injunction. The prisoners defend relief addressing the same ongoing conditions presented administratively. No capital conviction or sentence is challenged.
* **Material Record:** After a consent trial before a magistrate judge, the court found persistent sanitation, plumbing, heat, pest, lighting and mental-health dangers in Unit 32-C. Russell used the emergency grievance route; the Commissioner addressed the substance and denied material concerns without requiring ordinary resubmission. The resulting class injunction contains specific corrective provisions, not a damages award. Some provisions originally reached other parts of Unit 32 without corresponding proof about those prisoners.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The existing Gates litigation was consolidated before the same court, so the asserted competing-decree concern does not require dismissal. An operative injunction is appealable even though compliance reports remain due. Exhaustion must precede suit; the actual emergency procedure and final administrative response matter. The certified class is the affected death-row group. No cross-petition seeking restoration of the provisions vacated below is established.
* **Relevant Dates:** The emergency request began January 31, 2002; the Commissioner’s substantive rejection followed April 15. Trial judgment was May 21, 2003; appellate judgment June 28, 2004. The scheduled April 27, 2005 merits event assumes timely review of that judgment.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Sections 1983 and 1997e(a), Rule 23, and 18 U.S.C. §3626 govern distinct questions. Trial findings receive clear-error review, constitutional sufficiency is legal, and relief must satisfy the PLRA’s necessity, narrowness and least-intrusive-means requirements. Continued danger, not merely historical misconduct, supports prospective relief.

##### Issue 1: Common prospective claims and exhaustion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Booth v. Churner requires completion of an accessible responsive process even when it cannot give the requested damages. Porter v. Nussle covers all prison-life claims, individual or systemic, while preserving actual availability. Neither decides representative exhaustion for a class seeking one common prospective correction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing doctrine requires exhaustion before bringing the action and supplies no general futility exception. Formal process existence does not establish actual availability. The emergency procedure’s own endpoint must be determined rather than replaced with an invented certificate requirement.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1997e(a) speaks of an action brought by a prisoner; Rule 23 permits representative adjudication but cannot excuse a prerequisite the statute actually imposes. Both provisions require a construction respecting the named claimant’s exhausted complaint and the class’s common relief.
* **Material Other Authority:** The written Mississippi grievance process and Commissioner’s responses determine whether this proceeding reached the available administrative endpoint.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The mandatory character of exhaustion is settled. Whether every unnamed class member must repeat an identical systemic grievance remains open; the statutory question cannot be labeled resolved by Porter.
* **Open Point:** The effect of a properly exhausted representative grievance for a certified class seeking the same indivisible prospective relief.

##### Issue 2: Combined conditions and the scope of correction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Farmer v. Brennan requires actual awareness of a substantial serious risk and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits an inference but is not strict liability. Helling v. McKinney allows preventive relief without waiting for disease, with an objective serious-risk inquiry. Wilson v. Seiter requires objective deprivation and culpability, allowing interacting conditions that deny an identifiable human need.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Current standards preserve reasonable-response defenses and require present danger and continuing culpable nonresponse. The later statutory provisions separately require findings supporting the scope of prospective relief; constitutional liability alone does not establish those findings.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1). Courts must give substantial weight to public-safety and criminal-justice effects and may not order relief broader than necessary to correct the federal violation.
* **Material Other Authority:** The trial findings and expert testimony are evidence, not a rule making every professional accreditation standard a constitutional minimum.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Current law governs dangerous combined conditions and ordinary proof of knowledge. It does not prescribe a universal prison temperature, lighting level, laundry rule or psychiatric schedule.
* **Open Point:** Which challenged commands actually correct the proved unit-specific dangers, and whether any reference to outside professional standards exceeds that remedial connection.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Officials argue that all class members must exhaust, that Russell failed to complete the normal three-stage procedure, and that an old class-action framework excludes this suit. The prisoners answer that Russell used the available emergency route, obtained the Commissioner’s merits response, and presented the common conditions now covered by the class. Requiring identical grievances from every unnamed member, they argue, would not provide an additional administrative opportunity to fix that indivisible practice.

On the merits, officials dispute the severity of conditions and point to repair efforts, security considerations, and the risk of judicial management of prison operations. The prisoners rely on credited evidence of prolonged exposure and repeated notice, including interacting heat and insect dangers and serious deficiencies in mental-health care. They seek prevention, so absence of a completed heat illness or infection is not dispositive.

The Fifth Circuit preserved cleaning, heat mitigation, screens, plumbing, lighting and mental-health relief, while eliminating written-maintenance, laundry and exercise provisions and narrowing unit-wide commands. Its analysis does not make every institutional standard constitutionally binding. The officials’ general PLRA-findings objection was also treated as unpreserved below; the Court should not invent a preserved demand for a new trial. No separate Solicitor General rule is established.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Exhaustion could be found satisfied through the actual emergency route and the representative’s common claim; that permits review of the injunction. Requiring every absent member to exhaust would require identifying how that command follows from the statute and what follows for the named prisoner’s own exhausted claim. Failure by the named claimant to complete an actually available step would require dismissal without prejudice, not denial of the constitutional claim on its merits.

On the conditions, the Court can affirm supported relief, vacate provisions lacking a connection to proved serious danger, or remand a specific overbroad command while maintaining independent necessary protection. It cannot replace actual-knowledge findings with mere constructive notice or overturn trial credibility determinations because officials assert improvement. Adequate cessation and nonrecurrence would affect present relief if proved.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A representative-exhaustion rule can cover the same certified prospective conditions claim without covering different incidents or individual damages. Constitutional relief can address interacting risks to sanitation, health and sleep while remaining tied to the proved unit and necessary correction. A court cannot convert accreditation standards wholesale into federal rights.

## 63. Granholm v. Heald

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Granholm v. Heald
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 03-1116, 03-1120 and 03-1274; 544 U.S. 460
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-16 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Commerce Clause, read with the Twenty-first Amendment and federal alcohol statutes, permits Michigan and New York to favor in-state wineries in access to direct shipment to consumers.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit in the two Michigan dockets, invalidating the discriminatory direct-shipping system; Second Circuit in the New York docket, sustaining its system.
* **Relief Requested:** Michigan and its wholesalers seek reversal of the judgment requiring nondiscriminatory treatment. The New York winery challengers seek reversal and relief from discriminatory conditions. No unconditional right to avoid all alcohol licensing is requested.
* **Material Record:** Michigan lets qualifying in-state wineries ship directly while requiring outside wineries to use distribution channels denied the same direct access. New York’s local-premises and related requirements impose a substantially different direct-sale route on outside wineries. Small producers and consumers challenge the competitive disadvantage. The States invoke tax collection, minors’ access, accountability and their distribution systems; the record must support why nondiscriminatory licensing and enforcement would not suffice.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Each docket presents a concrete producer or consumer burden under an operative regulatory system. The cases are consolidated for the common constitutional question, but the judgments and state severability inquiries remain separate. The Court need not decide every feature of a three-tier system or a neutral ban on all direct shipping.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged statutes and factual record precede the December 7, 2004 argument. The merits event is May 16, 2005; later state amendments do not govern the challenged laws.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The two Michigan petitions address the same system from governmental and wholesaler positions. The New York petition attacks a local-presence pathway rather than Michigan’s direct out-of-state prohibition. They receive one listed brief but distinct judgment instructions.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Express discrimination triggers the State’s justification burden; ordinary incidental-burden balancing cannot replace that inquiry. Remedial equality and the State’s lawful severability choices must be distinguished from a judicial decision to adopt a preferred distribution policy.

##### Issue 1: Discriminatory access to the wine market

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Oregon Waste Systems v. Department of Environmental Quality requires a legitimate local purpose that reasonable nondiscriminatory alternatives cannot adequately serve. C & A Carbone v. Town of Clarkstown prevents compulsory private-market favoritism from becoming market participation merely through public regulation. Camps Newfound/Owatonna v. Town of Harrison requires attention to the actual service and protects interstate participants despite local activity or exemption form.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Current standards evaluate the actual linked regulatory operation and forbid using ordinary balancing to validate geographic favoritism. Actual governmental purchases and genuinely distinct public services retain bounded treatment, neither established merely by licensing private wineries.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause; Twenty-first Amendment §2; Webb-Kenyon Act and Wilson Act as effective at the event. Those alcohol-specific sources must be read together; their existence cannot be ignored or treated as a general repeal of constitutional nondiscrimination.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bacchus Imports v. Dias supplies the surviving alcohol-specific rule against protectionist discrimination. The States may contest its reach, but no prior decision overrules it. North Dakota v. United States concerns regulatory powers and materially different import arrangements, not automatic approval of these producer preferences.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The ordinary discrimination rule already governs economic favoritism. Applying the alcohol-specific constitutional and statutory framework to these direct-shipping exceptions is the material question; this is not a new general claim that States lack authority over alcohol.
* **Open Point:** Whether §2 and the federal statutes authorize the identified preferences, whether the supported enforcement concerns defeat neutral alternatives, and which lawful equal-treatment remedies remain under each State’s law.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Michigan and the wholesalers emphasize control of importation, reliable tax collection and prevention of sales to minors. New York stresses its licensed local presence and administrative accountability. Their strongest rule treats direct importation as within a distinctive §2 power rather than ordinary economic regulation. They must explain why that power permits benefits for local producers unavailable on equivalent terms to outside producers.

The wineries and consumers distinguish regulation of alcohol from protection of local businesses. They propose evenhanded permits, tax obligations, jurisdictional consent, delivery verification and sanctions. A State can preserve effective regulation without making geographic origin the condition for a preferred sales channel. They do not claim that the Constitution requires a particular tax rate or unregulated Internet sales.

The Sixth Circuit rejected Michigan’s preference; the Second Circuit accepted New York’s reliance on §2. Those opposite judgments present the real conflict. General assertions of enforcement difficulty are not findings that every neutral alternative fails.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If §2 authorizes these geographic preferences, the Michigan judgments are reversed and New York affirmed. If the ordinary nondiscrimination limit survives, the Court must still test actual justification rather than stop at labeling the systems discriminatory. Failure of that justification supports affirming Michigan and reversing New York, with state-specific relief. Remand on genuinely unresolved evidence would be permissible but cannot be used merely to supply a missing justification after a fully developed opportunity to prove it.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule can forbid unequal direct-shipping access while preserving neutral three-tier or direct-shipping systems. It does not establish that any local office is unconstitutional in every regulatory context or that all alcohol restrictions receive identical treatment regardless of their actual purpose and operation.

## 64. American Civil Liberties Union of Nevada v. Heller

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** American Civil Liberties Union of Nevada v. Heller
* **Citation or Docket:** 378 F.3d 979; Ninth Circuit No. 01-15462
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-16 (scheduled merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a State may require organizations financing printed communications about candidates or ballot questions to disclose sponsors’ names and addresses on the communications while exempting individuals acting independently?
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, vacating the district court’s judgment sustaining Nevada’s law and directing proceedings under its facial-invalidation holding.
* **Relief Requested:** Nevada seeks restoration of the sponsor-identification requirement. The ACLU of Nevada and its executive director seek prospective protection for covered communications they wish to publish without the mandated identification.
* **Material Record:** Nevada Revised Statutes §294A.320 requires the names and addresses of those paying or responsible for paying for printed election-related material. It exempts independently acting natural persons and specified candidate or party communications. Its coverage reaches information relating to candidates and ballot questions without an expenditure threshold or a fixed pre-election window. The amended complaint identifies actual intended speech chilled by the law; it is not merely a general ideological objection.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The prior standing remand was followed by specific allegations of intended covered communications. Standing does not depend on waiting for prosecution. Facial overbreadth remains distinct from concrete reprisal relief and from a claim that all sponsor identification is invalid. A narrowing construction must be textually available; the Court cannot rewrite all information relating to an election into express advocacy by declaration.
* **Relevant Dates:** Appellate judgment: August 6, 2004. The scheduled merits event is May 16, 2005; later statutory amendments do not govern.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Anderson v. Spear concerns polling-place electioneering and campaign financing limits, not this identification mandate.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** First Amendment facial review requires examining the law’s actual coverage, legitimate applications and asserted burdens. Ordinary civil proof governs disputed operational facts. A protected request to avoid threats or reprisals must be substantiated; government retains the burden of justifying remaining exposure.

##### Issue 1: Anonymous leaflets and financially grounded identification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission protects ordinary independently produced individual political leaflets and expressly reserves organizational campaign-finance settings. Kentucky Right to Life v. Stengel sustains payer name-and-address identification for the presented organized paid candidate advocacy. McConnell v. Federal Election Commission sustains the challenged extension of sponsor identification beyond express advocacy where the required material electoral-information or source-tracing connection is established.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing doctrine imposes government-borne justification of the disclosure’s material connection and retains supported associational protection. On-message identification has its own trigger; it does not inherit a separate reporting provision’s $10,000 threshold. McIntyre’s individual rule and its open organizational questions coexist with the later specific holdings.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; the then-effective Nevada statute and its enumerated exemptions. The individual exemption is consistent with McIntyre’s protection for independently produced individual leaflets.
* **Material Other Authority:** The actual Nevada text determines the identified financer and covered publication. NAACP v. Alabama and Brown v. Socialist Workers supply supported associational-injury protection; they do not establish that every organizational payer is entitled to anonymous financed advocacy.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law rejects both universal organizational anonymity and universal disclosure immunity. It expressly permits some on-message identification beyond express advocacy. The lower court’s categorical inference from the individual leaflet case cannot resolve this broader statute.
* **Open Point:** Whether Nevada’s broader information coverage, payor class and address requirement meet the existing material-connection standard across their actual applications, and what facial or protective relief any demonstrated excess warrants.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit treated on-publication identification as materially different from financial reports and extended McIntyre to collective speech. It found Nevada’s all-information reach, exemptions and absence of threshold or time limits insufficiently fitted to fraud prevention, voter information and enforcement. Those are real statutory features, but a categorical dismissal of organizational source information cannot be reconciled with the controlling Stengel and McConnell holdings.

Nevada argues that identifying the financer supplies voters and enforcers useful information, and that exempting an independently acting individual protects the special privacy interest recognized in McIntyre. Its strongest rule permits factual sponsor identification without a spending cap or compelled ideological endorsement. It still must justify the actual breadth and exposed address, rather than assert that every incorporated or collective speaker loses privacy.

The challengers answer that pooling a small amount should not automatically eliminate the anonymity available to one wealthy individual. They also question demands reaching neutral issue information remote from a campaign. Their soundest claim asks whether these applications have the required electoral connection and whether ordinary financial reporting already addresses the asserted enforcement need.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can vacate the facial judgment and remand for application of the current differentiated standard, preserving consideration of both overbreadth and actual protective burdens. It could affirm invalidity upon a sufficient showing that unjustified applications are substantial relative to the law’s legitimate reach, without denying all organizational disclosure. It could sustain ordinary financed electoral applications while requiring supported protection for concrete injuries. A wholesale restoration of the district court’s validity conclusion is not compelled merely by finding error in the appellate standard.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The supported rule preserves individual anonymity and legitimate organized-finance identification, with material justification for actual exposure. No fixed dollar threshold, election window or express-advocacy wording is a universal constitutional prerequisite; their presence or absence matters to the actual fit and burden.
