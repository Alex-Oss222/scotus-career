## 76. Alaska v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Alaska v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 128, Original; 545 U.S. 75
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Original merits action on Alaska's exceptions to the Special Master's report.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-06 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Alaska owns the disputed submerged lands in the Alexander Archipelago as historic inland waters or juridical bays, and whether the United States retained submerged lands within Glacier Bay National Monument at statehood.
* **Question Granted:** Whether Alaska owns the disputed submerged lands in the Alexander Archipelago as historic inland waters or juridical bays, and whether the United States retained submerged lands within Glacier Bay National Monument at statehood.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Special Master recommended judgment for the United States on the disputed Alexander Archipelago waters and Glacier Bay. This is an original action, not appellate review of a lower-court judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Alaska seeks rejection of the adverse recommendations and a declaration of state title. The United States seeks acceptance of the report and a decree recognizing federal title. The separately disclaimed Tongass claim requires no adverse title adjudication.
* **Material Record:** The Alexander Archipelago contains islands, channels and expanses farther than three geographical miles from the ordinary coast. Alaska invokes historic treatment of the waters and an assimilation of island-fringed waters to mainland bays. Glacier Bay presents a different question: inland submerged lands within a federal monument enlarged before statehood, including marine habitat integral to the reservation. The location of the waters, the relevant reservation instruments, Congress's Statehood Act and the historical assertions and recognition of sovereignty matter independently. Geographic enclosure alone is not proof of historic title. The United States disclaimed the contested Tongass lands, removing that adverse claim.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Original jurisdiction rests on the controversy between the State and United States. The exceptions define the live issues; a disclaimer cannot support an advisory adjudication of federal ownership. The earlier United States v. Alaska decree concerned another original action and does not itself transfer the Alexander Archipelago or Glacier Bay lands.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Glacier Bay monument and its enlargement predate Alaska's January 3, 1959 admission. The Special Master's report preceded the February 28, 2005 argument. Only title as fixed by the operative prestatehood acts and the admission legislation is at issue; later implementation cannot retroactively supply a missing retention intention.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Alaska addressed Arctic submerged lands, Dinkum Sands and federal reservations. Its legal rules govern where applicable, but its geographic findings and decree do not decide this original action.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Article III original jurisdiction, the equal-footing rule, the Submerged Lands Act and the Alaska Statehood Act govern the title dispute. The State bears the burden of establishing the asserted historic-waters departure; the United States must establish the requirements for defeating equal-footing title through reservation.

##### Issue 1: Alexander Archipelago inland-water claims

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Alaska applies the Convention's ordinary baseline and island rules and rejects a general island-fringe enlargement of the State's submerged-land entitlement. Specific historic title remains possible on proof appropriate to those waters; international expansion of the territorial sea does not enlarge the statutory three-mile grant.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve the distinction between an actual qualifying island or baseline and an asserted extension of state title. No reusable rule converts the archipelago as a whole into a single mainland coastline.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Submerged Lands Act grants submerged lands beneath inland waters and the specified coastal belt. The Convention on the Territorial Sea and the Contiguous Zone supplies the relevant baseline and juridical-bay definitions adopted in domestic submerged-lands law.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Ordinary coastal and island belts are governed. The historical evidence must independently establish the exceptional inland-water claim; baseline convenience is insufficient.
* **Open Point:** Whether this record proves historic inland waters, and whether the specific proposed bay-closing lines satisfy the Convention rather than an unrecognized island-assimilation theory.

##### Issue 2: Prestatehood retention of Glacier Bay submerged lands

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Alaska requires clear inclusion of submerged lands in a prestatehood reservation and clear intent to defeat future state title for the reservation's public purposes. Idaho v. United States likewise requires examination of the actual reservation and Congress's treatment, not a categorical presumption that every federal reservation retains submerged lands.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The two substantive retention requirements remain distinct. Boundaries identify the property; the purpose and operative legislative treatment must establish retention through admission.
* **Other Governing Law:** The monument proclamations and the Alaska Statehood Act's reservation and wildlife provisions govern whether the equal-footing transfer was displaced for Glacier Bay.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The clear-intent standard is settled. Whether the instruments and marine reservation purposes meet it is an application to this particular reservation.
* **Open Point:** Whether the prestatehood reservation and admission legislation sufficiently identify submerged lands and preserve them in federal ownership.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Alexander Archipelago inland-water claims

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Master found the historical showing insufficient and rejected treating offshore islands as mainland headlands to close the claimed bays.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Alaska argues that governmental practice and the configuration and treatment of the archipelago establish inland waters, or support bay lines incorporating the islands.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** The United States argues that the asserted historical acts do not establish the requisite claim and foreign acquiescence, and that the Convention does not authorize the proposed assimilated coast.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Neither side's position changes ordinary title within a valid three-mile belt. The question concerns the disputed additional waters, not every channel or enclosed water in southeastern Alaska.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** The United States is a party and advances the federal position stated above. No additional distinct Solicitor General or amicus proposal is established by the identified record.

##### Issue 2: Prestatehood retention of Glacier Bay submerged lands

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Master concluded that Glacier Bay's boundaries, purposes and statutory treatment establish federal retention of the disputed submerged lands.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Alaska invokes the strong equal-footing presumption and argues that reservation of a monument or waters does not by itself demonstrate the required intention to withhold submerged title at statehood.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** The United States relies on the expressly marine character of the reservation, its boundaries and Congress's preservation of the relevant federal property and functions.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A scenic or wildlife purpose does not automatically retain every submerged parcel. Conversely, Congress need not use a single prescribed formula if its operative instruments establish both requirements clearly.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** The United States is a party and advances the federal position stated above. No additional distinct Solicitor General or amicus proposal is established by the identified record.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** Original jurisdiction rests on the controversy between the State and United States. The exceptions define the live issues; a disclaimer cannot support an advisory adjudication of federal ownership. The earlier United States v. ##### Issue 1: Alexander Archipelago inland-water claims

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Rejecting the exceptions leaves federal title in the disputed extra-coastal waters. A supported historic-title or Convention-bay finding would require identifying the waters and directing a corresponding title decree, not a statewide declaration of inland seas.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may apply settled baseline rules to the particular geography and historical record, or articulate the limits on island assimilation. A general power to select any archipelagic baseline exceeds the governing instruments.

##### Issue 2: Prestatehood retention of Glacier Bay submerged lands

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Accepting retention sustains federal title in the reserved Glacier Bay lands. Finding either requirement absent yields state title under equal footing and requires a decree locating that title. The disclaimed Tongass claim should be resolved consistently with the disclaimer rather than litigated abstractly.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A reservation-specific construction of the Glacier Bay instruments can decide the controversy without weakening the clear-intent rule for other States or reservations.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The final decree must use the Master's established geographic descriptions and the actual disclaimer. The decree requires the report's geographic descriptions; the Court should not construct new boundary coordinates.

## 77. Spector v. Norwegian Cruise Line Ltd.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Spector v. Norwegian Cruise Line Ltd.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1388; 545 U.S. 119
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-06 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act applies to foreign-flag cruise ships operating in United States waters.
* **Question Granted:** Whether Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act applies to foreign-flag cruise ships operating in United States waters.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit held that Title III does not apply to foreign-flag vessels absent a sufficiently clear congressional statement, reversing the district court's contrary coverage ruling.
* **Relief Requested:** Passengers seek reversal of the categorical exclusion and consideration of their discrimination and accessibility claims. Norwegian seeks affirmance of dismissal on the foreign-vessel ground.
* **Material Record:** Disabled passengers and their companions traveled on Norwegian's foreign-flag cruise ships departing from United States ports. They allege surcharges and discriminatory policies, restrictions on access to programs and facilities, and physical barriers affecting routes, cabins and evacuation. These categories differ: eliminating a discriminatory charge does not necessarily alter a vessel's permanent structure or its internal maritime operations. The pleadings do not establish the feasibility, safety or international compatibility of every requested structural modification.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The granted question concerns statutory coverage, not a final adjudication that each alleged barrier must be removed. The clear-statement principle for a foreign ship's internal affairs requires examination of the particular regulated activity. No finding resolves every structural claim, and the absence of ship-specific accessibility regulations was not the Fifth Circuit's dispositive ground.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Fifth Circuit decided the appeal in January 2004. The relevant statutory enactment is the ADA of 1990. The merits event is scheduled for June 6, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material beyond the authorities addressed below.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** An effective earlier Clark v. Martinez decision could bear on when a single statutory text receives a uniform construction across different applications. None is effective in the current baseline. Any such decision must be applied at its actual scope, distinguishing a construction of statutory meaning from an existing limit concerning foreign vessels' internal affairs.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Domestic application of federal law to ships visiting United States waters differs from extraterritorial regulation. Statutory interpretation must respect established maritime limits without converting them into an exemption for all dealings with passengers.

##### Issue 1: Domestic disability obligations aboard a foreign-flag cruise ship

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision decides the ADA's application to foreign cruise vessels. Existing statutory-construction principles require reading the enacted terms and identifying the actual maritime interference; no historical outcome in this litigation supplies the answer.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No reusable standard creates a general foreign-flag exemption. The inquiry must distinguish statutory coverage, the substance of an individual duty and the available remedy.
* **Other Governing Law:** Title III, 42 U.S.C. §§12181-12189, prohibits specified disability discrimination by public accommodations and transportation services. Barrier removal is required when readily achievable; safety, fundamental alteration and other statutory limitations remain material. The statute contains no express exemption for a ship's restaurants, lodging and entertainment merely because its flag is foreign.
* **Material Other Authority:** Benz v. Compania Naviera Hidalgo and McCulloch v. Sociedad Nacional de Marineros protect foreign vessels' internal labor affairs from regulation without a clear congressional instruction. Their internal-affairs concern must be related to the obligation actually proposed.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The ADA's substantive limitations already govern whether a particular requested accommodation is required. The open coverage question cannot be resolved by assuming every accommodation alters internal maritime affairs.
* **Open Point:** Whether Title III reaches these domestic passenger transactions and how the maritime clear-statement principle limits particular applications.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Domestic disability obligations aboard a foreign-flag cruise ship

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Fifth Circuit treated the foreign flag as sufficient to withhold Title III's application because Congress had not expressly included foreign ships. It did not resolve the merits of the several categories of alleged discrimination.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The passengers argue that ships providing public accommodations and transportation from American ports fall within the statutory text. They seek the ordinary protections against discriminatory treatment and feasible accessibility measures, subject to the ADA's existing limitations.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Norwegian argues that imposing duties on foreign vessels intrudes into their internal affairs and may create conflicts with international safety and design requirements. It urges a categorical clear-statement prerequisite.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Coverage does not establish that every structural alteration is readily achievable or safe. Nonstructural commercial practices and permanent changes to ship design cannot simply be treated as identical. No oral-argument concession is assumed.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** The United States supports application of Title III to foreign cruise ships in United States waters, with maritime and statutory limitations addressed to particular obligations. Its position does not require deciding every accessibility specification before rejecting a categorical exemption.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The granted question concerns statutory coverage, not a final adjudication that each alleged barrier must be removed. The clear-statement principle for a foreign ship's internal affairs requires examination of the particular regulated activity. No finding resolves every structural claim, and the absence of ship-specific accessibility regulations was not the Fifth Circuit's dispositive ground.

##### Issue 1: Domestic disability obligations aboard a foreign-flag cruise ship

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Reverse the categorical exemption and remand for separate consideration of discriminatory policies and physical barriers, including safety and feasibility. Alternatively, a genuine application-specific internal-affairs restriction could exclude particular duties. Affirming the categorical exemption would dispose of all claims on flag status regardless of their domestic commercial character.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A coverage holding can extend to foreign cruise ships' domestic public-accommodation operations without regulating every aspect of foreign maritime governance. A structural-duty ruling requires an identified alteration and the relevant safety and treaty evidence.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The record at this stage does not support a comprehensive reconstruction order or establish all costs and safety constraints. The particular incidents, standing for prospective relief and scope of any class must be verified on remand before relief is fixed.

## 87. Dodd v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dodd v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-5286; 545 U.S. 353
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the one-year period in the then-operative §2255 paragraph 6(3) runs from the Supreme Court's initial recognition of the asserted right or from the later determination that the right applies retroactively on collateral review.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the one-year period in the then-operative §2255 paragraph 6(3) runs from the Supreme Court's initial recognition of the asserted right or from the later determination that the right applies retroactively on collateral review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eleventh Circuit affirmed dismissal of Dodd's §2255 motion as untimely, measuring from the initial Richardson decision rather than its later circuit retroactivity decision.
* **Relief Requested:** Dodd seeks reversal and consideration of his conviction challenge under a clock beginning with retroactivity recognition. The United States seeks affirmance under the initial-recognition date stated in the statute.
* **Material Record:** Dodd was convicted of a continuing criminal enterprise and related offenses and received a 360-month prison sentence. His conviction became final August 6, 1997. On April 4, 2001, he filed a §2255 motion invoking Richardson's requirement that jurors agree on the particular predicate violations composing the continuing series. He relied on the Eleventh Circuit's April 19, 2002 retroactivity ruling to argue for a later statutory starting date. The district court also rejected equitable tolling; the granted question concerns the statutory clock, not a proved extraordinary impediment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The right asserted must be identified from the governing law. Richardson v. United States recognized predicate unanimity on June 1, 1999. A later explanation, application or retroactivity determination does not automatically create a different initial recognition. This case does not concern a newly invented Booker right or authorize treating a proposed same-term brief as a Court decision.
* **Relevant Dates:** Finality: August 6, 1997. Initial recognition in Richardson: June 1, 1999. Motion: April 4, 2001. Circuit retroactivity decision relied on: April 19, 2002. The motion was more than one year after the actual initial recognition.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** This is distinct from Dodd v. Hood River County, which concerns preclusion. Sentencing decisions involving other rights do not replace the recognition date for this asserted continuing-criminal-enterprise unanimity right.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The one-year limitation uses the latest of the statutory triggering events when its conditions are satisfied. Eligibility for a newly recognized right, retroactivity and the clock's starting event are distinct inquiries. Successive-motion authorization has separate requirements.

##### Issue 1: Initial recognition, retroactivity and accrual of the special filing period

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Richardson v. United States requires unanimous agreement beyond a reasonable doubt on the particular qualifying violations composing the continuing series; it became effective June 1, 1999 and reserved harmless-error questions. Clay v. United States identifies ordinary finality when the certiorari period expires. Tyler v. Cain concerns the distinct requirement for making a constitutional rule retroactive in the successive-application setting and does not itself rewrite §2255's limitation trigger.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve Richardson's specific element rule and the distinction between finality, new-right recognition and collateral eligibility. Recognition of an existing rule's application is not necessarily initial recognition of a new right.
* **Other Governing Law:** The then-operative 28 U.S.C. §2255 paragraph 6(3) specifies the date the right was initially recognized by the Supreme Court, conditioned on a newly recognized right made retroactively applicable on collateral review. Its wording and numbering at this event, rather than a later codification alone, govern.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The initial recognition date for the asserted Richardson right is fixed by the controlling decision. Whether the retroactivity condition supplies a different clock is the disputed statutory question.
* **Open Point:** Whether the later retroactivity determination is a condition of using the initial-recognition provision or a separate event that starts a fresh year.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Initial recognition, retroactivity and accrual of the special filing period

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Eleventh Circuit read the provision as identifying one date, the Supreme Court's initial recognition, and treated retroactivity as an eligibility condition. It therefore affirmed the limitations dismissal.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Dodd argues that a period running before the right is established as retroactive can expire before a prisoner can use it, especially when successive-motion rules prevent an earlier filing. He reads the provision as operative only when all conditions are complete.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** The United States argues that Congress expressly chose initial recognition as the date and used the retroactivity clause to limit which rights qualify. Practical difficulties do not authorize substitution of a different event.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** The government's reading does not prove that every prisoner lacks an alternative trigger or available equitable argument. Dodd's reading cannot simply ignore the words identifying initial recognition. This record supplies no new tolling fact or separate authorization entitlement.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** The United States is a party and advances the federal position stated above. No additional distinct Solicitor General or amicus proposal is established by the identified record.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The right asserted must be identified from the governing law. Richardson v. United States recognized predicate unanimity on June 1, 1999. A later explanation, application or retroactivity determination does not automatically create a different initial recognition. This case does not concern a newly invented Booker right or authorize treating a proposed same-term brief as a Court decision.

##### Issue 1: Initial recognition, retroactivity and accrual of the special filing period

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Affirm because the motion was filed more than a year after the actual initial recognition, assuming the retroactivity condition in Dodd's favor; reverse under a later-trigger construction and remand the remaining conditions; or address a separately preserved alternative trigger only if supported. No path automatically establishes a Richardson violation or entitlement to release.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A construction of the statutory date applies to first and successive motions using this paragraph, while leaving their distinct eligibility and authorization requirements intact.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** No independent Supreme Court retroactivity determination for the asserted Richardson right has been established. The statutory accrual question can be resolved assuming retroactivity in Dodd's favor, without deciding that separate condition.

## 89. Graham County Soil & Water Conservation District v. United States ex rel. Wilson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Graham County Soil & Water Conservation District v. United States ex rel. Wilson
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-169; 545 U.S. 409
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the six-year period in 31 U.S.C. §3731(b)(1) governs an employee's retaliation action under the then-operative §3730(h), or whether an analogous state period must be borrowed.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the six-year period in 31 U.S.C. §3731(b)(1) governs an employee's retaliation action under the then-operative §3730(h), or whether an analogous state period must be borrowed.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit reversed dismissal of Wilson's retaliation claim, holding that the federal six-year period applied. The district court had used a three-year North Carolina period and found the claim untimely.
* **Relief Requested:** The governmental defendants seek reversal of the federal-period ruling and application of the appropriate state limitation. Wilson seeks reinstatement of her retaliation claim under the six-year provision.
* **Material Record:** Wilson worked as a secretary for the conservation district. She alleges that officials retaliated after she alerted federal authorities to suspected false claims and cooperated with an investigation, eventually forcing her resignation in 1997. She filed a qui tam action and an independent retaliation claim in 2001. The retaliation allegations do not themselves prove that any false claim was actually submitted, and success on the retaliation claim does not depend on a successful fraud recovery.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The granted issue concerns the retaliation limitations period, not the merits or jurisdictional status of the accompanying qui tam claim. Accrual, the correct state analogue and any applicable tolling remain distinct. The 1986 creation of the retaliation cause predates §1658's post-December 1, 1990 criterion.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress added the relevant retaliation remedy and revised the limitations section in 1986. The alleged forced resignation occurred in 1997; the action was filed in 2001. The Fourth Circuit's reviewed decision issued in 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material beyond the authorities addressed below.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** A court first asks whether federal law supplies an applicable limitations period. When it does not, the ordinary rule borrows an appropriate analogous state period, subject to the limited established exception for a clearly closer and significantly more suitable federal analogue.

##### Issue 1: The limitation applicable to an independent retaliation claim

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States ex rel. McKenzie v. BellSouth Telecommunications holds that §3730(h) has independent protected-activity, employer-knowledge and causation requirements and does not depend on successful qui tam recovery. North Star Steel Co. v. Thomas preserves ordinary state borrowing and confines substitution of a federal period. Jones v. R. R. Donnelley & Sons Co. applies §1658 when a post-1990 enactment made the asserted claim possible; this 1986 claim does not qualify on that ground.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve the independent retaliation claim and claim-specific identification of the enactment that made liability possible. Borrowing cannot precede interpretation of a potentially applicable express federal period.
* **Other Governing Law:** The then-operative §3731(b)(1) refers to civil actions under §3730 but starts six years from a violation of §3729. Section 3730(h) addresses retaliation, which can occur without a completed §3729 fraud. No later statutory retaliation period may be imported into the 2005 analysis.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** McKenzie establishes why fraud and retaliation cannot be assumed coterminous. It does not decide this limitations question. The relationship between the broad cross-reference and the fraud-based trigger remains open.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statutory context limits §3731(b)(1) to fraud-enforcement actions and, if so, which state period properly governs Wilson's retaliation claim.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The limitation applicable to an independent retaliation claim

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Fourth Circuit emphasized that retaliation is a civil action under §3730 and applied the six-year period. The district court instead borrowed a state period and dismissed.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The defendants argue that a clock triggered by a false claim cannot coherently govern retaliation that may occur before, after or without an actual false claim. They seek ordinary state borrowing.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Wilson argues that the provision's reference to civil actions under §3730 includes subsection (h), and that Congress's simultaneous 1986 amendments support a uniform federal limitation protecting whistleblowers.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A court cannot disregard the broad cross-reference, but must also give effect to the specified triggering violation. Borrowing a state period does not authorize selecting a hostile or unrelated limitation simply to defeat the federal remedy.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** The United States supports applying the federal six-year period to retaliation claims. Its position favors nationwide uniformity and reads the statutory cross-reference to include subsection (h); the consequence would be restoration of Wilson's claim against the limitations ground decided below.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The granted issue concerns the retaliation limitations period, not the merits or jurisdictional status of the accompanying qui tam claim. Accrual, the correct state analogue and any applicable tolling remain distinct. The 1986 creation of the retaliation cause predates §1658's post-December 1, 1990 criterion.

##### Issue 1: The limitation applicable to an independent retaliation claim

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Apply the six-year provision and remand any remaining timeliness issues; hold it inapplicable and remand selection of the closest state analogue; or consider another federal period only if the established borrowing exception is actually satisfied. The Court should not affirm an unexamined state analogue merely because it rejects the federal one.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A construction of the 1986 text distinguishes fraud enforcement from retaliation without narrowing the substantive protection recognized in McKenzie or importing later amendments.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The district court's three-year selection is not automatically endorsed. The record must establish the correct analogy and all relevant dates before dismissal can be sustained. Later amendments to the False Claims Act (FCA) cannot retroactively replace the operative text by assumption.

### 28 U.S.C. §2255 (2004), limitation and successive-motion text

A 1-year period of limitation shall apply to a motion under this section. The limitation period shall run from the latest of—
(1) the date on which the judgment of conviction becomes final;
(2) the date on which the impediment to making a motion created by governmental action in violation of the Constitution or laws of the United States is removed, if the movant was prevented from making a motion by such governmental action;
(3) the date on which the right asserted was initially recognized by the Supreme Court, if that right has been newly recognized by the Supreme Court and made retroactively applicable to cases on collateral review; or
(4) the date on which the facts supporting the claim or claims presented could have been discovered through the exercise of due diligence.
Except as provided in section 408 of the Controlled Substances Act, in all proceedings brought under this section, and any subsequent proceedings on review, the court may appoint counsel, except as provided by a rule promulgated by the Supreme Court pursuant to statutory authority. Appointment of counsel under this section shall be governed by section 3006A of title 18.
A second or successive motion must be certified as provided in section 2244 by a panel of the appropriate court of appeals to contain—
(1) newly discovered evidence that, if proven and viewed in light of the evidence as a whole, would be sufficient to establish by clear and convincing evidence that no reasonable factfinder would have found the movant guilty of the offense; or
(2) a new rule of constitutional law, made retroactive to cases on collateral review by the Supreme Court, that was previously unavailable.

### 31 U.S.C. §3731 (2004)

§3731. False claims procedure
(a) A subpena requiring the attendance of a witness at a trial or hearing conducted under section 3730 of this title may be served at any place in the United States.
(b) A civil action under section 3730 may not be brought—

### 31 U.S.C. §3730(h) (2004)

(h) Any employee who is discharged, demoted, suspended, threatened, harassed, or in any other manner discriminated against in the terms and conditions of employment by his or her employer because of lawful acts done by the employee on behalf of the employee or others in furtherance of an action under this section, including investigation for, initiation of, testimony for, or assistance in an action filed or to be filed under this section, shall be entitled to all relief necessary to make the employee whole. Such relief shall include reinstatement with the same seniority status such employee would have had but for the discrimination, 2 times the amount of back pay, interest on the back pay, and compensation for any special damages sustained as a result of the discrimination, including litigation costs and reasonable attorneys’ fees. An employee may bring an action in the appropriate district court of the United States for the relief provided in this subsection.
