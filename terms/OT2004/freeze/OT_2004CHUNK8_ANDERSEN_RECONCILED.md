## Reconciled propositions

**A1 — Knowing corrupt persuasion.** Retain A1 in full. Section 1512(b)(2), as applicable to the charged 2001 conduct, requires conscious wrongful persuasion with the prescribed withholding or impairment purpose. The Government need not prove knowledge of the statutory citation or a legal conclusion about the precise offense. Neither ordinary persuasion, retention policies, legal advice nor invocation of valid rights is inherently corrupt, while none of those labels shields knowingly wrongful obstruction. This charge's honest-lawful-belief permission and factfinding-impediment definition fail to communicate that element.

**A2 — Particular contemplated proceeding.** Retain A2, with the particularity already inherent in its rejection of an abstract possible future governmental interest made explicit: the persuader must contemplate an identifiable official proceeding in which the records might be material, and must act with the prescribed purpose toward that use. The proceeding need not be pending, about to begin, certain to occur or assigned a known date; no subpoena or success is required. This does not transplant section 1503's pendency rule or create a separate likely-success element. The historical reliance on Aguilar reinforces the required statutory object; it does not override section 1512(e)(1).

The qualifications in frozen A2 stand independently. Subsection (e)(2) removes admissibility and absence of privilege as prerequisites. Subsection (f) removes knowledge of federal character, not contemplation and intent concerning the proceeding. Subsection (d) is the separately burdened affirmative defense for solely lawful conduct with the sole purpose of inducing truthful testimony; it is not a general document-destruction exemption or a shift of the Government's element burden. Later amendments do not govern this charge. The unpreserved separate informal-inquiry coverage theory is not decided.

**A3 — This verdict and further proceedings.** Retain the setting aside of this conviction for prejudicial instructional error and further lawful proceedings under correctly stated elements. The Government cannot establish beyond reasonable doubt that the errors did not contribute to this verdict: the charge affirmatively admitted the contested insufficient mental state, and neither its required findings nor a reliable whole-record connection supplies the missing disputed finding. Deliberate withholding under this charge is not necessarily conscious wrongful persuasion toward contemplated official use. A prior deadlock neither independently proves prejudice nor cures the error.

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer retain the bounded reasoned extension of Neder's qualified method to these identified instruction defects. An adequate reasonable-doubt jury adjudication occurred, but with defective definitions or omission of identified elements; this differs from Sullivan's destruction of the reasonable-doubt framework. The extension and the nonharmless application are confined to these errors. Scalia and Ginsburg join nonharmlessness and the trial-error remedy while withholding assent from the broader inferential method and its classification extension; genuine equivalent findings do not exist here. Their narrower position cannot be counted as assent to the six Associates' method, and the six's method cannot be restated as a universal classification rule.

The further-harmlessness-review alternative receives substantive consideration. Neder's fraud remand supports such an inquiry where a reliable actual-verdict connection remains available; here the charge expressly removed the contested protection and the supplied evidence leaves competing culpability inferences. The historical immediate reversal is consistent with rejecting another inquiry on this particular record. No independent insufficiency ruling is supplied, so no acquittal follows. The statutory judgment and conviction must be reversed/set aside with remand, rather than leaving the conviction operative while simply announcing new law.
