## 65. Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-163; 544 U.S. 528
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-23 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the substantially-advances-legitimate-state-interests formulation supplies an independent test for a regulatory taking, and whether it supports invalidating Hawaii’s statutory limit on service-station rents.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, affirming a judgment invalidating Hawaii’s rent cap under the substantially-advances formulation after judicial evaluation of its economic efficacy.
* **Relief Requested:** Hawaii seeks reversal of the takings invalidation. Chevron seeks to preserve relief against the rent cap based on its asserted failure to advance the State’s objective.
* **Material Record:** Hawaii limited rents charged by oil companies to dealer-operated service stations. Competing economic evidence concerned whether the law would help consumers or instead encourage rent restructuring and market effects contrary to the State’s aim. Chevron’s surviving theory tests the regulation’s efficacy rather than claiming a physical appropriation, a demanded property dedication, or total elimination of use. No trial finding converts the efficacy dispute into one of those different takings categories.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged theory is properly presented as a facial takings ground. No independent due-process disposition is established for this review. A decision that the chosen test is wrong does not resolve an unpresented compensation claim under a different standard or find every rent cap constitutional.
* **Relevant Dates:** The rent-cap enactment and trial record precede the February 22, 2005 argument. The May 23 event fixes the operative law. No later takings decision, including later scheduled Kelo or San Remo matters, is imported.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Kelo concerns public use in an actual condemnation; San Remo concerns federal litigation after state adjudication. This case concerns the substantive test for an alleged regulatory taking. Those distinct issues cannot supply a prospective result here.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The constitutional question is reviewed de novo. A court must identify the source and nature of the property injury before selecting compensation or invalidation. Economic disagreement with regulation is not automatically a taking, and an unpreserved due-process theory cannot be decided by changing the label.

##### Issue 1: Property burden versus regulatory efficacy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council governs total beneficial-use deprivation with substantiated background-property limits. Dolan v. City of Tigard imposes nexus and rough proportionality for demanded property dedications. Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency rejects categorical segmentation of a temporary restriction and preserves ordinary Penn Central analysis. Del Monte Dunes at Monterey v. City of Monterey actually accepted the received Agins purpose-relationship alternative in a municipal damages verdict and assigned particular application facts to a jury.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing standards retain these separate fields and explicitly preserve the received Agins formulation in Del Monte Dunes without comprehensive approval of every version. Thus the efficacy question is not settled against Chevron; rejecting that independent predicate requires reconsidering Agins and the corresponding Del Monte Dunes holding.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments; Hawaii’s then-effective rent-cap legislation. The constitutional compensation requirement and substantive due process have distinct operations. A newly adopted judicial rule is not an amendment to the state statute.
* **Material Other Authority:** Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp. treats compelled permanent physical occupation as a taking without balancing; this rent regulation does not authorize occupation. Agins v. City of Tiburon supplies the formulation at issue; Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City supplies the surviving partial-regulatory-burden inquiry. These authorities perform different work and cannot be reduced to one generalized reasonableness test.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Entering law already distinguishes physical taking, total deprivation, partial regulatory burden and demanded dedication. Del Monte Dunes preserves a particular purpose-relationship application, but does not conclusively determine whether a general rent cap’s efficacy alone establishes a compensable taking.
* **Open Point:** Whether the purpose-relationship formulation remains an independent takings predicate, what becomes of the corresponding prior application, and what precise relief follows for Chevron’s chosen theory.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The lower court treated substantial advancement as a freestanding takings inquiry and weighed competing economic explanations of the rent cap’s likely effects. Chevron defends that approach as an established formulation protecting owners from regulations disconnected from legitimate public purposes. Its strongest argument is not mere reliance on historical language: the Del Monte Dunes judgment actually allowed a purpose-relationship alternative to support compensation.

Hawaii answers that efficacy measures the wisdom or rationality of governmental action, not the character or severity of the burden placed on property. The same ineffective regulation could burden property little or destroy its value, and monetary compensation would not cure a law forbidden because it lacks a valid regulatory basis. The State seeks rejection of that independent takings theory, while ordinary property-burden and genuinely preserved due-process rules remain separate.

The United States supported rejection of the substantially-advances test in the historical litigation. No oral-argument concession about a surviving alternative claim is inferred from an opinion; the actual scope of Chevron’s preserved claim controls.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance could retain substantial advancement as an independent taking test and sustain the lower court’s supported efficacy findings; it would require explaining the relationship between that test and compensation. Reversal could reject that predicate and limit Agins and Del Monte Dunes at the corresponding scope. A narrower ruling could reject heightened judicial efficacy review for this general economic regulation while leaving particular-denial applications intact, but that would need a principled source-based distinction. A remand cannot manufacture a Penn Central or due-process claim that Chevron did not preserve.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A categorical rejection of efficacy as a freestanding taking predicate would reshape that part of entering law while preserving recognized property-burden and exaction rules. A narrower general-regulation ruling would leave the accepted particular-denial theory for future cases. Neither formulation determines the validity of all economic regulation or every compensation remedy.

## 66. Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165; 544 U.S. 550
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-23 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether mandatory beef-promotion assessments violate the First Amendment when the challenged advertising is effectively controlled by the Federal Government, and whether asserted attribution to producers establishes the presented facial defect.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit, affirming a judgment against the compulsory beef advertising assessment; the district court certified the First Amendment component while other challenges remained.
* **Relief Requested:** The Secretary and intervening producers seek vacatur of the compelled-subsidy judgment and enforcement of the statutory assessment. Objecting producers seek continued protection from financing the advertising, with distinct unresolved claims preserved.
* **Material Record:** The Beef Act imposes a $1-per-head assessment and funds promotional and other projects. The Secretary appoints the Beef Board, has removal authority over the relevant committee, and approves advertising content; department representatives participate in its development. The ads promote generic beef, which some producers believe impairs their differentiated marketing. Some messages identify the broad producer community as the funding source. The statute does not require a particular dissenter personally to endorse the advertisement.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Government-speech attribution was raised and decided below, unlike the unpresented defense in United Foods. The certified judgment permits review of the decided component without adjudicating every statutory or producer-communications claim. A facial attribution claim requires attention to what the statute mandates, while an individual-attribution injury requires supporting facts.
* **Relevant Dates:** The operative 1985 Act and implementing order precede the December 8, 2004 argument. The May 23, 2005 event controls. No later amendment or subsequent program administration is presumed.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The two dockets involve governmental and intervenor defenses of the same assessment. They do not create a second listed brief. United Foods concerned the independent private advertising theory on the argument and record then presented.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The court identifies actual control over the challenged message, not merely the nominal status of the board. Compelled payment, compelled personal utterance and an independently justified private collective program are distinct legal questions. A finding on one does not dispose of every constitutional or statutory claim.

##### Issue 1: Operational government control and compelled funding

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott permits germane generic promotion within a sufficiently collective regulatory program on its stated safeguards. United States v. United Foods rejects mandatory private advertising in an otherwise independent market absent an independent sufficient common function. It expressly reserves operational government-speech attribution and does not hold that a marketing board can never convey governmental speech.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Current standards distinguish actual government speech funded through lawful public exactions from the private-association subsidy defect. Industry benefit, majority support and the right to counteradvertise do not alone cure a private-subsidy violation. Neither precedent supplies a universal government-label exemption.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; Beef Promotion and Research Act, 7 U.S.C. §§2901 and following; the applicable Beef Order. Congress’s message and funding directions, actual executive supervision and statutory authority must each be identified.
* **Material Other Authority:** Keller v. State Bar of California distinguishes a body’s general governmental status from the particular expression funded. Ordinary government may convey policy messages with public funds; Barnette and Wooley retain their separate protection against compelled personal expression.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The independent-private-market advertising rule is settled. Whether this operationally controlled campaign is the Government’s message and whether targeted financing changes that result were not decided in United Foods.
* **Open Point:** The necessary operational control for this claim, the significance of targeted assessments, and the extent to which concrete attribution rather than funding alone creates a different injury.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Eighth Circuit treated even governmental speech as potentially subject to the compelled-subsidy objection. The Secretary answers that government may choose its own lawful message and finance it through an enacted assessment, and that actual control of this campaign is extensive rather than nominal. The intervening producers defend the same program; industry approval alone is not their strongest constitutional ground.

The objectors emphasize the private industry role, the board’s producer composition and the ads’ apparent identification with producers. They argue that government cannot convert a private association into its own speaker through reserved approval authority, particularly where accountability is obscure and funding comes from unwilling competitors. Their strongest limiting principle distinguishes real governmental authorship and control from a private message bearing a government-approved label.

United Foods does not settle that factual attribution question because the defense there was not developed below. Conversely, rejecting the private-subsidy theory here would not dispose of the distinct claims about producer communications or statutory administration left unresolved. No claim of individual endorsement is presumed proved from the generic funding line alone.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If actual governmental control determines the message, the Court may vacate the compelled-subsidy judgment without requiring a general tax rather than a targeted assessment. If the campaign remains private in operation, United Foods requires an independent collective justification and industry benefit is insufficient. A supported compelled-attribution claim may require a different remedy from invalidating the whole Act. Unreached claims may return for ordinary proceedings rather than being silently dismissed.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule can distinguish government selection and supervision of its own message from supervision of a private speaker’s compliance. It does not exempt governmental religious establishment, unlawful discrimination, compelled personal endorsement or speech beyond statutory authority from their own constitutional or statutory constraints.

[Judicial record, statute and opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/550/).

## 67. Clingman v. Beaver

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Clingman v. Beaver
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-37; 544 U.S. 581
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-23 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Oklahoma may prevent a political party from inviting registered members of other parties to vote in its primary while allowing the party to invite unaffiliated voters.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit, reversing judgment for Oklahoma and invalidating the challenged semiclosed-primary restriction.
* **Relief Requested:** Oklahoma seeks reversal and preservation of its membership condition. The Libertarian Party of Oklahoma and voters registered with other parties seek recognition of their mutually desired participation in its primary.
* **Material Record:** The Libertarian Party sought to invite all registered voters into its primary. The State permitted independents but excluded voters retaining another party registration. The voters seek participation in the invited primary without changing that registration. The challenged condition concerns a party’s chosen electorate, not a demand to vote twice or enter an objecting party’s nomination process. Broader complaints about other registration and ballot-access provisions were not litigated below.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The original primary’s completion does not dispose of the continuing challenged election rule. The party and excluded invited voters assert concrete associational injury. The Court must not invalidate additional ballot-access laws that were neither raised nor decided; contextual facts may explain burden without turning them into new claims.
* **Relevant Dates:** The party’s invitation arose in 2000 and the case proceeded through a bench trial and appellate reversal. Argument was January 19, 2005. Relief is prospective for the operative rule; no completed election is reopened.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The later Green Party matter concerns retention of enrolled affiliations after ballot-status loss, not cross-party primary invitations. Its proposed result cannot govern this case.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Anderson and Burdick require evaluating actual burden, precise governmental interests and fit. Severe burdens require compelling justification and narrow tailoring; lesser burdens still require a genuine connection to sufficient regulatory interests. A reciprocal choice to associate cannot be treated as the absence of any protected interest simply because state registration is unchanged.

##### Issue 1: Voluntary party invitations and actual burdens

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party invalidates the categorical ban on a consenting, qualified cross-nomination under a substantial-but-not-severe burden analysis. It rejects preservation of established parties’ competitive position as sufficient stability. California Democratic Party v. Jones protects an objecting party from compulsory outsider nomination and expressly leaves voluntary invitations and ordinary registration rules unresolved.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing standards protect meaningful legally operative association rather than treating external endorsement as an adequate substitute. They preserve contextual scrutiny and genuine electoral administration, not automatic strict scrutiny for all primary rules. Timmons’s invalidation of the cross-nomination ban supplies the operative voluntary-association precedent.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; Oklahoma’s operative primary statute. The State retains authority to administer elections, prevent duplicate voting and apply lawful registration rules, subject to their actual constitutional effects.
* **Material Other Authority:** Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut protects a party’s invitation to independents but leaves the invitation to other parties’ registrants open. Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee protects party governance while preserving legitimate election administration.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The party’s and voter’s reciprocal association is protected, and burden-sensitive review is settled. Jones does not decide voluntary crossover by analogy to forced crossover. The actual invitation to voters retaining other registrations remains an open application.
* **Open Point:** Whether the membership-change condition is sufficiently justified for these consenting participants, and how relief can preserve an objecting party’s exclusion and one-person, one-primary administration.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court found the burden modest and accepted interests in identifiable parties and accurate primary results. The Tenth Circuit deemed the burden severe and found no narrowly tailored compelling justification. Neither characterization can simply substitute for the controlling contextual inquiry.

Oklahoma emphasizes the ease of changing registration, party continuity, prevention of raiding and orderly nomination. Its strongest argument is that a primary is a legally consequential selection process, not merely an invitation to discuss politics. The State may require a meaningful electoral choice and prevent a voter from participating in competing primaries. It must nevertheless connect those aims to excluding a voter whom this party deliberately invites.

The party and voters argue that partial, temporary or overlapping support remains association. Forced disaffiliation burdens both the voter’s existing affiliation and the inviting party’s chosen outreach. The party accepts the risk of sincere or strategic crossover, while neutral single-primary rules can prevent multiple selection. Their claim does not authorize entry into an unwilling party’s primary. No concession about actual duplicate voting or successful raiding is inferred.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may uphold the restriction after finding a limited burden and sufficient fit, without declaring reciprocal participation unprotected. It may affirm invalidation under the existing contextual standard without adopting a rule that every invitation restriction is severe. Relief should then prohibit enforcement against the voluntary invitation while preserving lawful single-primary and election-administration rules. A new challenge to the cumulative operation of unlitigated statutes cannot be decided here, though a properly preserved future challenge remains possible.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The question supports a rule about consensual participation in an inviting party’s primary, not a universal right of outsiders to nominate another party’s candidates. It does not determine national convention credentials, every affiliation deadline, sore-loser candidacy or multiple-primary voting.

[Judicial opinions and Oklahoma record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/544/581/).

## 68. Deck v. Missouri

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Deck v. Missouri
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-5293; 544 U.S. 622
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-23 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Constitution permits visible shackling throughout a capital penalty proceeding without a defendant-specific security justification, and what prejudice or harmless-error showing governs the remedy.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Supreme Court of Missouri, affirming Deck’s death sentence after a new penalty proceeding.
* **Relief Requested:** Deck seeks vacatur of the sentence and a lawful new penalty proceeding. Missouri seeks affirmance based on the convicted status, asserted security considerations and lack of demonstrated prejudice.
* **Material Record:** Deck’s convictions remained intact after an earlier sentence was set aside. At the replacement penalty proceeding he appeared in handcuffs, leg irons and a belly chain. Counsel repeatedly objected to the jury’s seeing the restraints. The trial court referred to his conviction and the reassurance restraints would provide, without a particularized finding of current danger or escape risk. The challenged proceeding selected punishment; guilt was not retried.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The repeated objections preserve the federal claim. Whether restraints were visible is material; the record of the objections and court responses supports visibility, rather than a claim about unseen security measures. No new challenge to the underlying convictions or general capital-punishment validity is presented.
* **Relevant Dates:** The replacement sentencing followed the 2002 vacatur of the prior sentence. Missouri affirmed in 2004; argument occurred March 1, 2005. The May 23 event concerns direct review, not collateral retroactivity.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Roper concerns eligibility for execution by offense-age category, while this case concerns sentencing procedure for an eligible defendant. No age-based issue is presented here.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Due process governs the court’s use of visibly prejudicial restraints. Justification is assessed from actual case-specific circumstances; direct-review harmlessness is separate from establishing the violation. No automatic structural-error rule or burden of proving a counterfactual jury vote may be assumed.

##### Issue 1: Courtroom security, punishment selection and prejudice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Riggins v. Nevada requires actual overriding justification for an objectionable compelled trial measure and recognizes that its supported effects may be impossible to reconstruct from a transcript. It does not itself adopt a universal Chapman rule or decide the unpreserved capital-sentencing-display theory. Its later treatment through Sell concerns medication, not a general authorization of restraints.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules retain individualized justification, reasonable alternatives and protection of a fair defense. Riggins does not itself decide Chapman’s application to visible shackling during capital sentencing; that question remains open.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause. Chapman v. California supplies the general direct-review standard for preserved constitutional trial error; Illinois v. Allen permits necessary courtroom control; Holbrook v. Flynn addresses inherently prejudicial security measures.
* **Material Other Authority:** Estelle v. Williams distinguishes compelled appearance from measures accepted without timely objection. Its waiver setting does not erase Deck’s repeated objections or establish harmlessness.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The State’s authority to maintain security is settled, as is the need to distinguish actual necessity from routine prejudicial appearance. Entering law has not categorically resolved visible restraints during this separate capital sentencing stage.
* **Open Point:** Whether that stage retains the constitutional protection, what defendant-specific finding is required, and whether harmlessness rather than defendant-proved prejudice controls the consequence.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Missouri’s court accepted the penalty-stage distinction and emphasized that Deck had not shown actual prejudice. The State argues that the presumption of innocence no longer applies after conviction and that visible restraint reassures jurors and supports security. Its sound limiting point is that a court must remain able to respond to a real danger, including danger demonstrated by the defendant’s conduct and history.

Deck answers that punishment remains undecided, and visible chains can communicate current dangerousness at the very moment jurors choose between life and death. Restraints also affect communication with counsel and the dignity of adjudication. A prior conviction alone cannot justify visible restraint for every capital defendant, and the resulting prejudice cannot reliably be measured from a cold record.

The controversy is not whether any security measure is forbidden or whether a jury must ignore the conviction. It concerns the court’s additional message through a compelled visible condition without a particularized need.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain a penalty-stage exception, leaving the sentence intact, or require particularized justification at sentencing as at trial. Under the latter rule it must decide whether the record contains that justification and whether the sentence can survive ordinary constitutional harmless-error review. A justified security measure requires no new sentencing; an unjustified measure requires relief unless the State proves harmlessness under the applicable standard. A rule automatically reversing every security error would exceed the supported distinction.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The question supports protection against visible restraints in the jury’s capital punishment selection. It does not settle every noncapital hearing, concealed restraint, accidental brief sighting or emergency response. Actual necessity remains capable of justifying appropriate measures.

## 69. Medellín v. Dretke

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Medellín v. Dretke
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-5928; 544 U.S. 660
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-23 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal court is bound by the International Court of Justice’s Avena judgment requiring reconsideration of Medellín’s Vienna Convention claim without procedural default, and whether a federal court should give that judgment effect through judicial comity and uniform treaty interpretation.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, denying a certificate of appealability from denial of federal habeas relief.
* **Relief Requested:** Medellín seeks review and reconsideration of his consular-notification claim, not automatic acquittal. He also requests that further Supreme Court proceedings await the new Texas application. Texas defends the federal certificate denial. The United States supports that denial while advancing a distinct state-court implementation route.
* **Material Record:** Medellín, a Mexican national convicted of capital murder, first raised consular notification in collateral review. The state court rejected the claim on several grounds, including lack of established harm; the federal district court denied relief. During appellate certificate proceedings the ICJ issued Avena. After certiorari was granted, the President issued a memorandum concerning state-court compliance and Medellín presented new grounds to the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals. That application remains pending at this event.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review concerns a certificate denial, not an authorized appellate merits judgment. Section 2253’s constitutional-right requirement, §2254’s applicable restrictions, treaty collateral cognizability, nonretroactivity, default, actual prejudice and exhaustion require distinct analysis. The President’s new memorandum was not adjudicated in the judgment under review. The new Texas proceeding does not itself establish mootness or guarantee relief.
* **Relevant Dates:** Avena was delivered March 31, 2004. The Fifth Circuit ruled May 20, 2004. The President’s memorandum is dated February 28, 2005. The new state application was filed March 24 and remained pending at the March 28 argument.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The Breard proceedings involved interim requests and different domestic remedial submissions. They did not decide the individual-right or final-ICJ-judgment merits now asserted.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Slack v. McDaniel and Miller-El v. Cockrell permit review of a judicial certificate denial and require reasonable debate under the actual habeas framework rather than completed merits proof. They do not convert a treaty allegation automatically into a constitutional claim. Merits eligibility, Article III authority, discretionary review and any execution stay must remain separate.

##### Issue 1: Domestic review gateways and changed posture

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Slack and Miller-El govern certification. Reed v. Farley separates federal-law jurisdiction from collateral relief and requires the specified fundamental defect for the ordinary nonconstitutional statutory claim it decided. Breard v. Greene and Republic of Paraguay v. Gilmore made confined interim insufficiency rulings, expressly reserving treaty enforceability and ultimate default, prejudice and remedy questions.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The current certificate standard requires a substantial constitutional showing and, after procedural dismissal, reasonable debate on both the constitutional claim and the procedural disposition. Existing law contains no general consular merits rule contradicting the reservations in Holdings. Reed cannot silently be enlarged into a categorical bar on all treaty claims.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1254, 2253 and 2254; Vienna Convention Article 36; Optional Protocol; U.N. Charter Article 94; the ICJ Statute; the February 2005 presidential memorandum. International obligation, domestic enforceability, constitutional executive authority and procedural remedy are distinct questions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Avena is the international judgment whose domestic effect is disputed. Sosa v. Alvarez-Machain preserves the need to identify the domestic legal source and remedial channel rather than equating an international norm with an available domestic action.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Certification review and the limited effect of the prior interim consular orders are settled. The direct domestic force of Avena and the memorandum remain unresolved in the governing law; neither automatic supremacy nor categorical nonenforcement follows.
* **Open Point:** Whether this federal certificate posture is the proper vehicle to resolve those questions while an unadjudicated state application invokes a new implementation source; if reached, each gateway and the precise source of a review remedy must be decided.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit treated the consular claim as procedurally barred and lacking an individually enforceable right, declining to give Avena dispositive force. Its historical reliance on Breard’s broader real-world language cannot be transferred into the controlling confined interim decisions.

Medellín argues that the United States consented to the ICJ’s resolution of the treaty dispute and must provide the review the judgment specifies. Alternatively, uniform treaty interpretation and comity warrant giving its construction effect. He seeks assessment of actual prejudice, not a presumption that notification failure invalidates the conviction. His motion to await Texas action recognizes the new domestic implementation route while preserving the federal controversy.

Texas invokes default, absence of established prejudice, the limited certificate statute and the distinction between an international obligation and a privately enforceable domestic remedy. The United States supports the federal judgment on certificate and habeas grounds, yet argues that the President’s memorandum directs state-court implementation of Avena. That is materially different from both an automatic federal writ and unqualified denial of all review, and its asserted authority must not be assumed proved.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may dismiss the writ as improvidently granted because the new state proceeding and unadjudicated presidential ground materially change the vehicle, without affirming treaty nonenforceability. It may retain the case for a bounded period while that proceeding runs, subject to the term’s scheduling constraints and a real disposition rather than an invented later result. It may vacate the certificate denial if the statutory showing is satisfied and remand, but a debatable treaty issue alone cannot replace the required constitutional component. A final merits ruling would require resolution of the actually applicable gateways before determining whether binding effect or comity supplies review and reconsideration. No path automatically awards release or suppression.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A vehicle disposition establishes no rule about every treaty or presidential foreign-affairs act. A merits decision could address Avena’s particular binding or persuasive force, but would still need a lawful domestic channel and the specified prejudice review. International judgment, individual right and remedy cannot be merged into one conclusion.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** The record does not establish the new Texas proceeding’s result, any execution date requiring an interim order, or final resolution of each federal gateway. The judicial text contains differing references to the state filing chronology; the material established fact is that the March 2005 application preceded argument. No 2008 ruling is eligible.

## 70. Lofton v. Secretary of the Department of Children & Family Services

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lofton v. Secretary of the Department of Children & Family Services
* **Citation or Docket:** 358 F.3d 804 (Eleventh Circuit 2004)
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-23 (scheduled merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Florida violates the Equal Protection Clause by categorically excluding gay and lesbian adults from adoption while permitting them to serve as foster parents and legal guardians? Whether the Due Process Clause protects established family relationships between foster parents or legal guardians and the children they have raised against a categorical statutory bar to adoption?
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, affirming judgment for Florida on the constitutional challenge to its adoption exclusion.
* **Relief Requested:** The adult and child plaintiffs seek removal of the categorical statutory barrier and an opportunity for adoption consideration. Florida seeks preservation of the exclusion. No finding establishes that each requested adoption serves the particular child’s interests.
* **Material Record:** Lofton raised Doe from infancy; Houghton cared for Roe from age four and became his co-guardian after a placement by Roe’s biological father. The father later consented to termination of parental rights. Lofton declined a proposed guardianship unless it led toward adoption because it would sacrifice foster-care payments and threaten Medicaid coverage. These facts support the asserted practical importance of permanency, without deciding the availability or adequacy of alternative protection. Other adult plaintiffs seek adoption eligibility. Florida permits gay adults to foster or serve as guardians but categorically bars them from adopting under §63.042(3), while permitting unmarried heterosexual applicants. The lower court relied on a claimed preference for stable mother-and-father households and the possible future marriage of heterosexual single applicants. The record does not establish that the excluded adults are unfit or that their actual homes harm the children.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The categorical eligibility barrier supplies a live injury to adults seeking consideration; the existing-care claims require attention to each actual relationship. The parties stipulated that there is no general fundamental right to adopt or be adopted, but contested whether existing family relationships and constitutional equality forbid the exclusion. No removal order or final child-placement determination is under review.
* **Relevant Dates:** Panel judgment January 28, 2004; rehearing denied July 21, 2004, supplying the stated timely OT 2004 route. The proposed May 23, 2005 event excludes later changes in Florida adoption law.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review of the summary judgment must use the actual governing scrutiny and preserve genuine material disputes. A statutory-benefit label does not remove equal-protection review. Invalidating a discriminatory eligibility criterion is different from awarding adoption or displacing neutral child-welfare determinations.

##### Issue 1: Sexual-orientation exclusion and child welfare

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Shahar v. Bowers already requires intermediate scrutiny for sexual-orientation classifications, including conduct rules serving as deliberate status proxies. Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati v. City of Cincinnati applies that standard outside employment and rejects unsupported class-specific exclusion. Romer v. Evans remains an ordinary-review floor, not the exclusive tier.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government must establish a genuine important objective and substantial relationship to the actual classification; stereotype, private bias, moral disapproval and merely conceivable post hoc reasons do not carry its burden. Supported professional judgment matters but is not conclusive. The standard requires neither least restrictive means nor an individual exception in every case.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause; Florida Statutes §63.042(3) and the surrounding eligibility and child-interest provisions. The state conduct-based construction does not itself defeat review when same-sex relationship status is the operative reason for exclusion.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lawrence v. Texas protects private consensual adult intimacy against moral-disapproval-based criminal punishment and leaves civil relationship status open. It does not displace Shahar’s independently operative equality rule.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Intermediate scrutiny is already controlling, not a new tier requested here. The legal label attached to the adoption exclusion cannot reduce it to rational basis. The actual child-welfare fit remains the application question.
* **Open Point:** Whether Florida’s categorical exclusion is substantially related to the genuine child-welfare objective on this record, and how to remove the barrier while preserving individual adoption adjudication.

##### Issue 2: Existing care relationships and a requested new legal status

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Troxel v. Granville protects a fit parent’s authority against displacement by mere judicial preference but supplies no complete common scrutiny formula or automatic right of foster caregivers to become adoptive parents. Lawrence did not decide entitlement to civil family status. The governing equality rule remains independently applicable regardless of a claimed fundamental adoption right.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Current standards require identifying the actual protected relationship, governmental interference and claimed remedy. Neither emotional closeness alone nor the State’s statutory role conclusively determines every family-liberty question. The baseline does not establish an automatic conversion of foster care or guardianship into adoption.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment Due Process Clause; the actual state-created foster and guardianship arrangements, including their legal duration and retained rights.
* **Material Other Authority:** Smith v. Organization of Foster Families for Equality and Reform distinguishes the source and expectations of foster care from natural parenthood without declaring every foster relationship constitutionally irrelevant.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing parental liberty is protected, but a request to create a new permanent status is distinct from an order removing a child or overriding a legal guardian’s present authority. No such removal order is before the Court.
* **Open Point:** Whether the categorical eligibility barrier impermissibly burdens the integrity of these established family relationships independently of equal protection. No entitlement to an automatic adoption decree is claimed.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Eleventh Circuit applied rational basis and treated any conceivable developmental benefit of a heterosexual household as sufficient. That is not the entering governing standard. Florida’s strongest argument is its responsibility to select permanent homes in children’s interests, a task requiring predictive judgment and attention to stability. It distinguishes temporary foster care from permanent adoption and argues that unmarried heterosexual applicants can eventually form the preferred household.

The challengers identify the mismatch: a categorical sexual-orientation bar excludes caregivers regardless of their demonstrated parenting, while the State entrusts children to them in other legal forms and admits heterosexual single applicants. They seek actual justification, not a presumption that every proposed placement must succeed. Their family-integrity argument additionally concerns the insecurity of children already raised in these homes, rather than a universal wish to obtain an unrelated child.

Florida answers that meaningful personal bonds do not create a constitutional entitlement to a new legal status and that the record contains no order dissolving the existing relationships. That answer is relevant to the independent liberty remedy but cannot cure discriminatory eligibility.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The equality judgment may be reversed because the wrong scrutiny was used, with a remand for genuine application if necessary findings are missing, or with an injunction against the categorical bar if the developed record establishes no substantial fit. Affirmance requires satisfying the actual intermediate standard, not merely identifying a conceivable legitimate aim. If equality does not supply complete relief, the Court must determine whether the categorical eligibility barrier violates protection of existing family integrity, distinguishing a constitutional opportunity to seek permanent legal parentage from protection of current care and custody. The plaintiffs expressly disclaimed a general right to adopt; rejecting an automatic adoption entitlement would not answer their actual due-process theory. An affirmative family-liberty holding would need to identify the protected existing relationship and the actual interference, rather than equate every emotionally close household with legal parenthood.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The equality rule reaches the categorical orientation barrier and permits ordinary neutral fitness and child-interest decisions. If that ground supplies complete relief, the alternative substantive-due-process theory need not be reached. The family-liberty question is limited to the adoption-status claim actually pleaded; it does not decide an absent removal, visitation, custody or marriage case.

## 71. Arthur Andersen LLP v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Arthur Andersen LLP v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-368; 544 U.S. 696
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-05-31 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the instructions adequately required knowing corrupt persuasion and the necessary connection to an official proceeding under the version of 18 U.S.C. §1512(b)(2) governing the 2001 conduct.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed the Southern District of Texas conviction, rejecting the instructional objections and sustaining the construction reported at 374 F.3d 281.
* **Relief Requested:** Andersen seeks reversal of its obstruction conviction for erroneous instructions; the Government seeks affirmance under its construction of corrupt persuasion and proceeding intent.
* **Material Record:** Andersen audited Enron and provided related services. In autumn 2001, accounting problems, a Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) inquiry, anticipated litigation, internal legal discussions and directions to follow the firm’s document policy preceded extensive destruction of paper and electronic records. The SEC opened a formal investigation on October 30 and served subpoenas on November 8. A November 9 message stopped shredding. The indictment concerned persuasion between October 10 and November 9, not a prosecution under later document-destruction legislation. The charge permitted conviction despite an honest and sincere belief that the conduct was lawful, omitted dishonesty from its definition, and included merely impeding government factfinding. The jury deliberated, reported deadlock, received an additional charge and ultimately convicted. These facts permit competing culpability inferences; the verdict must be examined under the instructions actually given.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenge concerns preserved construction and instructional issues after a final conviction. A trial-error ruling does not itself establish evidentiary insufficiency or corporate innocence. The relevant statutory version is the 2000 Code applicable to 2001 conduct; later amendments cannot supply missing elements retroactively. Neither destruction before a subpoena nor use of an ordinary company policy conclusively resolves culpability.
* **Relevant Dates:** Charged conduct: October 10 through November 9, 2001. Indictment: March 2002. Merits event: May 31, 2005. The later enactments in 2002 do not govern the charged offense.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Each actual element must be submitted to the jury and proved beyond reasonable doubt. Preserved element error is assessed under the qualified actual-verdict inquiry of Neder v. United States: the Government must establish beyond reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to this verdict, with attention to the charge, actual findings and disputed evidence. Strong evidence alone is insufficient.

##### Issue 1: Knowing corrupt persuasion and the official-proceeding object

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Aguilar requires a known relationship between the charged endeavor and pending judicial work under §1503. Its six-Justice statutory rationale does not construe every obstruction provision or impose a universal subpoena requirement. Its distinct eight-Justice interception holding likewise preserves offense-specific knowledge and purpose.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the enacted culpability language and object together. An unsuccessful obstructive endeavor can be criminal, but a possible eventual effect on official work cannot replace the relationship and knowledge the particular statute requires.
* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable §1512(b)(2)(A) and (B) address knowing corrupt persuasion of another person, with intent to cause withholding or impairment of evidence for an official proceeding. Section 1512(e)(1), as then numbered, says the proceeding need not already be pending or about to be instituted. Section 1515 supplies relevant proceeding definitions.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Fifth Circuit treated an improper-purpose intent to impede factfinding as sufficient and rejected a consciousness-of-wrongdoing requirement. That is the construction under review.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Aguilar’s pending-proceeding rule is specific to §1503. Section 1512 expressly reaches some anticipatory conduct, so importing pendency would contradict its text. Conversely, removing pendency does not necessarily remove every contemplated-proceeding connection.
* **Open Point:** Whether knowingly modifies corrupt persuasion, what wrongful awareness that phrase demands, and how specifically a future proceeding must be contemplated remain to be resolved under §1512.

##### Issue 2: Instructional consequences and available relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Neder v. United States establishes jury determination of actual elements and the Government’s burden to prove that preserved element error did not contribute to the verdict. Its seven-Justice qualified inference method permits more than mechanically equivalent findings, while preserving reliable-verdict limits.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine what the jury actually had to find. Instructions authorizing conviction on an insufficient mental state cannot be cured simply by an appellate court finding that guilt could have been established under a correct charge.
* **Other Governing Law:** Ordinary appellate correction and double-jeopardy limits distinguish trial error from legally insufficient proof. Reversal for instruction error ordinarily permits further lawful proceedings; an acquittal remedy requires its own predicate.
* **Material Other Authority:** The trial record contains substantial evidence about knowledge, internal discussions and timing, but also a charge expressly denying the asserted culpability protection. Those features matter to the error’s actual contribution.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Neder does not make every element error structural, nor does it authorize a court to select a missing finding from genuinely disputed evidence.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual charge and findings establish harmlessness under the correctly construed elements, and whether an independently preserved sufficiency claim requires separate disposition.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Andersen argues that knowingly corrupt persuasion requires awareness of wrongdoing, not merely an intent to prevent government access to records. Routine document management and advice to assert valid rights illustrate why persuasion and withholding cannot themselves establish corruption. It also argues that the charge eliminated a contemplated proceeding to which the records could matter. The Government emphasizes the statute’s anticipatory reach, the firm’s awareness of Enron’s mounting legal exposure, the timing and scale of destruction, and the danger of allowing destruction until formal process arrives. It reads corrupt persuasion through an improper obstructive purpose rather than knowledge of legal prohibition. Both sides can accept that the law does not require knowledge of a statutory citation; their dispute is whether the instructions allowed conviction for nonwrongful conduct and abstract future government interest.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may adopt the Government’s construction and affirm if the actual charge satisfies it; construe the culpability and nexus requirements more narrowly and reverse for material instructional error; or remand for a bounded harmlessness determination if the existing verdict permits that inquiry. Acquittal requires an independent insufficiency determination, not an inference from defective instructions. A rule requiring a pending subpoena would contradict the enacted anticipatory provision, while a rule criminalizing all destruction of potentially interesting records would omit the statute’s limiting language.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The charged persuasion offense, its awareness and proceeding connection, and the consequences of this instruction. No general ban on document retention policies, generalized legal-advice immunity, or construction of subsequently enacted offenses is necessary.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Court reviews the legal basis for dismissing statutory claims, not a final order compelling individual religious accommodations. A federal statutory protection may exceed the constitutional minimum within an independently valid congressional power, but its application remains subject to the Constitution and ordinary remedial requirements.

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet permits accommodation beyond what free exercise independently commands while prohibiting denominational allocation of civil authority. Agostini v. Felton retains Lemon as one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function. Its former independent prongs are no longer a separate or fallback method.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify a specific prohibition or distinct applicable doctrine, an established permissible relationship, or otherwise actual attribution and function. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody is governed by accommodation principles, not automatically classified as forbidden governmental devotional financing.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Kiryas Joel permits accommodation but does not authorize governmental theology, denominational privilege or religious control of public authority. Agostini does not make secular benefit labels conclusive or abolish the distinct accommodation inquiry.
* **Open Point:** Whether this general institutional protection itself amounts to prohibited sponsorship and how accommodation limits should shape its administration without rewriting the statutory burden.

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** City of Boerne v. Flores invalidates the Religious Freedom Restoration Act of 1993 (RFRA)’s universal state and local command as Fourteenth Amendment enforcement while preserving the distinction between constitutional minimum and permissible legislation supported by another power. Its reserved-power holding does not establish RLUIPA’s validity or invalidity under Spending or Commerce authority.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish the enacted statutory right, the source of legislative power, constitutional limits and available relief. Actual burden precedes the statute’s government justification burden; security assertions must be evaluated in the institutional setting rather than accepted as conclusions.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 3 includes federal-financial-assistance or commerce predicates. The Act authorizes appropriate relief against a government. The present grant does not resolve every funding condition, commerce application, damages question or individual defendant’s liability.
* **Material Other Authority:** Officials invoke safety, order, resources, effects on other inmates and potential incentives for strategic claims. The claimants invoke selective denial of practices already permitted to other faiths and accommodation of religious needs government confinement makes difficult to meet.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Sincere religious exercise does not automatically prevail over other persons’ rights or concrete security needs. Conversely, the general existence of prison management concerns does not make every application unconstitutional.
* **Open Point:** Which claims meet coverage and substantial-burden thresholds, what particular restrictions satisfy the statutory defense, and what prospective relief remains available to each claimant.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The inmates argue that the statute restores opportunities for private practice that government confinement removes, without preferring one faith or requiring religious adherence. The United States defends Congress’s neutral accommodation design and the statute’s capacity to account for legitimate institutional interests. Officials emphasize the asymmetry between religious and nonreligious demands, the burden on guards and other prisoners, potentially dangerous practices, administrative expense and the possibility of opportunistic religious claims. The inmates answer that sincerity, actual burden, statutory coverage and a context-sensitive justification inquiry address those concerns, and that hypothetical abusive applications do not establish facial invalidity. Neither side’s general framing supplies missing findings about a particular item, ceremony or staffing demand.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject facial invalidity and remand the statutory claims, reserving their individual merits and distinct power defenses. It can accept the categorical religious-preference theory and sustain dismissal, which would require reconciling Kiryas Joel’s express accommodation permission. It can construe an actually ambiguous statutory feature to preserve constitutional limits, but cannot replace compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means requirements with ordinary rationality review. No lawful disposition on this record commands every accommodation requested.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The facial Establishment Clause challenge to institutionalized-persons protection. Land-use provisions, all constitutional power questions, particular accommodations and damages are outside the necessary holding.

## 73. Tory v. Cochran

* **Case Name:** Tory v. Cochran

* **October Term:** 2004

* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, affirmed a Superior Court permanent injunction obtained by Johnnie Cochran against Ulysses Tory and Ruth Craft.
* **Relief Requested:** Tory and Craft seek relief from the speech injunction. After Cochran’s death, his widow seeks substitution; respondent’s side suggests mootness while maintaining that enforceable relief remains necessary. Petitioners agree to substitution but deny mootness.

Those findings support a real remedial interest and are not erased by the constitutional challenge. The decree nevertheless extends beyond adjudicated false assertions: it prohibits picketing, signs and oral statements about Cochran and his firm in any public forum by petitioners and their agents or representatives. Cochran died after oral argument. The order contains no automatic death termination; the respondent continues to claim enforceability, including an asserted interest in the law practice. The firm itself was not a party to the original action.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Determine whether the still-operative decree creates continuing restraint, not merely whether a decedent retains a defamation cause of action. Substitution does not adjudicate the widow’s independent entitlement to every form of relief. Current validity and remedy must account for death; the Court need not decide every possible injunction against repeated adjudicated defamation.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** An ongoing enforceable injunction may preserve a live controversy after a change affecting its justification. Constitutional review separates the predicate wrong, responsibility of persons bound, breadth of prospective relief and any remaining party’s entitlement. A prudential dismissal differs from a jurisdictional mootness disposition and cannot be described as a merits approval.

##### Issue 1: Prospective speech restraints following adjudicated misconduct

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Madsen v. Women’s Health Center requires provision-specific remedial fit and forbids suppressing unrelated lawful advocacy merely because defendants previously acted unlawfully. Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York preserves those limits and confines party extension to actual concert with notice. Alexander v. United States distinguishes punishment that does not prohibit future publication from an actual future-speech ban and preserves an independent expressive-burden inquiry.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify what the decree actually forbids and whom it binds. The more-demanding injunction standard in Madsen concerns content-neutral relief; it cannot automatically validate a content-based ban on a person or topic. Past liability is not a license for unlimited prospective silence.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First and Fourteenth Amendments govern state-court restraints. State defamation and coercion remedies remain available within constitutional limits; a finding of some false speech does not adjudicate every future statement false.
* **Material Other Authority:** The lower courts relied on persistent defamatory conduct and an intention to continue it absent court intervention. The injunction’s text reaches truthful or otherwise protected statements as well as repetition of the adjudicated misconduct.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Madsen and Schenck supply fit and responsibility principles, not a definitive rule for all injunctions against adjudicated defamation. Alexander’s classification depends on its lack of a future-publication prohibition, a feature absent here.
* **Open Point:** Whether this topic-wide decree can be justified, and what narrower relief, if any, could validly address adjudicated false assertions or independently unlawful conduct.

##### Issue 2: Death, continuing restraint and remedial scope

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The governing law require an actual live controversy and relief tied to present injury. Existing speech-injunction decisions preserve independent lawful remedies while removing invalid restraints and their operative predicates; no prior decision resolves this exact post-death posture.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A change in underlying circumstances can weaken the justification for prospective relief without automatically extinguishing the judicial command or the burden it imposes. Determine actual enforceability and present claim of authority.
* **Other Governing Law:** California law and the decree’s terms control whether the order remains operative and who may seek enforcement or modification. State procedures allowing later constitutional objections do not themselves eliminate the present restraint.
* **Material Other Authority:** The parties disagree about mootness, but agree to substitution. Respondent asserts continued need and enforceability. No identified provision automatically dissolves the order at death.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The Court can decide the constitutional validity of an operative restraint without deciding all state-law rights in the deceased lawyer’s practice or allowing a nonparty to obtain new relief automatically.
* **Open Point:** Whether to decide the existing decree’s breadth now, return first for consideration of changed circumstances, or dismiss the writ prudentially while leaving modification to the state courts.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Petitioners distinguish a damages judgment for adjudicated defamation from an advance ban on any public discussion of a person and firm. They argue that even truthful criticism and unrelated speech are forbidden, that death does not terminate the command, and that the decree remains a concrete restraint. Respondent invokes repeated coercive misconduct, the threat of continuation, the difficulty of protecting against persistent false statements through damages alone, and remaining interests in the law practice. The suggestion of mootness turns on death and the absence of a posthumous reputational claim; the assertion of continued enforceability points in the opposite direction. A prudential alternative would leave changed-circumstances questions to California without deciding the original broad constitutional question. That option does not establish that the decree is constitutionally valid.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may find a continuing controversy and vacate the overbroad restraint, while reserving properly tailored new relief. It may remand for clarification of state-law enforceability and present justification if those uncertainties genuinely preclude constitutional resolution. A dismissal as improvidently granted remains procedurally possible after the vehicle changes. A mootness dismissal requires actual elimination of the live restraint; death alone cannot supply that missing premise while the decree is asserted to remain enforceable. Affirmance requires justification for the decree’s actual breadth, not merely the original wrong.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The existing injunction’s complete public-topic prohibition and the concrete consequence of death. No universal holding on all post-adjudication defamation injunctions, sanctions for threats, or ownership of the law firm is necessary.

Experts linked expense and provider loss to delayed care and health risk. Arizona invokes patient safety, including a patient’s death after gravely deficient care; providers dispute the scheme’s fit and motivation. The on-site physician must have admitting privileges and remain until patients are stable and ready to leave recovery. Inspectors receive immediate operating-hours access, broad unredacted records, and copies. A separate requirement sends identified ultrasound prints to contractor APEX, whose contract provides seven-year retention with inadequately specified later storage. Serious-incident reports go to professional licensing boards under distinct confidentiality statutes. Enforcement was stayed by stipulation during the district proceedings and appeals.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Cross-motions do not eliminate disputed facts; view each motion against its nonmovant. Provider injury and representation of affected patients must remain tied to the actual duties. The incident-report privacy objection was first raised on appeal and entertained there; address that limited adjudicated issue without inventing district findings. Other unbriefed vagueness objections were abandoned. Some evidence was challenged by motions to strike but not relied on for the appellate disposition; no contested submission becomes an admitted fact here. The remanded access claim prevents assuming final liability or a final severability package.
* **Relevant Dates:** Original opinion June 18, 2004; amendment and denial of rehearing August 23, 2004. The scheduled May 31, 2005 review concerns the amended judgment.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Greenville Women’s Clinic v. Commissioner is a prior binding decision concerning other regulations, not a companion grant. Its fact-specific determinations cannot be transferred to Arizona.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review legal conclusions independently and preserve genuinely disputed material facts under ordinary summary-judgment rules. Prospective injury can be proved without waiting for actual closure or a completed disclosure. Each constitutional claim retains its own predicates and burden; failure on one theory cannot erase another. Severability is governed by Arizona law after the precise invalid duties and applications are identified.

##### Issue 1: Cumulative burdens and the complete reproductive-liberty standard

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies the complete six-step previability framework through five direct joins. Greenville Women’s Clinic v. Commissioner and Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall preserve its separate operation for inspection and disclosure burdens. No later simulated authority reduces it to substantial-obstacle review alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify actual duty, protected baseline and exceptions; identify operation and purpose; establish attributable interference, comparison and stable affected population, including supported subgroups and cumulative effects; apply the protections in order; protect clinical judgment, truthful information and timely necessary care; then tailor relief. Baseline invasion, effective private veto, denial of necessary care, obstructive purpose and substantial obstacle independently fail. Remaining severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, answering a concrete practicable substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference, independent constitutional protections remain.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects the previability choice without medical-necessity proof or official approval of reasons. Viability and necessary life-and-health protection remain individualized. State health regulation is permissible when its actual duties satisfy the governing protections.
* **Material Other Authority:** Specific projected expenses, lost provider capacity and delays are material evidence rather than automatically insufficient predictions. The lower court’s historical substantial-obstacle formulation does not supply the full governing standard.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** No universal dollar threshold, numerical facial formula, mandatory new study or cheapest-policy rule applies. Claimants prove duty, causation, population and triggering interference; the State then bears the applicable justification and fit burden.
* **Open Point:** Which retained requirements, individually or together, cause the demonstrated interference and satisfy their corresponding justification standard after factual disputes are resolved.

##### Issue 2: Provider distinctions and independent sex equality

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Virginia requires genuine important objectives and substantial fit for actual sex classifications, without role stereotypes. Klinger v. Department of Corrections distinguishes an actual classification from a genuinely neutral decision challenged for purposeful discrimination. Casey preserves independent constitutional protections; it is not a categorical substitute for equal protection.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the classification and comparators before selecting review. Ordinary professional and volume distinctions require rational relationship to a legitimate end; actual sex classifications invoke demanding intermediate scrutiny. Disparate effects alone do not prove purposeful discrimination in a genuinely neutral choice. Patient access and confidentiality retain their separate tests.
* **Other Governing Law:** The scheme differentiates procedure categories and monthly volume. The challengers also compare abortion services sought by women with comparably risky services sought by men. A health objective does not by itself establish the fit of an actual sex distinction.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Ninth Circuit accepted rational explanations for professional and numerical categories and treated the patients’ sex-equality objection as subsumed by its access analysis. The latter proposition must be tested against governing law rather than adopted as precedent.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Neither every abortion regulation nor every medical distinction automatically triggers strict scrutiny. Equally, an actual sex classification receives no categorical medical-regulation exemption.
* **Open Point:** Whether the preserved patient claim establishes an actual sex classification or purposeful sex disadvantage, and whether the relevant distinction is justified under the governing independent standard.

##### Issue 3: Warrantless entry and medical premises

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Ferguson v. City of Charleston requires justification for the actual medical search operation and rejects treating attendance for care as waiver. Its holding concerns a coordinated testing and prosecution program, not every administrative inspection. The governing law preserve purpose-limited administrative inspection without deciding that all private medical clinics are closely regulated industries.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Commercial premises remain protected. A claimed administrative exception must establish its actual predicates and limits; adoption of a new pervasive regulatory scheme does not by itself erase reasonable privacy expectations or supply unrestricted discretion.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment applies through the Fourteenth Amendment. The challenged regulation requires immediate access during operating hours; related statutes authorize compliance and complaint inspections. Ordinary consent, warrants and genuine exigencies remain separate possible authority.
* **Material Other Authority:** New York v. Burger supplies the closely regulated industry exception and the need for a constitutionally adequate substitute for a warrant. The statutory object and time restriction do not resolve discretion over rooms, patients and records.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Ferguson does not categorically invalidate all mixed-purpose programs or establish that disclosure alone is a separate search. Patient privacy and inspectors’ facility-entry authority must be analyzed at their actual scope.
* **Open Point:** Whether Arizona has established an exception authorizing this mandatory entry and whether the scheme sufficiently bounds officials’ discretion.

##### Issue 4: Identified records, ultrasound prints and serious-incident reports

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Greenville Women’s Clinic v. Commissioner requires a sufficient connection among information, recipients, official function, scope, uses and safeguards. Its six-Justice information rationale governs within its actual holding. Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall and Herring v. Keenan require recipient-specific assessment of authorized transfer and redisclosure.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Lawful confidential oversight is permissible; each datum need not be indispensable. Broad confidentiality labels do not establish actual access limits, and hypothetical unauthorized leakage alone does not establish invalidity. Redaction, coding, consent or targeted follow-up matter where adequate but are not universal prerequisites. Proved additional access burdens independently receive Casey review.
* **Other Governing Law:** Department record access, APEX ultrasound review and professional-board incident investigation are different authorizations. The last concerns abortion-related death or serious injury and has separate state-law public-disclosure protections.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record identifies broad department access without supported need-to-know controls, inadequately protected contractor access and retention, and narrower board reporting linked to serious incidents. An intended coding policy is not a binding operative safeguard.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** No universal compelling-need rule governs all identified medical inspection. Nor do reporting holdings establish unrestricted governmental entitlement to identities or all medical histories.
* **Open Point:** Which permissions are unjustified on the established record, what lawful oversight survives, and what precise restraint repairs the proved defect without creating a universal redaction command.

##### Issue 5: Notice and independent dignity-based sanctions

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Greenville Women’s Clinic v. Commissioner sustains ascertainable clinical and facility duties but forbids independent sanctions based on undefined additional practices selected by administrators. Its notice holding and six-Justice residual-sanctions holding distinguish professional judgment from standardless compulsory duties.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read terms in their actual professional and enforcement setting. Reasonable judgment is permitted; a missing legal criterion cannot be supplied by mitigation, a warning or official assurance. Criminal and serious licensing consequences increase the importance of ascertainable obligations.
* **Other Governing Law:** The rule requires consideration, respect and full recognition of dignity and individuality. It is part of a regulatory regime carrying civil and criminal consequences; no authoritative limiting construction makes these words defined clinical terms.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record identifies no professional standard determining when full recognition is achieved. Other actual safety and treatment obligations are distinct and are not invalid simply because they involve qualitative medical judgments.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The prohibition concerns an undefined independently enforceable branch, not every qualitative standard or an aspirational patient-care statement.
* **Open Point:** Whether this language supplies a sufficiently determinate duty and how relief can leave definite professional obligations intact.

##### Issue 6: Admitting privileges, private control and retained access review

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Greenville Women’s Clinic v. Commissioner permits objective qualifications and emergency-care arrangements but prohibits an outsider’s arbitrary legally uncontrolled licensing veto. Its unanimous qualification holding does not validate every admitting-privileges condition or extinguish Casey review.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Examine actual private discretion, legal constraints, reasonably usable review and supported prospective denial. Public-hospital constraints do not automatically bind private institutions; existing operation is evidence, not a requirement to wait for closure.
* **Other Governing Law:** Arizona decisions require hospital standards related to legitimate institutional interests, notice and hearing, and judicial review for arbitrariness and evidentiary support. The on-site requirement applies even though the operating physician need not personally hold privileges in a sufficiently staffed clinic.
* **Material Other Authority:** The record does not establish an actual or supported impending arbitrary refusal based on abortion practice. Hospital interests and limited judicial review nevertheless require examination of actual operation rather than reliance on a label.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Rejecting a standardless-delegation theory does not establish that the condition’s staffing, application, meeting and on-call costs are justified under Casey. Privileges are not constitutionally required simply because the State labels them protective.
* **Open Point:** Whether the present legal constraints defeat the facial arbitrary-control claim, while the condition’s cumulative patient-access effects remain to be tried.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Providers challenge both the package and discrete duties. They rely on specified costs, reduced staffing and procedure availability, delay-related risk, comparative safety and substantial contrary medical testimony. They argue that the State may not dismiss those predictions for lack of a single exact fee increase or ignore the burdens of a condition that survives another constitutional theory. Arizona invokes competent staffing, reliable emergency care, correction of serious malpractice and administrable licensing categories. It argues that regulation can improve safety without replicating rules for every procedure and that manageable incremental costs do not establish constitutional injury.

For equality, providers contest both profession-based selectivity and differential treatment of women’s health needs. Arizona relies on targeted oversight and the disproportionate administrative burden of licensing very small practices. These arguments require distinguishing ordinary professional line drawing from an actual sex classification or prohibited purpose. For searches, Arizona invokes pervasive regulation; providers point to protected medical premises and open-ended entry. For information, the State asserts audit and complaint needs and confidentiality training; challengers identify unnecessary identifiers, insufficient recipient controls and nonbinding promises. Serious-incident reports involve a materially more specific function and safeguards.

The dignity dispute pits an asserted ordinary expectation of respectful care against unpredictable punishment based on subjective views. On delegation, providers emphasize hospital boards’ influence over clinic operation; Arizona invokes substantive standards and judicial review. Both the existence of review and its practical ability to prevent arbitrary control matter. No actual arbitrary denial is established.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirming the remand on access burdens does not award providers ultimate invalidation; the developed disputes require adjudication under the full entering framework. The Court may preserve rational professional and volume distinctions while correcting the categorical absorption of sex equality into access review. Fourth Amendment entry and informational transfer require separate dispositions: unlawful open-ended entry does not eliminate every lawful records inspection, and a fitted serious-incident report need not share the fate of broad department or contractor access. A confined vagueness ruling can remove independent punishment under the undefined dignity clause while preserving ascertainable duties. The private-control objection can fail on this record while admitting-privileges burdens remain part of the access inquiry. Once all defects are fixed, Arizona severability law determines what regulatory package remains; no automatic whole-scheme invalidation or blanket reinstatement follows.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Six issue blocks address all five granted questions, separating search from information privacy because they require different predicates and remedies. The decision must specify preserved duties, prohibited applications, remaining fact questions and the treatment of the stipulated interim restraint.
* **Record and Source Uncertainty:** The access and patient-equality claims require the specified additional findings. The August 23, 2004 amendment and Part VI.C uphold limited serious-incident reporting; the inconsistent introductory sentence does not alter that amended disposition.

## 75. Gonzales v. Raich

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Gonzales v. Raich
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1454; 545 U.S. 1
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-06 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Congress may apply the Controlled Substances Act to intrastate cultivation and possession of marijuana for personal medical use recommended by a physician and permitted by California law.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit, at 352 F.3d 1222, reversed denial of preliminary relief and directed entry of an injunction based on likely success of the as-applied Commerce Clause claim. The Northern District of California had denied a preliminary injunction.
* **Relief Requested:** Federal officials seek relief from the Commerce Clause ruling and the injunction it requires. Raich, Monson and the caregivers seek protection against federal enforcement affecting their asserted state-authorized medical cultivation, acquisition and possession.
* **Material Record:** Monson grows marijuana for her own medical use. Raich cannot grow it herself and receives locally cultivated marijuana without charge from two caregivers; they and she process it into forms used for treatment. Their physicians described serious conditions, unsuccessful conventional treatments and the medical importance of cannabis; Raich’s physician warned of severe consequences without it. On August 15, 2002, federal agents seized and destroyed Monson’s six plants despite local officials’ conclusion that her use complied with California law. The complaint asserted commerce, due-process, Ninth and Tenth Amendment and necessity theories. The district court recognized the serious claimed harm but rejected likely legal success; the court of appeals isolated state-authorized noncommercial medical cultivation and possession as a distinct class beyond federal power. No criminal conviction, patient-specific diversion finding or adjudicated violation of an independent liberty right is before the Court.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenged enforcement and threatened repetition supply a concrete prospective controversy. Review concerns a preliminary injunction’s legal premise, not final liability or a damages award. The question does not require resolving every other pleaded constitutional ground. Patient medical evidence must be credited at its appropriate procedural status without converting a physician’s recommendation into federal statutory authorization. California’s choice to exempt conduct from state prohibition neither supplies federal power nor negates otherwise valid federal power.
* **Relevant Dates:** California’s Compassionate Use Act: 1996. Monson seizure: August 15, 2002. Ninth Circuit judgment: December 16, 2003. Merits event: June 6, 2005. The federal scheduling and authorization regime is evaluated as it stood before this event; later law is excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers’ Cooperative previously decided the distributor’s statutory necessity claim and equitable enforcement limits, reserving the independent Commerce question and individual-patient issues. GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton is separately listed earlier in this term and concerns protected wildlife, not drug production or possession.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** GDF Realty presents the same necessary-inclusion doctrine for a different regulated class. No effective decision in that matter is assumed; wildlife taking and drug production require separate application.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** A preliminary injunction cannot rest on a legally unavailable merits theory. Resolution of that theory does not decide other preserved claims or guarantee relief under them. Congress’s power, the scope of its enacted prohibition, proof of a violation and lawful enforcement remedies are separate questions.

##### Issue 1: Local production and possession within a valid interstate regime

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Lopez requires an eligible commerce connection fitted to the enacted class before aggregation. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt permits intrastate inclusion that is reasonably adapted and integral to an independently valid interstate regulatory regime. Gibbs v. Babbitt and Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton apply that rule without individual border-crossing, commercial-motive or independently substantial-effects proof.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the independently valid interstate regulation, the class Congress actually covered, and a genuine functional relationship connecting the two. Courts determine those boundaries independently while respecting reasonable congressional judgments about enforcement. Statutory comprehensiveness alone is insufficient; no least-restrictive-means test, fixed quantity of findings or proof that every exemption causes measurable collapse is required. Challengers may show an application outside the class, an invalid interstate regime, or a merely nominal or pretextual relationship.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I’s Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The CSA regulates manufacture, distribution and possession through a closed authorization system, including §§841 and 844, with interstate-market findings in §801. Marijuana was classified in Schedule I at the relevant time. The enacted class is not limited to sales or cross-border transactions.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Ninth Circuit emphasized physician supervision, state legality, local production and absence of sale. The Government invokes the actual interstate drug market, fungibility, production and supply control, diversion and enforcement against unauthorized possession. Those are competing accounts of the statutory relationship, not proof that these particular patients trafficked drugs.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Governing law already permits some noneconomic intrastate conduct as necessary inclusion. It rejects generalized health, crime or productivity costs as a substitute for a commerce anchor. The independent interstate-externality route remains available but need not be satisfied if inclusion is established.
* **Open Point:** Whether the CSA’s inclusion of this intrastate medical cultivation and possession has the required genuine functional relationship to its independently valid interstate controls.

##### Issue 2: State medical permission, statutory boundaries and retained claims

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Oakland Cannabis Buyers’ Cooperative rejects a statutory medical-necessity exemption for that distributor’s prohibited manufacture, distribution and possession with intent to distribute. It preserves individual-patient questions and independent constitutional claims. Its unanimous participating rationale permits lawful §882 enforcement tailoring but forbids recreating the rejected substantive exemption through free-floating equity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** State permission and a medical recommendation do not themselves confer federal authorization. A constitutional power ruling does not prove statutory liability or resolve a distinct individual right. Direct federal regulation of private conduct must be distinguished from commandeering state officials to administer a federal program.
* **Other Governing Law:** California’s Compassionate Use Act exempts specified physicians, patients and caregivers from state prosecution; its exemption does not amend the federal CSA. Federal research, registration and scheduling routes remain subject to their actual enacted conditions. No later change to those routes is presumed.
* **Material Other Authority:** The patients’ medical evidence may matter critically to independent claims, irreparable harm and lawful remedies. It does not automatically define the enacted federal commerce class. The United States does not need a state criminal prohibition to enforce an otherwise valid federal law.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Oakland Cannabis supplies neither a universal ruling against every patient’s necessity defense nor an existing decision of this Commerce issue. No merits finding here resolves whether an independent patient claim can satisfy its own standard.
* **Open Point:** The grant requires the power question. Separately pleaded patient-liberty or necessity claims remain for their own proper adjudication if live and preserved.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The United States argues that a closed national control system cannot depend on identifying the eventual destination or claimed medical purpose of every locally produced quantity. Unauthorized production and possession concern the very substance whose interstate supply and distribution Congress regulates; the local medical class is not insulated simply by absence of payment. The Government asks for rejection of the purported constitutional exemption, not an appellate finding that these patients diverted their supply.

The patients argue that their conduct is local, unpaid, physician-recommended and permitted under a state system regulating medical access. They seek an administrable as-applied boundary, warning that calling a statute comprehensive could turn all household production and consumption into federal commerce. They distinguish their proposed class from trafficking and stress that serious medical need, not commercial demand, motivates it. Their strongest limiting argument concerns proof of a genuine enforcement relationship rather than mere national social consequences. The Government answers that excluding production and possession of a fungible controlled substance would affect control of its unauthorized supply and make the prohibition’s enforcement dependent on exemptions outside the federal authorization system. State sovereignty does not supply a categorical exemption from direct regulation of private persons, but a nominal claim of comprehensiveness would not meet entering law.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can sustain federal power under the established necessary-inclusion rule and vacate the Commerce-based injunction direction, leaving genuinely preserved other claims. It can find the asserted relationship nominal or inadequately supported and preserve appropriately confined preliminary protection if the remaining equitable predicates are satisfied. A constitutional evidentiary remand is warranted only if material unresolved facts are necessary to assess the relationship; Rancho Viejo permits text and structure to establish the connection without routine remand. Reliance solely on generalized drug-related social costs would conflict with Lopez. A ruling that all noneconomic conduct is immune would conflict with Home Builders, Gibbs and Rancho Viejo. Neither holding automatically selects a criminal sentence, state regulatory policy or ultimate medical treatment.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** An application of the existing necessary-inclusion rule to unauthorized production and possession of a substance subject to an actual interstate supply-control regime. The case does not require a general commerce power over personal health choices, every item made at home, or any conduct placed in a statute described as comprehensive.
