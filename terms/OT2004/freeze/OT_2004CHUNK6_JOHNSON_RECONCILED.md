## Reconciled propositions and application

**J1 — Vacatur as fact: all eight.** A state order vacating the particular conviction used to enhance a federal sentence is a fact supporting the prisoner's challenge under event-date section 2255 paragraph 6(4). Its issuance changes the predicate's status; it is not merely a later judicial opinion stating general law. The Government's old-facts-only approach would start time before the needed state order exists and undermine the proper-channel route Custis and Daniels preserve. A timely motion proceeds under the otherwise applicable requirements for relief from the current sentence.

**J2P — Procurement and discovery: O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.** Notice of the state vacatur starts the one-year paragraph 6(4) period when the prisoner acted with reasonable diligence to obtain it after entry of the enhanced federal judgment and to learn of the order. Entry of that judgment establishes the federal stake; reasonable diligence does not demand the fastest conceivable action. The express diligence condition has work to do where the prisoner initiates the process producing the new fact. State collateral rules protect state judgments, but do not replace the distinct federal interest in timely challenges to federal sentences.

The adopted rule includes all three qualifications: (1) after diligent initiation, delay in state proceedings not attributable to the prisoner does not defeat the later trigger; (2) if facts underlying the state challenge were not discoverable through diligence until after federal judgment, diligence is measured from their discoverability, and diligent pursuit then permits time to run from notice of the resulting vacatur; and (3) even a prisoner who did not act diligently may use paragraph 6(4) by showing that the federal motion was filed within one year of the notice date diligent pursuit would have produced. These are parts of the rule, not optional equitable exceptions.

**J3P — Affirm on this ground: the same four.** Johnson did not begin the state challenge until February 6, 1998, after the November 29, 1994 federal judgment. Even crediting all time before April 24, 1996, he waited more than twenty-one months. His stated limited education, self-representation and waiting for inmate assistance do not explain reasonable efforts or a specific impediment during that protracted interval. The federal extension request did not pursue state vacatur. The application assesses those supplied explanations and the known interval; it infers no default from unavailable research materials. On that record, the four retain the judgment that prompt pursuit would have yielded notice more than a year before the eventual federal motion, so the hypothetical-diligent-notice qualification does not save this application. This is a legal application to the known sequence, not a finding of an invented exact notice date or an automatic rule that every delay defeats paragraph 6(4). The lower judgment is affirmed while its categorical refusal to treat vacatur as fact is rejected.

**J2D — Discovery alone: Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg.** Paragraph 6(4) requires diligent discovery of the existing order and federal filing within one year of when diligent discovery would reveal it. It does not impose an additional federal schedule for bringing the order into existence. An event cannot be discovered before it occurs. Custis and Daniels direct the challenge into the state channel; success there supplies the particular new fact, while state procedural rules and federal claim requirements retain their separate force. The ordinary relative promptness of notice does not erase the discovery requirement, which still governs delayed learning of an order.

**J3D — Reverse and remand: the same four.** Reverse the categorical timing dismissal and return for proceedings under that discovery rule and the remaining requirements. The supplied interval of just over three months between vacatur and federal filing supports the bounded return; actual notice and filing dates govern any remaining application of the adopted trigger. The disagreement concerns the limitation rule, not a present award of sentence reduction.

## Separate-writing substance and join boundaries

The discovery-only position needs a substantive separate explanation if the procurement rule controls. Its grounds are that discovery and creation are different; the federal requirement has no enacted start date for obtaining state relief; state rules can protect state judgments; the federal-judgment starting point burdens defense resources during direct review and can generate unnecessary state proceedings if the federal judgment is reversed; and an undefined procurement schedule creates avoidable collateral litigation. A discussion of finality as a less disruptive alternative to judgment is criticism of the chosen trigger, not assent to a procurement duty beginning at finality. The writing would reverse under its discovery rule, preserve ordinary discovery diligence and retain the actual merits requirements.

The procurement position answers that the federal sentence has its own finality interest, the movant-controlled origin of the event makes pursuit part of the express diligence demand, and entry of the enhanced judgment supplies a concrete federal reason to act. Reasonableness and the three express qualifications answer real impediments without allowing unexplained postponement. If the discovery-only rule instead controls, the four procurement Justices retain this opposing rule and affirmance ground. Historical rhetorical hypotheticals need not be imported to state either position adequately.
