## 52. Johnson v. United States, No. 03-9685 — April 4, 2005

### Governing law and components

The exact current standard is **Federal-prisoner limitation and successive-motion certification**, with **Present operation effective: Clay v. United States, March 4, 2003, for the ordinary finality trigger; Triestman's incorporated review bar and the enacted alternative triggers and certification predicates retain their authority.** The four latest-event triggers remain distinct. Paragraph 6(4), the event-date designation, concerns discovery through due diligence of facts supporting the claim. It is not the new-right trigger or a promise that the substantive claim succeeds.

The exact related standard is **Collateral attacks on state predicates at federal sentencing and under section 2255**, with **Present operation effective: Daniels v. United States, 2001-04-25, for the section 2255 extension; Custis's sentencing-forum rule remains effective from 1994-05-23.** Custis and Daniels direct the covered constitutional attack into its authorized original-conviction channel and preserve a possible later authorized application following invalidation. Daniels's independently expired counseled-predicate restriction does not decide the status of an actual state vacatur. Its complete unconstitutional counsel-deprivation exception retains its own preservation, proof and adverse-effect requirements; no independent invocation of that exception in Johnson's original federal proceeding is supplied. Booker and Shepard establish neither retroactive reopening nor this filing trigger.

There are three components: J1, whether the order vacating the particular predicate is a supporting fact; J2, whether statutory diligence includes timely pursuit of that order after the federal judgment makes its relevance apparent; J3, the application and remedy. The historical April 22, 1996 certiorari-denial recital is not an entered event and supplies no finality finding here. The ordinary finality date and any transition grace period are separate from the paragraph 6(4) diligence inquiry.

### Two lawful paths and operative rules

**Procurement-and-discovery path.** A state order vacating a conviction used to enhance a federal sentence is a new fact supporting a paragraph 6(4) motion. Notice of that order can start a fresh one-year period when the prisoner exercised due diligence in obtaining it after entry of the federal judgment supplied reason to challenge the predicate and in learning that relief was granted. Reasonable diligence, rather than the fastest imaginable action, is required; actual obstacles and supported explanations must be considered. The prisoner cannot postpone the federal limitation indefinitely by leaving an available predicate challenge unpursued. Successful state relief does not itself establish federal sentencing relief.

The strongest objection is textual: discovery of a fact is different from causing the fact to exist. The answer supporting this path is that a judgment-changing event obtained at the prisoner's initiative is not equivalent to a wholly external event; the statutory diligence condition would lose its limiting function if the prisoner could control commencement by wholly unexplained delay. Custis's proper-channel route remains practically usable if pursued diligently. It never promised an unlimited future filing opportunity.

On the supplied chronology, Johnson waited from November 1994 until February 1998 to start the state case. Even crediting every day before AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment and imposing no preenactment diligence consequence, he waited more than twenty-one additional months. The extension request did not pursue state vacatur; its existence does not establish the relevant diligence. Limited education, self-representation and waiting until another inmate could assist do not, without a specific impediment or identified reasonable effort during that period, explain this protracted failure to initiate the necessary challenge. The factual account supplies those explanations as his own, rather than an absence inferred from missing papers. This path affirms the untimeliness judgment on the diligence ground, while rejecting the appellate court's categorical refusal to treat vacatur as a fact. Exact vacatur and later federal-filing dates are unnecessary to that independently sufficient conclusion because the conceded roughly three-month interval is not its basis.

**Discovery-of-existing-order path.** The new fact is the state order itself. Paragraph 6(4) requires diligence in discovering that existing order and filing within one year of when such diligence would reveal it; it does not require creating the order on an earlier judicially selected schedule. Custis and Daniels keep the underlying state attack outside this federal proceeding, while the four statutory triggers retain their enacted work. This path reverses the categorical legal dismissal and remands for proceedings under that discovery rule and all remaining applicable requirements. The conceded interval strongly supports timeliness, but no invented vacatur, notice or filing date is needed for the reversal. The lower court applies the actual dates if any discovery-timing issue genuinely remains.

Its strongest objection is the prospect of a prisoner manufacturing a later federal deadline. The answer is that Congress selected discovery of supporting facts, preserved other procedural conditions and did not enact a separate limitation on obtaining state relief. State procedural rules and the independent federal claim requirements remain; a finality concern cannot supply a duty omitted from this particular trigger. This path grants neither invalidation of the federal judgment nor a sentence reduction.

**Rejected old-facts-only path.** The constitutional facts underlying the old plea are not the only facts supporting this current claim. An entered order changing that particular conviction's legal status is an objective event, not merely a later insight into precedent. A rule that no vacatur can count would deprive the proper-channel route preserved in Custis and Daniels of its actual character. All eight Associates reject that categorical rule, without treating any new judicial opinion of general applicability as a new factual predicate.

## 53. Rousey v. Jacoway, No. 03-1407 — April 4, 2005

### Governing law and operative rules

The exact related standard is **Federal-law transfer restrictions and exclusion of trust interests**, with **Present operation effective: Patterson v. Shumate, June 15, 1992.** Patterson's established ERISA-trust exclusion under section 541(c)(2) is not this exemption and expressly reserved the section 522 payment/corpus issue. Polleys' current student-loan hardship holding neither supplies the support standard nor shifts the Rule 4003(c) exemption burden.

Section 522(d)(10)(E) exempts a debtor's right to receive a payment under the listed plans or a similar plan or contract, on account of illness, disability, death, age or length of service, only to the extent reasonably necessary for the support of the debtor and dependents. The complete enacted exception is in the frozen statutory supplement. It applies only when all three conditions coincide: an insider employer established the plan or acted as its sponsor when the debtor's rights arose; the payment is on account of age or service; and the plan fails to qualify under sections 401(a), 403(a), 403(b) or 408. The supplied section 408 qualification means this exception does not disqualify these accounts. The support limitation remains independently operative.

**R1, similar plan.** These qualifying rollover IRAs provide a right to retirement-income payments similar to the listed pension and annuity arrangements because their legal structure substitutes for wages in retirement. Their required late-life distributions, early-withdrawal additional tax and inherited pension function are objective features; an account's retirement label or the debtor's subjective purpose alone would not suffice. The catchall requires relevant similarity, not identity in sponsor, funding source or every withdrawal term. A right to future payment can fall within the statute; present collection of a periodic benefit is not a categorical prerequisite.

**R2, on account of age.** Age has a causal role in the right to receive the whole account without the ordinary ten-percent early-distribution tax. That substantial age-linked restriction does not disappear because early withdrawal remains possible at a cost. The ordinary threshold is age 59½. The then-effective minimum-distribution starting age is 70½, with the specified April 1 commencement and fifty-percent deficiency tax. These account terms, not later amendments, supply the comparison.

Age need not be the sole possible distribution trigger. The supplied exceptions include death, disability, qualifying substantially equal periodic payments, qualifying medical expenses, unemployment-related health insurance, higher education, first-home acquisition and a section 6331 levy. Each remains subject to its own statutory terms, including the health-premium same-year OR succeeding-year alternative and sixty-day reemployment limit, the education/first-home qualifications and ceilings, and periodic-payment modification recapture. The IRA-inapplicable age-fifty-five separation and domestic-relations exceptions are not imported. These limited alternatives do not turn the accounts into ordinary unrestricted savings. No finding that the Rouseys qualify for any particular early-distribution exception is required.

**R3, result and remedy.** Reverse the categorical denial and remand for remaining exemption administration under the support ceiling and any genuinely unresolved account-specific objection. The objector retains Rule 4003(c)'s burden of proving the exemption improperly claimed; identifying the accounts and claimed statutory right remains necessary. The category ruling does not exempt every dollar, adjudicate a hardship standard borrowed from section 523, or establish a general exemption for any investment earmarked for retirement. The legal direction to turn over the accounts solely because they permit taxed early access cannot stand.

### Alternative-path comparison

The exclusion path stresses that the debtor can demand money immediately and that a tax changes net value rather than the legal right to receive a payment. Its strongest point is that an unlimited functional analogy could exempt ordinary savings. The statutory-eligibility path answers with actual federal conditions: age changes the ability to receive the full balance, late-life distribution is mandated, and the enumerated exception expressly references section 408 qualification. Reading similarity to demand every listed plan's institutional form would nullify the catchall. Its strongest counterargument is answered by confining the rule to the accounts' supported legal structure and preserving the support cap. All eight conclude that statutory eligibility is the sound path; none supports categorical whole-balance exemption.

## 54. Consumers Energy Co. v. FERC, 367 F.3d 915 — April 4, 2005

### Governing law, preservation and rule

The current standard **Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard**, with **Present operation effective: 1998-01-26**, requires substantial evidence under the agency's actual substantive rule and consideration of contrary evidence; Allentown Mack does not authorize de novo technical choice. New York v. FERC's statutory holdings preserve the jurisdictional grant and the mandatory section 206 duty after the necessary unlawful-rate/practice finding. Lockyer's unanimous reasoned-review holding requires the agency's actual grounds and meaningful consideration. Timken's unanimous current holding distinguishes a lawful permissible method from a compelled method. Mead's force-of-law threshold does not replace independent resolution of statutory power or authorize counsel's new explanation.

**C1, preserved comparability challenge.** FERC may assess a foreign affiliate's nondiscriminatory transmission access through functionally comparable arrangements rather than require an identical domestic tariff, if it explains on the actual record how the arrangement addresses material market-power and access concerns and fulfills the Federal Power Act's just-and-reasonable and nondiscrimination requirements. Section 825l(b) makes findings supported by substantial evidence conclusive; the agency's technical application is reviewed for arbitrary action or legal error, and statutory authority remains independently reviewable.

FERC corrected the affiliation premise on rehearing and gave an actual alternative comparability explanation. Paired sell-in and buy-out transactions use the common market price absent congestion. Import and export financial transmission rights offset the specified intertie price differences when congestion arises, with the separate export fee remaining. These hedges are not physical reservations; their function concerns price exposure. The agency assessed the arrangement as a whole, including equal transaction rules, successful trading by at least twelve United States marketers, lack of OPG generation market power outside Ontario, and the speculative character of claimed discriminatory congestion manipulation. Consumers identified no actual discriminatory operation. Those findings support the agency's conclusion in this record that the inability to obtain a domestic-form fifty-megawatt reservation did not establish inferior discriminatory access.

The strongest objection is that a financial hedge does not guarantee physical capacity or all commercial certainty a firm reservation supplies. The adopted answer does not equate those instruments. Neither the statute nor the actual comparable-access policy requires institutional identity, and the supported explanation addresses the claimed price and access problem through the combined mechanism. Hypothetical manipulation does not overcome the identified actual operation. An actual discriminatory barrier, material ignored disadvantage or unsupported technical equivalence would require a different administrative assessment under the same rule. This case establishes none.

**C2, distinct unpreserved objection.** Section 825l(b) does not permit consideration of an objection omitted from the application for rehearing unless reasonable ground excuses that failure. Using independence to establish affiliation did not present the distinct theory that lack of Order No. 2000 independence itself defeats comparability. The agency corrected affiliation; it had no opportunity to address the different theory. No successful reasonable-ground showing is supplied. Decline to decide that theory's merits. The statute's separate additional-evidence mechanism requires material evidence and reasonable grounds for not adducing it before the agency; it is not an automatic cure for an unraised legal objection. No additional evidence is ordered here.

**C3, disposition and limits.** Affirm the judgment denying the petition for review. The challenged United States market-based-rate authorization remains effective. The decision does not command Canadian restructuring, a particular physical reservation, a replacement tariff, a refund or damages. Rehearing and review do not themselves stay the agency order under section 825l(c); no separate stay is supplied. Future rate or access complaints retain their statutory channels and predicates, rather than being adjudicated in advance.

Affirmance follows from the actual rehearing explanation and the supported combined-market evidence. ## 55. Dura Pharmaceuticals, Inc. v. Broudo, No. 03-932 — April 19, 2005

### Governing law and operative holdings

The exact current standard **Culpable affirmative assistance to a securities fraud**, with **Present operation effective: Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A, 1994-04-19**, retains the underlying-fraud, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable-loss elements under the ordinary civil burden. Its expansion to qualified affirmative assistance does not dispense with loss. The exact standard **Ordinary pleading and substantive proof requirements**, with **Present operation effective: Swierkiewicz, February 26, 2002, for the employment-discrimination evidentiary-framework distinction; Leatherman's municipal pleading rule remains effective March 3, 1993**, forbids inventing a heightened checklist from an evidentiary framework and retains fair notice and substantive requirements.

**D1, substantive causation.** An allegation or showing that a public-market purchaser paid an artificially inflated price does not by itself establish the economic loss and causal connection necessary for private section 10(b) damages. The plaintiff must prove an actual economic loss caused by the challenged fraud, as the PSLRA's section 78u-4(b)(4) requires. Transaction inducement and loss causation are separate. At purchase the buyer receives an asset at the prevailing price; a later disposal while the inflation persists may yield no loss from the falsehood, and an intervening decline may result from other business or market changes. That does not prove that overpayment can never contribute to a compensable loss; it defeats treating inflation alone as both necessary loss and its causal explanation.

Basic's reliance framework supplies the transaction connection, not a presumption that every later drop was caused by every alleged statement. Central Bank preserves causal loss even for its received assistance action. The PSLRA confirms the ultimate causation burden; it does not authorize converting fraud law into insurance against unrelated price declines. The earnings announcement and the device announcement are distinct. An earlier profitability-related decline cannot be assigned to the device representation without a supported causal account.

**D2, pleading.** Even applying ordinary Rule 8 fair-notice pleading, the complaint must give a short, intelligible indication of what economic loss is claimed and how the alleged fraud caused it. An inflation-only assertion supplies no such causal-loss account for the reviewed device branch. It fails the ordinary standard without deciding whether any additional Rule 9 or PSLRA particularity requirement independently applies to loss causation. Swierkiewicz prevents demanding trial proof at pleading; it does not make a legal theory sufficient after a necessary causal element has been removed. No completed sale, single formal corrective announcement, specified econometric method or exclusive correction mechanism is imposed.

**D3, judgment and remedy.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's reinstatement insofar as it rests on the inflation-only rule and remand for ordinary proceedings, including any amendment properly allowed under the applicable rules. The reviewed device theory cannot proceed merely by repeating that purchase price was inflated. The separate profitability and scienter branches keep their existing scope; this grant does not newly adjudicate them. No damages or fraud finding follows, and the remand is not a direction that amendment necessarily must be allowed or must fail.

### Adversarial alternatives

The inflation-as-loss path argues that paying more than true value immediately diminishes wealth and therefore states both injury and causation. Its strongest concern is that investors should not have to sell to recognize injury. The adopted path does not impose a sale requirement; it distinguishes a legally recoverable causal loss from a price that can contain inflation later passed to another buyer or lost for unrelated reasons. The contrary path would permit recovery without explaining what loss the claimed fraud caused. The alternative of affirmance on another sufficient pleaded causal theory requires such a theory in the actual reviewed allegations; the clean packet supplies none beyond inflation for this branch. The Court therefore resolves that legal insufficiency and returns ordinary application rather than inventing a corrective-disclosure allegation.

## 56. Monsanto Co. v. McFarling, 363 F.3d 1336 — April 19, 2005

### Governing law and separable components

The exact current standard **Selecting plant-variety regimes and amended-protection notices**, with **Present operation effective: J. E. M. Ag Supply, December 10, 2001, for concurrent statutory protection; the certificate amendment and transition remain effective April 4, 1995**, keeps utility-patent coverage and certificate exceptions distinct. The saved-seed tradition and Asgrow's former certificate construction supply no general defense to a valid utility patent. J. E. M. did not validate every restriction.

The exact current standard **Equipment competition and single-brand aftermarket power**, with **Present operation effective: Trinko, January 13, 2004, for independent monopoly maintenance in regulated input access; CSU, June 4, 2001, for patent and copyright defenses; Kodak's market and proof rules remain effective June 8, 1992**, requires actual market and competitive analysis, gives statutory exclusivity substantial force and rejects conclusive immunity for the defined section 2 claim. This case presents an express proposed extension of that principle to section 1 tying; CSU did not already decide the present tie.

M1 concerns the saved-seed and scope-based misuse attack on the no-replanting promise and the contract breach determination. M2 concerns the independent tying counterclaim/defense and any legally proper consequence for enforcement. M3 preserves the existing actual-damages remand and the boundary of the partial final judgment. The unfinished patent causes are not transformed into final patent infringement judgments. Patent validity was not preserved for this appeal; assumed coverage of the later-generation seeds is the operative premise, not a new adjudication of validity.

### M1: no categorical saved-seed defense and no enlargement shown by this promise alone

The no-replanting promise concerns the use and production of additional seeds embodying the assumed valid patent claims. Enforcing that promise does not, merely because the seeds descend from a lawful purchase, extend the utility patent to an unpatented product or import the Plant Variety Protection Act's saved-seed exception into a different statutory right. McFarling's offer to pay a technology fee does not itself confer a license to reproduce the protected later-generation seed. The granted one-season planting permission and the conceded saving and replanting support breach. The agreement's other research, breeding and distribution restrictions are not all adjudicated merely because the no-replanting restriction survives this categorical attack.

The strongest contrary argument is that selling seed whose ordinary biological function is reproduction should carry the liberty to replant its crop, especially where the trait fee can be paid separately. J. E. M.'s coexistence rule answers the asserted statutory immunity, and assumed claim coverage distinguishes ownership of a purchased item from permission to make and use additional patented items. That is a confined reproduction holding. It does not announce that every restriction labeled within patent scope is immune from misuse or competition law.

All eight reject the categorical PVPA/saved-seed defense and the claimed extension beyond patent scope on these assumed claims. Stevens and Breyer retain their published J. E. M. objections to the coexistence holding but accept it as governing this dispute; their earlier dissent alone supplies no new disposition here. Affirm the breach determination and rejection of the presented categorical defenses, subject to any independent enforcement consequence actually established under a preserved antitrust ground. No present tying violation has been found.

### M2: competing antitrust paths

**Independent-tying adjudication path.** A restriction's relation to a valid patent does not conclusively defeat an otherwise supported section 1 tying claim. The claimant must establish distinct products under actual separate consumer demand, conditioning, sufficient tying-market power, the requisite affected commerce, competitive injury and causal entitlement to the particular relief. A patent, trait, brand or single product label neither proves those elements nor conclusively merges separately demanded products. Statutory exclusion and justified returns from invention remain substantial legal interests, and merely requesting a different reproduction license does not establish a tie.

The doctrinal step from CSU is explicit: if patent scope does not categorically immunize independently unlawful monopoly maintenance, the same scope label cannot categorically immunize a condition that independently satisfies the established tying offense. Kodak and Jefferson Parish supply the section 1 demand and conditioning inquiry; CSU supplies the coexistence of patent entitlement and independent antitrust prohibition. Trinko reinforces separate offense and remedy predicates, not a compulsory license.

The strongest objection is that McFarling seeks only another way to obtain the single patented second-generation product, not a compelled purchase of something distinct. The lower court acknowledged the demand-based inquiry but declined to decide it. That objection may defeat the claim under the correct rule; it does not sustain the appellate categorical immunity rationale or authorize this Court to invent an unmade demand finding. Vacate the antitrust rejection and remand for the preserved tying theory under ordinary evidentiary and summary-judgment requirements. Ordinary absence of proof can support renewed judgment. This is no finding that a tie exists, no compulsory reproduction license and no automatic new trial or discovery order. Any preserved effect of a proven antitrust wrong on contract enforcement must be addressed under its own governing requirements.

**No independently unlawful tie on the presented conduct path.** The proposed transaction is a reproduction license for the patented second-generation seed while paying only the technology fee. A refusal to offer that desired license is not converted into a tie merely because obtaining a newly authorized seed requires purchase of seed embodying the technology. These Justices preserve liability for an independently unlawful tie and do not adopt universal patent immunity. They conclude that the presented conduct is protected refusal to authorize another reproduction transaction, with no sufficient distinct coercive acquisition shown. Affirm rejection of the antitrust component without a compulsory license.

The strongest objection is Kodak's actual-demand inquiry and the Federal Circuit's deliberate refusal to resolve it. These Justices answer at the conduct level: the claim actually presented is a demand for an ungranted right to use the patented second-generation item, not an identified obligation to buy an unpatented item as the condition of an otherwise licensed use. Their disagreement concerns whether that supported theory identifies a separate antitrust wrong requiring further proceedings. They will not join the lower court's universal statement that conduct within patent scope can never violate antitrust law.

### M3: damages, mandate and boundaries

All eight preserve vacatur of the $780,000 contractual multiplier and the existing remand for actual damages; the grant supplies no basis to revive the penalty or fix the amount. The reviewed lower judgment applied Missouri's reasonable-forecast and difficulty-of-estimation requirements, while preserving its alternative actual-loss comparison; Monsanto did not defend this grossly disproportionate sum through that alternative. A uniform 120 multiplier tied to purchased rather than replanted bags lacked the required relation to this saving-and-replanting harm. This is the existing lower-court ruling retained by the mandate, not a newly generalized federal contract-damages test.

Actual damages remain subject to the adjudicated liability and any independently established defense or offset, the proper saved/replanted measure and the bar on double recovery. The patent damages claims and Monsanto's request for injunctive relief remain within their own unfinished proceedings and legal prerequisites. No new multiplier or injunction is approved. The reserved question concerning a forecast for supplying or selling saved seed to third parties is not answered by this replanting dispute.
