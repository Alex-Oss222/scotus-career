## Record propositions and disputed characterizations

The original packet continues to establish the section 478.2(2) annual $100 charge on Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely interstate, its vehicle-identifying information and decal, the apparent waiver of the federal-system $10 fee for covered carriers using Michigan as their registration State, the charge's older origin, and the lower court's reliance on separate regulatory administration. It does not establish that payment of the $100 is legally necessary to complete the federal registration process.

The additional supported position is Michigan's contention that sections 478.2(1) and (2) should be assessed together. On its characterization, their combined effect imposes the same $100 fee on for-hire vehicles plated in Michigan without selecting those vehicles because they operate interstate. The carriers dispute that characterization. The supplied record does not settle whether the combined scheme is in fact neutral in the legally relevant sense. Equal dollar amounts alone do not decide that question, and no finding of equal legal coverage, identical registration conditions, or identical operational treatment is added here.

The absence of an established federal-registration payment condition is a separate proposition. It does not establish neutrality under a statute construed to reach registration imposed because of interstate status. Conversely, a fee's express application to a category of interstate vehicles does not itself resolve the State's argument about the combined operation of its two provisions. The proper unit and legal basis of characterization are questions for analysis under the selected statutory construction.

## Exact statutory structure that each construction must address

Section 14504(a) defines the relevant standards by reference to forms and procedures required by the Secretary's regulations to prove the lawfulness of the covered motor-carrier transportation. Subsection (b)'s first sentence addresses a State's demand for registration by a motor carrier providing the specified transportation in that State and recognizes registration completed under the Secretary's subsection (c) standards. Its second sentence treats obligations exceeding the Secretary's standards as an unreasonable burden to the extent of the excess. The breadth of the state-registration category in those sentences is the disputed interpretive question.

Subsection (c)(1) prescribes annual registration with one State through evidence of federal registration, full compliance by the registration State, and satisfaction of the other States' registration requirements. Subsection (c)(2)(A) separately identifies the registration State acting in that capacity as the State that may demand the listed federal-registration evidence, insurance proof, direct payment through the allocated fee system, and local service-agent information. This restriction should not be restated as protecting the carrier only against other participating States: the limiting registration-State language and its specified subjects must retain their actual breadth. Participation separately matters to fee allocation, receipts of revenue, and eligibility under subsection (c)(2)(D).

Subsection (c)(2)(B)(iii) addresses the Secretary's standards and forbids their requiring the specified means of individual vehicle identification. It is not a free-standing conclusion that every state decal violates the Act. Subsection (c)(2)(B)(iv) establishes the insurance-proof fee system and the November 15, 1991 benchmark with a $10-per-vehicle ceiling. Subsection (c)(2)(B)(v) separately forbids authorization of fees for filing and maintaining evidence of federal registration. Subsection (c)(2)(C) addresses fees under this section that fail to comply with the specified fee system. Those commands must not be merged into a universal $10 cap on every state payment made by an interstate carrier.

## Three distinct interpretations and their strongest competing considerations

| Interpretation | Supported reasoning | Principal objection that requires an answer | Scope and potential consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defined federal-registration subject | Read both subsection (b) sentences with subsection (a)'s definition and subsection (c)'s detailed federal-registration system. The same registration terminology can identify one subject throughout the provision. | The second sentence may prohibit excess registration obligations rather than merely regulate obligations on the listed subjects. Limiting it to those subjects requires an explanation of the enacted general language, rather than reliance on the former system's scope or the State's regulatory label. | A genuinely separate program outside the defined subjects can survive. Actual duplication of, or a payment condition on, covered federal registration remains independently material. Information, a decal and a waiver are evidence to classify, not automatic legal answers. |
| Registration imposed because the entity is an interstate motor carrier | Read subsection (b)'s described carrier and transportation setting as delimiting registration imposed by reason of interstate status. The second sentence then prohibits excess obligations in that category, while general neutral registration remains outside subsection (b). Subsection (c)'s distinct commands continue to constrain demands for its listed matters. | The statute identifies a carrier providing covered transportation; it does not expressly state a motive or discriminatory-trigger test. This construction must explain why the carrier description limits the reason for registration and why the two subsection (b) sentences remain coherent when tied to subsection (c)'s standards. Neutrality cannot become an exemption from subsection (c)'s separate commands. | The combined operation of sections 478.2(1) and (2) becomes legally material. A regulatory-purpose explanation alone does not decide whether the actual registration obligation depends on interstate status. A statutory-characterization remand is available if the disputed combined-scheme issue has not been resolved under this standard. |
| Every state carrier-registration obligation imposed on an interstate carrier | Read subsection (b)'s general terms to reach registration demands whenever the regulated entity is an interstate carrier operating in the State, subject to the federal standards. | The construction must address subsection (a), subsection (c)'s defined system, ordinary neutral tax or licensing registration, and the statute's actual limits. Burden reduction alone cannot supply unlimited displacement. | Potential coverage extends beyond the second construction. Criticism of this broad interpretation does not itself defeat the narrower interstate-status interpretation. Any invalidation must identify the obligation and extent of excess actually displaced. |

The second interpretation is legally distinct from both the defined-subject construction and the broadest all-registration construction. It must receive its own analysis even if the independent model ultimately finds its textual objection decisive. This neutral validation establishes a supported alternative for testing, not the construction the Court must adopt.

## Remedy alternatives and limits

On the defined-subject construction, affirmance can follow if the supplied separate program falls outside the federally covered registration subject. A contrary actual-linkage or duplication determination would require supported facts and a precise account of the displaced obligation. The apparent $10 waiver alone does not establish such a condition.

On the interstate-status construction, a permissible alternative is vacatur and remand to assess the State's combined-scheme characterization and the carriers' objection under that legal standard. This remand concerns the legal characterization and actual operation of the two existing provisions. It does not depend on finding a missing condition that makes the $100 payment necessary to complete federal registration. It does not establish that either subsection is invalid, direct a refund, prescribe an amount, or make the lower court's regulatory-purpose finding dispositive. Affirmance or invalidation under that construction would instead require an adequately supported resolution of the material classification and scope questions.

A remand is not compelled merely because the supplied supplement reports a dispute. The independent model must assess whether adopting the proposed construction makes the unresolved characterization material and whether the available record permits lawful resolution without further proceedings. If remand is selected, its instructions should identify that exact inquiry and retain the distinction between state-program neutrality under subsection (b) and compliance with subsection (c)'s independent prohibitions.
