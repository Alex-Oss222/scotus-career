## 14. Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys
* **Citation or Docket:** 356 F.3d 1302; lower-court No. 02-8059.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-12-13 (scheduled simulated merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the undue-hardship exception to student-loan nondischargeability under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(8) requires a certainty of hopelessness, or permits discharge under the *Brunner* framework based on a realistic assessment of future repayment ability and good-faith efforts to avoid default?
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit affirmance of the district court and bankruptcy court's discharge of the student debt. The simulated grant concerns that merits judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** ECMC seeks rejection of the discharge under a more demanding undue-hardship standard; Polleys seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** The bankruptcy court found persistent impairment of Polleys's earning ability, minimal resources and no discretionary income sufficient for the loan payment. The approximately $51,000 balance required about $420 monthly. She had made no payments but had sought work, including work outside her training. Her mental-health limitations and resulting employment difficulties were supported findings. The dispute distinguishes inability from deliberate avoidance, not merely an asserted preference for a lower income.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The §523(a)(8) adversary proceeding produced a discharge judgment. Undisturbed factual findings are reviewed for clear error; the legal standard and legal effect of those findings receive independent review. No finding of permanent total disability or deliberate creation of hardship should be invented.
* **Relevant Dates:** The lower appellate decision is February 4, 2004; the assigned event is December 13, 2004. The pre-2005 Bankruptcy Code governs. Later bankruptcy amendments, repayment programs and agency discharge policies cannot be imported.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-12-13.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(8), bankruptcy adversary procedure and appellate review. The debtor must establish the statutory exception under the ordinary civil proof standard.

##### Issue 1: Meaning and proof of undue hardship

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No earlier Supreme Court holding prescribes a student-loan-specific test. Lamie v. United States Trustee enforces enacted bankruptcy conditions rather than rewriting them to achieve a desirable policy result.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ordinary text and statutory structure control. An exception cannot be erased by an extra-statutory demand for certainty, nor converted into a general equitable cancellation power. Findings and legal conclusions retain their distinct review standards.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 523(a)(8) requires undue hardship to the debtor or dependents. Its operative version supplies neither a certainty-of-hopelessness formula nor a permanent-disability prerequisite.
* **Material Other Authority:** Brunner v. New York State Higher Education Services Corp. is persuasive lower-court authority organizing present minimal living needs, likely persistence and good faith. Grogan v. Garner supports the ordinary preponderance framework in discharge litigation; it does not itself select this student-loan test.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory hardship requirement controls, but the Supreme Court has not resolved the precise formulation as of June 30, 2004. Circuit disagreement is not itself binding simulated law.
* **Open Point:** Whether a realistic, evidence-based application of the three-part framework gives effect to the exception and supports the discharge on the existing findings.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The appellate court adopted Brunner with a realistic future-ability inquiry and good faith focused on legitimate efforts rather than judicial moral judgments; it found the existing findings sufficient. **ECMC:** Nondischargeability is the statutory norm, future improvement remains possible, and lack of payment undermines good faith. **Polleys:** Supported long-term earning limits and a bare subsistence budget establish hardship; no payments are unsurprising when payment is impossible. **Alternatives and limits:** A return for missing findings is proper only if a necessary finding is actually absent. Fresh-start policy cannot eliminate the statute, and an imagined future improvement cannot defeat supported evidence. No verified separate Solicitor General submission is identified.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Affirm under a realistic three-part standard if the established inability, persistence and good-faith findings satisfy the debtor's burden; the debt remains discharged.
* Reverse or remand if the legal test omits a statutory requirement or the findings do not support it; the creditor then retains its collection rights subject to the bankruptcy judgment and any further determination.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule may reject certainty of hopelessness and permanent disability while requiring sustained, evidence-supported hardship. It need not decide partial discharge, every repayment-plan option or the effect of later amendments.

**Uncertainty:** The grant is hypothetical and supplies no additional Supreme Court briefing. The record supports the identified findings; no current income update or later repayment option is assumed.

## 15. Whitfield v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Whitfield v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 03-1293 and 03-1294, consolidated with Hall; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 209.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-11 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether conviction for money-laundering conspiracy under 18 U.S.C. §1956(h) requires allegation and proof of an overt act in furtherance of the agreement.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 349 F.3d 1320, affirming the challenged conspiracy convictions and rejecting the requested overt-act instruction.
* **Relief Requested:** Whitfield and Hall seek reversal based on omission of an overt-act requirement; the United States seeks affirmance of the instruction ruling.
* **Material Record:** The defendants were prosecuted for an agreement to commit money laundering. They requested an instruction requiring an overt act, which the district court refused. The preserved question is the content of §1956(h), not whether the jury actually found every other element or whether a different conspiracy count would require an act.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The consolidated dockets constitute one listed matter. The statutory instruction issue is preserved. The Court should resolve neither an unpresented sufficiency challenge nor a separate venue objection merely because conduct in furtherance appears in a venue provision.
* **Relevant Dates:** Section 1956(h) was enacted in 1992. The later venue provision does not retroactively add an element without operative statutory text. The event is January 11, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Hall is the consolidated companion within this consolidated matter. Timken, scheduled the same day, concerns delegated antidumping administration and creates no sentencing or conspiracy dependency.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-11.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal criminal review; the Government must prove the actual offense elements beyond a reasonable doubt. A requested instruction is required only if it accurately states an applicable legal element.

##### Issue 1: Agreement and the omitted overt-act element

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Shabani holds that §846 requires the unlawful agreement and knowing participation, without an additional overt act; it expressly does not announce a universal result for every conspiracy statute. Ratzlaf v. United States reserves lenity for ambiguity remaining after ordinary construction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the offense's own text and structure, distinguish the general conspiracy statute, and retain the Government's burden on agreement and knowing participation. A penalty clause can help define an offense without incorporating every element of another statute.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§1956(h), 1956(i), 1957 and 371. Section 371 expressly includes an overt act; §1956(h) contains no such requirement and does not cross-reference §371.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nash v. United States and Singer v. United States explain the common-law agreement baseline and the significance of Congress's selection of statutory language. Their relevance is textual context, not a universal conspiracy exemption from proof.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Shabani provides a close interpretive analogy but its holding concerns §846. Whether §1956(h) creates an independent conspiracy offense must be resolved here.
* **Open Point:** Whether the text's same-penalties formulation incorporates §371 or instead independently punishes agreement to commit the identified laundering offenses.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The appellate court treated §1956(h) as a separate conspiracy offense without an overt-act element. **Defendants:** The provision primarily raises punishment for a conspiracy otherwise defined by §371; its placement, legislative history and venue language support retaining the overt-act safeguard. **United States:** The provision directly identifies the prohibited agreement, omits any §371 incorporation, and parallels the text interpreted in Shabani. **Limits and alternatives:** Absence of an overt-act element does not eliminate proof of an actual agreement, guilty knowledge or the charged unlawful object. Venue is a separate question. No independently verified oral concession is assumed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Affirm the no-overt-act instruction ruling by construing §1956(h) as an independent agreement offense; other preserved grounds remain subject to their own disposition.
* If the statute incorporates §371, determine the consequences of the instructional omission under the governing preserved-error rules rather than automatically acquit on an untried element.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The language and structure support a rule for §1956(h) and materially equivalent text, not all conspiracies or all references to conduct in venue statutes.

**Uncertainty:** The review concerns the overt-act question. No additional element finding, independent reversal ground or new sentencing issue is assumed.

## 16. Timken Co. v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Timken Co. v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 354 F.3d 1334, reviewing 240 F. Supp. 2d 1228; no Supreme Court docket identified.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-11 (scheduled simulated merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the antidumping statute permits Commerce to replace negative dumping margins with zero in an administrative review, despite the fair-comparison requirement and the United States’ international trade obligations?
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Federal Circuit affirmance of the Court of International Trade's judgment sustaining Commerce's calculation for the identified Japanese tapered-roller-bearing review.
* **Relief Requested:** Koyo seeks rejection of zeroing and recalculation; the United States defends Commerce's method. Timken's separate adverse-facts calculation cross-appeal is part of the historical litigation but outside the identified zeroing question.
* **Material Record:** Commerce reviewed the October 1998 through September 1999 period. It aggregated positive margins while treating transactions above normal value as zero rather than offsets. Koyo relies on the statutory fair-comparison requirement and a World Trade Organization (WTO) report addressing a different country's investigation. The dispute concerns legal permission for this method, not a finding that every comparison, adjustment or numerical input was independently correct.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The final trade-court and appellate judgments permit review of the method. The caption does not make Timken the proponent of Koyo's zeroing objection. The hypothetical grant does not extend to the separate entered-value versus sales-value cross-appeal.
* **Relevant Dates:** Commerce's final results issued in March 2001; the Federal Circuit decided January 16, 2004. The assigned event is January 11, 2005. Later WTO disputes, administrative changes and later judicial deference doctrines are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-11.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 19 U.S.C. §1516a review asks whether the determination accords with law and is supported as required. Mead and Haggar require actual delegated authority and its exercise before controlling deference; expertise or longstanding practice alone is insufficient.

##### Issue 1: Domestic statutory permission and delegated method

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Mead Corp. retains Chevron within actual delegated force-of-law authority and otherwise requires independent persuasive consideration. United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. enforces statutory boundaries before considering a permissible administrative choice. Barnhart v. Thomas likewise distinguishes an authorized choice from the sole possible construction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify Congress's delegation, the mechanism used, and the statute's actual limits before addressing reasonableness. An agency may not transform a calculation instruction into authority to disregard a contrary enacted command.
* **Other Governing Law:** 19 U.S.C. §§1675(a), 1677(35) and 1677b(a), including the definition of dumping margin, aggregation and fair comparison. Commerce administers duty liability through the prescribed review and final determination mechanism.
* **Material Other Authority:** Murray v. Charming Betsy supplies an interpretive aid where compatible construction is available; it does not authorize rewriting domestic law. The Uruguay Round Agreements Act specifies domestic effect and implementation of international obligations.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law establishes the review framework but does not decide whether these provisions require negative offsets. The specific aggregation question remains open.
* **Open Point:** Whether the authorized review mechanism and statutory language permit zeroing, and what weight the international report has in resolving that domestic question.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit found the margin definition compatible with positive excesses, treated the specified comparison adjustments as consistent with zeroing, and deferred to Commerce's authorized methodology. **Koyo:** Aggregating margins while ignoring favorable transactions distorts the required fair comparison; international obligations reinforce an offsetting construction. **United States:** The statute targets transactions below normal value, leaves aggregation room and does not require above-value sales to cancel dumped sales; the WTO report does not bind this domestic court. **Timken and other grounds:** Timken's distinct cross-appeal cannot broaden this grant. **Limits:** Lack of direct WTO enforceability does not make international materials irrelevant to interpretation; administrative discretion does not make zeroing mandatory.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Sustain zeroing as a permissible, explained method within actual delegated authority and the statute's bounds; the challenged determination remains effective on this issue.
* Construe the aggregation and fair-comparison provisions to require offsets and return for recalculation under that construction.
* If the actual mechanism lacks delegated interpretive force, independently construe the statute while evaluating Commerce's reasons; absence of deference does not itself decide the answer or require a new agency proceeding.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision may settle permission for this administrative-review method under the operative provisions. It need not approve every investigation methodology, declare zeroing compulsory or adjudicate the United States' compliance in an international forum.

**Uncertainty:** No new administrative explanation or international ruling after the event date is assumed. Calculation challenges outside the question presented remain outside this review.

## 17. United States v. Booker, consolidated with United States v. Fanfan

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Booker, consolidated with United States v. Fanfan
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 04-104 and 04-105, Booker and Fanfan consolidated; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 220.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-12 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether judicial findings that increase a binding Federal Sentencing Guidelines range violate the jury and proof guarantees, and, if so, what statutory remedy follows.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Booker: Seventh Circuit, 375 F.3d 508, setting aside the enhanced sentence. Fanfan: certiorari before judgment in the First Circuit, reviewing the District of Maine's refusal to impose an enhancement unsupported by the verdict.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States principally seeks permission for judicial enhancement findings and alternatively asks that the Guidelines cease to bind in affected cases. The defendants seek sentences confined by facts lawfully established and preservation of the statutory system insofar as it operates constitutionally.
* **Material Record:** Booker's verdict-supported range was 210 to 262 months; additional judge-found quantity and obstruction raised the range to 360 months to life, and the court imposed 360 months. Fanfan received 78 months after the judge declined to use additional findings that would have produced a 188-to-235-month range. These figures describe the historical sentencing record, not a new adjudication that every other input satisfies the simulated charging and factfinding rules.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The two dockets require separate judgment consequences. Fanfan's lawful refusal to enhance cannot be treated as the same constitutional error as Booker's imposed enhancement. Preservation and any independently challenged calculation must be assessed on each record; no general waiver, harmlessness or inevitability finding is assumed.
* **Relevant Dates:** Fanfan's judgment issued June 30, 2004; Booker's appellate judgment issued July 9, 2004. The scheduled event is January 12, 2005. Angle and Harris were already effective in 2002, Gatewood in 2003, and Blakely in June 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Shepard later concerns predicate-conviction documentation and Dodd a collateral limitations question. Neither prospective brief supplies an effective modification of the rules here.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-12.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments, Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, 18 U.S.C. §§3553 and 3742, and the operative Guidelines. Federal indictment requirements are independent of the jury and proof requirements. Existing preservation, plain-error and collateral-review rules remain separate.

##### Issue 1: Binding sentencing facts

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Apprendi requires jury proof beyond a reasonable doubt or a valid admission for facts authorizing greater punishment. Angle already applies that principle to increases in a binding Guidelines ceiling or floor; Harris rejects a mandatory-minimum exception. Blakely applies the existing authorization rule to the range supported by the verdict or plea. Gatewood rejects a categorical adult-recidivism exception.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ask what punishment the established verdict, valid element admission or lawful jury waiver authorizes before the disputed fact is added. A fact that increases a binding ceiling or floor receives the applicable charging, jury and beyond-reasonable-doubt protections. Judicial discretion within a lawfully established range remains distinct.
* **Other Governing Law:** The binding directions of §3553(b)(1), the operative Guidelines and the federal grand-jury guarantee. An agency's promulgation of the range does not reduce the constitutional protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mistretta v. United States addresses the Commission's institutional validity, not permission to dispense with jury protections for range-increasing facts. United States v. Watts cannot override the later simulated authorization rule.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The constitutional application to binding federal ranges is already settled by Angle. It is not an open issue first created by the historical Booker litigation. Particular input validity and preserved remedial questions may remain.
* **Open Point:** The record-specific constitutional consequence and the system-level statutory response, which Angle did not fully decide.

##### Issue 2: Severability and relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Angle leaves the Guidelines binding while enforcing constitutional factfinding and does not adopt an advisory replacement. Blakely vacates the unlawful enhanced sentence without automatically authorizing a new enhancement proceeding. Gatewood likewise separates sentence vacatur from authority to institute additional factfinding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A remedy must cure the actual violation through a lawful source. Severability requires identifying what provision or application cannot operate constitutionally and whether the remainder can function consistently with Congress's enacted arrangement. Olano's four requirements remain distinct for a forfeited claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 3553(a), 3553(b)(1), 3742 and the governing charging and trial procedures. The PROTECT Act's then-effective appellate-review amendments are enacted law, not a prediction that a historical remedial excision will occur.
* **Material Other Authority:** Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock supplies severability principles, not authority to design a preferred sentencing policy. Griffith v. Kentucky governs ordinary application on direct review; collateral finality and statutory gateways remain independent.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law bars unlawful enhancements and preserves the binding framework unless lawfully changed. Whether the statutory response should instead remove binding force is a genuinely reserved issue.
* **Open Point:** Whether enforcing jury-compatible mandatory ranges is a lawful complete correction, or whether severability requires excision of binding and review provisions.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Booker's appellate court treated the judge-found enhancement as unconstitutional. Fanfan's court withheld the unsupported enhancement and sentenced within the verdict-authorized range. **United States:** Commission rules should be distinguished from statutory maxima; alternatively, Congress would prefer discretionary sentencing informed by advisory Guidelines to binding ranges based on jury findings. Its historical prior-conviction exception is incompatible with Gatewood under controlling precedent. **Defendants:** Legal constraint, not the identity of the rulemaker, triggers the guarantee; the courts should preserve binding ranges and require lawful proof rather than enlarge judicial discretion. **Alternative grounds and limits:** An advisory remedy would materially change available sentences in both dockets and must be justified as severability, not called an automatic constitutional command. A claim that the same sentence would have been chosen does not establish lawful authorization or satisfy every remedial prerequisite. No oral-argument concession is assumed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Apply Angle and preserve binding Guidelines subject to lawful charging and factfinding. Booker's enhanced sentence cannot stand on the identified findings; Fanfan's refusal to enhance is lawful, subject to any independent calculation issue.
* Adopt an expressly reasoned severability holding removing binding force and associated review provisions. This changes the existing statutory operation and requires new sentencing instructions and separate treatment of both dockets, including preservation and effect.
* A constitutional exception for federal Guidelines would require openly overruling or limiting Angle and related authority; a historical distinction alone cannot make it an application of current law.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The constitutional rule governs any fact that changes a legally binding punishment boundary, while the remedy may be confined to the Federal Sentencing Reform Act. Neither path establishes a categorical right to relitigate a final sentence or a new jury proceeding without independent authorization.

**Uncertainty:** No effective same-term decision alters the baseline. Any disputed admission, charge, waiver, recidivist input, independent calculation or forfeiture issue requires its actual record. The identified facts do not establish an inevitable lawful alternative sentence.

## 18. Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-674; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 335.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-12 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether 8 U.S.C. §1231(b)(2)(E)(iv) requires the receiving country's advance acceptance before removal to the country of the noncitizen's birth.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit, 329 F.3d 630, reversing the district court's acceptance-based habeas relief.
* **Relief Requested:** Jama seeks a bar against removal to Somalia absent advance acceptance; the Government seeks affirmance of its statutory country-selection authority.
* **Material Record:** Jama was born in Somalia, entered as a refugee and later lost that status following a criminal conviction. He did not designate a destination. The agency selected Somalia under the birthplace clause. His statutory challenge rests on Somalia's lack of a functioning government capable of providing advance acceptance, not a new adjudication that he qualifies for withholding or protection against torture.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The habeas claim concerns legal authority for the selected destination. Existing legal-review channels do not automatically reopen every removal determination. A statutory selection power is distinct from physical feasibility and from independently available protection against removal to persecution or torture.
* **Relevant Dates:** The relevant administrative removal proceedings followed termination of refugee status in 2000. The appellate decision issued in 2003; the event is January 12, 2005. The REAL ID Act has not yet been enacted at this event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Clark v. Martinez, listed the same day, concerns duration of post-order detention when removal is not foreseeable. Permission to name a destination does not establish a significant likelihood that removal can occur.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-12.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No effective same-day Clark v. Martinez holding is identified or assumed. Zadvydas v. Davis already preserves the distinct inquiry into actual foreseeable removal.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2241 legal review subject to the applicable immigration jurisdiction provisions; 8 U.S.C. §1231(b)(2)'s sequential country-selection commands and exceptions.

##### Issue 1: Acceptance in the sequential selection scheme

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** INS v. St. Cyr preserves review of a legal eligibility question through the applicable channel; it does not confer substantive removal protection. Zadvydas v. Davis distinguishes statutory removal authority from indefinite detention when actual removal is not foreseeable.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read each destination command and its stated conditions in context. A condition expressly attached to one fallback may not be inserted into every preceding clause without textual or structural support.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1231(b)(2)(A) through (E): the person's designation, nationality and connected-country alternatives precede the clause (vii) fallback to another accepting country. Clause (iv) identifies birthplace without express acceptance language.
* **Material Other Authority:** Murray v. Charming Betsy may inform a genuinely available construction consistent with international obligations; it supplies no independent rewrite of Congress's selection sequence. Grammar is relevant but the entire structure controls.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law does not settle whether clause (iv) carries an implied advance-acceptance requirement. Habeas review and the distinct detention limitation remain effective.
* **Open Point:** Whether the clause (vii) acceptance phrase or the sequence's structure imposes an additional condition on birthplace removal.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Eighth Circuit read the express acceptance requirement as confined to the fallback clause. **Jama:** The sequence presupposes an actual receiving sovereign, and allowing removal to a country that cannot accept him defeats the earlier stages' safeguards and practical premise. **Government:** Congress specified acceptance where required and omitted it from the connected-country clauses; feasibility and separate protection statutes remain operative without adding an across-the-board condition. **Alternative grounds and limits:** A lawful destination choice does not adjudicate actual danger, feasibility, withholding, torture protection or continuing detention. No verified oral concession supplies a waiver of those independent requirements.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Affirm that clause (iv) does not require advance consent, leaving the agency bound by the sequence, actual statutory predicates and independent removal protections.
* Require acceptance as a structural condition and bar this destination absent the required assurance; the agency would need another lawful destination or a changed factual basis.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision can construe connected-country selection without deciding all detention powers or granting the Executive an extra-statutory foreign-affairs exception.

**Uncertainty:** The record does not adjudicate every potential protection claim or establish that physical removal will occur soon. Those gaps do not prevent answering the identified statutory condition.

## 19. Clark v. Martinez

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Clark v. Martinez

* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-12.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No effective same-day Jama decision is identified. A destination-selection ruling would not itself establish foreseeable removal in these Cuban records.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2241 and §1231(a)(6); supervision and parole provisions have distinct functions. Section 1226a is an enacted separate detention route requiring its own national-security or terrorism predicates and procedures, not a free-floating exception.

##### Issue 1: The reserved inadmissible-person application

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Zadvydas v. * Resolve a genuinely moot component only if the record demonstrates elimination of the challenged restraint and risk; temporary parole under contested authority does not by itself establish that condition.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The common statutory rule may cover all categories in §1231(a)(6) while preserving separate detention statutes. It need not decide constitutional admission rights or authorize unconditional release after exactly six months.

* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment and §2254(d)(1), with the 1984 federal-law baseline for the pertinent direct adjudication. State v. Dicks supplies the state construction, not federal authority to ignore the actual jury and appellate processes.
* **Material Other Authority:** Godfrey v. Georgia and Proffitt v. Florida supply pre-1984 narrowing principles. Later explanations may illuminate an old rule but cannot automatically become clearly established law at an earlier cutoff.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The difference between substantive narrowing and a missing citation is governed by existing principles. The actual state adjudication, guidance and any curative operation must still be assessed on this record.
* **Open Point:** Whether the state court actually applied the constitutionally sufficient construction and whether the federal writ rests on an impermissible citation-based inference.

##### Issue 2: Gateway, jury influence and remedy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lambrix v. Singletary distinguishes default and retroactivity, rejects automatic cure through the label of independent judicial review, and requires actual correction of a properly reached influential-jury defect. Its old-rule determination is tied to its own 1986 finality date. Schriro v. Summerlin permits watershed retroactivity for the defined indispensable Ring eligibility finding, but does not eliminate §2254's separate requirements.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First resolve the actual adjudication and applicable gateway, then the precise violation, then an authorized cure or effect inquiry. The simulated Brecht standard is State-borne harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; remaining aggravators and proportionality review do not alone prove it.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 2254(d) and (e), ordinary default rules, and state authority for any new sentencing proceeding. A jury-guidance claim and an indispensable eligibility-fact claim are not interchangeable.
* **Material Other Authority:** Clemons v. Mississippi permits lawful appellate correction within its conditions. It does not authorize a federal court to invent a state reweighing or disregard a distinct jury entitlement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The 2002 mandate leaves this ground open, but no new hearing or writ follows automatically. Any later Ring claim requires its own preserved posture and statutory route.
* **Open Point:** What disposition corrects the Sixth Circuit's premise while preserving the actual-cure inquiry and all genuinely remaining claims.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Sixth Circuit treated mandatory state review as sufficient presentation, found the aggravator vague and found no adequate narrowing application. **Tennessee:** State courts need not repeat a citation to settled state law, and the actual suffering analysis tracks the narrowing construction; AEDPA does not permit the federal inference. **Cone:** A vague jury standard affects the weighing decision, and appellate discussion of disturbing facts does not necessarily establish a constitutionally adequate correction. **Alternative grounds and limits:** The preserved default dispute and actual-merits premise must be identified. Rejecting a citation requirement does not establish that every overlooked claim was adjudicated or that any appellate factual discussion cures jury error. No separate Government theory is established.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Reject the citation-based premise and deny this ground if the actual record establishes the sufficient narrowing and lawful correction under the applicable statutory framework.
* Vacate the writ and remand for a correct assessment of actual state adjudication, applicable default and §2254 predicates, and any surviving jury-influence or cure issue that the erroneous premise obscured.
* Preserve sentence relief only if the petitioner crosses the applicable gateway and establishes an uncured constitutional defect, with the State failing any required beyond-reasonable-doubt effect burden; specify lawful conditional correction.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case may establish that a missing citation is not itself constitutional error while preserving actual adjudication and cure requirements. It does not support a presumption that every unaddressed claim was silently decided or a blanket reinstatement of the entire federal judgment.

**Uncertainty:** The precise state-merits treatment and procedural bar cannot be replaced by assumptions. The historical opinion's assertion that Ring is nonretroactive conflicts with Schriro v. Summerlin under controlling precedent, but a distinct Ring claim is not thereby added to the grant.
