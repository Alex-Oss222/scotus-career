## Reconciled component result and governing terms

All eight Associates reverse the plea-invalidity holding, vacate the prosecutorial-inconsistency portion of the judgment to permit separate treatment of the death sentence, and remand. All eight join the shared plea rule and the defined phase-specific remand. Their common remand does not establish either of the competing separate theories of ultimate capital relief.

1. A knowing and intelligent plea requires real understanding of the offense's nature and essential elements. Competent counsel can provide the explanation; a judge need not personally recite every element when the record accurately establishes that counsel explained it and the defendant understood. Counsel's express representation and Stumpf's personal confirmation establish notice here. His denial of being the shooter is compatible with Ohio aggravated-murder complicity committed with specific intent to cause death. Henderson protects real notice, Brady and Boykin require informed voluntary assent, and the actual Bousley rule preserves accurate knowledge of the offense. A retrospectively disappointing bargain alone does not invalidate an informed plea.
2. The later inconsistent shooter account does not invalidate this plea: shooter identity was not essential to purposeful complicity, and the later presentation does not establish that the earlier assent lacked understanding. That is a distinct plea holding, not a decision that shooter responsibility is immaterial to sentencing.
3. The independent capital claim returns for separate adjudication of the prosecution's factual accounts, the later evidence, the actual basis for selecting death, and the constitutional significance and material prejudice of the supported conduct. The Sixth Circuit's intertwined treatment does not establish how it would decide that independent claim with guilt intact. Relief on an individualized death challenge cannot be rejected merely because the conviction survives. The reviewing court must address the actual claim and its predicates; ultimate sentencing entitlement remains for that adjudication because its material predicates and distinct appellate rationale were not resolved.

**Clarification of the frozen common-ground paragraph:** Napue/Giglio's knowing-false-evidence rule is an established route, not an exclusive prerequisite that all eight require every capital-fairness claim to satisfy. Souter and Ginsburg preserve the broader fundamental-fairness contention described below. Thomas and Scalia reject that contention and accept the common remand without endorsing it. An opinion making either ultimate theory the unanimous rule would exceed this handoff. This clarifies the original component commitments and separate-writing possibilities; it changes no judgment vote or common-opinion join.

## Separate-writing substance

Souter, joined by Ginsburg, identifies a due-process claim about the State maintaining contradictory accounts of the single shooter's identity as reasons for death: first against Stumpf, then against Wesley, and then in defense of Stumpf's sentence. His concern is fundamental fairness and the heightened reliability needed for capital selection. It does not depend on accusing the prosecutor of deliberately misleading either tribunal. Stevens's Jacobs statement supplies the concern about inconsistent sovereign positions; Berger supplies the prosecutor's obligation to justice; Gregg, as discussed in Lewis v. Jeffers, supplies the concern about arbitrary death selection. If a violation is established, the appropriate correction and burdens require attention to the sentencing record. The concurrence does not enter a violation or select a final remedy.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, disputes that newly obtained evidence and a later prosecution theory can by themselves impair the earlier proceeding's reliability or fairness. Ordinary adversarial testing exposes conflicts, and his Herrera-based analysis treats a newly discovered-evidence contention as different from an established constitutional defect. The historical writing also points to uninvoked Teague and default defenses, but this reconciliation does not decide either defense, presume forfeiture, add a mandatory procedural bar, or alter the frozen remand. Those observations remain source verification of the writing's scope. The actual simulated Herrera authority, rather than an unexamined historical formulation, governs any future use of that doctrine.

## Original independent commitment, preserved verbatim

## 80. Bradshaw v. Stumpf

**Component dispositions:** All eight Associates would reverse the determination that the plea was unknowing or unintelligent on the stated intent ground. All eight would return the distinct sentencing challenge for its own adjudication rather than reinstate a final denial of all relief solely because guilt survives. The Sixth Circuit's plea-based relief is reversed; its sentence-related disposition is vacated to the extent it rests on the same invalid plea premise, and the case is remanded for the independent capital claim.

**Plea ground:** Due process requires actual notice and understanding of the essential offense, which competent counsel may provide and the record as a whole may establish. The judge need not personally recite every element. Here counsel represented that the elements had been explained and Stumpf personally acknowledged that advice. His denial of having fired the fatal shots does not contradict purposeful participation under Ohio's aggravated-murder and complicity law. The colloquy, the factual-basis proceedings and the particular denial do not establish that the intent requirement was omitted or misunderstood.

**Plea alternatives:** Henderson v. Morgan, 426 U.S. 637, protects a defendant who lacked real notice of a necessary intent element; Boykin, 395 U.S. 238, and Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742, require an intelligent and voluntary plea. Bousley, May 18, 1998, preserves real notice of an accurately described offense. Stumpf's strongest alternative rests on intellectual limitations, counsel's factual resistance and the judge's incomplete personal account. Those circumstances justify examination rather than a conclusive presumption, but they do not rebut the direct explanation and acknowledgement or make shooter identity an element Ohio law does not require. Lindh and its preserved application-date principle put this claim under the applicable pre-AEDPA merits law.

**Sentencing ground and competing paths:** A valid purposeful-participation plea does not settle the accuracy and fairness of the individualized death decision. Napue v. Illinois, 360 U.S. 264, and Giglio v. United States, 405 U.S. 150, prohibit knowing use of materially false evidence; Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, preserve individualized consideration of responsibility and mitigation. The remand must identify the prosecution's actual factual representations, the later evidence, what was known, the basis on which death was selected, and the material prejudicial effect of any constitutional defect. The strongest State path is that different evidence may support different permissible inferences and both participants may intentionally aid murder. That establishes neither false evidence nor an automatic bar to prosecuting both. The strongest prisoner path is that the State used contradictory claims of principal responsibility to obtain death. That possibility cannot be rejected as irrelevant merely because complicity supports guilt. The existing appellate analysis improperly merged the phases; unresolved material predicates justify the remand under Engine §8(c).
