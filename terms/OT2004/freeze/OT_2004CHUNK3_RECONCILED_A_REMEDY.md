## Existing authority and actual legal work

The complete *Casey* rule, as applied in *Voinovich* and *Stenberg*, already matches relief to demonstrated duties, applications and affected groups; preserves independently lawful provisions; and requires treatment of sanctions enforcing an unconstitutional command. Challengers first establish the duty, causation, affected population and triggering interference. The rule permits broader relief when its supporting violation and scope are proved, while rejecting an automatic whole-statute injunction based on an isolated defect.

*Anderson* adds a concrete current-term public example. All nine joined the general remedial direction requiring relief against adjudicated unlawful policy operation and allowing the State to establish that a narrower remedy fully cures the proved violation. The same opinion requires precise terms, preservation of lawful rules and actual state-law construction/severability rather than federal rewriting. Its general remedy coalition is express; it is not inferred from agreement on only one merits component.

Together, those rules support the proposed Taft language when its initial condition retains its full force: the common policy's unlawful operation and the affected group must first be established. The State's narrower-cure showing concerns remedial adequacy after that proof. It does not move the initial constitutional or population proof to Ohio, presume that every application is unlawful, or revive the independent third-party claim dismissed for lack of standing. The unappealed standing disposition remains effective, and the injunction reaches the actual defendants and persons lawfully bound through them. Protection against enforcement consequences dependent on an established invalid application is different from adjudicating that unappealed independent claim.

The interim direction remains conditional on ordinary equitable standards, the supported record and the protection needed for the established or adequately supported application. It requires prompt adjudication and appropriate protection once the predicates are established; it does not order automatic blanket interim relief merely because the case is remanded. An expedited evidentiary schedule addresses genuinely unresolved material facts when their determination is needed. The courts may decide legal construction and adequate existing-record applications without a new evidentiary hearing. The formulation establishes no new study, filing requirement, numerical risk threshold or clinical fact.

These qualifications follow the actual proof and tailoring conditions in the proposed sentence and frozen commitments. They are not new public disclaimers that must be separately recited. The affirmative operative language should retain the proof trigger, scope of the cure, ordinary equitable conditions and actual need for factual work, so that the opinion cannot reasonably be read to eliminate them.

## Kennedy's precise relationship to judgment and writing

Kennedy supports rejection of the categorical ordinary-D&E ground, vacation of the appellate judgment's conclusive health rejection, and remand to apply the supported necessary-care baseline through a lawful construction or protection at the proved scope. He supports the associated severability, dependent-sanction and prompt conditional interim-protection directions. The proposed remedial precision is compatible with every one of those commitments.

His freeze also expressly withholds the additional remand instruction requiring the residual lesser-burden inquiry he rejected in T4. That is narrower than opposition to the health vacatur or to remand itself, but broader than disagreement with incidental reasoning if the Court's mandate actually directs that extra review. When the final mandate includes T4's residual-review command, his writing is accurately described as **concurring in part and dissenting in part**: he concurs in the health vacatur/remand and its necessary-care implementation, and dissents only from the additional residual-review instruction and supporting framework. The component table must not count him against the health disposition or count him for the full additional command.

If a final opinion merely identifies T4 as existing law without adding an operative residual-review direction, an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment could accurately describe his relationship to that narrower mandate. That alternative is a statement about opinion form, not authority to delete or soften the five Associates' frozen full-framework mandate. Under the T5 mandate actually frozen, which incorporates T4's additional inquiry, the limited concurrence-and-dissent description is the precise one. No new author is selected.

His separate writing has substantive work: retain the necessary-care baseline and effective relief, explain why the current Ohio text and supported clinical applications need adjudication, and distinguish that inquiry from his rejected general residual-interference substantiation rule. It should not imply that ordinary D&E availability alone validates every necessary-care application or that a generally accepted remedy changes his constitutional framework.
