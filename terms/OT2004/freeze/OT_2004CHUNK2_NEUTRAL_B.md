## 17. United States v. Booker, consolidated with United States v. Fanfan

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Booker, consolidated with United States v. Fanfan
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 04-104 and 04-105, Booker and Fanfan consolidated; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 220.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-12 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether judicial findings that increase a binding Federal Sentencing Guidelines range violate the jury and proof guarantees, and, if so, what statutory remedy follows.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Booker: Seventh Circuit, 375 F.3d 508, setting aside the enhanced sentence. Fanfan: certiorari before judgment in the First Circuit, reviewing the District of Maine's refusal to impose an enhancement unsupported by the verdict.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States principally seeks permission for judicial enhancement findings and alternatively asks that the Guidelines cease to bind in affected cases. The defendants seek sentences confined by facts lawfully established and preservation of the statutory system insofar as it operates constitutionally.
* **Material Record:** Booker's verdict-supported range was 210 to 262 months; additional judge-found quantity and obstruction raised the range to 360 months to life, and the court imposed 360 months. Fanfan received 78 months after the judge declined to use additional findings that would have produced a 188-to-235-month range. These figures describe the historical sentencing record, not a new adjudication that every other input satisfies the simulated charging and factfinding rules.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The two dockets require separate judgment consequences. Fanfan's lawful refusal to enhance cannot be treated as the same constitutional error as Booker's imposed enhancement. Preservation and any independently challenged calculation must be assessed on each record; no general waiver, harmlessness or inevitability finding is assumed.
* **Relevant Dates:** Fanfan's judgment issued June 30, 2004; Booker's appellate judgment issued July 9, 2004. The scheduled event is January 12, 2005. Angle and Harris were already effective in 2002, Gatewood in 2003, and Blakely in June 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Shepard later concerns predicate-conviction documentation and Dodd a collateral limitations question. Neither prospective brief supplies an effective modification of the rules here.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-12.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments, Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure, 18 U.S.C. §§3553 and 3742, and the operative Guidelines. Federal indictment requirements are independent of the jury and proof requirements. Existing preservation, plain-error and collateral-review rules remain separate.

##### Issue 1: Binding sentencing facts

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Apprendi requires jury proof beyond a reasonable doubt or a valid admission for facts authorizing greater punishment. Angle already applies that principle to increases in a binding Guidelines ceiling or floor; Harris rejects a mandatory-minimum exception. Blakely applies the existing authorization rule to the range supported by the verdict or plea. Gatewood rejects a categorical adult-recidivism exception.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ask what punishment the established verdict, valid element admission or lawful jury waiver authorizes before the disputed fact is added. A fact that increases a binding ceiling or floor receives the applicable charging, jury and beyond-reasonable-doubt protections. Judicial discretion within a lawfully established range remains distinct.
* **Other Governing Law:** The binding directions of §3553(b)(1), the operative Guidelines and the federal grand-jury guarantee. An agency's promulgation of the range does not reduce the constitutional protection.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mistretta v. United States addresses the Commission's institutional validity, not permission to dispense with jury protections for range-increasing facts. United States v. Watts cannot override the later simulated authorization rule.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The constitutional application to binding federal ranges is already settled by Angle. It is not an open issue first created by the historical Booker litigation. Particular input validity and preserved remedial questions may remain.
* **Open Point:** The record-specific constitutional consequence and the system-level statutory response, which Angle did not fully decide.

##### Issue 2: Severability and relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Angle leaves the Guidelines binding while enforcing constitutional factfinding and does not adopt an advisory replacement. Blakely vacates the unlawful enhanced sentence without automatically authorizing a new enhancement proceeding. Gatewood likewise separates sentence vacatur from authority to institute additional factfinding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A remedy must cure the actual violation through a lawful source. Severability requires identifying what provision or application cannot operate constitutionally and whether the remainder can function consistently with Congress's enacted arrangement. Olano's four requirements remain distinct for a forfeited claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 3553(a), 3553(b)(1), 3742 and the governing charging and trial procedures. The PROTECT Act's then-effective appellate-review amendments are enacted law, not a prediction that a historical remedial excision will occur.
* **Material Other Authority:** Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock supplies severability principles, not authority to design a preferred sentencing policy. Griffith v. Kentucky governs ordinary application on direct review; collateral finality and statutory gateways remain independent.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law bars unlawful enhancements and preserves the binding framework unless lawfully changed. Whether the statutory response should instead remove binding force is a genuinely reserved issue.
* **Open Point:** Whether enforcing jury-compatible mandatory ranges is a lawful complete correction, or whether severability requires excision of binding and review provisions.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** Booker's appellate court treated the judge-found enhancement as unconstitutional. Fanfan's court withheld the unsupported enhancement and sentenced within the verdict-authorized range. **United States:** Commission rules should be distinguished from statutory maxima; alternatively, Congress would prefer discretionary sentencing informed by advisory Guidelines to binding ranges based on jury findings. Its historical prior-conviction exception is incompatible with Gatewood under controlling precedent. **Defendants:** Legal constraint, not the identity of the rulemaker, triggers the guarantee; the courts should preserve binding ranges and require lawful proof rather than enlarge judicial discretion. **Alternative grounds and limits:** An advisory remedy would materially change available sentences in both dockets and must be justified as severability, not called an automatic constitutional command. A claim that the same sentence would have been chosen does not establish lawful authorization or satisfy every remedial prerequisite. No oral-argument concession is assumed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Apply Angle and preserve binding Guidelines subject to lawful charging and factfinding. Booker's enhanced sentence cannot stand on the identified findings; Fanfan's refusal to enhance is lawful, subject to any independent calculation issue.
* Adopt an expressly reasoned severability holding removing binding force and associated review provisions. This changes the existing statutory operation and requires new sentencing instructions and separate treatment of both dockets, including preservation and effect.
* A constitutional exception for federal Guidelines would require openly overruling or limiting Angle and related authority; a historical distinction alone cannot make it an application of current law.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The constitutional rule governs any fact that changes a legally binding punishment boundary, while the remedy may be confined to the Federal Sentencing Reform Act. Neither path establishes a categorical right to relitigate a final sentence or a new jury proceeding without independent authorization.

**Uncertainty:** No effective same-term decision alters the baseline. Any disputed admission, charge, waiver, recidivist input, independent calculation or forfeiture issue requires its actual record. The identified facts do not establish an inevitable lawful alternative sentence.

## 18. Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-674; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 335.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-12 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether 8 U.S.C. §1231(b)(2)(E)(iv) requires the receiving country's advance acceptance before removal to the country of the noncitizen's birth.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit, 329 F.3d 630, reversing the district court's acceptance-based habeas relief.
* **Relief Requested:** Jama seeks a bar against removal to Somalia absent advance acceptance; the Government seeks affirmance of its statutory country-selection authority.
* **Material Record:** Jama was born in Somalia, entered as a refugee and later lost that status following a criminal conviction. He did not designate a destination. The agency selected Somalia under the birthplace clause. His statutory challenge rests on Somalia's lack of a functioning government capable of providing advance acceptance, not a new adjudication that he qualifies for withholding or protection against torture.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The habeas claim concerns legal authority for the selected destination. Existing legal-review channels do not automatically reopen every removal determination. A statutory selection power is distinct from physical feasibility and from independently available protection against removal to persecution or torture.
* **Relevant Dates:** The relevant administrative removal proceedings followed termination of refugee status in 2000. The appellate decision issued in 2003; the event is January 12, 2005. The REAL ID Act has not yet been enacted at this event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Clark v. Martinez, listed the same day, concerns duration of post-order detention when removal is not foreseeable. Permission to name a destination does not establish a significant likelihood that removal can occur.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-12.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No effective same-day Clark v. Martinez holding is identified or assumed. Zadvydas v. Davis already preserves the distinct inquiry into actual foreseeable removal.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2241 legal review subject to the applicable immigration jurisdiction provisions; 8 U.S.C. §1231(b)(2)'s sequential country-selection commands and exceptions.

##### Issue 1: Acceptance in the sequential selection scheme

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** INS v. St. Cyr preserves review of a legal eligibility question through the applicable channel; it does not confer substantive removal protection. Zadvydas v. Davis distinguishes statutory removal authority from indefinite detention when actual removal is not foreseeable.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read each destination command and its stated conditions in context. A condition expressly attached to one fallback may not be inserted into every preceding clause without textual or structural support.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1231(b)(2)(A) through (E): the person's designation, nationality and connected-country alternatives precede the clause (vii) fallback to another accepting country. Clause (iv) identifies birthplace without express acceptance language.
* **Material Other Authority:** Murray v. Charming Betsy may inform a genuinely available construction consistent with international obligations; it supplies no independent rewrite of Congress's selection sequence. Grammar is relevant but the entire structure controls.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law does not settle whether clause (iv) carries an implied advance-acceptance requirement. Habeas review and the distinct detention limitation remain effective.
* **Open Point:** Whether the clause (vii) acceptance phrase or the sequence's structure imposes an additional condition on birthplace removal.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Eighth Circuit read the express acceptance requirement as confined to the fallback clause. **Jama:** The sequence presupposes an actual receiving sovereign, and allowing removal to a country that cannot accept him defeats the earlier stages' safeguards and practical premise. **Government:** Congress specified acceptance where required and omitted it from the connected-country clauses; feasibility and separate protection statutes remain operative without adding an across-the-board condition. **Alternative grounds and limits:** A lawful destination choice does not adjudicate actual danger, feasibility, withholding, torture protection or continuing detention. No verified oral concession supplies a waiver of those independent requirements.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Affirm that clause (iv) does not require advance consent, leaving the agency bound by the sequence, actual statutory predicates and independent removal protections.
* Require acceptance as a structural condition and bar this destination absent the required assurance; the agency would need another lawful destination or a changed factual basis.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision can construe connected-country selection without deciding all detention powers or granting the Executive an extra-statutory foreign-affairs exception.

**Uncertainty:** The record does not adjudicate every potential protection claim or establish that physical removal will occur soon. Those gaps do not prevent answering the identified statutory condition.

## 19. Clark v. Martinez

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Clark v. Martinez
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 03-878 and 03-7434, with Benitez v. Rozos; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 371.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-12 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether §1231(a)(6)'s limitation to detention reasonably necessary for removal also governs inadmissible noncitizens who have not been admitted, and what relief the two custody records require.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Martinez: Ninth Circuit affirmance of conditional release after the district court found removal unforeseeable. Benitez: Eleventh Circuit, 337 F.3d 1289, affirming denial of habeas relief despite the same lack of foreseeable removal.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks authority for continued detention of inadmissible persons; Martinez and Benitez seek the removal-related temporal limit and enforceable release protection.
* **Material Record:** Both men arrived from Cuba in 1980 and were paroled without admission, later became subject to final removal orders, and remained detained beyond the ordinary removal period. The district courts accepted that removal to Cuba was not reasonably foreseeable. Martinez was released under the judgment under review. Benitez received temporary parole during Supreme Court proceedings, with the Government retaining asserted authority to return him to custody.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The custody litigation remains live despite conditional or temporary release because the challenged authority and resulting restraint are not conclusively extinguished. The two dockets require different formal dispositions. Parole is not legal admission; a statutory release ruling does not change immigration status.
* **Relevant Dates:** The two lower federal judgments predate this term. Benitez's temporary release occurred October 15, 2004. The event is January 12, 2005. The 2001 national-security detention enactment already exists but its predicates are not established here.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Jama concerns authority to select a country, not whether removal to it is significantly likely within a reasonably foreseeable period. Benitez is a consolidated companion within this listed matter.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-12.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No effective same-day Jama decision is identified. A destination-selection ruling would not itself establish foreseeable removal in these Cuban records.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2241 and §1231(a)(6); supervision and parole provisions have distinct functions. Section 1226a is an enacted separate detention route requiring its own national-security or terrorism predicates and procedures, not a free-floating exception.

##### Issue 1: The reserved inadmissible-person application

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Zadvydas v. Davis construes §1231(a)(6) for previously admitted persons as authorizing only detention reasonably necessary for removal, with a six-month presumption and a burden-shifting foreseeable-removal inquiry. It expressly reserves the unadmitted category rather than already deciding this case.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** After the presumptive period, the detainee supplies good reason to believe removal lacks a significant likelihood in the reasonably foreseeable future; the Government must produce sufficient evidence to rebut it. Six months is not an unconditional release deadline, and foreign refusal is not automatically the detainee's obstruction.
* **Other Governing Law:** The same operative detention language covers the listed §1231(a)(6) categories. Sections 1182(d)(5) and 1231(a)(3) govern parole and supervision; they do not automatically confer permanent preventive confinement. Section 1226a remains a separate, predicate-dependent enactment.
* **Material Other Authority:** Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei concerns a distinct entry setting and does not decide the meaning of this later statute. INS v. St. Cyr preserves the relevant legal-review distinction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The admitted-person rule and its actual burdens are settled; extending the statutory construction to the expressly reserved class requires a new holding. Constitutional parity across every admission category is not required to decide statutory scope.
* **Open Point:** Whether the common detention text has the same removal-related limit for these inadmissible persons and whether either record supports continued custody under it.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit applied the removal-related limit; the Eleventh distinguished inadmissible persons and allowed longer custody despite no foreseeable removal. **Government:** Admission status changes the constitutional concerns supporting Zadvydas and preserves broader entry-related detention authority, with parole as a safety valve. **Detainees:** The common statutory grant serves removal for all its subjects; a reservation of a category is not an enacted authorization for indefinite custody. **Alternative grounds and limits:** Separate national-security detention requires its own predicates, and release may carry lawful supervision. The temporary parole does not concede a permanent legal limit. No unverified concession or new danger finding is attributed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Extend the statutory removal-related limitation to inadmissible persons, affirm Martinez's release protection and reverse Benitez's denial; impose lawful supervision without treating release as admission.
* Distinguish the reserved category under the statute and sustain custody only through the resulting defined authority, with any independently preserved constitutional claim separately addressed.
* Resolve a genuinely moot component only if the record demonstrates elimination of the challenged restraint and risk; temporary parole under contested authority does not by itself establish that condition.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The common statutory rule may cover all categories in §1231(a)(6) while preserving separate detention statutes. It need not decide constitutional admission rights or authorize unconditional release after exactly six months.

**Uncertainty:** The records contain no operative national-security certification or rebuttal establishing foreseeable Cuban removal. Later diplomatic developments cannot be used to fabricate a current likelihood.

## 20. Nordyke v. King

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Nordyke v. King
* **Citation or Docket:** 319 F.3d 1185; rehearing denied, 364 F.3d 1025; district-court No. CV-99-04389-MJJ.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-12 (scheduled simulated merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether private individuals may invoke the Second Amendment to challenge a county ordinance prohibiting firearm possession on county property, including at gun shows? Whether prohibiting firearm possession at county fairgrounds violates gun-show organizers’ and participants’ First Amendment rights by preventing firearm displays associated with their advocacy?
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit affirmance of denial of preliminary injunctive relief; 2004 rehearing proceedings preserved the identified timely route. California's highest court separately answered the state preemption question.
* **Relief Requested:** The organizers seek preliminary restraint of the county possession ban; the county seeks to retain its regulation of county property and defeat the constitutional claims.
* **Material Record:** Alameda County prohibits bringing or possessing loaded or unloaded firearms and ammunition on county property, subject to exceptions. The fairgrounds previously hosted gun shows with sales, exhibits and advocacy. The restriction makes a conventional show difficult to operate but does not in terms forbid every sale or offer. The record does not establish the burden on all available acquisition channels, the conditions of every expressive display or the necessity of every exception.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a preliminary-injunction record, not a final adjudication of all applications. Denial of an individual right or its application to the States conflicts with Love v. Pepersack. Personal injury and each claim's proper claimant still require ordinary standing analysis; the existing individual right does not automatically prove all plaintiffs' injuries or an injunction entitlement.
* **Relevant Dates:** The ordinance dates to 1999; the appellate decision issued February 18, 2003, and rehearing was denied April 5, 2004. The scheduled event is January 12, 2005. Later Nordyke proceedings and later historical Second Amendment cases are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-12.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal-question and §1983 review of local regulation; interlocutory injunction review under 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1). Preliminary relief requires actual merits likelihood, irreparable injury and the remaining equitable prerequisites; a legal premise's correction is not an automatic injunction.

##### Issue 1: Individual right, incorporation and actual burden

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Love v. Pepersack recognizes an individual right, incorporates it against the States through due process, and protects necessary acquisition incident to keeping arms. United States v. Wright supplies coverage and burden-based justification; United States v. Emerson applies intermediate scrutiny to the supported protective-order restriction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify protected arms and conduct, then the actual burden. A categorical destruction of meaningful ordinary functional defensive home possession is forbidden; severe core burdens require the stated strict justification, substantial burdens the stated intermediate fit, and incidental nonobstructive regulation the applicable reasonableness inquiry. Safety labels alone do not establish fit.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Second and Fourteenth Amendments and the ordinance's actual possession rule and exceptions. Public ownership is relevant to the setting but does not repeal an incorporated right.
* **Material Other Authority:** The historical circuit's collective-right authorities cannot displace Love. State preemption is separately resolved under state law and does not answer the federal constitutional claim.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Individual protection and incorporation are settled. Whether this fairgrounds restriction materially obstructs protected acquisition or possession, and what justification its actual burden requires, needs record application.
* **Open Point:** The actual constitutional burden and justification of the county-property restriction, not whether the right exists.

##### Issue 2: Expression and preliminary relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Texas v. Johnson protects sufficiently expressive conduct without turning every possession into speech. United States v. O'Brien distinguishes regulation aimed at expression from a justified incidental burden. The Second Amendment inquiry does not automatically resolve the First Amendment claim.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual communication and regulatory operation; assess content or viewpoint targeting if shown, otherwise apply the proper conduct or forum rule. A facial challenge needs its own breadth justification; isolated expressive possibilities do not automatically invalidate every possession application.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments, ordinance exemptions and ordinary preliminary-relief requirements. The county's proprietary role and actual forum use matter without permitting viewpoint discrimination.
* **Material Other Authority:** The lower-court record distinguishes possession, offers to sell and overtly expressive displays. Motive evidence must be connected to the enacted rule's operation rather than used as a substitute for the applicable test.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing expression rules govern the type of claim. The actual protected display, comparative exemptions and relief breadth require their specific records.
* **Open Point:** Whether a sufficiently supported First Amendment theory warrants preliminary relief beyond correction of the erroneous Second Amendment premise.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The appellate court rejected individual Second Amendment enforcement under historical circuit precedent and rejected the broad facial speech attack while distinguishing possession from offers to sell. **Organizers:** The county cannot extinguish an ordinary acquisition venue by denying the individual right, and the show combines possession with political and educational communication. **County:** A public-property safety rule leaves other acquisition channels and advocacy available; possession is not inherently speech, and a facial attack exceeds any demonstrated display injury. **Alternatives and limits:** The county must justify the burden under the simulated standard, not merely invoke ownership. The organizers must establish actual protected conduct and equitable prerequisites; a gun show is not automatically the constitutional equivalent of defensive home possession. No additional verified Government rule is identified.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Vacate the denial insofar as it rests on the nonexistent individual right and return for the settled burden-and-justification inquiry and all necessary preliminary-relief findings.
* Sustain the denial on an independently sufficient developed record showing no requisite burden or equitable entitlement, without repeating the incorrect rights premise.
* Grant appropriately bounded preliminary relief if the actual record establishes every prerequisite, including a supported speech or acquisition violation; do not invalidate all county-property applications from an unproved generalization.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision may reaffirm incorporation and necessary acquisition while applying the established burden framework to public property. The First Amendment path can distinguish ordinary possession from actual expression and facial from demonstrated relief.

**Uncertainty:** The identified preliminary record lacks findings sufficient to classify every burden and exemption. Later operational accommodations are not event-date facts. Historical collective-right reasoning cannot be repaired by importing later historical incorporation doctrine.
