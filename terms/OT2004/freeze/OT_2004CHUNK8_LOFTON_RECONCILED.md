## Comparator limit and governing sources

The frozen comparator identifies only the Eleventh Circuit's judgment and supplies no Supreme Court merits result, opinion or Justice lineup. No petition disposition is used to infer a merits vote. The historical lower court used rational basis and rejected the family-integrity and private-intimacy claims. That scrutiny premise conflicts with controlling current law and supplies no reason to demote it.

The authorized neutral A packet and required fresh validation supply the facts: categorical exclusion from adoption, eligibility of unmarried heterosexual applicants, actual foster/guardian reliance, established care relationships, the father's placement and later consent in Houghton's case, and Lofton's stated financial/Medicaid concern. None establishes every applicant's fitness, an automatic adoption entitlement, inadequacy of every protective alternative or an order removing a child.

The exact current authority is Shahar's March 2, 1998 intermediate scrutiny and status-proxy rule, Equality Foundation's December 3, 1998 application outside employment, Romer's ordinary-review floor, Troxel's bounded fit-parent proposition without a complete common scrutiny rule, and Lawrence's protected private criminal-intimacy category with civil status reserved. The mapped public Shahar, Equality Foundation, Troxel and Lawrence writings establish each Associate's own positions. Current Johnson supports deciding issues resolved by the actual record and reserving genuinely unresolved material questions; its racial test and prison-remedy statute are not adoption law.

## Components and complete scope

**F1 — Existing equality rule.** Sexual-orientation classifications, including deliberate status proxies, are subject to intermediate scrutiny. Florida must establish a genuine important objective and substantial relationship to the actual classification. Moral disapproval, private bias, stereotypes and merely conceivable post hoc reasons do not satisfy that burden. Supported professional judgment and reasonable prediction matter without requiring scientific certainty, least restrictive means or an individualized exception in every statutory rule.

**F2 — Application and remedy.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support reversal and remand to remove the categorical orientation barrier from adoption consideration. Stable permanent homes and child welfare are important objectives, but the permanent-versus-temporary distinction and possibility of later marriage by heterosexual single applicants do not establish the required substantial fit on this developed record. Existing foster and guardianship reliance does not prove every proposed adoption appropriate; it does prevent treating orientation itself as established parenting incapacity. Heterosexual-single eligibility also means that a preference for a presently two-parent, mother-and-father household does not describe the line the statute actually draws.

The prospective declaration and injunction must correct the categorical policy's unconstitutional eligibility operation while preserving neutral fitness, existing parental rights, lawful procedure and child-interest determinations. The State may establish that narrower relief fully cures the same policy defect under the operative common-policy remedial principle; the remedy does not predetermine individual placements. The remand implements the resolved equality rule and adjudicates actual adoption requests; it is not an automatic reopening of the already unsupported categorical justification or a new scientific-proof requirement.

**F3 — Alternative family-liberty claim.** The six supporting F2 do not reach a separate substantive-due-process holding because equal access to consideration supplies the requested complete opportunity. The pleaded alternative concerns established families and care relationships, not a general right to obtain a child. Troxel and Smith v. Organization of Foster Families distinguish actual protected relationships, their legal sources and the interference at issue; neither automatically converts foster care or guardianship into adoption. Lawrence does not decide civil family status. No adverse declaration about a disclaimed universal adoption entitlement substitutes for adjudication of the actual preserved theory.

**F4 — Opposing proposal.** Scalia and Thomas would affirm both claims by expressly rejecting Shahar's intermediate-tier rule to the extent needed to apply their maintained ordinary-rationality position, and by rejecting the existing-family claim at the particular new-status boundary. They do not assert that rational basis is already current law or that conceivable reasons satisfy intermediate scrutiny. Under their proposed ordinary review, permanent-placement responsibility and the possibility of future mother-and-father households justify the distinction notwithstanding imperfect coverage. They would not infer unfitness, present harm or removal. For the independent liberty claim, the challenged law does not itself dissolve existing care or displace present custody authority, and the established family precedents do not compel conversion to adoptive status; the biological father's consent removes an obstacle without establishing every legal condition for adoption.
