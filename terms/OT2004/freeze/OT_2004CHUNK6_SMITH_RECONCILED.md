## Scalia's agency premise and separate position

**Reconciliation correction:** The initial commitment treated Scalia's agreement with compatible textual reasoning as a full join to independent judicial construction. The complete comparator expressly distinguishes those levels: he accepts that reasoning to establish the agency interpretation's reasonableness, while withholding an independent construction. No changed premise supports erasing that distinction. Reconciliation therefore corrects the join here without rewriting the initial handoff or claiming a historical departure.

His separate position explains that Congress authorized ADEA implementation through section 628 and that the EEOC exercised that authority in its longstanding interpretation recognizing adverse-impact restrictions. The national rule and its asserted statutory delegation differ from Christensen's advisory response and Mead's revisable transaction-specific classification letters. The entering Mead rule requires actual delegated authority and its exercise; formal rulemaking is evidence, not an exclusive test. Scalia considers that requirement satisfied here and independently adheres to his broader official-agency approach. This is his sufficient reason to accept recognition, not a new controlling administrative-law rule.

The two propositions in section 1625.7(d) remain distinct. Its recognition that facially neutral practices can violate the ADEA is, for Scalia, a reasonable exercise of the delegated authority. Its business-necessity restriction on reasonable nonage factors conflicts with the enacted standard. Sharing a regulatory paragraph does not make the propositions legally inseparable. He therefore joins the statutory reasonableness rule and this plan's application while refusing to turn his agency ground into a full independent-construction join. The source receipt and initial commitments already identify this institutional premise; reconciliation discovers no new operative factual premise requiring a new neutral packet.

## No-impact separate position and qualifications

O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas retain a substantive concurrence in the judgment. They read because-of-age language as requiring an age-based employment decision; the effects words in subsection (a)(2) identify harmful forms of classification rather than an additional unintentional-effects wrong. The reasonable-factors clause confirms a nonage safe harbor. The ADEA's distinction between prohibited arbitrary age discrimination and educational or other responses to age-related employment disadvantages, together with the possibility that legitimate tenure and compensation practices correlate with age, counsels against automatically transferring Griggs. Griggs followed the ADEA's enactment and its Title VII setting does not settle this statute's different structure.

They also reject Scalia's agency ground: in their view the regulation principally explains the reasonable-factors exemption without adequately exercising authority to construe the prohibitory subsection, and its business-necessity requirement misreads the exemption. They conditionally agree that, if impact claims exist, they require a particular identified practice and that reasonable nonage factors provide narrower liability than business necessity would allow. Their historical alternative also assigns the employee the burden of disproving a produced nonage explanation under their Wards Cove reading. That is a position of this separate writing, not a burden holding of the five Associates who join the lead application; no joined opinion may silently adopt it. Nothing in the supported plan application requires allocating an unresolved factual tie.

Their conditional acceptance does not supply an alternative Court holding, a recognition join, or author eligibility for the independent-construction part. The lead independent-construction rationale answers their principal argument by explaining the separate effects language and the reasonable-factors clause's work; Scalia's separate position answers the agency objection at its own level.

## Application, law and sources

The initial commitment's complete reasonable-factors rule and application remain adopted: the officers must isolate a practice and establish causal disparity; this plan is upheld even assuming the identified allocation caused the reported disparity, because regional wage competitiveness, survey-based job ranges, existing salary, rank and tenure are supported reasonable nonage factors. A more equal percentage allocation or another workable method does not establish unreasonableness. Hazen Paper's actual-motivation and age-proxy rules remain for intentional treatment. Cline retains directional coverage. The 1991 Title VII amendments do not amend the ADEA. A blanket rule that every claimed cost saving is reasonable is not adopted.

**Bounded difference from the historical application:** The historical opinion also treated failure to identify a particular practice as an independently sufficient ground. The clean source packet deliberately does not treat that judicial characterization as an objective litigation fact, and the complete original claim/brief record has not been recovered. For each of Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, the separately sufficient reasonable-factors application supports the same judgment on supplied facts; each retains the identification rule without asserting an unsupported factual failure. This is a bounded adjudicative-record difference, not a finding that litigants omitted papers or lacked an opportunity to respond. The Court can fully resolve this impact claim on the actual alternative ground requested by the City. If a Record labels the omission a historical departure in grounds, it must name this precise evidentiary limit and the independently sufficient ground; it must not describe a changed merits vote.
