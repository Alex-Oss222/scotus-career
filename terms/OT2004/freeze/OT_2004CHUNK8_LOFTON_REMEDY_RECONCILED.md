# Lofton — additive remedy compatibility receipt

**Event:** May 23, 2005. **Scope:** Administrative effectuation of the eligibility remedy in the completed `OT_2004CHUNK8_LOFTON_RECONCILED.md`. This receipt adds precision without amending that frozen file or the independent commitments. It receives no current Chief position and supplies no final Court result or assignment.

## Proposal tested and governing source

The Department removes the invalid categorical orientation criterion from its instructions and application decisions, informs personnel responsible for administering eligibility, and promptly reconsiders rejections resting only on that criterion through the ordinarily applicable procedures. The district court establishes and enforces a compliance schedule. Neutral child-specific welfare review remains intact. The State may establish that narrower relief fully cures the same policy defect.

The frozen F2 remedy requires a prospective declaration and injunction correcting the categorical policy's unconstitutional eligibility operation, with neutral fitness, existing parental rights, lawful procedure and individual child-interest determinations preserved. The effective Anderson v. Spear public projection, November 30, 2004, provides the general implementing principle: precise relief corrects a proved common policy unless the State shows that narrower relief fully cures the violation; written directions must conform and the decree remains enforceable. All six F2 supporters joined that general remedial direction. Its election-specific substantive scrutiny is not transferred to adoption.

## Exact compatibility determination

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the proposed directions as ordinary effectuation of their complete F2 remedy, subject to these intrinsic limits:

1. Removing the criterion means removing orientation as the adjudicated categorical eligibility barrier from the Department's relevant written directions and decisions. It does not amend unrelated state law or prohibit independently justified, neutral consideration of actual conduct or the child's circumstances.
2. Personnel notice concerns the officials who must implement the decree. It supplies an effective administration requirement, not a new public-education program or a judicially prescribed message about disputed social policy.
3. Prompt reconsideration restores a lawful opportunity for applicants whose operative rejection rests only on the invalid barrier. It proceeds through ordinary applicable application, renewal or review procedures. It does not automatically reopen final judicial judgments, disturb completed adoptions or others' existing parental rights, waive an independent lawful procedural requirement, or require approval of any adoption. A live application cannot continue to be denied solely because the invalid criterion appeared in an earlier administrative refusal.
4. The district court may set concrete dates and enforce compliance after accounting for the actual administrative tasks. The direction to set an effective schedule does not predetermine an unsupported numerical deadline, displace ordinary equitable administration or authorize federal supervision of unrelated adoption decisions.
5. The State's opportunity to establish a fully curative narrower scope remains real. The burden is to show cure of the same proved common-policy defect; a party label alone does not establish that cure. Neutral child-welfare and fitness determinations remain for the responsible decisionmakers under lawful procedures.

O'Connor and Kennedy join these implementing directions while applying the existing intermediate-scrutiny rule; their earlier nonjoins in that rule's original adoption do not require a narrower present remedial join. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join on the same F2 basis. None adds a new substantive-due-process holding, individual fitness finding, automatic adoption entitlement or scientific-proof requirement.

Scalia and Thomas continue to oppose the merits injunction under their frozen F4 positions. Their agreement with ordinary equitable administration in other cases is not a current join in relief they regard as unwarranted. No Associate disposition or historical comparison changes.

**Validation:** All six F2 supporters join the specified implementation with its ordinary-procedure, finality, child-specific and scope limits. The additions implement the already frozen eligibility remedy and do not adjudicate a new claim. Research cutoff: May 23, 2005.
