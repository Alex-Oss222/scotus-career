## Smith v. Massachusetts — validated packet

**Question and reach.** The issue is the federal effect of the midtrial Rule 25(a) insufficiency ruling and its later retraction before closing arguments. This is direct review, so Price's independent section 2254(d)(1) barrier is inapplicable. Price's completed-acquittal rule and whole-episode inquiry do apply. The state court's characterization is considered as part of the procedural setting; it does not itself resolve the federal acquittal issue.

**Supported episode.** The prosecution's weapon evidence consisted of the victim's description of a pistol or revolver appearing to be a .32 or .38. Barrel length below sixteen inches was an element. The judge granted Smith's insufficiency motion at sidebar; the allowance was endorsed on the motion and entered on the docket. The jury was not informed. The prosecutor earlier said he would request reopening but made no reopening motion before resting. After the sidebar and prosecution rest, the codefendant called a witness and both defendants rested. During the recess before closings the prosecutor supplied Sperrazza, concerning an inference of barrel length from a handgun description. The judge retracted the allowance and sent the count to the existing jury. Smith did not request reopening after retraction. The jury convicted him on all three charges; a later bench proceeding found the firearm repeat-offender element. The firearm term was ten to twelve years, concurrent with the other sentences.

The brief's phrase about the defense proceeding therefore means the codefendant presented one witness and both defendants rested. It does not establish that Smith testified or personally introduced evidence. The Appeals Court's statement that Smith claimed no changed strategy is a characterization of his litigation position, not a factual finding that acquittal could have no strategic effect.

**State-law setting.** Rule 25(a) requires a ruling at the close of the prosecution's evidence and requires a not-guilty finding when the evidence is legally insufficient. It does not expressly address reconsideration. Rule 42 addresses clerical mistakes and errors of oversight or omission. Haskell describes a general common-law power to reconsider while a case remains pending. The Appeals Court treated the absence of a Rule 25 prohibition and the continued jury trial as permitting correction. The source establishes no statute or rule expressly making this category of allowance provisional. Whether the general common-law rule gives this ruling provisional status remains a legal question; it is not assumed away as a missing fact.

**Entering law.** The operative Standards entry is **Completed substantive acquittals and provisional trial remarks**, effective Price, May 19, 2003. Price Part I, joined by all nine, protects a completed substantive insufficiency ruling despite legal error and without an independent signature or jury-announcement prerequisite. Its Part II, joined by all eight Associates, sustained Michigan's provisional characterization as objectively reasonable under the habeas statute because the judge invited more argument in the same episode and continued to reserve the issue. That disposition neither finds every midtrial ruling provisional nor makes the habeas conclusion the direct-review rule. Martin Linen, Smalis, Fong Foo, and Scott supply the substantive distinction. The Sattazahn and Dixon canonical supplements establish their own bounded finality and successive-prosecution rules; neither directly determines whether this trial judge completed the Rule 25 decision.

**Supported paths.** One path treats the unqualified insufficiency allowance, its implementation, and the completed transition to the defense as an actual acquittal. It removes the restored firearm conviction and punishment and bars renewed adjudication of that count. The alternative treats the ongoing trial and general reconsideration law as leaving this non-jury ruling provisional, permitting affirmance without a second prosecution. Its strongest support is the same jury, a correction based on existing evidence rather than newly supplied proof, the absence of a claimed strategy change, and Smith's failure to request reopening. The first path's strongest answer is that actual acquittal finality concerns renewed guilt determination and does not depend on a second jury or proof of tactical prejudice; a later legal correction cannot itself establish that the prior decision remained open. Whether this was an actual acquittal must be decided from the whole episode before either consequence follows.

**Remedial boundary.** A firearm reversal preserves the two independent assault convictions and requires ordinary state treatment of any sentence consequences dependent on the removed firearm conviction. Concurrent terms do not preserve an invalid conviction. No facts support disturbing the independent convictions. A hypothetical different express reconsideration regime is outside the presented state-law setting.

**Readiness.** Ready for independent modeling. The authoritative published account supports the live completion and provisionality arguments. No proposed path relies on a new transcript detail or invented concession.

## Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co. — validated packet

**Question and record.** The granted question concerns the vessel definition, not a separately granted challenge to the section 905(b) capacity holding. The Super Scoop transported its equipment and crew on water; tugs moved it long distances, including to Boston from California through the Panama Canal, and anchors and cables moved it shorter distances. It ordinarily moved thirty to fifty feet every few hours while dredging. Its captain, crew, lights, ballast tanks, and dining area corroborate its operational character. Its temporary stop related to a scow's engine fault; it was not withdrawn from service or rendered incapable of waterborne movement. The dredge's bucket moved Scow No. 4, where Stewart was feeding wires through an open hatch; the scow struck the dredge and the jolt caused his fall.

**Two judgments.** The Jones Act summary judgment used the primary-business and actual-movement approach. For that motion Dutra acknowledged crew membership, ninety-nine percent work aboard the dredge, and contribution to its function. Those acknowledgments must be honored at their procedural scope; a remand may not invent disputes on the conceded motion facts. The later section 905(b) summary judgment rested on employer-capacity rather than vessel-owner negligence, with vessel status conceded. Correcting the Jones Act vessel premise therefore does not itself invalidate the independent capacity judgment.

**Entering law.** The operative Standards entry is **Jones Act seaman status and LHWCA overlap**, with the fleet boundary effective Harbor Tug, May 12, 1997, and the carried Gizoni and Chandris components retaining their stated dates and joins. Gizoni rejects categorical occupational exclusions and preclusion from voluntary unadjudicated compensation. Chandris separately requires contribution and substantial nature-and-duration connection, uses a nonabsolute temporal guide at its five-Justice scope, and rejects categorical temporary-repair exclusions. Harbor Tug separately adjudicates employment connection and the vessel-negligence appeal. Its Part III had all eight Associates' rationale joins; the maritime public-prior supplement identifies the material separate positions precisely. None of these cases makes every floating structure a vessel or decides this primary-purpose restriction.

The relevant enacted setting is 1 U.S.C. §3; then-current 46 U.S.C. App. §688(a); and 33 U.S.C. §§902(3)(G), 902(21), and 905(b). The vessel definition uses water-transport use or capability. Its enactment and the relevant LHWCA structural provisions predate the divergence; no displaced post-divergence enactment predicate appears.

**Supported paths.** The practical-transport path treats this dredge as a vessel and rejects a requirement that transport be its principal business or that it move at the injury instant. Its limiting principle is practical waterborne transportation, with actual operational condition considered, rather than flotation or a merely theoretical possibility of movement. The primary-purpose path treats dredging as construction and temporary movement as incidental; its strongest argument is that broad vessel classification could blur the line between maritime crews and workers on floating workplaces. The contrary answer is that the statute addresses capability while worker connection and the available cause of action perform separate limiting work. A vessel finding does not merge the Jones Act and LHWCA remedies.

**Required remedial discrimination.** The Jones Act branch can be reversed on the vessel ground and returned for the remaining proceedings under the corrected legal premise and the motion's actual concessions. The section 905(b) capacity judgment is independent and lies outside the granted definition question. The model must decide its surviving status expressly, not automatically vacate it because the vessel rule changes. Harbor Tug involved an unresolved appeal from the separate vessel judgment; here the appellate court actually decided that separate appeal, so its mandate cannot be copied without this distinction.

**Readiness.** Ready for modeling at the vessel-question scope. A proposed merits holding resolving owner-versus-employer negligence beyond that scope and the available recitations would require targeted further record review. A limited disposition respecting that judgment requires no invented negligence findings or filings.

## Karaha Bodas — validated recognition packet

**Corrected framing.** The contracts chose Geneva as the arbitral site and UNCITRAL rules; the award states Geneva as its place. The hearing physically occurred in Paris. They generally selected Indonesian governing law and expressly referred to Indonesian provisions, but did not expressly select a national procedural law for the arbitration. The neutral brief's statement that Swiss procedural law governed is therefore a proposed legal characterization, not a fact that excludes Pertamina's argument. Pertamina claims the actual governing-law clause, particular waivers, and other Indonesian procedural references together select Indonesian procedural law. It does not rely only on the proposition that substantive law automatically creates annulment authority.

**Exact contract materials.** `NEUTRAL_TEXTS_B.md` mechanically preserves the opinion's quoted provisions: UNCITRAL arbitration, arbitrator appointment arrangements, Geneva site, English language, finality and enforcement, the general Indonesian-law clause, waiver of the six-month arbitrator-appointment and completion limits in Articles 650.2 and 620.1, the Section 631 authorization concerning strict rules of law and the contract's spirit and intent, and the Section 641 appeal provision with the specified appellate waivers. The Section 631 reference is affirmative decisional authority, not simply another waiver. The quoted passages contain source ellipses and brackets and are not represented as a recovered complete contract. Their content suffices for the actual clause-selection issue; no omitted clause is invented.

**Procedure and relief.** The December 2000 award preceded the December 2001 United States recognition judgment. The Swiss challenge was dismissed for untimely payment of costs, with reconsideration denied; it was not a Swiss merits decision approving the award. The Indonesian action began in March 2002, and annulment occurred August 27, 2002. The recognition appeal included both recognition summary judgment and denial of Rule 60(b) relief. Its other Convention defenses were addressed below. The granted review concerns the Article V(1)(e) annulment question; it does not silently reopen every previously decided defense. The earlier anti-suit review is a different judgment.

**Entering law and operative text.** The Standards entry **Foreign anti-suit relief protecting domestic award enforcement**, effective March 9, 2004, requires a concrete inadequately addressable threat, lawful authority, ordinary equity, and fitting scope before extraordinary restraint. Kennedy's unanimous Karaha opinion expressly left this recognition allocation open. Its Article VI description is permissive: adjournment depends on the enforcing court considering it proper, while suitable security additionally requires the enforcing party's application.

The mechanically copied Convention Article V(1) places the subsection (e) request-and-proof requirement on the resisting party. Subsection (e) identifies the country in which, or under whose law, the award was made, and a competent authority's set-aside or suspension. Article VI concerns an application to that qualified authority; it does not require adjournment. Section 207 requires confirmation absent a Convention refusal or deferral ground. These enactments predate the divergence. No new statute is admitted here.

**Supported paths.** The arbitral-procedure construction treats the second country description as the national procedural law governing the arbitration, distinct from the law resolving contractual rights. On this reading the actual clauses must then be construed: the Geneva seat and UNCITRAL arrangements support Swiss supervision, while the Indonesian references can govern discrete substantive or contractual matters without selecting the entire Indonesian supervisory regime. Its strongest counter is that the broad Indonesian governing-law clause, deliberate procedural waivers, and affirmative Section 631 incorporation together may show a wider procedural choice; reference to particular exclusions can imply that other Indonesian rules remain applicable.

The competing construction either regards substantive-law choice itself as enough or finds an actual Indonesian procedural selection from the combined clauses. Its strongest objection is that it turns ordinary governing-law and limited incorporated provisions into a second national annulment regime without an agreement selecting that regime; the treaty distinguishes annulment authority from ordinary recognition forums. A limited remand would be justified only by a genuinely unresolved material contract or foreign-law premise, not merely by the existence of the parties' interpretive disagreement. No separate unknown clause or supported conflicting legal-seat fact has been identified.

**Remedial boundary.** Rejection of the Indonesian annulment defense on the granted question leaves the recognition judgment operative and the denial of reopening intact at that scope. Acceptance would require a further lawful disposition addressing Article V's operation, not an automatic assumption of mandatory refusal. The force of a qualifying primary-country annulment is not decided merely by rejecting an order from a country that fails the treaty criterion. Recognition is distinct from particular-asset execution and the already-vacated anti-suit restraint.

**Readiness.** Ready for independent modeling with the corrected procedural-selection framing and exact bounded clause extracts. The recorded source limits do not establish litigant default or negate a preserved argument. No remaining material factual assumption is adopted.
