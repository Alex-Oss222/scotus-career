## Propositions and exact scope

**J1 — Actual governmental message.** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer join this rule and its application: when government determines the promotional policy and actually selects, supervises and finally approves the challenged messages, those messages are governmental for the presented compelled-subsidy objection. Here official participation and final control amount to actual direction, rather than dormant regulatory review of an independent private speaker. Producer drafting and the producers' otherwise independent business decisions do not defeat that classification. Nominal official status alone would be insufficient under Keller; Lebron's governmental-actor classification does not automatically attribute every message to government.

**J2 — Funding that governmental message.** The same four join the conclusion that the First Amendment does not require a lawful targeted assessment financing the actual governmental message to be replaced with general-revenue taxation merely because assessed producers disagree. No universal immunity for government action follows. Statutory authority, religious establishment, unlawful discrimination, compelled personal expression and other independently applicable limits retain their own rules and predicates. J1–J2 do not sustain selected private advertising merely because it benefits the industry, has majority support or leaves dissenters free to counteradvertise.

**J3 — Limited facial-attribution ruling and reservation.** The same four join the distinct holding that the statute does not require a named dissenter personally to endorse the advertising, and a generic producer-funding identification does not establish that every payer has been made a personal endorser. The presented facial attribution objection therefore supplies no independent ground for the reviewed statutory bar. A supported claim that a particular person's or organization's unwanted message was involuntarily attributed to it remains a separate question on its own facts and procedural presentation. Governmental identity, source of money and individual endorsement are distinct inquiries. No finding of actual individual attribution or finding that such a claim can never succeed is made.

All remaining Associates accept the absence of an established personal-endorsement finding. That common record limit does not make them joins to J1–J2. Ginsburg does not need to adopt an additional facial holding beyond preserving the separate attribution question under her own remand. Stevens, Kennedy and Souter's sufficient objection is institutional accountability for compelled support, not a claim that each individual has been proved an apparent endorser.

**J4 — Accountability limitation and current private-support rule.** Stevens, Kennedy and Souter accept actual executive control but reject its sufficiency for this campaign presented as the industry's message. To claim the government-speech justification for these targeted compulsory payments, government must take publicly identifiable responsibility for its expression. In this setting that responsibility cannot be supplied by hidden official selection behind producer presentation. Their rule does not categorically forbid private drafting or demand general taxation; it requires accountable governmental authorship before the exemption applies. They do not decide that public identification would answer every possible objection to a discrete group's compelled financing of even openly governmental speech.

Absent that justification, current United Foods governs the advertising-centered association of otherwise independent producers. Abood and Keller require an independently sufficient common function before asking whether financed expression is germane to it; advertising cannot justify its own compulsory association. Glickman remains valid for its qualifying collective program, and Southworth confirms that sufficient common functions need not invariably involve price controls, output controls or an antitrust exemption. The research allocation does not by itself establish that this advertising is ancillary to another sufficient function. The presented commercial defense is considered and rejected as an independent cure under United Foods, even assuming economic advancement and sensible design. This is a legal answer, not a finding that beef promotion failed empirically.

Ginsburg withholds the government-message classification but does not thereby join the three Justices' entire affirmative accountability rule or their conclusion that United Foods should dispose of the case. Her separate commercial proposal supplies a distinct ground and remedy.

**J5 — Ginsburg's proposed independent commercial justification.** Ginsburg would allow government to justify this commercial subsidy by establishing a substantial governmental interest, direct advancement of that interest and reasonable fit, even where the selected private promotion lacks the independent collective function required by United Foods. Her proposal expressly modifies United Foods to that extent; it is not an exception already adopted by that decision and does not claim that economic regulation lies outside the First Amendment. Her own current United Foods dissent recognizes the compelled-expression burden and requires this justification. The beef industry's welfare identifies the asserted interest, while the actual governmental role may bear on the inquiry; neither identifies all predicates as satisfied.

Under J5, the appellate court's return to the separate germaneness defect is not the complete commercial inquiry Ginsburg would require. Its legal rejection does not establish empirical failure under her test. The published record likewise does not establish affirmative empirical satisfaction. She would return genuinely unresolved application to the existing bench-trial record and such supplementation as ordinary procedure actually requires. The Government bears the proposed test's burden. No particular study, quantitative demand coefficient, guaranteed producer profit, least restrictive conceivable alternative, automatic new trial or proof of voluntary-campaign failure is prescribed.

O'Connor and Breyer retain their refreshed conditional willingness to propose this doctrinal route if the sufficient governmental-message ground is unavailable. They do not adopt it as an independently sufficient current application, and no commercial merits holding or present commercial-remand vote is attributed to them when J1–J3 supplies their selected disposition. Scalia and Thomas do not support this independent cure; the three J4 supporters reject it under the current rule. J5 is Ginsburg's individual proposal, not a controlling alteration of United Foods established by this Associate handoff.

## Exact dispositions and remedies in both dockets

**G — Government-message disposition: O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer.** Vacate the reviewed First Amendment judgment and remand. Remove the declaration that the Act and Order are unconstitutional on this compulsory-financing ground and the whole-checkoff collection injunction dependent on it. J1–J3 removes that predicate for both advertising and nonadvertising collection restrictions. No severability ruling is necessary: these Justices neither approve the lower nonseverability conclusion nor determine severability against every hypothetical future defect.

The judgment does not award a collection amount, order a refund or approve every expenditure. Separately pending producer-communications, referendum, statutory-power, administration and accounting claims retain their actual status, including the lawful effect of independently supported relief. No former appellate stay is assumed presently operative or automatically expired.

**P — Accountability disposition: Stevens, Kennedy and Souter.** Affirm the constitutional judgment insofar as it protects against compulsory financing of the challenged private generic advertising; vacate the final determination that every remaining statutory function necessarily falls and the corresponding categorical nonseverability declaration; remand for actual separability and remedial scope. This is affirmance in part, vacatur in part and remand, not full affirmance of the actual decree.

The prospective restraint on integrated checkoff collection remains during that remedial remand unless and until the lower court enters a conforming order permitting a constitutionally lawful, legally separable operation. That interim continuation protects against the adjudicated unconstitutional financing; it is not a final ruling that research, consumer information, industry information or every other nonadvertising activity is unconstitutional. The lower court must determine whether the enacted scheme permits a lawful remainder to function and can separate the offending financing without rewriting the statutory assessment or program. Advertising's centrality and deletion of the former severability clause remain material, but neither is an affirmative legislative sentence settling every remaining function's survival.

If lawful separation is possible, the decree must allow that operation while fully protecting against unconstitutional compulsory promotion. If it is not, the whole-collection restraint may continue on that determination. The research percentage alone supplies no authority to collect a fractional rate. No replacement funding arrangement, earmark, refund or new rate is prescribed. Protection addresses the common adjudicated operation, not only the objectors' names; independently pending claims remain separate.

**C — Ginsburg's commercial disposition.** Vacate the reviewed constitutional judgment and its dependent permanent whole-checkoff injunction, and remand for application of J5 to the actual record. This shares G's formal vacatur and remand but does not join its conclusion that the governmental-message classification defeats the subsidy challenge or its instruction that this constitutional question has been resolved in the Government's favor. C does not declare the beef program constitutionally valid. It returns the unresolved justification under her expressly proposed rule; a failure to establish that justification would require constitutional relief fitted to the actual statute and violation.

Vacatur of the permanent decree does not itself decide a request for properly supported interim protection during C's remand. Such relief would require an actual application of ordinary equitable standards; this commitment neither orders an automatic continuing injunction nor forbids lawful interim protection. It creates no collection award, automatic new trial, refund entitlement or predetermined research fraction. Separately pending claims and independent relief remain unaffected. Because C supplies no final finding of constitutional failure, it does not finally determine severability either; if that issue becomes necessary, the lower court must assess actual statutory operation rather than treating deletion of the former clause as conclusive affirmative text.

The three remedies must not be combined into a fictitious common mandate. G and C share the formal act of vacatur and remand but prescribe different unresolved work. P preserves an adjudicated advertising defect and its effective protection while reconsidering statutory survival. The institutional mandate and controlling propositions remain for actual assembly from supported joins.

## Historical departures and writing functions

Ginsburg's refreshed provisional governmental-classification join is corrected because the historical source supplies an express withheld join on materially unchanged message-presentation facts. This correction does not mechanically import her historical economic-regulation rationale: the current prior decisions and her own published dissent supply a different premise for that alternative. Her C disposition is supported by the fresh independent commitment's explicit conditional commercial route, rather than an invented new preference.

**Historical departure:** Ginsburg retains historical judgment-only vacatur and remand and her refusal to classify this producer-presented campaign as government speech, but does not declare the assessment permissible merely as economic regulation. Her own current United Foods dissent recognizes the expression burden and requires substantial interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit, while the current majority expressly makes private-support insufficiency independently decisive. She therefore proposes modifying that rule and returns genuinely unresolved beef-specific application instead of treating historical economic approval as an established present factual conclusion.

**Historical departure:** Stevens, Kennedy and Souter retain their historical accountability ground and protection against compelled private advertising, but replace full affirmance of the whole-program decree with P's partial affirmance and remedial vacatur. Each joined this Court's current United Foods Part III, which expressly leaves independently lawful nonadvertising operations and actual severability to their own predicates. The actual decree's categorical inference from the deleted savings clause therefore requires statutory-survival review, with effective collection protection retained pending that inquiry; their votes do not presume that a lawful remainder ultimately exists.

**Historical departure:** Breyer retains his historical complete government-speech join, but his alternative justification follows his own current United Foods dissent's recognition of a compelled-expression burden and commercial scrutiny, rather than a general exclusion of economic regulation from the First Amendment. The altered prior writing changes the content of that alternative, not his J1–J3 vote or the G remedy. O'Connor's preserved commercial alternative rests on her join in the same current dissent and supplies no additional present holding.

Scalia, O'Connor and Thomas retain their historical government-control dispositions on materially corresponding facts; the newly clarified whole-decree scope sharpens G's mandate without changing their merits premise. Thomas's historical separate contains an affirmative attribution proposition beyond the common opinion's reservation: when a message is objectively and involuntarily attributed to an identified individual or organization, actual government control does not answer that distinct injury. That position must be preserved in a short separate function unless a common writing itself adopts it at the same scope. The absence of its factual predicate here remains common; no amendment or successful claim is guaranteed.

Stevens, Kennedy and Souter can share an opposing writing adopting J4 and P. Kennedy's reservation about even openly governmental targeted financing must be explicit; it may be included in that shared writing without a redundant separate. Ginsburg requires a judgment-only explanation of C, J5, her withheld government-classification join and the actual rule she proposes to change. Breyer need not repeat his commercial dissent merely because its historical counterpart did so, provided his current alternative is not misstated as an economic immunity. No historical authorship is assigned to any current opinion.
