## 30. Johnson v. California, No. 03-636

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Johnson v. California, No. 03-636
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-636; 543 U.S. 499 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of summary judgment in a §1983 action challenging a prison reception-center housing policy.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-02-23; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether California’s express racial classification in reception-center double-cell assignments is governed by the constitutional standard for governmental racial classifications or by general deference to prison regulations.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed judgment for prison officials under Turner v. Safley’s deferential penological standard; the district court had relied on qualified immunity.
* **Relief Requested:** Johnson seeks reversal, adjudication of the policy under the governing equality standard, damages from former directors for their own conduct and prospective relief against the responsible official. California seeks affirmance based on security and individual immunity.
* **Material Record:** California generally assigns reception-center cellmates by race for up to sixty days on entry and transfer while assessing permanent placement. Johnson experienced the practice on initial imprisonment and subsequent transfers. Officials cite racial gangs and violence; other prison areas are integrated. The policy’s near-categorical operation is acknowledged, but whether particular periods and assignments require separation is not established merely by those general descriptions. The record distinguishes prospective policy correction from personal damages for earlier conduct.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The policy personally classified Johnson. The case concerns reception housing, not an uninjured observer’s objection. Current standing and official substitution must support prospective relief; damages require personal responsibility and conduct-date immunity analysis. The other Johnson v. California matter on the list concerns jury selection and is distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** Complaint filed February 24, 1995; appellate judgment reported in 2003; argument November 2, 2004; event February 23, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964, concerns peremptory strikes. Neither shared caption nor race-related subject merges the records or remedies.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Brosseau v. Haugen could affect the individual-immunity framework if an effective decision changes it before this event. No such decision is identified; existing Harlow and Anderson law remains operative, and the race-classification merits are separate.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment cannot resolve material security facts against the nonmovant. Under the Saucier v. Katz, no compulsory merits-first sequence is imposed; immunity remains independent of prospective relief. Any prison injunction must satisfy 18 U.S.C. §3626.

##### Issue 1: The governing racial-classification standard

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña establishes one common substantial-justification standard for federal, state and local racial classifications, displacing categorical strict-scrutiny formulation under governing law. Brown v. City of Oneonta confirms that deliberate racial selection requires that justification even without hostility. General prison deference does not itself decide whether that specific equality standard is displaced.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government must establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, and justification for coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives that comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes and allocation for their own sake are impermissible.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; 42 U.S.C. §1983; 18 U.S.C. §3626 for prospective prison relief.
* **Material Other Authority:** Turner v. Safley supplies its surviving prison-regulation framework; Lee v. Washington rejects racial segregation as an ordinary incident of incarceration while recognizing genuine security circumstances. Their scopes must be reconciled with the equality rule, not with historical Adarand language.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The common rule governs deliberate classifications across public functions. Custodial danger affects the evidence, alternatives and practical fit; it is not an exemption from equality.
* **Open Point:** How the common standard applies to blanket reception separation and what present evidence supports each part of the policy, including repeated transfers.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Johnson argues that the State classifies him by race and must justify that treatment rather than invoke ordinary prison discretion. California argues that reception officials lack information, gangs are organized racially and short-term separation prevents violence. The court below accepted general penological reasonableness. The strongest legitimate limit on Johnson’s claim is that actual threats and feasibility can justify fitted precautions; the strongest limit on California’s claim is that security expertise does not validate racial assumptions without an operational fit. The United States, as amicus, urges strict scrutiny and a finding that the broad reception policy fails even that fact-sensitive inquiry, emphasizing known nonviolent transferees, the absence of specific reception-cell violence and the State’s contrary use of integration elsewhere. It also argues that the policy fails Turner. Those operational objections bear on the common substantial-justification inquiry, but the brief’s historical strict-scrutiny premise is advocacy rather than entering law.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacatur permits application of the correct equality standard with distinct adjudication of damages and prospective relief. A fully supported justification could sustain particular assignments or durations. Failure of justification could support policy-wide correction at the proved scope, while conduct-date immunity could still defeat individual damages. Dismissal of a prospective branch requires an actual standing or proper-defendant defect, not merely the availability of damages.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The common-standard path establishes no categorical immunity for prisons and no categorical invalidity of every emergency precaution. A general-deference path would require identifying and defending a limitation of the existing racial rule. Relief must distinguish a proved common policy violation from individual historical responsibility.
* **Material Uncertainty:** No final finding establishes that all applications are necessary or that all former officials lacked fair warning. Historical nonparticipation does not determine the bench.

## 31. Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare
* **Citation or Docket:** 32 Cal. 4th 910; California Supreme Court No. S109306.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the state supreme court’s federal-preemption ruling.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-02-23; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether federal drug law preempts California’s Proposition 65 warning for nicotine-replacement products when the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically prohibited that warning, notwithstanding Congress’s preservation of preexisting state warning requirements? Whether the FDA may prohibit an otherwise truthful state-required warning because it concludes that the warning would mislead consumers about comparative health risks?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The California Supreme Court held that the savings clause defeats the express uniformity preemption provision but does not displace an independently demonstrated actual conflict. It found the requested warning incompatible with the FDA’s specific labeling decisions and reversed the intermediate appellate judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Dowhal seeks enforcement of the state warning requirement and associated statutory relief. The manufacturers and retailers seek judgment rejecting that claim on federal-preemption grounds.
* **Material Record:** The products deliver nicotine to assist smoking cessation. California listed nicotine as a reproductive toxicant. FDA-approved labeling advises pregnant users to consult a health professional, explains smoking’s danger and addresses the comparative risks and uncertainty of replacement therapy. FDA repeatedly considered and rejected the additional Proposition 65 warning, concerned that it could discourage cessation or imply a false risk comparison. This is a dispute over the legal effect of those specific actions, not an independent judicial finding that the products are safe during pregnancy.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The judgment turns on federal preemption of an identified state duty. The claimant’s state statutory enforcement standing and remaining remedies must not be confused with a federal right to enforce the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FDCA). No general damages cause under federal labeling law is asserted.
* **Relevant Dates:** California Supreme Court decision April 15, 2004; assigned event February 23, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC is scheduled later and concerns pesticide-labeling preemption under a different statute; it supplies no entering rule here.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Identify the exact state duty and legally operative federal action before finding conflict. Review the scope of agency authority independently and assess authorized technical judgments on their actual administrative record.

##### Issue 1: Saved state requirements and actual conflict

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. preserves claims under savings language while permitting independently demonstrated conflict with an operative federal command or supported regulatory objective. Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick requires an operative federal basis, not regulatory absence. Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr preserves compatible state duties; approval alone supplies no general immunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the express clause and savings clause together; then identify the particular federal command, its authority and the precise incompatible state duty. General regulatory objectives or approval alone do not establish conflict.
* **Other Governing Law:** 21 U.S.C. §§352(a), 355 and 379r, especially §379r(d)(2); California Health and Safety Code §25249.6. The 1997 provision preserves qualifying preexisting initiative requirements from that section’s express preemption, not expressly from all valid federal law.
* **Material Other Authority:** The contemporaneous FDA approval conditions and specific warning determinations supply the possible conflict. Later federal labeling amendments are not backdated.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law permits actual-conflict analysis despite savings language while demanding an operative federal source. It does not itself decide the FDA’s authority or the reach of the particular prohibition.
* **Open Point:** Whether the retained California duty requires the warning the FDA validly forbade and whether a genuinely compatible form of compliance remains.

##### Issue 2: FDA authority over literally true but misleading labeling

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The governing decisions preserve judicial responsibility for statutory delegation and require agency action to rest on its authorized domain and actual record. Federal preemption cannot enlarge agency power; the statutory misbranding rule must authorize the prohibition before supremacy attaches.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish literal accuracy from the message conveyed in context. Identify statutory labeling authority, an authoritative action, its evidentiary basis and its treatment of material contrary information. Do not supply a litigation rationale for the agency.
* **Other Governing Law:** 21 U.S.C. §§321(n), 352(a) and 355 authorize evaluation of misleading labeling in context and approval of new-drug labeling.
* **Material Other Authority:** The agency’s comparative-risk explanation is a technical judgment relevant to misleadingness. It is not a power to suppress any truthful statement simply because officials prefer another public-health message.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory term misleading can reach context and omission, not only statements false in isolation. The authority remains product- and labeling-specific.
* **Open Point:** Whether the challenged comparative-risk determination rests on the actual statutory criteria and administrative basis.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Dowhal relies on Congress’s deliberate preservation of Proposition 65 and the warning’s factual accuracy. The companies rely on FDA’s specific rejection and the impossibility of obeying both commands. The State’s broad preservation argument must account for the clause’s reference to this section; the companies must account for the distinction between approval and prohibition and for compatible compliance alternatives. FDA’s concern was a misleading comparison with smoking, not an assertion that nicotine lacks reproductive risk. No unverified oral concession or broader FDA policy is imported.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A valid and actual conflict supports judgment for defendants on the identified warning claim. If FDA lacked authority, issued only a nonbinding preference or left a compatible compliance route, the preemption judgment would require reversal or a targeted remand. A broader express-preemption holding would disregard the initiative savings provision.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case can establish that saved state requirements remain subject to demonstrated conflict with valid particular federal commands, and that misleadingness is contextual. It cannot resolve all state product duties or immunize all FDA-approved products.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The legal force and scope of each FDA communication must be traced to the contemporaneous approval framework. The historical state opinion supplies that account; no new safety conclusion or later labeling rule is assumed.

[California Supreme Court opinion and contemporaneous FDA record](https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/dowhal-v-smithkline-beecham-33399/); [initiative savings provision](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/379r); [misbranding provision](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/352).

## 32. Roper v. Simmons

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Roper v. Simmons
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-633; 543 U.S. 551 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of a state postconviction judgment replacing a death sentence with life imprisonment without release eligibility.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-01; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit executing an offender who was younger than eighteen when the offense was committed, and whether Stanford v. Kentucky should retain authority at that scope.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Missouri Supreme Court concluded that developments since Stanford v. Kentucky established a constitutional bar to executing offenders under eighteen at the crime and substituted life without release eligibility.
* **Relief Requested:** Missouri seeks reinstatement of death eligibility and reversal of the state postconviction judgment. Simmons seeks preservation of the noncapital judgment.
* **Material Record:** Simmons was seventeen when he planned and participated in Shirley Crook’s murder and eighteen when tried. The record contains serious evidence of planning and cruelty as well as age-related mitigation. The question is categorical eligibility, not innocence or whether this offense was aggravated. State legislation, actual sentencing practice and executions provide the domestic evidence concerning juvenile punishment; the legal inference from that evidence is contested.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The state judgment rests on federal constitutional law and is reviewable under §1257. This is state postconviction review, not a federal §2254 petition; the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA)’s gateway is not imported. A lower court’s duty to follow controlling precedent and this Court’s power to reconsider it are distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** Offense committed when Simmons was seventeen; state postconviction judgment in 2003; argument October 13, 2004; event March 1, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the question presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Categorical proportionality requires examination of the protected class, objective domestic evidence and culpability in relation to legitimate punishment purposes. The judgment may preserve conviction while changing only the permissible sentence.

##### Issue 1: Capital punishment for conduct committed before eighteen

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Atkins v. Virginia bars execution of persons with established intellectual disability and uses objective domestic evidence, culpability and legitimate punishment purposes. It does not decide youth. Stanford v. Kentucky remains the prior age-specific rule unless this Court changes it. Thompson v. Oklahoma retains its narrower juvenile scope.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the precise class, evaluate enactments and actual practice, and determine whether the punishment is disproportionate in relation to culpability and legitimate penal purposes. Individual mitigation is distinct from a categorical restriction when a class cannot reliably be selected for death.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; the applicable Missouri murder and sentencing provisions. International instruments may inform comparison but cannot independently change the United States constitutional rule.
* **Material Other Authority:** Trop v. Dulles supplies evolving standards; Coker v. Georgia and Enmund v. Florida illustrate categorical proportionality. The source of the asserted right remains the Constitution, not foreign majorities.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Atkins v. Virginia establishes that individualized sentencing does not foreclose a supported categorical limitation. It leaves juvenile eligibility unresolved. Stanford v. Kentucky is not silently displaced by analogy.
* **Open Point:** Whether the domestic development and class-based culpability evidence justify replacing Stanford v. Kentucky with an under-eighteen rule.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Missouri argues that the relevant states retaining capital punishment have not produced a sufficiently clear consensus, that legislatures and sentencing juries can evaluate youth and that the crime demonstrates the need for individualized judgment. Simmons argues that enactments, actual rarity and the common developmental characteristics of youth make death categorically disproportionate. The state court relied on developments after Stanford v. Kentucky. A valid comparison must distinguish abolition of death generally, age-specific prohibitions and actual practice rather than manipulate the denominator. Neither international agreement alone nor the brutality of one crime answers the constitutional category.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance can rest on an expressly announced substantive age rule preserving the noncapital judgment. Retaining Stanford v. Kentucky would require reversal of the federal constitutional basis, with state sentencing consequences determined on remand. A narrower individualized-mitigation ruling would not answer the categorical question unless supported by a preserved independent defect; none is manufactured.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The presented record supports a binary age-at-offense capital-eligibility rule or retention of individualized eligibility for sixteen- and seventeen-year-old offenders. It does not support adjudication of life-without-parole, age twenty-one or the overall death penalty.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The state-law consequences of reversal should not be presumed to permit execution without further lawful process. Exact contemporary counts must be tied to offense and decision dates rather than current law.

#### E. SOURCES

[Judicial record, parties’ theories and historical comparative evidence](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/543/551/); [official argument calendar](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcript/2004).

## 33. Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma v. Leavitt

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Cherokee Nation of Oklahoma v. Leavitt
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 02-1472 and 03-853, the consolidated contract-support disputes; 543 U.S. 631 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of opposite appellate judgments concerning payment of tribal self-determination contract support costs.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-01; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the United States is contractually obligated to pay promised tribal contract support costs when unrestricted appropriations were legally sufficient for the individual contracts but the agency’s allocation was insufficient to pay all program demands.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Tenth Circuit rejected the tribes’ contract claims in No. 02-1472. The Federal Circuit sustained the contract appeals board’s payment ruling in No. 03-853. The two judgments present different remedial postures within the listed consolidated matter.
* **Relief Requested:** The tribes seek payment of the promised support costs through the Contract Disputes Act. The United States seeks relief from payment based on statutory availability language, aggregate appropriation limits and later appropriations provisions.
* **Material Record:** The Cherokee Nation and Shoshone-Paiute Tribes undertook health services normally supplied by the Indian Health Service under self-determination contracts for fiscal years 1994 through 1997. Annual funding agreements promised support costs. Congress supplied lump-sum appropriations, while the agency allocated resources among competing demands. The relevant distinction is between a legally unavailable appropriation and an administrative decision not to devote otherwise available funds to a contractual promise. Contract amounts, conditions and recoverable loss remain independently ascertainable.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The contracts and Contract Disputes Act supply the asserted payment channel. Tribal status does not create a freestanding damages promise. The Government’s authority to enter the undertakings and the appropriations applicable to each fiscal year must be identified.
* **Relevant Dates:** Contracts concern fiscal years 1994 through 1997; argument November 9, 2004; event March 1, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The cross-caption in No. 03-853 is part of the consolidated historical matter, not an additional separately briefed case. City of Sherrill v. Oneida Indian Nation of New York concerns taxation, not these contract promises.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the statutory and contractual obligations independently. Distinguish liability from computation and the source from which a judgment is paid; the Court does not appropriate money or reorder every agency program.

##### Issue 1: Contractual liability and appropriations availability

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Hercules, Inc. v. United States requires an actual authorized undertaking rather than a promise implied only from fairness. United States v. Winstar Corp. enforces actual financial undertakings and allocated risk while preserving Congress’s legislative authority and separate proof of loss.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the authorized contract, its conditions, the legally available appropriation and any actual statutory limitation. Agency allocation choices do not automatically rewrite a binding promise. Liability, causation and amount remain distinct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act, then 25 U.S.C. §§450f and 450j-1; Contract Disputes Act; the fiscal-year appropriations; the later appropriations language identified as §314. Historical section numbering controls rather than later recodification.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lincoln v. Vigil permits allocation of a lump-sum appropriation within lawful agency discretion; that administrative principle does not itself decide liability for an actual contract. Committee-report expectations do not become enacted caps.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering contract rules govern real undertakings and preserve valid congressional limits. They do not yet determine whether these availability clauses excuse an agency’s aggregate underallocation.
* **Open Point:** Whether sufficient unrestricted appropriations for the contracts satisfy the availability condition despite competing program demands, and whether the later provision extinguishes accrued contract liability.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The tribes argue that Congress authorized binding contracts, that sufficient lump-sum funds were available for their promises and that ordinary contract law requires payment despite administrative overcommitment. The United States argues that availability language and the need to preserve services to other tribes qualify each promise, and invokes later appropriations restrictions. The opposite lower-court judgments reflect whether aggregate program shortfalls become a defense to individual contractual liability. The Government’s position is its own party position; no additional independent Solicitor General theory is invented. Liability does not establish an unproved cost amount.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Enforcement of the promises supports reversal of the Tenth Circuit and affirmance of the Federal Circuit, with any remaining amount and implementation questions remanded. A valid enacted cap or actual contract condition could support the Government as to a particular undertaking. The Court can decide the statutory and contract grounds without a freestanding constitutional entitlement to appropriations.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule can govern actual contracts backed by available lump-sum appropriations while retaining genuine legislative caps and conditions. It need not decide an agency’s power to make promises beyond its statutory authority or general tribal trust damages.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The record must preserve differences between the two appellate proceedings and the relevant fiscal-year texts. No assumption is made that every later support-cost contract has identical funding language.

#### E. SOURCES

[Judicial opinion, contracts and fiscal-year statutory account](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/543/631/); [official OT2004 journal](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/journal/jnl04.pdf).

## 34. Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 365 F.3d 385; historical petition No. 04-190.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of appellate preclusion rulings remanding a federal quiet-title dispute.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-01; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether a final judgment protecting one mineral servitude from prescription bars the United States from contesting distinct servitudes acquired through related land transactions? Whether intervening changes in federal choice-of-law principles permit renewed litigation of an issue otherwise subject to collateral estoppel?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The district court treated the earlier Nebo Oil judgment as preclusive. The Fifth Circuit reversed and remanded because the later action involved distinct servitudes and the relevant federal choice-of-law issue had not actually been litigated; it also discussed intervening legal change.
* **Relief Requested:** Petro-Hunt seeks restoration of preclusion and recognition of its claimed continuing mineral rights. The United States seeks adjudication of prescription and title for each disputed servitude rather than extension of the earlier judgment.
* **Material Record:** The earlier litigation adjudicated a servitude associated with approximately 800 acres. The new quiet-title action concerns numerous separate servitudes on roughly 180,000 acres within the Kisatchie National Forest, derived from multiple conveyances. Louisiana ordinarily extinguishes mineral servitudes by prescription after nonuse; the parties dispute the effect of the 1940 state legislation and federal acquisition law. Shared historical ownership and related instruments do not themselves establish identity of the property rights actually adjudicated.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1254 permits review of the federal appellate judgment despite further proceedings on remand. The Quiet Title Act’s waiver, limitations and the actual federal interest remain separate from preclusion. The interlocutory preclusion ruling does not determine title to every parcel.
* **Relevant Dates:** Opinion March 31, 2004; revised judgment May 28; rehearing denied June 1; historical petition filed August 5, 2004. Assigned event March 1, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the question presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Identify the claim, property right and issue actually decided before applying preclusion. Federal common law governs a federal-question judgment; Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.’s ordinary incorporation rule for diversity judgments is not automatically controlling.

##### Issue 1: Identity of adjudicated claims and issues

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. protects the completed adjudication of a private federal case from legislative reopening; it does not expand that judgment to unadjudicated property. Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. requires attention to the source and actual effect of preclusion. New Hampshire v. Maine keeps judicial estoppel distinct from claim and issue preclusion.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Claim preclusion requires the same claim or transaction as defined by the governing law; issue preclusion requires an issue actually litigated, necessarily determined and otherwise eligible for preclusion. Similar legal arguments or common ownership do not replace those predicates.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2409a; applicable federal land-acquisition law; Louisiana mineral-servitude and prescription provisions as they operated for these instruments.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Nebo Oil Co. supplies the actual prior judgment, not a universal substantive immunity from prescription. Commissioner v. Sunnen preserves the distinction between an adjudicated obligation and a general legal issue applied to different transactions.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law protects the prior judgment at its true scope. It neither authorizes reopening the adjudicated servitude nor binds the United States on every later property dispute involving a successor.
* **Open Point:** Whether the deeds and prior record establish actual claim or issue identity for any of the now-disputed servitudes.

##### Issue 2: Intervening law and an otherwise preclusive issue

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Existing finality law distinguishes final-judgment integrity from the law governing later distinct transactions. They supply no blanket power to reopen completed title judgments merely because legal reasoning later changes.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First establish actual issue preclusion. Only then examine a recognized exception for a changed governing legal context; distinguish prospective legal application from vacatur of the original final judgment.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal common-law preclusion; the federal land-acquisition and choice-of-law rules applicable to the separate servitudes.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Little Lake Misere Land Co. is the asserted intervening choice-of-law authority. The parties dispute both its materiality and whether the earlier issue was actually litigated.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A prior judgment’s binding disposition and the precedential value of its general legal reasoning are different. Neither can be conflated with the other.
* **Open Point:** The broader changed-law exception would arise only if the predicate issue identity and necessary adjudication were established; those predicates are disputed and independently dispositive here.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Petro-Hunt argues that the federal acquisition transactions formed one connected arrangement and that the Government already litigated the legal premise of imprescriptibility against its predecessor. The United States answers that distinct mineral servitudes are separate property rights, that the critical choice-of-law issue was not litigated in Nebo Oil and, alternatively, that Little Lake Misere changed the governing legal context. The Government’s filed opposition provides a verified account of its positions and procedural dates; its factual assertions are not judicial findings merely because the Solicitor General signed the brief. The independent nonidentity ground must be addressed before any broad legal-change theory.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance on nonidentity and absence of actual litigation permits the title action to proceed without disturbing Nebo Oil’s own judgment. Proof of identity could support preclusion for a specifically identified issue or parcel. Only that latter path would require decision of an otherwise material changed-law exception. Neither path automatically establishes prescription or awards ownership of every servitude.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The narrow preclusion rule governs actual separate property rights while preserving the prior adjudication. A broad rule reopening final judgments whenever legal doctrine changes is not necessary and is unsupported by the posture.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The complete chain of deeds and use history must be proved on remand. The Government’s brief supplies arguments and dates, not an independent merits judgment. The exact scope of prior actual litigation is legally material.

[United States’ filed opposition, procedural chronology and quoted record](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/petro-hunt-v-united-states-opposition); [Quiet Title Act](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/2409a).

## 35. Tenet v. Doe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Tenet v. Doe
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1395; 544 U.S. 1 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the refusal to dismiss claims arising from alleged covert-service promises.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-02; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether former covert agents may maintain estoppel and due-process claims whose success depends on establishing an alleged secret espionage agreement with the United States, notwithstanding Totten v. United States.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit permitted portions of the action to continue, treating the state-secrets evidentiary privilege and later protective proceedings as the means to address confidentiality.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks dismissal of the claims under Totten v. United States. The Does seek enforcement of promised lifetime financial and personal support, or procedures for adjudicating that asserted entitlement.
* **Material Record:** The Does allege that the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) induced them to remain in a foreign country and conduct espionage before defecting, promising continuing support in return. They assert that support was later reduced or stopped. These allegations are the premise of the motion, not adjudicated confirmation of an intelligence relationship. The claims described depend on the alleged secret agreement; a separate public statute conferring an independent entitlement is not established.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The asserted bar concerns judicial enforcement of the secret undertaking itself. Subject-matter jurisdiction, the proper waiver and any independent statutory claim remain distinct, but a threshold bar may resolve the pleaded agreement-dependent suit without trying the alleged intelligence relationship. The case does not concern ordinary private conduct incidentally involving classified evidence.
* **Relevant Dates:** Ninth Circuit judgment reported in 2003; argument January 11, 2005; event March 2, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the question presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Assess the legal character of the pleaded claim without making factual findings confirming or denying covert service. An evidentiary privilege and a rule barring a category of suit have different predicates and consequences.

##### Issue 1: Scope of the secret-agreement bar

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No intervening decision displaces Totten v. United States’s surviving prohibition on suits enforcing covert espionage agreements. Christopher v. Harbury requires an actual actionable injury and remedial channel; its reservations do not decide a state-secrets or covert-contract claim. Existing federal-contract principles require an authorized obligation but do not themselves make every obligation judicially enforceable.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether success necessarily requires establishing the secret espionage relationship and enforcing its alleged undertakings, or instead rests on an independent public legal duty. The state-secrets privilege separately concerns particular evidence; relabeling the same promised benefits as estoppel or property does not establish independence.
* **Other Governing Law:** Applicable federal jurisdiction and waiver statutes; the Fifth Amendment for the pleaded process theory. No enacted cause of action overriding Totten is identified in the pleaded claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** Totten v. United States concerns the inherently secret bargain; United States v. Reynolds supplies an evidentiary privilege rather than an automatic replacement for that distinct rule. Webster v. Doe concerns review of a different statutory employment decision and does not itself validate covert-contract enforcement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing bar reaches claims whose necessary premise is the secret espionage undertaking. It does not place all CIA conduct, public employment or independent constitutional injury outside judicial review.
* **Open Point:** Whether due-process and estoppel labeling supplies an independent claim here, and whether the evidentiary privilege superseded the categorical agreement rule.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Government argues that allowing the suit to continue would require proving the very relationship the bargain requires to remain secret, and that contract, estoppel and process labels cannot change the premise. The Does argue that their asserted entitlement and right to a fair decision can be resolved using confidentiality procedures, and that the evidence privilege is a sufficient safeguard. The Ninth Circuit favored that procedural approach. The limiting question is whether a claim has an independent legal source; a general assertion that the defendant is an intelligence agency is insufficient to bar it. No admission of the alleged relationship is inferred from the Government’s defense.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Applying Totten supports dismissal of the agreement-dependent claims without deciding their truth. A genuinely independent statutory claim would require ordinary jurisdiction, waiver and merits analysis, with a properly invoked evidentiary privilege addressed separately. Rejecting the existing categorical bar would require an express reason for changing precedent and an administrable replacement; it cannot be achieved merely by changing the complaint’s label.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The narrow rule can govern secret-agreement enforcement without immunizing all covert operations. An evidentiary-only approach would materially change the treatment of that category and must confront the necessary proof of the relationship.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The facts are allegations. No judicial confirmation, breach finding or established administrative remedy appears in the record. The Court cannot promise benefits through a channel not established by law.

## 36. Catholic Charities of Sacramento, Inc. v. Superior Court

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Catholic Charities of Sacramento, Inc. v. Superior Court
* **Citation or Docket:** 32 Cal. 4th 527; California Supreme Court No. S099822.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Listed MERITS matter arising from state review of denial of a preliminary injunction; statutory finality must be established before federal merits review.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-02; scheduled merits date, subject to §1257 finality
* **Question Presented:** Whether requiring a church-affiliated social-service organization to include contraceptive coverage in its prescription-drug insurance violates the Free Exercise Clause when the religious-employer exemption excludes organizations serving and employing substantial numbers of nonadherents? Whether limiting that exemption to organizations that inculcate religious values and primarily employ and serve adherents impermissibly discriminates among religious organizations under the Establishment Clause?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The California Supreme Court rejected the constitutional challenges and affirmed denial of preliminary relief in a mandate proceeding. The record does not identify a later final trial-court judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Catholic Charities seeks an injunction against enforcement of the coverage requirement as applied to it and relief from the restrictive exemption criteria. State regulators defend the statute and denial of preliminary relief.
* **Material Record:** The organization offers social services to people of diverse faiths, employs a religiously mixed workforce and provides prescription-drug coverage. It objects religiously to contraceptive coverage. The exemption requires a purpose of inculcating religious values, primarily employing and serving adherents, and specified religious tax status. Catholic Charities concedes it fails the statutory definition. Eliminating all prescription coverage is alleged to conflict with its separate obligation to provide adequate employee benefits; that contention establishes the asserted burden, not an exemption by itself.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The reviewable state order concerns preliminary relief. Under §1257, a final decision on a federal issue is not necessarily a final judgment. The jurisdiction-invoking party must establish a final disposition or a specific Cox Broadcasting practical-finality category; the importance of religious freedom supplies no exemption from that burden. No qualifying later judgment is invented.
* **Relevant Dates:** California Supreme Court decision March 1, 2004; assigned event March 2, 2005. Timeliness alone would not cure nonfinality.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The later Cutter v. Wilkinson concerns statutory accommodation in custody; Eulitt v. Maine Department of Education concerns education funding. Neither supplies entering law or resolves this employment-benefit record.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257(a); Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn. Resolve finality before constitutional merits. A preliminary-injunction record also requires attention to provisional factual findings and the precise relief requested.

##### Issue 1: Finality of the state preliminary-relief judgment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Johnson v. California, decided May 3, 2004, requires a final state judgment or an established Cox practical-finality exception where substantive proceedings remain and later review can be effective. Florida v. Thomas and Jefferson v. City of Tarrant likewise distinguish a settled federal issue from a completed case.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify what state proceedings remain, whether the outcome is genuinely preordained, whether the federal issue can survive for later review and whether a recognized exception’s particular conditions are met. Party agreement cannot confer jurisdiction.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257(a).
* **Material Other Authority:** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn preserves four defined practical-finality categories: a conclusive or practically preordained outcome; a federal issue that necessarily survives regardless of later state proceedings; a final federal determination that later cannot be reviewed; and a federal reversal that would terminate the litigation where deferral would seriously erode federal policy. The record must identify which category and its actual predicates. Here the remaining merits proceedings, possible independent state-law grounds, future review route and claimed harm from postponement are not established sufficiently to invoke one; a settled federal discussion alone does not select a category.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The judgment denies preliminary relief. The record supplies no final decree or established factual predicate for a Cox exception.
* **Open Point:** Whether additional state-proceeding information establishes finality or a specific exception; without it the merits cannot be adjudicated.

##### Issue 2: Free exercise and neutral employment-benefit regulation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah requires strict scrutiny for objective religious targeting or substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm. Employment Division v. Smith’s neutral, generally applicable-law rule survives at its retained scope. Religious institutional autonomy and secular employment obligations remain distinct.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the burden, the actual risk the law regulates, any comparable secular exclusions, discretionary exemption mechanism and evidence of targeting. A religious accommodation does not automatically make every remaining secular rule nonneutral.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; California Health and Safety Code §1367.25 and Insurance Code §10123.196 as enacted for this dispute.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statutory exemption’s actual criteria and the record concerning employee benefits are controlling facts. The federal Religious Freedom Restoration Act does not independently govern this state law after City of Boerne v. Flores.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing neutrality and comparability tests govern; the precise exemption architecture and employee-benefit burden need application. Corporate affiliation alone resolves neither autonomy nor statutory coverage.
* **Open Point:** If jurisdiction is established, whether the limited exemption or other exclusions defeat general applicability and whether any required scrutiny is satisfied.

##### Issue 3: Religious-employer definition and establishment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Agostini v. Felton retains Lemon only as a unified inquiry into the actual governmental act and religious authority or function. Purpose, effect and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not independent or fallback prongs. A specific denominational-classification rule or established permissible accommodation controls at its actual scope. Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats v. Weiss forbids governmental theological choice while allowing objective secular administration.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether the criteria classify denominations or instead identify a legally relevant institutional function, whether administering them requires theological judgments and what concrete burden the exemption or its expansion transfers to employees.
* **Other Governing Law:** Establishment Clause; the four statutory religious-employer criteria; the integrated insurance-coverage requirement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos concerns religious employment accommodation, while Larson v. Valente addresses denominational preference. Their different predicates must be examined, not merged.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An accommodation may have lawful limits, but the government cannot prefer denominations or adjudicate the truth of religious obligations. Neither proposition alone resolves these four criteria.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statutory line is a permissible institutional accommodation or impermissibly favors inward-facing religious organizations, and what severable relief would follow.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Catholic Charities argues that its religious obligation to serve outsiders should not disqualify it from an accommodation, that the State pressures institutional conduct and that the criteria favor some religious forms over others. Regulators argue that the law protects employees against unequal health benefits, applies to secular employment activity, and confines an accommodation to predominantly internal religious organizations. The California court accepted the latter account. If strict scrutiny applies, employee access, coverage alternatives and transferred costs require evidence; if the ordinary neutral-law rule applies, sincerity alone does not create an exemption. These merits positions remain neutral and conditional on jurisdiction.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

An established final judgment or a proved Cox exception would permit examination of the preserved constitutional issues. Otherwise dismissal for want of jurisdiction leaves the state litigation and existing preliminary-relief disposition in place without federal merits approval. On a reviewable record, possible merits paths include sustaining neutral coverage regulation, rejecting impermissible exemption criteria, or ordering further factual findings; severability and the rights of employees would determine the actual relief.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Finality can be resolved without any religion holding. A later merits ruling could address the exemption’s institutional line or its actual discriminatory administration; it need not create a general exemption from employment law or decide all religious accommodation.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The missing finality record is material and stops federal merits adjudication only for this case. No later final state judgment is identified.
