## 100. Van Orden v. Perry — No. 03-1500

### Question, competing paths and common law

The reviewed final judgment sustains Texas's continuing presentation. The alternatives are affirmance because the entire presentation performs historical commemoration, or reversal because it presents religious commands as the State's own authoritative civic message, followed by effective prospective relief. No Associate requires a new trial merely because the original visitor exhibits have not been newly retrieved. Their existence and described function are supported; constitutional adequacy is the disputed legal application.

Agostini's modified Lemon inquiry governs. Governmental ownership and acceptance establish responsibility for this presentation but not its forbidden function. Robinson distinguishes historical preservation from a faith's permanent civil badge. Marshfield requires attention to actual presentation and effective cure, without creating a removal rule for all monuments. Porter confirms that government cannot adopt devotion through private delivery; its classroom setting does not decide this monument. Cutter's custody accommodation does not supply a historical-display exemption.

The strongest affirmance case combines the 1961 gift inscription, numerous civic memorials, ordinary donated-monument treatment, the visitor materials and the setting's museum-like presentation. Its counterargument is that the independently prominent imperative religious text, retained between governmental lawmaking institutions, remains a current official declaration despite historical surroundings. The strongest reversal case treats those features together as continuing adoption. Its counterargument is that this turns an actual historical exhibit into religious profession simply because the preserved artifact contains commandments. Neither path may deny the brochure or equate monument age with an exemption. The 1993 reinstallation is part of current presentation; the landmark designation does not separately protect each monument.

## 101. Town of Castle Rock v. Gonzales — No. 04-278

### Components and adversarial comparison

Component A is whether Colorado's actual scheme and individualized order secure a substantive service entitlement protected by due process. Component B is whether the municipal complaint may proceed on a deprivation-without-process theory, with policy, causation and relief separately proved. The individual-officer and substantive-due-process dispositions are outside this review.

The statutory duties concern every reasonable enforcement means and arrest, or pursuit of a warrant when arrest is impractical, on probable cause of violation and service or actual notice. The permanent order permits alternate-weekend and summer parenting time, a midweek dinner arranged with reasonable notice, and pickup for parenting time. The allegation of taking without notice must be assessed against those permissions; contact is not itself a forbidden act. Printed reverse-side notice is not a decree directed to nonparty police. The statute supplies any police obligation.

The service-entitlement path distinguishes discretion over means from discretion to refuse the legally prescribed response to a named beneficiary. Its strongest objection is Doe v. District of Columbia's unanimous separation of mandatory enforcement steps from substantive property: the commanded act governs officials' treatment of another person rather than necessarily conferring a privately held benefit. The no-entitlement path therefore cannot rest on the word reasonable alone, or on a general right-to-protection label. It must examine the specific arrest-or-warrant direction and explain why those duties do not establish constitutional property.

The United States' separate process path assumes an interest and argues that repeated opportunities to supply the material facts were enough. Its strongest support is Connecticut Doe: process need not supply a substantive exception or an irrelevant hearing. Its strongest objection is that an alleged policy preventing genuine consideration can make repeated speaking opportunities empty. Mathews concerns the useful process actually required, not the correct tactical decision or successful protection. The complaint cannot be treated as proof either that municipal refusal occurred or that an eligibility determination actually took place. Zinermon and the municipal-responsibility rules preserve proof of foreseeable policy-caused deprivation without converting every unauthorized error into municipal liability.

## 102. Bell v. Thompson — No. 04-514

### Authority, exercise and competing paths

All eight distinguish authority to act before mandate issuance from lawful exercise of that authority. Rule 41(b) permits an extension of ordinary issuance time, (c) makes the mandate effective upon issuance, and (d)(2)(D) directs immediate issuance when the certiorari-denial order is filed. The further rehearing stay ended; no subsequent explanation or notice accompanied the extended withholding. The petition was filed in 1998, so applicable AEDPA provisions remain operative independently of mandate authority.

The rule-limited path treats the specific immediate-issuance provision as defeating residual enlargement after completed review. Carlisle is its strongest support. Its strongest objection is the court's continuing jurisdiction before actual issuance and the possibility of exceptional, rule-consistent correction rather than an unlimited party reopening remedy. The abuse path assumes the strongest available authority and treats the actual unexplained interval, expired announced stays, State reliance and subsequent merits amendment together as an unreasonable exercise. Its strongest objection is that the omitted psychological material supports a serious first-review mitigation issue and that a hearing order, while the mandate never issued, differs materially from recall of an executed mandate.

The sustaining path permits exceptional correction of the incomplete first review before the mandate becomes effective. It requires a concrete omitted-evidence problem, not capital status or disagreement with an earlier answer alone. Its strongest objection is that silent nonissuance cannot preserve unlimited merits power, especially when the court's ordinary review and announced stays have ended. No sustaining commitment rests on invented clerical fault, counsel diligence, a particular internal request, established innocence, present incompetence or already proved Strickland prejudice. The materially disputed point is whether this supported first-review deficiency and procedural stage justify the actual correction despite the delay, not whether absent evidence should be presumed in Thompson's favor.

Calderon's controlling recall category does not literally apply: no mandate issued here. Its finality principles are relevant by analogy, but the model does not impose Schlup or Sawyer as a new universal prerequisite to pre-mandate action. Rhines confirms that actual pendency matters, while its mixed-petition conditions are not a Rule 41 test. Rompilla separates investigation, prejudice and the state-adjudication threshold and supplies no reopening power. A Gonzalez continuation remains required.

## 103. McCreary County v. ACLU of Kentucky — No. 03-1693

### Question and two supported paths

The Court reviews preliminary protection against the third, present display, not a final judgment forbidding every future exhibit. Agostini makes the actual governmental function controlling; the original and continuing official resolutions, changes in the display and present explanatory material are evidence of that function. Marshfield recognizes that formal change can be genuine but does not itself defeat a continuing public presentation. Porter and Cutter distinguish public adoption from genuinely secular instruction and accommodation. No same-day Van Orden action enters this analysis.

The injunction-supporting path finds a sufficient likelihood that the counties continue their official religious presentation: the initial commands, religious resolutions, selected religious extracts, unrepealed commitments and successive arrangement together explain the present act. Its strongest objection is that the third arrangement is materially different, with nine equally framed complete documents, a historical title and actual explanatory statements. Those changes must receive substantive consideration; past religious motivation does not create permanent taint.

The injunction-opposing path treats that actual change as a permissible explanation of the religious sources of American legal culture. Its strongest objection is that a new title and equal frames can preserve rather than end the government's adoption when the same official commitments and message remain. The disagreement cannot be resolved by erasing history or by requiring formal repeal of every old resolution as a universal condition. The pre-event United States brief's recitations of the nine documents and explanation are consistent with the packet; its advocacy does not establish constitutional sufficiency. See [United States merits amicus](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/mccreary-v-aclu-amicus-merits), statement and argument concerning the current display.

### Components and supported competing rules

Component A asks whether purposeful promotion of actual copyright infringement is an independent ground of secondary responsibility despite substantial lawful capability and decentralized operation. Component B concerns the distributors' summary judgment, independent supervision theory and fitted proceedings on remand. Each Associate reaches both components.

Sony protects distribution of a useful product against an inference of the necessary culpability from capability alone. The unanimous Napster opinion preserves liability for knowing material assistance to identified infringement and separately requires direct benefit plus a right and practical ability to supervise for vicarious liability. Kalem supplies the surviving purposeful-promotion principle. None of those propositions makes the product's useful capabilities immunity for affirmative action intended to encourage infringement.

The supported rule is that a distributor who promotes a device's use to infringe copyright, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps taken for that object, is responsible for resulting acts of direct infringement. Ordinary distribution, lawful capability, awareness of possible misuse, general product support, revenue, present infringement rates or failure to filter does not alone establish that object. Evidence may be considered together in context, but the rule requires culpable promotion and resulting infringement rather than a statistical threshold or a general technology-design duty.

The strongest opposing path would sustain product-capability protection because a subjective-purpose rule could punish innovation and decentralized technologies for users' conduct. The answer is the objectively supported affirmative-promotion predicate and causal direct infringement, which preserve Sony's distribution protection while refusing immunity for distinct culpable conduct. Conversely, an expansive owner theory making nonfiltering or advertising receipts sufficient fails under Napster's actual knowledge/control distinctions and the absence of a freestanding design obligation.

The marketing, statements, assistance and business-plan evidence require adjudication under that rule, separately for each defendant and relevant period. The lower court's categorical use of Sony and network architecture to foreclose that route cannot stand. The governing rule does not itself determine final liability, damages or all injunctive terms. A purely vicarious route remains subject to proof of both benefit and actual supervisory power; the theoretical ability to stop distributing a program is not the latter.

## 105. NCTA v. Brand X Internet Services; FCC v. Brand X Internet Services — Nos. 04-277 and 04-281

### Components, exact statutory boundary and alternatives

Both dockets challenge the same Ninth Circuit judgment and receive the same modeled disposition. Component A concerns actual delegated interpretive authority and the legal force of Portland's prior circuit construction. Component B concerns the FCC's classification of this offered cable modem service. Component C concerns the preserved cable/DSL explanation and the ensuing administrative proceedings. No later technology, FCC order or deference doctrine enters.

Mead requires an actual congressional grant of force-of-law interpretation and its exercise. The Communications Act's national implementing authority, including §201(b) as construed in Iowa Utilities Board, and this Commission-level nationwide declaratory ruling supply a materially different mechanism from Mead's revisable transaction letter. Formality is evidence of exercise, not its own source of power. The Court independently enforces the definitions before allowing a reasonable choice within a delegated range. Gulf Power reserved this classification; its pole-attachment result does not decide it. Thomas's Gulf Power writing, joined by Souter, makes the distinct offered transmission question particularly material. Neal preserves its specific statutory construction without already settling this broader prior-circuit question. Barnhart, Yellow Transportation and Smiley recognize lawful administrative choice and reasoned change within identified authority.

Section 153(43) concerns user-selected transmission without change in the information's form or content as sent and received. Subsection (46) requires an offering for a fee to the public or an effectively public user class, regardless of facilities. Subsection (20) covers offered information-processing capabilities via telecommunications but excludes their use for management, control or operation of a telecommunications system or management of a telecommunications service. Subsection (44)'s carrier definition excludes aggregators and confines common-carrier treatment to the provision of telecommunications services, subject to the stated fixed/mobile satellite qualification. Those qualifications remain statutory law; none provides a cable-specific exemption.

The integrated-offering path treats the actual combination of transmission and end-user processing as one information service. The statute expressly contemplates information supplied via telecommunications, so use of transmission need not mean a distinct public offering of telecommunications service. Its strongest objection is that purchasers also pay for user-selected, unchanged transmission and bundling cannot erase a service the definition covers. The component path therefore identifies the publicly sold function, rather than merely the provider's ownership of wires. Its strongest objection is that extracting an input from every integrated information service would erase Congress's distinction between use of telecommunications and offering telecommunications service.

For the sustaining path, the FCC's §§38–41 explanation supports genuine functional integration in the described retail offer, not an absolute rule that a provider can evade Title II by packaging any transmission with an unrelated extra. The management exception must be honored: network-management processing alone cannot transform transmission into information service. The actual offer includes end-user retrieval, storage, email and interactive information capabilities rather than only internal system management. Optional use of an offered capability does not by itself remove that capability from the service. For the rejecting path, those genuine information capabilities coexist with the actual paid transmission offer; they do not change the form or content of every user-selected communication or create a statutory standalone-sale requirement.

The prior-precedent paths are also distinct. A prior circuit interpretation cannot foreclose a later reasonable exercise of actual delegated choice merely because the court selected a preferred reading before the agency acted, when examination of that decision and the statute shows the matter remains within Congress's delegated range. A binding determination of the statutory boundary remains controlling unless properly reconsidered judicially. Adopting the former rule is a new holding at this scope; neither Mead nor Neal already supplies it. The strongest objection is that a court's authoritative construction normally fixes law and an agency cannot obtain power to revise it from the absence of a formulaic statement about ambiguity. The supporting path answers through substantive examination of the prior holding and actual delegation, not a search for magic words.

Portland treated the cable provider's facilities-based transmission to customers as a telecommunications service. The sustaining path rejects that as the only permissible statutory classification: its treatment of the component does not resolve the ambiguity about the legally relevant retail offering and cannot foreclose the lawful agency choice by circuit stare decisis alone. The rejecting path regards the component analysis as the enacted boundary, so the claimed policy choice never arises. The pre-event [federal petitioners' merits brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/national-cable-tellcom-assoc-v-brand-x-internet-servcies-brief-merits), statement and Parts I–II, supplies the parties' strongest positions and cited lower-court recitations; its descriptions of post-divergence Supreme Court law do not override the supplied entering-law holdings.
