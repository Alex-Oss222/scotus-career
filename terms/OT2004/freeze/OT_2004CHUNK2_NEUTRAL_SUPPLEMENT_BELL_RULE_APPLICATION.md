## State rules now supplied in actual terms

Dicks (1981) confines the category to a conscienceless or pitiless crime unnecessarily torturous to the victim. Pritchett (1981) requires torture inflicted before death or acts evincing a depraved state of mind, with the torture or depravity satisfying the heinousness standard; it treats instantaneous death as insufficient for the torture branch and a further shot after death as insufficient to establish the required depravity. Melson (1982) applies the rule to defensive injuries demonstrating time for awareness, fear and resistance, and to repeated crushing blows with the specified severe injuries. These are actual state legal constructions and applications, not new federal holdings.

## Cone's actual direct-review findings

The 1984 Tennessee Supreme Court expressly described multiple crushing blows to both victims' skulls, defensive wounds indicating resistance, noninstantaneous deaths and the terror from the sequence in which one victim was killed before the other. It connected those facts to the jury's heinousness finding and stated that the jury could have considered the victims' terror, fright and horror. Those are state judicial findings and reasoning. This handoff does not replace the court's conditional account of what the jury could consider with a finding of what every juror necessarily decided, and does not convert evidentiary support into an already performed new sentencing balance.

The reported state appellate submissions addressed Pritchett, Melson and the victims' suffering. No date for a particular brief is inferred from its citation alone. The 1984 direct opinion itself supplies the dated application.

## Actual jury language and distinct correction questions

The reported instruction defines heinous as extreme wickedness or shocking evil, atrocious as outrageous wickedness and vileness, and cruel as deliberate high pain, indifference to or enjoyment of suffering, or pitilessness. The source supplies these actual definitions, not a transcript of every additional sentence of the penalty charge. They must be compared with the narrower state constructions without presuming identity. No independently established finding of constitutional inadequacy, harmful weighing or actual correction follows merely from copying the instruction.

The separate risk-to-others aggravator was rejected with an express state beyond-reasonable-doubt harmlessness ruling. That ruling concerns the risk factor only. Its reasoning and adequacy are not imported as a finding that a distinct heinousness or jury-guidance defect was harmless.
