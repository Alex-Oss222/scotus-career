## Shared sufficient ground and remedy

The charge permitted conviction on a disputed mental state insufficient under the statute, and the verdict contains no actual equivalent findings establishing the required culpability and proceeding relationship. The record also leaves competing mental-state inferences. On this combination of defects and findings, the Government cannot establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the erroneous instructions did not contribute to the verdict. The conviction must therefore be set aside and the case remanded for lawful further proceedings.

That conclusion is case-specific. It does not treat a reviewing court?s view of the most persuasive inference as a jury finding, announce that every omitted or misdescribed element is governed by a newly extended classification, decide how a different verdict containing actual equivalent findings should be treated, or require an acquittal. The statutory construction still requires conscious wrongdoing and contemplation of a particular official proceeding with the requisite documentary relationship; it does not require a pending proceeding or knowledge of its federal character. A1?A2?s remaining qualifications and the charged statutory version remain those in the existing reconciliation.
