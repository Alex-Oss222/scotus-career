## 76. Alaska v. United States

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court rejected Alaska's exceptions on June 6, 2005. The Alexander Archipelago conclusions were unanimous; the Glacier Bay retention conclusion was 6-3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote for the Court. Justice Scalia dissented from the Glacier Bay portion, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justice Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** The archipelago claims did not establish the asserted inland-water title, and the federal government retained the Glacier Bay submerged lands. The distinct Tongass claim had been removed by disclaimer.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 75; No. 128, Original.
* **Historical Sources:** [Official United States Reports opinion](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep545/usrep545075/usrep545075.pdf).

## 77. Spector v. Norwegian Cruise Line Ltd.

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court reversed and remanded. Five Justices supported the full judgment; Justice Thomas additionally supported application to matters outside internal affairs but would exclude structural obligations. Three Justices favored affirmance across the board.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Kennedy wrote the Court's Parts I, II-A-1 and II-B-2 for himself and Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; other passages had only Stevens and Souter, while Part III-A also had Thomas. Ginsburg concurred in part and in the judgment with Breyer. Thomas concurred and dissented in part. Scalia dissented with Rehnquist and O'Connor, and Thomas joined only Part I-A of that dissent.
* **Historical Holding:** Title III applies to foreign cruise ships in United States waters, but particular applications implicating internal affairs require further analysis. The different opinions disagreed about the breadth and operation of that limitation.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 119; No. 03-1388.
* **Historical Sources:** [Reported opinion and separate writings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/545/119/).

## 79. Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court reversed, 8-1.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for eight Justices. Justice Breyer concurred; Justice Thomas dissented.
* **Historical Holding:** Batson's first step requires evidence sufficient to permit an inference of discrimination, not proof that discrimination is more likely than not.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 162; No. 04-6964. This is distinct from the historical prison-segregation Johnson opinion and from the earlier finality dismissal.
* **Historical Sources:** [Reported Batson opinion and separate writings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/545/162/).

## 80. Bradshaw v. Stumpf

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court unanimously reversed in part, vacated in part, and remanded the Sixth Circuit judgment: it rejected the grounds for setting aside the plea and returned the distinct sentencing question for further consideration.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice O'Connor wrote for the Court. Justice Souter, joined by Justice Ginsburg, concurred; Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, also concurred separately.
* **Historical Holding:** The plea was not invalid for failure to understand intent on the stated record. The consequences of the differing prosecution accounts for the death sentence required separate consideration on remand.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 175; No. 04-637.
* **Historical Sources:** [Reported opinion and concurrences](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/545/175/).

## 83. Miller-El v. Dretke

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court reversed, 6-3, and held Miller-El entitled to habeas relief.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Souter wrote for the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Justice Breyer concurred. Justice Thomas dissented, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justice Scalia.
* **Historical Holding:** The combined strike statistics, juror comparisons, questioning practices, shuffles and historical evidence established purposeful discrimination despite the state court's contrary finding.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 231; No. 03-9659.
* **Historical Sources:** [Reported merits opinion and separate writings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/545/231/).

## 86. San Remo Hotel, L.P. v. City and County of San Francisco

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court unanimously affirmed the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Stevens wrote for five Justices. Chief Justice Rehnquist concurred in the judgment, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, and questioned the continuing justification for the state-litigation requirement.
* **Historical Holding:** Section 1738 contains no exception for takings plaintiffs who litigated in state court to satisfy Williamson County. The historical concurrence's criticism did not overrule that requirement.
* **Historical Authority:** 545 U.S. 323; No. 04-340.
* **Historical Sources:** [Reported opinion and concurrence](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/545/323/).

## 89. Graham County Soil & Water Conservation District v. United States ex rel. Wilson

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** The historical Court reversed and remanded, 7-2 in the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Justice Thomas wrote the principal opinion, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, and by Justice Souter except for one footnote. ## 90. American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission

### SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT
