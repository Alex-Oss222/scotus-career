## 104. Grokster — corrected decisional components

### Inducement and the current-version judgment

The intentional-promotion rule remains supported: affirmative expression or other conduct undertaken to promote infringement can establish secondary responsibility for resulting direct infringement even if substantial lawful product capability is established. Sony's protection against capability-based imputation and Napster's specific-assistance rule do not make a useful device's distributor immune from its own purposeful encouragement. Kalem supplies the distinct purposeful-promotion principle. The rule preserves lawful distribution, ordinary support and technological capability without turning high infringement, revenue or failure to filter into sufficient inducement by itself.

The judgment under review protects distribution of the then-current versions. It does not decide damages for excluded earlier versions or other past activities. Respondents' strongest scope objection is that a conduct theory imports the excluded acts into this limited judgment. The answer is narrower: the chronology of evidence and the conduct for which judgment is sought are different. Earlier activity may establish the purpose and continuing operation of current distribution when the evidence actually connects them. It cannot supply current liability merely because an earlier wrong occurred. That connection must be assessed for Grokster and StreamCast separately. The remaining evidence of statements, marketing and user assistance, considered with the business evidence at its supported status, prevents categorical immunity on the lower court's product-capability and architecture grounds. This does not depend on treating the identified promotional drafts as public communications.

The drafts can be relevant to purpose if admissible and connected to the pertinent defendant and distribution. Their unverified publication prevents treating them as proved communications to users or as proof that those communications caused infringement. The answer to respondents therefore preserves both limits: unpublished planning can be evidence of an object, but neither the object alone nor a presumed transmission of the drafts establishes the complete conduct-and-causation case. Ordinary summary-judgment treatment governs the actual evidence and inferences.

### The distinct Sony distribution route

The lower court's conclusion that substantial noninfringing capability existed was contested by the owners. The independent model now treats that conclusion as a disputed judicial determination, not a concession or an independently established fact. The legal inducement question is conditional: affirmative culpable promotion is actionable even if the device satisfies Sony. That conditional holding does not decide that this device does satisfy it.

The separate distribution theory remains in the litigation. Sony's commercially significant lawful-capability protection, as preserved in the unanimous Napster opinion, governs that theory. Actual and prospective lawful use must be assessed under that existing rule. Neither present infringement rates alone nor the abstract possibility of exchanging any lawful file supplies the complete inquiry. The available-file survey does not establish actual-download percentages. Its residual ten percent is unclassified, not proved lawful use; a copyrighted-file classification does not by itself establish an unauthorized transfer. The record also describes actual authorized and public-domain uses, whose significance cannot be erased because the residual survey category is unknown.

The government's alternative would make the comparative role of infringement in each particular business central, and in its strongest formulation make overwhelming infringement plus business dependence sufficient. Its serious argument is that trivial hypothetical uses should not extinguish copyright protection for a business organized around infringement. Its serious objection is that economic dependence and present prevalence can replace Sony's protection of genuine present or prospective capability with liability based on popularity and revenue. Napster requires the actual culpability and assistance or control predicates for its independent routes; its observation that infringement can attract users proves neither all contributory liability nor supervisory control. The supported inducement rule addresses deliberately organized encouragement directly and does not require adopting the government's separate business-centered standard.

Each Associate retains Sony and Napster rather than adopting a new numerical threshold, compulsory filter condition, or rule making every identified lawful example sufficient. This is not a final ruling either that respondents win the distribution-only claim or that the owners win it. The actual and prospective significance of each defendant's lawful uses remains application work under the surviving rule if that separate theory requires decision below. The Court's controlling question about deliberate promotion can be resolved completely without announcing an additional minimum-use formulation. Such a formulation is a separate proposed expansion beyond the conditional conduct question decided here; it is not silently decided by the inducement holding. The mandate must not present the distribution claim as conceded, extinguished or adjudicated for either side.

### Both cross-motions, existing direct infringement and further proceedings

The district court established underlying direct infringement of the protected media files at issue, and that conclusion was not contested on appeal. The baseline statement that direct infringement must be established remains an element of the legal rule, but cannot describe that already established predicate as wholly awaiting first proof. It also does not establish every transfer, each defendant's secondary responsibility, a causal connection between particular conduct and infringement, or the extent of recoverable loss.

All eight Associates retain vacatur of the distributors' current-version summary judgment and a bounded remand. Their stated ground defeats the distributors' categorical immunity; it does not automatically grant the owners' cross-motion. Each motion must be considered independently under the correct rule and the inferences applicable to the nonmovant. The lower courts must determine what the record establishes about each defendant's relevant affirmative conduct, object and causal connection, recognizing the uncontested direct infringement already established. Summary judgment remains available to the extent the actual record warrants it; this disposition does not command a trial of every element or retry uncontested direct infringement. Nor does it direct entry of final secondary liability on the present qualified source recitations.

This further-proceedings direction is justified by the lower court's use of a categorical immunity that excluded the independent conduct ground, and by the resulting need to apply the announced rule to the actual cross-motions. It is not a production order to cure unavailable research sources. Any precise appellate command granting an owner's cross-motion would require the actual defendant-specific motion record and a supported determination eliminating genuine material disputes. The supplied neutral refresh does not establish those conditions, and no such grant is modeled.

The independent vicarious route remains governed by direct benefit plus the right and practical ability to supervise. Advertising dependence does not prove control; a hypothetical ability to redesign a product to acquire control does not create an independent duty to do so. Its actual application remains for properly preserved proceedings under Napster if needed. Liability, causation, damages, equitable relief and any invoked section 512 category retain their separate predicates and conditions. The remedy must preserve the current-version boundary and may not resolve excluded historical damages by relabeling old evidence as a new award.
