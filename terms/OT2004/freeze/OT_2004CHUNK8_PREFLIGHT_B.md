## 71. Arthur Andersen LLP v. United States

### Record, governing law and qualification checks

The question is the adequacy of the charged section 1512(b)(2) instructions, on preserved review after conviction. The source is the 2000 Code governing 2001 conduct. It criminalizes knowing use of intimidation, physical force, threats, corrupt persuasion or misleading conduct with the prescribed intent. This indictment concerns corrupt persuasion of another person to withhold or impair records for an official proceeding. Subsection (e)(1) expressly removes any requirement that the proceeding already be pending or about to be instituted; subsection (e)(2) also removes admissibility or freedom from privilege as prerequisites. Subsection (f) removes a need to prove knowledge of the proceeding's federal character, rather than removing the offense's intent toward official proceedings. The statutory affirmative defense in (d) is separately confined to conduct consisting solely of lawful conduct with the sole intention of encouraging truthful testimony, proved by the defendant by a preponderance. It is not a universal lawful-document-destruction exception, nor a substitute for the Government proving actual offense elements.

The accepted packet's chronology and charge are supported by a targeted check of the archived joint appendix, apparent JA 212–214. The OCR gives the improper-purpose definition in terms of subverting, undermining or impeding factfinding; separates the persuading agent's purpose from the persuaded person's; requires the specific purpose of making records unavailable; dispenses with pending proceedings and subpoenas; and permits guilt despite an honest and sincere belief in lawfulness. OCR omits words near a page transition, so public exact quotation needs page-image checking; the supplied neutral paraphrase does not depend on filling that OCR gap.

Aguilar's known nexus to pending judicial work governs section 1503, not all obstruction offenses. Its six-Justice rationale requires a relationship in time, causation or logic and knowledge that the endeavor is likely to affect the proceeding; it does not require a subpoena. Its different eight-Justice section 2232(c) construction illustrates offense-specific knowledge and purpose without universal knowledge of statutory illegality. Section 1512's express anticipatory reach therefore must remain operative. Neder supplies the jury's duty to find actual elements, the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden for preserved error, and a qualified actual-verdict inquiry. Strong evidence of guilt under a correct charge alone cannot establish what this jury found under this charge.

### Supported alternatives and their strongest objections

1. The Government's improper-purpose construction can support affirmance only if the charge as a whole requires the culpability and proceeding intent the enacted phrase demands. Its strongest argument is that specific intent to suppress evidence for official use, combined with deliberate persuasion, sufficiently excludes innocent housekeeping, while a statutory-illegality requirement would protect intentional obstruction before formal process. The objection is that the charge defines improper purpose through mere interference with factfinding and expressly allows an honest lawful belief; that formulation may fail to distinguish legitimate persuasion to exercise rights or follow genuine retention rules from conscious wrongdoing. Knowledge of the statutory prohibition and consciousness of wrongdoing are different possible requirements and must not be conflated.
2. A construction requiring knowing wrongful persuasion and a contemplated proceeding of an identifiable kind can yield reversal for instructional error. The best objection is that requiring a pending or imminent proceeding would contradict subsection (e), and borrowing all of Aguilar's section 1503 requirements would ignore statutory differences. A narrower construction must therefore articulate future-proceeding contemplation without importing pendency, a subpoena or guaranteed success.
3. If error is found, the Court can decide nonharmlessness from this charge and the genuinely disputed mental-state record or direct a confined first proper harmlessness inquiry. The Government must connect the actual instructions, required findings and record to noncontribution, rather than substitute appellate factfinding. The competing concern against an immediate new trial is Neder's express allowance of reliable whole-record inference beyond mechanically equivalent findings. The competing concern against another inquiry is the charge's affirmative removal of the disputed culpability protection. No assumed result follows merely from a deadlock report or eventual conviction.
4. Reversal for trial error permits lawful further proceedings. Acquittal requires an independent insufficiency ground properly within the case; a construction ruling alone does not establish corporate innocence. The packet does not authorize a new insufficiency finding or adjudication of later-enacted destruction offenses.

## 72. Cutter v. Wilkinson

### Record, statutory operation and governing boundary

This is a facial Establishment Clause challenge to RLUIPA's institutionalized-persons protection, not a judgment awarding every accommodation. The certified interlocutory posture, sincerity stipulation, claims of restricted exercise and distinct unresolved power defenses are supplied. Sincerity at this stage does not prove substantial burden, safety, entitlement to any requested item or present prospective standing for each former inmate.

The original Act, Pub. L. 106-274, section 3, forbids a covered government's substantial burden on a confined person's religious exercise, even through a generally applicable rule, unless government demonstrates that imposing that burden on that person furthers a compelling interest through the least restrictive means. Coverage requires the federal-assistance or commerce predicate in section 3(b). Exercise need not be compelled by or central to a belief system. Section 8 defines demonstrates to include production and persuasion. Section 4(a) authorizes appropriate relief and retains ordinary Article III standing; section 4(e) preserves the PLRA. Section 4(g) contains its specific commerce-only limitation. Section 4(b)'s separately worded burden allocation refers to a Free Exercise or section 2 claim; do not silently relabel it a section 3 clause. The claimant must establish the substantial burden necessary to invoke section 3, whose own language places its stated defense on government.

Sections 5 and 6 are material qualifications: the Act creates no religious funding entitlement, although operations may incur expenses to avoid a substantial burden; government may eliminate the burden through the several lawful alternatives described by section 5(e); broad construction operates within the Act and Constitution; severability is express; and the Establishment Clause remains unaffected. These provisions neither grant every inmate request nor authorize denial under mere rationality review once section 3 applies.

Kiryas Joel permits accommodation beyond the Free Exercise minimum but prohibits denominational allocation of governmental power and particular sectarian privilege. Agostini expressly leaves restoration of private exercise restricted by custody to the applicable accommodation inquiry; its former independent Lemon prongs are not a separate fallback. Current-term Doe v. Porter prohibits the public institution's adoption of devotional instruction as its own program. Accommodation of private inmate practice must therefore be classified by actual function, rather than equated either with official devotion or with an automatically permissible secular label.

### Supported alternatives and objections

The general accommodation path treats section 3 as relieving exceptional government-created limits on private exercise on neutral terms. Its strongest objection is that religious requests receive protections unavailable for comparable nonreligious demands and may burden guards, other prisoners, resources and security. The answer must concern constitutional accommodation and actual statutory limits, not a claim that religion always defeats those interests. Particular third-party costs, unequal denominational treatment, disguised gang activity or interference with institutional order can present different application questions.

The officials' categorical preference path treats enhanced religious protection itself as establishment. Its strongest obstacle is the preserved permission to accommodate beyond constitutional minimums, particularly where government custody makes private practice unusually dependent on official permission. A facial invalidation needs to explain why that established permissible relationship fails across the provision's operation; comparison to constitutional rationality review in Turner and O'Lone does not alone show that Congress cannot legislate greater protection under another valid power.

A construction preserving security and constitutional limits is possible only within actual statutory ambiguity. Institutional expertise and evidence of safety, order, cost and effects on others matter to application, but cannot erase compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means language. Equally, the claimant's sincerity cannot erase the government defense. Reversal of the facial Establishment Clause dismissal permits ordinary adjudication of the statutory claims and any genuinely preserved distinct defenses; it supplies no damages rule, universal exemption, automatic prison entry, land-use holding, or necessary resolution of Spending and Commerce authority.

## 73. Tory v. Cochran

### Record and governing law

The accepted record establishes adjudicated false statements and coercive conduct, a decree forbidding public picketing, signs and oral statements concerning Cochran and his firm, and Cochran's death after argument. Those predicate findings remain facts; they do not adjudicate all future expression false. The decree has no automatic death-termination term and the respondent continues to assert enforceability. Substitution is agreed but does not confer new substantive rights, make the nonparty firm an original plaintiff or settle the widow's entitlement to every restraint.

Alexander distinguishes retrospective punishment without a future-publication prohibition from an actual prospective ban. Its independent expressive-burden inquiry does not immunize forfeiture or validate future silence. Madsen requires provision-specific fit for content-neutral relief and preserves lawful advocacy despite prior wrongs. Schenck retains that fit and limits extension to responsible parties and actual concert with notice. Because this decree is keyed to its subject, Madsen's content-neutral test cannot be treated as a sufficient constitutional safe harbor. Near, Keefe and Nebraska Press are surviving pre-divergence authorities against unsupported prior restraints; the question is this actual topic-wide restraint, not an abstract immunity for defamation or threats.

### Supported alternatives and objections

The continuing-injury path recognizes that an operative, assertedly enforceable command remains a present restraint notwithstanding the death. It permits adjudication of the topic-wide ban and relief addressing its current justification. The strongest opposing argument is that death changes the original vehicle and state-law beneficiary questions, making state reconsideration the better initial route. That concern supports a confined remand or prudential DIG if warranted; it does not itself establish jurisdictional mootness while the command persists.

The merits-invalidity path focuses on the prohibition of truthful, unrelated and otherwise lawful statements and the reduced prospective justification after death. Its strongest objection is the trial court's supported coercion findings and forecast of continued wrongdoing. A sound distinction preserves available remedies for actual unlawful conduct while requiring fit between that conduct and the future restraint. It need not decide whether every precisely limited post-adjudication defamation injunction is forbidden.

A remand for clarification of enforceability and changed circumstances is available if a genuinely necessary state-law issue prevents constitutional resolution. Its strongest objection is that the decree and the respondent's asserted enforcement already identify continuing constitutional injury, and an indefinite intact ban would perpetuate it. A DIG carries no merits approval. A true mootness disposition requires the actual disappearance of the live restraint, not merely a conclusion about the decedent's ability to sue for new defamation. Affirmance requires a constitutional justification for the decree's full present breadth, not only the earlier wrong.

## 74. Tucson Woman's Clinic v. Eden

### Amended judgment and material factual status

Review is of the August 23, 2004 amended judgment, 379 F.3d 531, not the original 371 F.3d 1173 text. The amendment expressly replaces the sentence describing the fourth informational-privacy requirement: limited serious-incident reporting does not violate informational privacy. Part VI.C gives that same operative disposition. A general introductory or concluding description of privacy claims cannot reverse that specific amended holding.

The retained review components are: cumulative access burdens; professional, volume and patient sex-equality classifications; entry under the Fourth Amendment; separate transfers of intimate records; the dignity provision's notice; and hospital privileges/private control. The claims' evidence and burdens remain separate. Cross-motions do not turn predicted provider losses or disputed motives into adjudicated historical facts. Providers presented more than a bare cost assertion: specified equipment, staffing, overtime and administration expenses, predicted departure from practice and reduced physician capacity, and expert links to delays and risk. They sought a trial on the cumulative access claim. Evidence disputed by unresolved motions to strike was not relied on by the appellate court and is not treated as admitted here.

The scheme reaches five or more first-trimester abortions in a month and any later-trimester abortion. Its duties must be identified provision by provision. The on-site privilege holder need not always be the operating physician, but must remain until the patients are stable and ready to leave recovery; smaller practices may therefore bear distinct staffing costs. Neither a generic alternative provider nor an exception from another state's regulations may be invented.

### Component paths, governing rules and strongest counterarguments

**Access and purpose.** The full Casey six-step standard controls, with its five direct original joins and later application in Lawall and Greenville. A baseline invasion, effective private veto, denial of timely necessary life-and-health care, proved obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle independently fails. Remaining severe interference receives compelling-justification/narrow-tailoring review; remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to a concrete practicable substantially less burdensome adequate alternative. Below that trigger independent constitutional protection remains. Claimants prove the duty, causal interference and stable affected population before the corresponding government burden arises. A challenged interacting package receives cumulative analysis without double counting. The required comparison is with and without the actual obligation, holding other relevant circumstances constant.

The supported path is a factual remand on genuinely disputed burdens under the complete governing standard, not a finding that providers already won ultimate invalidation. Arizona's best counterargument is that safety regulation may impose manageable expense and need not exactly match every other procedure; specific reasonable evidence can substantiate safety without a new study in every case. Providers' best answer is their concrete predicted loss of capacity, delay and substantial contrary medical evidence, which ordinary summary-judgment rules do not permit the court to resolve against them. No exact dollar increase or completed clinic closure is required. Conversely, suspicion of hostile motives or legislative opposition to abortion does not itself prove obstructive purpose. The lower court found its cited motive evidence inadequate for its rational-basis animus theory; do not convert that limited finding into a conclusion that every justification and cumulative effect is established.

**Equality.** Separate provider categories and monthly-volume categories may survive rational review for targeted oversight and administrability, subject to the actual record; physicians performing abortions are not automatically a suspect class. The independent patient claim cannot be extinguished by declaring it absorbed into Casey. United States v. Virginia requires a genuine important objective and substantial fit for an actual sex classification; Klinger first identifies the real governmental classification and relevant opportunity, and requires purposeful discrimination for a genuinely neutral decision challenged on that ground. A limited remand can identify the actual classification, comparators, purpose and applicable scrutiny without deciding discrimination already occurred. The strongest objection to automatic heightened review is that pregnancy-related treatment distinctions do not all constitute an express sex classification. The strongest objection to automatic rationality review is the preserved allegation of unequal treatment of women's health needs compared with comparably risky services for men. Neither is resolved by the adjective medical.

**Entry.** Commercial medical premises retain Fourth Amendment protection. The administrative-inspection exception must fit its actual predicates. New York v. Burger requires substantial governmental interest, necessity of warrantless inspection and a constitutionally adequate warrant substitute in a genuinely closely regulated industry; new extensive regulation does not by itself establish the historical premise or remove all privacy. Operating-hours limits alone do not settle discretion over rooms, patients and records. Ferguson supplies examination of the actual medical-search operation and rejects attendance for care as consent; its coordinated testing/prosecution facts are not this case. Arizona's best path is a confined purpose-limited administrative inspection with genuine statutory limits. Its obstacle is the mandate for immediate access without the controls required to justify that breadth. Invalidating that authority need not forbid consensual inspections, valid warrants or genuine exigencies.

**Information transfers.** Greenville's six-Justice rule requires sufficient connection among information, actual recipients, function, scope, use and safeguards; Lawall and Herring reject inferring authority for new recipients from lawful initial possession. The test is not a universal compelling-need or indispensability rule. Department access under R9-10-1511(A)(4)(b),(c) reaches identifiers and complete histories; the lower record found no supported need-to-know access restriction, only vague training testimony, uncertain sanction coverage and broad copying. APEX receives identified ultrasound prints under section 36-2301.02(B),(C), with seven-year retention, only minimal secure-storage specification for the first two years and no adequate employee access constraints; intended coding is not binding policy. Against these permissions, fit may fail even without a completed leak. Arizona's strongest argument is genuine audit and complaint work plus statutory nondisclosure protections. The response must identify why those protections do or do not constrain the actual recipients and uses; the fact of government employment is not itself the answer. Redaction, coding or targeted follow-up are concrete alternatives to examine, not commands applicable to every medical audit.

The distinct incident provision, R9-10-1504(B),(C), reports an abortion-related death or serious injury to a patient or viable fetus, with the patient's name, incident description and follow-up action, to the responsible professional board. Sections 32-1451.01(C) and 32-1855.03(D) supply separate public-disclosure safeguards. The amended court upheld that limited transfer because serious-incident discipline supplies a specific need and the scope and protections fit it. The contrary challenge cannot rely on the absence of the department/contractor provisions, which do not govern these separate boards. The issue was first raised on appeal and entertained there; no district factfinding on it is invented. Relief must state which access and transfers are restrained and preserve lawful confidential oversight and the separately upheld report if supported under the controlling rule.

**Notice.** R9-10-1507's independently enforceable consideration/respect/full-recognition-of-dignity-and-individuality duty differs from definite clinical requirements. Greenville unanimously sustained contextual professional duties, but six Justices rejected independent punishment based on an undefined additional best practice. The strongest defense is that professional context gives intelligible respectful-care content and particular unforeseeable applications can be challenged later. The opposing ground is that full recognition and dignity supply no settled clinical criterion, enabling sanctions based on subjective reactions rather than ascertainable conduct. Warnings or mitigation cannot supply a missing legal standard. A confined disposition can remove independent sanctions under this branch while preserving definite care requirements and voluntary aspirations. Other unbriefed vagueness claims remain abandoned.

**Privileges and delegation.** Greenville unanimously permits objective qualifications and emergency-care arrangements while forbidding arbitrary legally uncontrolled private licensing consent. Arizona's Holmes, Peterson and Blende authorities require hospital-related legitimate standards, notice and hearing, and narrow judicial review for reasonableness and substantial evidentiary support. A record showing no actual or supported impending arbitrary exclusion can support rejection of this facial arbitrary-control claim. Providers' best counterargument is that private hospitals retain substantive discretion, and a merely nominal review mechanism may leave effective control untouched. The analysis must address the actual constraints and their usable operation; it cannot import rules binding public hospitals into private institutions without a source. Failure of delegation does not settle Casey: application expenses, meetings, on-call obligations and on-site staffing remain part of cumulative access review.

**Severability and interim protection.** Arizona severability law applies after the invalid provisions/applications and the remaining access findings are determined. The lower court reserved severability because the access remand remained unresolved. No automatic invalidation of the whole scheme or automatic blanket reinstatement is warranted. Enforcement was stayed by stipulation through district proceedings and appeals. The eventual mandate must identify the operative restraint and treatment of retained duties while remaining claims are adjudicated; it cannot silently turn a partial constitutional ruling into permission to enforce the entire package.

## 75. Gonzales v. Raich

### Changed entering law and supported record

The neutral packet's statement that no GDF decision is effective must be updated: GDF Realty was decided January 24, 2005. Breyer's opinion, joined in full by all eight other Justices, applies Home Builders and Rancho Viejo's necessary-inclusion rule. It requires the independently valid interstate regime, enacted covered class and genuine functional relationship, independently assessed by the court, while rejecting species-specific border crossing, market or separate-effects prerequisites. Its outside-class, invalid-regime and nominal-or-pretextual limits remain. This is controlling law and a published position for every current Justice; its wildlife facts do not themselves prove the drug relationship.

The preliminary-injunction record supplies local cultivation and personal use, caregiver delivery without charge, physician evidence of serious need and failed alternatives, and seizure of Monson's six plants. Medical assertions have their proper affidavit status; they are not a final constitutional-liberty or statutory-authorization finding. The question concerns Congress's power over the challenged class and the preliminary injunction's legal foundation, not guilt, sentence, damages, a newly found diversion or the merits of all other pleaded claims.

The official 2004 CSA text supplies a concrete interstate supply-control system. Section 801 distinguishes generalized health harms from interstate traffic, local production and possession, supply/demand, practical differentiation and enforceability. Under present Lopez law the generalized health finding alone is insufficient; the supply/enforcement relationship is the candidate constitutional mechanism. Sections 841(a) and 844(a) govern unauthorized manufacture/distribution and possession, with their enacted authorization exceptions. Section 802 defines production to include planting, cultivation, growing and harvesting and distribution through delivery rather than payment. Its manufacture definition also has its actual preparation/compounding and practitioner limitations; these are not a general cultivation exception. Schedule I and the controlled authorization process must be used as they stood at the event, with no later rescheduling imported. Section 903 does not occupy the whole field of state law; it preserves state law except the stated positive conflict. State permission consequently does not amend federal authorization, while federal power does not require California to criminalize the same conduct.

### Supported paths and objections

The necessary-inclusion path identifies the actual interstate controlled-substance market, the enacted unauthorized production/distribution/possession class and the relationship of included local supply to enforceability of the closed authorization regime. It does not require proof that these particular patients sold, crossed a border, or diverted any marijuana. Congress's practical judgment receives latitude within an independently established genuine relationship; the rule requires neither least restrictive means nor quantified proof that every exemption would collapse enforcement. The strongest contrary argument is that purely local, unpaid, physician-supervised use is an administrable class whose exclusion leaves the interstate market scheme intact and that comprehensiveness alone cannot supply an enumerated power. The Government's response must identify supply substitution, fungibility and enforceability in this actual system, rather than recite national social costs.

The patients' confined-exemption path can succeed under present law only by demonstrating a relevant outside-class application, invalid underlying interstate regime or nominal/pretextual relationship, or another supported defect in applying the governing rule. Locality and absence of payment alone cannot defeat necessary inclusion after Rancho Viejo and GDF. Its strongest version challenges the government's actual functional account and the boundary between the enacted class and the proposed medical subset. Its strongest difficulty is that these activities produce and possess the very substance whose interstate supply is regulated; identifying an unpaid subclass does not itself sever that connection. No fictional patient-specific diversion finding is necessary or permissible.

An evidentiary remand is available only for genuinely material unresolved facts needed to decide that legal relationship. Rancho Viejo and GDF reject a routine new hearing where text and structure adequately establish the connection. Conversely, a label or unsupported market assertion cannot be cured by deference alone. A broader aggregation rationale, if proposed, must face the operative Lopez sequence and enacted-class fit, not resurrect generalized effects as current law. No economic motive is silently inserted in the CSA.

If the Commerce ground cannot support the injunction, relief from that direction leaves genuinely preserved due-process, necessity and other pleaded claims for their own adjudication. Oakland Cannabis rejected the distributor's statutory necessity exception and forbids equity recreating it, but expressly reserved individual-patient and independent constitutional questions. It supplies neither a universal patient-necessity rejection nor a preexisting answer to this power question. Conversely, preservation of a claim does not guarantee preliminary relief. California's exemption is distinct from commandeering state administrators; direct federal regulation of private persons does not itself require the State to administer the federal program.
