## Alaska — validated corrections and framing

The argument date is January 10, 2005. The February 28 date in the approved neutral packet is clerical and must not propagate. The objective extraction establishes this date without transmitting the same-matter Court's disposition or rationale.

Counts I and II concern the specified Alexander Archipelago pockets and enclaves beyond three nautical miles from the mainland and every individual island. Alaska must prove inland status through historic title or the Convention's juridical-bay rule. The United States accepts that established inland status of these pockets would carry the state-title presumption it cannot rebut. Count IV is different: Glacier Bay is accepted as inland, and retention against statehood is disputed. The Tongass claim has been removed by disclaimer.

The Master's historic-waters recommendation rests on failure to prove a continuous assertion of authority to exclude innocent passage. Russian fishing and trading arrangements, the 1903 arbitration position, the Marguerite seizure and British protest, and the 1934 treatment of Canadian fishing are evidence of different kinds of authority. Neither fishing regulation nor an isolated disputed seizure is automatically equivalent to continuous sovereignty excluding innocent navigation. Alaska contests the Master's assessment; the published recitations are evidence of what the record contains, not a substituted Court finding.

Count II includes two independently live grounds. Alaska's proposed constructive peninsula joins Kuiu, Kupreanof, Mitkof and Dry Islands to the mainland to produce North Bay and South Bay. The Master rejected that assimilation except for two channels insufficient to create the claimed bays. Independently, he found that the proposed waters fail Article 7(2)'s well-marked-indentation requirement even assuming assimilation. That requirement considers penetration relative to mouth width, landlocked character and more than coastal curvature; the semicircle area requirement is additional. United States v. Alaska (1997) rejects a general fringe rule but does not foreclose every particular assimilation permitted by United States v. Louisiana (1969) and United States v. Maine (1985). A decision may rest on the independent indentation ground without converting the 1997 holding into a categorical assimilation ban.

For Glacier Bay, the Master found inclusion of the submerged lands. Alaska filed no formal exception to that finding; its four-sentence disagreement in a brief footnote does not present a developed inclusion exception. The live retained-title exception addresses the second, distinct clear-intent requirement. Alaska's argument that §6(e)'s reservation proviso is limited to the initial transfer clause is material: that clause concerns property specifically used solely for fisheries and wildlife conservation under three named enactments, while Glacier Bay was created under the Antiquities Act. The proviso itself describes lands withdrawn or otherwise set apart as refuges or reservations for wildlife protection and associated facilities, with a separate general-research category. Its terms must not be collapsed into the initial clause. Alaska also disputes whether the monument was set apart for wildlife protection. The United States relies on the monument's marine habitat, protection purposes, actual prestatehood reservation and §§5, 6(e), 6(m) together. Both constructions remain available for independent evaluation.

Entering law is United States v. Alaska (1997), including its actual §6(e) retention holding and separate-inclusion/title-defeat inquiry, its 2000 decree only at that decree's own geography, and Idaho v. United States (2001). Idaho requires timely enacted treatment rather than an automatic reservation presumption; it supplies no universal requirement of special words. The public Alaska dissent and Idaho dissent supply Justice-specific limits at their respective statutory settings.

## Spector — validated lower-court posture and remand scope

The United States' December 2004 merits brief independently confirms the control extraction: the district court rejected a general foreign-ship exclusion but dismissed the barrier-removal claims because ship-specific construction/alteration guidelines had not issued. It allowed the remaining claims to proceed. Both sides appealed. The Fifth Circuit applied its categorical foreign-flag exclusion and did not decide the distinct regulatory-guidelines rationale. Reversal of that coverage ground must return that remaining rationale for appellate adjudication; it must not be described as an existing Court-approved exemption or a barrier-removal entitlement.

Passengers allege discriminatory fares and surcharges, access policies, inaccessible facilities and evacuation equipment, and association discrimination against companions. They seek declaratory and injunctive relief and allege expected future cruises. These are allegations at dismissal, not liability findings. Ordinary customer obligations can be separated from duties that materially regulate the internal order or permanent design of the vessel. Whether the clear-statement rule reaches each kind of duty, or instead requires uniform exclusion of Title III as a regime, is a live legal disagreement.

Title III's public-accommodation and specified-public-transportation provisions both matter. The statute's readily-achievable barrier-removal requirement, necessary reasonable policy modification with fundamental-alteration defense, auxiliary-aid provisions and their distinct qualifications must remain distinct. Actual safety and treaty conflicts require attention to the asserted duty; a possible conflict does not itself prove every domestic passenger claim unavailable. The federal agencies' proposed guidelines are not final rules. The argument that regulations are needed to enforce existing barrier removal remains for the lower court under the granted coverage disposition.

Entering law includes PGA TOUR v. Martin's recipient-specific coverage and individualized duty/defense analysis, Clark v. Martinez at its enacted-text scope, and predivergence Cunard, Benz, McCulloch, Ariadne and the Charming Betsy construction principle. Aramco concerns extraterritorial regulation rather than a categorical exemption for foreign entities present here. The distinction between uniform statutory meaning and activity-specific maritime limits must be tested rather than assumed resolved by Clark.

## Dodd — validated timing question

The official 2004 §2255 text confirms paragraph 6(3)'s identified date of initial Supreme Court recognition and its distinct new-right/retroactivity condition. Paragraph 6(4)'s supporting-fact discovery trigger and the successive-motion certification paragraph are separate text. Richardson initially recognized the asserted predicate-unanimity right on June 1, 1999; Dodd filed April 4, 2001. The asserted later circuit retroactivity determination occurred April 19, 2002. A later retroactivity ruling is not itself a new initial recognition of the Richardson right.

Two supported paths are initial-recognition timing, treating retroactivity as eligibility, and later-retroactivity timing, treating the clause as an integrated condition before the special year can operate. The latter addresses a period that otherwise may expire before a successive movant can qualify; the former gives effect to Congress's express date and preserves independent authorization. Current Johnson v. United States concerns an actual predicate vacatur under paragraph 6(4), not judicial recognition under paragraph 6(3). Its discovery-only rule adds no later-recognition date. Tyler permits express or logically compelled Supreme Court retroactivity holdings in its successive-application setting and supplies no general reset.

The supplied record does not establish an independent Supreme Court Richardson retroactivity holding. The accrual question can be decided assuming the qualification in Dodd's favor, so that uncertainty does not require a stop. The granted statutory question supplies no new extraordinary-impediment fact or preserved equitable-tolling entitlement. The district court's rejection of equity is part of the posture, not an unexamined new ground supplied by this stage.

## Graham County — validated statutory mismatch

The official 2004 text confirms that §3731(b) begins with an action-under-§3730 cross-reference, but paragraph (b)(1) measures six years from a violation of §3729. Paragraph (b)(2) separately uses the responsible federal official's knowledge and a ten-year outer limit; its terms are not a retaliation-accrual provision. Section 3730(h) independently protects the employee against listed forms of retaliation for qualifying lawful conduct, including investigation or assistance before an action is filed, and authorizes employee relief in district court.

McKenzie unanimously establishes the independent retaliation conditions; successful fraud recovery is unnecessary. The central alternatives are to apply the six-year federal period to the newly added retaliation remedy through the broad reference and contextual treatment of the incompatible trigger, or to construe the fraud-based clock as limited to fraud-enforcement actions and borrow an appropriate state period. North Star Steel requires ordinary state borrowing unless a federal analogue is clearly closer and significantly more suitable. Jones v. R. R. Donnelley supplies no §1658 period for this 1986 cause. The 1986 enactment's timing is not proof of an unprovided legislative statement.

Rejecting the federal period does not establish that the district court selected the correct North Carolina analogue or properly resolved accrual and tolling. Those matters return for adjudication. The retained independent retaliation claim does not turn on the disposition of the separate qui tam case.
