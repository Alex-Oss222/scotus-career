## Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys

**Event:** December 13, 2004; merits review of the Tenth Circuit judgment, lower-court No. 02-8059, 356 F.3d 1302.

**Provisional Associate judgment:** Eight votes to affirm the discharge. All eight may join the common rule and the application stated below. None requires an additional finding or a new hardship proceeding on the supplied record. There is no State-immunity question.

### Common sufficient ground and scope

Under the operative §523(a)(8), a debtor establishes undue hardship by a preponderance when repayment would prevent maintenance of a minimal standard of living for the debtor and dependents, concrete circumstances show that inability is likely to persist for a significant portion of the repayment period, and the debtor has made genuine good-faith efforts to address the obligation within the debtor's means and limitations. The Brunner framework is lawful when applied through these realistic inquiries. It does not require certainty of hopelessness, permanent total disability, or proof that improvement can never occur. An unsupported possibility of future improvement does not defeat supported evidence of durable inability.

Good faith requires an actual inquiry into efforts to obtain work, maximize reasonably available income, limit avoidable expenses and address repayment within the debtor's real abilities. It does not permit an extra-statutory judgment about whether the debtor chose an admirable life. Actual voluntary evasion or self-created inability may defeat the showing; nonpayment alone does not when payment was not reasonably possible. The rule neither excuses a debtor who merely prefers a lower income nor imposes an absolute payment prerequisite that an indigent debtor cannot satisfy. No fact showing deliberate creation of hardship is present here.

Polleys's stipulation that no medical or physical condition prevents her from retaining work is given effect. The Court does not find total inability to work or permanent total disability. The distinct bankruptcy findings concern the amount and reliability of income she can earn: emotional difficulties outside her control, medication adversely affecting memory and communication, low stress tolerance and employment difficulties, persistence into the foreseeable future, and inability to meet the debt even with modest improvement. Ability to retain some work is not equivalent to ability to earn enough for minimal living expenses for the debtor and her dependent and the required payment. Those supported findings, subsistence resources and absence of discretionary income establish present and durable repayment inability. The approximately $51,000 consolidated balance required about $420 monthly over twenty years; the analysis considers that actual duration without demanding proof that circumstances cannot improve during every year. These findings support the statutory conclusion under the ordinary civil burden. Factual findings remain subject to clear-error review; statutory meaning and the findings' legal sufficiency receive independent review.

ECMC's reliance on Polleys's departure from better-paying work to move to Wyoming is considered together with the complete efforts record. The move does not establish deliberate creation of hardship in light of the supported limitations, reduced household expenses through parental housing, later attempts at accounting employment and self-employment, unsuccessful CPA examinations, and pursuit of work outside her training. Her consolidation, participation in deferral programs, attempted negotiation after an earlier $800 monthly demand, and decision not to seek immediate discharge when repayment first came due independently strengthen the conclusion that she tried to address the obligation. The earlier $800 demand is not substituted for the approximately $420 consolidated payment at trial. Nonpayment remains relevant evidence, but the complete record does not support treating it or the move as conclusive bad faith.

The lower concurrence's proposal for evidence meeting reasonable medical probability and expert assessment of disability lasting the loan's full duration supplies the strongest evidentiary alternative. Ordinary evidentiary reliability and the preponderance burden remain applicable; the Court creates no exemption allowing unsupported self-diagnosis to establish enduring incapacity. But §523(a)(8) does not make medical disability an indispensable condition, add a universal expert-testimony requirement, or demand a medical prediction covering the whole loan. The supported earning-capacity findings and actual employment history permit the significant-period conclusion here. No supplied evidentiary objection or missing necessary finding requires remand under that lawful standard.

Hood supplies the debtor's continuing burden to establish the education-debt exception. Lamie requires fidelity to enacted Code conditions and therefore cuts both ways: fresh-start policy cannot erase undue hardship, and a creditor cannot add an impossibility or permanent-disability condition absent from the statute. Grogan supplies the ordinary preponderance standard, not the content of this particular exception. Brunner supplies a useful lower-court framework; its persuasive status before this decision is not mistaken for a prior Supreme Court command. The general statutory-burden discipline of KP and enacted-text discipline of Koons are compatible current-term law, but neither selects the hardship standard.

The holding resolves the standard challenged here and its application to this record. It does not require evaluation of a newly invented repayment option or a modern administrative program. It makes no decision about partial discharge or subsequent amendments, neither of which supplies a ground of the requested judgment.

### Two lawful paths and the material counterarguments

**Path P1 — affirm under realistic Brunner.** This gives operative effect to both nondischargeability and its express exception. Its strongest objection is that treating ordinary financial difficulty and no payment as sufficient would turn student loans into ordinary dischargeable debt. The objection fails on this record because the persistent, supported impairment and subsistence findings do more than show temporary unemployment or preference, while the work efforts address good faith. The rule requires concrete duration evidence and real efforts; it does not presume hardship from filing bankruptcy.

**Path P2 — reverse or remand under a stricter formulation or contrary application.** A court could lawfully reverse if sustained inability were omitted, the capacity stipulation contradicted a necessary finding, or the move established deliberate hardship; remand could follow a genuinely absent necessary finding. The strongest support is the statutory word undue, the capacity stipulation, the previous better-paying work, and the lower concurrence's concern that an undefined significant period invites inconsistent discharge. Those points require disciplined proof, but the stipulation concerns ability to retain work rather than adequate earning capacity, and the full employment and repayment history defeats a finding of contrived hardship. The supported persistent limitations and twenty-year payment obligation permit a significant-period assessment without absolute hopelessness or an expert prediction spanning the entire loan. Ordinary reliability requirements continue to apply. A new categorical medical-probability or full-duration requirement is not supplied by the statutory text or controlling law. No Associate adopts P2 on this complete record.

## Timken Co. v. United States

**Event:** January 11, 2005; merits review of the Federal Circuit's judgment, 354 F.3d 1334, on zeroing only.

**Provisional Associate judgment:** Eight votes to affirm the challenged judgment on the zeroing question. All eight can join the specific delegation, statutory-permission and international-material holdings below. Scalia's broader position in Mead remains a prior published position; it is unnecessary to the narrower sufficient ground here.

### Common sufficient ground and scope

Koyo's domestic statutory challenge is reviewable. Section 3512(c) does not bar an appeal asserting that Commerce misconstrued United States antidumping law and using international obligations as interpretive support. That claim is distinct from an independent private claim to enforce the Uruguay Round agreements or directly execute a WTO report. The Court rejects the asserted §3512(c) bar before resolving the merits; all eight Associates join that threshold conclusion.

The operative antidumping provisions permit Commerce, in this administrative review, to aggregate positive dumping margins while treating above-normal-value transactions as zero rather than as offsets. They do not command that method. The United States and Timken's primary mandatory-reading argument is expressly rejected: exceeds can support a positive-excess approach, but a signed mathematical difference can also be negative, and the aggregate definition supplies no unequivocal command to discard those differences. The statutory structure and identified legislative materials do not make zeroing compulsory. The Government's alternative permissible-choice argument remains available. Sections 1677(35)(A) and (B) define the dumping margin and its weighted average through an excess of normal value over export price and aggregation of margins. They reasonably permit Commerce to treat transactions showing no such excess as supplying no positive dumping amount. The definition of the aggregate does not clearly require those transactions to cancel the dumping established on other sales.

Section 1677b(a)'s fair-comparison direction remains binding. Commerce must make the comparisons and adjustments that the statute requires; methodological discretion cannot excuse violation of those commands. Considering that direction together with the aggregate definition and this review's method does not establish a requirement of negative offsets. The Court does not adopt the lower court's categorical proposition that fair comparison can never bear on aggregation or can only identify enumerated normal-value adjustments. The narrower sufficient ground is that the binding fair-comparison command, properly considered here, does not make this particular delegated method unreasonable. Koyo's strongest objection is that ignoring transactions above normal value produces a higher weighted average than netting all price differences. That identifies the practical consequence of the method, not a statutory instruction requiring every such transaction to negate dumping elsewhere. The statute's transaction-sensitive excess definition leaves the specific choice open, and Commerce's method reasonably measures the dumped sales within that choice.

The delegation is concrete. Section 1675(a) assigns Commerce the administrative review and determination of the duties, including the statutory normal-value, export-price and margin calculations. Commerce exercised that assignment through the review's final determination governing duty liability. This is an adjudicative determination under the prescribed statutory mechanism, not merely an advisory statement or informal classification letter. Sections 1677(35) and 1677b supply enforceable boundaries on that assignment. Judicial review under §1516a tests those boundaries and the lawful support for the determination; it does not transfer the permissible methodological choice to a court.

Under Haggar and Mead, those features establish actual delegated authority and its exercise before Chevron's reasonable-choice inquiry. Barnhart v. Thomas confirms that upholding a permissible implementation does not make it the only permissible statutory reading. Expertise, complexity and consistency may illuminate the reasonableness of the choice, but none independently establishes its legal force. This reasoning neither overrules Mead nor makes all agency interpretations controlling.

The WTO report concerning the European Community's bed-linen investigation is considered for its persuasive reasoning, including its concern that all transactions be reflected in a fair comparison. Koyo's contention that the methods are functionally equivalent and that the investigation/review distinction is immaterial is addressed on that basis. The report neither binds the United States determination nor establishes that the pertinent agreement provisions require the same aggregation rule in this administrative review. Its all-transactions reasoning supports an alternative method, but does not eliminate the domestic statute's permitted positive-excess construction and entry-based duty administration. The fact that the United States was not a party explains the absence of binding effect; it does not make the reasoning irrelevant.

Charming Betsy preserves interpretation consistent with international obligations where the statutory and international-law premises support it. Here the report concerns an investigation and does not definitively establish the asserted international obligation governing this distinct administrative review; neither the report nor the domestic fair-comparison text removes the permissible methodological choice. The Uruguay Round Agreements Act preserves its own domestic operation, but §3512(c) does not defeat Koyo's statutory claim or its interpretive use of international materials. The result rests on the considered domestic construction and reasonable method, not a categorical denial of international-law relevance. The Court need not reach the separate §3533 preclusion contention. It approves no other investigation or review method in advance.

### Two lawful paths and the material counterarguments

**Path T1 — sustain permissible zeroing.** Congress delegated the determination and left the identified aggregation choice open; Commerce's method fits the defined excess and the duty-calculation task. The strongest contrary argument invokes fair comparison and the aggregate as a whole: selecting positive differences may appear to measure something other than net exporter dumping. The explicit definition and distinct comparison/aggregation functions nevertheless leave room for the chosen treatment, and no required adjustment or comparable input is shown to have been disregarded.

**Path T2 — require offsets and return for recalculation.** This would be lawful if the aggregate and fair-comparison provisions, construed with the pertinent international obligations, required netting negative differences or made zeroing unreasonable here. Koyo's argument is fully available in this domestic appeal. The statutory text and report's persuasive reasoning do not establish that result for this review. The report resolves an investigation and does not conclusively identify the alleged obligation applicable to this administrative-review method; the domestic definition and entry-based administration continue to support the chosen approach. No Associate adopts T2 on the supplied record.

**Path T3 — hold zeroing mandatory.** The Government and Timken's strongest argument is that exceeds means a positive excess, supported by the statute's anti-dumping function. All eight reject a compulsory construction because signed differences also fit the calculation language and the aggregate provision does not expressly select only positive values. Permission suffices to affirm. This separate rejection is compatible with the recorded statutory-boundary approach of Lamie, Mead and Barnhart v. Thomas.

**Separate threshold alternative — no delegated interpretive force.** If the mechanism were only an advisory interpretation, Mead would require independent construction with Skidmore consideration; denial of controlling weight would not itself prohibit zeroing or require a new agency proceeding. That alternative fails here because the identified final administrative-review determination exercises the statutory duty-setting assignment. The commitments depend on that supplied mechanism, not on an assumption that every Commerce communication carries the same authority.

## Competing lawful readings and their consequences

**Independent agreement offense.** The provision identifies the prohibited conspiracy, its specified unlawful objects and the resulting punishment. The ordinary agreement baseline described in Nash and Singer supplies meaning to the chosen term; section 371 demonstrates that Congress knows how to demand a further act when it wishes. Shabani is a close and unanimously accepted analogy, rather than a substitute for reading this statute. The strongest contrary argument is that a clause phrased in terms of the same penalties primarily enhances an already-defined section 371 offense. But a provision can define liability and prescribe punishment in the same sentence; its direct identification of conspirators is operative, and no cross-reference incorporates the general statute's additional condition.

**Incorporated section 371 offense.** This reading would retain an overt-act safeguard and make the penalty provision apply to a conspiracy otherwise defined by section 371. Its strongest support is the defendants' placement, history and venue argument. Its decisive weakness is the absence of an operative incorporation and the meaningful textual contrast with Congress's explicit overt-act statutes. A later venue rule may use conduct as a geographic connector without making conduct an additional guilt element or retroactively altering the earlier enactment. The Court does not decide that venue rule's entire reach. All eight reject the proposed extra element; no harmless-error adjudication is needed for omitting an instruction the law did not require.

**Lenity.** Ratzlaf preserves lenity for genuine ambiguity remaining after ordinary construction; it does not require an added element whenever litigants offer competing readings. The statute-specific analysis resolves the present choice. Ratzlaf's distinct knowledge-of-illegality holding for willful structuring does not migrate into this laundering-conspiracy ruling.

## 6. The separate risk-factor ruling does not decide this disagreement

The Tennessee Supreme Court expressly rejected the risk-to-others aggravator and characterized its inclusion as harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. The eight preserve that as an actual state ruling about that factor. No Justice treats it as a determination that a heinousness error was harmless. Nor do the six reopen the risk-factor issue under the guise of this grant or declare its harmlessness explanation constitutionally sufficient solely because it used the proper burden's words. Any independently pending challenge to it remains governed by its own scope and predicates.

For the heinousness question, the existence of other valid aggravators and the strength of evidence are not a substitute for actual cure when a reviewable constitutional weighing error is established. Conversely, cure is not required merely because a party alleges an error: the violation and review prerequisites must first exist. The State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden governs an available nonstructural error's effect, not whether every allegation crosses section 2254 or establishes a substantive violation.
