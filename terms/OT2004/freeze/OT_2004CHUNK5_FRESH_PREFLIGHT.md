### Entering law and alternative paths

Taylor supplies the statutory generic-burglary inquiry and its focus on what the conviction necessarily established. The live statutory fork is whether the identified plea establishes generic burglary through appropriate conviction materials, or whether reliable police reports and complaint applications may show the shared understanding of a broader charge. The United States' strongest supported position is that an adequately established shared understanding distinguishes a limited identification of what was admitted from a new trial of the conduct. Shepard's strongest answer is that reliability of an investigative account does not establish adoption of its contents, especially where a broader statute and charge did not require the building fact and that adoption is disputed. Neither position may assume that a police account was expressly incorporated when the record handoff does not establish that fact.

Gatewood, effective June 30, 2003, controls the constitutional allocation for necessary adult-recidivist facts raising a binding maximum or minimum. The entering Standards and Tests entry is **Facts changing binding punishment ranges**, with Gatewood supplying the adult-predicate, qualification, chronology, and federal-charge components; its prior present-offense components retain their dates. A valid old conviction proves what it actually adjudicated. Judicial statutory interpretation and legal classification of established facts remain distinct from finding disputed punishment-authorizing facts. Applicable charging protection, valid element admission, lawful waiver, and the proper factfinder's reasonable-doubt determination cannot be replaced by reliability alone.

Booker, January 12, 2005, confirms the constitutional rule and retains the binding Guidelines in lawful applications. Sections 3553(b)(1), 3742(e), and their enacted review/remand structure remain effective to that extent. Unsupported enhancements are withheld; ordinary discretion and lawful departures operate within independently authorized punishment. The packet's conditional suggestion that no effective Booker decision is known must not survive into modeling. An advisory-Guidelines remedy is not entering law.

The statutory and constitutional inquiries remain separable. Excluding unadopted investigative material under Taylor can support disposition of the presented evidentiary question without conclusively deciding every federal charge or waiver issue. Conversely, permitting a defined conviction-document inquiry would not itself authorize the First Circuit's enhancement direction: the resulting enhancement would still require compliance with Gatewood and Booker. A position seeking to replace their constitutional allocation must identify that reconsideration expressly rather than treat an unrestricted prior-conviction exception as existing law. The supplied Gatewood and Booker public opinions and separate positions support the relevant preexisting jurisprudential alternatives; this receipt assigns none of them to any Justice here.

## Bounded addendum — City of Sherrill

### Record and full competing presentation

The source handoff identifies three Sherrill actions: the Nation's declaration action, the removed tax-sale/eviction action, and the City's action against Tribal Council members. The district court entered separate final judgments, granted the Nation summary judgment in the lead and eviction actions, dismissed the City's counterclaims and member action, denied the City's request to restrain further acquisition, and denied leave to amend; the Second Circuit affirmed the Sherrill judgments. The separate Madison County remand does not enter this mandate.

Both parties describe ordinary 1997–1998 purchases of business parcels and the City's subsequent assessments, nonpayment, and collection/eviction measures. Both trace the parcels through the 1805 conveyance to Cornelius Dockstader and 1807 conveyance to Peter Smith. The legal applicability of federal restrictions and the consequences of any unauthorized transfer remain contested; the common title chronology is not an agreement with either party's legal theory.

The City's actual principal paths must remain visible:

1. It argues that the state-origin reservation lacks the federal set-aside and superintendence relationship and proposes applying Venetie's requirements to all §1151 categories. The Nation relies on the reserved character of the 1788 cession and the federal treaty guarantees, supervision, and dispute-resolution arrangements. Entering Venetie concerns §1151(b) dependent communities and expressly leaves reservation and allotment categories distinct. Extension beyond that category would need a reasoned new holding; it cannot be attributed to the earlier case.
2. It argues that the 1838 Treaty, removal arrangements, Kansas lands, and later compensation demonstrate disestablishment. The Nation distinguishes the Wisconsin-interest exchange, conditional Oneida arrangements, nonremoval provisions, and the explicit Seneca/Tuscarora conveyances. Hagen and Yankton require clear intent from the operative instrument in context, without mandatory magic words or a fixed-payment formula. Later demographics and local governance remain subordinate evidence.
3. It argues that a historical lapse in tribal existence has legal consequences. The Nation answers with present recognition and succession, continuing federal treatment, and its cited historical evidence. The present recognized status and an asserted earlier lapse must not be conflated. The briefs' competing assessments are not fresh judicial findings about extinct or revived identity.
4. It invokes local fiscal and governmental disruption and the trust-acquisition procedure. The Nation distinguishes equitable restrictions on ejecting current possessors from prospective tax protection for land it already owns. This issue must be tested as a possible ground with its own legal basis and remedial consequences, rather than a previously adopted categorical bar or a previously foreclosed proposal.

### Instruments and doctrinal limits

The copied A136–A184 text supplies the complete reproduced 1788 agreement, Canandaigua Treaty, Buffalo Creek Treaty with attached conveyances and assents, and §§1151, 177, 233, and former 465. The entire reproduced Buffalo Creek instrument was read. Article 1's Wisconsin cession and exception, Article 2's western-land consideration, Article 3's nonacceptance/removal consequence for that western interest, Article 13's satisfactory-arrangements condition for the New York Oneidas, and the distinct express conveyance provisions for other tribes must retain their actual subjects. One provision's exception cannot be moved to another.

Section 233 grants New York civil jurisdiction with distinct qualifications concerning preserved tribal law/custom, hunting and fishing, reservation taxation, execution, alienation, and pre-September-13-1952 transactions. The tribal-member land-use/possession exception qualifies execution; it is not a general tax authorization. The statute's refusal to authorize taxation is not automatically a new universal exemption from every independently authorized tax. Former §465 authorizes discretionary acquisition and trust title, with express tax exemption for interests acquired under its terms. It supplies a lawful mechanism but does not by its own wording make trust acquisition the exclusive possible source of every preexisting reservation protection.

The entering Standards and Tests entries are **State taxation of qualifying General Allotment Act fee land** (Yakima, January 14, 1992), **Taxation after completed Nelson Act disposal and tribal repurchase** (Cass County, June 8, 1998), and **Survival of separately retained treaty use rights** (Mille Lacs, March 24, 1999). Yakima and Cass County require their actual statutory land-tax authorization and qualifying parcel chain. They do not establish a general alienability rule, boundary change, personal jurisdiction, or permission for every collection device. Mille Lacs concerns the actual treaty rights retained, not a universal identity between use rights and all territorial powers.

The same excerpt shows that the City identified laches, estoppel, waiver, limitations, unclean hands, and consent among the issues for which it sought discovery in opposing summary judgment. The lower court found the stated discovery need inadequate in the discussion supplied. Thus equitable defenses were actually mentioned below; the preflight must not assert their complete absence. This partial excerpt establishes neither complete later preservation nor waiver, and does not establish the ultimate truth of any defense.

Affirmance can protect the identified reacquired parcels against these taxes and associated measures without resolving every regulatory power. Reversal on a treaty/status ground requires the supported construction and its parcel-specific consequence. A new equitable restriction requires an articulated doctrine, its actual factual predicate, an answer to the prospective-owned-land distinction, and an explanation of the effects on tax liability and effective relief. Recognizing practical reliance does not silently enact a congressional authorization. Nor does the source-specific tax rule mechanically resolve whether a separately supported equitable limit may be adopted.

If remand is chosen, it must name the actual unresolved legal or record predicate and required work. It may not invite an unsupported search for a disposal statute merely because the City lost under existing law. The three Sherrill judgments, tax incidence, collection remedy, and any member-action immunity must be traced separately to the adopted ground. No automatic foreclosure, retrospective ejectment, transfer of title, compulsory trust application, or resolution of the Madison County dispute follows from a tax ruling alone.

## Bounded addendum — Jackson

### Pleading status and neutral framing correction

The United States' contemporaneous substantive amicus brief supports the stated complaint history and lower-court grounds. Jackson alleges retaliation after complaints about unequal treatment of the girls' basketball team, negative evaluations, removal from coaching in May 2001, and loss of coaching pay while retaining his teaching job. These are allegations accepted for the categorical dismissal question, not findings of the underlying discrimination, retaliatory causation, institutional responsibility, coverage, or loss.

The Eleventh Circuit rejected the statutory theory, the distinct §1682/regulatory theory, and alternatively protection for a person complaining of discrimination against others. The complaint invokes the statute itself. Jackson's personal coaching and pay injury must remain distinct from any attempt to assert the students' damages.

The original packet's formulation under **Current Reusable Doctrine** that directs identification of intentional adverse treatment because a person opposed sex discrimination must be read as the proposed retaliation construction to be tested, not already controlling law. Whether retaliation on that basis falls within §1681 is the question presented. Likewise, the claim that proof of the girls' underlying discrimination is unnecessary must be confined to the stated pleading question; the packet cannot silently establish a comprehensive protected-activity rule for every mistaken complaint, a new causal standard, or a burden-shifting system.

### Existing authority and strongest alternatives

Cannon supplies the established private Title IX action. The entering **Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination** rule dates to Franklin, February 26, 1992, with recipient attribution supplied only at the scopes of later cases. Franklin permits compensation for proved intentional statutory discrimination and rejects a categorical funding-notice objection; it does not answer whether this conduct is within the statute. Pennhurst's funding-condition notice principle remains relevant to that construction.

The **Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX** entry has present operation effective June 22, 1998 for Gebser's additional component. Actual notice to a corrective official is not the exclusive teacher route; the material-authority-plus-recipient-fault conjunction remains limited. The separate **Recipient responsibility for peer harassment under Title IX** entry, effective May 24, 1999, states Davis's sufficient controlled-peer route. Neither third-party framework is automatically a prerequisite for a recipient's own alleged retaliatory decision. Conversely, direct-retaliation characterization does not establish that the Board actually made or is legally responsible for the act.

Powell/Sandoval and South Camden retain a bounded direct prospective action in the particular Title VI regulatory context. Their Standards and Tests entry, **Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations**, preserves distinct operative dates of April 3, 2000, April 24, 2001, and May 28, 2002. Those holdings neither impose the historical categorical no-action rule described in the amicus brief nor automatically create a Title IX damages action from a regulation. Historical descriptions in that brief must be replaced with the supplied entering law wherever inconsistent.

The reversal path reads intentional punishment because a complaint concerns sex discrimination as discrimination within §1681's broad language. Sullivan supplies a pre-enactment advocacy-retaliation analogy protecting a person injured for asserting another's rights; Cannon and Franklin supply existing action and remedial structure. The longstanding antiretaliation rule and effective-enforcement purpose support the construction. The strongest objection is that the immediate reason for adverse treatment is complaining, not the person's sex; an explicit Title VII retaliation clause and spending notice may counsel against extending the received action.

The affirmance path treats retaliation as outside the statutory prohibition or the received action, distinguishing its target and causal basis from direct sex discrimination and Sullivan's property context. It must answer the breadth of the statute's protection of persons, the absence of an own-sex formulation, the pre-enactment analogy, and the significance of allowing a recipient to punish those who bring sex discrimination to light. Applying this timeline's Sandoval requires its actual statute-specific analysis; its displaced historical holding cannot supply the answer.

The United States actually proposes a narrower sex-subject causal rationale: retaliation against complainers without regard to what they complain about would fall outside that rationale. This is an attributed litigation position, not an adopted judicial exception or established fact about the Board. The copied regulation separately covers interference with protected rights and retaliation for a complaint, testimony, assistance, or participation in a covered investigation, proceeding, or hearing. Its broader terms must not be merged into the statute by assumption. The United States expressly offers direct statutory construction without needing deference or a regulation-only cause of action.

### Actual work after disposition

Reversal of categorical dismissal permits ordinary proceedings on the pleaded recipient claim. The lower courts must establish coverage where genuinely contested, the Board's legally attributable conduct, the complaint and retaliatory connection, supported defenses, causal loss, and the basis and extent of any monetary or employment relief. It orders no immediate coaching reinstatement, damages sum, liability finding, or compulsory trial. Affirmance on the statutory scope issue would resolve the appealed Title IX claim, without silently disposing of independently preserved claims not within this review.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent modeling of statutory scope, protected claimant, notice, and the bounded pleading disposition. No new finding or reconstructed filing is needed. Any materially broader protected-activity, causation, regulatory-action, or employment-remedy proposition requires its own date-eligible support and adversarial consideration.
