## Complete statutory architecture used in the renewed assessment

Section 770.3a's provisions perform different work. The section's default denies appointed appellate counsel to defendants pleading guilty, guilty but mentally ill, or no contest, subject to subsections (2) and (3). Four independent circumstances require appointment for an indigent plea defendant: the prosecutor seeks leave to appeal; the sentence exceeds the upper limit of the minimum range under the applicable sentencing guidelines; either Michigan appellate court grants the defendant's leave application; or the defendant seeks leave from a conditional plea under Michigan Court Rule 6.301(C)(2) or its successor. The conditional-plea cross-reference is retained in full, and no fact establishes that this plea falls within it.

Subsection (3) permits, but does not require, appointment only when all three supplied conditions hold: the defendant seeks leave concerning alleged improper scoring of an offense variable or prior-record variable; the scoring objection was made or the issue otherwise preserved; and the imposed sentence departs upward from the upper limit of the minimum range the defendant alleges should have been scored. The last comparison is with the alleged correct range, whereas subsection (2)(b) concerns the applicable guidelines range. A general sentencing complaint does not establish all three conditions, and eligibility under subsection (3) is not a mandatory appointment.

Subsection (4) separately commands advice before plea acceptance that, except as otherwise provided in the section, acceptance means waiver of publicly appointed assistance in filing a leave application or pursuing other postconviction remedies; the court must determine whether the defendant understands that waiver. It also commands provision at sentencing of a nontechnical, easily understood leave-application form. These are legal duties. Their enactment does not establish that each was actually performed in this case, that the form was furnished, or that a federal constitutional waiver occurred. Equally, the statutory advice duty is relevant to the context of the reported plea proceeding and cannot simply be omitted.

The supplied record supports no independent finding that Halbert met an unaddressed statutory exception. The renewed decision therefore concerns constitutional assistance in the first-tier process and the actual waiver defense; it does not grant relief under an invented exception predicate. The precise statute is mechanically copied from the fresh neutral handoff in the annex below.

## Components and operative commitments

**R1 — Entitlement in the initial process:** The Fourteenth Amendment requires appointed advocacy for an indigent plea-convicted defendant seeking this first appellate examination of alleged error. An equivalent advocate-assisted screening procedure may perform that function; appointed counsel need not advance frivolous grounds. The four mandatory and one conjunctive discretionary appointment categories do not, on the supplied denial and process, provide such assistance to prepare Halbert's initial application. In particular, counsel appointed after leave has been granted acts after the challenged initial merits-screening decision. A simple application form, even if furnished, does not itself identify and present legal issues or supply an advocate's legal assessment.

**R2 — Actual waiver:** A plea does not automatically waive the distinct counsel assistance needed for initial appellate review. A knowing, intelligent, and voluntary surrender can be established from the whole record without special words or a personal judicial recital of every consequence. On this reported record, the combination of advice about loss of appeal as of right, descriptions of the affirmative appointment categories, Halbert's confirmations, counsel's general compliance answers, and the general plea-validity finding does not sufficiently establish intentional informed surrender of the separate assistance at issue. His confirmed understanding of the matters described does not establish the missing residual consequence solely by presuming that subsection (4)'s full duty must have been performed.

**R3 — Relief:** Vacate the denial of the uncounseled leave application and remand for constitutionally adequate first-tier consideration with appointed assistance. Robbins's rule restores an omitted advocacy function without preliminary proof that a particular appellate argument probably would prevail. Ordinary issue preservation and adequate frivolousness screening govern the restored process, and the conviction remains in force during it.

**R4 — Contrary sufficient ground:** Scalia and Thomas would affirm because the State's discretionary first-tier leave procedure for plea convictions falls outside Douglas's appeal-as-of-right entitlement and within the permission they read Ross to recognize. The new exceptions strengthen the description of this selective process but do not change that ground. They do not adopt an actual-waiver alternative holding. Their affirmance therefore supplies no vote for a finding that this particular record establishes surrender.

## Mechanical annex — complete operative statute from the validated neutral refresh

```text
Sec. 3a. (1) Except as provided in subsections (2) and (3), a defendant who pleads guilty, guilty but mentally ill, or
nolo contendere shall not have appellate counsel appointed for review of the defendant's conviction or sentence.

    (2) The trial court shall appoint appellate counsel for an indigent defendant who pleads guilty, guilty but mentally
ill, or nolo contendere if any of the following apply:

    (a) The prosecuting attorney seeks leave to appeal.
    (b) The defendant's sentence exceeds the upper limit of the minimum sentence range of the applicable sentencing
guidelines.
    (c) The court of appeals or the supreme court grants the defendant's application for leave to appeal.
    (d) The defendant seeks leave to appeal a conditional plea under Michigan Court Rule 6.301(C)(2) or its successor
rule.

    (3) The trial court may appoint appellate counsel for an indigent defendant who pleads guilty, guilty but mentally ill,
or nolo contendere if all of the following apply:

    (a) The defendant seeks leave to appeal a sentence based upon an alleged improper scoring of an offense variable or
a prior record variable.

    (b) The defendant objected to the scoring or otherwise preserved the matter for appeal.

    (c) The sentence imposed by the court constitutes an upward departure from the upper limit of the minimum
sentence range that the defendant alleges should have been scored.

    (4) While establishing that a plea of guilty, guilty but mentally ill, or nolo contendere was made understandingly and
voluntarily under Michigan Court Rule 6.302 or its successor rule, and before accepting the plea, the court shall advise
the defendant that, except as otherwise provided in this section, if the plea is accepted by the court, the defendant
waives the right to have an attorney appointed at public expense to assist in filing an application for leave to appeal or
to assist with other postconviction remedies, and shall determine whether the defendant understands the waiver. Upon
sentencing, the court shall furnish the defendant with a form developed by the state court administrative office that is
nontechnical and easily understood and that the defendant may complete and file as an application for leave to appeal.
```
