## Polleys: supported facts, arguments and qualifications

**Statutory correction.** Section 523(a)(8) addresses undue hardship on the debtor **and** the debtor's dependents. The original neutral packet's shortened description using “or” must not be carried forward. The existing commitment formulation already uses the conjunctive relationship. The pre-2005 statute controls; no later repayment or discharge program is supplied.

**Capacity stipulation and distinct findings.** Polleys stipulated that she had no medical or physical condition preventing retention of work. The lower court distinguished that stipulation from an absence of conditions affecting the ability to work or earn substantial income. The bankruptcy findings identify debilitating emotional difficulties outside her control, necessary medication adversely affecting memory and communication, emotional outbursts and low stress tolerance affecting earning ability, persistence into the foreseeable future, and inability to repay $51,000 plus interest even with modest income improvement. The stipulation must be given effect: the record does not establish complete inability to retain any work or permanent total disability. The separate supported findings concern earning capacity and durable insufficient resources, rather than contradicting the stipulation by declaring all employment impossible.

**Time and amount.** At trial, Polleys was 45 and had a teenage daughter. She obtained her accounting degree in 1993. The consolidated balance was approximately $51,000, requiring about $420 each month over twenty years. Since 1997 her annual income had ranged from approximately $3,000 to $16,000; the reported recent household resources were approximately $9,800, including child support and part-time earnings. She and her daughter depended on family housing without rent or ordinary utilities, had no emergency budget, and lived below the poverty threshold. These are reported trial-period facts, not current income findings as of the scheduled Supreme Court event.

**Departure from work and genuine repayment efforts.** ECMC relies not only on no payments but also on Polleys's departure from better-paying employment to move to Wyoming and live with her parents. That asserted choice is a real counterargument requiring consideration; a move cannot simply be omitted from good-faith analysis. The lower record also reports consolidation, participation in deferral programs, and attempted negotiation when creditors demanded $800 monthly. That earlier demand is distinct from the approximately $420 consolidated payment at trial. She did not seek discharge immediately when repayment first came due. She tried accounting employment and self-employment, unsuccessfully attempted the CPA examination, and sought jobs outside accounting, including nursery work from which she was laid off. These facts support evaluation of actual repayment and earning efforts rather than inferring good faith solely from present poverty.

**Lower separate view.** Judge Lucero concurred in the result but proposed an objective reasonable-medical-probability standard for evidence used to establish medical disability. He also preferred medical evidence addressing disability over the loan's duration instead of a potentially indeterminate significant-period inquiry. He allowed nonmedical factors to contribute to the ultimate hardship determination. This view supplies a distinct evidentiary and duration alternative; it is not a finding that the bankruptcy evidence fails, nor a Supreme Court evidentiary rule. The neutral model must assess whether §523(a)(8), ordinary proof principles and the supported findings require that added condition, or whether competent evidence and a realistic significant-period forecast suffice.

**Lawful paths.** Affirmance remains available if the supported earning-capacity findings satisfy the significant-period inquiry despite the ability to retain some work, and if the full repayment and employment history establishes genuine good faith despite the move and nonpayment. A contrary application is possible if the stipulation actually defeats a necessary finding or if the move establishes deliberate hardship; neither conclusion may be presumed from the isolated fact. An evidence-specific remand is available only if the chosen lawful standard identifies a genuinely missing material finding. Adoption of the lower concurrence's special proof or full-loan-duration requirement must be justified independently rather than attributed to the existing Supreme Court law. No source gap requires a presumed disability finding or an invented repayment option.

## Timken: domestic cause, mandatory-reading contention and international interpretation

**Domestic review remains available.** Koyo brought a domestic statutory challenge to Commerce's determination and invokes international obligations to aid construction of domestic law. It does not seek relief on an independent URAA cause or a direct right to enforce the WTO report. Section 3512(c) therefore does not bar this appeal. Its restriction on direct private agreement-based claims remains distinct from the presented domestic-law claim. The corrected neutral framing must not treat the claim as jurisdictionally excluded or suggest that its interpretive use of WTO material is impermissible.

**Mandatory versus permissible zeroing.** The United States and Timken argue first that the word “exceeds,” statutory structure and legislative materials compel zeroing; alternatively they argue that zeroing is a reasonable delegated choice. The Federal Circuit rejects the mandatory reading. It recognizes that a mathematical excess can be expressed as a signed difference and finds no unambiguous command that only positive margins count. The same wording nevertheless supports the agency's permissible positive-excess construction. These are separate conclusions: the source supports a permissible-method affirmance without adopting the proponents' claim of statutory compulsion.

**Actual method and statutory mechanism.** Commerce's final results and accompanying decision memorandum address the 1998–1999 review. Individual dumping margins are aggregated into the weighted-average margin, and the duty process operates through the prescribed entry-based administration. Congress's assignment and Commerce's final determination remain the identified mechanism for the Mead/Haggar threshold. The lower court's general references to expertise and longstanding practice are not substitutes for that threshold in this timeline. No new defect in an actual comparison input has been supplied.

**Fair comparison and the WTO report.** Koyo contends that omitting negative margins distorts the aggregate and conflicts with the statutory fair-comparison language implementing the international agreement. The WTO report concerns an investigation of European Community imports of Indian bed linen, and the lower source reports its view that all transactions must be accounted for. Koyo asserts that the practices are functionally alike and that the investigation/review distinction does not defeat the argument. Those arguments are relevant to statutory meaning and reasonableness even though the report is not binding in this case.

The Federal Circuit offers a broad reading confining the fair-comparison requirement to enumerated normal-value adjustments, and alternatively finds zeroing reasonable even with the report considered. The neutral model must distinguish these grounds. Affirmance does not require a categorical statement that fair comparison can never bear on aggregation. A bounded rationale may consider the command and report on their merits while concluding that neither requires offsets in this administrative review. It may not treat lack of binding effect as lack of persuasive relevance.

**Unneeded preclusion theory and excluded cross-appeal.** The Government and Timken also invoked §3533; the Federal Circuit did not decide that preclusion theory after resolving the substantive claim. No §3533 preclusion issue needs to be reached here. The entered-value versus sales-value adverse-facts dispute is described in the source but remains outside the identified zeroing grant. No failure to provide information in that separate branch may be imported as a waiver or default of Koyo's zeroing claim.

**Lawful paths.** Reject the §3512(c) bar and resolve the domestic merits. On the merits, the Court may sustain zeroing as a permissible delegated method after considering fair comparison and the report; require offsets if ordinary construction establishes that command; or adopt mandatory zeroing only if the statutory text actually compels it. Absence of controlling deference, if the threshold were not met, would require independent construction and persuasive consideration rather than automatic prohibition of zeroing. The supplied final-review mechanism remains sufficient for the threshold question.
