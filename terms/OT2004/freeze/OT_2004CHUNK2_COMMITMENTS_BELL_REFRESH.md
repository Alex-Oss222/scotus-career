## 1. Common conclusions now established

**All eight Associates reject the lack-of-state-narrowing premise on the supplied record.** The 1984 Tennessee Supreme Court actually applied the narrowed heinousness category; this is no longer reserved for another inquiry. Dicks identifies unnecessarily torturous, conscienceless or pitiless killing. Pritchett requires the relevant predeath torture or sufficiently grave depravity and excludes instantaneous death from its torture branch. Melson explains why defensive injuries, awareness, fear, resistance and repeated crushing blows can establish that narrowed category. Cone's direct opinion did the same substantive work: it identified defensive injuries, resistance, multiple crushing blows, noninstantaneous deaths and the terror caused by the sequential killings. This is a concrete application of the narrowing construction, not merely repetition of the statutory adjectives. The parties' references to Pritchett and Melson corroborate the setting but are not substituted for the court's actual analysis.

An omitted Dicks citation therefore cannot sustain the Sixth Circuit's writ. Early and Visciotti require substantive whole-adjudication review; Sochor's actual-judicial-narrowing holding confirms that a case-name formula is unnecessary when the substantive construction is applied. The eight also reject the converse rule that mere familiarity or a missing citation conclusively establishes application. The dated state passage, rather than an abstract presumption, supplies the decisive application here.

**All eight recognize section 2254(b)(2)'s independent permission.** A court may deny a habeas claim on its merits notwithstanding nonexhaustion; it need not decide exhaustion first and is not required to use that route. Thus the dispute over prior determination, actual adjudication and the federal district court's default treatment does not itself require a remand before the Court can reject this lack-of-narrowing theory. This portion fails even without giving the State the benefit of a disputed section 2254(d) merits predicate. Nothing in section 2254(b)(2) proves that every other constitutional theory fails or creates a merits adjudication that did not occur.

**The remaining material disagreement concerns the scope of final denial and mandate.** All eight remove the existing writ. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer vacate and remand for a confined inquiry into the already asserted, distinct jury-guidance and weighing issue, subject to actual claim identity, review barriers and the proper federal-law cutoff. Scalia and Thomas would reverse the reviewed aggravator-based writ and end this reviewed claim on the actual-narrowing merits, returning only genuinely independent remaining claims under the 2002 Bell mandate. They would not reopen this claim merely to seek an additional curative formula. The six's remand and the two's final rejection are different mandates, despite agreement that the existing writ cannot stand.

## 2. The six-Justice controlling candidate: bounded remand after actual narrowing is resolved

The six vote to **vacate the Sixth Circuit's aggravator-based sentence-relief judgment and remand**, conclusively rejecting failure of state judicial narrowing and the missing-citation inference. On return, the court may not reconsider that resolved judicial-application question or demand a Dicks citation. It must address the distinct question Cone already asserts: whether the actual jury guidance permitted an unconstitutional sentencing determination and, if that claim is lawfully reviewable and meritorious, whether the actual appellate proceeding or another authorized process corrected its influence on the death sentence.

The distinction is concrete. The reported jury definitions describe extreme wickedness, outrageous vileness and cruelty that includes indifference or pitilessness. They do not reproduce the state construction's unnecessarily torturous killing or Pritchett's qualified predeath-torture/depravity rule. The direct court's detailed account establishes that the evidence satisfied the narrowed category. Its statement about what the jury could consider does not itself establish that the jury used the narrowed rule, that any broader branch was harmless, or that the appellate court performed a new constitutional sentencing determination addressing such a defect. The six do not hold that an instruction must repeat a state precedent verbatim, that these supplied excerpts are the entire penalty charge, or that the jury's actual decision was unconstitutional. They reject both presumed error and presumed cure.

Sochor itself separates a sufficient judicial narrowing application from the distinct jury-instruction and weighing issues. Richmond requires actual constitutional correction of established weighing error and rejects automatic cure from remaining factors. Lambrix preserves the legal significance of an influential jury determination despite independent judicial review. These principles justify preserving this actual asserted issue; they do not establish that Cone crosses section 2254, overcomes a valid default, proves a violation or is entitled to relief.

The independent-denial alternative is expressly addressed. Section 2254(b)(2) resolves the procedural objection to denying a meritless claim; it does not make the now-established validity of judicial narrowing independently sufficient to reject a distinct harmful-jury or weighing claim. If the actual record permits denial of that remaining theory under the proper legal framework, the court may deny it without unnecessary default adjudication or factual proceedings. A grant of relief, however, must identify the actual state merits treatment and satisfy every applicable statutory gate. The 1984 direct adjudication uses federal law clearly established at that date; the 1993 previously-determined description and 1995 affirmance do not silently create a new merits date. Godfrey and Proffitt are available at the direct-review cutoff. Sochor, Richmond and Lambrix cannot be mechanically backdated, and Lambrix's 1986-finality holding does not decide a 1984 cutoff by itself.

If an available, nonstructural constitutional sentencing error is established, the governing Brecht rule puts beyond-reasonable-doubt harmlessness on the State; alternatively, there must be an actually authorized and adequate constitutional cure. Federal courts cannot invent a missing state sentencing balance. No writ, hearing, new sentencing proceeding, release or execution authorization is directed now. The 2002 Bell mandate's other properly pending claims remain intact.

## 5. Adversarial comparison of the actual disposition paths

### A. Final denial on actual narrowing

This is now a supported path, not a hypothetical dependent on missing state text. The state opinion's finding of defensive wounds, resistance, noninstantaneous death and terror corresponds to Pritchett and Melson's substantive limitations. The Eight reject the Sixth Circuit's contrary no-application inference. State familiarity is corroborative at most; the actual analysis supplies the result. Section 2254(b)(2) defeats an exhaustion-only objection to this denial.

The strongest counterargument concerns a different constitutional operation: a jury instructed through general wickedness and pitilessness language may not have used the appellate court's narrower construction. The six find that argument sufficiently concrete and already asserted to preserve its distinct adjudication; the two would require it to stand as a genuinely independent claim rather than continue the reviewed narrowing ground. The disagreement is over the scope and required continuation, not whether the State must cite Dicks or whether judicial narrowing actually occurred.

### B. Bounded remand concerning an influential jury and any actual correction

This path draws directly on Sochor's separate holdings and the actual-cure rules in Richmond and Lambrix. The reported instruction and direct court's conditional account of what the jury could consider identify the remaining legal distinction. The six do not derive constitutional error from words alone and do not treat the legal rule's existence in 2005 as sufficient proof of the section 2254(d) gateway at a 1984 merits adjudication.

The strongest objection is unnecessary further litigation after a sufficient narrowed application. The six answer with a limiting mandate: judicial application is settled, no Dicks-citation inquiry may recur, and no new hearing or remand work is required where an independently sufficient lawful denial already resolves the actual remaining theory. What remains is whether this already asserted jury/weighing theory is distinct and available and, if so, whether its own merits and correction requirements are met. Unresolved default alone is expressly insufficient to require the exercise.

### C. Preserve the present writ or order immediate sentence relief

No Associate selects this path. The present lack-of-state-narrowing premise is wrong on the supplied actual state rules and application. Neither the jury excerpts nor the separate risk-factor ruling establishes that Cone crosses the applicable statutory gateway and proves an uncured constitutional violation. No conditional writ can rest solely on a case-name omission, assumed default exception, presumed merits date, or an instruction comparison divorced from its actual constitutional operation.

## 6. The separate risk-factor ruling does not decide this disagreement

The Tennessee Supreme Court expressly rejected the risk-to-others aggravator and characterized its inclusion as harmless beyond a reasonable doubt. The eight preserve that as an actual state ruling about that factor. No Justice treats it as a determination that a heinousness error was harmless. Nor do the six reopen the risk-factor issue under the guise of this grant or declare its harmlessness explanation constitutionally sufficient solely because it used the proper burden's words. Any independently pending challenge to it remains governed by its own scope and predicates.

For the heinousness question, the existence of other valid aggravators and the strength of evidence are not a substitute for actual cure when a reviewable constitutional weighing error is established. Conversely, cure is not required merely because a party alleges an error: the violation and review prerequisites must first exist. The State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden governs an available nonstructural error's effect, not whether every allegation crosses section 2254 or establishes a substantive violation.
