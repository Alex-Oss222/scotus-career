## 81. Merck KGaA v. Integra Lifesciences I, Ltd.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Merck KGaA v. Integra Lifesciences I, Ltd.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1237; 545 U.S. 193
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether uses of patented inventions in preclinical research, including experiments whose results are not ultimately submitted to the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), fall within 35 U.S.C. §271(e)(1).
* **Question Granted:** Whether uses of patented inventions in preclinical research, including experiments whose results are not ultimately submitted to the FDA, fall within 35 U.S.C. §271(e)(1).
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit affirmed rejection of Merck's regulatory-use defense under a restrictive reading of §271(e)(1), while requiring further proceedings on damages. A jury had found infringement and rejected the defense.
* **Relief Requested:** Merck seeks vacatur of the safe-harbor ruling and review of the evidence under the correct statutory standard. Integra seeks preservation of infringement liability for research it characterizes as drug discovery rather than information development for FDA review.
* **Material Record:** Integra's patents concern RGD peptides. Merck sponsored research by Scripps investigators into peptide candidates affecting angiogenesis, including work relevant to cancer treatment. The disputed experiments concerned potential biological effects, mechanisms, efficacy and safety before human trials. Some candidates or experiments did not become part of an eventual FDA submission. The legal question does not establish that every challenged experiment had a reasonable regulatory relationship; the project and the particular use must be examined on the trial record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The grant concerns the statutory defense, not a general judicial research exception. The Court must respect the trial evidence and the posture of Merck's challenge to the verdict. Whether patents covering research tools receive identical treatment is not established merely because patented compounds were used experimentally here.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress enacted §271(e)(1) in 1984. The Federal Circuit decided the relevant appeal in 2003. The claim concerns the law governing the historical research, not later regulatory or patent amendments.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material beyond the authorities addressed below.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None specifically identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Patent protection and exemptions are statutory. Courts must give effect both to the protection of patented inventions and to the enacted exemption without converting a policy preference for research into an unbounded defense.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable relation to the development and submission of regulatory information

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling decision decides the disputed preclinical reach of §271(e)(1). Eli Lilly & Co. v. Medtronic remains relevant for the statute's coverage of inventions used in the federal drug-regulatory process; it does not itself decide whether each Merck experiment qualifies.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Existing law provides no exemption for all scientific research. Statutory use must be linked to the regulatory information-development function Congress described, assessed through the circumstances of the use rather than hindsight alone.
* **Other Governing Law:** 35 U.S.C. §271(a) defines infringement and §271(e)(1) excludes specified uses reasonably related to development and submission of information under federal drug regulation. The Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FDCA) and FDA investigational-new-drug and new-drug-application requirements include preclinical information relevant to safety and efficacy.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statute covers uses reasonably related to developing and submitting regulatory information. Whether it includes unsuccessful candidates or results never submitted, and where basic research falls, remain the questions presented.
* **Open Point:** How to distinguish protected preclinical development from basic research lacking a sufficiently identified regulatory objective, and whether unsuccessful candidates or unsubmitted results are categorically excluded.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reasonable relation to the development and submission of regulatory information

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Federal Circuit treated the exemption as closely tied to information ultimately relevant to the regulatory submission process and regarded the disputed work as insufficiently connected to that process. Its construction substantially limited early research coverage.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** Merck argues that identifying and testing a viable candidate requires preclinical work and that a reasonable relationship cannot depend on whether the research ultimately succeeds or its results are submitted.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Integra argues that an expansive exemption would permit commercial drug discovery to consume patent rights merely because the research might eventually yield a regulated product. It seeks a concrete limit separating basic discovery from regulatory development.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** A speculative possibility that any experiment could eventually assist drug approval is insufficient. Conversely, a researcher cannot know in advance which reasonable experiments will succeed or which results FDA will require. No oral-argument concession is assumed.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** The United States supports an exemption for preclinical experiments reasonably connected to development of information of the kind relevant to FDA approval, including unsuccessful research. That approach requires record-specific application rather than automatic immunity for all Merck-funded activity.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The grant concerns the statutory defense, not a general judicial research exception. The Court must respect the trial evidence and the posture of Merck's challenge to the verdict. Whether patents covering research tools receive identical treatment is not established merely because patented compounds were used experimentally here.

##### Issue 1: Reasonable relation to the development and submission of regulatory information

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Vacate and remand under a reasonable regulatory relationship standard; affirm if the restrictive construction is correct or the trial record independently compels rejection under the proper standard; or direct judgment only if the preserved evidentiary posture permits it. A legal correction does not itself determine damages or every act of infringement.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The holding can cover preclinical safety, efficacy and mechanism work on candidate drugs without deciding all research tools, unrestricted basic science or later commercial sales.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The verdict cannot be replaced by a blanket judicial finding that all work was protected. The reviewing court must identify the challenged uses, the governing instruction, preserved evidentiary objections and what the record permits under the corrected construction.

# Earlier-authority neutral correction for the June 13 patent matter

**Authority:** Eli Lilly & Co. v. Medtronic, Inc., 496 U.S. 661 (1990), No. 89-243, decided June 18, 1990. This is pre-divergence authority.

**Verified writing and participation:** Justice Scalia delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Rehnquist and Justices Brennan, Marshall, Blackmun and Stevens. Justice Kennedy dissented, joined by Justice White. Justice O'Connor took no part in consideration or decision.

**Question and existing rule:** Eli Lilly construed §271(e)(1) in the setting of a medical device subject to federal premarketing approval. The Court applied the statutory reference to a federal law regulating drugs to the FDCA's broader regulatory scheme and allowed the statutory exemption to cover qualifying medical-device uses. The decision addresses the statutory class covered by the reference, rather than the specific later question of the regulatory relationship of particular unsuccessful preclinical compound experiments.

**Earlier disagreement:** Kennedy's dissent favored reading the text as referring to particular legal provisions regulating drugs, rather than the entire Act containing them. He disputed the majority's broader device coverage and its inferences about Congress's regulatory compromise. The majority gave effect to the broader statutory reference and the relation between the exemption and patent-term restoration. The separate writings establish earlier interpretive commitments; their effect on the later case requires independent assessment.
