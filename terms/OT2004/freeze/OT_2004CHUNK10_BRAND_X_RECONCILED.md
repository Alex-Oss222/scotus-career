## A. Delegation and Portland's precedential force

Seven Associates identify the Communications Act's implementing authority, including sections151 and201(b) as construed in Iowa Utilities Board, and the Commission's actual exercise of that authority in its nationwide declaratory ruling; Scalia recognizes the same assigned role on his official-position ground. This is materially different from the revisable transaction letter in Mead. The court independently examines the definitions and the scope of the delegation before accepting a reasonable administrative choice within the lawful range. Smiley permits explained change within authority; neither inconsistency nor the timing of administrative action itself destroys eligibility for that review. An unexplained change remains separately reviewable under the APA.

The four reversal supporters' prior-circuit proposition is that an earlier court of appeals' selection of a preferred reading does not exhaust a choice Congress actually delegated to the agency if the prior holding and statute leave that range open. A binding determination that the statute requires a particular answer remains a legal boundary. The distinction requires substantive examination, not a search for an express ambiguity formula. In their view Portland's treatment of the cable transmission component did not resolve the offer question as one having only a single lawful answer, so circuit precedent is no automatic bar to the FCC's later permissible choice. Either proposition is a new holding at its scope, not something current Mead or Neal has already decided.

The four affirmance supporters' proposition is that an authoritative judicial construction of statutory meaning binds within the judicial hierarchy that issued it, a court of appeals' within its circuit and this Court's throughout the federal system, until lawfully reconsidered. An agency departing from an appellate court's settled classification must show that the earlier decision left an identifiable delegated choice open; the remedy for a mistaken precedent is judicial reconsideration. Portland fixed the classification of the transmission component and identified no delegated choice left open. The strongest objection is that this lets the first court to construe a statute exhaust a choice Congress gave the agency; the four answer that a decision recognizing a delegated range constrains no choice within it and that judicial review remains available to remedy a mistaken construction. The four reversal supporters answer in turn that the court must first enforce Congress's allocation of a genuine interpretive choice. Stevens's qualification concerning Supreme Court precedent remains distinct: the reversal position can resolve Portland without asserting that every Supreme Court construction yields whenever it omitted an unambiguity sentence.

Souter and Ginsburg accept the FCC's actual administrative role but do not join the proposed displacement rule or its application as a reason to sustain this order. They support the bounded binding-construction rule and its application to Portland; Souter's Lechmere join and both Justices' Neal joins supply it. Their statutory conclusion independently eliminates the claimed permissible range in this case. Their stance does not adopt Scalia's judicial-finality theory or a rule that every earlier judicial reading forever forecloses administrative choice.

## B. Offered-service classification

The governing definitions concern different things. Section153(43) defines telecommunications as transmission of user-chosen information between user-specified points without changing its form or content as sent and received. Section153(46) requires an offering for a fee directly to the public or to a class effectively available to the public, regardless of facilities. Section153(20) covers the offered information-processing capabilities via telecommunications while excluding capabilities used for managing, controlling or operating a telecommunications system or managing a telecommunications service. Section153(44)'s carrier definition and qualifications remain as supplied in the independent freeze; common-carrier treatment extends only to providing telecommunications services. Neither a facilities label nor marketing alone supplies the answer.

The four reversal supporters sustain a permissible construction of the described retail service. The Commission's paragraphs38–41 describe genuine end-user information capabilities and their integration with transmission: retrieval, storage, email, access and related functions. The statute expressly contemplates information service provided via telecommunications; it does not necessarily make every such input a distinct public telecommunications offering. The management exception remains enforceable: internal network management alone does not transform transmission into information service. The described offer includes end-user capabilities beyond that exception.

The strongest contrary argument identifies a real paid transmission function whose information arrives unchanged even though optional processing accompanies it. The four answer that functional integration of this retail service permits the Commission's finished-offering classification; mere technical identification of a transmission input is not conclusive about the public service offered. Conversely, packaging an independent telephone transmission service with voicemail or an unrelated information feature would not erase the independent service. The rule is tied to the actual described offering rather than a categorical bundling exemption. Optional nonuse of a feature does not by itself remove that feature from what the provider offers.

Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer regard the actual transmission pathway sold to this subscriber as retaining an independent identity under the statute. They distinguish delivery of the finished information to the consumer from telecommunications an information provider merely uses upstream. Thus their rule does not classify every information service as a common carrier. A standalone sale is not a statutory prerequisite; selling a defined function with another function still offers it when its independent identity remains. Management, routing or incidental processing cannot erase a telecommunications service that otherwise satisfies the definition. This is the shared statutory ground in historical Part I, including its answer to the claim that the dissent would regulate every ISP. Breyer reaches it on his own actual-offering and delegation-limit premises: the question is close, but the transmission a subscriber buys is offered for a fee, and complexity does not enlarge the delegated range.

The four also preserve Part I's remedial and statutory caution: any permissible deregulation through section160 must satisfy that provision's own conditions, the Commission must forbear when it makes the required findings, and potential Title I ancillary powers cannot automatically be treated as an alternative authority to impose every common-carrier obligation on an entity classified outside telecommunications service. The carrier limitation in section153(44) remains relevant. They identify the difficulty without adjudicating a future access order that is not before the Court.

## C. APA review and disposition

For the four reversal supporters, the classification is within the statute and the preserved differential-treatment objection therefore requires its own answer. The FCC's paragraphs42–44 identify the historical Computer II treatment of telephone-company facilities, their bottleneck role, and the decision not to extend that framework automatically to the distinct cable setting. Emerging alternative broadband platforms and the Commission's stated investment and innovation objectives support reconsideration within its lawful authority. These are the agency's reasons, not a judicially invented replacement explanation. The agency can address the different regimes incrementally; the present disposition does not decide a later DSL classification or a particular Title I access mandate.

The four affirmance supporters do not need an independent final arbitrariness ruling. Regulatory history cannot cure what they identify as the threshold statutory error. They would preserve invalidation of the telecommunications exclusion and remand for implementation of the correct statutory category and any properly invoked statutory forbearance. They do not prescribe access terms, rates, net-neutrality rules, penalties or an all-purpose broadband regime themselves.

## Separate-writing functions and assembly requirements

The shared affirmance position comprises Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer on classification and the bounded binding-construction rule: the retail transmission retains its statutory identity, standalone sale is unnecessary, delivery to the subscriber differs from an upstream input, and internal management cannot erase an otherwise defined service. Its substantive summary must include its answer to the claim that every ISP becomes regulated, its reliance on lawful forbearance rather than a rewritten definition, and the limited observation about Title I and section153(44). Its remedy is statutory implementation, not a judicially designed access scheme.

Scalia's additional position concerns judicial authority: executive displacement of judicially fixed meaning raises the judicial-finality concern illustrated by Waterman. The bounded binding-construction rule shared by the four affirmance supporters does not depend on that ground. The precedent question requires decision because Portland is the Ninth Circuit's ground and the petitioners seek authority to displace it; a position joining a rule that decides it cannot also maintain that the question need not be decided. Scalia's separate disagreement with Mead's mechanism threshold limits his join of any proposition restating it. Do not attribute the finality ground or the Mead disagreement to Souter, Ginsburg or Breyer.

Stevens's function is a narrow Supreme Court-precedent caveat while joining all reversal propositions properly confined to Portland and circuit precedent. Historical language that the result would not necessarily apply to this Court is not a categorical contrary rule. The independent freeze's statement that no separate writing is necessary can stand only if the writing he joins actually honors the scope and does not imply that all judicial constructions are interchangeable. Same judgment alone is insufficient to eliminate the qualification.

Breyer's concurrence function is likewise substantive but limited: the challenged order lies outside the delegated range, although near its boundary, because the definitions turn on what is actually offered; formal proceedings do not independently establish that range and their absence does not categorically defeat it; and within the statutory category the Commission keeps broad implementation and forbearance discretion. He supports the source-specific delegation result while answering an over-formalized portrayal of Mead. Current Mead already makes those formal-process limits explicit, so a lead opinion that accurately states them can accommodate the legal point. Do not manufacture a new broad basic-question exception or convert his full join into a judgment-only vote.
