### Hartford Fire Insurance v. California, No. 91-1111 and 91-1128

Merits disposition, 1993-06-28

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued 1993-02-23; decided 1993-06-28. On writ of certiorari to Ninth Circuit, In re Insurance Antitrust Litigation, 938 F.2d 919 (1991).

**Question:** Whether domestic insurers lose McCarran-Ferguson antitrust protection by alleged agreement with foreign reinsurers; which pleaded conduct falls within the boycott exception; and whether international comity bars the Sherman Act claims against the London defendants. No. 91-1111 presents the two McCarran-Ferguson questions. No. 91-1128 presents extraterritorial application to the foreign conduct. These are distinct reviewed components of the same listed consolidated matter.

**Material record:** Nineteen States and private plaintiffs filed 36 complaints alleging concerted changes in commercial general liability insurance and reinsurance, including policy forms, pollution exclusions, and terms of coverage. The allegations include withholding broader reinsurance business to compel adoption of disfavored terms, as well as joint drafting without alleged refusal to deal. Other counts concern London reinsurers’ coordinated terms for American risks. These are allegations on motions to dismiss, not proven cartels. The London defendants invoke a British regulatory regime permitting their practices; they do not identify British law requiring conduct that American law forbids.

**Relief sought:** Domestic defendants seek reinstatement of dismissals under the insurance exemption. The London defendants seek dismissal of the foreign-conduct claims on comity grounds. State and private plaintiffs seek continued litigation of the challenged conspiracies.

All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

No. 91-1111: affirmed in part, reversed in part. No. 91-1128: affirmed on the reviewed foreign conduct ground. Consolidated litigation remanded; no antitrust liability determined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Insurance exemption follows activity rather than association

**Controlling proposition:** Otherwise qualifying business of insurance regulated by state law does not lose McCarran-Ferguson § 2(b) antitrust protection merely because domestic insurers agree with foreign reinsurers. Whether the activity itself qualifies and is state-regulated remains essential; neither domestic nor foreign nationality establishes that condition categorically.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the exemption’s object is the activity Congress described. Royal Drug distinguishes the business of insurance from all business done by insurance companies; its discussion of agreements with outsiders does not create an entity-based contamination rule. Pireno likewise examines risk transfer, the policy relationship and whether the practice remains within the insurance industry. The alleged agreements here concededly concern the business of insurance, and foreign reinsurers are not pharmacies or other parties wholly outside that industry. Even assuming their own activities lack state regulation, the domestic activity does not automatically lose otherwise established protection through association. The contrary analogy to exemptions for designated agricultural producers overlooks different statutory language. This ruling removes one appellate rationale without foreclosing the boycott exception or deciding the actual regulatory status of every reinsurer. Fabe’s separate first-clause protection of state insurance-regulatory law remains distinct. Qualifying activity, actual regulation and applicable statutory exceptions must still be evaluated.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Group Life & Health Insurance v. Royal Drug, 440 U.S.205: activity/entity distinction applied; loose outsider analogy confined to its context.
- Union Labor Life v. Pireno, 458 U.S.119: practice-specific criteria retained.
- SEC v. National Securities, 393 U.S.453: regulation of insurance business distinguished from regulation of companies.
- Case-Swayne v. Sunkist, 389 U.S.384, and United States v. Borden, 308 U.S.188: producer/entity-based exemptions distinguished.
- Fabe, June 11, 1993: separate first-clause holding not expanded into the antitrust proviso.

##### Collateral refusals distinguish boycott from agreement on terms

**Controlling proposition:** A concerted refusal of collateral or unrelated transactions to compel desired terms in a targeted transaction can constitute a McCarran-Ferguson § 3(b) boycott; a mere joint insistence on terms of the refused transaction does not, without more, do so. The pleaded broader withdrawals permit California I–IV/Connecticut I–II to proceed, subject to proof and whether other business genuinely affects the reinsured risk; California VII/Connecticut VI allege no refusal mechanism and cannot defeat domestic exemption on the reviewed grounds.

**Authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the boycott exception must leave room for the activity exemption Congress enacted. Barry involved withholding alternative insurance to force acceptance of another insurer’s terms, while Eastern States used unrelated dealings to compel a collateral trade restriction. Those examples distinguish coercive leverage from a collective demand about the very risk or transaction offered. Reinsurance terms depend on the underlying coverage, so refusing to reinsure an objectionable primary policy is not automatically collateral. The complaints nevertheless allege broader withdrawal even from otherwise acceptable business and linked demands that may cross that boundary. McLain and Conley require crediting that permissible pleaded account at this stage, not deciding the operational facts against the plaintiffs. Defendants may show that the other business genuinely affected the proposed risk. The model umbrella/excess counts contain no corresponding refusal allegation. The Court decides neither actual conspiracy nor antitrust injury, and supplies no categorical definition of every independent coercion or intimidation claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- St. Paul Fire & Marine v. Barry, 438 U.S.531: collateral-pressure application retained; not limited to boycotts of competitors.
- Eastern States Retail Lumber v. United States, 234 U.S.600: unrelated trade used as leverage illustrates the boundary.
- United States v. South-Eastern Underwriters, 322 U.S.533: coercive exclusion distinguished from mere agreement on terms; not abrogated.
- McLain v. Real Estate Board, 444 U.S.232, and Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S.41: then-operative pleading standard applied; no actual liability finding.

##### The asserted British permission does not bar these American-market claims

**Controlling proposition:** The pleaded London conduct intended to produce, and producing, substantial effects on American insurance coverage may be adjudicated under the Sherman Act; British permission without an incompatible duty or another established ground requiring nonapplication here does not warrant the asserted comity dismissal. The Court reserves a universal comity rule and §6a’s precise applicability, while preserving its qualifying-effects and claim-connection requirements wherever applicable.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these allegations connect the challenged foreign conduct to the American market the statute protects. Alcoa and Matsushita recognize the Sherman Act’s reach to intended substantial domestic effects, and Continental Ore does not equate foreign legality with immunity from that law. The London defendants allegedly coordinated coverage restrictions aimed at American buyers; those alleged changes supply both the domestic effect and the asserted injury connection. Assuming §6a applies and adds requirements, the alleged direct, substantial and reasonably foreseeable domestic effect gives rise to these asserted claims. The Court does not decide the provision’s wider reach or every interaction with import commerce. British regulation and sovereign interests deserve consideration, but the defendants identify no British command inconsistent with American compliance. The contrary prescriptive-comity approach would displace established statutory reach on these allegations without a sufficient legal basis. The Court leaves broader comity questions open and requires proof below. Federal subject-matter power, substantive coverage and ultimate antitrust liability remain separate; none is established merely by labeling foreign practices objectionable.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Aluminum Co. of America, 148 F.2d 416, and Matsushita, 475 U.S.574: intended substantial domestic effects standard applied.
- Continental Ore v. Union Carbide, 370 U.S.690: foreign permission does not itself immunize conduct.
- Société Nationale Industrielle Aérospatiale, 482 U.S.522: actual foreign/domestic conflict considered; Blackmun’s separate analysis identified as not the whole Court’s holding.
- Charming Betsy, 2 Cranch 64: interpretive canon retained; does not require the asserted dismissal here.
- Aramco, 499 U.S.244, and Sale, June 21, 1993: source-specific geographic scope preserved; different statutes do not erase established Sherman Act reach.

#### Current-Law Effect

Effective June 28, activity-specific McCarran-Ferguson exemption is unanimous; collateral boycott boundary is adopted by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; foreign claims continue under the case-specific effects/comity rule adopted by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. No universal comity-impossibility test, automatic reinsurer exemption, or proven antitrust liability enters. Both dockets and count-specific remedies remain separate.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Souter, joined by White, Blackmun and Stevens on the boycott question, would recognize primary insurers’ enlistment of reinsurers to enforce their desired market terms as coercive enforcement beyond ordinary cooperative drafting. Barry and South-Eastern Underwriters do not, in their view, confine every boycott to formally collateral transactions. They agree that the principal counts survive and that the model umbrella/excess counts lack any refusal allegation. Scalia, joined by O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas, dissents from allowing the London-only claims. He accepts federal subject-matter jurisdiction over nonfrivolous statutory claims and Congress’s power, but reads Charming Betsy, Lauritzen and Romero as requiring source-based prescriptive comity. The foreign actors, center of activity and British regulatory interest favor nonapplication even without an incompatible legal command. He would dismiss for failure to state a claim rather than invoke lack of judicial power. Stone’s concurrence preserves the actual §6a claim connection requirement where applicable and separates that statutory limit from discretionary comity. The American-market operation alleged here supports continued adjudication; he adopts no power over wholly foreign injury lacking the required connection and no general rule that comity is irrelevant whenever two laws can both be obeyed.

### Agostini v. Felton and Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553

Merits decision, June 23, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Agostini v. Felton, No. 96-552, consolidated with Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, No. 96-553. Decided June 23, 1997. Argued April 15, 1997, on writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit.

The Board and a new group of parents filed motions for Rule 60 relief in October and December 1995. On August 7, 1996, the Second Circuit affirmed denial of relief from the September 26, 1985 permanent injunction implementing Aguilar. The question under Rule 60(b)(5) is whether that continuing onsite prohibition should remain in light of the governing Establishment Clause law and the preserved request to reconsider Aguilar. The two dockets concern the same prospective injunction and share one decision. Public teachers provide supplementary secular Title I instruction to independently eligible children under public control; the injunction requires offsite delivery.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded in both consolidated dockets, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Second Circuit judgments affirming denial of relief are vacated. Proceedings return for a district-court order removing the injunction's onsite prohibition to the extent it rests on the categorical ground rejected today. Any genuinely independent basis must be identified and decided under the surviving law; absent such a basis the prohibition must be dissolved. The City must obey the operative decree until judicial modification. Existing lawful services may continue during prompt implementation, and children currently receiving them should continue to receive them with existing statutory duties preserved. No new service entitlement, fixed timetable, damages or repayment is imposed.

#### Holdings

All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding and operative rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Precedent treatment:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding and operative rule:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

##### III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding and operative rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

##### IV. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reinstate that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

Aguilar no longer supplies a categorical constitutional bar to the separately administered, safeguarded public Title I service described in Holding II, and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale has no contrary force at that scope. Ball's completed judgment, Community Education and unaddressed holdings remain undisturbed. Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

For this continuing injunction, the Court's substantive change satisfies Rule 60(b)(5)'s legal-change requirement. Removal of the now-unsupported onsite prohibition must follow through a tailored district-court order. Rufo's other requirements and Rodriguez de Quijas's rule for lower courts remain. ### Neder v. United States, No. 97-1985

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes use fraud as a legal term with settled content. Morissette supplies the principle that Congress ordinarily carries that established meaning into the statute; the absence of a separate materiality adjective does not strip fraud of its significance requirement. Durland includes fraudulent future promises, but does not make an inconsequential falsehood a fraudulent scheme or import every element of a completed civil tort. The statutory focus on schemes, and §1344's inclusion of attempts, explains why actual reliance and completed injury are unnecessary. Mail or wire use remains a distinct requirement, and the communication need not itself contain the falsehood if it executes the scheme.

Section 1344 separately covers executing or attempting a scheme to defraud a covered institution and executing or attempting a scheme to obtain its property by false or fraudulent pretenses. Both routes retain materiality for the deceptive schemes presented here; their other possible differences, including mental-state requirements in other applications, remain open. Wells construes the different wording of §1014, which does not require objective materiality in addition to its false-statement or overvaluation and purpose-to-influence requirements. It does not turn fraud language into an unrestricted false-statement prohibition. A purpose to influence under §1014 does not automatically establish the separate fraud element.

The natural-tendency formulation, reflected in Kungys, concerns the targeted decision in context. The Court does not exhaust every common-law formulation, unusual recipient preference, omission duty, or nonrepresentational scheme. Nor does it decide materiality under every differently worded offense. The judgment rejecting materiality under the three fraud provisions is reversed.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin assigns the application of an actual element to the jury. Section 7206(1) independently supplies materiality through its express prohibition of willfully subscribing a return declared under penalties of perjury that the signer does not believe true and correct as to every material matter. Part I establishes the element under the fraud statutes. The express tax and bank directions withheld that application, while the mail and wire charges omitted it. The judge's separate materiality ruling is not a jury finding.

Chapman places the burden on the Government for these preserved constitutional errors. Failure to offer a separately labeled factual materiality defense supplies neither waiver nor forfeiture and does not require Neder to prove innocence. Johnson's refusal to correct forfeited error under the fourth condition of plain-error review does not govern these preserved objections. Genuine findings elsewhere in an actual verdict can establish an equivalent determination; whether they do so here, and whether other supported inferences suffice, are distinct questions addressed in the following parts. Neither the Court's recognition of error nor Gaudin alone decides those remedial questions.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Controlling explanation:** Roy permits examination of a particular omitted element without converting every omission into a structural defect requiring automatic reversal. Its qualified method concerns the error's effect on the jury that returned this verdict. Yates supplies the instructional-focus requirement: a reviewing court must examine whether the instructions directed the jury to evidence supporting the proposed inference, rather than assume the jury decided a question removed from its task. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient, but not invariably indispensable to a reliable noncontribution conclusion.

Sullivan addresses a different defect, an invalid reasonable-doubt standard vitiating all factual findings. These instructions left an otherwise valid reasonable-doubt standard governing the submitted elements. Their express removal of materiality heightens the need to justify the connection; it does not itself destroy every remaining finding or forbid the inquiry Roy allows. Gaudin's allocation rule remains fully operative at trial.

Powerful and unchallenged evidence may support an inference, but neither label creates an automatic safe harbor. A reviewing court must assess defense disputes for what they concern and determine whether the actual findings resolve them. Sufficient evidence for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction is not the required showing. Mitchell likewise distinguishes actual contribution from enough other evidence to sustain a result. This holding does not classify every omitted element, constructive amendment, or total denial of jury trial. Richardson's reserved harmlessness questions concerning its distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect remain unresolved.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The instruction required knowing, purposeful falsity of the reported total income: $53,625 for 1985 and minus $980,377 for 1986. It did not permit conviction for an unidentified collateral entry. The jury considered the defenses that the receipts were repayable loans and that professional advice or good faith negated culpability. Its findings reject those defenses insofar as they would make the specified totals truthful or their falsity innocent. The verdict does not resolve every subsidiary advice dispute, but its particular subject and culpability findings furnish the connection required by Roy's actual-contribution inquiry. Annual income is information used to determine income-tax liability and administer the returns.

The objection is substantial: the jury was expressly forbidden to decide materiality, so a court must not supply that missing decision. The Court does not suppose that jurors disobeyed the instruction, treat the judge's ruling as theirs, or declare that they necessarily made the omitted evaluation. It instead asks what effect that withdrawal had on an otherwise valid verdict about these identified income statements. The charge's specific focus and findings establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt under Roy's permitted method.

The conclusion requires no unanimous numerical finding of the alleged additional income, acceptance of every transaction or witness, unpaid-tax calculation, actual IRS response, or successful deception. It does not excuse future courts from submitting materiality. The Government retains the burden in each case; this record-specific conclusion neither creates a tax exception to Gaudin nor decides the fraud counts.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals rejected the antecedent statutory element and therefore did not undertake the required fraud harmlessness analysis. Unlike the tax charge's specified annual-income findings, the fraud instructions and theories require a distinct inquiry into what each verdict resolved. Inflated appraisals, concealed relationships and down payments, presales, and advance requests may bear strongly on lending decisions. Yet the charge did not require proof of every alleged detail, expressly removed bank materiality, and used cross-references for mail and wire fraud. A generic intent-to-deceive finding does not automatically resolve capacity to influence, and Wells prevents treating a §1014 purpose-to-influence finding as an automatic equivalent. The lending-purpose and transaction-significance disputes must be considered in context.

Part V-B applies Roy's qualified method and Chapman's burden to that unfinished appellate task. The tax affirmance proves neither harmlessness nor harmfulness for any fraud count. No present determination requires a new trial on every affected fraud count or sustains any one of them automatically. If the Government fails the governing showing on an affected conviction, instructional-error relief permits retrial; it does not establish evidentiary insufficiency or require acquittal.

The mandate does not independently dispose of §1014, conspiracy, or racketeering claims, reopen acquittals, or calculate a sentence, restitution, or release date. Any properly available dependent consequence must follow the actual predicate and ordinary mandate proceedings. Those boundaries are part of the limited remand, not an advance resolution of unpresented claims.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, II, and III. Fraud's settled meaning supplies materiality, Wells concerns another enactment, and Gaudin requires submission of actual elements. Roy makes these omissions reviewable trial error and permits reliable whole-record inferences beyond necessary equivalent findings. ### Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, No. 99-62

Plenary merits decision, June 19, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 29, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, reviewing 168 F.3d 806 (1999). The granted question concerns the football policy. The District Court rejected its unrestricted form but permitted the nonsectarian/nonproselytizing backup; the Fifth Circuit rejected both football versions. The graduation disposition and affirmed damages denial are not reopened.

The October 1995 policy permits a brief invocation and/or message during home-varsity-football pregame ceremonies to solemnize the event, promote sportsmanship and student safety, and establish an appropriate competitive environment. Each spring the student council, upon the principal's advice and direction, conducts a secret-ballot election of the high-school student body on whether to include the statement or invocation. If the students vote to include it, they elect one volunteer, who may select the message or invocation consistently with the policy's goals. The backup repeats the policy with a nonsectarian and nonproselytizing condition if the unrestricted version is enjoined. It neither requires the selected student to pray nor opens the event to every student speaker.

The prior chaplain practice and August elections precede the challenged October policy. No delivered October prayer or official review of a particular message is found. #### Holdings

##### The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority

**Holding and operative rule:** A public secondary school may not organize an invocation as a representative component of its own football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that official component will include religious exercise. The challenged board-created election mechanism violates the Establishment Clause under both its unrestricted and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing versions; relief reaches that mechanism while preserving genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for the rule, explanation and limited affirmance. The particular government-created representative religious choice is essential to all seven joins; the Court adopts no independent religious-pressure holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini requires identification of the governmental act and its function, using purpose, effect and institutional relationships as evidence within the modified Lemon inquiry. Here the board creates an invocation or message slot within its own ceremony; the student council conducts a majority election under the principal's advice and direction; the electorate selects a single volunteer; and the speaker must serve the board's prescribed purposes. This is an official representative component, not generally available access for independently chosen private speakers. Lee forbids official school organization of worship, and Ingebretsen explains why student initiation or delivery cannot alone make that exercise private. A majority vote cannot privatize the board's delegated religious choice.

The District stresses secular purposes, student authorship, no required prayer, optional attendance and the possibility of silence or a secular message. Those features do not remove the existing governmental mechanism. The Court assumes no official textual preapproval and no delivered October prayer; it does not rest on a finding that every secular purpose is a sham. The ability to decline use of the delegated religious authority does not transform it into neutral access. Pinette and Bronx Household protect actually private expression, where government has not adopted the devotion as its own event component. Tanford likewise distinguishes official organization from private composition, without making its adult setting identical to this school policy.

The backup's religious-content limits retain the same official mechanism and do not cure it. The Court does not direct officials to edit private religion into acceptable language. Nor does it prohibit every election, invited student speaker or religious message. The existing mechanism suffices to affirm; Lee and Ingebretsen's separate protection against substantial practical religious pressure remains intact without requiring legal compulsion or compulsory attendance. No additional pressure finding, universal facial-challenge rule, new forum entitlement, graduation ruling or damages award is necessary.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act/function inquiry; purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not independent surviving prongs.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues in the form Agostini adopted; the Court revives neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Its official-organization rule applies to this school-created invocation mechanism. Its independently sufficient substantial-pressure rule remains, without a compulsory-attendance prerequisite; no new pressure holding is made here.
- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Its rejection of automatic insulation by student delivery and its protection of lawful private expression apply. Its provision-sensitive interim ruling is not recast as a universal rule invalidating every future religious message.
- **Tanford v. Brand:** Preserved for its established official devotional program and its distinction between independent wording and private access; the Court does not transplant every feature of university commencement to football.
- **Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Its actual generally available private forum is distinguished from this selective representative event component; religious content and government location remain insufficient by themselves to establish attribution.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its independently organized private use remains protected; the school's own representative ceremony presents a different governmental function.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District:** Its protection of religious viewpoints within genuine comparable private access remains intact; no schoolwide private forum is inferred here.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its independently directed private-expression relationship remains intact; it does not authorize government-organized worship.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio v. City of Cincinnati:** Its contextual attribution inquiry remains; neither a forum label nor location alone supplies the answer.
- **Strout v. Albanese:** Its distinction between genuine private expression and governmental performance or purchase of a religious function is preserved; no football-specific rule is imported from its tuition setting.

#### Law After Decision

The official character of the challenged representative religious-choice mechanism supplies the constitutional defect even without a delivered October prayer or official editing of its words. Government cannot make its own religious choice private by delegating it to a student majority. Agostini's unified governmental-act/function inquiry governs; no independent purpose or endorsement test returns. Genuinely private religious expression retains its protection. The independent substantial-pressure rules of Lee and Ingebretsen continue without requiring legally compulsory attendance; this decision need not decide that additional ground.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, dissenting, joined by Justice Thomas.** They would reverse the facial invalidation of the unrestricted October policy and remand for bounded adjudication of any actual official-adoption or unconstitutional-pressure application. Agostini requires examination of what government actually does, and they accept its prohibition on government-organized worship. But the present wording permits a secular message, a religious message or no message and leaves the selected volunteer the content choice. In their view, the election and prior prayer history do not sufficiently establish that every implementation of this policy is official devotion. Their Ingebretsen objection distinguishes anticipated official participation from supported implementation; Pinette and Bronx Household preserve genuine private access. They do not require a government-written script before attribution is possible, treat student authorship as conclusive, or deny Tanford's established official-attribution holding. Tanford's adult ceremonial dispute supplies no general permission for official secondary-school worship.

For the conditional backup, they would vacate and remand its football disposition with the unrestricted-policy proceedings so that the lower courts can determine the lawful reach and severability of any needed relief. They would not restore mandatory screening of private speech for nonsectarian and nonproselytizing content, and they do not hold the backup independently valid. It becomes operative only if the unrestricted version is enjoined; no independent continuing application is presumed. Their component remedies preserve genuine voluntary expression while leaving supported actual official worship and substantial practical pressure open to adjudication. They would neither reopen graduation or damages questions nor authorize suppression of private religious viewpoints.

Earlier election stipulations establish their own chronology, without a finding that their results necessarily determine October's legal operation. No unproduced prayer text, actual editorial review or later separate lawsuit is treated as a trial finding.

The Fifth Circuit's football-policy judgment remains in force at the stated official-mechanism scope. Supreme Court merits review of the granted question is complete. No new policy, compulsory content screen, forum entitlement, damages award, evidentiary hearing or Supreme Court return date is ordered. The separate graduation and damages dispositions remain outside this review.

### Artuz v. Bennett, No. 99-1238

Merits decision, November 7, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

531 U.S. 4. Argued October 10, 2000; decided November 7, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, 199 F.3d 116, which reversed a federal habeas limitations dismissal and remanded. The question is whether a state collateral application otherwise satisfying filing requirements becomes improperly filed under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) because its claims encounter mandatory state rules governing entitlement to relief.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The Second Circuit's remand remains in place. The district court must calculate timeliness without converting the identified New York relief bars into filing defects while addressing genuinely open dates and remaining defenses. No writ, retrial, or release is awarded.

#### Opinion Topology

Ginsburg delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. That unanimous opinion supplies the holding and its stated limits.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Filing an application is distinct from entitlement to relief on its claims

**Holding and operative rule.** A state collateral application is properly filed under §2244(d)(2) when delivery and acceptance comply with the rules governing filing the application. Claim-specific rules governing whether relief must be denied do not make an otherwise properly filed application improper; New York Criminal Procedure Law §§440.10(2)(a) and (c) are such relief rules.

**Authority.** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above controls this statutory distinction and its application.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress makes the application the object of the filing requirement and separately refers to the judgment or claim reviewed. A court may receive a properly filed application and nevertheless be required to deny its claims. Treating every mandatory relief bar as a filing defect erases that distinction and makes the filing inquiry depend on a later decision about relief. New York paragraph (a) requires denial when the ground or issue was previously decided on the merits on appeal, unless a subsequent retroactively effective change in controlling law has occurred. Paragraph (c) requires sufficient facts in the underlying record for adequate appellate review and the absence of review caused by an unjustifiable failure to take or perfect a timely appeal or to raise the issue on a perfected appeal. Those conditions require claim-level inquiries into prior adjudication, legal change, record sufficiency, and unjustified omission. They govern denial of relief, not delivery and acceptance of the motion. Their mandatory language changes neither function. The warden's concern that futile applications may prolong the federal period cannot supply the relief-eligibility condition Congress omitted. The Court therefore rejects improper filing as a ground for restoring this limitations dismissal. Lindh's separate temporal construction does not answer the filing question or exempt this February 1998 federal application from AEDPA.

**Filing conditions and limits.** Acceptance by a clerk does not cure violation of an actual filing requirement. Form, delivery deadlines, proper court or office, required fees, and filing preconditions remain distinct from the two claim-relief provisions; the Court does not classify every such rule without its actual terms. Whether exceptions to a filing deadline can keep a late application from being improperly filed remains open because the limited grant does not present that question. That reservation concerns filing deadlines, not the exceptions and conditions of the two New York relief bars.

**Record and ungranted questions.** Bennett's state conviction became final before AEDPA. A state court orally denied his collateral motion on November 30, 1995; he alleges that he never received a written denial despite requests. The Second Circuit assumed the absence of a written order and treated the application as pending because an appeal required that order. The Court makes no finding about receipt or absence of an order and decides no precise pending period, transitional grace period, exhaustion, procedural default, or ultimate habeas entitlement. Those matters fall outside this grant. Nor does the Court find that either New York bar or its exception actually governs a particular Bennett claim.

#### Precedent Treatment

**Lindh v. Murphy, June 23, 1997:** Preserves its provision-specific construction of AEDPA's temporal reach. Its treatment of pre-enactment applications supplies no exemption for Bennett's post-enactment federal application and no answer to the distinct proper-filing question.

#### Law After Decision

Section 2244(d)(2)'s pending-state-review exclusion turns on a properly filed application, not success or relief eligibility of every claim within it. Courts must distinguish actual application-filing requirements from claim-relief bars. The one-year limitations framework and separate exhaustion and procedural-default doctrines remain in force; the ruling fixes no tolling duration.

#### Source Notes

The record and statutory text appear in [Artuz v. Bennett, 531 U.S. 4](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-4.pdf), including the complete New York provisions at pages 7–8, note 1. The description of the absent written order preserves the lower court's assumption and Bennett's allegation; it is not a finding by this Court. The grant is stated in substance, not as a verbatim quotation from the petition.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Further federal habeas proceedings continue under the Second Circuit's remand. Actual timeliness and the remaining defenses must be resolved consistently with the filing distinction. The Court grants no habeas relief.

### Cleveland v. United States, No. 99-804

Merits decision, November 7, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

531 U.S. 12. Argued October 10, 2000; decided November 7, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, which affirmed Cleveland's convictions. The March 20 grant was limited to whether Louisiana's video-poker licenses are property in the State's hands for purposes of mail fraud under 18 U.S.C. §1341. The separate materiality question was not granted. The government also asserts that the statute independently reaches obtaining a license valuable in the recipient's hands and distinguishes renewal from initial issuance.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The two mail-fraud convictions resting on acquisition of these licenses are set aside. Each actually dependent conviction and sentence must be reconsidered under the indictment and verdict; independent convictions and surviving independent bases are not erased. No acquittal on an unreviewed theory or separate tax, laundering, or racketeering disposition is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Thomas delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. This common unanimous coalition directly supports both statutory answers and their application to renewal.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Sovereign licensing authority is not the State's property

**Holding and operative rule.** A State's authority to issue, withhold, renew, regulate, or revoke a license held solely in its sovereign regulatory capacity is not property in its hands under §1341. Louisiana's video-poker licenses have that character, and their initial issuance or renewal through deception does not satisfy the charged State-property element merely because the permission is valuable to its recipient.

**Authority.** The unanimous Opinion of the Court identified above controls this rule and its application to both initial issuance and renewal.

**Controlling explanation.** Louisiana grants permission to operate and tests the applicant's suitability; it does not operate the gaming business or transfer an owned commercial asset. The power to exclude, restrict transfer, renew, and revoke expresses regulation, not proprietary ownership. Expected licensing and gaming revenue does not change the object this prosecution charged: permission to operate, rather than unpaid money owed to the State. McNally preserves the statute's money-or-property boundary. Carpenter recognizes genuine commercially held intangible property; it does not convert every economically valuable governmental decision into such property. A license may become valuable property in the holder's hands without making the State its co-owner. That answers the renewal argument as well: continuing regulatory control does not turn the 1994 and 1995 renewals underlying the two mail-fraud convictions into acquisitions of State property. Material deception cannot replace the missing property object. The same distinction governs comparable sovereign regulatory licenses, while actual government money and commercially held assets remain protected.

##### The acquisition clause retains the victim-property requirement

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 1341's acquisition-by-false-pretenses clause describes a means of property fraud; it does not independently criminalize obtaining something valuable in the recipient's hands when the charged scheme does not target property in the deceived victim's hands. The government's alternative license-acquisition theory therefore cannot sustain these convictions.

**Authority.** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this independent answer to the government's alternative statutory route.

**Controlling explanation.** The government's disjunctive reading and the absence of express language requiring acquisition from another identify textual features, but they do not overcome McNally's construction of the statute as a whole. The acquisition clause describes methods of accomplishing the property fraud the statute addresses. Its separate enactment does not establish a distinct offense whenever governmental approval creates private economic value. Reading the clause to dispense with the victim's property would erase the boundary McNally recognized. This is a requirement about the scheme's object, not a demand for completed economic loss or actual reliance; Neder's separate treatment of those matters remains intact. Because Louisiana's charged interest remains regulatory, the acquisition clause supplies no alternative basis for either license-renewal conviction.

**Limits and questions outside the grant.** The Court does not hold that a license is worthless to its holder or resolve every government franchise. A scheme actually targeting public money, unpaid revenue, or a genuine commercial asset has a different object and must satisfy its own elements. No independent honest-services route is asserted in the reviewed theory. Materiality was not granted. Count-specific work on remand determines actual dependence on the invalid predicates; it is application of the remedy, not an open property question.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **McNally v. United States, 483 U.S. 350:** Applies its money-or-property limitation to the statute as a whole, including the acquisition clause; sovereign regulatory permission does not supply the missing property object.
- **Carpenter v. United States, 484 U.S. 19:** Preserves protection of genuine intangible commercial property and distinguishes that asset from licensing authority.
- **Neder v. United States, June 10, 1999:** Preserves materiality as a separate element and its rejection of completed-loss and actual-reliance requirements. It neither supplies the omitted property element nor brings the ungranted materiality issue before the Court.

#### Law After Decision

Comparable permissions held by the government solely as regulator fall outside §1341's property requirement on the theory decided here, including deceptive renewals. The statute continues to reach schemes aimed at genuine public money or property. Recipient value alone cannot convert a regulatory scheme into property fraud under either statutory clause.

#### Source Notes

The record and licensing provisions appear in [Cleveland v. United States, 531 U.S. 12](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-531/pdf/USREPORTS-531-12.pdf). The [United States' merits brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/cleveland-v-united-states-merits) supplies its acquisition-clause and renewal arguments and describes the prosecution. Different descriptions of the laundering provision do not establish a particular additional conviction's legal fate; that question must follow the actual indictment, verdict, and judgment on remand.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns for the consequences of setting aside the two mail-fraud convictions and for examination of the actual dependence of other convictions and sentences. Surviving independent grounds remain available for ordinary adjudication. The Court decides no unreviewed count or tax, laundering, or racketeering element.

**End of entry: Cleveland v. United States, merits decision, November 7, 2000.**

### Duncan v. Walker, No. 00-121

Merits decision, June 18, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 26, 2001; decided June 18, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, which reinstated Walker's federal habeas application because it treated the pendency of his earlier federal application as other collateral review under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2). The question is whether that subsection excludes federal habeas pendency from the one-year limitation period.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower courts must calculate time without the claimed federal-pendency exclusion, address independently preserved and legally available alternatives, and dismiss as untimely if none applies. No writ or automatic extension issues.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Justice Stevens filed a concurrence, joined by Justice Souter; both join the Court's opinion and judgment. Justice Breyer filed a dissent, joined by Justice Ginsburg, that would affirm reinstatement and remand.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Federal habeas pendency is outside section 2244(d)(2)

**Holding and operative rule:** The word State in 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2) modifies both postconviction review and other collateral review. A federal habeas application's pendency therefore is not excluded from the limitation period by that subsection, even when the application was timely and dismissed without prejudice because exhaustion was unestablished; a civil-rights pleading accompanying it does not change that classification.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt Holdings 1 and 2 on the shared textual and structural ground. The opinion does not adopt a categorical objection to independent equitable relief.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2244(d)(2) excludes time spent in a properly filed application for State post-conviction or other collateral review concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. In this coordinated phrase, the sovereign modifier identifies both forms of review. Reading other collateral review to include every federal application would leave State with little independent work. The broader category remains useful because state procedures vary and state custody may arise outside an ordinary criminal conviction. Congress's express distinction between state and federal proceedings elsewhere reinforces that reading.

Federal habeas is collateral review in ordinary usage, as Walker argues. But membership in that broad category does not answer which sovereign's proceedings Congress selected. State collateral litigation can complete exhaustion; federal habeas invokes the forum subject to the filing period. Rose v. Lundy supplies that exhaustion background without changing the enacted exclusion. A delayed federal dismissal can create serious practical difficulty, but this statutory construction does not depend on a promise that equity saves every applicant or on a conclusion that all equitable protection would frustrate the statute.

Lindh v. Murphy preserves provision-specific temporal treatment of the identified pre-enactment application; it does not exempt every later application about the conviction. Slack v. McDaniel preserves the first-application classification of its exhaustion return while expressly retaining independent timing requirements. Artuz v. Bennett distinguishes filing conditions from claim-relief bars; it does not turn a federal application into state review. Thus neither an initial timely filing nor non-successive status permanently satisfies the limitation for a distinct later application. Proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim, and actual pendency remain independent requirements even for state proceedings.

##### 2. The statutory error requires reversal, with actual independent alternatives preserved

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Circuit's reinstatement cannot rest on excluding Walker's first federal proceeding under §2244(d)(2). On remand the courts must calculate timeliness without that exclusion and adjudicate any independently preserved, legally available commencement or equitable ground under the applicable law and actual record; if none makes the application timely, it must be dismissed as untimely.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices directly join the remedial holding; agreement by Ginsburg and Breyer that independent alternatives remain available if their statutory view fails is not needed to establish its controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** Walker's first application was dismissed without prejudice on July 9, 1996 because its allegations did not establish exhaustion. His May 20, 1997 application came after the appellate court's April 24, 1997 transitional deadline. That deadline is the premise of this review, not a ruling on every commencement dispute. The first dismissal left roughly nine months under that calculation, the later claims differed, and Walker did not return to state court. Those facts do not establish that court delay necessarily caused the late refiling. They also do not adjudicate every equitable contention.

The Second Circuit did not reach the equitable argument actually raised. Rejection of a statutory exclusion cannot substitute for adjudication of that independent argument on its own legal and factual terms. The decision establishes no equitable entitlement or finding of diligence, impediment or identical claims and grants no automatic extension. There is no still-pending first application or stay request for this Court to administer. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted application on the merits; it does not require such denial. The Court orders no universal warning, election, dismissal deadline, or stay-and-abeyance procedure.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general availability and conditions of equitable tolling, including court misdirection or extraordinary external impediments, are outside the statutory question reviewed and lack supporting findings for a ruling here. The requirements for staying a mixed petition likewise lie outside that question. The underlying constitutional claims, ultimate entitlement to habeas relief, and comprehensive classification of state filing rules are not decided. Adjudicating a preserved alternative on remand is remaining legal and factual work under its own applicable law, not an award of relief. The first dismissal remains nonmerits; no writ, release, resentencing or automatic extension issues.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Rose v. Lundy supplies the exhaustion distinction between state review and federal habeas; its actual exhaustion rule remains unchanged and creates no compulsory stay regime here.
- Lindh v. Murphy retains its provision-specific protection of the identified pre-enactment application; it is distinguished from a permanent exemption for later applications concerning the same conviction.
- Slack v. McDaniel is reaffirmed within its exhaustion-return, non-successive-application scope. That classification supplies no limitations reset and leaves independent timing, exhaustion and default requirements intact.
- Artuz v. Bennett remains controlling on application-filing conditions as distinct from claim-relief bars. It does not decide the sovereign category or excuse a genuine filing defect.
- Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs and United States v. Brockamp do not decide this AEDPA statutory exclusion; their provision-sensitive treatment of equity supplies no ruling here either granting Walker tolling or foreclosing every lawful equitable ground.

#### Current Law

Section 2244(d)(2) excludes qualifying state collateral pendency, not federal habeas pendency. A timely first federal application and a later application's non-successive classification do not by themselves satisfy the later application's limitation period. The Court resolves the sovereign-category question while retaining proper-filing, pertinent-judgment-or-claim and pendency conditions. Independent commencement rules and properly preserved equitable contentions require their own legal and factual grounds; the decision neither grants relief through them nor extinguishes them. Lindh, Slack and Artuz retain their distinct force.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Souter, concurs. They join the Court's textual construction and remand, but emphasize that this subsection's omission of federal pendency does not itself extinguish otherwise lawful equitable protection or permissible retention of a federal application while exhaustion is completed. Rose's exhaustion requirement should not be mistaken for a categorical prohibition on lawful protection against loss of a timely federal opportunity. Irwin's treatment of equitable tolling and Brockamp's attention to a particular statutory scheme require examination of the actual source and conditions, not a universal equation between every equitable accommodation and amendment of §2244(d)(2). They establish no entitlement for Walker, compulsory stay rule, warning duty or automatic suspension. Their affirmative safeguard explanation is separate from, and broader than, the Court's preservation of the actual alternative without deciding its general availability.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Ginsburg, dissents. Federal habeas is other collateral review concerning a state judgment, and the competing grammatical and neighboring-statutory arguments do not uniquely require State to govern that separate category. They would read the subsection to exclude qualifying federal pendency so that ordinary exhaustion administration does not randomly consume a timely first opportunity through the duration of a nonmerits federal proceeding. The rule would still require proper filing, a connection to the pertinent judgment or claim and actual pendency; it would not make one timely filing permanently sufficient. They would affirm reinstatement and remand for the application's independent exhaustion and other remaining requirements, awarding no writ. If the Court's construction governs, they agree that the actual equitable alternative must remain open for lawful adjudication.

#### Source Notes

The [petition, both merits briefs and record](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386019_1654) supply the filings, exhaustion dismissal, claims difference, dates and lower judgment. The respondent's merits brief, page 5, identifies the equitable argument not reached below. [Title 28, 2000 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title28/pdf/USCODE-2000-title28-partVI-chap153-sec2244.pdf) supplies §2244; §2254(b)(2) separately permits merits denial of unexhausted claims. The legal question does not require a finding that an unexamined pleading established exhaustion or equitable entitlement.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Second Circuit's judgment is reversed and the case remanded for proceedings consistent with the statutory construction and preserved-alternative instructions. The federal habeas proceeding returns for timeliness adjudication; no merits determination or release is ordered. The April 24, 1997 deadline remains the review premise rather than a universal transition holding. If no independently available basis establishes timeliness after the required calculation, dismissal as untimely follows.

**End of entry: Duncan v. Walker, merits decision, June 18, 2001.**

### Carey v. Saffold, No. 01-301

Merits decision, June 17, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 27, 2002. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit's reversal of a federal habeas limitations dismissal. The questions concern intervals between state collateral-review filings, California's original-petition procedure, and whether alternative merits language establishes timely filing. Saffold pursued successive California court levels; roughly four and one-half months separated the Court of Appeal denial from his state supreme court filing. The latter denial referred to both merits and lack of diligence. The warden seeks enforcement of the federal limitation; Saffold seeks the qualifying interval exclusion.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.


**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would reverse the California interval-exclusion ruling and remand without that exclusion.

The Ninth Circuit must determine the state order's meaning and actual timeliness, obtain lawful clarification if needed, and calculate the federal period from exact dates. A qualifying interval is excluded; an interval made untimely by an effective state determination is not. Independently preserved lawful commencement or equitable grounds retain their own requirements. If no available ground makes the petition timely, it must be dismissed as untimely. The remaining habeas restrictions retain their force.

#### Opinions

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SOUTER, and BREYER, JJ., joined. KENNEDY, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### Timely ordinary state review includes its intervals

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a properly filed state collateral application remains pending during timely continuation through the State's ordinary review process, including the interval before a timely filing at the next available level. California's successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function; an abandoned or completed process, an unreasonable gap or an unrelated later attack does not receive interval exclusion merely because another collateral petition is possible.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by the other five members of the six-Justice coalition identified in Opinions; all six adopt both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Pending describes a review process awaiting completion, not only a paper physically awaiting one judge's order. A contrary construction would consume the federal period during steps ordinarily needed to complete state review and encourage premature federal filings. California's original-petition terminology does not change that practical function. The dissent correctly insists that a properly filed application, not a merely possible future claim, is required. The Court's rule retains that requirement and confines interval protection to timely continuation of the ordinary course; extraordinary or unrelated filings do not qualify automatically. Artuz distinguishes filing conditions from claim-relief bars and leaves pendency separate. Duncan requires state rather than federal review. Boerckel explains the ordinary state-review round, without making every extraordinary remedy part of it.

##### State timeliness and the actual federal calculation remain necessary

**Holding and operative rule:** An alternative merits discussion does not establish timely state filing or override a valid independent state determination that the relevant interval was unreasonable. The Ninth Circuit must determine the California order's actual meaning, the applicable state timing standard, the supported reasons for this interval and the exact elapsed time, then recalculate the federal limitation without excluding any interval that fails the governing requirements.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion adopts this rule and bounded remand; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also reject a conclusive merits-language inference but do not join the Court's functional-pendency rule or resulting mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The California Supreme Court referred both to the merits and lack of diligence. Its merits phrase therefore cannot alone resolve whether the four-and-one-half-month interval complied with California law. A clear state determination of untimeliness defeats interval exclusion even if merits were also considered. Where the order's meaning is uncertain, the federal court must identify the actual state ground and relevant timing circumstances; certification or another lawful clarification may be used if needed, but is not automatically required. Neither a fixed nationwide deadline nor an assumption that this interval is necessarily reasonable or necessarily late supplies the answer. Proper filing, pendency and reasonable state timing remain distinct. Artuz does not turn an actual filing deadline into a relief bar. Duncan forecloses counting federal habeas pendency as state review and preserves independently available commencement or equitable grounds only on their own requirements. Unpresented equitable tolling and the conviction's constitutional merits are outside this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current Law

- Artuz v. Bennett, November 7, 2000: applied to keep application-filing conditions distinct from claim-relief bars; its actual-deadline-exception reservation remains.
- Duncan v. Walker, June 18, 2001: applied to preserve the state-review category and independent timing requirements; federal habeas time is not included.
- O'Sullivan v. Boerckel, June 7, 1999: applied for the ordinary state-review round, with extraordinary and repetitive procedures distinguished.
- Lee v. Kemna, January 22, 2002: distinguished; exceptional inadequacy of a state procedural ground does not automatically establish statutory tolling.
- Young v. United States, March 4, 2002: distinguished; its bankruptcy collection disability does not supply an equitable exception to these statutory conditions.

Timely ordinary state collateral review remains pending between qualifying successive-level filings, including California original petitions performing that function. State timeliness and proper filing continue to constrain the exclusion. **Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** Section 2244(d)(2) protects a pending application. ### F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. v. Empagran S.A., No. 03-724

Merits decision, June 14, 2004

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 26, 2004. On writ of certiorari to the District of Columbia Circuit, 315 F.3d 338, which allowed the foreign purchasers' antitrust claims under the domestic-effects exception. Foreign purchasers allege a worldwide vitamin cartel and purchases abroad. The question assumes their foreign injuries were independent of domestic price effects. They also assert that maintaining domestic prices was necessary to sustain foreign prices; that alternative's preservation and sufficiency require separate consideration.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 8–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Not participating:** O'Connor.

The appellate judgment allowing claims based on independent foreign injury is set aside. The lower courts must dismiss theories relying solely on that independent injury. The court of appeals must determine whether the asserted dependence on domestic price effects was preserved and, if so, consider its sufficiency under §6a and direct any further proceedings that consideration requires.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER and GINSBURG, JJ., joined, and in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined as to Parts I and III. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg throughout; Stone-Zsela in Parts I and III | Vacates and remands | Part I: coverage, text, structure and claim connection; Part II: territorial deference and enactment context; Part III: application and remand |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | — | Supports vacatur and the conditional remand | Joins Parts I and III; rests on the text, statutory structure and actual causal connection |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Scalia | Thomas | Supports vacatur and the conditional remand | Text permits the claim-specific reading; customary territorial deference supplies the sufficient interpretive reason |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The qualifying effect must give rise to the claimant's own claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 6a excludes nonimport foreign commerce, including wholly foreign transactions, from the Sherman Act unless the conduct has a direct, substantial and reasonably foreseeable effect on domestic or import commerce, or on the foreign export commerce of a person engaged in that commerce in the United States, and that effect gives rise to the plaintiff's asserted antitrust claim. A foreign injury independent of the domestic effect does not qualify merely because that effect gives another person a claim; import trade remains outside the general exclusion, and coverage restored only through the export branch permits recovery only for injury to export business in the United States.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Stone-Zsela: six direct joins. Part II's additional explanation has the five-Justice coalition of Breyer, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas support the operative construction in their separate judgment concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the exception requires a connection to the claim being litigated. The purchasers' reading of the indefinite reference to a claim is grammatically possible, but the text also permits the claim-specific construction. The statute first removes nonimport foreign commerce and then restores coverage for the specified effects giving rise to a claim. A domestic injury suffered by someone else does not supply the connection between the qualifying effect and these purchasers' independent foreign losses. The exception protects the claims arising from that effect.

The territorial interpretive principle preserved by Charming Betsy favors avoiding unreasonable interference with foreign sovereigns' regulation of their own commercial affairs. Applying American damages law to foreign injury independent of domestic harm would create that interference without the required claim connection. This is a construction of the statute, not an empirical demand for incompatible foreign commands or a country-by-country balancing defense. The enactment's clarification of geographic reach, including its change from export-only wording to nonimport foreign commerce, supports this reading and confirms that wholly foreign transactions fall within the exclusion. The legislative context reinforces rather than contradicts the enacted language.

Hartford Fire involved American-market effects giving rise to the claims before the Court and expressly preserved §6a's operation. Its rejection of the asserted foreign-permission defense did not erase the interpretive canon. The purchasers' deterrence argument and government enforcement cases involving broad remedial decrees do not establish a private damages claim for independent foreign injury. Claims of foreign injury dependent on domestic harm require their own analysis.

##### II. The assumed independent injury is excluded; the alternative requires its own procedural and statutory inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** On the assumed independence of these purchasers' foreign injuries, §6a's effects exception is unavailable. The judgment is vacated, and the court of appeals must determine preservation of the alternative claim that domestic price effects caused the foreign loss and, if preserved, assess its legal sufficiency under the statute and direct appropriate further proceedings.

**Authority:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Stone-Zsela, has six direct joins; Scalia and Thomas independently support the same mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The question decided rests on independence, so the asserted global cartel cannot itself establish the missing causal connection. The purchasers' alternative theory says domestic price maintenance was necessary to sustain foreign prices. That contention was not decided below; its preservation and sufficiency are therefore left for the appellate court, which must apply the claim-specific requirement rather than assume dependence from worldwide pricing. Its legal sufficiency is the genuinely unaddressed question; applying a settled rule to supported facts remains ordinary remand work.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Hartford Fire Insurance v. California, 509 U.S. 764: distinguished because its substantial American-market effects gave rise to the claims it sustained; its reserved §6a question is now resolved for independent foreign injury, and its retained territorial interpretive canon remains effective.
- Murray v. Schooner Charming Betsy, 2 Cranch 64: applied as an interpretive principle favoring a permissible construction consistent with customary territorial restraint. The principle operates in construing §6a, not as an automatic defense based on foreign permission.

#### Law After Decision

The effects exception restores coverage only when the qualifying statutory effect gives rise to the actual claim for relief. Independent foreign injury falls outside that exception even when the same conspiracy harms American commerce. Import commerce retains its distinct statutory treatment, and an export-only basis retains the limitation to injury to export business in the United States. If preserved, the dependent-injury theory must be evaluated under those conditions; a worldwide cartel allegation alone does not establish them.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The text and statutory structure require the domestic effect to give rise to the plaintiff's own asserted claim. Hartford Fire preserves its domestic-injury holding but does not decide independent foreign injury. International enforcement interests cannot erase the statutory connection. The Chief joins Parts I and III and would require identification of the challenged transactions and causal theories, determination of preservation, and application of the domestic-effect and causation requirements under ordinary pleading and proof rules. Unsupported independent-injury theories must be dismissed; a properly preserved dependent theory receives its own analysis, without assuming dependence from global pricing.

**Scalia, concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** The text readily permits the claim-specific construction, and customary deference to other nations' regulation within their territories makes that construction sufficient to decide this case. These Justices accept the operative coverage rule, the independent-injury result and the defined remand. They withhold assent from the Court's legislative-history and broader explanatory route. Their territorial canon is a rule of statutory interpretation, not discretionary comity dependent on proof of incompatible foreign commands.

542 U.S. 155; No. 03-724; Empagran S.A. v. F. Hoffmann-La Roche, Ltd., 315 F.3d 338; 15 U.S.C. §6a(1)(A)–(B), (2), and final sentence; Sherman Act; Clayton Act §4. Independence of the foreign injury is the assumption underlying the reviewed question; causal dependence is an alternative contention whose preservation and sufficiency remain for consideration.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The court of appeals must give effect to vacatur, determine whether the alternative domestic-effects theory was preserved, and, if it was, consider statutory sufficiency and the further proceedings needed. The claim-specific rule governs that work. Claims based solely on the assumed independent foreign harm cannot proceed under the exception.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. A court-presented dismissal election requires minimum neutral notice

**Controlling proposition:** When a federal court directs an unrepresented prisoner with a timely mixed petition to choose between proceeding on exhausted claims and dismissing to exhaust other claims, and the materials before it disclose a substantial risk that dismissal will defeat timely return, it must give neutral notice that dismissal without prejudice does not preserve the original filing date and that a later petition may be time barred. The court must provide a meaningful opportunity to delete unexhausted claims and proceed on exhausted claims, subject to their ordinary requirements.

**Authority:** Part I, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** A court controls the election it places before a litigant. Accurate explanation protects the existing filing opportunity without selecting the prisoner's strategy. Castro v. United States supervises judicial recharacterization that can consume a first-motion opportunity; its reasoning supports this new supervisory protection for a different judicial act. Duncan v. Walker supplies the concrete risk because federal pendency does not stop the limitation period.

The duty requires neither investigation of missing records, precise deadline computation, evaluation of claim strength, strategic advice nor a promised stay. Mere statutory complexity or an unsuccessful filing is not its trigger. It is a minimum duty, not an exhaustive statement of judicial responsibility when a court presents available options. The objection that Ford selected habeas does not answer responsibility for an election the court itself directs; the duty arises from that intervention and the apparent filing risk, rather than pro se status alone.

Ford's timely filings had only five days remaining, and the period expired while they were pending. The actual communications and responsive conduct require separate examination for each petition. The protection applies to these pending dismissal consequences and later qualifying elections; it supplies no new full limitation period.

##### II. A proved court-created loss can receive equitable protection

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2244(d) permits equitable tolling for omission of the required notice or another materially misleading judicial direction that causes loss of an otherwise timely federal proceeding or prevents timely return, when the petitioner establishes the required causal connection and reasonable diligence. A notice violation alone does not establish reliance, causation or diligence, and causal loss alone does not establish that tolling is the legally available remedy for the actual timing defect.

**Authority:** Part II, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch, without joining the omitted-notice branch.

**Controlling explanation:** Duncan excludes ordinary federal pendency from statutory tolling while leaving independent equity open. The Court resolves that reservation for the defined court-created impediment. The rule responds to a supported loss caused by the court's own procedure; it does not automatically convert processing delay, pro se status or an unfavorable calculation into an obstacle. The court must identify a lawful remedy that can cure the actual defect, distinguishing loss of the still-pending timely original from prevention of timely later filing.

The objection that this rewrites Duncan mistakes the source of protection. Federal pendency itself remains outside §2244(d)(2). Relief depends instead on judicial interference, causation, diligence and an effective lawful remedial basis. That distinction also prevents an incomplete election from becoming automatic reinstatement. Carey preserves the actual requirements for qualifying state pendency, and Slack preserves independent timing despite a renewed application's first-application status. Those requirements govern the resulting calculation.

##### III. The petitioner must prove an effective causal remedy

**Controlling proposition:** The petitioner must prove the factual predicates for this equitable protection by a preponderance of the evidence, including reasonable diligence during the relevant period and after the obstacle ended; diligence is assessed in the actual circumstances, not as perfect diligence or a duty to anticipate and correct the very misleading direction at issue. The court must identify the precise interval requiring protection, justify its causal and lawful remedial basis, exclude independently avoidable delay, and explain how the remedy cures the whole timing defect, including any expiration before the election.

**Authority:** Part III, directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; O'Connor also joins these requirements for the actual materially misleading judicial direction branch.

**Controlling explanation:** Grogan v. Garner and Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston identify preponderance as the ordinary civil allocation of factual uncertainty, subject to the statute, interests involved and any applicable higher standard. These procedural facts determine whether ordinary adjudication may proceed. The prisoner's liberty interest supplies no reason to increase the prisoner's burden, while finality is protected by the causal and remedial requirements. The Court applies those proof principles to this inquiry; distinct statutory or remedial predicates retain their own standards.

Both original petitions were timely when filed, but their period expired before dismissal. Protecting only days after dismissal cannot simply be assumed to make the returns timely. The court must distinguish a lost timely original opportunity from prevented later filing. A later omission need not physically cause earlier elapsed days, but the supported causal chain and chosen lawful remedy must actually reach that expiration. A tolling theory must justify the interval protected; a reopening theory must meet its independent lawful prerequisites. Automatically subtracting all federal pendency would contradict Duncan. Without a lawful basis curing the defect, the later application remains untimely.

##### IV. Omitted warnings do not automatically join separate actions

**Controlling proposition:** A separate later petition does not automatically become an amendment to a closed original action because required warnings were omitted. Relief depending on reopening requires an independently lawful reopening predicate; whether relation back follows an independently established improper dismissal and lawful reopening remains unresolved because that procedural predicate has not been established here.

**Authority:** Part IV, directly adopted by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Ninth Circuit's remedy treated the repeated claims as though Ford had deleted unexhausted claims, obtained a stay of retained exhausted claims and timely amended the still-pending petitions after exhaustion. Those procedural acts cannot be supplied merely by finding defective advice. A genuinely retained and stayed original differs from a terminated action, and Slack's first-application classification does not turn one into the other. The omitted-warning theory therefore cannot sustain automatic relation back. A separately supported lawful correction must be assessed on its actual conditions.

General authority and standards for mixed-petition stays remain unresolved because no position on that distinct question commands a majority. The minimum notice rule does not require deciding that broader procedure.

##### V. The repeated claims return for individualized adjudication

**Judgment and authority:** Seven Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—vacate the repeated-claim disposition and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Souter adopt Part V's affirmative-rule rationale; the other four support vacatur on their no-advisement ground. Ginsburg and Breyer would preserve the lower vacatur of the repeated-claim dismissals by partial affirmance. Parts I–IV supply the controlling rules for further proceedings despite that remedial disagreement.

**Application and remand:** Loguercio's express election and Weed's nonresponse must receive separate treatment. The lower courts must determine preservation, actual communications, responsive conduct, causal loss and reasonable diligence, then identify an authorized remedy curing each petition's timing defect or apply the time bar. The two new Loguercio assistance claims remain dismissed, and factual development concerning legal-paper access on the five new Weed claims continues under the existing direction.

### Caron v. United States, No. 97-6270

Decision, June 22, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 21, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the First Circuit. The question is whether §921(a)(20) preserves a conviction's federal firearms consequences when state restoration of civil rights retains a restriction on some firearm possession.

Massachusetts restored Caron's civil rights by operation of law. He held the required identification permit and could possess rifles and shotguns because the convictions were more than five years old, but could not possess handguns outside his home or business. A December 1993 search recovered six rifles and shotguns and ammunition. A jury returned four §922(g)(1) convictions. The initial enhancement counted one California and three Massachusetts convictions. After an en banc decision rejected the requirement of individualized restoration, the District Court excluded the Massachusetts convictions; the reviewed First Circuit judgment counted them because a significant firearms restriction remained.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Souter and Thomas.

The Massachusetts convictions remain countable under the reviewed restoration question. The Court imposes no new sentence and decides no independent constitutional challenge, other predicate dispute or sentencing issue outside that question.

#### Opinions and Joins

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. They join the complete conviction-status rule, its application to the actual residual restriction, the reservations and affirmance. Justice Thomas dissents, joined by Justices Scalia and Souter; they would reverse and remand on the express-prohibition construction and, independently, on residual lenity.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A continuing state firearms restriction retains the conviction's federal effect

**Controlling proposition:** When a State restores civil rights but expressly retains the substantial firearm disability presented here—prohibiting handgun possession outside the home or business—the restoration does not remove the conviction under 18 U.S.C. §921(a)(20). The retained conviction has the federal consequences prescribed by the applicable firearms statutes, including for rifles and shotguns the State permits the person to possess; those statutes' independent offense and enhancement requirements still must be met.

**Authority:** The six-Justice Opinion of the Court directly adopts the statutory rule, its explanation, application, limits and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 921(a)(20) excludes a restored conviction unless the restoration expressly retains the identified firearms prohibition. The conviction is the object classified, not a sequence of licenses for individual weapon transactions. Beecham directs the Court to the convicting jurisdiction's law to identify restoration. The federal statute then determines its consequences. Massachusetts's automatic restoration and permission to possess rifles and shotguns are accepted here, but its significant remaining handgun restriction prevents the conviction from being disregarded under the federal unless clause.

The contrary reading correctly emphasizes the actual state permission. It fails, however, to explain why Congress's qualification would disregard a continuing state firearms disability whenever some other possession remained lawful. The State need not prohibit every firearm in every place before the federal qualification has work to do. Congress borrowed state restoration status while prescribing its own broader consequence. That structure resolves the competing constructions; under Beecham and Chapman, lenity does not displace a meaning established by the ordinary interpretive inquiry. The separate statutes still determine which convictions qualify, what conduct and commerce connection are prohibited, and which enhancement applies.

The conclusion rests on the actual Massachusetts restriction, not a finding that Caron is individually dangerous. Love recognizes the personal and incorporated arms guarantee but does not resolve every convicted-person restriction. Neither that constitutional holding nor Staples's offense-specific factual-knowledge rule rewrites this restoration clause. Bryan addresses knowing unlawful dealing under a different provision. No distinct constitutional or mental-state issue is decided.

**Application and limits:** The Massachusetts convictions remain countable against the restoration objection, and the First Circuit's statutory judgment is affirmed. This is not a complete Massachusetts ban on handguns: possession in the home or business remained permitted. The Court accepts the parties' restoration premise without deciding every automatic restoration mechanism. It does not reconsider original violent-felony classifications, separately adjudicate the California predicate, pronounce every firearms disability constitutional, impose a new sentence or resolve an independently cognizable issue outside the presented restoration question. Section 922(g)(1)'s prohibition and commerce predicates remain distinct from §924(e)(1)'s three qualifying convictions, separate-occasion condition and minimum fifteen-year sentence.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States:** Applied to the convicting-jurisdiction selector and connected restoration text; this case decides the residual-restriction question that Beecham left open.
- **Chapman v. United States and United States v. Granderson:** Their contextual interpretation and residual role for lenity remain. The Court finds no unresolved ambiguity after examining this text and structure.
- **Dickerson v. New Banner Institute:** Congress's later state-law selector controls over its former federal conviction-definition rule. A general federal protective purpose cannot create an additional statutory predicate.
- **Love v. Pepersack:** Its personal-right and incorporation holdings remain governing; the validity of a particular firearms restriction requires its own properly presented constitutional question. Lewis's extant-felony disability remains undisturbed.
- **Staples v. United States and Bryan v. United States:** Their distinct, offense-specific knowledge rules do not supply an exception to the restoration provision or a newly adjudicated mental-state issue here.

#### Law After Decision

The Massachusetts convictions remain available for federal firearms consequences despite permission to possess the seized kinds of long guns. Section 921(a)(20) classifies the conviction; the substantive prohibition and enhancement provisions supply their separate legal effects and requirements. State restoration is not a weapon-by-weapon federal exemption. The decision settles no independent constitutional challenge, mental element or remaining sentencing question.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Thomas, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia and Souter.** The three would reverse and remand because Massachusetts did not expressly prohibit firearm possession in the sense required by the federal exception. It allowed rifles and shotguns and even allowed handguns in the home or business. A restriction on handgun possession elsewhere does not amount to the express prohibition that, on their reading, prevents restored convictions from being excluded.

Their construction also treats the conviction as the statutory unit. It does not make the same conviction exist federally for a forbidden handgun and disappear for a permitted rifle. They choose the defendant's side of the all-or-nothing inquiry: because the restoration does not contain the required complete possession prohibition, these restored convictions are excluded. Beecham's sovereign selector supports consulting the actual restoration but did not decide this partial-restriction question. Federal protective purpose cannot replace Congress's chosen express condition.

Independently, the textually natural restoration reading remains at least sufficiently unresolved after context and purpose are examined to require lenity. Chapman and Granderson reserve that principle for ambiguity surviving ordinary interpretation; the dissent relies on the actual statutory difficulty, not merely the existence of two proposed readings. It would remove this unless-clause basis for counting the Massachusetts convictions and return the reviewed sentencing consequences for lawful disposition. It orders neither acquittal on all counts nor exclusion of every other predicate, and it decides no constitutional challenge. Both the textual ground and residual-lenity ground have the assent of all three dissenters.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The First Circuit's statutory judgment remains effective. Any further sentencing proceedings must treat the Massachusetts convictions as retained against this §921(a)(20) objection while respecting the independently applicable offense, enhancement and procedural requirements. This Court orders no new sentence or separate constitutional disposition.
