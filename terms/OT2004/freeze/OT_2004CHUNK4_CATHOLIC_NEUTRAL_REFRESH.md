## Entering law

The exact holdings in the supplied entering-law B volume establish:

- **Johnson v. California, No. 03-6539, May 3, 2004:** A state judgment rejecting a federal reversal ground is not final while substantive independent appellate claims remain and effective review can follow an adverse final judgment, absent an established Cox exception. The party invoking jurisdiction must establish finality or the exception; agreement cannot confer jurisdiction. The importance of the federal right creates no general immediate-review exception.
- **Jefferson v. City of Tarrant, December 9, 1997:** Conclusive resolution of an issue under state law is not necessarily a final judgment where substantial liability, causation or other claim proceedings remain, the question need not survive every outcome, later correction remains effective, and postponement does not destroy the asserted federal interest. State law-of-the-case treatment alone creates no exception.
- **Florida v. Thomas, June 4, 2001:** A substantive remand and a genuine alternative ground can defeat finality when the federal issue may disappear and effective later review remains. All four Cox routes remain available on their actual predicates; a merely theoretical future appeal does not establish that later review is practically effective.
- **Richards v. Jefferson County, June 10, 1996:** The first Cox category permits review when the ruling conclusively bars the relevant remaining claims and leaves implementation preordained despite an interlocutory state label. Its actual conclusively terminated claims distinguish it from an assumption that every settled issue ends a case.

The four Cox categories are distinct. They concern proceedings practically concluded or implementation preordained; an issue necessarily surviving every remaining outcome; an issue for which effective later review is unavailable whatever the outcome; and a federal reversal that would preclude the relevant litigation where delay seriously threatens federal policy. None is an automatic exception for an injunction, a religious claim, a mandate proceeding, or a completed state appellate opinion.

## Threshold remedies and merits boundaries

If no final judgment or Cox exception is established, dismissal for want of jurisdiction leaves the state disposition in place without affirming, reversing or vacating its federal merits. It supplies no federal constitutional endorsement, no permanent-injunction ruling, no exemption, and no direction to enter a particular state judgment. Any later review would depend on its own properly presented judgment and applicable requirements.

If a supported practical-finality category permits review, the Court then considers the preserved constitutional questions and the actual preliminary-relief posture. A threshold holding that review is available does not establish entitlement to permanent relief.
