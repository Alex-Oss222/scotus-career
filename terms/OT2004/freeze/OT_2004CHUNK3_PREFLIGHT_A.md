## GDF Realty: validated neutral packet

### Facts, posture and corrections

The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the federal officials, 326 F.3d 622, with rehearing denied, 362 F.3d 286. The challenge concerns §9's application to six listed subterranean invertebrate species limited to two Texas counties, on land proposed for commercial development. The parties agreed that no factual dispute prevented cross-motions for summary judgment. The owners seek relief against the constitutional application; they do not obtain a permit through that claim.

The absence of a commercial market is supported. It must not be expanded into a finding that no specimen ever crossed a state line: the published account reports scientific work, travel and museum transfers involving other States. Those record facts are distinct from a constitutional holding about their significance. The agency's assessment that scaled-back development with specified habitat measures could avoid a take is an agency statement, not a presently issued permission or an unconditional judicial finding of developability. Incidental-take applications were denied; a separate compensation action was stayed. None of those facts establishes an adjudicated violation of the taking prohibition.

The asserted review channel, threatened injury and concrete as-applied constitutional question permit merits resolution. No independently supported threshold ground displaces it.

### Entering law and live alternatives

The operative register is **Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects**. *Lopez* (April 26, 1995) requires an eligible connection before magnitude or aggregation. *Home Builders* (June 8, 1998), Parts II-A and III, supplies the necessary-inclusion rule and identification requirement. *Rancho Viejo* (February 5, 2004), Parts II and III, applies that rule to another listed species confined to one State, with all nine direct joins. Its Part I retains the enacted-class rule with seven joins. The later firearm-component dates in the register do not change the effective dates or operation of these wildlife components.

The required inquiry identifies the independently valid interstate regulation, the covered taking class and a genuine functional relationship. The Act regulates exploitation, import, export, interstate movement, commercial disposition, and handling of unlawfully taken listed wildlife. Protection against unauthorized depletion preserves the populations and enforceability of that system. Courts determine the constitutional boundaries independently and respect reasonable congressional judgments within them. A national objective or a collection of unrelated provisions is insufficient; necessary inclusion is not a least-restrictive-means inquiry. A challenger may show an outside-class application, an invalid interstate regime, or a nominal or pretextual relationship. The absence of a market in an individual species supplies none of those showings by itself.

Path A affirms by applying that settled class-and-system relationship. Its strongest objection is that including an untraded local species makes the national scheme its own justification; the answer required by existing law is the identified population-protection and enforcement function, not the scheme's size or biodiversity's importance. Path B grants application-specific relief only upon a supported distinguishing defect in that relationship, or expressly changes *Home Builders* and *Rancho Viejo*. Its strongest obstacle is that the packet identifies no difference material to the already sustained listed-wildlife class. A new species-by-species market or independent-effects condition would be a doctrinal change, not an application of entering law. There is no remaining ecological fact necessary to resolve this power question.

*Sweet Home* separately governs actual habitat-caused injury, significant impairment of essential behavior, proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability; speculative injury or bare but-for linkage is insufficient. Its distinct statutory qualifications and §10's actual permit conditions remain legally operative. The Commerce judgment itself calls for no new determination on those independent claims.

## Women's Medical: validated and supplemented neutral packet

### Applicable enactment and litigation scope

The reviewed Sixth Circuit judgment, 353 F.3d 436, reversed the permanent declaratory and injunctive judgment against the revised Ohio enactment. The granted questions concern ordinary D&E coverage and the adequacy of necessary-health protection. The permanent district judgment addressed previability protection and postviability protection for women medically needing an abortion; it used the proposition that the method must be available when the evidence establishes it is safest. That proposition must be assessed against governing necessary-care law rather than accepted as a constitutional rule. The permanent judgment did not finally decide the D&E issue, which the preliminary decision had rejected and both parties requested the appellate court resolve. The published account establishes a broad enforcement injunction, but not its exact territorial or binding-party terms; those terms are not invented.

The district court applied Ohio recklessness requirements to viability and applicability of the health exception; neither party appealed that ruling. The providers did not appeal the standing rejection of their independent challenge to the third-party civil-action provision. Those matters are outside this review. Protection against an unconstitutional substantive prohibition must be matched to its lawful scope and actual defendants; this case does not revive the unappealed third-party standing claim.

House Bill 351 became effective August 18, 2000. Its drafting and adoption preceded the historical Supreme Court *Stenberg* disposition, following earlier lower-court litigation. The source identifies no statute-specific predicate of abrogating, codifying or modifying a Supreme Court decision absent from this timeline. The Engine §2 predicate check therefore supplies no statutory-existence blocker. The precise revised enactment, rather than the earlier statute reviewed in *Voinovich*, governs.

All four acts in (A)(3) must occur in sequence: intentional cervical dilation; intentional extraction of the specified part of an intact fetus beyond the vaginal introitus but short of complete delivery; the specified intentional act known to cause death; and completion of vaginal delivery. The anatomical extraction threshold differs between breech and cephalic presentation. These are not interchangeable alternatives, and an intention attached to one act cannot erase the other enacted conditions.

Division (F) expressly excludes suction curettage, suction aspiration and D&E. Division (A)(1) distinguishes D&E from dilation and extraction. Division (G) independently excludes a legal abortion where the death-causing act occurs before partial birth, even if death occurs afterward. Division (E)'s patient immunity is distinct from a physician's exception. Divisions (B) and (C) independently govern viable and nonviable fetuses; both use the same complete life-or-health necessity language. Division (D) makes violation a second-degree felony.

The prohibition applies when the procedure is not necessary in reasonable medical judgment under the enacted life-or-health criterion. Division (A)(5) defines the serious-risk category through a medically diagnosed condition complicating pregnancy and substantial **and** irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The statutory wording must remain visible. It is not the former *Voinovich* alternative-method affirmative defense, and no former prima-facie showing or prosecutor burden shift is imported. The full statute is copied mechanically in the extract; this paraphrase does not replace it.

### Clinical record and competing constructions

The source reports district findings and expert evidence that the intact procedure may reduce infection; trauma from repeated instruments; disseminated intravascular coagulation; blood loss and anesthesia time; and retained fetal tissue. Comparisons with induction include duration, labor-agent adverse effects, hemorrhage, infection and surgery to remove retained placenta. Hysterectomy and hysterotomy are more traumatic. Inducing demise first may add risks from digoxin or cord severance, including embolism, internal injury, arrhythmia for patients with cardiac problems, perforation and hemorrhage. These are reported clinical findings and evidence, not a finding of universal intact-method superiority or necessity in every listed circumstance.

The lower majority construed (A)(5) prospectively, without requiring an already existing complication, to cover predicted significant comparative procedural risks. Its description used substantial **or** irreversible impairment. That is its judicial description, not the enacted conjunction and not an authoritative Ohio high-court construction. The lower dissent read the diagnosed-condition language as excluding healthy pregnancies exposed to future procedural risks; it accepted a protective construction for certain postviability physical conditions while disputing mental-health coverage. These competing constructions require legal assessment against the actual words and applicable Ohio law.

The record contains evidence concerning rape, incest and abuse victims and severe depression, obsessive-compulsive illness and manic-depressive illness. The published account expressly identifies the absence of detailed final district findings on mental-risk applications. The theory must be accurately framed: it is grave mental danger from continued pregnancy combined with the clinically necessary physical method of an indicated abortion, not simply distress caused by selecting D&E rather than the intact procedure. Neither that theory nor the reported diagnoses establishes that every affected patient requires the prohibited method.

### Entering law, complete paths and remand work

The operative register is **Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation**, present operation effective *Casey*, June 29, 1992. Its whole rule has a direct majority and needs no combination of separate rationales. *Voinovich* (April 20, 1998) and *Stenberg* (June 28, 2000) apply it, retaining the independently sufficient necessary-care baseline, the actual-conduct inquiry, clinically usable alternatives, proof allocation, and violation-matched relief. A prohibited baseline injury, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle cannot be rescued by justification. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated justification and fit, including an answer to an identified concrete, practicable and substantially less burdensome alternative. Challengers first establish duty, affected population, causation and triggering interference. Clinical disagreement supplies neither a professional veto nor automatic defeat of supported necessary care.

Path A sustains a precisely defined intact-method restriction if actual exceptions protect timely necessary care and the remaining interference satisfies the complete standard. Its strongest contrary point is that usable ordinary D&E for many patients does not establish safe and suitable alternatives for a supported subgroup, and the enacted health category cannot be widened by replacing its conjunction. Path B protects established serious-risk applications and returns the unresolved clinical and construction applications for adjudication under the settled rule. Its strongest contrary point is that comparative advantage alone is not necessity and this revised statute supplies safeguards Nebraska lacked. The record therefore does not support automatic transplantation of *Stenberg*'s whole application or an automatic whole-statute injunction.

The ordinary-D&E question can be answered through the actual sequential elements and express exclusions, without a categorical assumption that labels control. The health question requires recognition that necessary risk reduction includes supported serious harm from compelled substitution; it cannot depend exclusively on an independently diseased pregnancy. Grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury retains *Voinovich*'s protection. No patient must await catastrophe. The lower court must apply those rules to the credited comparative-risk record and supported subgroups, determine any protective construction permitted by Ohio law, and protect any established excluded applications. A construction may not insert an unlimited exception or treat unsupported preference as necessary care.

The ordinary factual, clinical and remedial work on remand is not unresolved constitutional law. The full standard governs remaining previability interference. Ohio §1.50, *Geiger* and *Maurer*, as applied in *Voinovich*, govern independent operation, legislative intent and the limits on judicial insertion or fundamental disruption. Actual dependent sanctions must follow the scope of any constitutional protection; the independent, unappealed third-party standing determination remains effective. The lower court must promptly adjudicate the needed interim protection under ordinary equitable standards and the existing record. No new evidentiary hearing is automatic, and no current stay is inferred from the mere fact of review.
