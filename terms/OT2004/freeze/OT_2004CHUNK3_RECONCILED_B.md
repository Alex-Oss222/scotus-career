### Final reversal ground and remedy — five Associates

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas join the following rule, application and remedy in full.

An unqualified ruling that the prosecution's evidence is insufficient on a count is a completed acquittal when the ruling is implemented and the trial proceeds through the defense phase without a reserved sufficiency question or a preexisting procedure plainly making that kind of ruling provisional. A general power to reconsider ordinary interlocutory orders does not by itself establish that status for this required finding of not guilty. Once the acquittal is complete, renewed guilt factfinding is forbidden despite legal error, continuation before the same jury, or the absence of demonstrated tactical harm.

Here Rule 25(a) required a ruling at the close of the prosecution's evidence. The judge unqualifiedly allowed the insufficiency motion; the allowance was endorsed and entered; the prosecution then rested without a reopening motion; the codefendant presented a witness; and both defendants rested. The judge reserved neither further argument nor a later sufficiency determination. The subsequent authority supplied a reason to regard the original evidentiary assessment as legally wrong, not evidence that the ruling had remained unfinished. Smith need not personally have testified for that completed procedural transition to matter.

*Price* supplies substantive completion and the whole-episode inquiry. *Martin Linen* makes the insufficiency determination an acquittal despite its label; *Smalis* protects it against renewed factfinding; *Fong Foo* preserves finality despite legal error; and *Scott* distinguishes a termination unrelated to factual guilt. The Commonwealth's strongest answer is that its appellate court recognized ordinary reconsideration throughout a continuing jury trial. The five conclude that this general power does not establish the necessary provisional status of an unqualified required finding of not guilty after the defense phase has proceeded. State law supplies the procedural setting; it does not finally determine the federal effect of the substantive acquittal. Rule 42's clerical-error correction does not answer an attempted reconsideration of a legal conclusion.

A separately established genuinely provisional procedure presents a different completion inquiry. The present rule does not settle the validity of every such procedure or freeze every favorable interlocutory ruling. This qualification preserves *Price*'s boundary without an advisory design for other states.

The five vote to reverse the Massachusetts judgment insofar as it sustains the firearm conviction and to remand for removal of that conviction and its dependent punishment, with no renewed guilt adjudication on the acquitted count. The two independent assault convictions remain operative. The state courts implement the lawful sentencing consequences of removing the firearm count; its concurrent term does not preserve the conviction. No new trial or general reopening of the assault judgments follows.
