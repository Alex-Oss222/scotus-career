No same-matter historical Supreme Court opinion, current commitment, reconciliation, controlled supplement or private Record was opened. No excluded-material exposure occurred.

The Fifth Amendment's public-use and compensation conditions are distinct. The constitutional text supplies the restriction; it does not itself settle every contested meaning of public use. A source establishing condemnation power for governmental functions does not necessarily exhaust constitutionally permissible uses, and a source establishing a private-use prohibition does not necessarily settle whether this comprehensive redevelopment is public or private.

The relevant primary sources and their actual work are:

- **Cole v. City of La Grange, 113 U.S. 1, 6–8 (1885):** The case rejects municipal bonds aiding an enterprise whose purely private character was admitted by the demurrer. Its discussion of Missouri's compensation provision treats the provision as presupposing a private-use prohibition. The opinion distinguishes a toll mill serving all customers and railroads accommodating public travel. This supports an analogy distinguishing public functions from ordinary private manufacturing, but the actual financing holding and admitted private character do not adjudicate the public character of the redevelopment record here. [Opinion text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/113/1).
- **Kohl v. United States, 91 U.S. 367, 371–374 (1876):** The opinion recognizes federal condemnation power needed to carry out constitutional governmental functions, illustrated by the postal and judicial facilities sought there. It separates the national power from a State's permission and examines statutory authorization and the judicial channel. It establishes governmental use as a valid category, not an exhaustive rule forbidding every privately implemented public-purpose taking. Its separate opinion contests judicial authority and the statutory route, not the full definition of constitutionally permissible public use. [Opinion text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/91/367).
- **Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432, 446–450 (1985):** The majority rejects a new heightened classification while examining whether the actual permit distinction has a rational connection to legitimate concerns. Irrational prejudice and unsupported fears do not become valid reasons through local opposition. This is an equal-protection example of meaningful rationality review, not a public-use holding or an automatic redevelopment scrutiny rule. [Opinion text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/473/432).
- **Department of Agriculture v. Moreno, 413 U.S. 528, 533–538 (1973):** The opinion rejects a food-assistance classification lacking a rational relation to legitimate objectives, distinguishing a bare desire to harm an unpopular group from a permissible interest and examining the asserted fraud rationale against the law's actual operation. Its analogy concerns legitimate ends and genuine rational connection. It does not hold that every incidental private benefit is forbidden or prescribe a burden for redevelopment condemnations. [Opinion text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/413/528).

## Existing law and proposed change

The existing controlling rule accepts a rationally supported public purpose and permits private implementation. Berman v. Parker and Hawaii Housing Authority v. Midkiff supply that pre-divergence law; the controlling Boston & Maine holding applies it to compensated acquisition followed by private reconveyance for passenger service while excluding naked private favoritism. None confines all public use to government ownership or universal public access. A lower court remains bound by those propositions unless this Court changes them.

A proposed original-use construction would instead require governmental ownership or actual governmental use, or a legally enforceable public entitlement to use the property. Common-carrier facilities illustrate how private title could satisfy that construction without making incidental employment or revenue benefits sufficient for ordinary private premises. This is a distinct legal argument that may be considered by this Court, not a statement of present law or a conclusion established by the few earlier examples identified here. Calling it an original-use argument does not establish the historical meaning it advocates; any broad historical claim beyond these sources would require its own support.

The strongest reason for that proposal is that the text's public-use condition must do work independent of compensation and of ordinary legislation promoting prosperity. A possible consequence is that a private commercial successor's greater economic productivity cannot supply the missing public use. The strongest contrary argument is stare decisis and the established distinction between purpose and title mechanics: the public-purpose precedents expressly reach beyond direct government use, and a comprehensive project cannot be invalidated simply because private actors implement it. Adoption of the proposed restriction requires candid treatment of Berman, Midkiff and the broader rationale of Boston & Maine, including which holdings or propositions are displaced and which actual service applications remain valid.

Boston & Maine's actual passenger-service arrangement may be distinguishable under an enforceable-public-use theory because it secured continuing passenger use. That possible distinction cannot erase the broader rationale the case adopted. Nor does an agency-explanation objection to that order decide the constitutional meaning of public use. The Court may preserve a case's concrete result while changing part of its rationale only by identifying that treatment accurately.

The separate possible categorical exclusion for ordinary economic redevelopment must also be distinguished from the broader original-use proposal. A line allowing removal of harmful land conditions while excluding economic improvement of ordinary homes would need to explain Berman's area-based redevelopment and Midkiff's treatment of concentrated ownership on their actual terms. Those cases cannot be converted into uncompensated nuisance regulation. A power to prohibit harmful conduct, a power to acquire property, and the obligation to compensate for an acquisition are different questions.

## Meaningful review within the existing rule

Retaining the public-purpose rule does not require accepting a recited economic benefit when supported evidence shows that the undertaking's actual object is private favoritism and the public explanation is pretextual. Courts can examine the record and the relationship between the stated purpose and the action while preserving the ordinary presumption of lawful public action. The relevant question is the actual public character of the undertaking, not a demand that courts choose the best development policy or guarantee its success.

Cleburne and Moreno help test the argument that rationality review must be wholly perfunctory. They supply analogies concerning impermissible ends and irrational connections; neither automatically transforms redevelopment review into heightened scrutiny. A conclusion that a supported accusation of private favoritism deserves examination does not itself shift the ultimate burden, require a checklist of planning features, or make every identified private beneficiary presumptively disqualifying. Such additional rules would be legal changes requiring their own grounds and a record presenting their triggers.

The strongest objection to ordinary deference is that any transfer to a more profitable private user might be described as increasing employment or tax revenue, allowing the constitutional condition to disappear. A meaningful anti-pretext boundary must therefore remain real. The strongest objection to a stronger general rule is that it could substitute judicial assessment of development forecasts and chosen implementation for the accepted public-purpose inquiry. The independent model should test whether enforcing the existing private-favor boundary answers the concern on this record, without assuming that every possible future transfer shares this plan's circumstances.

Whether a distinctly suspicious class of transfers might warrant stronger scrutiny can remain an unresolved doctrinal boundary. If that question is not required to decide the supplied case, the model need not invent its triggers, allocate a new burden, or issue a categorical rule. A reservation should identify the legal question and why this record does not require its resolution, rather than imply that the Court has adopted an unspecified heightened standard.

## Record and remedial limits

The supplied record concerns an adopted comprehensive plan responding to economic distress, with multiple projected uses and private implementation. The homes were not individually condemned as blighted. The packet reports no finding of a sham solely favoring an identified recipient. That last proposition is not a new affirmative finding that all possible pretext evidence was absent or that every future transaction will be lawful. Economic forecasts remain forecasts; later success, failure, legislation and recipient behavior are outside this event.

The available branches remain: apply the existing public-purpose rule with meaningful anti-pretext review; adopt and explain a narrower economic-development restriction; adopt and explain the more general original-use restriction; or remand if a supported material public-purpose or pretext issue was not properly adjudicated. A remand cannot be justified by invented parcel facts or an imagined private recipient. A finding of unconstitutional private use would not be cured merely by increasing compensation. A ruling sustaining federal public use does not displace stricter state-law requirements or resolve compensation amounts.

Lingle's rejection of regulatory efficacy as an independent compensation-based taking test remains distinct from this direct public-use challenge. San Remo Hotel's issue-preclusion rule likewise does not convert direct review of the Connecticut condemnation judgment into a separate collateral attack. The original entering-law cutoff and published prior positions remain unchanged.

This source supplement is sufficient for modeling these legal branches. It establishes no present Justice's choice among them and adds no factual reconstruction premise.
