## Reconciled substantive alternatives

**K1 — Public-purpose affirmance.** A comprehensive redevelopment undertaking can satisfy public use even though private parties implement it and particular homes are not individually blighted. The undertaking must serve an actual, rationally supported public purpose. A supposed public justification cannot save an acquisition whose actual object is favoring a private recipient and whose public benefits are merely incidental or pretextual. The ordinary presumption of lawful public action remains; a supported accusation of impermissible favoritism deserves meaningful examination of the record, rather than acceptance of recited benefits alone.

Berman permits area-wide treatment and private implementation. Midkiff makes the public end, rather than the mechanics of the conveyance, decisive. Boston & Maine carries that principle into the present law. The adopted plan addresses prolonged economic distress through coordinated commercial, residential and public components. On the supplied record it is an actual public undertaking, and no supported omitted pretext determination requires a remand. Its forecasts remain forecasts. The Court does not require proof that projected employment or revenue will actually materialize, adjudicate future transactions, or hold that any isolated transfer yielding more taxes necessarily qualifies.

The owners' strongest objection is that greater productivity could always justify replacing an existing owner. K1 answers through the public character of the actual undertaking and the enforceable private-favor prohibition. It rejects both a categorical exclusion of economic redevelopment and a rule confining privately implemented public acquisitions to removal of existing harmful land conditions. Governmental ownership and enforceable public access remain sufficient examples, not exhaustive constitutional requirements under the existing precedents. Compensation and stricter state law retain independent force.

**K2 — Precedent-based economic-successor restriction.** Ordinary private property cannot be condemned for transfer to new private users solely because their ordinary activities are predicted to produce greater economic benefits. Public ownership and actual public service remain valid categories. Berman's harmful-area condition and Midkiff's harmful ownership concentration distinguish their acquisitions from the displacement of these ordinary homes: in those cases the acquisition itself removed the identified harmful condition. The common dissent can leave those precedents' particular results intact while rejecting an extension to ordinary successor benefits here.

This position must acknowledge that the broad police-power and public-purpose language in the precedents favors the City. It confines that language at the proposed economic-successor boundary rather than claiming that an existing categorical exclusion already controls. The dissent does not convert compensated acquisitions into nuisance regulation, find corruption, or assert that a larger payment could cure a taking for an impermissible use. Sincerity and comprehensive planning do not answer its substantive objection because incidental public gains accompany almost every ordinary private use. It would reverse and remand for relief preventing these challenged condemnations under that rationale.

**K3 — Further public-use reconsideration.** A distinct position reads the public-use condition to require governmental ownership or actual governmental use, or a legally enforceable public entitlement to use the acquired property. Common-carrier property can meet that requirement despite private title. Predicted employment and tax benefits from ordinary private premises cannot. Cole and Kohl illustrate public functions and the independent private-use prohibition, but neither alone proves an exhaustive founding-era practice. The argument rests here on the constitutional text and structure and the demonstrated limits of those illustrations, without an unsupported historical consensus.

That approach calls for reconsidering Berman's and Midkiff's broader public-purpose propositions and any incompatible permission for private transfers. It would also replace Boston & Maine's broader rationale while leaving its actual passenger-service acquisition supportable on the narrower secured-use basis. A noncontrolling proposal to reconsider those precedents does not itself overrule them. The case can be decided against the present taking through the narrower K2 alternative while the further constitutional proposal is preserved separately.

## Reconciliation changes and precise writing functions

The clean refresh cured the earlier absence of a developed K3 path. It does not supply a changed in-world fact or precedent authorizing disregard of the comparator. Thomas's refreshed refusal to join any K2 preservation of Berman and Midkiff is corrected at its overly categorical scope. The historical source expressly combines his join of the common dissent with a further call to reconsider those precedents; the supplied current law contains no new Thomas public-use commitment that makes that combination unavailable. His prior agency objection did not settle this constitutional question. The supported formulation therefore treats K2 as an alternative resolving the present case under distinctions in existing law and states his broader disagreement separately. It does not attribute permanent approval of the harmful-condition precedents to him, or turn his further proposal into current law.

That correction follows the full source and the absence of a material changed premise, not the desire to copy a historical opinion title or lineup. The independent refreshed legal analysis supporting K3 is retained. The complete common K2 alternative remains available to all three reversal supporters; its text must preserve Thomas's express distinction between use of an existing precedent and approval of its constitutional correctness.

Kennedy's historical concurrence supplies two substantive functions: supported private-favor accusations require genuine record review under the ordinary presumption, and affirming this comprehensive plan does not foreclose stronger scrutiny for a different, narrowly drawn suspicious category. Both fit the refreshed commitment. They must be preserved in the common explanation or a separate concurrence without inventing a burden, compulsory checklist, universal presumption of invalidity, or unverified planning findings. Mere presence of an incidental private beneficiary is not enough. No historical concurrence is copied solely because it was historically published.

The K2 dissenting explanation must address why an authentic plan still lacks the proposed substantive public-use condition, why earlier harmful-condition acquisitions differ, and why future economic benefits and state-law safeguards cannot substitute for the asserted federal boundary. Thomas's further writing must state his text-and-structure basis, the specific public-purpose propositions he would revisit, and the narrower basis potentially preserving actual passenger-service acquisitions. It may explain that private-use and compensation restrictions serve independent purposes and that ordinary regulation and eminent domain are distinct; it must not add unsupported historical anecdotes, demographic findings or future consequences.

## Remedy, law and source map

Affirmance sustains the federal public-use judgment and permits the approved condemnation process subject to compensation and applicable state law. It determines neither a compensation amount nor later implementation. Reversal supporters would remand for relief preventing the challenged acquisitions under their proposed constitutional restriction. No Associate seeks a remand to investigate an invented pretext theory.

Only the final Court's controlling opinion can change law. Under the five-Associate K1 position, Berman and Midkiff retain their public-purpose force, Boston & Maine remains a service application of that rule, Lingle retains its separate regulatory-taking boundary, and San Remo Hotel remains confined to its preclusion function. K2's limiting construction and K3's further reconsideration remain noncontrolling unless independently adopted at assembly by the required coalition.
