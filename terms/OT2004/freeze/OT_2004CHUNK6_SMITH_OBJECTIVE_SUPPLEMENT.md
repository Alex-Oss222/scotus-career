## Objective record recitations

The source describes adoption of the citywide pay plan on October 1, 1998, followed by a May 1, 1999 revision for police and police dispatchers. That revision was motivated at least in part by bringing police starting salaries to the regional average. Officers with fewer than five years of tenure received larger percentage raises; some officers over forty were in that group. Most older officers had more than five years of service.

The plan arranged each of five positions — police officer, master police officer, police sergeant, police lieutenant and deputy police chief — into steps and half-steps. The wage ranges came from a survey of comparable southeastern communities. Each employee was assigned the lowest step in the employee's position that still provided a two-percent raise. The three lowest ranks contained employees on both sides of forty; the two highest ranks contained only officers over forty. The latter received larger dollar raises but smaller percentage raises because their salaries were higher.

The officers' statistical evidence showed that 66.2 percent of officers below forty received raises above ten percent, compared with 45.3 percent of officers over forty. The average percentage raise for officers with fewer than five years of tenure exceeded that for those with longer tenure. These are reported figures, not an independent causal or reasonableness finding.

## Scope limits

The source reports that the district court entered summary judgment on both intentional-treatment and impact theories, and that the appellate court returned the intentional-treatment branch for further discovery. That branch must not be silently terminated by resolution of the impact question.

A judicial characterization that the officers failed to isolate a specific practice is not treated here as a freestanding objective fact. If that claimed omission is to become an independent dispositive ground, the actual claim and briefing need examination. The present supplement supplies the pay-plan operation and proffered statistics; it does not establish a litigant concession, procedural waiver or the opportunity to address a newly selected appellate ground. The cited published factual recitations may support legal evaluation of the plan's stated nonage rationale without treating a missing research document as a litigation failure.
