## G1 — Classification and court authority

The supplied motion challenges only the federal limitations ground that prevented adjudication of the guilty-plea claim already pleaded. It does not ask the court to replace a federal merits determination, add a custody claim or receive a new factual merits supplement. Under *Abdur'Rahman*, it is a proper procedural Rule 60 request rather than a second or successive application. Section 2244(b) therefore does not require appellate authorization for this request. An actual new or renewed merits component would require its own successive treatment; the supplied record contains none to separate. The majority rule already exists and is applied, not newly created.

Florida's strongest contrary position is that reopening allows a prisoner to obtain custody relief after final judgment and thus circumvents AEDPA. That argument would erase *Abdur'Rahman*'s operative distinction. Classification concerns what the present filing asks the federal court to correct, while Rule 60's demanding independent requirements protect finality. A procedural label alone is insufficient, but the eventual possibility of reaching an originally unadjudicated claim is not a renewed merits demand. Scalia's and Thomas's former objections to the express merits and cumulative prayer in *Abdur'Rahman* must be distinguished rather than converted into a categorical position they did not publish.

All eight Associates directly join G1, including its actual-content and merits-exclusion limits. No Associate proposes overruling *Abdur'Rahman* in this case.

## G2 — Extraordinary relief: two supported remedial paths

**G2-A — Affirm the denial on the independent Rule 60 ground.** A later interpretation of a procedural statute, combined with an original nonmerits dismissal, does not establish extraordinary circumstances on this record. Gonzalez abandoned further certificate review after April 2000, then waited months after *Artuz* before seeking reopening, and supplies no separately established external impediment explaining that course. *Abdur'Rahman* specifically makes omitted ordinary review and the intervening development separate considerations rather than granting reopening upon reclassification. The combination here fails the extraordinary-circumstances showing, so the judgment denying relief is affirmed on this alternative ground despite rejection of the circuit's categorical successive reasoning.

The strongest counterargument is that the original claim has never received a federal merits hearing and *Artuz* corrected the threshold legal understanding that prevented one. Those circumstances matter, but do not render ordinary review optional or make every such judgment perpetually reopenable. The rule is an assessment of this actual combination, not a categorical holding that a legal change can never contribute to extraordinary circumstances or that a certain number of months is always excessive. It does not impose the later *Rhines* procedure retroactively or treat every procedural change as a new filing opportunity.

**G2-B — Vacate and remand for a proper exercise of Rule 60 discretion.** The procedural nature of the judgment and intervening correction of the barrier may, in combination, have exceptional weight because the claim has never received merits adjudication. The circuit's legally categorical disposition did not supply an ordinary Rule 60 evaluation on that premise. The proper remedy is to return the motion for an exercise of discretion considering that combination, the available ordinary review, actual timing and finality; it is not an order reopening the petition or granting habeas relief.

The strongest objection is that *Abdur'Rahman* itself directs attention to abandoned review and the present record supplies enough to deny relief without a factual remand. G2-B does not posit an undiscovered external impediment. It disputes the legal weight and the first-instance exercise of discretion, contending that the absence of an external obstacle is not an indispensable condition for all exceptional process correction. Months of delay and an unpursued certificate route are significant, but do not categorically settle how the erroneous nonmerits barrier and *Artuz* operate together. This path's remand is for adjudication under the correct legal category, not to manufacture evidence.

## Frozen coalitions, relief and separate-writing functions

Their partial concurrence/dissent function is substantive: preserve classification agreement while disputing the adequacy of appellate denial under the extraordinary-circumstances standard. No author is assigned. Their disagreement is not that every legal change requires reopening or that *Artuz* proves the original petition timely.

The affirmance-compatible explanation must answer the remand position by stating why this supported combination can be resolved without inventing a factual dispute or replacing discretion with a fixed delay rule. A later statutory interpretation and a procedural dismissal can contribute to relief in a different combination; this movant's showing remains insufficient. The surviving original federal judgment continues to bar merits adjudication unless lawfully reopened. Under the dissenting remedy the lower court would adjudicate Rule 60 first; original habeas timeliness and merits would follow only if reopening is granted. *Artuz* and *Pace* do not decide those underlying facts here. Independent certificate requirements retain their own triggers; no certificate is supplied merely by classification. These are provisional commitments for separate reconciliation and do not establish current law.
