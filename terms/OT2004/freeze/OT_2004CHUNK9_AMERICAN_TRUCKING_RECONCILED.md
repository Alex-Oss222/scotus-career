## Actual question and retained controlling framework

The challenged assessment is Michigan §478.2(1)'s $100 annual per-truck fee for point-to-point intrastate commercial hauling. It applies to carriers undertaking that activity regardless of whether they also engage in interstate hauling. The actual record supports local size, weight, insurance and safety regulation and fails to substantiate the alleged discrimination or clearly excessive interstate burden. Those are record-based conclusions, not a premise that missing research materials establish litigant failure. The separate §478.2(2) charge and Mid-Con preemption question do not decide this constitutional case.

For the six Associates accepting the governing framework, the actual charged local activity supplies the starting point. Jefferson Lines preserves independent nexus, fair-apportionment, nondiscrimination and fair-relation requirements. A genuine local charge does not tax an interstate passage merely because the truck also carries interstate loads. General services supporting the activity can establish fair relation without dollar-for-dollar or mile-for-mile equality. If all States assess the same local-hauling fee, a carrier pays repeatedly only when it undertakes distinct local hauling in those States.

NEMA requires actual Pike review even for an evenhanded measure, without a threshold showing of disparate interstate impact. The challenger must substantiate the asserted interstate burden and show that it is clearly excessive relative to putative local benefits. Here the per-local-mile comparison alone does not establish that the per-truck local-regulation charge discriminates, and the claimed practical interstate disruption is not substantiated. Scheiner's charges for highway use, including interstate passage, do not invalidate every flat local-business fee.

## Alternative path, remedy and formulation

The challengers' strongest contrary argument invokes Scheiner's unequal practical incidence and the cumulative cost if every State imposes a flat fee. The six-Associate response turns on the genuine local-hauling event and the actual evidentiary record. Local business in several States is several taxable or regulated local activities; no governing rule guarantees equal annual fees per mile. The local label alone does not exempt the charge from review, but the existing record supplies no ground for invalidation or a new evidentiary hearing. Scalia's different response is the absence of facial discrimination and of an indistinguishable previously invalidated measure. Thomas's response is his foundational constitutional objection to the asserted doctrine.
