## Reconciled propositions and application

**J1 — Vacatur as fact: all eight.** A state order vacating the particular conviction used to enhance a federal sentence is a fact supporting the prisoner's challenge under event-date section 2255 paragraph 6(4). Its issuance changes the predicate's status; it is not merely a later judicial opinion stating general law. The Government's old-facts-only approach would start time before the needed state order exists and undermine the proper-channel route Custis and Daniels preserve. A timely motion proceeds under the otherwise applicable requirements for relief from the current sentence.

**J2P — Procurement and discovery: O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.** Notice of the state vacatur starts the one-year paragraph 6(4) period when the prisoner acted with reasonable diligence to obtain it after entry of the enhanced federal judgment and to learn of the order. Entry of that judgment establishes the federal stake; reasonable diligence does not demand the fastest conceivable action. The express diligence condition has work to do where the prisoner initiates the process producing the new fact. State collateral rules protect state judgments, but do not replace the distinct federal interest in timely challenges to federal sentences.

The adopted rule includes all three qualifications: (1) after diligent initiation, delay in state proceedings not attributable to the prisoner does not defeat the later trigger; (2) if facts underlying the state challenge were not discoverable through diligence until after federal judgment, diligence is measured from their discoverability, and diligent pursuit then permits time to run from notice of the resulting vacatur; and (3) even a prisoner who did not act diligently may use paragraph 6(4) by showing that the federal motion was filed within one year of the notice date diligent pursuit would have produced. These are parts of the rule, not optional equitable exceptions.

**J3P — Affirm on this ground: the same four.** Johnson did not begin the state challenge until February 6, 1998, after the November 29, 1994 federal judgment. Even crediting all time before April 24, 1996, he waited more than twenty-one months. His stated limited education, self-representation and waiting for inmate assistance do not explain reasonable efforts or a specific impediment during that protracted interval. The federal extension request did not pursue state vacatur. The application assesses those supplied explanations and the known interval; it infers no default from unavailable research materials. On that record, the four retain the judgment that prompt pursuit would have yielded notice more than a year before the eventual federal motion, so the hypothetical-diligent-notice qualification does not save this application. This is a legal application to the known sequence, not a finding of an invented exact notice date or an automatic rule that every delay defeats paragraph 6(4). The lower judgment is affirmed while its categorical refusal to treat vacatur as fact is rejected.

**J2D — Discovery alone: Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg.** Paragraph 6(4) requires diligent discovery of the existing order and federal filing within one year of when diligent discovery would reveal it. It does not impose an additional federal schedule for bringing the order into existence. An event cannot be discovered before it occurs. Custis and Daniels direct the challenge into the state channel; success there supplies the particular new fact, while state procedural rules and federal claim requirements retain their separate force. The ordinary relative promptness of notice does not erase the discovery requirement, which still governs delayed learning of an order.

**J3D — Reverse and remand: the same four.** Reverse the categorical timing dismissal and return for proceedings under that discovery rule and the remaining requirements. The supplied interval of just over three months between vacatur and federal filing supports the bounded return; actual notice and filing dates govern any remaining application of the adopted trigger. The disagreement concerns the limitation rule, not a present award of sentence reduction.

## Separate-writing substance and join boundaries

The discovery-only position needs a substantive separate explanation if the procurement rule controls. Its grounds are that discovery and creation are different; the federal requirement has no enacted start date for obtaining state relief; state rules can protect state judgments; the federal-judgment starting point burdens defense resources during direct review and can generate unnecessary state proceedings if the federal judgment is reversed; and an undefined procurement schedule creates avoidable collateral litigation. A discussion of finality as a less disruptive alternative to judgment is criticism of the chosen trigger, not assent to a procurement duty beginning at finality. The writing would reverse under its discovery rule, preserve ordinary discovery diligence and retain the actual merits requirements.

The procurement position answers that the federal sentence has its own finality interest, the movant-controlled origin of the event makes pursuit part of the express diligence demand, and entry of the enhanced judgment supplies a concrete federal reason to act. Reasonableness and the three express qualifications answer real impediments without allowing unexplained postponement. If the discovery-only rule instead controls, the four procurement Justices retain this opposing rule and affirmance ground. Historical rhetorical hypotheticals need not be imported to state either position adequately.

The actual rehearing explanation supports this approval. ## Reconciled propositions and remedy

**D1 — Economic loss and causal connection.** A public-market purchaser does not establish the economic loss and loss causation needed for private section 10(b) damages merely by alleging or proving that the purchase price was artificially inflated by the challenged fraud. The plaintiff must establish an actual economic loss proximately caused by that fraud; the PSLRA's section 78u-4(b)(4) assigns the proof burden. A price inducement and the source of a compensable loss are distinct.

At purchase the buyer receives a security bearing the prevailing market price; sale before the inflation dissipates can pass that inflation to another purchaser. A later price decline may reflect other economic, industry or issuer conditions. These possibilities do not foreclose an actual loss partly traceable to the earlier inflation, but defeat the claimed inference from inflation alone. Basic's reliance framework connects deception with the transaction, not every later loss with the deception. Central Bank preserves the actual loss and causation elements of the received action. The device representation and profitability statements concern separate causal histories; the earnings-related decline cannot simply be assigned to the device claim.

**D2 — Even ordinary fair notice is missing.** Assuming for purposes of decision that ordinary Rule 8 supplies the full demand for pleading economic loss and loss causation, the complaint must give a short intelligible indication of the loss claimed and its causal connection to the challenged fraud. The reviewed device branch supplies only inflation and consequent damages, and therefore fails even that modest demand. The Court need not choose a separate heightened Rule 9 or PSLRA loss-causation requirement to reach that result. Swierkiewicz prevents transformation of trial proof into a pleading checklist; it does not cure the absence of a legally adequate causal-loss account.

The principal contrary argument treats overpayment as an immediate diminution of wealth. It does not explain an actual causal loss in a security whose market value may still carry the inflation or may later change for unrelated reasons. The adopted response requires an account of loss and link, not a completed sale, a single formal corrective announcement or an econometric model. Those requirements are neither supplied by the statute nor necessary to reject this inflation-only theory.

**D3 — Judgment and remand.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's reinstatement of the reviewed device branch on its inflation-only rule and remand for ordinary proceedings. The existing complaint cannot proceed on that theory. Any proposed amendment receives the treatment the ordinary rules authorize: amendment is neither commanded nor forbidden, and a legally insufficient theory remains subject to dismissal. The separate profitability/scienter branch remains outside the granted loss-causation review. The remand supplies no new factual allegation linking the distinct announcements.

## Divided application of the independent-tying rule

**M2R — Vacatur and remand: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** The appellate patent-scope ground does not decide whether the presented tying claim satisfies its independent elements. The court acknowledged the demand inquiry and expressly declined to resolve it; this Court should not replace that missing inquiry with an invented conclusive finding about product identity. Vacate the antitrust rejection and remand under the correct rule, leaving ordinary evidentiary and summary-judgment burdens in place. A supported failure of proof may yield renewed summary judgment. Any effect of a proven wrong on contract enforcement requires its own preserved legal ground.

These five answer the reproduction-license objection by accepting it as a substantial merits defense rather than a categorical substitute for the declined demand inquiry. The express step from CSU is that a patent-scope label cannot conclusively immunize a condition meeting the established section 1 offense, just as it cannot conclusively immunize independently unlawful monopoly maintenance. Kodak's section 1 rule supplies the relevant elements; section 2's elements are not transplanted. Trinko confirms separate offense and remedy predicates. Neither a compulsory license nor a present tie follows merely from removal of the legal immunity.

**M2A — Affirmance on a confined alternative ground: O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas.** The actual theory seeks authorization to use patented second-generation seed while paying a technology fee rather than buying newly licensed seed. In these Justices' assessment, refusal to offer that reproduction transaction does not identify a distinct coercive acquisition or independently unlawful tie. The presented demand is for an ungranted right in the additional patented item, not a demonstrated requirement to buy an unpatented product as the condition of an otherwise licensed use. Affirm the antitrust rejection on that confined absence of an independent wrong.

They preserve liability for a genuine independent tie and do not adopt the lower court's universal scope immunity. They answer the remand group's strongest objection at the conduct level: reconsidering the product label or the claimant's preferred transaction does not cure the failure of the presented theory to identify the required wrong. Their Kodak concerns about proprietary-product dependence, CSU refusal analysis and Trinko independent-offense objection support that result. They do not join M2R's conclusion that the record requires the further demand inquiry.

## Separate-writing function and audit conclusion

The three affirmers' separate position must explain the reproduction-license distinction, why the presented conduct fails to identify an independent tie, the category of genuine independent antitrust wrong they preserve, and why they would end this antitrust component while accepting the common patent holding and damages mandate. It is a partial dissent from remand, not a universal patent-immunity opinion. The five's controlling rationale answers that conduct defense as a matter to be assessed under actual separate demand and ordinary proof rather than the conclusive legal ground below.

The material difference from the historical appellate disposition is explained by effective CSU and Kodak law. Stevens's prior CSU/Trinko reasoning, Kennedy's Kodak/CSU/Trinko joins, Souter's same joins, Ginsburg's independent-offense and causal-injury positions, and Breyer's substantial-justification approach each support the five's remand. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas preserve the historical appellate result on a different, confined ground grounded in their own prior public positions. No Supreme Court historical-departure claim is made because no such comparator exists. No frozen vote changes, new material neutral fact or unresolved blocker results.
