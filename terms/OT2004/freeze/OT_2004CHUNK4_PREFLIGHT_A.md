### Governing paths and strongest objections

The current racial-classification standard places justification on government: an important lawful objective, evidence-supported substantial relationship, supported coverage and burdens, and an answer to concrete practicable alternatives providing comparable effectiveness with materially less racial disadvantage. Oneonta applies that obligation despite absence of hostility and despite another person's description supplying the racial criterion. Genuine custodial threats can matter to justification. Neither ordinary prison deference nor the label protective automatically supplies the required showing.

The leading supported path corrects the governing standard and returns its actual application, with damages immunity and prospective relief separately resolved. Its strongest objection is the operational need to act safely with incomplete information; that objection must be tested within the governing rule, preserving supported urgent or individualized precautions. A broader present invalidation path relies on the policy's near universality, repeated transfers and available individualized information; its strongest objection is that the appellate court used a different legal burden and did not fully resolve application under the controlling standard. A Turner-only affirmance path must explain how the specific racial rule and Lee survive its proposed prison exception; it cannot simply treat Adarand as absent. A strict-scrutiny proposal must be identified as reconsideration or a separate view, because that categorical formulation is displaced here.
