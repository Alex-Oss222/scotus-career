## 14. Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys
* **Citation or Docket:** 356 F.3d 1302; lower-court No. 02-8059.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-12-13 (scheduled simulated merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the undue-hardship exception to student-loan nondischargeability under 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(8) requires a certainty of hopelessness, or permits discharge under the *Brunner* framework based on a realistic assessment of future repayment ability and good-faith efforts to avoid default?
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tenth Circuit affirmance of the district court and bankruptcy court's discharge of the student debt. The simulated grant concerns that merits judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** ECMC seeks rejection of the discharge under a more demanding undue-hardship standard; Polleys seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** The bankruptcy court found persistent impairment of Polleys's earning ability, minimal resources and no discretionary income sufficient for the loan payment. The approximately $51,000 balance required about $420 monthly. She had made no payments but had sought work, including work outside her training. Her mental-health limitations and resulting employment difficulties were supported findings. The dispute distinguishes inability from deliberate avoidance, not merely an asserted preference for a lower income.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The §523(a)(8) adversary proceeding produced a discharge judgment. Undisturbed factual findings are reviewed for clear error; the legal standard and legal effect of those findings receive independent review. No finding of permanent total disability or deliberate creation of hardship should be invented.
* **Relevant Dates:** The lower appellate decision is February 4, 2004; the assigned event is December 13, 2004. The pre-2005 Bankruptcy Code governs. Later bankruptcy amendments, repayment programs and agency discharge policies cannot be imported.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-12-13.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(8), bankruptcy adversary procedure and appellate review. The debtor must establish the statutory exception under the ordinary civil proof standard.

##### Issue 1: Meaning and proof of undue hardship

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No earlier Supreme Court holding prescribes a student-loan-specific test. Lamie v. United States Trustee enforces enacted bankruptcy conditions rather than rewriting them to achieve a desirable policy result.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ordinary text and statutory structure control. An exception cannot be erased by an extra-statutory demand for certainty, nor converted into a general equitable cancellation power. Findings and legal conclusions retain their distinct review standards.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 523(a)(8) requires undue hardship to the debtor or dependents. Its operative version supplies neither a certainty-of-hopelessness formula nor a permanent-disability prerequisite.
* **Material Other Authority:** Brunner v. New York State Higher Education Services Corp. is persuasive lower-court authority organizing present minimal living needs, likely persistence and good faith. Grogan v. Garner supports the ordinary preponderance framework in discharge litigation; it does not itself select this student-loan test.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory hardship requirement controls, but the Supreme Court has not resolved the precise formulation as of June 30, 2004. Circuit disagreement is not itself binding simulated law.
* **Open Point:** Whether a realistic, evidence-based application of the three-part framework gives effect to the exception and supports the discharge on the existing findings.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The appellate court adopted Brunner with a realistic future-ability inquiry and good faith focused on legitimate efforts rather than judicial moral judgments; it found the existing findings sufficient. **ECMC:** Nondischargeability is the statutory norm, future improvement remains possible, and lack of payment undermines good faith. **Polleys:** Supported long-term earning limits and a bare subsistence budget establish hardship; no payments are unsurprising when payment is impossible. **Alternatives and limits:** A return for missing findings is proper only if a necessary finding is actually absent. Fresh-start policy cannot eliminate the statute, and an imagined future improvement cannot defeat supported evidence. No verified separate Solicitor General submission is identified.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Affirm under a realistic three-part standard if the established inability, persistence and good-faith findings satisfy the debtor's burden; the debt remains discharged.
* Reverse or remand if the legal test omits a statutory requirement or the findings do not support it; the creditor then retains its collection rights subject to the bankruptcy judgment and any further determination.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule may reject certainty of hopelessness and permanent disability while requiring sustained, evidence-supported hardship. It need not decide partial discharge, every repayment-plan option or the effect of later amendments.

**Uncertainty:** The grant is hypothetical and supplies no additional Supreme Court briefing. The record supports the identified findings; no current income update or later repayment option is assumed.

## 15. Whitfield v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Whitfield v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 03-1293 and 03-1294, consolidated with Hall; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 209.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-11 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether conviction for money-laundering conspiracy under 18 U.S.C. §1956(h) requires allegation and proof of an overt act in furtherance of the agreement.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 349 F.3d 1320, affirming the challenged conspiracy convictions and rejecting the requested overt-act instruction.
* **Relief Requested:** Whitfield and Hall seek reversal based on omission of an overt-act requirement; the United States seeks affirmance of the instruction ruling.
* **Material Record:** The defendants were prosecuted for an agreement to commit money laundering. They requested an instruction requiring an overt act, which the district court refused. The preserved question is the content of §1956(h), not whether the jury actually found every other element or whether a different conspiracy count would require an act.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The consolidated dockets constitute one listed matter. The statutory instruction issue is preserved. The Court should resolve neither an unpresented sufficiency challenge nor a separate venue objection merely because conduct in furtherance appears in a venue provision.
* **Relevant Dates:** Section 1956(h) was enacted in 1992. The later venue provision does not retroactively add an element without operative statutory text. The event is January 11, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Hall is the consolidated companion within this consolidated matter. Timken, scheduled the same day, concerns delegated antidumping administration and creates no sentencing or conspiracy dependency.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-11.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal criminal review; the Government must prove the actual offense elements beyond a reasonable doubt. A requested instruction is required only if it accurately states an applicable legal element.

##### Issue 1: Agreement and the omitted overt-act element

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Shabani holds that §846 requires the unlawful agreement and knowing participation, without an additional overt act; it expressly does not announce a universal result for every conspiracy statute. Ratzlaf v. United States reserves lenity for ambiguity remaining after ordinary construction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the offense's own text and structure, distinguish the general conspiracy statute, and retain the Government's burden on agreement and knowing participation. A penalty clause can help define an offense without incorporating every element of another statute.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §§1956(h), 1956(i), 1957 and 371. Section 371 expressly includes an overt act; §1956(h) contains no such requirement and does not cross-reference §371.
* **Material Other Authority:** Nash v. United States and Singer v. United States explain the common-law agreement baseline and the significance of Congress's selection of statutory language. Their relevance is textual context, not a universal conspiracy exemption from proof.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Shabani provides a close interpretive analogy but its holding concerns §846. Whether §1956(h) creates an independent conspiracy offense must be resolved here.
* **Open Point:** Whether the text's same-penalties formulation incorporates §371 or instead independently punishes agreement to commit the identified laundering offenses.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The appellate court treated §1956(h) as a separate conspiracy offense without an overt-act element. **Defendants:** The provision primarily raises punishment for a conspiracy otherwise defined by §371; its placement, legislative history and venue language support retaining the overt-act safeguard. **United States:** The provision directly identifies the prohibited agreement, omits any §371 incorporation, and parallels the text interpreted in Shabani. **Limits and alternatives:** Absence of an overt-act element does not eliminate proof of an actual agreement, guilty knowledge or the charged unlawful object. Venue is a separate question. No independently verified oral concession is assumed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Affirm the no-overt-act instruction ruling by construing §1956(h) as an independent agreement offense; other preserved grounds remain subject to their own disposition.
* If the statute incorporates §371, determine the consequences of the instructional omission under the governing preserved-error rules rather than automatically acquit on an untried element.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The language and structure support a rule for §1956(h) and materially equivalent text, not all conspiracies or all references to conduct in venue statutes.

**Uncertainty:** The review concerns the overt-act question. No additional element finding, independent reversal ground or new sentencing issue is assumed.

## 16. Timken Co. v. United States

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Timken Co. v. United States
* **Citation or Docket:** 354 F.3d 1334, reviewing 240 F. Supp. 2d 1228; no Supreme Court docket identified.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-11 (scheduled simulated merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the antidumping statute permits Commerce to replace negative dumping margins with zero in an administrative review, despite the fair-comparison requirement and the United States’ international trade obligations?
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Federal Circuit affirmance of the Court of International Trade's judgment sustaining Commerce's calculation for the identified Japanese tapered-roller-bearing review.
* **Relief Requested:** Koyo seeks rejection of zeroing and recalculation; the United States defends Commerce's method. Timken's separate adverse-facts calculation cross-appeal is part of the historical litigation but outside the identified zeroing question.
* **Material Record:** Commerce reviewed the October 1998 through September 1999 period. It aggregated positive margins while treating transactions above normal value as zero rather than offsets. Koyo relies on the statutory fair-comparison requirement and a World Trade Organization (WTO) report addressing a different country's investigation. The dispute concerns legal permission for this method, not a finding that every comparison, adjustment or numerical input was independently correct.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The final trade-court and appellate judgments permit review of the method. The caption does not make Timken the proponent of Koyo's zeroing objection. The hypothetical grant does not extend to the separate entered-value versus sales-value cross-appeal.
* **Relevant Dates:** Commerce's final results issued in March 2001; the Federal Circuit decided January 16, 2004. The assigned event is January 11, 2005. Later WTO disputes, administrative changes and later judicial deference doctrines are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-11.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 19 U.S.C. §1516a review asks whether the determination accords with law and is supported as required. Mead and Haggar require actual delegated authority and its exercise before controlling deference; expertise or longstanding practice alone is insufficient.

##### Issue 1: Domestic statutory permission and delegated method

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Mead Corp. retains Chevron within actual delegated force-of-law authority and otherwise requires independent persuasive consideration. United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. enforces statutory boundaries before considering a permissible administrative choice. Barnhart v. Thomas likewise distinguishes an authorized choice from the sole possible construction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify Congress's delegation, the mechanism used, and the statute's actual limits before addressing reasonableness. An agency may not transform a calculation instruction into authority to disregard a contrary enacted command.
* **Other Governing Law:** 19 U.S.C. §§1675(a), 1677(35) and 1677b(a), including the definition of dumping margin, aggregation and fair comparison. Commerce administers duty liability through the prescribed review and final determination mechanism.
* **Material Other Authority:** Murray v. Charming Betsy supplies an interpretive aid where compatible construction is available; it does not authorize rewriting domestic law. The Uruguay Round Agreements Act specifies domestic effect and implementation of international obligations.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law establishes the review framework but does not decide whether these provisions require negative offsets. The specific aggregation question remains open.
* **Open Point:** Whether the authorized review mechanism and statutory language permit zeroing, and what weight the international report has in resolving that domestic question.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit found the margin definition compatible with positive excesses, treated the specified comparison adjustments as consistent with zeroing, and deferred to Commerce's authorized methodology. **Koyo:** Aggregating margins while ignoring favorable transactions distorts the required fair comparison; international obligations reinforce an offsetting construction. **United States:** The statute targets transactions below normal value, leaves aggregation room and does not require above-value sales to cancel dumped sales; the WTO report does not bind this domestic court. **Timken and other grounds:** Timken's distinct cross-appeal cannot broaden this grant. **Limits:** Lack of direct WTO enforceability does not make international materials irrelevant to interpretation; administrative discretion does not make zeroing mandatory.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Sustain zeroing as a permissible, explained method within actual delegated authority and the statute's bounds; the challenged determination remains effective on this issue.
* Construe the aggregation and fair-comparison provisions to require offsets and return for recalculation under that construction.
* If the actual mechanism lacks delegated interpretive force, independently construe the statute while evaluating Commerce's reasons; absence of deference does not itself decide the answer or require a new agency proceeding.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The decision may settle permission for this administrative-review method under the operative provisions. It need not approve every investigation methodology, declare zeroing compulsory or adjudicate the United States' compliance in an international forum.

**Uncertainty:** No new administrative explanation or international ruling after the event date is assumed. Calculation challenges outside the question presented remain outside this review.
