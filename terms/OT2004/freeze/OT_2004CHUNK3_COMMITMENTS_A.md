## GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton

### Common judgment and complete necessary-inclusion ground

All eight Associates vote to **affirm the Fifth Circuit judgment against the presented Commerce Clause challenge**, deny the owners' requested constitutional exemption and injunction, and end this merits review. No Commerce Clause evidentiary remand is needed. Each joins the following ground at the same level of generality:

Under *Home Builders*, Parts II-A and III, as directly applied in *Rancho Viejo*, Parts II and III, Congress may include the taking of listed wildlife within the independently valid interstate wildlife regime when that class is a reasonably adapted and integral means of preserving the populations and enforceability of the system. The court identifies the valid interstate regulation, the covered class and the genuine functional relationship; reasonable congressional predictions and design choices receive latitude within those boundaries. The Act's regulation of interstate exploitation, movement, importation, exportation, commercial disposition and handling of unlawfully taken listed wildlife supplies the valid regime. Protection against unauthorized taking supplies the population-protection and enforcement relationship. The cave species are within that covered class, and their lack of a commercial market or interstate range supplies no material distinction from the listed fly and toad applications already sustained.

The contrary theory would require each local species to supply an independent economic or substantial-effects showing. That is inconsistent with the established necessary-inclusion rule. A bare declaration of national importance or a nominal interstate provision would be insufficient; this application rests on the statute's identified operative relationship. The owners identify no outside-class application, independently invalid interstate regime, or nominal or pretextual connection. The reported scientific activity is not needed to construct a new species-specific market theory. The independent externality route likewise need not supply this judgment.

The judgment affirms constitutional power subject to the Act's actual enforcement elements and exceptions. *Sweet Home* continues to govern habitat-caused injury and causation, and §10 continues to govern permission. Those propositions preserve the operative division between constitutional authority and ordinary statutory application. They require no new adjudication of the permit denials, compensation action or an actual take.

## Women's Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft

### Separable decisional propositions

**T1 — Statutory scope and ordinary D&E.** The revised law requires all enacted sequential acts and intentions, including the specified extraction of an intact fetus beyond the vaginal introitus and the death-causing act at the specified stage. The express D&E exclusion and distinct prior-death-causing-act exclusion must be given effect. Ordinary D&E does not fall within the prohibition merely because a physician intentionally removes a fetal part, because a medical label overlaps, or because an intact delivery occurs without the enacted intention and sequence. The categorical ordinary-D&E ground for invalidation is rejected. A label does not excuse conduct that actually satisfies the complete defined intact procedure. This resolves the statutory coverage objection without treating an enforcement assurance as an authoritative state construction.

**T2 — Necessary life-and-health protection.** *Casey*, *Voinovich* and *Stenberg* require timely necessary care where compelled substitution creates a medically supported material risk of death or serious health harm and the prohibited method is necessary for safe and suitable care. The protection reaches serious comparative procedural risk without requiring an independently diseased pregnancy. It also preserves grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury, including a medically necessary abortion for that danger when a particular physical method is required for safe and suitable treatment. Unsupported preference, transient distress and every marginal safety advantage do not establish that necessity. Competent disagreement does not eliminate an otherwise supported application. The patient need not await catastrophe.

**T3 — Actual Ohio construction and record application.** The Sixth Circuit's conclusive rejection of the health challenge cannot stand on the present application. Its prospective reading is material but cannot silently substitute “or” for the statute's “and,” or answer the mental-health theory by asking only whether one method causes more psychological distress than another. The lower court must determine whether Ohio law permits a construction of the actual medically diagnosed-condition and major-bodily-function language that protects every constitutionally required application supported here. If it does, apply it; if it does not, preserve constitutional protection against the proved excluded applications. The credited physical-risk evidence and supported mental-health subgroups must receive actual legal application. No new clinical conclusion or universal superiority premise is adopted, and no new evidentiary hearing is automatic.

**T4 — Remaining previability interference.** The complete six-step *Casey* framework remains governing law. Once statutory scope and necessary-care applications are properly identified, any remaining proved previability interference receives that full inquiry: independent baseline, obstructive-purpose and obstacle bars; compelling justification and narrow tailoring for remaining severe interference; substantiated permissible justification and fit for remaining meaningful interference, with an answer to supported practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The providers first establish duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The existing findings and ordinary factual-review rules govern; the existence of an abortion regulation does not alone shift a burden to the State.

**T5 — Disposition and relief.** Reject the categorical ordinary-D&E ground, vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it conclusively rejects the necessary-health challenge, and remand for the legal application and relief described in T2–T4. The lower court must identify and protect the established applications, use Ohio's actual severability rules, and promptly decide interim protection under ordinary equitable principles on the existing record. A permanent blanket reinstatement or immediate blanket enforcement does not follow automatically from the remand. Enforcement consequences dependent on an unconstitutional application must conform to the protection actually entered. The unappealed recklessness construction and independent civil-standing determination remain effective; the remand does not reopen them. The constitutional rules are settled; construction permitted by Ohio law, clinical application and appropriately scoped relief are the work remaining below.

T1 is supported by every Associate. T2 and T3 are supported by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. T4 is supported in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those five support T5 in full. Kennedy supports T5's statutory, necessary-care, severability and prompt interim-protection directions but not the instruction requiring his rejected residual lesser-burden review as his own preferred rule. Scalia and Thomas support affirmance of the Sixth Circuit judgment within the granted scope and oppose the constitutional health/remand components on their expressly stated constitutional ground.

### Opposing-path and remedy reconciliation

The six vacatur/remand votes share a direct necessary-care ground; Kennedy's narrower reasoning is not combined with T4 to manufacture a new rule. The five full-framework Associates directly join T4 at its existing level of generality. No narrowest-ground synthesis is required or proposed. Scalia and Thomas's agreement on T1 and ordinary severability adds no vote to T2 or T3, and their proposed affirmance rests on an explicit constitutional disagreement rather than the proposition that the entering rule already validates the statute.

The source does not support permanent blanket relief now, a conclusive judgment that no health application needs protection, or a rule requiring a physician to choose the intact method whenever it offers any comparative advantage. The remand's constitutional standard is fully specified. Its work concerns application to the clinical findings and supported subgroups, a construction permitted by Ohio law and relief at the proved scope. A state's general authority to define a procedure does not decide that work; the prohibition's exact exclusions and health criterion remain independent components.

All writing limits above are ordinarily honored by omitting the barred proposition. Public negative statements or separate writings are needed only where the adopted opinion's affirmative language would otherwise adopt a barred rule, where a party pressed the contrary rule, or where the actual opinion/remedy difference requires explanation. Kennedy's full-framework disagreement and Scalia/Thomas's opposing constitutional judgment have such substantive functions if those propositions appear. No other separate writing is manufactured from a cautionary limit.
