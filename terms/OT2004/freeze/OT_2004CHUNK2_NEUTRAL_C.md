## 21. Illinois v. Caballes

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Illinois v. Caballes
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-923; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 405.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-24 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Fourth Amendment requires reasonable, articulable suspicion to justify using a drug-detection dog to sniff a vehicle during a legitimate traffic stop.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Illinois Supreme Court, 207 Ill. 2d 504, 802 N.E.2d 202, reversing the conviction because the canine examination expanded the investigation without drug suspicion.
* **Relief Requested:** Illinois seeks vacatur of the suspicion requirement and restoration of the prosecution's ability to use the evidence; Caballes seeks suppression and preservation of the state judgment.
* **Material Record:** A trooper stopped Caballes for speeding. While the trooper completed traffic business, another officer conducted an exterior canine examination; an alert led to a trunk search and discovery of marijuana. The trial court found the alert reliable and the duration lawful. The granted question accepts the lawful traffic duration and assumes no independent articulable drug suspicion. It does not establish a universal rule about dog accuracy or authorize added detention.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal ground controls the reviewed judgment. The limited question is the independent suspicion requirement on the accepted stop facts, not a fresh factual determination of elapsed time. Any genuinely independent preserved state-law protection remains available on return.
* **Relevant Dates:** The stop occurred November 12, 1998. The event is January 24, 2005. The preconduct date does not itself create an exclusionary-rule reliance defense; such a defense requires an available supported basis.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Devenpeck concerns probable cause for an arrest; it does not authorize additional searches during every justified stop. The later Muehler matter concerns questioning and detention during warrant execution, a different source and scope of authority.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-24.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None. Devenpeck’s alternate-offense question does not determine whether a canine examination is a search.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fourth Amendment search and seizure inquiries remain distinct, followed by any exclusion issue. An initial traffic ground does not confer general authority to search a car or prolong its detention.

##### Issue 1: Exterior detection and the limits of traffic authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Whren v. United States permits an objectively justified initial stop while preserving independent scope and duration limits. Ohio v. Robinette requires separate authority after traffic justification ends. Knowles v. Iowa rejects a full search based on citation alone. City of Indianapolis v. Edmond requires authority for the seizure before any canine examination and expressly leaves classification of that examination open.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify each asserted intrusion: lawful presence, restraint, investigative examination and later physical search. A valid stop does not automatically validate another intrusion. Kyllo v. United States protects concealed domestic information but preserves the distinction for contraband-confined detection under Place.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment and ordinary automobile-search probable cause. The State must establish the predicates of an exception or claimed absence of added constitutional intrusion, not merely point to the discovery of contraband.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Place treats a properly limited canine sniff as distinct from a search exposing lawful private contents while independently restricting detention of the luggage. United States v. Jacobsen similarly distinguishes a contraband-only test from a broader invasion.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Duration and ordinary search limits are settled. Applying the surviving contraband-confined detection distinction to an exterior vehicle sniff during a lawful stop remains the presented question.
* **Open Point:** Whether this examination itself adds a protected privacy intrusion or restraint requiring suspicion, despite the accepted lawful duration.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The state court treated the change from traffic enforcement to drug investigation as an unjustified expansion. **Illinois:** An exterior, contraband-confined sniff adds no search and the lawful stop is not prolonged. **Caballes:** A motorist remains seized for a limited purpose; an unrelated targeted examination is a material expansion and dogs are not infallible devices revealing only contraband. **United States:** Supported Illinois, distinguishing a nonsearch investigative step from an independent detention and relying on the already lawful stop. **Limits and alternatives:** No party's success licenses a full citation search, a coerced consent, an extended stop or entry into a protected area. Reliability for probable cause is not established for all dogs by this one record; no oral concession is inferred from the opinion.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Hold that an actually limited exterior sniff adds neither a search nor unlawful restraint on the accepted facts, vacate the categorical suspicion rule and return for any independently preserved ground.
* Treat the examination as a constitutionally material search or expansion, identifying the protected interest and the rule limiting or changing Place; then adjudicate suppression under the existing remedial rules.
* Resolve any actual factual predicate left open without converting the accepted duration premise into permission for a longer detention.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A nonsearch holding can be confined to contraband-confined exterior examination with lawful access and no unlawful added restraint. A broader suspicion requirement must explain its relationship to Place; neither path decides home entry, digital examination or all canine reliability disputes.

**Uncertainty:** The grant assumes lawful duration and no drug suspicion. The record does not establish that every canine method reveals only contraband or that a short unauthorized extension is always permissible.

## 22. Commissioner v. Banks

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Commissioner v. Banks
* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 03-892 and 03-907, Banks and Banaitis; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 426.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-24 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the portion of an otherwise taxable litigation recovery paid to counsel under a contingent-fee agreement is included in the client's gross income, including when state law gives counsel a superior lien.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, 345 F.3d 373, and Ninth Circuit, 340 F.3d 1074, excluding the fee portions on different assignment and state-property rationales.
* **Relief Requested:** The Commissioner seeks reversal in both dockets and inclusion of the fee portions; the taxpayers seek preservation of the exclusions.
* **Material Record:** Banks settled employment claims and paid counsel from the recovery under a contingent agreement. Banaitis likewise resolved employment-related litigation, with a contractual portion paid directly to counsel. The underlying recoveries' taxable character is the premise of this question. The clients retained control of the claims, including settlement decisions. Oregon's lien protects payment, but the dispute is whether it transfers the income-producing claim or secures the lawyer's compensation.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The two tax judgments require separate remands for lawful calculations. Inclusion, exclusion and deduction are different questions. New theories not presented or developed below cannot be treated as established alternative grounds. No separately awarded statutory fee is shown in the Banks settlement.
* **Relevant Dates:** The transactions and tax years predate the American Jobs Creation Act of October 22, 2004. Section 703 requires both qualifying payments and judgments or settlements after enactment. Its prospective deduction is effective law by January 24, 2005 but does not govern these earlier transactions.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Banaitis is the consolidated companion within this consolidated matter; the Oregon lien argument supplies a distinct legal route and is not omitted merely because both taxpayers contest fee inclusion.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-24.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Internal Revenue Code §§61, 62 and the then-applicable deduction and alternative-minimum-tax provisions. State law identifies property rights; federal law determines the resulting federal tax attribution.

##### Issue 1: Attribution of the contingent-fee recovery

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Commissioner v. Schleier and O'Gilvie v. United States require receipt-specific statutory analysis rather than a general equitable exclusion of litigation proceeds. United States v. Burke supplies no controlling general remedial-menu rationale. None decides the distinct assignment question here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine who owns and controls the income-producing claim, then apply the broad income provision and any actually applicable exclusion or deduction. A payment of another person's fee can satisfy the owner's contractual obligation without ceasing to be income attributable to that owner.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 61 and the operative deduction rules. The 2004 Act prospectively addresses covered litigation costs, but does not amend the earlier tax years or prove the meaning of a previously undecided Supreme Court holding.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lucas v. Earl and Helvering v. Horst prevent anticipatory diversion of income from changing its attribution while the taxpayer retains the relevant source. A genuine transfer of the underlying property is analytically different from an agency fee or payment lien.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Broad income attribution and actual statutory exceptions govern. Whether this contractual and lien arrangement transfers the source or pays an agent is the open application.
* **Open Point:** Whether the clients' retained claim control requires gross inclusion despite uncertain recovery value and counsel's protected fee interest.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Sixth Circuit treated an uncertain claim and counsel's contribution as supporting assignment of property; the Ninth treated Oregon's superior lien as pivotal. **Commissioner:** The client owns the legal injury and controls the claim; counsel acts as agent, and direct payment discharges the client's fee obligation. **Taxpayers:** Counsel earns a contingent share through labor and risk, with a property interest stronger than an ordinary creditor's; taxing the client on money never personally received creates a serious deduction mismatch. **Alternatives and limits:** The mismatch is real under the applicable alternative minimum tax (AMT) rules but does not itself create an exclusion. A genuine independent statutory fee entitlement or different ownership arrangement needs its own preserved facts. The United States is a party, not a separate amicus proposing an additional remedy.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Include the contractual fee portion where the client retains the claim and counsel acts as agent, then remand for the correct tax calculation and any independently applicable deduction.
* Treat an actual transfer of the income-producing property as distinct if the legal arrangement supports it; a lien securing payment alone may be insufficient.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The ordinary contingent-fee rule can cover materially similar arrangements across States while reserving genuine changes in claim ownership and separately awarded statutory fees. The Court need not rewrite the AMT or make the 2004 deduction retroactive.

**Uncertainty:** The record does not establish the unpreserved alternative tax theories or a separate statutory fee award. Exact resulting liabilities must be calculated under the applicable tax-year law.

## 23. Howell v. Mississippi

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Howell v. Mississippi
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-9560; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 440.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; case submitted for decision, including the expressly presented federal-preservation question. A threshold disposition does not change the identified matter classification.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-24 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the refusal of lesser-included-offense instructions in this capital prosecution violates the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments, and whether that federal claim was properly presented to or addressed by the Mississippi Supreme Court for §1257 review.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Mississippi Supreme Court, 860 So. 2d 704, affirming the capital conviction and sentence and finding the requested instructions unsupported under the state-law rule.
* **Relief Requested:** Howell seeks relief from the capital judgment based on denial of a supported lesser-offense option; Mississippi invokes failure to present the federal claim and defends the instruction decision.
* **Material Record:** Howell contested identity and the attempted-robbery component of capital murder and requested simple-murder and manslaughter instructions. His state appellate submission argued instruction error through state authorities. He now relies on a citation chain leading indirectly to Beck and on similarity between the state rule and the federal claim. The state court addressed the evidentiary state-law instruction issue rather than expressly deciding the newly articulated constitutional theory.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Court must decide federal presentation before the merits. Meaningful notice, not a mandatory citation formula, controls. An argument that a state court could discover a federal theory is distinct from actual presentation or adjudication. The possible jurisdictional or prudential characterization need not be settled if no justified exception exists under either.
* **Relevant Dates:** The charged killing occurred in May 2000; the reviewed decision issued in 2003. Baldwin v. Reese became effective March 2, 2004, before this January 24, 2005 event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-24.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257 requires a properly reviewable federal question in a final state judgment. State-law error alone does not supply this Court's corrective authority.

##### Issue 1: Federal presentation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Baldwin v. Reese requires meaningful identification of federal nature through a provision, pertinent authority or understandable description; no fixed citation or formula is mandatory. A court need not search a separate lower opinion to discover an unidentified federal claim. The decision separately declined a late equivalence theory without creating a universal jurisdictional prohibition.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Examine the submission actually made and any valid incorporation or actual state adjudication. Similar facts and overlapping state standards do not automatically identify a federal right. The context may provide understandable notice without using a particular label.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 1257 and ordinary preservation doctrine. A distinct state-law instruction rule remains enforceable in state court without necessarily presenting a federal claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** Illinois v. Gates and the established state-review presentation requirement distinguish a claim actually presented from a new federal theory. Beck v. Alabama describes the substantive question, but does not dispense with review prerequisites.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Baldwin's notice principle is settled in exhaustion; its reasoning is relevant to the analogous direct-review presentation question. It does not establish that every indirect citation is inadequate or that identical state and federal rules can never suffice.
* **Open Point:** Whether this actual state-law submission gave meaningful federal notice or the state court actually resolved the federal question.

##### Issue 2: The instruction issue if review is available

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Schad v. Arizona and Hopkins v. Reeves preserve Beck's concern with an unavailable supported noncapital alternative while respecting the actual offense structure and evidentiary prerequisites; they do not require instructions on offenses that are not legally lesser included.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the charged capital elements, an actually available lesser offense, supporting evidence and the alternatives the jury had. An alibi theory does not by itself remove every independently supported lesser theory.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments and Mississippi's actual homicide elements. The State's later punishment choice is not automatically identical to a guilt-stage lesser-offense option.
* **Material Other Authority:** Beck v. Alabama supplies the federal anti-distortion principle. State interpretation of its own elements remains distinct from the federal adequacy question.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The general framework exists, but the precise application cannot be adjudicated unless the federal claim is reviewable and the relevant record is properly presented.
* **Open Point:** Whether the evidence supported an available lesser conviction and whether the omission created the prohibited capital all-or-nothing choice.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The state court found no evidentiary basis under its instruction rule. **Howell:** His description of an all-or-nothing capital verdict and cited state authorities conveyed the substance of Beck, even without a direct federal citation; he also disputes sufficient proof of the robbery element. **Mississippi:** The submission invoked a distinct state rule, and tracing several citations does not identify the federal claim; alternatively the evidence did not require the requested instructions. **Limits and alternatives:** Baldwin rejects magic words but still requires federal-nature notice. The Court need not resolve the broader identical-standards question because the State's generally applicable rule and its understanding of Beck are not shown identical. No unverified oral concession is attributed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Dismiss review as improvidently granted if the federal claim was neither meaningfully presented nor addressed and no valid exception warrants review; leave the state judgment without a federal merits endorsement.
* If actual context supplies meaningful federal presentation, resolve the instruction issue on the proper elements and evidence; any constitutional error then requires its governing effect and remedy inquiry.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A threshold ruling can reject this indirect-discovery route without imposing mandatory words or a universal rule for equivalent state and federal standards. A merits ruling, if lawfully reached, must address the actual supported lesser offense rather than a general entitlement to any requested instruction.

**Uncertainty:** The record establish the pertinent appellate argument but not a separate valid incorporation or actual federal adjudication. A later constitutional formulation cannot be backdated into that filing.

## 24. Bell v. Cone

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Bell v. Cone
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-394; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 447.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-24 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the state supreme court's treatment of the heinousness aggravator failed to apply an authoritative narrowing construction, and whether the Sixth Circuit could grant habeas relief on the premise that absence of a citation established that failure.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit, 359 F.3d 785, granting sentence relief on the aggravator claim after the prior remand in Bell v. Cone.
* **Relief Requested:** Tennessee seeks reversal of the aggravator-based writ; Cone seeks preservation of sentence relief because the jury used an inadequately narrowed aggravator and the appellate proceeding did not supply an adequate cure.
* **Material Record:** The jury returned a death sentence after finding several aggravators, including the statutory heinousness category. The Tennessee Supreme Court discussed the victims' conscious suffering and the manner of killing without citing Dicks by name. The Sixth Circuit treated the absent citation and phrasing as evidence that the state court had not used the narrowing construction. The parties also dispute the significance of mandatory state review and prior procedural treatment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The 2002 simulated mandate rejected a confined counsel claim but expressly returned remaining sentence claims; it did not preclude this different ground. Whether the vagueness issue was actually adjudicated on the merits cannot be inferred merely from its presence in a petition. Procedural default, the §2254(d) gateway, constitutional defect and actual cure remain distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** The trial was in 1982; the material direct state review was in 1984; later state collateral proceedings and the July 1, 1997 federal petition followed. Amended §2254 governs, with clearly established law measured at the relevant state merits adjudication. Later simulated decisions cannot simply be backdated into 1984.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Florida v. Nixon concerns concession strategy, not aggravator narrowing. The prior Bell v. Cone mandate controls the scope of the return. Ring and Schriro v. Summerlin concern a distinct eligibility-fact claim; their historical nonretroactivity treatment cannot be imported here.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2005-01-24.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None. Florida v. Nixon’s counsel question does not resolve this aggravator issue.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §2254(d), the actual state adjudication and ordinary default rules. The petitioner must satisfy the applicable statutory gateway before relief; if a properly reached nonstructural constitutional error requires an effect inquiry, the simulated Brecht rule places beyond-reasonable-doubt harmlessness on the State.

##### Issue 1: Actual narrowing and the state adjudication

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Bell v. Cone establishes the applicable review regime under the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA) and preserves remaining properly pending sentence claims. Sochor v. Florida permits an authoritative, specific judicial narrowing application but requires substantive correction of a proved weighing error; no verbal formula is required or sufficient. Richmond v. Lewis rejects automatic cure through surviving factors.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the controlling state construction, its actual application and the relevant federal-law cutoff. The absence of a case citation alone proves neither failure to follow state law nor constitutional unreasonableness; conversely, a presumption of familiarity cannot invent an unperformed adjudication or cure.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment and §2254(d)(1), with the 1984 federal-law baseline for the pertinent direct adjudication. State v. Dicks supplies the state construction, not federal authority to ignore the actual jury and appellate processes.
* **Material Other Authority:** Godfrey v. Georgia and Proffitt v. Florida supply pre-1984 narrowing principles. Later explanations may illuminate an old rule but cannot automatically become clearly established law at an earlier cutoff.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The difference between substantive narrowing and a missing citation is governed by existing principles. The actual state adjudication, guidance and any curative operation must still be assessed on this record.
* **Open Point:** Whether the state court actually applied the constitutionally sufficient construction and whether the federal writ rests on an impermissible citation-based inference.

##### Issue 2: Gateway, jury influence and remedy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lambrix v. Singletary distinguishes default and retroactivity, rejects automatic cure through the label of independent judicial review, and requires actual correction of a properly reached influential-jury defect. Its old-rule determination is tied to its own 1986 finality date. Schriro v. Summerlin permits watershed retroactivity for the defined indispensable Ring eligibility finding, but does not eliminate §2254's separate requirements.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** First resolve the actual adjudication and applicable gateway, then the precise violation, then an authorized cure or effect inquiry. The simulated Brecht standard is State-borne harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt; remaining aggravators and proportionality review do not alone prove it.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 2254(d) and (e), ordinary default rules, and state authority for any new sentencing proceeding. A jury-guidance claim and an indispensable eligibility-fact claim are not interchangeable.
* **Material Other Authority:** Clemons v. Mississippi permits lawful appellate correction within its conditions. It does not authorize a federal court to invent a state reweighing or disregard a distinct jury entitlement.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The 2002 mandate leaves this ground open, but no new hearing or writ follows automatically. Any later Ring claim requires its own preserved posture and statutory route.
* **Open Point:** What disposition corrects the Sixth Circuit's premise while preserving the actual-cure inquiry and all genuinely remaining claims.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Sixth Circuit treated mandatory state review as sufficient presentation, found the aggravator vague and found no adequate narrowing application. **Tennessee:** State courts need not repeat a citation to settled state law, and the actual suffering analysis tracks the narrowing construction; AEDPA does not permit the federal inference. **Cone:** A vague jury standard affects the weighing decision, and appellate discussion of disturbing facts does not necessarily establish a constitutionally adequate correction. **Alternative grounds and limits:** The preserved default dispute and actual-merits premise must be identified. Rejecting a citation requirement does not establish that every overlooked claim was adjudicated or that any appellate factual discussion cures jury error. No separate Government theory is established.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Reject the citation-based premise and deny this ground if the actual record establishes the sufficient narrowing and lawful correction under the applicable statutory framework.
* Vacate the writ and remand for a correct assessment of actual state adjudication, applicable default and §2254 predicates, and any surviving jury-influence or cure issue that the erroneous premise obscured.
* Preserve sentence relief only if the petitioner crosses the applicable gateway and establishes an uncured constitutional defect, with the State failing any required beyond-reasonable-doubt effect burden; specify lawful conditional correction.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case may establish that a missing citation is not itself constitutional error while preserving actual adjudication and cure requirements. It does not support a presumption that every unaddressed claim was silently decided or a blanket reinstatement of the entire federal judgment.

**Uncertainty:** The precise state-merits treatment and procedural bar cannot be replaced by assumptions. The historical opinion's assertion that Ring is nonretroactive conflicts with Schriro v. Summerlin under controlling precedent, but a distinct Ring claim is not thereby added to the grant.
