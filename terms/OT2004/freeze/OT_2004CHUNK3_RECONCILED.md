## Women's Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft

### Separable decisional propositions

**T1 — Statutory scope and ordinary D&E.** The revised law requires all enacted sequential acts and intentions, including the specified extraction of an intact fetus beyond the vaginal introitus and the death-causing act at the specified stage. The express D&E exclusion and distinct prior-death-causing-act exclusion must be given effect. Ordinary D&E does not fall within the prohibition merely because a physician intentionally removes a fetal part, because a medical label overlaps, or because an intact delivery occurs without the enacted intention and sequence. The categorical ordinary-D&E ground for invalidation is rejected. A label does not excuse conduct that actually satisfies the complete defined intact procedure. This resolves the statutory coverage objection without treating an enforcement assurance as an authoritative state construction.

**T2 — Necessary life-and-health protection.** *Casey*, *Voinovich* and *Stenberg* require timely necessary care where compelled substitution creates a medically supported material risk of death or serious health harm and the prohibited method is necessary for safe and suitable care. The protection reaches serious comparative procedural risk without requiring an independently diseased pregnancy. It also preserves grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury, including a medically necessary abortion for that danger when a particular physical method is required for safe and suitable treatment. Unsupported preference, transient distress and every marginal safety advantage do not establish that necessity. Competent disagreement does not eliminate an otherwise supported application. The patient need not await catastrophe.

**T3 — Actual Ohio construction and record application.** The Sixth Circuit's conclusive rejection of the health challenge cannot stand on the present application. Its prospective reading is material but cannot silently substitute “or” for the statute's “and,” or answer the mental-health theory by asking only whether one method causes more psychological distress than another. The lower court must determine whether Ohio law permits a construction of the actual medically diagnosed-condition and major-bodily-function language that protects every constitutionally required application supported here. If it does, apply it; if it does not, preserve constitutional protection against the proved excluded applications. The credited physical-risk evidence and supported mental-health subgroups must receive actual legal application. No new clinical conclusion or universal superiority premise is adopted, and no new evidentiary hearing is automatic.

**T4 — Remaining previability interference.** The complete six-step *Casey* framework remains governing law. Once statutory scope and necessary-care applications are properly identified, any remaining proved previability interference receives that full inquiry: independent baseline, obstructive-purpose and obstacle bars; compelling justification and narrow tailoring for remaining severe interference; substantiated permissible justification and fit for remaining meaningful interference, with an answer to supported practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The providers first establish duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The existing findings and ordinary factual-review rules govern; the existence of an abortion regulation does not alone shift a burden to the State.

**T5 — Disposition and relief.** Reject the categorical ordinary-D&E ground, vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it conclusively rejects the necessary-health challenge, and remand for the legal application and relief described in T2–T4. The lower court must identify and protect the established applications, use Ohio's actual severability rules, and promptly decide interim protection under ordinary equitable principles on the existing record. A permanent blanket reinstatement or immediate blanket enforcement does not follow automatically from the remand. Enforcement consequences dependent on an unconstitutional application must conform to the protection actually entered. The unappealed recklessness construction and independent civil-standing determination remain effective; the remand does not reopen them. The constitutional rules are settled; construction permitted by Ohio law, clinical application and appropriately scoped relief are the work remaining below.
