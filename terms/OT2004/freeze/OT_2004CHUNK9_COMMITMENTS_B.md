## 79. Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964

**Question and disposition:** Seven Associates would reverse the first-stage threshold ruling and remand for completion of Batson's process. Thomas would affirm California's first-stage rule and the reviewed judgment. All reach the granted merits question on the supplied final-judgment posture.

**Common reversal ground:** A defendant triggers the prosecutor's explanation duty by facts sufficient to permit an inference of purposeful racial exclusion. Requiring proof that discrimination was more likely than not before the prosecutor explains the challenged strikes improperly demands an ultimate persuasion showing at the production stage. Striking all three qualified Black prospective jurors from this venire satisfies the required inference on the supplied record. The trial court must receive and evaluate the actual reasons and determine purposeful discrimination with the ultimate burden remaining on Johnson. A retrospective hearing must permit a reliable determination; if the lapse or state of the record prevents that constitutionally adequate adjudication, the state court must provide a new trial. This is a remedial consequence of the unsuccessful original inquiry, not a present finding that the strikes were discriminatory.

**Authorities and competing paths:** Batson, 476 U.S. 79, makes the surrounding circumstances and an inference the gateway to an explanation and reserves ultimate persuasion to the opponent. Hernandez v. New York, 500 U.S. 352, distinguishes production from the final credibility inquiry. Franklyn/Gonzalez, decided October 22, 1998, requires an actual final purpose finding; it cannot supply an alternative affirmance when reasons were never required. Miller-El v. Cockrell, February 25, 2003, preserves different thresholds for different procedural stages. California's strongest competing path invokes Batson's latitude for state implementation and the need to keep peremptories from becoming automatic challenges for cause. That path cannot justify making an objector prove concealed intent before the State discloses its actual explanation, but it supplies Thomas's narrower account of permissible prima facie administration. The meaningful inference requirement itself answers the concern that every strike triggers an explanation.

## 80. Bradshaw v. Stumpf

**Component dispositions:** All eight Associates would reverse the determination that the plea was unknowing or unintelligent on the stated intent ground. All eight would return the distinct sentencing challenge for its own adjudication rather than reinstate a final denial of all relief solely because guilt survives. The Sixth Circuit's plea-based relief is reversed; its sentence-related disposition is vacated to the extent it rests on the same invalid plea premise, and the case is remanded for the independent capital claim.

**Plea ground:** Due process requires actual notice and understanding of the essential offense, which competent counsel may provide and the record as a whole may establish. The judge need not personally recite every element. Here counsel represented that the elements had been explained and Stumpf personally acknowledged that advice. His denial of having fired the fatal shots does not contradict purposeful participation under Ohio's aggravated-murder and complicity law. The colloquy, the factual-basis proceedings and the particular denial do not establish that the intent requirement was omitted or misunderstood.

**Plea alternatives:** Henderson v. Morgan, 426 U.S. 637, protects a defendant who lacked real notice of a necessary intent element; Boykin, 395 U.S. 238, and Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742, require an intelligent and voluntary plea. Bousley, May 18, 1998, preserves real notice of an accurately described offense. Stumpf's strongest alternative rests on intellectual limitations, counsel's factual resistance and the judge's incomplete personal account. Those circumstances justify examination rather than a conclusive presumption, but they do not rebut the direct explanation and acknowledgement or make shooter identity an element Ohio law does not require. Lindh and its preserved application-date principle put this claim under the applicable pre-AEDPA merits law.

**Sentencing ground and competing paths:** A valid purposeful-participation plea does not settle the accuracy and fairness of the individualized death decision. Napue v. Illinois, 360 U.S. 264, and Giglio v. United States, 405 U.S. 150, prohibit knowing use of materially false evidence; Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586, and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104, preserve individualized consideration of responsibility and mitigation. The remand must identify the prosecution's actual factual representations, the later evidence, what was known, the basis on which death was selected, and the material prejudicial effect of any constitutional defect. The strongest State path is that different evidence may support different permissible inferences and both participants may intentionally aid murder. That establishes neither false evidence nor an automatic bar to prosecuting both. The strongest prisoner path is that the State used contradictory claims of principal responsibility to obtain death. That possibility cannot be rejected as irrelevant merely because complicity supports guilt. The existing appellate analysis improperly merged the phases; unresolved material predicates justify the remand under Engine §8(c).

## 81. Merck KGaA v. Integra Lifesciences I, Ltd.

**Disposition:** All eight Associates would vacate the restrictive §271(e)(1) ruling and remand for application of the correct statutory standard to the preserved trial-record challenge.

**Shared operative rule:** The exemption covers preclinical use of a patented compound when the researcher has a reasonable basis to believe it could produce the biological effect under investigation and uses it to develop information of a kind relevant to an investigational or marketing submission under the federal drug laws. It is not confined to clinical trials, a compound ultimately selected for approval, or experiments whose results are ultimately submitted. The inquiry concerns the reasonable regulatory relationship of the use when undertaken. General basic research without the identified reasonable regulatory relationship does not obtain exemption merely because it may someday assist drug development.

**Authorities and adverse paths:** Section 271(e)(1)'s reasonable-relationship language, rather than a judicial preference for either research or strong patents, controls. Eli Lilly & Co. v. Medtronic, 496 U.S. 661, gives the statutory regulatory reference its enacted scope; it does not make approval success a condition. The FDCA and 21 C.F.R. §§312.23 and 314.50 require relevant preclinical safety and effectiveness information. The United States' contemporary brief explains why safety, efficacy and mechanism experiments may be part of that development. Integra's strongest path warns that every exploratory commercial project could claim eventual FDA relevance and consume the patent grant. Requiring a concrete reasonable relationship to information of the regulatory kind answers that objection; requiring actual later submission instead uses hindsight to exclude reasonable experiments that fail or generate negative results. Monsanto's current patent-scope treatment preserves the statutory grant and distinct legal limits but creates neither a research exemption nor immunity from the enacted one.

## 82. Wilkinson v. Austin

**Component dispositions:** All eight Associates recognize a protected interest in avoiding the OSP placement at issue and would uphold the New Policy's described procedures as constitutionally sufficient. They would affirm the liberty-interest conclusion on the controlling simulated ground, reverse the requirement for additional procedures beyond this adequate policy, and remand for conforming disposition of the injunction and any supported continuing implementation issue. The appellate rejection of new substantive placement restrictions remains effective.

**Protected-interest rule and application:** Sandin's governing rule is a sufficient, nonexclusive substantive-constraint rule: state law creates protected prison liberty when it meaningfully limits authority to impose a significant deprivation. Ohio requires a qualifying substantive placement basis, with evaluative discretion exercised within those grounds. That constraint, together with the severe isolation, indefinite duration and exclusion from otherwise available parole consideration, establishes a protected interest. Mere procedural promises are not the source. The combined record permits application rather than another abstract inquiry into whether any constraint exists.

**Process rule and application:** Mathews requires consideration of the interest, error risk and value of added safeguards, and governmental burdens. The described New Policy supplies notice of the factual basis, an opportunity for oral and written rebuttal, stated reasons and several levels of review, a terminating negative recommendation, a chance to object before final decision, prompt review after placement and annual reconsideration. In this security classification setting that combination reasonably guards against erroneous placement. Wolff and Hewitt permit procedures responsive to prison safety; full adversarial witness presentation and disclosure of every item are not constitutionally required by this record. Notice and reasons must still identify the actual basis sufficiently for the rebuttal the policy provides. The practical distinction between that requirement and disclosure endangering informants is part of the adopted adequacy analysis.

**Enhanced competing-path test:** The class's strongest path rests on demonstrated errors under the former process and the danger that general allegations or periodic paper review will continue those errors. Lewis v. Casey, Benjamin v. Fraser and current Gates v. Cook make actual proved injury and remedial necessity decisive and allow measures beyond a bare minimum when required to correct the violation. But the State's replacement incorporates meaningful checks, and earlier defects do not prove the additional witness, evidentiary and administrative specifications are necessary now. Ohio's strongest opposite path would deny any liberty in a classification left to professional judgment. The substantive predicates and magnitude of this particular restraint defeat that account even while officials retain substantial discretion within those predicates. The former Sandin dissent's hardship theory also supports an interest on these exceptional facts, but it does not replace the controlling sufficient rule.

## 83. Miller-El v. Dretke

**Relief coalition's ground:** Under §2254(d)(2), the rejection of purposeful discrimination is an unreasonable determination of the facts on the state-court record. The extreme strike disparity, similarly situated non-Black jurors retained despite concerns used against Black jurors, disparate questioning, the pattern of shuffles, and contemporaneously relevant office history reinforce one another. ## 84. Grable & Sons Metal Products, Inc. v. Darue Engineering & Manufacturing

**Disposition:** All eight Associates would affirm federal-question and removal jurisdiction and the reviewed judgment within the grant's jurisdictional scope.

**Shared operative rule:** A state-law claim arises under §1331 when its well-pleaded theory necessarily raises an actually disputed and substantial federal issue whose federal adjudication is consistent with the federal-state allocation Congress established. A federal cause of action is sufficient but not invariably required. Grable's only pleaded defect in Darue's title turns on the meaning of the federal tax-sale notice requirement. The United States' substantial interest in reliable federal tax sales, the controlling significance of that legal issue, and the confined class of comparable title controversies support federal jurisdiction without transferring ordinary state litigation into federal court.

**Competing paths and authority:** Smith v. Kansas City Title & Trust Co., 255 U.S. 180, recognizes the embedded-federal-issue route. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals v. Thompson, 478 U.S. 804, treats the absence of a private federal action as significant in the context of routine state tort litigation adopting federal standards; its result does not establish a categorical cause-of-action prerequisite. Franchise Tax Board v. Construction Laborers Vacation Trust, 463 U.S. 1, and the well-pleaded complaint rule require the federal issue in the claimant's own necessary theory. The strongest contrary path reads the missing federal action as Congress's refusal of jurisdiction; that inference is weaker here because the dispute concerns title generated by the federal Government's revenue machinery, and a rare federal-title challenge imposes a different jurisdictional burden from a new federal forum for ordinary tort suits. A federal defense or the private monetary stakes would be insufficient; neither is the asserted basis here. Steel Co. supplies jurisdiction-before-merits discipline.

## 85. Golden v. Golden

**Disposition:** All eight Associates would affirm the sufficient diversity-amount ruling and the restoration of the independently cognizable personal claims, vacate the broader probate-exception dismissals, and remand for claim-and-relief-specific adjudication under the rule below. The same court may reject a legally unsupported tort or apply established preclusion after jurisdiction is correctly addressed; those are distinct tasks.

**Shared operative rule:** The probate exception prevents a federal court from probating or annulling a will, administering an estate, or taking control of property already under a state probate court's custody. It does not exclude an otherwise proper diversity claim seeking personal liability for wrongdoing merely because a trust functions as a will substitute, the controversy concerns an inheritance, or the adjudication could reach facts inconsistent with a probate determination. A personal-damages label is not controlling if the relief actually requires the prohibited probate, administrative or property-control act. A trust's label or practical testamentary function alone decides neither direction.

**Application to the actual claims:** The Third Circuit's inference that probate of the pour-over will necessarily accepted the trust and addendum cannot, by itself, turn every fraud, forgery or undue-influence damages claim into probate. The personal slander and interference-with-inheritance claims remain available for ordinary adjudication. The formerly dismissed claims require examination of their actual requested relief: a personal judgment can proceed within diversity jurisdiction, whereas an order vacating probate, allocating the corpus or administering the executor's accounting remains outside this forum's authority. The fiduciary-duty count particularly requires this distinction rather than a categorical executor label. State law may limit whether a freestanding tort exists; a State's internal assignment of cases to its Orphans' Court does not itself contract §1332.

**Amount, capacities and state judgments:** Leah Golden's claim uses her citizenship despite an attorney-in-fact; Helen Earwood's estate representative takes the decedent's citizenship under §1332(c)(2).
