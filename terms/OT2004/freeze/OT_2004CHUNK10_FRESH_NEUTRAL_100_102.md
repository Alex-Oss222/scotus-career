## 100. Van Orden v. Perry

### Event, record and source status

No. 03-1500. The Court reviews the Fifth Circuit's affirmance of judgment for Texas. The question concerns the Ten Commandments monument on the State Capitol grounds in its actual governmental and historical setting. Van Orden seeks a declaration and prospective relief ending the challenged display; the complaint requested removal. Texas seeks affirmance. The personal encounters described in the supplied packet support this identified-display claim; no generalized taxpayer premise is necessary.

The approximately six-foot granite monument was a 1961 gift from the Fraternal Order of Eagles. Texas accepted it by concurrent resolution and placed it between the Capitol and the State Supreme Court building. The grounds cover 22 acres and contain 17 monuments, numerous plaques and other images commemorating Texas people, ideals and events. Most monuments were donated. The monument includes the religious commands and theological assertions, two tablets in ancient script, an eye inside a pyramid, an eagle grasping the American flag, Stars of David and the Chi-Rho symbol. A conspicuous inscription identifies the Eagles' gift to the people and youth of Texas. No government seal appears on the monolith according to the cited record recitation. These features bear on attribution and function; neither the donor inscription nor the religious text decides that legal inquiry alone.

The State's acceptance resolution commended the Eagles' work against juvenile delinquency. The Fifth Circuit found no record contradiction of that stated secular purpose, regarded the monument as recognizing legal heritage, and relied on its setting among civic memorials. Those are the lower court's findings and reasoning under review. Calling the text nonsectarian is the quoted description in the source, not an independent theological finding by this packet.

The monument was temporarily removed during construction beginning in 1990 and reinstalled in 1993 at its original location, reoriented toward the west entrance and a new sidewalk. The source describes the State staff's placement as intended to reflect the Commandments' role in law. Six other monuments occupy the same quadrant; the Statue of Liberty replica and two war memorials are visible from the immediate area. The U.S. brief further identifies the State's visitor brochure, signage accompanying memorials and trial videotape as support for a unitary historical presentation. The Fifth Circuit expressly relied on that museum-like presentation. The actual brochure pages and videotape are not newly read here; the pre-event brief's specific record recitations are sufficient under Engine §14. Their existence must not be omitted or replaced by an assumption that the record contains no explanatory presentation. Their constitutional adequacy remains contested.

The grounds are a National Historic Landmark and the parties stipulated that the monument is an element of that landmark. The U.S. brief's footnote qualifies that point: the federal designation does not extend to the individual monuments themselves. The monument's duration may support a preservation rationale; neither formal landmark protection of this object nor a constitutional exemption follows.

### Entering law and exact operation

The operative **Lemon test** is Agostini's unified governmental-act and function inquiry, effective June 23, 1997. Its later financed-function and civil-certification applications in the register do not replace the original rule governing this display. A specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls where it resolves the act; an established permissible relationship supplies its own answer; otherwise a sufficient unresolved claim is adjudicated through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence within that inquiry, not independent fallback prongs. Government may not adopt doctrine as governmental truth or solicit adherence through public authority. A secular aim cannot justify a prohibited religious means; religious content, location or private religious motivation alone does not prove such a function. No actual conversion or compelled participation is needed once prohibited adoption is established.

**Official religious identification in governmental symbols**, effective Robinson v. City of Edmond, March 31, 1997, establishes that private design does not prevent an adopted municipal seal from being government expression. Its independent prohibition on using a faith's badge as a permanent civil identifier concerns that actual function; it is not a categorical rule invalidating every religious object on public grounds. Accurate contextual preservation of history remains permissible.

**Municipal divestiture and continuing presentation of a religious display**, effective City of Marshfield, February 27, 2001, requires examination of the continuing public presentation even after a genuine sale. No one feature or possible confusion establishes the violation. Its cure must actually end the established municipal religious function while preserving private rights; no universal fence, sign, sale, relocation or removal requirement follows. Van Orden has no supplied private conveyance, so neither the majority's compound attribution finding nor the dissent's private-operation premise transfers automatically.

Doe v. Porter (April 26, 2005) unanimously prohibits a public school's adoption of devotional instruction through private delivery; it makes the present governmental function decisive but does not equate a passive capitol monument with classroom instruction. Cutter (May 31, 2005) unanimously sustains neutral accommodation of private exercise under custody constraints; it supplies no monument exemption. Pinette and Chabad retain their bounded private-access holdings. Texas's acceptance of donated monuments does not itself establish a generally available private forum.

### Supported positions and opposing paths

**Sustain as historical commemoration.** Texas and the United States rely on the complete civic setting, gift attribution, visitor materials, equal treatment of donated memorials, legal-history placement and longstanding preservation. The strongest application of that argument under current law is that government is preserving and explaining a historical artifact rather than declaring the religious commands to be its own binding truth. It need not prove that each secular-law prohibition originated exclusively in the Commandments. The opposing argument is that the prominent theological commands and the State's continuing installation may perform official religious affirmation despite a sincere secular purpose. Brochures and neighboring memorials must change the display's actual function, not merely furnish a description disconnected from what the State presents.

**Find prohibited governmental adoption.** Van Orden's strongest route is that the State presents the commands as an authoritative civic message without a sufficiently explanatory setting separating historical recognition from present religious affirmation. Official acceptance, location between lawmaking institutions and current presentation may be evidence of that function. The strongest counterargument is the affirmative record of an integrated visitor presentation, gift inscription, ordinary treatment and preservation history; religious content plus ownership cannot substitute for assessing that evidence. A conclusion that explanation is inadequate requires legal evaluation of the supplied presentation, not an invented finding that no brochure or signage exists.

**Narrower record or remedy route.** A remand is available only for a genuinely material unresolved application or remedial issue. The record does not compel a new trial simply because original exhibits were not retrieved for this preparation. If a violation is established, prospective relief must effectively end the governmental religious function; no destruction or damages award is supported. Removal, clarification or other lawful redesign must be assessed for actual effectiveness, rather than treating a small disclaimer as invariably sufficient or removal as invariably required. If the display is sustained, no broad approval of all old monuments, all donated messages or all Commandments displays follows.

The brief's strict-scrutiny, endorsement, coercion and history arguments are advocacy to be translated into or expressly tested against current law. They do not displace Agostini. In particular, a categorical age exception would change the entering rule and would require an express adjudicative basis, not a silent description as settled law.

## 101. Town of Castle Rock v. Gonzales

### Event, record and source status

No. 04-278. The Court reviews the Tenth Circuit's en banc reversal of the Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of the municipal procedural-due-process claim. The substantive-due-process rejection and the individual officers' qualified-immunity dispositions are outside this merits question. The Town seeks dismissal; Gonzales seeks proceedings on the municipal claim, not an already established damages award.

The temporary order of May 21, 1999 prohibited molestation or disturbance of the peace, restricted access to the home and addressed property. Mr. Gonzales was served June 4. The parties then stipulated to making it permanent with modifications permitting alternate-weekend and summer parenting time, a midweek dinner arranged on reasonable notice, and pickup from the home for parenting time. The children were taken June 22 without notice according to the complaint. The permitted visits and the notice condition must be retained when evaluating the alleged violation; the order did not absolutely prohibit every contact with the children.

The complaint alleges repeated requests to police over the evening, presentation of the order and information that the children were at a Denver amusement park; requests for a check and bulletin; successive instructions to wait; and a final station visit at which a report was taken without further action. It alleges a municipal policy or custom tolerating nonenforcement. The husband later fired on the police station and was killed; the children were found dead in his truck. These are complaint-stage allegations or supported procedural recitations, not findings of municipal policy, culpability, feasible rescue or causation.

The order's printed reverse-side notice paraphrased enforcement law; it is distinct from the judge's decretal commands to the restrained party. The U.S. brief argues that the notice cannot itself impose a decree on nonparty police. The lower court instead relied on the notice together with the statute. An individualized order may identify the beneficiary and the restraint without by itself creating an enforceable property right against police.

The en banc court required assessment of a valid order, probable cause of violation, and probable cause of notice to the restrained party; if those conditions did not qualify the request for mandatory enforcement, the recipient was to receive the decision and its reason. It allowed the policy claim to proceed based on allegedly untimely consideration. The dissenting grounds, recited in the pre-event U.S. brief, include preserved enforcement discretion, the distinction between procedural and substantive complaints, the limited force of printed notices and the sufficiency of the opportunities Gonzales already had to speak to police. These are separate grounds, not merely different formulations of no property interest.

### Conduct-time legal text and its limit

The supplied U.S. brief is dated December 2004 and expressly labels its statutory appendix as the 2004 compilation. Its statement and argument report the core enforcement commands as the statutory basis of the 1999 dispute and the lower court's decision. Under Engine §14, those supported recitations supply the reasonable-means command and the arrest-or-warrant duty triggered by probable cause of violation and service or actual notice. The exact source text is copied in the appendix below. Its later definitions and transition language are not silently treated as the full 1999 code. No wholesale finding that every 2004 provision applied in 1999 is made.

Preserve these distinct qualifications: the general command concerns every reasonable means; arrest has probable-cause conditions; impractical immediate arrest redirects action toward seeking a warrant; registry information and actual service or notice are different concepts; and authority to transport a victim or child under subsection (6) is not the command in subsection (3). The protected person's opportunity to pursue contempt is a separate remedy, not proof of timely police protection. None of these provisions guarantees prevention of the private violence.

### Entering law and exact operation

Roth requires a legitimate claim of entitlement grounded in an independent legal source, not a need, expectation or mandatory phrase standing alone. Allen and the surviving substantive-predicate cases permit state-created interests even where implementation involves judgment. DeShaney rejects a general constitutional duty to protect against private violence; it does not resolve an independently created entitlement merely by labeling the claim noncustodial.

The controlling Sandin rule (June 19, 1995) examines genuine substantive constraints and a meaningful deprivation in its prison setting, without adopting the dissent's exclusive atypical-hardship framework. **Mandatory investigative procedures and substantive entitlements**, effective Doe v. District of Columbia, June 2, 1997, unanimously requires a substantive interest rather than nonperformance of investigative steps. Its independently created-entitlement reservation remains. The separate CAPTA right holding and dissent concern a federal statutory right under §1983, whereas Gonzales invokes due process for a state-created interest; neither side of that CAPTA division automatically answers this claim.

Connecticut Department of Public Safety v. Doe (March 5, 2003), reflected in **Hearings for risk-based and conviction-based notification**, requires identifying facts material under the substantive rule; process does not create a substantive exception that the governing law lacks. Wilkinson v. Austin (June 13, 2005) applies Sandin's substantive-constraint rule and practical notice/rebuttal/reasons safeguards to its prison classification. It confirms the need for real constraints and usable process, without making its prison procedures the required police response.

Mathews requires process fitted to the actual interest, risk of erroneous deprivation, value of additional safeguards and governmental burdens. Zinermon distinguishes an established or foreseeable deprivation process from an unauthorized act for which advance safeguards are impracticable. **Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation**, with its Fairley and Gibson qualifications effective June 23 and January 14, 2003, requires separate proof of a federal injury and municipal attribution, fault where applicable, and actual causation. Carter's March 13, 2000 wording preserves Canton requirements for an omission or training theory. Employment alone establishes no Town liability; individual immunity does not establish absence of a municipal constitutional deprivation.

### Supported positions and opposing paths

**No protected entitlement.** The Town and United States argue that enforcement remains discretionary within reasonableness, resources and ordinary police judgment; mandatory wording may guide officials without conferring an individual substantive benefit. Independently, a duty to take enforcement steps against a third party might confer only an incidental benefit or prescribed procedure. Their strongest authorities include Roth, DeShaney, the specific procedural-interest boundary in Doe v. District of Columbia and the surviving enforcement-discretion background. The contrary argument is that a particular order and specific probable-cause trigger may secure a concrete protective service even though methods and success remain discretionary. Police discretion to choose means is not necessarily discretion to refuse every reasonable response.

**Protected bounded service.** Gonzales argues that the scheme deliberately constrains nonenforcement for named beneficiaries on defined predicates. The strongest lawful formulation is a claim to the prescribed reasonable enforcement response, not successful arrest, guaranteed rescue or a fixed national police timetable. The principal objection remains that the interest is simply the procedure used to pursue another person's violation and that the whole statute preserves practical enforcement discretion. Recognizing a duty under state law and finding constitutional property are separate steps; neither can be inferred solely from tragic injury or legislative purpose.

**Adequate process even assuming an interest.** The United States expressly proposes reversal because Gonzales repeatedly supplied the relevant information and requested action; more elaborate factfinding would not resolve operational priorities or add an eligibility fact that was actually contested. This is a distinct material alternative, omitted from the original issue-2 amicus field. Its contrary argument is that repeated opportunities to speak do not necessarily constitute meaningful consideration when a policy allegedly prevents any genuine assessment. The specific function of additional safeguards, not a courtroom-style hearing, must be shown. The complaint stage prevents a finding that contested policy and process facts have already been proved against either side.

**Further proceedings or state-law clarification.** If a cognizable interest and a sufficient municipal-process theory survive, the remaining deprivation, actual process, policy attribution, fault where required, causation and remedy remain for adjudication. If a genuinely unresolved Colorado construction becomes dispositive, certification remains available; it is not mandatory simply because a source appendix uses a later compilation. Nominal relief for an established denial of process is distinct from compensation for the deaths. No police-reorganization injunction, damages amount, individual-officer liability or guaranteed survival may be supplied by inference.

## 102. Bell v. Thompson

### Event, record and source status

No. 04-514. The Court reviews the Sixth Circuit's June 23, 2004 amended habeas judgment vacating denial of relief and directing an evidentiary hearing. Tennessee seeks reversal of that amendment and a mandate reflecting the earlier denial; Thompson seeks preservation of the hearing direction. The supplied record identifies the original federal petition as filed in 1998, so the applicable AEDPA framework is not displaced by the pre-AEDPA posture of Calderon.

The supplied authorized neutral packets establish that Thompson's federal habeas lawyers obtained Dr. Sultan's report and deposition addressing serious mental illness, that the material was not properly included in the district-court record, and that the Sixth Circuit initially affirmed denial and denied rehearing. Its mandate was stayed for certiorari and then rehearing. Certiorari was denied December 1, 2003; rehearing was denied January 20, 2004, and the order was filed below January 23. No mandate had issued when the June 23 amendment was entered. After the stated stays there was no further explanatory order or notice of continued withholding. During the more-than-five-month interval Tennessee set an execution date and separate competency proceedings began.

These facts establish a distinction between withholding and recall, as well as a real question about the court's reconsideration of omitted material. They do not establish that the omission was a clerical court error, that counsel acted diligently in every respect, that an internal judicial request occurred on a particular date, that the evidence proves innocence or present incompetence, or that it necessarily establishes Strickland prejudice. No such additional premise is adopted. The material supports a contested mitigation claim. The original packets' objective recitations are supplied inputs; this validator has not read the same-matter Supreme Court disposition or the underlying report and deposition.

### Entering law and exact operation

The term-effective Rule 41 text, with amendments through December 1, 2002, is reproduced mechanically below. Subsection (b) permits shortening or extending ordinary issuance time; subsection (c) makes the mandate effective when issued. Subsection (d)(2)(B) supplies the certiorari-stay duration conditions, while (d)(2)(D) directs immediate issuance when a copy of the denial order is filed. The relation between these provisions and any exceptional pre-mandate correction must be construed; a later amendment cannot supply the answer.

**Recall reopening completed state-prisoner habeas merits**, effective Calderon v. Thompson, April 29, 1998, requires Schlup's conviction gateway or Sawyer's distinct eligibility gateway when actual recall reopens completed merits. It preserves genuinely different process defects, including clerical error, fraud or a mandate failing to express judgment, and does not classify every court-initiated act as a successive application. Mitigation concerning sentence selection is not automatically Sawyer eligibility innocence. The lack of an issued mandate here prevents a mechanical application of Calderon's defined recall category; its finality concerns remain relevant by reasoned analogy.

**Timely postverdict acquittal and limits on substitute authority**, effective Carlisle, April 29, 1996, forbids relabeling the same act to circumvent a specific national-rule restriction. It does not abolish inherent authority in a genuine gap or equate every continuing proceeding with completed appellate review. The specific language and procedural stage therefore matter independently.

Rhines (March 30, 2005) permits a bounded stay of a timely pending mixed petition under cause, potential-merit, anti-abuse and deadline rules. It also requires an independently valid ground to reopen a terminated action. Its retention rationale supports examining actual pendency; its conditions cannot be imported wholesale as a Rule 41 standard. Rompilla (June 20, 2005) separates reasonable investigation, cumulative Strickland prejudice and the state-adjudication statutory threshold. It gives no appellate reopening power and makes no finding about Thompson's evidence. A relevant earlier-in-chunk Gonzalez decision, when entered, requires a later public-law refresh rather than anticipation here.

### Supported opposing paths and remedies

**Rule-limited authority.** Tennessee may argue that the immediate-issuance command ends the stated stay and that inherent power cannot defeat the specific rule. The strongest counterargument is that no mandate issued, ordinary enlargement authority remains in the rule, and exceptional correction of the court's own incomplete first review may differ from a party's untimely new application. An authority holding must address that textual and institutional argument, rather than equating the mere passage of time with actual issuance.

**Abuse even assuming authority.** The Court may assume some exceptional authority and assess whether this exercise was reasonable given the unexplained extended withholding, lack of notice, completion of the announced review steps and State reliance. The counterargument is that mitigation evidence material to a first capital habeas review warrants a limited hearing rather than rigid enforcement of a mandate that never became effective. The answer requires distinguishing a supported process correction from ordinary reconsideration of merits; capital status or the description of the evidence as serious cannot supply that distinction by itself. Nor does State reliance make every unannounced delay abusive without examining the actual posture and correction sought.

**Sustain a bounded correction.** Thompson may rely on the pre-mandate stage and the concrete omitted-evidence problem as grounds to permit a hearing that completes the original review. The strongest objection is that a court cannot preserve unlimited merits power by simply failing to issue the mandate required by the rule, especially without notice or prompt explanation. A sustaining rationale must fit the supplied facts and rule, with no invented clerical mistake, diligence finding, internal chronology or already proved entitlement to the writ. If a particular dispositive rationale requires one of those absent facts, isolate the needed proposition or select a lawful ground the supported record permits; absence from this packet is not litigant default.

Reversal can remove the amendment and require the mandate corresponding to the earlier judgment without imposing a new execution date, adjudicating present competency or foreclosing an independently valid proceeding. Affirmance preserves the hearing direction, not an automatic writ or final finding of ineffective assistance. A remand must identify the genuine question to be resolved rather than use incomplete operator access as its judicial purpose.

(3)(a)	Whenever a protection order is issued, the protected person shall be provided with a copy of such order.  A peace officer shall use every reasonable means to enforce a protection order.

(b)	A peace officer shall arrest, or, if an arrest would be impractical under the circumstances, seek a warrant for the arrest of a restrained person when the peace officer has information amounting to probable cause that:

### Federal Rule of Appellate Procedure 41

Contents. Unless the court directs that a formal mandate issue, the mandate consists of a certified copy of the judgment, a copy of the court’s opinion, if any, and any direction about costs.

On Petition for Rehearing or Motion. The timely filing of a petition for panel rehearing, petition for rehearing en banc, or motion for stay of mandate, stays the mandate until disposition of the petition or motion, unless the court orders otherwise.

(2)

Pending Petition for Certiorari.

(A)

A party may move to stay the mandate pending the filing of a petition for a writ of certiorari in the Supreme Court. The motion must be served on all parties and must show that the certiorari petition would present a substantial question and that there is good cause for a stay.

(B)

The stay must not exceed 90 days, unless the period is extended for good cause or unless the party who obtained the stay files a petition for the writ and so notifies the circuit clerk in writing within the period of the stay. In that case, the stay continues until the Supreme Court’s final disposition.

(C)

The court may require a bond or other security as a condition to granting or continuing a stay of the mandate.

(D)

The court of appeals must issue the mandate immediately when a copy of a Supreme Court order denying the petition for writ of certiorari is filed.
