## Result of the premise test

The independent freeze proposed eight Associate votes for constitutional vacatur, with seven supporting an additional independent determination that the decree was unconstitutional before Cochran's death. The historical evidence does not support preserving those proposals without a concrete changed premise.

The historical majority deliberately declined both the categorical defamation-injunction question and the decree's original constitutional validity because death made those questions unnecessary to present relief. Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer each joined that limited ground. Thomas and Scalia sought dismissal as improvidently granted because death removed the vehicle for the granted question, state courts remained available, and the new dispute was case-specific error correction. They did not affirm the decree as constitutional or declare the case jurisdictionally moot.

The neutral record here presents the same material chronology, all-topic command, adjudicated coercion, death, asserted continuing enforceability and opportunity for state modification. Current Madsen, Schenck and Alexander preserve exact fit and responsible-party limits, but none mandates decision of a decree's original validity after the basis for current relief changes, or requires this Court to retain every changed vehicle. Those prior positions therefore do not supply a Justice-specific reason for the additional T2 holding or for reversing Thomas and Scalia's prudential judgment. Describing the injunction as especially broad merely repeats a premise present historically. The strengthened articulation of current fit principles is not a new fact about the vehicle.

Reconciliation consequently narrows the six majority-aligned Associates to the present-circumstances ground and restores Thomas and Scalia's DIG position. This is a reasoned correction after testing the provisional proposals; it is neither automatic historical substitution nor an inference that historical silence decides every merits issue.

## Reconciled operative propositions

**T1 — Current controversy and substitution, six joins.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find a continuing controversy because the command remains effective on its terms and is asserted to be enforceable. Death alone does not eliminate the restraint. The Court need not resolve every state-law consequence of death to recognize the continuing burden. They permit the agreed substitution for this appeal; it gives the widow no automatic substantive entitlement to every remedy and does not make the firm an original party. Thomas and Scalia do not join the affirmative jurisdictional adjudication; they would dismiss prudentially without resolving the contested mootness question. They identify no independent objection to the agreed representative substitution but provide no merits or jurisdictional join from it.

**T2 — Independent predeath invalidity, no reconciled adoption.** The provisional independent original-breadth holding is not retained by any Associate in this handoff. The decree's actual breadth remains indispensable to assessing its present burden, but that assessment must be coupled to the changed justification after death. The general rule about every injunction following adjudicated defamation and this decree's validity before death are unnecessary to the present constitutional remedy. No contrary merits holding is implied by withholding those questions.

**T3 — Present overbreadth after death, six joins.** The complete public-topic restraint cannot be justified on the accepted present record because the underlying goal of preventing coercion of Cochran into paying money has disappeared or materially diminished after his death. The surviving record does not newly adjudicate a justification for the same complete ban based on an interest in the firm. This is an unjustifiably broad prior restraint under the present circumstances. The past findings remain facts; they do not themselves supply a current reason to suppress every public statement. Near, Keefe, Nebraska Press and the exact-fit principles of Madsen and Schenck support the requirement of a present, fitted justification, without treating Madsen's content-neutral formula as a safe harbor for a subject-based command. Alexander's distinction confirms that this is an actual future-speech restraint, not merely a retrospective sanction.

Thomas and Scalia do not adjudicate T3. Their disagreement concerns whether this Court should reach the new constitutional question in the changed vehicle, not an affirmative conclusion that coercion of a deceased person remains possible or that all-topic silence is valid.

**T4 — Present remedy, six joins.** Vacate the California appellate judgment and remand for proceedings conforming to T3. The currently unsupported all-topic prohibition and enforcement dependent on it cannot continue unchanged. The state courts may entertain any proper party's request for new, supported relief suited to the changed circumstances; no replacement decree is commanded or constitutionally approved in advance. The original liability findings are not erased by this prospective correction. Independently lawful remedies for threats, coercion or actionable false statements retain their own elements. Existing Madsen/Schenck party-responsibility law remains applicable to any future decree; its notice-plus-actual-concert limits are preserved rather than newly adjudicated against a person whose conduct is not before the Court. The firm acquires no automatic party status or new entitlement through this mandate.

**TD — Prudential dismissal, two joins.** Thomas and Scalia would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. Death prevents resolution of the granted controversy in its original posture; the new need for interpretation, modification or dissolution can be addressed in California, including the available constitutional objections already described by the neutral packet. A DIG is neither affirmance on the merits nor a constitutional endorsement of the still-operative command. It creates no substantive rule of validity, and it does not itself declare the matter moot. Those Justices oppose T4's constitutional vacatur/remand, not the possibility that California will dissolve or modify the decree.

## Strongest contrary positions and completeness

The six's strongest objection to a DIG is that the explicit, assertedly enforceable command presently restrains speech and its lost justification can be addressed without settling the original categorical question. They do not require petitioners to await a new enforcement case merely because state relief is possible. The two's strongest answer is that case-specific correction does not restore the reason for granting the writ and state proceedings may dispose of the command on narrower grounds. Both positions address the same record; neither is a factual denial of the injunction's terms.

The broader T2 proposal has force as a potential merits argument: adjudicated falsity does not prove all future speech false. But the actual historical majority expressly considered that argument unnecessary after death, and no effective current precedent makes it necessary to complete present relief. A holding on original validity would therefore need an additional changed premise not established here. No such premise is supplied or selected.

There is no retained material departure from the historical Associate dispositions or the limited constitutional ground. The changes described above are changes from the independent provisional freeze, preserved here without altering that freeze. A separate published position explaining the DIG disagreement has substantive work; no separate narrower concurrence is needed merely to preserve Breyer's scope once T2 is removed. Current authorship is left for lawful assignment.
