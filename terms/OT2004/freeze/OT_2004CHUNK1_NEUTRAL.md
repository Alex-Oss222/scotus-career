## 1. Leocal v. Ashcroft

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Leocal v. Ashcroft
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-583; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 1.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-11-09 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a conviction under Florida law for driving under the influence and causing serious bodily injury, without a requirement of intentional force, is a crime of violence under 18 U.S.C. §16 and therefore an aggravated felony under 8 U.S.C. §1101(a)(43)(F).
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, unreported June 30, 2003 judgment dismissing review of the Board's removal decision under its circuit classification of the Florida offense.
* **Relief Requested:** Leocal seeks reversal of the aggravated-felony classification and correction of the removal order; the Government seeks to sustain the classification under either subsection of §16.
* **Material Record:** Leocal, a Haitian citizen and lawful permanent resident, pleaded guilty to two Florida DUI counts involving serious bodily injury and received a prison term exceeding one year. The statutory offense requires causation, but no intentional employment of force. The immigration charge rests on those convictions, not an adjudicated assault offense. Removal during review does not erase the classification's continuing immigration consequences.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The court may determine whether the conviction satisfies the predicate for the criminal-alien review restriction. Calcano-Martinez involved conceded qualifying convictions; it does not make the disputed classification conclusive. The actual offense elements, rather than a judicial reconstruction of the accident, define the issue. No new factual finding about recklessness is available.
* **Relevant Dates:** Florida convictions in 2000; appellate judgment June 30, 2003; historical argument October 12, 2004. The operative immigration and criminal definitions predate the event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Jama concerns the selection of a removal country; Clark v. Martinez concerns detention duration. Neither decides this conviction-classification question.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-11-09.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1254(1); the offense-predicate inquiry under 8 U.S.C. §1252(a)(2)(C). Statutory construction is reviewed independently; a jurisdictional predicate cannot be supplied by the challenged agency conclusion.

##### Issue 1: The force requirement and categorical offense classification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Bailey v. United States requires attention to active employment when construing statutory use of a firearm; its different statute does not itself decide §16. Calcano-Martinez preserves the difference between a conceded criminal predicate and a dispute about that predicate.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The received interpretive rules require the enacted definition, context and actual statutory elements. Agency responsibility alone does not establish authority to control a court's interpretation of a generally applicable criminal definition.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §16(a) addresses force as an offense element; §16(b) addresses a felony's substantial risk of force being used during its commission. Sections 1101(a)(43)(F) and 1227(a)(2)(A)(iii) supply the immigration consequence. Florida Statutes §316.193(3)(c)(2) supplies the predicate. The one-year sentence condition is separate.
* **Material Other Authority:** Taylor v. United States supplies the categorical distinction between offense definition and particular conduct. INS v. St. Cyr preserves effective review of legal eligibility without reweighing lawful immigration discretion.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing statutory and review principles govern the method, but no earlier controlling decision classifies this Florida offense under §16. A dangerous outcome and a force-use element are different propositions.
* **Open Point:** Whether accidental or negligent injury-producing operation qualifies as use of force under either subsection; recklessness is not necessary to decide the minimum conduct covered here.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The circuit treated injury-producing DUI as satisfying the force definition and dismissed review on that basis. **Leocal:** The Government must identify active force employment required by the offense, not merely physical causation; the same force language limits the residual provision. **Government:** Operating a vehicle uses physical force and injury establishes the required nexus; alternatively the offense inherently creates a substantial risk of force. Its merits brief expressly advances both statutory routes. **Alternatives and limits:** The immigration statute separately identifies DUI-related serious offenses elsewhere, which bears on context without making every overlap impermissible. Neither side's interpretation may convert this case into review of a different offense. No oral-argument concession is relied upon.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Exclude offenses permitting conviction for accidental or negligent injury under both subsections: reverse the classification and return for removal proceedings consistent with that rule; no automatic citizenship, admission or damages follows.
* Include such offenses under either subsection: sustain the aggravated-felony predicate, subject to independent preserved immigration issues.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A construction covering the minimum elements of injury-producing DUI supports treatment of materially identical offenses across jurisdictions, but not an answer about every reckless or intentional offense. The injury severity does not itself resolve the verb Congress used.

**Uncertainty:** The record supports the negligence/no-mental-state issue. It supplies no occasion to decide a recklessness-specific statute, another removal ground or the procedures for implementing relief after completed removal.

## 2. Smith v. Texas

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Smith v. Texas
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 04-5323; 132 S.W.3d 407 below; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 37.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-11-15 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Texas could reject Smith's mitigation claim through a severe-disability and causal-nexus relevance screen, and whether its supplemental instruction gave jurors an effective, coherent means to give his mitigation evidence effect.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Texas Court of Criminal Appeals denial of state postconviction relief, reasoning that the evidence lacked the required constitutional significance or that the supplemental instruction was distinguishable.
* **Relief Requested:** Smith seeks correction of his death sentence; Texas seeks to preserve the denial of collateral relief.
* **Material Record:** Smith presented intellectual-functioning and special-education evidence at capital sentencing. The special issues concerned deliberateness and future danger. A supplemental instruction allowed mitigation through a negative special-issue answer although the ordinary verdict directions tied those answers to the underlying factual questions. The issue is the lawful effect of mitigation, not an established categorical intellectual-disability diagnosis.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is direct Supreme Court review of state collateral adjudication, not a federal §2254 application. Any independently adequate state procedural ground must be identified rather than inferred from the merits denial. The current materials establish the mitigation question, but do not settle a complete harmless-error determination.
* **Relevant Dates:** The state decision preceded the simulated June 24, 2004 Tennard v. Dretke decision. That later effective interpretation governs this event subject to any independently applicable collateral finality rule; the earlier mitigation authorities already supplied the relevant right.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Brown v. Payton later concerns a different instruction and mitigation concerning postcrime conduct; it must not be assumed to decide this contradictory special-issue mechanism.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-11-15.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257(a), state collateral posture, ordinary preservation and applicable retroactivity requirements. Deference under the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA) cannot be imported into this direct review channel.

##### Issue 1: Mitigation relevance and effective verdict mechanism

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Tennard v. Dretke rejects a uniquely severe permanent-impairment or causal-nexus threshold for supported culpability mitigation. Penry v. Johnson requires an authorized coherent life-verdict mechanism when special issues do not capture the evidence's mitigating force.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Evaluate the complete charge and trial context for a reasonable likelihood of restricted mitigation use. Relevance does not guarantee favorable weight. Penry separately requires a State-borne beyond-reasonable-doubt effect inquiry for the preserved restriction it addressed.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; the actual Texas special issues and supplemental instruction. No categorical disability exemption may be inferred without the factual predicates for it.
* **Material Other Authority:** Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas preserve independent culpability mitigation; Boyde v. California supplies the complete-charge reasonable-likelihood inquiry.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The relevance screen is already rejected. The remaining application compares the actual instruction with the coherent-verdict rule; sentence relief requires attention to any distinct valid procedural or effect issue.
* **Open Point:** Whether this charge permits a juror to give effect to mitigation without contradicting a required factual certification, and what further effect adjudication is needed.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The state court used a heightened relevance threshold and alternatively distinguished the supplemental charge. **Smith:** His evidence could reduce culpability even if it did not negate deliberateness or future danger, and jurors could not lawfully express that judgment without contradicting the verdict instructions. **Texas:** The charge as a whole directed consideration of mitigation; differences in its wording and trial context should matter. **Alternative grounds:** A valid independent procedural bar or a properly demonstrated harmless error could limit relief, but neither may be manufactured. **Limits:** Low intellectual functioning need not establish categorical disability to be mitigating, and admission of evidence alone does not establish an effective verdict mechanism. No distinct verified government amicus rule or oral concession is relied upon.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Apply the existing relevance rule and find the charge constitutionally adequate in context: reject the instructional claim without requiring any favorable mitigation weight.
* Find the charge contradictory under Penry: reverse the merits rejection and return for genuinely remaining procedural and effect questions; if the State cannot meet the applicable harmlessness burden, require lawful penalty correction.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The rule may cover materially equivalent special-issue instructions, without prescribing a particular fourth question or converting all mitigation errors into structural error.

**Uncertainty:** The entire penalty record matters to effect. The record establishes neither intellectual disability nor a resolved procedural default; the instructional defect does not by itself require release.

## 3. United States v. $100,348.00 in U.S. Currency

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. $100,348.00 in U.S. Currency
* **Citation or Docket:** 354 F.3d 1110; Ninth Circuit consolidated appeals. No historical Supreme Court merits docket is identified.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-11-15 (scheduled simulated merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a lawful possessor with standing to contest forfeiture may challenge the entire forfeiture under the Excessive Fines Clause even though another person owns the currency? Whether forfeiting the full amount of lawfully sourced currency for failing to report its export is grossly disproportionate to the reporting offense?
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit affirmance of the pertinent civil-forfeiture rulings, including the bailee's full-amount challenge and the district court's reduction of the forfeiture to $10,000.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks full forfeiture; Mayzel seeks protection of his possessory interest against the entire punitive exaction. The owner's separate late claim does not automatically supply Mayzel's rights.
* **Material Record:** Mayzel carried $100,348, including $100,000 entrusted for delivery in Israel. The district court credited a lawful source and found no governmental financial loss. Mayzel was convicted of a false statement but acquitted of the criminal reporting count on a written-report rationale; the separate civil-forfeitability judgment remained. The owner filed an untimely claim. Some findings relevant to a hypothetical Guidelines fine between $5,000 and $30,000 were not explicit, but the courts found the full forfeiture excessive and $10,000 permissible.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The possessory claimant must establish an actual legally protected interest and comply with the claim procedure. The owner's rejected filing is not automatically revived. This proceeding predates the Civil Asset Forfeiture Reform Act of 2000 (CAFRA) and cannot acquire the later statute's burden merely because review occurs in 2004. The two questions presented do not independently reopen every statutory forfeiture ruling.
* **Relevant Dates:** Underlying transport and commencement preceded CAFRA's effective applicability to later-filed actions; court of appeals decision January 16, 2004. The November date is identified, with timely review assumed rather than a fabricated grant order.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-11-15.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1254(1); Article III injury and redressability; then-applicable supplemental forfeiture procedure. Independent constitutional proportionality review preserves clear-error review of supported facts.

##### Issue 1: Possessory injury and the reach of the challenge

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The forfeiture precedents distinguish a protected property interest from the legal scope of a forfeiture remedy. Austin v. United States establishes partly punitive coverage; it does not limit every challenge to bare legal title.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Establish the claimant's own legal interest and injury before deciding excessiveness. A civil label does not remove a punitive exaction from the Clause; standing and ultimate success remain separate.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth Amendment; 31 U.S.C. §§5316-5317 as applicable to the proceeding; state bailment law identifies the possessory interest and obligations.
* **Material Other Authority:** Republic National Bank of Miami v. United States preserves meaningful adjudication and possible return of forfeited proceeds, without deciding this bailee question.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A lawful possessory interest can suffer concrete injury independently of ownership; no earlier controlling decision resolves the full-amount challenge of this particular gratuitous bailee.
* **Open Point:** Whether the possessor's obligations and control support review of the whole entrusted fund, rather than only currency he personally owns.

##### Issue 2: Gross disproportionality and supported amount

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Bajakajian applies gross disproportionality to punitive reporting forfeiture and rejects a statutory-connection-only answer. United States v. Wagoner County Real Estate requires findings permitting an actual value, culpability, harm and penalty comparison and places the disproportionality burden on the claimant in its field.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** No fixed ratio, exhaustive checklist or automatic lawful-funds immunity applies. The legal proportionality judgment is independently reviewed; facts receive their ordinary deferential review.
* **Other Governing Law:** The punitive operation of §5317 and the applicable offense and penalty provisions; the old procedural regime remains distinct from CAFRA.
* **Material Other Authority:** Austin v. United States supplies coverage; United States v. Ursery separates the Double Jeopardy classification, which does not resolve Excessive Fines coverage.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Bajakajian's lawful-source and limited-harm findings are relevant analogies, not a universal $15,000 ceiling. A full forfeiture may be excessive without making every lesser amount valid.
* **Open Point:** Whether the supported record proves gross disproportionality of this entire fund and permits selection of a lawful lesser exaction.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The appellate court recognized the bailee's own injury, sustained the lawful-source finding and assessed culpability, limited harm and the available fine comparators. It found the full sum excessive and sustained $10,000. **Claimant:** Loss of entrusted property burdens his own possession and obligations; even the reduced amount requires justification. **Government:** A nonowner should not vindicate another person's rights, and the substantial statutory maximum supports full forfeiture. **Alternative grounds and limits:** Forfeitability and excessiveness are separate. The unproved higher Guidelines bracket cannot be treated as an adjudicated sentence, and lawful source alone creates no immunity. No oral concession is relied upon.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Recognize first-party possessory standing and conduct full-amount proportionality review, preserving the owner's separate default.
* Limit or deny standing if the asserted interest lacks legal substance or would not be redressed; that requires the actual bailment record, not an ownership-only rule.
* On the merits, sustain the supported exaction or require a reduced judgment after findings sufficient to apply gross disproportionality.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule for lawful possessors subject to duties concerning the whole fund fits this record. A universal right for strangers to challenge any forfeiture does not. The amount rule can govern reporting-only cases without granting lawful cash categorical immunity.

**Uncertainty:** The record does not settle every input to a hypothetical criminal fine. That does not prevent constitutional review of the actual $10,000 exaction if the result is supportable under either plausible benchmark. No unproved drug offense or automatic midpoint formula may supply the answer.

## 4. Koons Buick Pontiac GMC, Inc. v. Nigh

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Koons Buick Pontiac GMC, Inc. v. Nigh
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-377; 319 F.3d 119 below; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 50.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-11-30 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the 1995 amendment to 15 U.S.C. §1640(a)(2)(A) removed the $1,000 statutory-damages ceiling for individual Truth in Lending Act actions involving personal-property credit transactions.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit affirmance of an uncapped statutory-damages award calculated at twice the finance charge.
* **Relief Requested:** The dealer requests application of the $1,000 ceiling; Nigh seeks to retain the clause (i) award. Liability and independent claims must be kept separate from the reviewed statutory-damages amount.
* **Material Record:** The dispute arose from financing documents for a used truck and an improperly documented alarm charge. The jury awarded $24,192.80 in TILA statutory damages. The textual question results from the placement of the general bracket after the consumer-lease clause and the addition of a separate real-property-loan bracket.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A final civil judgment presents a preserved interpretation question. No constitutional damages limit or general consumer-credit policy judgment is needed. The enacted version applicable to the transaction controls; later amended monetary limits must not be substituted.
* **Relevant Dates:** The amendments of 1974, 1976 and 1995 supply the structural history; the transaction occurred in 2000. Historical argument October 5, 2004.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-11-30.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Independent statutory review; the judgment may be reversed only as far as the erroneous statutory-damages measure reaches, preserving independent components and properly determined fees.

##### Issue 1: Statutory hierarchy and the retained ceiling

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lamie v. United States Trustee requires enforcement of enacted eligibility and structural language despite an asserted drafting imperfection. Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank requires a textual entitlement before a remedy is awarded.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Text, statutory hierarchy and the operation of related clauses govern. A specific provision may qualify a general one without deleting the general rule for transactions outside the exception.
* **Other Governing Law:** The applicable §1640(a)(2)(A) calculates twice the finance charge in clause (i), provides a lease measure in clause (ii), retains a $100-to-$1,000 bracket for the subparagraph, and adds a specific $200-to-$2,000 bracket in clause (iii) for qualifying real-property transactions.
* **Material Other Authority:** The preamendment arrangement and successive enacted amendments are interpretive evidence. Lower-court decisions supply competing readings, not simulated Supreme Court law.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** No earlier controlling decision construes this exact TILA amendment. Received interpretive rules do not authorize replacing the statute with the remedy a court regards as better deterrence.
* **Open Point:** Whether the specific clause (iii) bracket qualifies the broader subparagraph bracket or instead leaves clause (i) uncapped.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The new clause's separate bracket was treated as showing that the adjacent old bracket applied only to leases. **Dealer:** Congress retained the word subparagraph, so the general bracket still governs clause (i), subject to the expressly different real-property bracket. **Nigh:** Placement and punctuation limit the old bracket to clause (ii); applying it across all clauses conflicts with clause (iii)'s express range. **Alternative analysis:** Specific-over-general construction can reconcile the two brackets without rewriting either. Legislative history may confirm, but cannot manufacture, that relationship. **Limits:** The statute separately authorizes actual damages and fees; a statutory cap is not a cap on all recovery. No oral concession or independently verified amicus rule is required to resolve this textual dispute.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Read the old bracket as generally applicable with the new bracket as a specific exception: reverse the excess statutory award and remand for a conforming judgment.
* Read the old bracket as clause-specific: affirm the twice-finance-charge statutory award, leaving other judgment components undisturbed.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Either interpretation governs the operative amended provision's individual credit actions, not class recovery or later statutory versions. A court need not decide every credit product to classify this personal-property loan.

**Uncertainty:** Any recalculation of associated fees or independent recovery depends on the preserved judgment components. The Court should not infer that the cap extinguishes a distinct proven claim.

## 5. Anderson v. Spear

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Anderson v. Spear
* **Citation or Docket:** 356 F.3d 651; Sixth Circuit No. 02-5529. No historical Supreme Court merits docket is identified.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-11-30 (scheduled simulated merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the First Amendment permits a State to prohibit electioneering, including distribution of write-in voting instructions, within 500 feet of polling places? Whether the First Amendment permits Kentucky to cap a candidate’s loans to his own campaign at $50,000 and prohibit post-election contributions used to retire campaign debt?
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Sixth Circuit reversal of summary judgment for state officials on the pertinent speech and financing claims, with further proceedings on remand.
* **Relief Requested:** The candidate seeks declaratory and injunctive relief against the three restrictions; officials seek to sustain their election-integrity and anticorruption measures.
* **Material Record:** A write-in gubernatorial candidate challenged restrictions on distributing voting instructions, lending personal funds and accepting contributions after the election. The challenged perimeter extends onto ordinary public streets and sidewalks. The historical action also contained other challenges; those separate claims are not additional questions in this assignment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The election's completion does not alone resolve recurrence or remaining debt injury. A temporary funding suspension must be evaluated provision by provision; it does not prove repeal of these restrictions. The federal appellate remand does not require state-court §1257 finality. Timely Supreme Court review is an assumption of the calendar placement.
* **Relevant Dates:** 1999 candidacy; temporary budget legislation in 2003; appellate decision January 16, 2004, with rehearing proceedings in 2004. No present-day Kentucky amendment is backdated.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** American Civil Liberties Union of Nevada v. Heller concerns compelled sponsor identification; Clingman concerns primary participation; Green Party concerns enrollment records. Their different restrictions require their own burdens and records.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-11-30.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** First Amendment §1983 action, live threatened enforcement and equitable relief. Facial breadth and severability must be evaluated under the actual statute; federal courts may not rewrite Kentucky law without a permissible construction.

##### Issue 1: Polling-place perimeter and speech coverage

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Burson v. Freeman establishes strict scrutiny for campaign-specific restrictions on traditional public forums, but no majority validity rationale or numerical safe harbor beyond its actual judgment. Ordinary streets do not lose their forum character merely because they are near polls.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The State must establish a compelling voting-integrity interest and necessity and tailoring for the actual scope. Viewpoint neutrality and speech elsewhere do not eliminate the content-based burden.
* **Other Governing Law:** First and Fourteenth Amendments; Kentucky Revised Statutes §117.235(3) and the actual definition of electioneering.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mills v. Alabama protects election-day advocacy; United States v. Grace preserves ordinary sidewalk status.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Preventing intimidation is legitimate and compelling, but the size and speech coverage of this 500-foot rule are not settled by the earlier 100-foot judgment.
* **Open Point:** Whether the State justifies this expanded perimeter and coverage of nonintimidating write-in instruction.

##### Issue 2: Personal loans and post-election debt contributions

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Buckley v. Valeo distinguishes a candidate's own campaign resources from third-party contributions. Russell v. Burris and Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC require an objectively grounded corruption interest, close fit and meaningful advocacy for contribution restrictions. McConnell v. Federal Election Commission does not establish a universal expenditure or timing exception.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Classify each regulated transaction by its actual operation. The State bears the justification required for the corresponding speech or association burden; raising funds after an election is not immune from anticorruption regulation, but a categorical prohibition needs its own fit.
* **Other Governing Law:** Kentucky Revised Statutes §§121.150(13), (16) and (21), in their applicable versions; the candidate's personal loan and later donor payment are distinct transactions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Federal Election Commission v. Beaumont preserves meaningful voluntary association and source-specific circumvention analysis; it does not turn personal financing into a donor contribution.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing rules permit fitted donor limits while protecting candidate expenditure. They do not decide these loan and timing prohibitions automatically.
* **Open Point:** Whether a blanket loan ceiling or complete debt-retirement contribution ban can be justified by the record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The panel rejected the relevant restrictions as insufficiently justified, while leaving further proceedings and distinct claims. **Candidate:** The zone suppresses ordinary campaign information far from voting; self-financing lacks the asserted donor-corruption rationale; a total post-election ban blocks lawful debt retirement more broadly than donor limits and disclosure. **Officials:** Poll access requires administrable protection, loans may later be repaid by influence-seeking contributors, and contributions after voting may be especially connected to officeholder favors. **Alternative grounds:** Tailored limits on later donor repayments may address the loan concern without limiting the candidate's initial speech. **Necessary limits:** No ruling should erase intimidation prohibitions, constitutional contribution ceilings or valid reporting. No asserted oral concession is used; other claims in the nine-claim historical action remain outside these questions.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Sustain one or more restrictions if their actual operation satisfies the governing burden, identifying the distinct rule and record for each.
* Invalidate the unsupported 500-foot reach and categorical financing restrictions, remanding for precise declaratory and injunctive terms and lawful severability.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A campaign-perimeter ruling may reject this reach without prescribing a replacement number. A personal-funding rule can distinguish later donor repayment. A debt-retirement holding may reject a total ban while preserving fitted contribution restrictions.

**Uncertainty:** Current threat, any surviving debt and exact severability provisions require the litigation record. A temporary appropriation measure is not presumed to make all claims moot.

## 6. San Diego v. Roe

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** San Diego v. Roe

* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-12-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether defense counsel's informed strategic concession of guilt in a capital trial, when the defendant neither agrees nor objects, automatically establishes ineffective assistance or instead requires the ordinary performance-and-prejudice inquiry.
* **Question Granted:** The questions presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Florida Supreme Court, 857 So. 2d 172, granting a new trial on an automatic ineffectiveness theory after state postconviction proceedings.
* **Relief Requested:** Florida seeks reversal of the automatic new-trial rule; Nixon seeks preservation of relief on the ground that conceding guilt required his express authorization.
* **Material Record:** Counsel Corin investigated the prosecution's substantial evidence, unsuccessfully sought a noncapital plea, and repeatedly discussed a concession strategy with Nixon. Nixon gave no responsive approval or objection. Counsel contested evidence and instructions and presented substantial penalty-phase advocacy. The jury still had to return a verdict after the State's proof; Nixon entered no guilty plea. The record concerns silence after consultation, not an express demand to maintain innocence.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is direct review of a state postconviction judgment deciding the federal standard, not a federal §2254 proceeding. The Court must distinguish Nixon's personal plea decision from counsel's conduct of a contested trial. No finding that Nixon validly waived a trial is available or necessary to the State's position.
* **Relevant Dates:** The charged events occurred in 1984. The reviewed state decision issued in 2003. Trial-date professional standards govern counsel's performance, while the Court's current simulated decisions identify the applicable federal analytical framework.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Bell v. Cone concerns substantive sentencing advocacy and particular omissions. It does not categorically authorize guilt concessions. The later listed Bell v. Cone matter concerns an aggravating circumstance, a different claim.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-12-13.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257 review of a final state federal-law judgment; the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments. Strickland places the performance and reasonable-probability prejudice burdens on the defendant unless a recognized presumed-prejudice category actually applies.

##### Issue 1: Consultation, concession and effective assistance

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Bell v. Cone rejects the use of Cronic's complete-failure category for particular choices within substantive advocacy; the actual adversarial role, not a count of courtroom acts, controls. Williams v. Taylor rejects an extra fundamental-unfairness hurdle for ordinary Strickland prejudice.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Strickland assesses objectively reasonable investigation and professional judgment without hindsight, then a reasonable probability of a different result. Cronic's recognized complete-denial or complete-failure categories remain distinct. A strategy label alone proves neither competent investigation nor reasonable performance.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Sixth Amendment; the defendant personally decides whether to plead guilty. A counsel argument does not automatically waive the State's burden, jury determination, confrontation or appeal.
* **Material Other Authority:** Boykin v. Alabama governs a guilty plea's actual relinquishment of rights. Brookhart v. Janis concerns a proceeding imposed contrary to the accused's asserted trial choice and cannot be reduced to a rule that silence authorizes a plea.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The controlling distinction between actual abandonment and specific alleged errors already applies. Whether a disclosed capital concession to which the client gives no answer belongs in the ordinary category requires application to this record.
* **Open Point:** Whether absence of express consent alone supplies a categorical deficiency or prejudice presumption, and what ordinary review remains.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Florida court treated a concession as functionally equivalent to a guilty plea and inferred automatic deficiency and prejudice without express consent. **Florida:** The trial retained its evidentiary and adjudicative structure; counsel's effort to obtain a life sentence must be assessed in context under Strickland. **Nixon:** Control over admitting guilt is fundamental, and a lawyer cannot replace authorization with silence merely because the prosecution's evidence is strong. **Alternative grounds and limits:** A conclusion that Cronic does not automatically apply leaves investigation, consultation and ordinary prejudice for lawful adjudication. Neither side may turn a silent-client record into a finding of an express objection. **United States:** Supported reversal and ordinary ineffective-assistance review. No oral-argument concession is assumed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Reverse the categorical rule and return the ordinary ineffective-assistance claim for assessment of consultation, investigation, strategy and prejudice. This establishes no general license to override an expressed trial objective.
* Treat the actual concession as a surrender of a protected personal decision, if its legal and practical operation warrants that characterization, and preserve relief on an identified waiver or abandonment ground. Mere unfavorable strategy is insufficient for that path.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule distinguishing a genuine guilty plea or complete abandonment from a disclosed strategy in an otherwise adversarial capital trial would govern similar silent-client records. A broader rule permitting concessions over objection needs a record and question that are absent here.

**Uncertainty:** The existing judgment used a categorical standard. The Court should not invent an adjudication of every ordinary Strickland allegation or presume that evidence of guilt proves all reasonable-strategy and prejudice questions.

## 13. Brosseau v. Haugen

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brosseau v. Haugen
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1261; historical reporter locator 543 U.S. 194.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; review of the identified judgment after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2004-12-13 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Brosseau is entitled to individual qualified immunity from Haugen's Fourth Amendment damages claim on the plaintiff-favorable summary-judgment record.
* **Question Granted:** The qualified-immunity question. The underlying force merits are outside the identified limited review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, 339 F.3d 857, reversing summary judgment for the officer and concluding that the shooting violated clearly established law.
* **Relief Requested:** Brosseau seeks restoration of her personal immunity judgment; Haugen seeks return of his damages action for lawful further proceedings.
* **Material Record:** Brosseau shot Haugen as his Jeep began moving during an effort to arrest him. Occupied vehicles and other officers were nearby. She asserted concern for their safety; Haugen disputes the asserted immediate danger. The reviewing court must accept the properly supported plaintiff-favorable account rather than resolve the vehicle's path or threatened persons in the officer's favor. Haugen survived. The claim invokes §1983.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The limited question concerns personal immunity, not a final liability determination. A legal immunity question may be resolved on assumed facts; genuinely disputed material facts cannot be settled through an appellate reconstruction favoring the movant. The separate underlying merits grant was not accepted.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged conduct occurred in February 1999. Clearly established law, under the entering defense, must exist by that conduct date. Saucier v. Katz and Hope v. Pelzer explain current analytical law but cannot themselves supply earlier factual notice.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the questions presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 30, 2004; assessed as of immediately before 2004-12-13.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None incorporated beyond the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified that must precede resolution on the June 30, 2004 baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 and ordinary summary-judgment rules; Harlow and Anderson provide the entering objective immunity defense. Johnson v. Jones separates reviewable legal issues from factual-sufficiency disputes.

##### Issue 1: Personal immunity on the lawful record

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Saucier v. Katz produces a binding remand under existing objective immunity, but no controlling new general rationale and no mandatory merits-first sequence. Harlow and Anderson remain effective. Hope v. Pelzer rejects a demand for a factual replica and permits fair warning from sufficiently clear principles.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the governing right and the level of particularity needed for legal notice, taking the proper plaintiff-favorable factual assumptions. Reasonable factual perceptions enter the Graham merits inquiry; entering law also retains its distinct objective legal-immunity inquiry.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourth Amendment, §1983 and summary-judgment procedure. The immunity issue asks about February 1999 law; the current decision date is not the notice date.
* **Material Other Authority:** Tennessee v. Garner restricts deadly force against a fleeing suspect without the required serious threat. Graham v. Connor evaluates force in actual context. Hunter v. Bryant does not authorize resolving disputed facts for the official.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The governing defense and the absence of a factual-twin requirement are settled. Their application to the assumed threat, vehicle movement and nearby persons remains record-specific.
* **Open Point:** Whether those assumed circumstances were sufficiently governed by preconduct authority to defeat immunity; whether the Court should revise the defense is a distinct doctrinal choice, not inherited law.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit found both a force violation and sufficient legal notice under Garner and Graham. **Brosseau:** The danger associated with a determined vehicular escape differs materially from the nonthreatening fugitive in Garner, and existing cases did not clearly prohibit her response. **Haugen:** A disputed possibility of danger cannot become an established immediate threat; general deadly-force limits can provide fair warning without a matching car case. **Alternative grounds:** The limited grant permits an immunity ruling without deciding every force-merits issue. A legal defense cannot depend on the officer winning a disputed factual account. No separately material verified Government rule is attributed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* Apply existing objective immunity and grant it if preconduct law did not give sufficient contextual notice on the correct assumed facts; this ends the individual damages claim without establishing constitutional permission.
* Deny immunity if the assumed conduct is an obvious or adequately established application of existing deadly-force limits; actual liability and damages remain for appropriate proceedings.
* Revise the personal defense through an express precedent decision, identifying its replacement and temporal operation. A prior separate opinion cannot silently accomplish that revision.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The existing-law paths can decide this vehicular-escape application without creating a categorical vehicle or flight exception. A replacement defense would extend throughout its expressly defined personal-damages domain and requires corresponding treatment of Harlow and Anderson.

**Uncertainty:** The material threat and timing facts are disputed. Later cases are unavailable to establish February 1999 notice. The actual existence of a force violation is not conclusively adjudicated by the limited immunity review.
