## Reconciliation result and actual changed law

There is no historical Supreme Court merits comparator for this case. The First Circuit's result is evidence of the judgment below, not evidence of an individual Associate's hypothetical Supreme Court vote. All eight independent commitments survive reconciliation, for the actual financed-function and claim-specific standing reasons below. The Court can affirm the rejection of the parents' own claim and their attempted assertion of the school's claim without adopting the lower court's broader understanding of religious funding.

The lower opinion treated historical Zelman as permitting qualifying private-choice tuition programs, then read historical Locke broadly to permit the State's decision against sectarian tuition, with rational-basis review after its free-exercise inquiry. That reasoning is not imported. The entering *Agostini*, *Strout*, *Zelman* and unanimous *McCallum* decisions instead ask what restricted public payment buys and prohibit purchase of proved integrated devotional work while protecting independently controlled secular work. *Locke* supplies bounded permission concerning an individual ministry-degree scholarship, not a general identity exclusion. *Columbia Union* and *K.D.M.* prevent the use of religious character alone to deny equal consideration of genuinely eligible secular work. The lower opinion's forum discussion can confirm the distinction between general tuition and private-expression access, but cannot enlarge or displace these specific rules.

The prior *Strout* decision resolved the granted Religion Clause issues concerning this kind of ordinary tuition. It did not dispose of these different parents' equal-protection claim. The present equality claim therefore receives a direct decision. Its requested purchase is the integrated ordinary tuition, expressly sought to obtain Catholic instruction and viewpoint. No separated secular-service application is supplied. The parents' real relationship with the school differs from *Kowalski*'s hypothetical future clients, but that relationship supplies no hindrance preventing the school from suing. The complete lower opinion confirms that its unsupported reprisal assertion established no such obstacle; the selected ground does not require a new finding about the school or its wishes.

## Operative propositions and remedies

**E1 — Parents' own claim.** Equal protection does not require Maine to purchase the integrated devotional instructional function presented by this ordinary-tuition request merely because the program pays for secular education at private alternatives. The relevant distinction is the demonstrated function the public payment buys, not religious identity, presumed diversion, an asserted lack of sincere religious compulsion, or a categorical permission to disadvantage religion.

The request falls within the actual-purchase rule of *Strout* and *Zelman*, reinforced by *McCallum*'s unanimous treatment of restricted provider reimbursement. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and a secular program objective do not change the purchased function. The competing equality argument has force for independently identifiable secular work under lawful program requirements and effective use controls; the present ordinary-tuition request does not supply that different application. *Columbia Union* and *K.D.M.* remain operative on their own terms. The school is not disqualified from every possible secular service by its affiliation, and no rule declares all its activities inseparable.

The disposition is affirmance of rejection of this personal equality claim. No public tuition, damages or separate-service payment is ordered, and no invented eligibility issue supplies an alternative ground. The local educational-needs condition, school approval requirements and sectarian exclusion remain distinct. The futility of applying under the exclusion does not prove compliance with every lawful neutral condition; conversely, the Court cannot invent a prior denial under a criterion never applied. The parents' own injury is cognizable separately from the school's rights. Religious hostility and exclusion from protected private-expression access are not justified by the actual-purchase rule.

**E2 — School's institutional claim.** The parents may not assert St. Dominic's separate equal-protection rights without a genuine obstacle to the school's own assertion, even if their existing relationship is close and the funding restriction injures them. The required hindrance is not established on this record. Their personal claim and the third party's claim must be treated separately.

The disposition is affirmance of rejection of representative standing, without adjudicating the merits of the school's independent claim. *Kowalski* reinforces the need to test the actual relationship and obstacle separately; its absent-client rationale cannot be mechanically applied to these parents. No school obstacle, litigation preference or disinclination to accept regulation is found by inference. No remand to search for an unasserted or unsupported obstacle is required. This is not a categorical bar on parents representing their children or on all school-related third-party claims.
