## Smith v. Massachusetts

### Judgment and sufficient common ground

All eight Associates provisionally support reversal of the Massachusetts judgment insofar as it sustains the firearm conviction, followed by remand to remove that conviction and its dependent punishment. They support a bar to another guilt determination on the firearm count. The two assault convictions remain operative; the state courts implement any lawful sentencing adjustment dependent on removing the firearm count. The concurrent ten-to-twelve-year firearm sentence does not make the conviction immune from correction.

All eight support the following complete ground at the same operative level: the judge completed an evidentiary-insufficiency determination by unqualifiedly allowing the Rule 25(a) motion before the trial proceeded beyond the prosecution's case and through the remaining defense presentation. An actual insufficiency acquittal cannot be retracted to obtain a jury determination of guilt merely because the judge later finds the legal assessment mistaken. The protection does not require a second trial or a showing that the defendant changed his strategy. The general common-law power to reconsider interlocutory matters does not itself render this completed acquittal provisional.

This application does not turn the endorsement, docket notation, or jury notice into an independent constitutional requirement. The prosecutor's earlier statement that he might seek reopening is considered, but he made no such motion before resting, and the judge did not reserve this allowance for further argument. The later authority showed an asserted legal error in a completed determination, rather than an unfinished ruling whose terms kept the question open.

## Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co.

### Component judgments and common vessel rule

All eight Associates support reversal of the Jones Act summary-judgment branch and remand for further Jones Act proceedings. The Super Scoop is a vessel under the applicable water-transport definition: it is practically capable of carrying equipment and people over water and actually does so in its regular operations. Transportation need not be its primary business, self-propulsion is unnecessary, and a temporary stop does not defeat that capacity. The governing definition concerns practical capability in the craft's actual condition; theoretical movement or flotation alone does not establish it.

All eight support leaving the separate section 905(b) summary judgment undisturbed. Its independent employer-capacity ground was reached with vessel status conceded, and no separate capacity question is within the granted review. The disposition is therefore **reversed in part and remanded on the Jones Act branch; the section 905(b) judgment remains operative**. This is not a merits affirmance of the capacity rationale. It respects the scope of review and the independence of the adjudicated claim.

On remand the lower court must give effect to the correct vessel classification and Dutra's motion-specific acknowledgments of crew membership, ninety-nine percent service aboard, and mission contribution. It may decide genuinely remaining Jones Act issues through ordinary proceedings; it may not re-create the rejected vessel ground or posit a dispute contradicting those acknowledged facts merely to require another status trial. ## Karaha Bodas recognition review

### Judgment and sufficient common ground

All eight Associates support affirmance of the recognition judgment against the granted Article V(1)(e) challenge and rejection of Rule 60(b) reopening on that annulment ground. The existing United States recognition judgment remains operative. There is no need for a new adjudicative remand on the interpreted clauses or the Indonesian annulment's qualification.

All eight support the same treaty rule: the country under whose law an award was made means the country whose national arbitral procedural law governs the arbitration, rather than any country whose law supplies the substantive contract rules. The resisting party must establish a qualifying annulment or suspension under Article V(1)(e). Choosing substantive law does not itself designate that country's courts as the Convention's competent annulment authority. A contractual incorporation of particular provisions must be assessed for what it selects; it does not automatically select the entire national supervisory regime.

The clauses here choose Geneva as the legal arbitral place and UNCITRAL arbitration, with the award made in Geneva. The hearing's physical presence in Paris does not displace that chosen place. The general Indonesian governing-law clause, specified temporal and appellate waivers, and Section 631 provision do not establish that Indonesian national arbitral procedural law governed the proceeding. The Article V(1)(e) country criterion therefore does not give this Indonesian annulment the claimed recognition-defeating force.

The Section 631 clause receives independent attention: it authorizes the tribunal to avoid strict legal rules inconsistent with the contract's spirit and the parties' intent, and directs its interpretation and enforcement of the contract. It is affirmative decisional authority, not merely a waived deadline. Read with the complete quoted arrangements, that particular authorization concerns how the tribunal resolves the contractual dispute; it does not assign national judicial supervision to Indonesia. The express waivers likewise remove particular possible limits and appellate rights without affirmatively selecting the remainder of an Indonesian supervisory system. This construction does not require every valid procedural-law choice to use a prescribed formula or to be express. It decides the actual combined clauses.
