## 96. Orff v. United States — June 23, 2005

**Issue and reach.** The surviving farmers' direct reclamation-contract action invokes 43 U.S.C. §390uu. The question is the scope of consent, independently of whether the farmers possess enforceable third-party-beneficiary rights. The district plaintiffs' earlier presence does not itself establish the required procedural posture of the surviving claim. The action seeks enforcement against the United States and its agents; the supplied record identifies no continuing independent dispute between nonfederal parties requiring the sovereign's addition.

**Law entering.** “Agency FTCA exclusivity and sue-and-be-sued immunity,” present operation effective *FDIC v. Meyer*, February 23, 1994, preserves the distinction between consent and cause of action and honors the breadth of the actual clause enacted. “APA waiver and substitute-payment equitable liens,” effective January 20, 1999, derives from unanimous *Blue Fox* and requires consent for the particular requested relief. “Adjudicatory waiver and monetary assessments under the McCarran Amendment,” effective *United States v. Idaho*, May 3, 1993, distinguishes consent to adjudication from consent to an additional obligation. These authorities do not replace the wording of §390uu with a universal narrowest-possible-construction rule. Congress's affirmative words, including the second sentence and venue sentence, must be given effect.

**Supported competing paths.** A joinder-specific construction permits adjudication when the United States is a necessary additional defendant in a qualifying reclamation-contract dispute, but does not authorize the farmers' surviving direct claim. Its strongest challenge is the breadth of any suit, contractual-rights adjudication, and the second sentence's amenability language: a construction must leave the statute substantial work and explain why those words remain tied to the initial consent. The farmers' broader construction treats the entire provision as authorizing direct adjudication of covered contract rights whenever the sovereign's participation is necessary to effective relief. Its strongest difficulty is that joining a necessary party has an established procedural meaning that would become nearly redundant if every direct suit against the Government qualified. A former contracting plaintiff does not remain a nonfederal adverse party merely because it was once present.

Neither path establishes beneficiary entitlement, water-delivery liability, a payment award, or an alternative waiver. If §390uu is insufficient, the appropriate disposition is jurisdictional dismissal or affirmance on that threshold, without an adjudication that reclamation beneficiaries can never recover through any lawful route. If the provision authorizes this direct suit, the unadjudicated contract issues return for ordinary consideration. A Tucker Act route must satisfy its own forum, claimant and remedy prerequisites; none is established by this packet.

**Justice-specific prior authorities and counterpaths.** Scalia authored unanimous *Blue Fox*; Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined its actual-remedy/actual-consent distinction. Thomas authored *Meyer*, joined by all then-sitting Associates, including Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; its treatment of broad sue-and-be-sued language is the counterweight against a reflexive narrowing of every clause. Breyer's own direct join of *Blue Fox* supplies his date-eligible position without importing an opinion from before his appointment. O'Connor's *Idaho* opinion and the joins of Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas reinforce the separation of the precise consent and additional requested obligation. ## 97. Halbert v. Michigan — June 23, 2005

**Issue and reach.** Two components require separate treatment: counsel for this first Michigan leave application after a plea, and whether Halbert knowingly relinquished that assistance. The application undertakes initial appellate merits screening, and no earlier counseled appellate brief or appellate merits determination has supplied the functions on which *Ross v. Moffitt* relied. That functional distinction must be assessed against Michigan's discretion, narrower post-plea issues, appointment exceptions and finality interest. A merits-screening label alone does not settle the constitutional classification.

**Law entering.** “Advocate-assisted screening of an allegedly frivolous first appeal,” present operation effective *Smith v. Robbins*, January 19, 2000, permits equivalent formats but requires an actual advocate-assisted screening function and restores a wholly missing function without preliminary proof that an omitted issue would win. The precise extension from an appeal as of right to Michigan's leave system remains open. “Representation on a first appeal as of right,” *Martinez v. Court of Appeal*, January 12, 2000, distinguishes appellate fair access from trial self-representation; it supplies no general right to dispense with advocacy through an uncounseled filing. “Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver,” present operation effective *Iowa v. Tovar*, March 8, 2004, retains *Godinez*'s June 24, 1993 separation of capacity and actual informed voluntary waiver and rejects a mandatory recital where real understanding is proved.

**Current-term clarification.** *Kowalski v. Tesmer*, December 13, 2004, resolves the attorneys' unidentified-future-client relationship defect only; it expressly leaves the Michigan appointment entitlement open for proper individual claimants. Halbert presents his own alleged completed injury. *Bradshaw v. Stumpf*, June 13, 2005, unanimously permits the whole record and counsel's actual explanation to establish informed assent, but does not create an irrebuttable presumption or conflate understanding one right with relinquishing another. Neither case establishes a waiver here.

**Supported competing paths.** The Douglas/Robbins path treats the application as the first merits opportunity and requires appointed advocacy or a genuinely adequate equivalent. Its strongest objection is that the State has made leave discretionary, a plea limits possible grounds, and counsel is available in specified circumstances. The response must explain the actual work the appellate court performs and the advocate's missing contribution; it cannot rest on disability or indigency alone. The Ross path treats the leave decision as a permissible selection stage after a conviction voluntarily narrowed by plea. Its strongest difficulty is the absence of the earlier lawyer-assisted appellate presentation and initial error correction that made later selection different in *Ross*. A reviewing court must identify an actual equivalent process, not infer it from the bare permission to file pro se.

The distinct waiver path depends on understanding and intentional relinquishment of appellate assistance. A plea's waiver of trial rights does not logically settle this later right. *Tovar* and *Stumpf* forbid demanding magic words, but equally do not manufacture actual understanding from ambiguous appointment warnings. Halbert's requests for counsel are record evidence bearing on surrender, not proof that every conceivable waiver is impossible. Relief on a counsel denial restores adequate first-tier consideration; it does not invalidate the conviction or establish success on any sentencing, voluntariness or representation claim.

## 99. Green Party of New York State v. New York State Board of Elections — June 23, 2005

**Record and posture clarification.** The November 17, 2004 Second Circuit opinion, 389 F.3d 411, has been saved and read in full. It confirms the supplied facts and the separate May and September 2003 orders. It reviewed the preliminary injunction for abuse of discretion, legal error and clearly erroneous facts, with the independent record examination it described for First Amendment issues. These are the lower court's stated standards; the Court must identify any rule it adopts rather than silently convert all of them into a new universal preliminary-injunction formula.

The revised form allows an Other write-in line for any political organization, but the order's continuing maintenance obligations cover the plaintiff organizations and persist while they retain sufficient support to place a statewide candidate on the ballot at the most recent gubernatorial election. The notice obligation requires best efforts to notify the already disenrolled Liberal and Right to Life voters that a new form permits reenrollment. The separate enrollment-data order gives the Green Party the same data in the same form as statutory Parties. These distinct terms must not become an immediate right to universal primary elections or every benefit of statutory party status. The lower court expressly confined its holding to the organizations before it; any broader common-policy relief must rest on effective intervening law and its actual remedial conditions.

**Law entering.** “Authorized party expansion and independently qualified slate components,” *Norman*, January 14, 1992; “Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system,” *Burdick*, June 8, 1992; and “Meaningful official recognition of a qualified party's consenting nomination,” *Timmons*, April 28, 1997, require examination of the specific burden and actual relation to the interest asserted. “Three levels of calibrated review of ballot-access burdens,” present operation effective *Belitskus*, June 10, 2004, retains *Cartwright*'s February 25, 2003 substantial-burden requirement of an important objective and demonstrated substantial relationship, within *Burdick*'s calibrated scale. These titles and complete operative qualifications are copied in the slice.

*Clingman*, May 23, 2005, adds unanimous recognition that reciprocal official political association is real despite another retained registration, with a five-Justice judgment requiring justification of the actual disaffiliation burden. Its prospective-remedy holding requires correction of the common policy for persons governed by it unless the State establishes a narrower fully effective cure, preserving independent eligibility and administrative rules. That remedy is relevant to the argument over a plaintiff-only enrollment decree; it does not make the preliminary evidentiary showing final or dispense with the support condition.

**Supported competing paths.** Affirmation may rest on a severe burden inadequately justified or a substantial burden whose actual reach lacks the required fit. The supported hearing findings concern identification, communication, organizing and fundraising, beyond primary administration. The strongest contrary argument is that the State created enrollment to operate closed primaries and should not be forced to supply every organization a subsidized organizing service. That objection requires confronting the system's actual ongoing nonprimary use and selective erasure; it cannot end with the unchallenged validity of the 50,000-vote party-status threshold. The State's confusion concern is legitimate and preventive; explanatory form wording is an identified less burdensome response. Administrative burden needs the concrete population and demonstrated statewide support, not a premise that the order maintains every invented party.

Reversal or modification remains a supported legal path if the actual preliminary proof fails the required likelihood/irreparability showing or particular affirmative directions exceed the injury. Its strongest challenge is that deleting existing data and refusing new affiliation entries continue the same organizing loss; preserving old copies alone is not necessarily effective relief. Any decision should keep the preliminary status, factual showing, applicable standard and precise implementation scope distinct. Completed elections need not be reopened, and the full statutory Party threshold remains outside the challenge.

## Mechanical source extract: Orff statutory text

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/43/390uu

Consent is given to join the United States as a necessary party defendant in any suit to adjudicate, confirm, validate, or decree the contractual rights of a contracting entity and the United States regarding any contract executed pursuant to Federal reclamation law. The United States, when a party to any suit, shall be deemed to have waived any right to plead that it is not amenable thereto by reason of its sovereignty, and shall be subject to judgments, orders, and decrees of the court having jurisdiction, and may obtain review thereof, in the same manner and to the same extent as a private individual under like circumstances. Any suit pursuant to this section may be brought in any United States district court in the State in which the land involved is situated.

(Pub. L. 97–293, title II, § 221, Oct. 12, 1982, 96 Stat. 1271.)
