## Reconciliation result and correction of provisional shorthand

The five complete-reversal supporters and two complete-affirmance supporters retain their modeled dispositions. Thomas's provisional full-reversal/remand wording, its assignment of S3 without a structural qualification, and its apparent assent to the shared treaty/safety statutory construction cannot stand as written. His complete historical opinion explicitly remands only claims not pertaining to ship structure and rejects the majority's conflict inquiry and discretionary sequencing. Current Martin supplies no changed premise for a broader Thomas structural-duty join: his passenger-recipient distinction answers service coverage, not ship design. His current Clark dissent confirms his rejection of extending a narrowing construction mechanically across settings. No altered statute, maritime record, or earlier public holding answers his concrete structural objection. Accordingly, history warrants a narrower corrected commitment, rather than an unsupported departure.

For Stevens, Kennedy and Souter, the frozen substantial-permanent-structure limit is preserved at the actual protected category: a proposed permanent and significant alteration of basic design/construction interfering with internal affairs. Their historical language does not adjudicate every physical barrier request in advance. They permit the lower court to apply Title III's own qualifications first, or the maritime limit first where appropriate; the individual claim and alteration remain to be identified. Thomas's categorical structural position is stronger and does not supply a join in that sequencing or in treating all structural claims as still open for statutory balancing.

For Ginsburg and Breyer, the frozen refusal to treat permanence alone as decisive is confirmed and sharpened. In this passenger case they allow no additional internal-affairs immunity once conflict with international legal obligations is avoided. An abstract management interest is insufficient. Their conditional agreement with an application-specific method is not assent to the three's independent internal-affairs exclusion. No changed premise justifies expanding their join.

These are corrections and scope clarifications to provisional commitments, not departures from strong historical evidence. No new neutral fact changes the modeled record.

## Record, qualifications and remedy

The district court dismissed barrier-removal claims for absent ship-specific regulations, while allowing other claims. The Fifth Circuit substituted categorical foreign-flag noncoverage and did not decide that regulations ground. The five complete-remand supporters return that actual ground for appellate consideration; Thomas would not reopen structural claims because his independent maritime ground already disposes of them. No-guidelines is neither an approved exemption nor a decided invalid defense.

Public-accommodation and specified-public-transportation coverage both matter. Eligibility criteria may screen out disability only when necessary to the offered service. Reasonable policy modification requires necessity and reasonableness, subject to the operator's fundamental-alteration defense. Auxiliary aids have their distinct fundamental-alteration and undue-burden qualifications. Barrier removal and alternative access each retain their readily-achievable conditions. The direct-threat exception for an individual posing a significant unremovable risk is distinct from the five's conclusion that a modification itself creating a direct health/safety threat is not readily achievable. A proposed guideline is not a final regulation. Allegations are not liability findings; each requested duty still requires its own predicates.

The five's actual international-obligation limit does not demand a survey proving consistency with every hypothetical foreign rule. Nor does the three's permission to begin with a maritime issue become a command to do so in every case. No structural modification is presently ordered. These are material limits of the disputed coverage judgment and remaining claims, not a public inventory of things the Court never considered.
