## Back v. Hastings on Hudson Union Free School District

### Components and provisional common result

All eight Associates would affirm the Second Circuit's restoration of the claims against Brennan and Wishnie on the two presented questions. All support both (1) the absence of a categorical male-comparator prerequisite for the supported intentional-sex-stereotyping theory and (2) rejection of personal qualified immunity on the supported plaintiff-favorable account. This is permission for ordinary trial and remedial adjudication, not a finding of discrimination or an award of tenure or damages. The district's and Russell's affirmed judgments remain outside the reviewed components.

The substantive rule is confined to adverse official action because of sex. Evidence that decisionmakers treated a mother's sex-linked family role as proof that she would lack professional commitment may establish that motive without identifying a differently treated father. The plaintiff must still prove each defendant's responsibility, discriminatory causation and any remaining liability elements; neither motherhood nor an unfavorable employment decision creates a presumption of liability. The indirect framework shifts production after a prima facie showing, not ultimate persuasion. The plaintiff retains persuasion by a preponderance, and disbelief of an explanation permits rather than compels an inference of discrimination.

The notice rule remains Harlow–Anderson as explained by Saucier, Hope and Brosseau. On the assumed facts, Virginia's preconduct constitutional prohibition on adverse sex-role generalizations and the already established employment-stereotyping principle make the conduct plainly unlawful without a school-tenure factual twin. Hibbs supports the current merits understanding but is not a source of 2000–2001 notice. The holding does not abolish qualified immunity or make all disputed facts defeat a separately decidable legal defense.

### Shared alternative-path comparison

**Affirmance path.** Specific maternal-role statements attributed to the recommending officials, considered with timing and disputed performance explanations, permit a reasonable inference of intentional sex discrimination. Virginia forbids treating sex as a conclusive proxy for an individual's capacities. Reeves preserves reasonable whole-record inferences and the plaintiff's ultimate burden. Price Waterhouse supplies the preconduct employment-stereotyping principle; it does not automatically transfer every Title VII remedial or burden rule into section 1983. The strongest objection is that parenting concerns are not necessarily sex discrimination and the employee may genuinely have performed poorly. That objection defeats automatic liability but does not entitle defendants to credibility choices or a mandatory comparator when the supported statements expressly tie the adverse recommendation to assumptions about mothers.

**Reversal or immunity path.** A sex-neutral attendance or performance decision, without evidence of sex-based treatment, would not become unconstitutional because its subject is a parent. Harlow and Anderson also protect a reasonable official where the actual constitutional application was insufficiently clear. The strongest objection to extending that path here is the assumed explicit sex-role cause. No newly recognized right to caregiver accommodation is needed to condemn a recommendation actually based on that cause. The clarity is supplied by the established discrimination boundary, not by the later enactment-specific Hibbs holding. If trial establishes a materially different account, ordinary merits, causation and any properly preserved defense consequences must be determined on those actual findings.

## Ballard v. Commissioner; Estate of Kanter

### Components and provisional result

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse both appellate judgments insofar as they uphold concealment of the original submitted special-trial-judge report, and remand for disclosure, inclusion in the reviewable record, and fresh consideration under Rule 183. Scalia and Thomas would affirm both judgments against the report-withholding challenge. No Justice treats the undisclosed report as proved favorable to the taxpayers or decides the ultimate deficiency and fraud findings in this review. The two consolidated components receive the same procedural treatment within each Justice's commitment.

For the six supporting reversal, Rule 183 requires a genuine two-stage process. The special trial judge submits findings and opinion; the deciding regular judge may adopt, modify or reject them but must give the prescribed regard to the hearing judge's credibility opportunity and the specified presumption of correctness. The submitted report must be preserved and disclosed so that parties and the reviewing court can test that legally required treatment. This requirement concerns the submitted Rule 183 report, not every draft, discussion, note or internal communication. It leaves final decisional authority with the regular judge and permits supported rejection of proposed findings. The rule does not make the report evidence of its own correctness or require adoption merely because the special judge heard the witnesses.

### Shared alternative-path comparison

**Disclosure/remand path.** Freytag's unanimous statutory discussion distinguishes a hearing officer's submitted findings from the regular judge's final decision. Rule 183 assigns specific legal weight to the former. Neither final decisional authority nor permissible consultation can erase the object of the required review. The strongest contrary argument is the 1983 deletion of automatic party-service and exception provisions: an omitted requirement ordinarily should not be reinserted. The answer is limited. The Court need not restore the old automatic-service timetable or exceptions procedure; the surviving report and deference commands, combined with actual appellate review under section 7482, require access to the submitted report used in the disputed adjudication. Disclosure allows review of the existing commands rather than restoring every deleted mechanism. Targeted confidentiality protection remains available.

**Withholding/affirmance path.** Sections 7443A and 7482 leave the regular Tax Court judge with final decisional responsibility, and Rule 183 no longer expressly orders service of the special judge's report. An internal proposed decision may be discussed and revised without giving litigants every prior judicial view. The strongest contrary argument is that presumptive correctness and special credibility regard become unreviewable if the original report is hidden. This path answers that appellate review addresses the final decision and the regular judge's obligation to follow the rule, and that a presumption of regular judicial performance is not displaced by unproved allegations about the initial report's contents. The disagreement is whether those safeguards are adequate under the retained rule, not whether a special trial judge may secretly become the final statutory decisionmaker.

Neither path requires a broad constitutional ruling granting or rejecting access to every internal judicial document. For the six supporting reversal, the rule ground fully supplies the remedy. For Scalia and Thomas, ordinary due process permits the final legally authorized judge to decide after full evidentiary proceedings; a provisional judicial report is not additional secret evidence or a new adversarial factual submission merely because it contains proposed credibility findings. This conclusion is confined to the established process and does not approve an actually proved denial of the required hearing, unauthorized adjudicator or substitution of undisclosed evidence.

## Wilkinson v. Dotson

### Components and provisional common result

All eight Associates would affirm the en banc judgment allowing both complaints to proceed under section 1983. The same vote covers Dotson's prospective challenge to retroactive guidelines and Johnson's prospective guidelines, panel-composition and speaking-opportunity claims at the vehicle stage. The claims seek new lawful parole consideration, retain official discretion to deny release, and neither invalidate the convictions and sentences nor command immediate or earlier release. Cognizability gives neither plaintiff a substantive constitutional victory or an immediate entitlement to a hearing order.

The operative rule is actual necessary legal consequence. An opportunity-only challenge may proceed under section 1983 when a successful judgment leaves the existing sentence and a lawful decision to continue confinement intact. A prisoner's ultimate desire for release, a possible favorable discretionary decision after new proceedings, or the fact that parole affects actual time served does not establish habeas exclusivity. The form of the request is not conclusive: a judgment actually invalidating a custody-producing legal determination or necessarily requiring release or reduced confinement needs the distinct channel analysis. No automatic conversion into habeas follows from a classification adverse to a plaintiff.

### Shared alternative-path comparison

**Civil-action path.** Cavanaugh already permits prospective restoration of discretionary parole consideration where the judgment awards an opportunity rather than release. Heck's independent-injury holding preserves claims compatible with the conviction and sentence; Edwards distinguishes genuinely independent process relief from invalidation of earned-credit loss; Muhammad and Nelson insist on the actual legal consequence and retain independent conditions. The strongest contrary argument is that invalidating a parole denial challenges the operative reason custody continues, and that future lawful denial cannot cure that past adjudication. The distinction is legal, not merely probabilistic. Lost earned credits alter a custody measure fixed by law; these prospective parole claims leave an independently valid sentence and the parole authority's discretion to require continued service. Their success establishes no right to a shorter term.

**Habeas-only path.** The parole procedure controls eligibility for earlier release, and state exhaustion could be evaded if prisoners can reframe every denial as faulty process. Edwards warns that the possibility of a later identical result does not save a challenge that invalidates a custody-producing decision. The strongest objection is that this account makes any meaningful opportunity claim a custody attack and conflicts with Cavanaugh's actual holding and later claim-specific doctrine. Exhaustion policy cannot supply a new general restriction after the actual civil claim has been classified as compatible. Administrative exhaustion under applicable prison-litigation law remains separate. The alternative remains available for materially different relief that actually establishes a shorter lawful custody period; the present claims do not do so.

## Governing distinctions and supported paths

**Statutory question.** Taylor, especially Part IV, makes the earlier conviction's necessary adjudicated content decisive. Generic burglary requires unlawful or unprivileged entry into, or remaining in, a building or structure with intent to commit a crime. A broader statute is not enough. Taylor's specified trial documents identify the necessary basis of the conviction; they do not license a fresh conduct trial. Its plea-bargain discussion independently warns that evidence of underlying conduct cannot enlarge what a plea established. The present extension concerns analogous plea records.

**Path A ? conviction-record limit.** Determine the necessary generic basis from the charging document together with the written plea agreement, plea colloquy, an explicit factual finding to which the defendant assented, or a comparable judicial record establishing the actual admission. A police report or complaint application, merely placed in the historical file and not made part of the accepted plea, cannot establish the building element. The objection is its unadopted status, not that every police description is untrustworthy. An expressly incorporated account must be assessed as the actual judicial admission; the present handoff establishes no such incorporation.

The strongest contrary argument is that state plea practices may leave their shared understanding in documents other than a transcript and that excluding reliable contemporaneous material makes equivalent convictions turn on recordkeeping. The answer is that Congress chose convictions, and the decisive object is what was necessarily admitted rather than what the judge now believes happened. The same difficulty affects trial evidence; Taylor accepted that cost to avoid reconstructing conduct and overriding plea bargains.

**Path B ? reliable evidence identifying the plea's agreed basis.** Taylor's named trial documents can be read as illustrations of a confined inquiry rather than an exhaustive documentary taxonomy. On that reading, reliable contemporaneous reports and complaint applications may help establish what the parties understood the plea to admit, provided the inquiry identifies the conviction's basis rather than independently retries criminal conduct. The strongest objection is that a reliable account of conduct does not establish a defendant's adoption, particularly where the broader charge made the building fact unnecessary and adoption is disputed. This path accordingly requires an actual finding about the scope of the plea; the allegations cannot establish agreement merely by being uncontradicted at a state proceeding or by appearing plausible now.

**Independent constitutional question.** Gatewood requires applicable federal charging and proper factfinding beyond a reasonable doubt, or valid admission and lawful waiver, for necessary adult-recidivist existence, identity, historical qualification and chronology that raise a binding maximum or minimum. Legal classification of established facts remains judicial. A permissible Taylor source does not itself satisfy the new federal charge or substitute a new punishment-authorizing factual determination. Booker reaffirmed that rule on January 12 and retained binding Guidelines in their lawful applications. Its holding excludes an advisory-system remand here. Taylor's older statement about alternative Guidelines consideration now operates only within the constitutionally authorized range under Angle and Booker.

**Disposition paths.** The five Path-A commitments below support reversal of the First Circuit's statutory rule and its enhancement direction, with a bounded sentencing remand. The three Path-B commitments reject that categorical statutory rule but support vacatur of the mandatory enhancement direction and remand under the independently controlling Gatewood/Booker requirements. Their recent constitutional dissents are maintained as objections to the rule's correctness; they do not assume a nonexistent recidivism exception, and this commitment does not request renewed overruling in a case whose stated question concerns conviction materials. No Justice's disagreement with a precedent mechanically decides whether that Justice must repeat an overruling demand in every subsequent application.

## Common scope determination and operative effect

The two questions presented ask whether the specified questioning and handcuff detention violated the Fourth Amendment. The officers seek a further holding that, even if their conduct violated that Amendment, the February 1998 law did not clearly prohibit it. That independent conduct-date defense is not fairly included in these constitutional questions as framed. The Court should decide the presented constitutional issues and their judgment consequences without independently granting or denying qualified immunity.

This is a case-specific application of the question limit, not a holding that immunity must always appear by name or can never be fairly included in a broader section 1983 question. The officers' actual argument is substantial: they identify the same conduct, overlapping precedents, treatment below, presentation in the petition, and merits briefing; they invoke Kolstad and Procunier. Those features address notice and practical readiness, but do not alone make the separate legal defense necessary to answer these particular constitutional questions. In particular, referring to earlier precedents to ask whether conduct was constitutional does not also ask whether an unconstitutional act was insufficiently clearly forbidden at a different legal cutoff. Mena's alternative answer to the defense is not consent to enlargement.

The original merits commitments already provide complete answers at their supported level. All eight reject a violation based solely on the topic of questions absent added restraint or protected intrusion. Four resolve the identified search-period force issue for the officers; four require proper appellate application of the force rule. All preserve the independently presented duration ground and the actual verdict's remedial effect. These dispositions do not require the Court to decide the separate personal defense. This practical point reinforces the question-specific scope conclusion; it is not a free-standing power to leave a properly reached question incomplete.

Accordingly:

- None of the eight supplies a present independent qualified-immunity holding or a separate judgment vote granting that defense.
- O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas retain their original substantive view that the identified search-period conduct would receive immunity if the issue were properly reached. That view is now expressly conditional and cannot be counted as an alternative holding of this decision.
- Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain their original conduct-specific approach. They do not obtain a present immunity denial from their refusal to decide the force issue categorically.
- On remand, any properly preserved independent immunity defense remains available under the existing February 1998 notice rule, on the conduct and factual account actually at issue and subject to ordinary preservation, finality, and mandate principles. This does not guarantee a new opportunity to litigate a forfeited or conclusively resolved matter.
- The existing distinction between current constitutional rules and conduct-date notice remains law. Explaining the standard governing a genuinely surviving defense on remand is not an adjudication of that defense in the officers' favor.

## City of Sherrill — supported paths

**Commitment:** Each Associate supports affirming the reviewed Sherrill tax-protection judgments at their bounded scope. This follows independent evaluation of the real City paths below, not an assumption that equity is unavailable or that prior law already decided this dispute.

**Rule supported by each Associate.** The City may not impose these ad valorem taxes or enforce assessments against these Nation-owned parcels within the retained Oneida reservation when no completed federal disposition or other congressional authorization has removed their tax protection. The particular federal treaties, retained reservation relationship and present Nation ownership supply the protection; mere alienability, unauthorized intervening transfers, local governmental reliance and later ordinary reacquisition do not supply the missing federal tax authorization. This rule does not make tribal purchase of any parcel sufficient, does not convert section 233's preservation clause into an independent universal exemption, and does not decide immunity from a collection action resting on an otherwise valid tax. The City's asserted equity grounds do not justify complete denial of prospective protection on this record for the reasons independently examined below.

**Federal reservation status.** The City's extension of Venetie's federal set-aside/superintendence inquiry from section 1151(b) to all Indian-country categories is a serious legal proposal, but the unanimous entering holding preserves reservation and allotment categories. Even a functional federal-relationship inquiry does not favor the City here: Canandaigua Article 2 recognizes the particular retained reservation and protects use and enjoyment, Article 4 confirms that arrangement, and Articles 6–7 supply federal supervision and protection. State origin in 1788 therefore does not establish absence of a later federal relationship. Section 1151(a) cannot be reduced to the continuing status of each fee patent. Recognition alone would not suffice; the particular protected land relationship does.

**Treaty alteration.** Hagen and Yankton require clear federal intent from the whole operative instrument and context, without mandatory cession words or a fixed purchase price. The City's removal-policy, western-home and compensation argument must be read against Buffalo Creek's actual allocation. Article 1 cedes the specified Wisconsin interests, with its own geographic exception; Article 2 provides western land in consideration of that cession. Article 3 forfeits the new western interest if the required acceptance/removal does not occur within its period, as extended by the President; it does not forfeit the New York reservation. Article 13 makes Oneida removal dependent on satisfactory arrangements with New York for purchase of the Oneida lands. The Seneca and Tuscarora provisions and attached conveyances expressly complete different transactions. Their terms cannot be assigned to the Oneidas. The instrument establishes neither satisfaction of Article 13's condition for these parcels nor a completed federal Oneida cession. This is a whole-instrument conclusion, not a magic-words demand. Competing characterizations of later compensation in the briefs do not supply a new adjudicated New York surrender.

**Recognition and succession.** Current recognition and succession to the treaty party are supplied, while the City's historical-lapse argument rests on contested accounts. Movement of some members, local government and population change do not themselves establish federal termination of the Nation or retained parcel protection. The court below rejected the discovery showing. These commitments do not substitute an unprovided status document or find every historical assertion false; they conclude that the asserted lapse supplies no legally established extinction ground on this record.

**Tax source.** Yakima and Cass County require the identified federal disposal authority and parcel chain, preserving independent protections. Their holdings refuse to make alienability or governmental impact alone sufficient. The present parcels lack the completed federal disposal decisive there. Unauthorized state-era transfers do not acquire that statutory consequence through time or ordinary repurchase. The relevant combination is current Nation ownership of these retained reservation parcels and no established intervening federal removal of their protection, not tribal purchase anywhere.

Section 233's civil-jurisdiction grant supplies no tax authorization; its tax-preservation clause is not itself a universal exemption. Its tribal-member use/possession exception qualifies execution, not taxation or alienation. Former section 465 supplies discretionary trust acquisition and the attached exemption, without making it the exclusive source of existing reservation protection. Its Navajo funding limitation concerns the acquisition appropriation, not an exception to Oneida tax protection.

**Independent City equity path.** As an analytical alternative under Engine section 14—not an assertion that every formulation was actually filed here—a Justice could invoke federal equitable repose and territorial acquiescence to prevent abrupt judicial restoration of dormant sovereignty after generations of contrary governance. Stevens's Oneida II dissent develops the analogous argument: extraordinary delay, innocent reliance, changes in land character and destabilization can limit ancient common-law claims. Its Felix and Wetzel examples concern disruptive recovery of developed property. The Oneida II majority expressly reserves further remedial equity, although its Ewert discussion warns against validating restricted transfers through laches. The City actually identified equitable defenses below and invokes governmental and fiscal reliance. Their absence or waiver is not assumed.

The strongest response, adopted here, concerns the actual right and remedy. The Nation challenges new assessments on land it now owns by voluntary purchase. ## Jackson — supported paths

**Commitment:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse the categorical dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings on the recipient's alleged retaliation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm the appealed statutory dismissal. Jackson's personal coaching/pay injury is distinct from the girls' injury under every position.

**Exact statutory rule supported by the reversal group.** A recipient of federal financial assistance violates section 1681 when, in its covered education program or activity, it intentionally takes adverse action against a person because that person complained of sex discrimination. A person personally injured by that retaliation may invoke Cannon's established Title IX action even when the reported discrimination targeted others. Liability still requires proof of legally attributable recipient retaliation and causal injury; Franklin permits appropriate compensation for the proved intentional statutory wrong. This resolves the pleaded removal from coaching and resulting pay loss without creating a general grievance action or importing every term of the retaliation regulation into statutory damages law. The three affirmance supporters reject the first sentence's statutory construction, not the existence of Jackson's alleged personal injury or Cannon's established action for sex discrimination.

**Reversal path.** Section 1681 protects any person against discrimination on the basis of sex in covered education. Deliberately punishing a person because he complains of sex discrimination is unequal treatment causally linked to the sex-equality demand. The reporter's own sex need not be the trait used by the recipient. Sullivan supplies a pre-enactment analogy protecting an advocate personally injured for asserting another's equality; Cannon supplies the established action and Franklin compensation once intentional statutory discrimination is proved. Title VII's detailed separate enumeration does not control Title IX's broader formulation.
