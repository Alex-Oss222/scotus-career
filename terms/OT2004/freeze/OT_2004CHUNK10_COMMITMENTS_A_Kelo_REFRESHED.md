## Independently tested alternatives

**K1 — Apply existing public-purpose law with meaningful review.** A comprehensive economic redevelopment plan may satisfy public use despite private implementation and inclusion of nonblighted homes when the undertaking has an actual, rationally supported public purpose. *Berman* permits area-based planning and private implementation; *Midkiff* makes the public end rather than title mechanics decisive; the controlling *Boston & Maine* opinion adopts that broader rationale while forbidding naked private favoritism. Courts preserve the ordinary presumption of lawful public action but examine a supported claim that private advantage is the undertaking's actual object. Merely reciting jobs or revenue does not answer contrary supported evidence. Those principles sustain this adopted plan on the supplied record without a judicial guarantee of economic success.

The strongest opposing argument is that every owner could be displaced by a more profitable one. K1 answers it with an actual public undertaking and meaningful scrutiny of alleged pretext, rather than deeming every projected tax increase sufficient. The supplied account reports a comprehensive plan addressing prolonged economic distress and no finding of a sham solely benefiting an identified recipient; it does not prove that every possible pretext fact was absent. Neither an unsupported accusation nor that limited record description establishes grounds to overturn the judgment. *Cleburne* and *Moreno* confirm by analogy that rationality review need not accept a legally impermissible end or an unsupported explanation. They do not compel heightened scrutiny, shift an ultimate burden, or create a mandatory list of planning features.

**K2 — Limit private economic-successor acquisitions while preserving the prior harmful-condition cases.** Condemnation of ordinary property for new private owners cannot rest solely on anticipated economic benefits of their ordinary private uses. Public ownership and actual public service remain valid categories, as do *Berman*'s removal of a harmful area condition and *Midkiff*'s destruction of a harmful ownership structure. Those conditions must be distinguished from merely preferring more economically productive successors. On this approach, reverse and remand to prevent the challenged condemnations on the supplied economic-redevelopment rationale.

The strongest objection is the broad public-purpose reasoning K2's proponents previously accepted. The proposed restriction therefore confines that reasoning expressly; it cannot be described as merely applying an existing categorical exclusion. The prior acquisitions remain compensated condemnations. They are not transformed into uncompensated nuisance regulation, and a power to suppress a harmful activity is not treated as itself deciding acquisition authority or compensation. The present distinction concerns what the acquisition accomplishes: removal of an established harmful property or ownership condition versus predicted benefits of successor activity. The City's sincere plan and economic distress do not remove that legal distinction on K2.

**K3 — Require governmental use or an enforceable public entitlement to use.** The Fifth Amendment's public-use condition would require government ownership or actual governmental use, or a legally enforceable public entitlement to use the property, even if private title remains. Actual passenger service and common-carrier facilities can satisfy that rule; incidental employment or revenue from ordinary private premises cannot. This is a proposed change from existing public-purpose law, supported as a textual and structural interpretation rather than as an established historical conclusion. *Cole*'s public-function/private-enterprise distinction and *Kohl*'s governmental-function acquisitions illustrate the proposed categories without proving their exhaustiveness.

The strongest counterargument is stare decisis and the public-purpose cases' express rejection of a direct-government-use requirement. Adoption of K3 must therefore displace the broad contrary propositions of *Berman* and *Midkiff*, including their permission for private transfers justified solely by the asserted public condition rather than an enforceable public use. It must also replace *Boston & Maine*'s broad rational-public-purpose rationale while preserving its concrete passenger-service outcome to the extent supported by the secured continuing passenger use. The necessary premise is legal: the text's distinct use limitation should not be reduced to ordinary legislative pursuit of prosperity. No empirical account of founding-era practice or universal historical tradition is inferred from the supplied examples.

**Review and remand alternatives.** None of these choices authorizes an invented suspicious recipient, finding of sham or parcel-specific fact. A remand for pretext adjudication would require a supported material omission below; this packet identifies none. The possibility that a genuinely suspicious class of transfers could require a stronger review standard is outside the facts needed to decide this comprehensive-plan case. No trigger, burden or heightened rule is adopted here; ordinary meaningful anti-pretext review suffices for K1, while K2 and K3 reject the use on substantive grounds without deciding bad faith.
