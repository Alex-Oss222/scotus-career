## Rhines and Johnson

Rhines permits bounded retention of a timely pending mixed section 2254 petition. Its initial good-cause condition means a reasonable explanation in the actual circumstances, with potential merit, absence of abuse or intentional delay, and reasonable definite exhaustion-and-return deadlines. It creates neither statutory tolling for federal pendency nor a new filing period or an automatic reopening power. Its protection against destruction of an already timely action does not construe section 2255 paragraph 6(4)'s discovery-of-supporting-facts trigger.

Johnson therefore retains the frozen distinction between the state vacatur as a supporting fact, which all eight Associates accept, and whether the statute's express diligence condition also governs pursuit of that order. Rhines neither supplies a good-cause exception to the section 2255 limitation nor makes intentional delay necessary to every statutory diligence failure. Conversely, Johnson's procurement path does not convert Rhines's reasonable-explanation condition into extraordinary circumstances or a procedural-default test.

The newly published individual positions are compatible with the frozen rows:

- Souter also retains that Johnson position. His Johnson conclusion construes an express statutory diligence condition governing a later commencement event. Rhines's anti-abuse and deadline limits, which he accepts, preserve rather than eliminate meaningful timing requirements.
- Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg retain Johnson's discovery-of-the-existing-order rule and reversal/remand. Their acceptance of reasonable cause and deadlines for retaining a pending mixed petition does not turn the words concerning discovery into a duty to create a vacatur earlier. Ginsburg's additional support for Souter's broader retention position supplies no contrary limitation construction.
