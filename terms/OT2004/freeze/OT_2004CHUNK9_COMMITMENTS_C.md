## 86. San Remo Hotel, L.P. v. City and County of San Francisco

### Question, rule and remedy

Each Associate would **affirm** rejection of the requested categorical exception to §1738. The rendering State's otherwise applicable issue-preclusion rules govern issues actually and necessarily resolved with a full and fair opportunity, notwithstanding the need to pursue state compensation or an attempted reservation of a federal cause. The statutory command and the unanimous Dodd v. Hood River County rule require that conclusion. The grant does not independently reopen the California preclusion determinations.

The Court should explain that preserving a federal cause under England differs from preserving a right to relitigate every common issue. The owners' federal-forum concern does not itself create an exception to the recognition statute. State courts may decide federal issues; the Constitution does not require that every constitutional claimant receive a second merits adjudication in a federal district court. The rule remains issue-specific, as Dodd requires, rather than a proposition that all state compensation litigation necessarily extinguishes all federal claims.

Lingle is effective and must be applied as current law. Its removal of the independent substantial-advancement predicate does not create an exception to §1738 or supply an independently preserved, distinct issue within this limited grant. Exxon Mobil confirms that preclusion and original jurisdiction are separate. The judgment sustaining the preclusion defense stands on the granted legal question.

### Competing paths and join test

**Path A: apply §1738.** Its strongest objection is that compelled state compensation litigation appears to promise a later federal forum and then destroys it through issue preclusion. Dodd's unanimously adopted distinction answers the decisive legal point: reserving the cause does not reserve a right to contradict an issue actually resolved under applicable state rules. England addresses the abstention reservation context rather than a general privilege to displace state judgment effect. All eight Associates accept that distinction in their recorded law, and Exxon Mobil recently restated the statute's independent operation.

**Path B: recognize a takings exception to preserve a federal forum.** Its strongest support is the combined effect of the state-compensation requirement and subsequent preclusion. Its fatal objection in this grant is the absence of a statutory or controlling-doctrinal exception and the conflict with Dodd. None of the eight Associates has a supplied published position supporting a categorical exception despite that holding. A disagreement with the underlying state-compensation route could support reconsideration in a proper case, but does not supply a disposition on this granted question.

**Join barriers and scope:** Every Associate accepts the common rule and affirmance. None is committed to a blanket equation of state compensation litigation and total federal claim extinction. That limit belongs in the operative statement because it answers the actual requested categorical exception and preserves Dodd; it does not require eight separate reservations. No additional separate-writing function is established. No Williamson County reconsideration vote is inferred from general concern about federal forums.

## 88. Rompilla v. Beard

### Components and alternative paths

The separable legal components are (A) the §2254(d)(1) performance gate, (B) actual constitutionally deficient investigation, (C) independent cumulative prejudice because Pennsylvania did not adjudicate that element, and (D) the resulting conditional sentence remedy. A conclusion that the gate is not crossed is sufficient for denial and supplies neither a no-deficiency nor a no-prejudice holding.

**Relief path:** Strickland required an informed decision about investigation. The accessible record of a prior conviction the prosecutor announced would be used in aggravation was a specific, obvious preparation source. Interviews and expert examinations directed to other sources did not reasonably answer the need to learn that file's contents. On the supplied record, approving the unexplained failure to inspect that concrete source is objectively unreasonable, and the omission itself is deficient. The file's leads would have produced a materially different mitigation account: serious childhood mistreatment, substance abuse and evidence bearing on mental impairment, considered cumulatively with the aggravation and the limited original presentation. The developed record creates a reasonable probability of a different lawful sentencing decision. An adequate penalty proceeding or lawful noncapital sentence is required within a reasonable definite period; the conviction survives.

**Denial path:** Strickland is contextual rather than a constitutional inventory of required documents. Counsel did investigate through the client, relatives and mental-health professionals, received limited useful information and faced a prior file associated with serious aggravation. A state court could reasonably regard the total effort as adequate without requiring examination of every prior-case file. The §2254(d)(1) range therefore protects Pennsylvania's adjudication even if another lawyer or court would have investigated more. The missing file's later yield cannot by itself establish an unreasonable stopping point at the time.

The principal objection to relief is that it transforms hindsight about the file into a checklist and discounts the interviews and evaluations. The relief rule answers by tying the duty to the specific known prosecution source, its ready availability and its independent relevance to defending aggravation; no general obligation to obtain all records is adopted. The principal objection to denial is that the file's known use and accessibility made it an evident gap even before its mitigating contents were known. The denying Justices regard that distinction as insufficient to remove this state judgment from Strickland's reasonable range given counsel's other actual efforts. This is the supported disagreement; neither side invents a finding of complete investigation or an automatic excuse from the client's unhelpfulness.

## 90. American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission

### Common disposition and controlling-rule candidates

All eight Associates would **affirm** the judgment sustaining §478.2(1)'s $100 annual per-truck fee on intrastate commercial hauling. The covered event is engagement in genuine Michigan local hauling, equally regulated for carriers that do and do not also engage in interstate carriage. The current record establishes neither discrimination against interstate commerce nor a clearly excessive interstate burden. A per-local-mile quotient by itself does not prove discrimination where the charge addresses the regulated local activity rather than highway mileage.

The six Associates accepting the governing framework—Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—would join the actual local-event analysis, nondiscrimination conclusion, and Pike application. Complete Auto and Jefferson Lines require attention to what is charged and its actual operation; NEMA forecloses a categorical exemption from Pike merely because the fee is evenhanded. General regulatory and governmental services can support the charge without exact dollar or mileage equivalence. Identical charges by every State would reach each State's distinct local-hauling activity, not repeated interstate passage through the States.

Scalia and Thomas accept the same disposition and the absence of established discrimination. Their prior Jefferson Lines and NEMA positions support a narrower rationale: no discrimination is shown and the local-hauling charge is distinguishable from the flat highway-use charge invalidated in Scheiner; even assuming Pike, the asserted showing fails. They withhold assent from an affirmative freestanding balancing rationale and the complete Complete Auto exposition.

### Adversarial paths and join barriers

**Sustain the fee.** The strongest challenge is Scheiner's recognition that a flat charge may burden interstate operators more heavily in practice, coupled with the hypothetical cumulative expense of identical fees in many States. The answer is the charge's actual trigger and record: Michigan assesses engagement in point-to-point local hauling, not access for interstate passage. A carrier undertaking local business in several States engages in distinct regulated activities. The record does not substantiate the claimed routing disruption or practical discriminatory burden; local regulation supplies a concrete benefit. Jefferson Lines requires that actual distinction rather than reliance on the statutory label alone.

**Invalidate or remand.** The carriers' stronger alternative treats the lower per-local-mile yield as a sufficient structural burden requiring mileage allocation or further proceedings. Its weakness is that no governing rule promises equal charge per mile for an annual local-business permission, and no supported factual dispute requires a new hearing. The supplied record permits resolution. O'Connor and Breyer's Jefferson Lines dissent does not support importing external interstate transportation into the present exclusively local charged activity.

**Framework split:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept ordinary Pike review without a disparate-impact threshold and conclude that the actual showing fails. Scalia and Thomas agree under an assumed-Pike alternative and reject an indistinguishable-precedent or discrimination ground; this is a materially different source of authority for the same judgment. An opinion presenting the complete balancing and Complete Auto framework must not count their votes as full joins. A separate explanation or precise limited join can convey that relationship without altering the majority rule.

**Scope:** The outcome concerns §478.2(1) and the supplied local-hauling record. The record's express distinction from the Mid-Con §478.2(2) fee prevents conflation with the separate preemption controversy; the same-date undecided matter supplies no entering law or blocker. The local label is not a categorical exemption, and facial neutrality does not eliminate actual-burden inquiry.
