## 104. Validated factual and procedural qualifications

1. **Both sides moved for summary judgment.** After discovery, the parties cross-moved. The district court limited its consideration to liability arising from distribution of the then-current versions of Grokster's and StreamCast's software. It left aside liability for damages arising from past software versions or other past activities. The judgment under review favored the distributors within that current-version scope. The existence of cross-motions does not establish agreement on every material fact. Each request requires its own legal and record analysis; rejection of the distributors' legal ground does not by itself award judgment to the owners or compel a trial on every issue.

2. **Underlying direct infringement was established below and uncontested on appeal.** The district court held that users who used the software to download the protected media files at issue directly infringed MGM's copyrights. The reported conclusion was not contested on appeal. The neutral packet's statement that direct infringement is a distinct element must not imply that this conclusion remains wholly unestablished. It does not establish that every file or transfer infringes, that each distributor is secondarily liable, or that every alleged injury was caused by each distributor's conduct. Identification and causation questions relevant to a particular liability or remedial demand remain distinct.

3. **Some promotional materials were drafts of unverified publication status.** StreamCast developed the specified promotional materials, but the supplied recitation does not establish whether it released them publicly. Describe those materials as developed promotional drafts with publication unverified, rather than as proven public advertisements or proven communications inducing particular users. The inference that internal materials can bear on purpose is a legal and evidentiary possibility to evaluate with other evidence; it is not a finding that the drafts were transmitted, persuaded anyone, or caused infringement. This qualification does not recategorize every separate item of marketing or user assistance as an unreleased draft.

4. **The residual ten percent was unknown.** The described survey classified 75 percent of available files as works owned or controlled by the plaintiffs and another 15 percent as likely copyrighted. It lacked enough information to classify the remaining ten percent, including what those files were and whether they infringed. Neither the unknown residual nor a bare copyright designation establishes authorization or infringement. The available-file survey also does not itself measure the proportions of actual downloads. The supplied record supports extensive infringement and the undisputed direct-infringement conclusion above, but it supplies no precise actual-download measure of lawful use. The amicus brief's broader statistical characterizations remain advocacy based on the source access it discloses, not a cure for these limitations.

5. **Sony's substantial-lawful-use issue remained disputed.** The courts below accepted substantial noninfringing capability; that judicial characterization is not a concession by MGM. The authorized extraction reports MGM's continuing contrary position in both the lower courts and the present briefing, with record and brief pinpoints. Lawful authorized and public-domain uses are described, but their legal sufficiency, actual and prospective significance, and the relevant unit of analysis remain contested. The packet's question about liability despite substantial lawful capabilities is a conditional legal question, not an admission that this record conclusively satisfies Sony. A neutral formulation is whether secondary liability may rest on purposeful promotion even if substantial lawful capability is shown, and whether the separate distribution theory survives under the applicable Sony rule.

6. **Judgment scope and evidentiary relevance are different questions.** Respondents contend that a conduct-based theory lies outside this review because the lower rulings concerned current software and excluded past acts. Their scope objection must be preserved and assessed. Evidence that predates the particular version is not, solely by that chronology, logically incapable of showing the purpose or character of its later distribution. That is an analytical possibility, not an adopted holding or permission to adjudicate excluded past damages. The opposing argument requires an actual connection to the current distribution and a lawful basis to consider the evidence; historical promotion cannot substitute automatically for proof of the relevant conduct, causal infringement, or properly reviewed claim. The present sources do not establish the contents and effect of every earlier act for each defendant.

## Legal paths preserved for independent modeling

The C entering-law slice supplies Napster's April 23, 2002 rules: Sony bars knowledge imputed from lawful product capability alone; knowing material assistance to identified infringement may independently support contributory liability; vicarious liability independently requires financial benefit plus the right and practical ability to supervise. Its operative Standards entries remain **Knowing material assistance and beneficial supervision of infringement** and **Function-specific copyright protections and injunction limits**. No aspect of this refresh changes those authorities, creates a monitoring obligation, or treats failure to qualify for one statutory protection as affirmative proof of another liability theory.

The United States' January 2005 amicus brief advances two distinct contributory theories. One proposes assessing commercially significant lawful use by the relationship of infringement to each defendant's particular business, including actual and reasonably foreseeable use, economic dependence, and subsidiary evidence about marketing, efficiency, and practical deterrent measures. The other proposes responsibility for active, intentional inducement independent of that distribution analysis. The concise packet captures the second proposal but must retain the first as a contested alternative. The government's proposed business-centered test is advocacy; it is not the rule already supplied by the C entering-law slice. Present infringement percentages are not the exclusive measure of lawful capability under that law. The amicus also rejects an independent obligation to redesign products or business relations to acquire supervisory control and proposes leaving the vicarious theory undecided if a sufficient contributory ground resolves the appeal.

For a conduct-based route, assess purposeful encouragement and resulting infringement separately from presumed knowledge based on product distribution. The owners' strongest scope response is that earlier conduct can evidence the purpose and continuing effects of current distribution without seeking judgment for the excluded past activity itself. The distributors' strongest response is that the court cannot use excluded acts, unpublished drafts, generalized awareness, or business revenue to bypass the required current conduct, causal connection, claim scope, and summary-judgment standard. A proposed inducement rule must answer both positions without assuming the result.

For a distribution-based route, assess the actual disputed Sony question and the government's alternative without treating lawful capability as conceded, the unknown residual as proven lawful, or high infringement as automatically sufficient. A disposition on an independently sufficient conduct theory may leave the minimum lawful-use threshold and the alternative distribution theory unresolved, but that nonresolution needs an explicit ground and defined scope. The mere existence of the inducement route does not erase the distribution claim from the packet.

For relief, distinguish the reviewed current-version judgment from excluded historical damages; the distributors' motion from the owners' cross-motion; direct infringement from secondary responsibility; each defendant's conduct from the other's; and liability from damages and injunctive predicates. The validated posture permits legal review and a properly bounded further-proceedings path. It does not establish a final damage amount, universal prohibition on the technology, technical feasibility of a particular filter, or any final claim-specific entitlement to judgment. A reviewing court must say which issues its legal ground resolves and which remain for adjudication.

### The United States' proposed distribution standard

January 2005 United States amicus brief, Summary of Argument. This is an advocacy position, not a governing rule or established finding.

```text
The touchstone for liability under Sony is whether the defendant is engaged in a business "substantially unrelated" to copyright infringement. The most salient considerations are the extent to which the defendant's product is, or reasonably foreseeably will be, utilized for infringement and, re-latedly, the extent to which the defendants' particular busi-ness depends on such illicit uses. If the defendant's product is overwhelmingly used for infringing purposes, and the viability of the defendant's business depends on the revenue and consumer interest generated by such infringement, such evidence alone suffices to support liability under Sony. Where the predominance of infringing uses is less stark, a court might also look to other indicia, including how the defendant marketed the product, whether the product would be efficient for the asserted noninfringing uses, and whether the defendant failed to take easily available steps to reduce the infringing uses or to focus on legitimate uses.
```
