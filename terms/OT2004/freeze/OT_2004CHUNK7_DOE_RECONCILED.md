## Common components and adversarial comparison

All eight Associates affirm the constitutional judgment and injunction against the Bible Education Ministry program as operated, the protective order, the affected families' standing determination and the challenged fee award.

**Constitutional rule and application.** Agostini's modified Lemon inquiry prohibits government adoption of doctrine as truth or solicitation of adherence through public authority. McCollum and Schempp supply the established instructional boundary. Here the Board supplies weekly classroom instruction within its elementary school day, while the actual lessons teach God's commands, divine creation, salvation and devotional conduct. Leaving composition and delivery to Bryan College volunteers does not change the function the school has adopted. Character education is a secular objective, but it cannot justify devotional means. The existence of some secular lessons does not eliminate the proved devotional program.

The strongest competing path would treat the arrangement as independently administered secular teaching or genuine private access protected by Agostini and Good News Club. Its decisive objection to affirmance is that neither a religious instructor's identity nor school location alone establishes governmental religion. That objection is accepted as a limit but does not describe this program: curricular adoption and actual devotional content establish the governmental function. Unlike Good News Club's independent adult leadership, parental authorization and after-school private access, these classes occupy the schools' own instructional opportunity. No material factual dispute identified in the supplied record requires trial on that operation. The rule does not exclude instructors by faith or prohibit objective academic study of religion.

**Relief.** Sustain cessation of the demonstrated devotional program. The Board's requested opportunity to formulate a new curriculum does not entitle it to continuation of this program. Courts may explain the constitutional boundary needed to administer their decree; they need not preapprove an unsubmitted replacement. The exact decree would be needed for an independent ruling that it restrains additional lawful programs, but none of the frozen positions rests on such a finding or enlarges its reach.

**Standing.** The sealed sworn affidavits place the affected children in the classes. Their concrete classroom exposure is traceable to the challenged program and redressable by its cessation. Pseudonymity does not substitute for proof; the affidavits supply proof of injury. The families support the identical injunction, making an independent organizational-standing ruling unnecessary to that relief. The strongest contrary argument would require a real absence of exposure or inability to test it, neither of which the supplied record establishes.

**Protective order.** Apply Roe II's unanimous Rules 10(a)/26(c) rule: substantial intimate privacy may outweigh the public-identification interest when a tailored order preserves meaningful defense access. Young children, religious privacy and the reported threats support protection. Defense counsel's discovery was unrestricted; the restriction governed further disclosure. The Board's asserted inability to investigate does not describe the actual order. The result affirms this exercise of discretion, not automatic anonymity for every constitutional plaintiff.

**Fees.** The district court removed $9,500 for unnecessary travel and another five percent for duplication. The Board's specific remaining $4,600 overlap objection acknowledged that some duplication was likely already removed. That record does not establish clear error, use of an incorrect legal rule or an inadequate reduction amounting to abuse of discretion. Affirm the actual challenged award; prevailing-party status alone is not the rationale for sustaining its amount.
