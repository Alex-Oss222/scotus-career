## Common reached question and actual record

All eight Associates reach the merits of the Fourth Amendment question. A compulsory blood draw and the protected examination of the material are searches. Kincade's undisputed armed-bank-robbery conviction, actual supervised-release status, statutory collection duty, officer's demand and later forced profiling establish a concrete controversy. The actual March 2002 demand had no individualized evidentiary basis concerning another crime. The later April 2003 drug test and lifting of the custody stay cannot retroactively provide one.

The judicially recited refusal disposition was four months' imprisonment and two years' supervised release; after the stay was lifted, a sample was forcibly obtained. Each Justice's disposition therefore addresses that actual judgment, with any independently valid subsequent consequence distinguished on the record. Prior ex post facto and other district-court theories were not pursued on appeal and are outside the granted search question.

## Competing propositions, distinct grounds and remedy paths

**R — Apply Rise.** A qualifying conviction and continuing supervision, coupled with ordinary future-crime identification, limited medical extraction and use restrictions, do not independently authorize this suspicionless bodily acquisition. Rise already considered reduced offender privacy, regulated samples, identification utility, correctional conditions and the fingerprint analogy. Knights supplies authority where a lawful known condition and individualized suspicion work together; its reservation does not create authority to dispense with both a bodily-search exception and suspicion. Reject the offered collection ground, reverse the appellate affirmance and remand for removal of the refusal basis and effective relief on the actual record.

The strongest objection to R is that current supervised release creates an unusually direct governmental duty to identify and monitor a convicted offender, while a standard draw and a restricted identification profile impose a limited burden. R answers that these considerations have real weight but are the very sort of considerations Rise found insufficient to justify ordinary database acquisition; recognition of correctional authority does not eliminate a distinct bodily-search justification.

**A — Sustain the defined supervised-offender program.** A medically ordinary draw from this qualifying convicted person during supervision, for the regulated identification profile and authorized uses, is constitutionally reasonable in light of the established correctional relationship, objective selection, limited immediate intrusion and identification/deterrence interests. This is a proposed exception or limitation of Rise, not a holding already supplied by Knights or a conclusion that the biological sample contains no private information. It supports affirmance of the actual release judgment on the search issue.

The strongest objection to A is the central ordinary-investigative use, coupled with acquisition of internal biological material and retention beyond immediate supervision. A answers that the government has a distinct ongoing relationship with a person serving a criminal sentence and can impose a defined identification obligation within that relationship; medical collection and real use restrictions, rather than the bare usefulness of data, must support its proposed extension. The absence of a medical-risk objection matters to this particular application; the statute's reasonable-means constraint is not an invented absolute health exemption or one-draw rule.

**S — Special supervisory need.** The lower concurrence's Griffin theory treats deterrence and prompt identification of new misconduct during supervision as the sufficient need. The counterargument is that this uniform national evidentiary program was not tailored to a supervising officer's concrete rehabilitation judgment and is designed to solve ordinary crimes. A future-crime purpose is not automatically a non-law-enforcement purpose. No Associate below commits to S as an independent sufficient ground; those supporting A do not need it, while the R group rejects it on this record. Griffin's actual supervisory authority remains distinct from an absolute ban on all mixed purposes.

## Provisional component reconciliation

- **Reach and search status:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer all reach the Fourth Amendment issue and recognize compulsory blood extraction and protected examination as searches.
- **Apply existing Rise rule and reverse:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg — five Associates.
- **Adopt the defined contrary identification exception and affirm:** Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer — three Associates. This is their proposed change to current law, not an operative exception in the existing register.
- **Special-needs alternative:** no separate affirmative commitment. The five reject it for this actual program; the three who would affirm rely on A and do not require a holding that ordinary future-crime detection automatically becomes a special need.

The three supporters of A may share a single substantive dissent if assembly leaves their operative proposition unchanged. The five supporters of R share a sufficient substantive rule and remedy. ## Remedy commitments

For the five on R: reverse the Ninth Circuit's affirmance of the challenged DNA-refusal ground and remand with instructions to remove that ground and its dependent punishment or release conditions. The lower courts must identify the actual operative custody and supervision judgments, give effective relief from consequences resting on unconstitutional refusal enforcement, and preserve independently supported later grounds at their lawful scope. The post-demand drug event is not treated as retroactive authorization for the original demand. The real sample and profile warrant disposition of any properly presented continuing-relief request under the acquisition holding and ordinary remedial law; collection, source-sample retention, index retention and future evidentiary use are not interchangeable.

For the three on A: affirm the reviewed judgment and refusal consequence because the challenged acquisition is reasonable under their proposed defined exception. Their affirmance supplies no separate adjudication of an unraised medical-risk claim or a hypothetical unauthorized use; those matters are not required public reservations. Their actual protected-data and medical-means limits define the proposition they are prepared to support.
