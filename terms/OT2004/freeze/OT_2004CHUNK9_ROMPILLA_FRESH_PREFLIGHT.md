The original packet's attribution of an affirmative live no-prejudice argument to Pennsylvania is therefore corrected. **What counsel obtained.** Counsel obtained the earlier rape victim's testimony transcript on the eve of sentencing after renewed notice that the prosecutor would use it. Counsel's postconviction testimony confirmed that timing. She also recalled possibly seeing some earlier files without identifying them or their source and had a criminal-history sheet identifying convictions and dispositions. The question is inadequate timely examination of the accessible prior-conviction file as a whole, not absolute failure to obtain any prior-offense document. This distinction is material to the strongest defense of the investigation and must survive the handoff.

**What remained in the file.** The prior-conviction materials included the trial transcript and imprisonment records, including an initial transfer petition with corrections-counselor information about childhood, education, juvenile history, alcohol-related conduct and mental/cognitive assessment. These were potential leads to a fuller account. Their presence does not prove counsel actually saw them. Counsel's unparticularized recollection cannot be converted into a finding that the full relevant records were examined. Conversely, failure of a retrieved source to establish additional document review is not itself evidence of absent review; the supplied postconviction testimony and record control.

**Aggravation and the original presentation.** The recorded jury findings were commission during another felony, torture, and a significant history of felonies involving use or threatened use of violence. Scanlon's body was repeatedly stabbed and burned, and the prior violent conviction supplied the recidivist aggravator. Trial mitigation consisted of brief family pleas for mercy, belief in innocence and descriptions of Rompilla as a good man, together with the fourteen-year-old son's love and willingness to visit him in prison. The jury identified the son's appearance and possible rehabilitation as mitigation but found aggravation weightier. These are recorded findings and presentation, not new findings by this preflight.

**Developed omitted evidence.** The imprisonment records described deprivation, early juvenile involvement, repeated alcohol-related incarceration and assaultive conduct, and markedly poor cognitive testing. Postconviction family and documentary evidence developed severe parental alcoholism, maternal drinking during pregnancy, violent conflict and beatings, confinement in a dirty dog enclosure, social isolation, inadequate clothing, lack of indoor plumbing and an unheated sleeping space. School and juvenile materials described maternal absence, neglect, poor conditions and low testing. Experts given these records reported organic brain damage, significant cognitive impairment, possible prenatal-alcohol effects and substantially impaired capacity at the offense. Their expert opinions retain that status; no new diagnosis or categorical capital ineligibility is inferred.

The same information has adverse aspects: violence, juvenile and adult misconduct and alcohol-associated recurrence. Prejudice requires comparing the complete supported picture, including these adverse facts, the original presentation and the established aggravators. Neither abuse alone nor mitigation volume alone supplies the legal conclusion. The developed history also differs materially from a generic plea that relatives love the defendant.

**Applicable law and supported paths.** Strickland's existing reasonable-investigation/informed-choice requirement governs performance at the relevant Pennsylvania adjudication. Later Wiggins explains the operation of that established principle and separately requires the §2254(d)(1) gateway, actual deficiency and actual prejudice; it cannot be used as a later-created rule already established when the State decided. The original packet establishes that the state court adjudicated performance but did not decide prejudice. Actual historical findings receive §2254(e)(1)'s separate treatment; no invented finding of full-file review or no-prejudice determination receives deference.

One path finds that the State unreasonably approved counsel's failure to examine a known, accessible, central aggravation source despite the late transcript and other mitigation efforts, finds actual deficient investigation, and independently evaluates prejudice. The other finds that client/family inquiries, expert evaluations and the eventual transcript leave the State's approval within the objectively reasonable range, even if a different lawyer would have investigated further. This second path can deny relief at the statutory gateway without deciding actual constitutional deficiency or prejudice. If the gateway is passed, the omission's cumulative penalty effect must be independently resolved rather than inferred from performance or the State's noncontest.
