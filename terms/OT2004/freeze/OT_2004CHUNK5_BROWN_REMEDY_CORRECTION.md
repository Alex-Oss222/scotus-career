## Why correction is required

Full comparison does not establish a concrete changed premise supporting the provisional departure from the historical dissent's remedy. The historical dissent's concluding effect judgment must be read with its preceding examination of the actual trial: the repeated express legal exclusion concerned the defense's principal mitigating case; the judge declined to correct it; the qualified all-evidence instruction and ensuing crime-centered catchall could reinforce it; the evidence concerned a sustained claimed conversion and conduct supported by eight witnesses; and the prosecutor also challenged sincerity and weight. Its final conclusion was an actual-record judgment of harmfulness, not merely a missing section heading or an automatic rule that every constitutional error warrants relief.

The stricter current State-borne beyond-reasonable-doubt burden strengthens rather than undermines the dissenters' conclusion that this record does not establish harmlessness. ## Separate actual-effect reasoning under current law

The State's strongest effect argument combines the grave aggravation, including the murder, sexual violence, attacks on other victims, prior violence and incriminating statements, with the extensive admission of the conversion evidence, defense counsel's explanation and the prosecutor's alternative weighing and sincerity arguments. These features could permit a properly guided jury to reject the mitigation. They do not establish what this jury actually did with a principal reason offered for choosing life.

The constitutional restriction went directly to the selection between death and life, not merely to an undisputed collateral detail. The prosecution repeatedly told the jury that the defense's postcrime evidence was legally unavailable. The court supplied no authoritative correction after objection, and its general direction to consider evidence remained subject to the later factor instructions. Defense advocacy did not supply the missing legal authorization. An alternative prosecution argument about weight did not withdraw the exclusion argument or establish that every juror instead weighed the evidence. The State therefore cannot demonstrate beyond a reasonable doubt that the legal restriction made no contribution to this death verdict. That is a distinct actual-sentence conclusion, informed by the full aggravating and mitigating presentation; it is neither a structural-error holding nor a general rule that finding Boyde error always establishes prejudice.

The existing complete record, as evaluated in the supplied sources and frozen preflight, supplies this determination. No unavailable document, invented concession or deemed default supports it. The State's burden does not require the prisoner to prove that a correctly guided jury would necessarily choose life, and strong evidence capable of supporting death in a hypothetical lawful proceeding does not establish absence of contribution to the actual verdict.
