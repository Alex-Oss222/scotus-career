## Controlling holdings

### Actual governmental control identifies the speaker

**Holding and operative rule:** A promotional message is governmental for the presented compelled-subsidy objection when government determines its policy and actually selects, supervises and finally approves its content. The Government must establish that operational direction. Appointment power, a governmental label or ordinary compliance review alone is insufficient; the operational direction established here makes the challenged beef campaign the Government's message.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, supplies five direct joins. That same coalition joins Parts II–IV in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Keller requires examination of the particular expressive function rather than a body's general legal status. The Secretary's actual participation in development and final control over content establish more than a dormant veto over an independent private speaker. Producer drafting and the producers' freedom to run their own businesses do not transfer the final message choice from the officials who exercise it. The accountability objection would additionally require public identification of each campaign as governmental. For this funding claim, however, the relevant responsibility follows actual official message selection; a generic identification of the source of funds does not itself turn the controlled campaign into private authorship. United Foods reserved this operational governmental defense because it had not been developed there. The defense is preserved and supported here, so the Court resolves that reservation on the actual control record.

### A lawful targeted assessment may fund that governmental message

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory financing of a message actually selected and controlled by government does not, merely because assessed producers disagree, violate the First Amendment protection against compelled support of private expression. Financing through a lawful industry assessment rather than general revenue does not change the speaker; compelled personal expression and independently applicable constitutional or statutory constraints retain their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Glickman permits germane promotion within its qualifying collective private program, while United Foods forbids selected private advertising from supplying its own compulsory-association justification. These are different funded relationships. Here actual governmental selection supplies the sufficient distinction, rather than industry benefit, majority approval or the opportunity to counteradvertise. The First Amendment does not require the Government to replace a lawful targeted exaction with general taxation whenever an assessed person opposes its message. Ginsburg's commercial proposal addresses an independent justification for private promotion. The government-message ground resolves this challenge before an application of that proposed alternative becomes necessary; the existing private-support rule continues to govern its category.

### Generic funding identification does not establish the facial attribution claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The statute's compulsory financing and generic identification of producers as the funding source do not establish that every objector must personally endorse the advertising. Because the statute does not require a named dissenter's endorsement, this facial attribution objection supplies no independent basis for the reviewed statutory bar; a properly presented individual-attribution claim requires its own facts and adjudication.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the limited facial holding and express reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Governmental authorship, the source of money and a particular person's apparent endorsement are distinct questions. Barnette and Wooley protect against compelled personal expression, but compulsory payment and a broad funding label do not themselves establish the necessary personal attribution. The Court's ruling therefore disposes of the presented facial theory while preserving the distinct claim of an identified person or organization whose unwanted message is attributed to it. That claim's governing application remains unadjudicated because its factual predicate has not been established here.

### The dependent whole-program decree loses its constitutional premise

**Holding and operative rule:** Both dockets require vacatur of the reviewed constitutional judgment, its dependent declaration against the Act and Order, and the whole-checkoff collection injunction resting on the rejected funding theory. The lower court must remove those dependent restraints while adjudicating the separately pending producer-communications, referendum, statutory-power, administration and accounting claims under their own law, preserving independently supported relief.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the operative mandate; Ginsburg supports the formal vacatur and remand but not this resolution of the constitutional claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual decree restrained all further collection, not merely advertising expenditures. Once the adjudicated compulsory-financing ground fails, it cannot sustain either the advertising or nonadvertising collection restraints. A severability ruling is unnecessary to remove a remedy whose constitutional premise has been rejected. The three opposing Justices' proposed continued restraint depends on sustaining that advertising defect. Their concern about lawful survival of other functions does not preserve the defect after the Court's governmental-message ruling. The lower court must identify the restrictions resting solely on the rejected ground and remove them, while addressing the actual remaining claims.

## Public Projection

## Event

Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association, Nos. 03-1164 and 03-1165. Argued December 8, 2004; decided May 23, 2005. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit. Both dockets concern the certified First Amendment judgment declaring the Beef Act and Order unconstitutional and restraining all further checkoff collection, while other claims remain pending. The questions are whether compulsory promotion assessments violate the First Amendment when the Government actually controls the advertising and whether asserted producer attribution establishes the facial defect.

The Act imposes a dollar-per-head assessment for promotion and other projects. The Secretary appoints the Beef Board, retains removal authority over the relevant committee, approves content, and participates through department representatives in message development. Producers otherwise make their own marketing decisions; some object to generic promotion and messages promoting imported as well as domestic beef. Government-speech and commercial-justification defenses were presented below.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

The Court vacates the reviewed First Amendment judgment and remands in both dockets. Five Justices direct rejection of the compulsory-financing claim on the governmental-message ground; Justice Ginsburg supports formal vacatur and remand on a different proposed commercial-justification ground.

## Holdings

### Actual governmental control identifies the speaker

**Holding and operative rule:** A promotional message is governmental for the presented compelled-subsidy objection when government determines its policy and actually selects, supervises and finally approves its content. The Government must establish that operational direction. Appointment power, a governmental label or ordinary compliance review alone is insufficient; the operational direction established here makes the challenged beef campaign the Government's message.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, supplies five direct joins. That same coalition joins Parts II–IV in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Keller requires examination of the particular expressive function rather than a body's general legal status. The Secretary's actual participation in development and final control over content establish more than a dormant veto over an independent private speaker. Producer drafting and the producers' freedom to run their own businesses do not transfer the final message choice from the officials who exercise it. The accountability objection would additionally require public identification of each campaign as governmental. For this funding claim, however, the relevant responsibility follows actual official message selection; a generic identification of the source of funds does not itself turn the controlled campaign into private authorship. United Foods reserved this operational governmental defense because it had not been developed there. The defense is preserved and supported here, so the Court resolves that reservation on the actual control record.

### A lawful targeted assessment may fund that governmental message

**Holding and operative rule:** Compulsory financing of a message actually selected and controlled by government does not, merely because assessed producers disagree, violate the First Amendment protection against compelled support of private expression. Financing through a lawful industry assessment rather than general revenue does not change the speaker; compelled personal expression and independently applicable constitutional or statutory constraints retain their own predicates.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Glickman permits germane promotion within its qualifying collective private program, while United Foods forbids selected private advertising from supplying its own compulsory-association justification. These are different funded relationships. Here actual governmental selection supplies the sufficient distinction, rather than industry benefit, majority approval or the opportunity to counteradvertise. The First Amendment does not require the Government to replace a lawful targeted exaction with general taxation whenever an assessed person opposes its message. Ginsburg's commercial proposal addresses an independent justification for private promotion. The government-message ground resolves this challenge before an application of that proposed alternative becomes necessary; the existing private-support rule continues to govern its category.

### Generic funding identification does not establish the facial attribution claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The statute's compulsory financing and generic identification of producers as the funding source do not establish that every objector must personally endorse the advertising. Because the statute does not require a named dissenter's endorsement, this facial attribution objection supplies no independent basis for the reviewed statutory bar; a properly presented individual-attribution claim requires its own facts and adjudication.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the limited facial holding and express reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** Governmental authorship, the source of money and a particular person's apparent endorsement are distinct questions. Barnette and Wooley protect against compelled personal expression, but compulsory payment and a broad funding label do not themselves establish the necessary personal attribution. The Court's ruling therefore disposes of the presented facial theory while preserving the distinct claim of an identified person or organization whose unwanted message is attributed to it. That claim's governing application remains unadjudicated because its factual predicate has not been established here.

### The dependent whole-program decree loses its constitutional premise

**Holding and operative rule:** Both dockets require vacatur of the reviewed constitutional judgment, its dependent declaration against the Act and Order, and the whole-checkoff collection injunction resting on the rejected funding theory. The lower court must remove those dependent restraints while adjudicating the separately pending producer-communications, referendum, statutory-power, administration and accounting claims under their own law, preserving independently supported relief.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by the five-Justice Court coalition, supplies the operative mandate; Ginsburg supports the formal vacatur and remand but not this resolution of the constitutional claim.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual decree restrained all further collection, not merely advertising expenditures. Once the adjudicated compulsory-financing ground fails, it cannot sustain either the advertising or nonadvertising collection restraints. A severability ruling is unnecessary to remove a remedy whose constitutional premise has been rejected. The three opposing Justices' proposed continued restraint depends on sustaining that advertising defect. Their concern about lawful survival of other functions does not preserve the defect after the Court's governmental-message ruling. The lower court must identify the restrictions resting solely on the rejected ground and remove them, while addressing the actual remaining claims.

United Foods, Inc. retains its independently sufficient private-support rule; its reserved operational government-speech question is resolved for an actually controlled governmental message. Its treatment of the preserved commercial alternative remains distinct from its refusal to reach an undeveloped governmental defense.
- Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott retains its permission for germane promotion within the qualifying collective private program and its conditions.

## Law After Decision

Actual governmental selection and control defeat the presented private-subsidy objection to financing that message, including through a lawful industry assessment. Nominal supervision of independent private speech is insufficient. The existing rule for compulsory private advertising remains controlling; governmental authorship, financial support and personal attribution remain distinct. The reviewed funding claim is resolved, while the independent pending claims proceed under their own requirements.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court in full and would also recognize a distinct constitutional injury when an unwanted message is objectively and involuntarily attributed to an identified individual or organization. Government control does not answer that personal injury. His position extends beyond the Court's reservation while respecting the absence of an established attribution predicate in this case.

**Justice Ginsburg, concurring in the formal vacatur and remand.** She withholds the governmental-message classification for this producer-presented campaign, without adopting every part of the dissent's affirmative accountability rule. Instead she would permit a commercial subsidy to be justified by a substantial governmental interest, direct advancement and reasonable fit. That approach expressly proposes modifying United Foods's independent-private-support rule; it does not treat economic regulation as outside the First Amendment. Her United Foods dissent supplies the proposed framework, and the consistent presentation of the commercial defense permits its consideration here. Industry welfare identifies the asserted interest, but does not establish all the test's predicates. She would vacate the permanent constitutional decree and return genuinely unresolved application to the actual bench-trial record, supplemented only as ordinary procedure requires, with the Government bearing the burden. Her proposal prescribes neither scientific or quantitative benchmarks nor an automatic new trial. Any interim protection would require ordinary equitable support. She therefore agrees in formal vacatur while withholding the Court's instruction that this constitutional funding question is resolved for the Government.

**Justice Souter, joined by Stevens and Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Actual executive control is accepted, but hidden governmental selection does not provide the publicly identifiable responsibility needed to claim government speech for a campaign presented as the industry's message. Private drafting and targeted financing are not categorically forbidden; accountable governmental authorship must first exist. The writing expressly leaves open whether a discrete group's compelled support could raise another objection even when the Government openly identifies its speech.

Without the governmental justification, Abood, Keller and United Foods require an independently sufficient common function: advertising cannot justify its own compelled association. Producers retain their marketing autonomy and promotion is the principal object. Research spending does not by itself establish an adequate independent function. The preserved commercial defense fails as an independent legal cure even assuming effective promotion and sensible design; the objection is not an empirical finding that advertising failed. Glickman's qualifying collective program and Southworth's separate educational function remain distinct permissible relationships.

The writing would affirm protection against compelled advertising, vacate the categorical nonseverability determination and remand for actual statutory survival and remedial scope. The integrated collection restraint would continue until a conforming order permits a lawful, legally separable operation. Advertising's centrality and the deleted savings clause matter, but the deletion is not an affirmative legislative command resolving every remainder. The lower court would determine whether lawful operations can survive without rewriting the assessment. If they can, relief must permit them while fully protecting against the advertising defect; otherwise the whole-collection restraint may continue on that determination. The research allocation alone authorizes no fractional collection.

## Procedure After Action

Both dockets return through the Eighth Circuit. The lower court must dissolve the declaration and collection restraints resting solely on the rejected compulsory-financing ground, and proceed with the actual remaining claims and independently supported relief. Any separately established attribution injury receives its own appropriate adjudication and remedy.
