## Decision kernel and posture

Tory v. Cochran, No. 03-1488, 544 U.S. 734. Argued March 22, 2005; decided May 31, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, which affirmed a permanent injunction obtained by Johnnie Cochran against Ulysses Tory and Ruth Craft. The questions concern a prohibition on all future public speech about an admitted public figure and the consequences of his death for the continuing controversy and relief.

The trial court found false statements, threatening letters, picketing and demands intended to coerce payment of money Cochran did not owe. Its decree nevertheless prohibits public picketing, signs and oral statements about Cochran and his firm by petitioners and their agents or representatives. Cochran died on March 29, after argument. The order has no automatic death-termination term, and respondent continues to assert its enforceability. The firm was not a party to the original action.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted.

## Controlling holdings and precedent treatment

### The continuing command preserves a live controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** When an injunction remains operative by its terms and is asserted to remain enforceable, the beneficiary's death does not eliminate the restrained speakers' live challenge merely because the original substantive interest has changed. The agreed substitution is permitted for this appeal, without determining a successor's independent entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven join this holding, the present-overbreadth holding and the corresponding vacatur and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The command still regulates petitioners' speech, and respondent continues to claim its enforcement. A speaker need not risk contempt to obtain review of that burden. California procedures allowing later challenges do not make the current command disappear. Death changes the available justification and potential claimants; it does not, on this record, eliminate the restraint. The Court can correct that present injury without settling the independent state-law ownership, survival and successor-entitlement questions outside this appeal.

### The present complete topic ban lacks a sufficient remedial justification

**Holding and operative rule:** In the present circumstances, the injunction's prohibition on all public statements about Cochran and his firm is an unjustifiably broad prior restraint. Cochran's death has eliminated or materially diminished its stated objective of preventing coercion of him into paying money, and the existing record supplies no new adjudicated justification for maintaining the same complete prohibition to protect the practice.

**Controlling explanation:** A prior restraint requires a justification fitted to the speech it forbids and the injury it prevents. Near, Keefe and Nebraska Press supply that protection against unsupported advance suppression. Madsen and Schenck require a remedial connection between wrongdoing and each restriction; their content-neutral formula does not itself validate this subject-based decree. Alexander distinguishes punishment for past conduct from an order actually forbidding future expression, which this injunction does.

The trial court's serious findings remain facts. They do not supply a current reason to suppress every truthful, unrelated or otherwise lawful statement after the central coercion-prevention objective has disappeared or materially diminished. An asserted interest in the firm cannot substitute for an adjudicated basis supporting this order's full reach. The continuing, express restraint also answers the argument for leaving all correction to California: present constitutional relief is available on the changed circumstances and existing record. State proceedings remain the place for any new supported remedy.

### Relief follows the defect in the existing command

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the present public-topic prohibition is vacated, and its dependent enforcement must be removed with it. On remand an appropriate party may seek a different, precisely supported remedy for independently unlawful conduct under the applicable substantive and constitutional requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Vacatur responds to the prospective restraint and preserves the lawful liability findings. A future decree must identify the party entitled to relief, the continuing wrong, the responsible persons and the speech or conduct properly restricted. Madsen and Schenck preserve the requirement of notice and actual concert before an injunction binds persons through concerted conduct; neither a general label nor the substitution makes the nonparty firm an original claimant. A prohibition on the same protected discussion cannot survive merely through a differently titled dependent clause. The constitutionality of a different injunction confined to adjudicated false statements requires an order and remedial record absent from this case; independently lawful remedies retain their own elements.

- **Near v. Minnesota:** Applied for the constitutional protection against unsupported advance suppression of publication.
- **Organization for a Better Austin v. Keefe:** Applied for the protection against an unsupported injunction suppressing public criticism.
- **Nebraska Press Association v. Stuart:** Applied for the demanding justification required for a prior restraint.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center:** Applied for provision-specific remedial fit and responsibility of the persons bound. Its content-neutral injunction standard is not a safe harbor for this topic-based prohibition.
- **Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network:** Applied for fitted restraints, notice and actual-concert limits, and removal of enforcement dependent on an invalid speech restriction.
- **Alexander v. United States:** Distinguished because this decree directly forbids future speech, whereas the prior-restraint classification there concerned punishment without a future-publication ban.

The existing complete public-topic restraint cannot survive its present lack of a sufficient remedial justification after Cochran's death. An operative judicial command asserted to remain enforceable is reviewable while it continues to burden the speakers. Relief removes that prohibition and dependent enforcement while preserving lawful liability and the opportunity for an appropriate party to seek a supported, constitutionally confined new remedy.

## Public Projection

## Event

Tory v. Cochran, No. 03-1488, 544 U.S. 734. Argued March 22, 2005; decided May 31, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the California Court of Appeal, Second Appellate District, which affirmed a permanent injunction obtained by Johnnie Cochran against Ulysses Tory and Ruth Craft. The questions concern a prohibition on all future public speech about an admitted public figure and the consequences of his death for the continuing controversy and relief.

The trial court found false statements, threatening letters, picketing and demands intended to coerce payment of money Cochran did not owe. Its decree nevertheless prohibits public picketing, signs and oral statements about Cochran and his firm by petitioners and their agents or representatives. Cochran died on March 29, after argument. The order has no automatic death-termination term, and respondent continues to assert its enforceability. The firm was not a party to the original action.

## Holdings

### The continuing command preserves a live controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** When an injunction remains operative by its terms and is asserted to remain enforceable, the beneficiary's death does not eliminate the restrained speakers' live challenge merely because the original substantive interest has changed. The agreed substitution is permitted for this appeal, without determining a successor's independent entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** The Court's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven join this holding, the present-overbreadth holding and the corresponding vacatur and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The command still regulates petitioners' speech, and respondent continues to claim its enforcement. A speaker need not risk contempt to obtain review of that burden. California procedures allowing later challenges do not make the current command disappear. Death changes the available justification and potential claimants; it does not, on this record, eliminate the restraint. The Court can correct that present injury without settling the independent state-law ownership, survival and successor-entitlement questions outside this appeal.

### The present complete topic ban lacks a sufficient remedial justification

**Holding and operative rule:** In the present circumstances, the injunction's prohibition on all public statements about Cochran and his firm is an unjustifiably broad prior restraint. Cochran's death has eliminated or materially diminished its stated objective of preventing coercion of him into paying money, and the existing record supplies no new adjudicated justification for maintaining the same complete prohibition to protect the practice.

**Controlling explanation:** A prior restraint requires a justification fitted to the speech it forbids and the injury it prevents. Near, Keefe and Nebraska Press supply that protection against unsupported advance suppression. Madsen and Schenck require a remedial connection between wrongdoing and each restriction; their content-neutral formula does not itself validate this subject-based decree. Alexander distinguishes punishment for past conduct from an order actually forbidding future expression, which this injunction does.

The trial court's serious findings remain facts. They do not supply a current reason to suppress every truthful, unrelated or otherwise lawful statement after the central coercion-prevention objective has disappeared or materially diminished. An asserted interest in the firm cannot substitute for an adjudicated basis supporting this order's full reach. The continuing, express restraint also answers the argument for leaving all correction to California: present constitutional relief is available on the changed circumstances and existing record. State proceedings remain the place for any new supported remedy.

### Relief follows the defect in the existing command

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the present public-topic prohibition is vacated, and its dependent enforcement must be removed with it. On remand an appropriate party may seek a different, precisely supported remedy for independently unlawful conduct under the applicable substantive and constitutional requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** Vacatur responds to the prospective restraint and preserves the lawful liability findings. A future decree must identify the party entitled to relief, the continuing wrong, the responsible persons and the speech or conduct properly restricted. Madsen and Schenck preserve the requirement of notice and actual concert before an injunction binds persons through concerted conduct; neither a general label nor the substitution makes the nonparty firm an original claimant. A prohibition on the same protected discussion cannot survive merely through a differently titled dependent clause. The constitutionality of a different injunction confined to adjudicated false statements requires an order and remedial record absent from this case; independently lawful remedies retain their own elements.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Near v. Minnesota:** Applied for the constitutional protection against unsupported advance suppression of publication.
- **Organization for a Better Austin v. Keefe:** Applied for the protection against an unsupported injunction suppressing public criticism.
- **Nebraska Press Association v. Stuart:** Applied for the demanding justification required for a prior restraint.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center:** Applied for provision-specific remedial fit and responsibility of the persons bound. Its content-neutral injunction standard is not a safe harbor for this topic-based prohibition.
- **Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network:** Applied for fitted restraints, notice and actual-concert limits, and removal of enforcement dependent on an invalid speech restriction.
- **Alexander v. United States:** Distinguished because this decree directly forbids future speech, whereas the prior-restraint classification there concerned punishment without a future-publication ban.

## Law After Decision

The existing complete public-topic restraint cannot survive its present lack of a sufficient remedial justification after Cochran's death. An operative judicial command asserted to remain enforceable is reviewable while it continues to burden the speakers. Relief removes that prohibition and dependent enforcement while preserving lawful liability and the opportunity for an appropriate party to seek a supported, constitutionally confined new remedy.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the Court's continuing-controversy, present-overbreadth and remedial grounds. He further concludes that the decree's blanket topic prohibition violates the First Amendment independently of Cochran's death. Findings of coercive defamation justify an appropriate remedy for proved unlawful conduct, but they do not establish that every future public statement about Cochran or his firm is actionable. The command suppresses truthful criticism, discussion of this lawsuit and other protected expression without differentiating those statements from the adjudicated wrong. Madsen and Schenck require a supported remedial connection, while Alexander confirms the significance of an express future-speech ban; the content-neutral standard in the former decisions is not itself the standard for this content-based order. Death further weakens the stated objective of preventing Cochran's personal coercion. An interest in the practice does not supply findings justifying the decree's entire breadth. He would remove that public-topic prohibition, including dependent enforcement, while preserving lawful liability findings and independently valid relief. A different injunction confined to established unlawful conduct requires a concrete order and continuing remedial record; his ground does not decide every injunction following adjudicated defamation or supply immunity for threats, coercion or actionable false statements.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** These Justices would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. Cochran's death removed the original vehicle for the broad question on which review was granted. The new need to interpret, modify or dissolve the decree can be addressed in California, where constitutional objections remain available. A case-specific need to correct the existing command does not, in their view, restore the reason for retaining this changed vehicle or justify reaching a new constitutional issue. Their position neither affirms the decree's constitutionality nor decides the contested mootness question; it leaves the parties to seek appropriate state-court correction.
