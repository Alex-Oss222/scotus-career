##### Components and shared premises

- **D — detention and initial control.** Summers authorizes detention of occupants during execution of this proper weapons warrant. Initial cuffs were reasonable given the anticipated armed gang risk and need to secure several occupants. Mere non-suspect status does not eliminate execution-related detention authority.
- **F — continuing search-period force.** Graham requires objective assessment of the actual continued cuffs. Holland rejects perpetual justification from an initial need, but does not demand the least restrictive method. Plaintiff-favorable supported historical facts remain distinct from the legal judgment of reasonableness; a general unreasonable-force verdict is not an express finding on every disputed safety predicate.
- **Q — immigration questions.** Reject a rule requiring separate suspicion solely because an ordinary information request concerns immigration. Questions alone, without additional restraint or compelled intrusion, do not add a Fourth Amendment seizure. Existing detention does not authorize an unrelated extension. Caballes supplies only its bounded rule about the particular contraband examination, not a universal nonprolongation safe harbor. No Fifth Amendment issue or independent purse-search claim is decided.
- **P — detention after the search.** Summers ends when its execution-related justification ends; independent lawful grounds or reasonable completion tasks must be identified. The preserved allegation of post-search custody requires adjudication on the actual record. The Court does not find a particular number of unlawful minutes or presume an immigration-related delay.
- **I — immunity.** Separate any surviving violation from particular notice on February 3, 1998. Current Holland and Brosseau explain the inquiry but cannot themselves create earlier notice. Graham, Summers, Anderson, Baker v. Monroe Township (1995), McDonald v. Haskins (1992), and Franklin v. Foxworth (1994), with factual distinctions, are relevant preconduct sources. Late-1998 Heitschmidt cannot supply February notice. Individual causation remains necessary; rank is insufficient.
- **R — judgment and relief.** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it rests on the erroneous categorical premises and remand. Preserve the actual jury findings and independent duration ground for their proper legal effect. The compensatory verdict was not allocated between force and duration; neither automatic restoration nor automatic elimination of all damages follows. Evaluate the actual instructions, verdict, causation, and valid remaining theories before choosing affirmance of an amount, reduction, or a properly limited new trial. Punitive relief retains its separate legal predicates. The defense verdicts on challenged premises searches and other officers' entry/property conduct remain undisturbed.

#### Common scope determination and operative effect

The two questions presented ask whether the specified questioning and handcuff detention violated the Fourth Amendment. The officers seek a further holding that, even if their conduct violated that Amendment, the February 1998 law did not clearly prohibit it. That independent conduct-date defense is not fairly included in these constitutional questions as framed. The Court should decide the presented constitutional issues and their judgment consequences without independently granting or denying qualified immunity.

This is a case-specific application of the question limit, not a holding that immunity must always appear by name or can never be fairly included in a broader section 1983 question. The officers' actual argument is substantial: they identify the same conduct, overlapping precedents, treatment below, presentation in the petition, and merits briefing; they invoke Kolstad and Procunier. Those features address notice and practical readiness, but do not alone make the separate legal defense necessary to answer these particular constitutional questions. In particular, referring to earlier precedents to ask whether conduct was constitutional does not also ask whether an unconstitutional act was insufficiently clearly forbidden at a different legal cutoff. Mena's alternative answer to the defense is not consent to enlargement.

The original merits commitments already provide complete answers at their supported level. All eight reject a violation based solely on the topic of questions absent added restraint or protected intrusion. Four resolve the identified search-period force issue for the officers; four require proper appellate application of the force rule. All preserve the independently presented duration ground and the actual verdict's remedial effect. These dispositions do not require the Court to decide the separate personal defense. This practical point reinforces the question-specific scope conclusion; it is not a free-standing power to leave a properly reached question incomplete.

Accordingly:

- None of the eight supplies a present independent qualified-immunity holding or a separate judgment vote granting that defense.
- O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas retain their original substantive view that the identified search-period conduct would receive immunity if the issue were properly reached. That view is now expressly conditional and cannot be counted as an alternative holding of this decision.
- Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer retain their original conduct-specific approach. They do not obtain a present immunity denial from their refusal to decide the force issue categorically.
- On remand, any properly preserved independent immunity defense remains available under the existing February 1998 notice rule, on the conduct and factual account actually at issue and subject to ordinary preservation, finality, and mandate principles. ## Holdings

**1. A proper premises search permits detention of occupants and reasonable initial safety measures.** Part I controls with all nine Justices. Officers executing this weapons warrant could detain the occupants and initially use handcuffs to secure the premises against the anticipated armed-gang danger. Individual suspicion that each occupant committed an offense is not a prerequisite to that search-related detention.

Summers supplies authority to control occupants while a lawful search is executed, protecting officer safety and orderly completion. Graham separately measures the force used by objective reasonableness. The anticipated weapons risk justified securing four occupants at the outset. Mena's status as an occupant who was not suspected of crime therefore does not itself invalidate the initial control. That detention authority and the reasonableness of its manner remain distinct inquiries.

**2. Continued handcuffing requires continuing objective justification under the actual conditions.** Part II controls with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Courts must assess the risks, degree of control, duration, restraint-related harm and available supervision as conditions develop, preserving the historical facts supported by the verdict. The lawful start of restraint cannot conclusively establish its reasonableness throughout the search; the Ninth Circuit must review the continued-force ground on that basis.

Graham supplies objective review of the force actually applied. Holland explains why a reasonable initial measure can become excessive as its justification changes. The weapons search, four adults and limited guards carry substantial weight, as the partial dissent emphasizes. They must be considered together with the supported account of compliance, control, discomfort, requests for release and the course of the search. A general force verdict does not establish every subsidiary fact, but the reviewing court must preserve the historical facts the evidence supports in its favor. Neither an appellate preference for a different tactic nor a categorical approval of all search-period cuffs supplies that review. The Constitution requires reasonable conduct, not the least intrusive feasible arrangement or a fixed handcuff time limit.

**3. The subject of an ordinary question does not alone create an additional seizure.** Part I controls with all nine Justices. Immigration questions during an otherwise lawful detention require no separate reasonable suspicion solely because they concern immigration, provided they add neither detention nor a distinct coercive intrusion. Actual added restraint must have its own adequate justification.

Bostick distinguishes a request for information from a seizure. When a person is already lawfully detained, the relevant inquiry concerns what the questioning adds to that restraint. Caballes preserves the need for independently lawful custody and attention to the protected interest actually invaded; it supplies no general permission for every investigative technique that takes no extra time. The Ninth Circuit's topic-based ground therefore fails. Whether these questions actually extended custody remains part of the factual duration inquiry.

**4. Detention must remain tied to an adequate lawful basis, and the verdict must be reviewed by its surviving grounds.** Part III controls with all nine Justices. The lower court must separately decide the preserved claim of post-search detention, identifying the actual interval, reasonable completion tasks and any independent lawful basis. It must then determine what the legally valid force and duration grounds, supported findings and instructions establish about liability and damages.

Summers links its detention authority to execution of the search; a valid initial detention cannot indefinitely sustain later custody. The separate duration ground was not resolved below. Because compensatory damages were not allocated between force and duration, correction of one theory does not by itself settle the whole award. The court must decide whether supported findings sustain an amount, require reduction or require a limited new trial; punitive damages retain their separate predicates and individual causation remains necessary.

The present questions concern constitutional detention, force and questioning. The officers' requested independent immunity ruling is outside that scope in this case: factual overlap and alternative briefing do not make the distinct conduct-date notice defense necessary to decide those questions. Any defense still properly available below remains subject to preservation, finality and the mandate. Its notice inquiry concerns the particular conduct on February 3, 1998; later decisions cannot supply earlier fair warning.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Michigan v. Summers:** Applied to occupant detention and initial control during this lawful search; its execution-related authority must remain tied to the detention's actual basis.
- **Graham v. Connor:** Applied to the objective reasonableness of the force used, separately from the authority to detain.

## Law After Decision

Search-related detention, the force used to maintain it, and any additional investigative restraint remain separate Fourth Amendment inquiries. Occupant status permits reasonable initial control; continued force depends on the actual changing conditions. An unrelated conversational subject alone supplies no additional seizure, while an extension of custody requires a lawful basis. These rules govern the remand; evaluation of the supported verdict facts and the award is ordinary application and remedial work.
