## Controlling holdings

### Required state litigation does not create a takings exception to §1738

**Operative rule.** A takings claimant’s compliance with a state-compensation prerequisite and reservation of federal causes do not exempt issues actually and necessarily decided by the state court from the rendering State’s lawful preclusion rules under §1738. The federal court must identify each binding common issue, with the required identity and full and fair opportunity to litigate; genuinely distinct issues not necessarily adjudicated remain outside that issue-preclusion bar.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join the complete Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation.** Section 1738 requires recognition of a state judgment with the same force it receives in the courts of that State. Dodd v. Hood River County distinguishes reservation of a federal cause from the binding effect of issues necessarily adjudicated in state court. That distinction applies to qualifying legal determinations as well as facts and governs the owners’ claimed compulsory-route exception.

England v. Louisiana State Board of Medical Examiners protects return to federal court after abstention permits a state court to resolve an antecedent state-law issue. It does not allow a litigant to broaden the state proceedings to secure a decision on the same substantive issues and then require their fresh adjudication under a reserved federal label. The owners pursued broader state takings claims beyond the antecedent mandamus issue; the lower courts treated the resolved issues as coextensive with those needed for their federal claims. Review of that particular California-law determination was not granted.

The owners’ strongest objection is that required state compensation litigation, followed by preclusion, may prevent a federal district-court merits determination. Allen v. McCurry rejects an unrestricted entitlement to relitigate state-decided issues merely to obtain a federal forum. Kremer v. Chemical Construction requires a statutory basis for displacing §1738; the proposed takings category supplies none. The obligation to recognize an existing judgment remains distinct from the justification for requiring the preceding state litigation.

Exxon Mobil keeps ordinary preclusion distinct from jurisdiction over an independent federal claim. Lingle’s rejection of regulatory efficacy as an independent takings test supplies no exception to recognition of the state judgment. Wholesale reconsideration of Williamson County’s state-compensation prerequisite lies outside the granted question.

**Precedent treatment.** Dodd is reaffirmed and applied; England retains its abstention-reservation protection at its proper scope; Allen and Kremer supply the recognition rule and the requirement of statutory displacement.

## Precedent treatment and resulting law

- **Dodd v. Hood River County:** Reaffirmed and applied to the asserted compulsory-state-litigation exception; reserving a cause does not erase binding common issues.
- **England v. Louisiana State Board of Medical Examiners:** Applied at its abstention scope. A reservation protects the appropriate return for a distinct federal issue, not a second decision of issues submitted to and decided by the state court.
- **Allen v. McCurry:** Applied to reject an unrestricted federal-forum entitlement to relitigate previously determined issues.
- **Kremer v. Chemical Construction Corp.:** Applied to require statutory grounds for an exception to §1738.
- **Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Industries Corp.:** Preserved at its distinction between jurisdiction and state-law preclusion.
- **Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.:** Its rejection of regulatory efficacy as an independent takings test is respected; it does not displace recognition of the state judgment.
- **Williamson County Regional Planning Commission v. Hamilton Bank:** Its state-compensation prerequisite supplies the asserted route into state court, not an exception to §1738; reconsideration of that prerequisite is outside the grant.

The issue-specific rule in Dodd governs even when the claimant pursued state compensation procedures to satisfy a prerequisite to federal litigation. Federal courts give the state judgment its lawful preclusive effect issue by issue, preserving the distinction between a reserved cause and a binding determination. State litigation alone does not establish that every later federal issue is barred.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

**O’Connor, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring.** The concurrence joins the full statutory holding and agrees that the existing state judgment must receive the recognition §1738 commands. It would reconsider Williamson County’s separate state-litigation prerequisite in an appropriate case. That requirement differs from obtaining a final administrative decision identifying how a regulation applies to the property. Once the government has made that decision, the concurrence questions the constitutional or prudential basis for requiring state compensation litigation before a federal takings action.

State courts’ competence to adjudicate federal rights does not explain why takings claims must uniquely begin there. Fair Assessment’s treatment of state taxation rests on a historically grounded comity principle and does not by itself establish a parallel land-use rule. Mandatory state litigation combined with ordinary preclusion can prevent a later federal district-court merits decision. That practical effect calls for examination of the prerequisite, not suspension of §1738. Neither party seeks its reconsideration here, the grant does not include it, and reconsideration would not benefit these petitioners.
