## Decision kernel and posture

Tucson Woman's Clinic v. Eden. Merits decision, May 31, 2005. On review after merits submission of the Ninth Circuit's amended judgment, 379 F.3d 531, as amended August 23, 2004, partly affirming and partly reversing cross-summary judgments of the District of Arizona. The questions concern cumulative abortion-clinic regulation burdens; professional, volume and patient sex-equality distinctions; compulsory entry and patient-information access; notice under an independently enforceable dignity requirement; and hospital-privilege requirements and private regulatory control.

Arizona's 1999 licensing scheme covers facilities performing at least five first-trimester abortions in a month or any later-trimester abortions. Providers offered specified equipment, staffing, overtime, follow-up and administrative costs, predicted loss of physicians and expert evidence connecting reduced capacity to delay and clinical risk. Arizona invokes patient safety, including a death following gravely deficient care. Providers requested trial on cumulative access burdens. The amended appellate ruling separately sustained the limited professional-board serious-incident report. Other unbriefed vagueness objections were abandoned. Enforcement was stayed by stipulation during the proceedings and appeals.

## Controlling holdings and precedent treatment

### Cumulative burdens require adjudication under the complete access standard

**Holding and operative rule:** Specific evidence of added expenditures, lost provider capacity and consequential delay precludes summary judgment for the State when it creates material disputes about attributable interference or justification under the complete previability framework. The lower court must examine each surviving obligation and the challenged interacting package, including supported affected subgroups, and apply the protections in their prescribed order.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rule and additional mandate in the Court's opinion, supplying six direct joins. Kennedy separately joins the remand concerning protected choice, a substantial practical obstacle and timely necessary care; his seventh judgment vote does not join the residual severe- and meaningful-interference requirements. The six-Justice holding adopts the following complete operative standard:

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

Challengers establish the duty, stable population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification and fit burden. Gestational age may support a medically justified safety requirement, but a trimester boundary does not itself change the standard. Necessary-care protection includes competent individualized medical support for a material risk of death or serious health harm, including grave medically established mental danger, when the method is necessary for timely, safe and suitable care; preference or marginal comparative advantage alone is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey supplies the whole rule; Lawall, Greenville and Taft preserve its independent operation for practical access and clinical protection. The providers offered concrete mechanisms: equipment expenses, nurse staffing, overtime and follow-up costs, predicted departures and loss of physician capacity, and resulting delay and clinical risk. These are evidence-supported forecasts requiring adjudication, not findings of completed closures. A general safety label and the absence of one exact predicted fee increase do not eliminate those disputes. Conversely, the providers' request for trial and the contested causation and justification record require factual proceedings under the rule.

The comparison must include the practical costs of surviving requirements, including privilege applications, meetings, on-call work and the actual on-site duty. The on-site privilege holder need not personally perform every procedure, but must remain until patients are stable and ready to leave recovery. Duties removed by the independent holdings cannot be counted as retained lawful obligations. The State's serious safety concerns receive their evidentiary force; they must answer the actual interference at the applicable level. The dissent's rational-regulation approach does not account for the protected constitutional choice governing this case, and limiting review to substantial obstacles would omit the framework's distinct protections for remaining severe and meaningful interference.

### Ordinary provider categories and independent patient sex equality

**Holding and operative rule:** The professional category and exemption for very small first-trimester practices survive the presented equal-protection challenges because targeted safety oversight and administrative burden provide rational relationships to legitimate ends. The independent patient sex-equality claim must be adjudicated separately: an actual sex classification in terms or administration requires a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, while a genuinely neutral classification challenged for discrimination requires a choice made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences; disparity and awareness alone are insufficient.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join these results and Klinger’s classification-and-purpose rule. The direct common proposition requires no automatic classification of every abortion regulation as a sex distinction.

**Controlling explanation:** The law covers five or more first-trimester abortions in a month or any later-trimester abortion. Practice volume can rationally bear on regulatory expense and the scale of potential harm, and the profession is not a suspect class. Those conclusions do not answer the separately pleaded comparison of women's health services with comparably risky services for men. Klinger requires the court to identify the actual governmental distinction and relevant opportunity before choosing review. A medical label cannot extinguish that independent inquiry, and an actual sex classification does not require an additional finding of hostile motive before intermediate scrutiny applies. The lower court's rejection of particular animus evidence under rational review did not resolve the classification and comparator questions it treated as absorbed into access analysis. On remand the court must identify the actual classification or purposeful administration, make the required findings and apply the corresponding standard.

### Immediate compulsory entry requires constitutionally adequate authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Arizona may not compel the challenged open-ended immediate clinic entry without a warrant or an established exception merely by newly imposing extensive regulation. The State must establish the exception on which it relies. In a genuinely closely regulated industry, Burger additionally requires a substantial governmental interest, necessity for warrantless inspection and a constitutionally adequate substitute constraining official discretion; this scheme does not establish those predicates for its full mandatory entry command.

**Authority:** All nine join the rule and remedy on Burger and protected-premises grounds. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also adopt Ferguson's operation-specific and no-general-waiver reasoning as corroboration.

**Controlling explanation:** Commercial medical premises retain constitutional protection. The newly imposed regulatory density cannot itself establish the historical regulated-industry premise or eliminate the privacy of examination rooms, patients and records. The operating-hours restriction supplies a time limit but not adequate control of the authorized intrusion. Legitimate urgent health oversight and professional licensure therefore do not sustain this breadth. Ordinary consent, an appropriately obtained warrant and a genuine exigency remain lawful sources for an inspection. Ferguson likewise requires attention to the actual medical operation and rejects treating attendance for care as general consent, while its coordinated prosecution setting differs from this administrative scheme. The restraint reaches compulsory entry without adequate authority and the applications and sanctions dependent on that entry command.

### Recipient-specific limits govern department and contractor information access

**Holding and operative rule:** Official access to intimate patient information requires a sufficient connection among the information, actual recipient, regulatory function, scope, uses, retention and safeguards. The established department access and copying under R9-10-1511(A)(4)(b),(c), and APEX identified-ultrasound access and retention under §36-2301.02(B),(C), lack that connection and must be restrained at their unjustified scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete privacy rule, these applications and corresponding relief in the Court's opinion. Scalia and Thomas dissent from these two privacy dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville, Lawall and Herring protect against unjustified authorized exposure, while allowing functionally justified identified audits, complaints and corroboration. Each identifier need not be indispensable. Here department access includes broad unredacted histories and copying without supported need-to-know restrictions; vague training assurances and uncertain sanctions do not adequately constrain its recipients and uses. APEX receives identified ultrasound prints with seven-year retention, minimal secure-storage terms for the first two years and inadequate later-storage and employee-access limits. A bare nondisclosure rule does not cure those permissions, and a proposed coding policy is not operative protection.

The constitutional injury concerns actual authorized access, not a presumption of unlawful leakage. Redaction, coding, consent and targeted follow-up are concrete alternatives when adequate to the task, rather than universal prerequisites. The dissent's constitutional-source objection cannot displace the recipient-specific protection established by Greenville and Lawall. The remedy must remove the proved excessive access and redistribution while preserving justified confidential oversight, with exact recipients, tasks and dependent consequences specified below.

### Bounded serious-incident reports serve a distinct disciplinary function

**Holding and operative rule:** The limited report under R9-10-1504(B),(C) concerning an abortion-related death or serious injury to a patient or viable fetus may supply the patient's name, incident description and follow-up action to the responsible professional board under the distinct confidentiality protections of §§32-1451.01(C) and 32-1855.03(D). That defined disciplinary function, limited subject and protected disclosure provide the required connection on this record.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join the complete privacy rationale. Scalia and Thomas join the rejection of this challenge and the permission for lawful bounded board reporting, giving the disposition nine votes without adding their joins to the substantive-privacy rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** A serious injury creates a concrete disciplinary inquiry for an identified professional board. The limited information and the board-specific protections materially distinguish that channel from department-wide access and contractor retention. Those other provisions' defects cannot be transferred to different recipients and safeguards. The amended appellate court entertained this particular issue and sustained the report; that ruling is affirmed at its actual scope.

### Undefined dignity language cannot independently support punishment

**Holding and operative rule:** R9-10-1507's unconfined requirement of consideration, respect and full recognition of dignity and individuality cannot independently support civil, criminal or adverse licensing sanctions without an ascertainable operative standard. Relief removes that independently punishable branch and dependent consequences while preserving definite clinical and professional duties.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this confined rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville distinguishes contextual professional standards from undefined additional duties selected by administrators. The value of respectful care does not identify the additional conduct required for full recognition of dignity and individuality. This record supplies no authoritative limitation or recognized clinical criterion that makes an independent sanction ascertainable. A warning, mitigation or promise of careful enforcement cannot create the omitted standard. The defect differs from a recognizable professional-core obligation: it concerns punishment based on an inspector's or complainant's subjective judgment under this abstract additional requirement. Removing that sanction branch leaves voluntary aspirations and independently ascertainable care requirements intact.

### Constrained hospital qualifications do not establish the alleged private veto

**Holding and operative rule:** The facial standardless-delegation challenge fails where Arizona law supplies hospital-related legitimate standards, notice and hearing, and judicial review for reasonableness and substantial evidentiary support, and the record establishes no actual or supported impending arbitrary refusal based on abortion practice. The costs and operational burdens of the qualification requirement remain part of the access proceedings at the scope described above.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join the qualification ruling and rejection of the present delegation claim. The access consequences retain the six-Justice complete-framework and Kennedy narrower joins already stated.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville permits objective professional qualifications and emergency-care arrangements while forbidding arbitrary, legally uncontrolled private licensing consent. Arizona's Holmes, Peterson and Blende rules provide actual constraints and review. The possibility of private discretion does not establish an uncontrolled veto under this record and those enforceable limits. A supported impending arbitrary exclusion can be challenged before a clinic closes; public-hospital restrictions cannot simply be assumed to govern every private institution. The present rejection concerns the private-control theory. Staffing, applications, meetings, on-call duties and the on-site requirement's effects still must be considered under the governing access rule.

### Remedy follows the precise violation and its proved scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The lower court must apply Arizona severability law to the identified unconstitutional duties and applications, remove their dependent enforcement consequences, preserve independently lawful obligations, and promptly specify the stipulated restraint's continuing operation. Further factual and equitable determinations must identify the actual violation, affected population and scope before selecting corresponding relief.

**Authority:** All nine join those common severability and implementation requirements for the independent defects they support. The complete-framework majority of six joins their application to all access grounds; Kennedy joins effective relief on protected-choice, obstacle, necessary-care and independent grounds; Scalia and Thomas retain their stated objections to relief resting on abortion or substantive informational privacy.

Once a common-policy violation and its scope are proved, the seven Justices supporting the protected-choice/necessary-care and independent privacy grounds also apply Taft's corrective-policy rule: relief must protect the governed patients and providers unless the State establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation. Six join its application when the predicate is the residual access framework. Challengers retain the initial burden to prove injury and scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The unresolved access and patient-equality questions require further adjudication before a final severability package can be fixed. The stipulation remains governed by its terms pending a prompt reasoned order; partial affirmance does not itself authorize blanket enforcement. The court must identify retained duties, prohibited applications, bound officials and dependent civil, criminal and licensing consequences. Any continuation or modification of protection requires the existing stipulation and ordinary equitable predicates. Taft's application of Anderson requires a remedy matching a proved common-policy injury, while preserving the State's opportunity to establish a complete narrower cure. The court should expedite genuinely material factual work and state the resulting obligations precisely.

- **Casey:** Applied in full. Its protected choice, individualized viability, timely necessary care, six-step sequence, proof allocation and tailored-remedy rules govern the access claim.
- **Lawall:** Applied for independent access review and the recipient-specific connection required for intimate-information transfer; necessary confidential handling differs from unjustified outside access.
- **Greenville:** Applied for independent access review and the recipient/function/scope/use/safeguard privacy rule, permitting justified identified oversight without universal indispensability. Its notice and qualification holdings preserve ascertainable professional duties and objective qualifications while forbidding undefined independent sanctions and arbitrary uncontrolled private licensing consent. Arizona's different duties and safeguards receive their own application.
- **Women's Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft:** Applied for the complete framework, competent individualized necessary-care protection and correction of a proved common-policy violation. Its Ohio-specific statutory and clinical applications remain distinct from Arizona's duties.
- **Klinger:** Applied to require classification-specific equality review and to distinguish actual sex classifications from purpose-based challenges to genuinely neutral administration. The rationality of professional categories cannot absorb the independent patient claim.
- **United States v. Virginia:** Supplies the same demanding important-objective/substantial-relationship standard for actual sex exclusion to the seven Justices joining its application; it supplies no automatic classification of every reproductive regulation.
- **New York v. Burger:** Applied for protected commercial premises and the actual predicates and discretion limits of warrantless inspection in a closely regulated industry.
- **Ferguson:** Supplies the seven-Justice corroborating operation-specific/no-waiver principle; its prosecution facts are distinguished.
- **Herring:** Applied, with Lawall and Greenville, to recipient-specific protection against unjustified onward exposure of intimate medical information.
- **Anderson v. Spear:** Applied through Taft for correction at the scope of a proved common-policy violation, with a narrower complete cure available on the State's showing.

The licensing scheme is subject to separate access, equality, search, privacy, notice and private-control requirements. Concrete prospective cost, capacity and delay evidence receives factual adjudication under the complete Casey framework; surviving professional categories and qualification rules remain subject to that independent access review. The patient sex-equality claim receives its own classification-and-purpose inquiry.

The established compulsory-entry command, excessive department/APEX permissions and undefined independent dignity sanctions must be restrained at their unconstitutional scope. Lawful inspections, fitted confidential oversight, the bounded serious-incident reports, definite professional duties and the qualification rule remain available under their governing conditions. Arizona-law severability, the remaining factual proceedings and the operative stipulation determine the resulting enforceable package and protection.

## Holdings

### Cumulative burdens require adjudication under the complete access standard

**Holding and operative rule:** Specific evidence of added expenditures, lost provider capacity and consequential delay precludes summary judgment for the State when it creates material disputes about attributable interference or justification under the complete previability framework. The lower court must examine each surviving obligation and the challenged interacting package, including supported affected subgroups, and apply the protections in their prescribed order.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete rule and additional mandate in the Court's opinion, supplying six direct joins. Kennedy separately joins the remand concerning protected choice, a substantial practical obstacle and timely necessary care; his seventh judgment vote does not join the residual severe- and meaningful-interference requirements. The six-Justice holding adopts the following complete operative standard:

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

Challengers establish the duty, stable population, causation and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification and fit burden. Gestational age may support a medically justified safety requirement, but a trimester boundary does not itself change the standard. Necessary-care protection includes competent individualized medical support for a material risk of death or serious health harm, including grave medically established mental danger, when the method is necessary for timely, safe and suitable care; preference or marginal comparative advantage alone is insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey supplies the whole rule; Lawall, Greenville and Taft preserve its independent operation for practical access and clinical protection. The providers offered concrete mechanisms: equipment expenses, nurse staffing, overtime and follow-up costs, predicted departures and loss of physician capacity, and resulting delay and clinical risk. These are evidence-supported forecasts requiring adjudication, not findings of completed closures. A general safety label and the absence of one exact predicted fee increase do not eliminate those disputes. Conversely, the providers' request for trial and the contested causation and justification record require factual proceedings under the rule.

The comparison must include the practical costs of surviving requirements, including privilege applications, meetings, on-call work and the actual on-site duty. The on-site privilege holder need not personally perform every procedure, but must remain until patients are stable and ready to leave recovery. Duties removed by the independent holdings cannot be counted as retained lawful obligations. The State's serious safety concerns receive their evidentiary force; they must answer the actual interference at the applicable level. The dissent's rational-regulation approach does not account for the protected constitutional choice governing this case, and limiting review to substantial obstacles would omit the framework's distinct protections for remaining severe and meaningful interference.

### Ordinary provider categories and independent patient sex equality

**Holding and operative rule:** The professional category and exemption for very small first-trimester practices survive the presented equal-protection challenges because targeted safety oversight and administrative burden provide rational relationships to legitimate ends. The independent patient sex-equality claim must be adjudicated separately: an actual sex classification in terms or administration requires a genuine important objective and substantial relationship, while a genuinely neutral classification challenged for discrimination requires a choice made at least partly because of sex or its adverse consequences; disparity and awareness alone are insufficient.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join these results and Klinger’s classification-and-purpose rule. The direct common proposition requires no automatic classification of every abortion regulation as a sex distinction.

**Controlling explanation:** The law covers five or more first-trimester abortions in a month or any later-trimester abortion. Practice volume can rationally bear on regulatory expense and the scale of potential harm, and the profession is not a suspect class. Those conclusions do not answer the separately pleaded comparison of women's health services with comparably risky services for men. Klinger requires the court to identify the actual governmental distinction and relevant opportunity before choosing review. A medical label cannot extinguish that independent inquiry, and an actual sex classification does not require an additional finding of hostile motive before intermediate scrutiny applies. The lower court's rejection of particular animus evidence under rational review did not resolve the classification and comparator questions it treated as absorbed into access analysis. On remand the court must identify the actual classification or purposeful administration, make the required findings and apply the corresponding standard.

### Immediate compulsory entry requires constitutionally adequate authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Arizona may not compel the challenged open-ended immediate clinic entry without a warrant or an established exception merely by newly imposing extensive regulation. The State must establish the exception on which it relies. In a genuinely closely regulated industry, Burger additionally requires a substantial governmental interest, necessity for warrantless inspection and a constitutionally adequate substitute constraining official discretion; this scheme does not establish those predicates for its full mandatory entry command.

**Authority:** All nine join the rule and remedy on Burger and protected-premises grounds. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also adopt Ferguson's operation-specific and no-general-waiver reasoning as corroboration.

**Controlling explanation:** Commercial medical premises retain constitutional protection. The newly imposed regulatory density cannot itself establish the historical regulated-industry premise or eliminate the privacy of examination rooms, patients and records. The operating-hours restriction supplies a time limit but not adequate control of the authorized intrusion. Legitimate urgent health oversight and professional licensure therefore do not sustain this breadth. Ordinary consent, an appropriately obtained warrant and a genuine exigency remain lawful sources for an inspection. Ferguson likewise requires attention to the actual medical operation and rejects treating attendance for care as general consent, while its coordinated prosecution setting differs from this administrative scheme. The restraint reaches compulsory entry without adequate authority and the applications and sanctions dependent on that entry command.

### Recipient-specific limits govern department and contractor information access

**Holding and operative rule:** Official access to intimate patient information requires a sufficient connection among the information, actual recipient, regulatory function, scope, uses, retention and safeguards. The established department access and copying under R9-10-1511(A)(4)(b),(c), and APEX identified-ultrasound access and retention under §36-2301.02(B),(C), lack that connection and must be restrained at their unjustified scope.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete privacy rule, these applications and corresponding relief in the Court's opinion. Scalia and Thomas dissent from these two privacy dispositions.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville, Lawall and Herring protect against unjustified authorized exposure, while allowing functionally justified identified audits, complaints and corroboration. Each identifier need not be indispensable. Here department access includes broad unredacted histories and copying without supported need-to-know restrictions; vague training assurances and uncertain sanctions do not adequately constrain its recipients and uses. APEX receives identified ultrasound prints with seven-year retention, minimal secure-storage terms for the first two years and inadequate later-storage and employee-access limits. A bare nondisclosure rule does not cure those permissions, and a proposed coding policy is not operative protection.

The constitutional injury concerns actual authorized access, not a presumption of unlawful leakage. Redaction, coding, consent and targeted follow-up are concrete alternatives when adequate to the task, rather than universal prerequisites. The dissent's constitutional-source objection cannot displace the recipient-specific protection established by Greenville and Lawall. The remedy must remove the proved excessive access and redistribution while preserving justified confidential oversight, with exact recipients, tasks and dependent consequences specified below.

### Bounded serious-incident reports serve a distinct disciplinary function

**Holding and operative rule:** The limited report under R9-10-1504(B),(C) concerning an abortion-related death or serious injury to a patient or viable fetus may supply the patient's name, incident description and follow-up action to the responsible professional board under the distinct confidentiality protections of §§32-1451.01(C) and 32-1855.03(D). That defined disciplinary function, limited subject and protected disclosure provide the required connection on this record.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join the complete privacy rationale. Scalia and Thomas join the rejection of this challenge and the permission for lawful bounded board reporting, giving the disposition nine votes without adding their joins to the substantive-privacy rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** A serious injury creates a concrete disciplinary inquiry for an identified professional board. The limited information and the board-specific protections materially distinguish that channel from department-wide access and contractor retention. Those other provisions' defects cannot be transferred to different recipients and safeguards. The amended appellate court entertained this particular issue and sustained the report; that ruling is affirmed at its actual scope.

### Undefined dignity language cannot independently support punishment

**Holding and operative rule:** R9-10-1507's unconfined requirement of consideration, respect and full recognition of dignity and individuality cannot independently support civil, criminal or adverse licensing sanctions without an ascertainable operative standard. Relief removes that independently punishable branch and dependent consequences while preserving definite clinical and professional duties.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this confined rule and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville distinguishes contextual professional standards from undefined additional duties selected by administrators. The value of respectful care does not identify the additional conduct required for full recognition of dignity and individuality. This record supplies no authoritative limitation or recognized clinical criterion that makes an independent sanction ascertainable. A warning, mitigation or promise of careful enforcement cannot create the omitted standard. The defect differs from a recognizable professional-core obligation: it concerns punishment based on an inspector's or complainant's subjective judgment under this abstract additional requirement. Removing that sanction branch leaves voluntary aspirations and independently ascertainable care requirements intact.

### Constrained hospital qualifications do not establish the alleged private veto

**Holding and operative rule:** The facial standardless-delegation challenge fails where Arizona law supplies hospital-related legitimate standards, notice and hearing, and judicial review for reasonableness and substantial evidentiary support, and the record establishes no actual or supported impending arbitrary refusal based on abortion practice. The costs and operational burdens of the qualification requirement remain part of the access proceedings at the scope described above.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join the qualification ruling and rejection of the present delegation claim. The access consequences retain the six-Justice complete-framework and Kennedy narrower joins already stated.

**Controlling explanation:** Greenville permits objective professional qualifications and emergency-care arrangements while forbidding arbitrary, legally uncontrolled private licensing consent. Arizona's Holmes, Peterson and Blende rules provide actual constraints and review. The possibility of private discretion does not establish an uncontrolled veto under this record and those enforceable limits. A supported impending arbitrary exclusion can be challenged before a clinic closes; public-hospital restrictions cannot simply be assumed to govern every private institution. The present rejection concerns the private-control theory. Staffing, applications, meetings, on-call duties and the on-site requirement's effects still must be considered under the governing access rule.

### Remedy follows the precise violation and its proved scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The lower court must apply Arizona severability law to the identified unconstitutional duties and applications, remove their dependent enforcement consequences, preserve independently lawful obligations, and promptly specify the stipulated restraint's continuing operation. Further factual and equitable determinations must identify the actual violation, affected population and scope before selecting corresponding relief.

**Authority:** All nine join those common severability and implementation requirements for the independent defects they support. The complete-framework majority of six joins their application to all access grounds; Kennedy joins effective relief on protected-choice, obstacle, necessary-care and independent grounds; Scalia and Thomas retain their stated objections to relief resting on abortion or substantive informational privacy.

Once a common-policy violation and its scope are proved, the seven Justices supporting the protected-choice/necessary-care and independent privacy grounds also apply Taft's corrective-policy rule: relief must protect the governed patients and providers unless the State establishes that a narrower remedy fully cures the violation. Six join its application when the predicate is the residual access framework. Challengers retain the initial burden to prove injury and scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The unresolved access and patient-equality questions require further adjudication before a final severability package can be fixed. The stipulation remains governed by its terms pending a prompt reasoned order; partial affirmance does not itself authorize blanket enforcement. The court must identify retained duties, prohibited applications, bound officials and dependent civil, criminal and licensing consequences. Any continuation or modification of protection requires the existing stipulation and ordinary equitable predicates. Taft's application of Anderson requires a remedy matching a proved common-policy injury, while preserving the State's opportunity to establish a complete narrower cure. The court should expedite genuinely material factual work and state the resulting obligations precisely.

- **Greenville:** Applied for independent access review and the recipient/function/scope/use/safeguard privacy rule, permitting justified identified oversight without universal indispensability. Its notice and qualification holdings preserve ascertainable professional duties and objective qualifications while forbidding undefined independent sanctions and arbitrary uncontrolled private licensing consent. Arizona's different duties and safeguards receive their own application.
- **Women's Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft:** Applied for the complete framework, competent individualized necessary-care protection and correction of a proved common-policy violation. Its Ohio-specific statutory and clinical applications remain distinct from Arizona's duties.
- **Klinger:** Applied to require classification-specific equality review and to distinguish actual sex classifications from purpose-based challenges to genuinely neutral administration. The rationality of professional categories cannot absorb the independent patient claim.
- **United States v. Virginia:** Supplies the same demanding important-objective/substantial-relationship standard for actual sex exclusion to the seven Justices joining its application; it supplies no automatic classification of every reproductive regulation.
- **New York v. Burger:** Applied for protected commercial premises and the actual predicates and discretion limits of warrantless inspection in a closely regulated industry.
- **Ferguson:** Supplies the seven-Justice corroborating operation-specific/no-waiver principle; its prosecution facts are distinguished.
- **Herring:** Applied, with Lawall and Greenville, to recipient-specific protection against unjustified onward exposure of intimate medical information.
- **Anderson v. Spear:** Applied through Taft for correction at the scope of a proved common-policy violation, with a narrower complete cure available on the State's showing.

## Law After Decision

The licensing scheme is subject to separate access, equality, search, privacy, notice and private-control requirements. Concrete prospective cost, capacity and delay evidence receives factual adjudication under the complete Casey framework; surviving professional categories and qualification rules remain subject to that independent access review. The patient sex-equality claim receives its own classification-and-purpose inquiry.

The established compulsory-entry command, excessive department/APEX permissions and undefined independent dignity sanctions must be restrained at their unconstitutional scope. Lawful inspections, fitted confidential oversight, the bounded serious-incident reports, definite professional duties and the qualification rule remain available under their governing conditions. Arizona-law severability, the remaining factual proceedings and the operative stipulation determine the resulting enforceable package and protection.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in part.** Kennedy agrees that supported predictions of lost provider capacity, delay and clinical risk require access proceedings concerning protected choice, a substantial practical obstacle and timely necessary care. His Casey, Lawall, Greenville and Taft positions support those protections, including competent individualized evidence of grave mental danger and serious health risk. He would permit reasonable safety regulation outside those boundaries and withholds the additional command to justify every remaining severe or meaningful interference under the complete residual framework. He joins the independent equality, search, recipient-specific privacy, board-report, notice and private-control holdings and effective remedies on the grounds he accepts. His disagreement with the residual tiers does not narrow those separate joins or oppose a necessary-care remand.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** These Justices would sustain Arizona on the constitutional-abortion theory and on the substantive informational-privacy challenges to department and APEX access. Their current Taft position rejects the constitutional abortion and necessary-health predicates and would permit reasonable safety, potential-life and medical-ethics regulation. Their Lawall and Greenville positions likewise reject the asserted substantive source for the privacy framework. They do not rest on a finding that the providers' forecasts are false or that Arizona has adequate recipient and retention controls; the actual Arizona deficiencies differ from Greenville's safeguards.

They join the independent Klinger classification inquiry and patient-equality remand; the traditional protected-premises and Burger grounds against unsupported compulsory entry; the bounded board-report result; the precise dignity-sanction ruling; and rejection of the present uncontrolled-delegation claim. Their prior acceptance of a recognizable clinical best-practices core does not supply an ascertainable criterion for this abstract independent dignity punishment. They also join ordinary severability, removal of dependent consequences and prompt clarification of protection for the independent violations they accept, while opposing an injunction based solely on the abortion and substantive-privacy theories they reject.
