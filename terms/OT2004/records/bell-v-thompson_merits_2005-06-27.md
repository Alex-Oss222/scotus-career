### Abuse of assumed authority to withhold the mandate

**Holding and operative rule:** Even assuming exceptional authority to withhold a mandate after completed certiorari review, a court abuses that authority when it leaves the parties without notice for months after its announced stay ends and then reopens the merits without a justification sufficient for that extraordinary course. The Sixth Circuit's combined unexplained delay, expired announced endpoints, earlier opportunities to consider the psychological material, and ensuing merits amendment require reversal here; nonissuance alone cannot justify indefinite reconsideration.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, controls this rule, its application and the mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 41 establishes when a mandate issues and makes the mandate effective upon issuance. Carlisle v. United States requires inherent judicial authority to operate within governing procedural rules. Calderon v. Thompson protects the finality of completed habeas review, but its specific mandate-recall rule does not directly govern a mandate that never issued. The Court therefore evaluates the exercise of assumed authority at the actual procedural stage.
