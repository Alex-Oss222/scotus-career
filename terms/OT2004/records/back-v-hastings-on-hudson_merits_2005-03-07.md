#### Shared alternative-path comparison

The strongest objection is that parenting concerns are not necessarily sex discrimination and the employee may genuinely have performed poorly. **Reversal or immunity path.** A sex-neutral attendance or performance decision, without evidence of sex-based treatment, would not become unconstitutional because its subject is a parent. The strongest objection to extending that path here is the assumed explicit sex-role cause. No newly recognized right to caregiver accommodation is needed to condemn a recommendation actually based on that cause. The clarity is supplied by the established discrimination boundary, not by the later enactment-specific Hibbs holding. If trial establishes a materially different account, ordinary merits, causation and any properly preserved defense consequences must be determined on those actual findings.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Both holdings and their complete explanations are adopted by all nine Justices in the Opinion of the Court identified above.

### 1. Intentional sex stereotyping can be proved without a male comparator

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employee may prove that officials intentionally denied an employment opportunity because of sex through their sex-role statements and related evidence, without identifying a similarly situated employee of the opposite sex. The employee retains persuasion by a preponderance on intentional discrimination and each defendant's causal responsibility; the indirect proof framework shifts only production of a legitimate explanation, and the whole record must support the claimed discriminatory cause.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an employer cannot turn a general assumption about mothers into a judgment about an individual woman's professional commitment. United States v. Virginia requires attention to the individual rather than unsupported generalizations about women's capacities and roles. Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins identifies the discriminatory work that sex stereotypes can perform in an employment decision. Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs confirms the constitutional significance of government action grounded in assigned family roles.

Back's supported account connects the supervisors' maternal-role predictions to their tenure recommendation. The contrast between earlier favorable evaluations and later criticism supplies additional evidence for a factfinder. A father comparison could help assess the inference, but making it indispensable would defeat an otherwise supported claim merely because the workplace lacked a comparable man. The performance explanation remains a genuine competing account. St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks preserves the employee's ultimate burden, and Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products permits an inference from the whole record without making disbelief of an explanation conclusive. Genuine work requirements applied on equal terms remain lawful. The disputed statements, performance and causal role of each supervisor are therefore matters for trial.

### 2. The alleged intentional discrimination was clearly prohibited when the supervisors acted

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials are not entitled to summary judgment on qualified immunity when the supported plaintiff-favorable account shows that they intentionally denied an employment opportunity because a woman's maternal role made her unsuitable, and law existing when they acted clearly prohibited that sex-role discrimination. They may contest the alleged conduct and motive through ordinary factfinding, but an identical prior tenure case is unnecessary when the application of the existing prohibition is clear.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton require notice of the constitutional prohibition in its concrete setting. Virginia had already rejected unsupported sex-role assumptions before the alleged statements and recommendation in 2000 and 2001. The asserted uncertainty concerns whether the supervisors acted for the alleged reason, not whether that reason was lawful. Framing the claim as a novel right belonging to caregivers obscures its actual premise: intentional discrimination because of sex.

Hope v. Pelzer explains why clear notice can exist without a factual replica, and Brosseau v. Haugen preserves that principle while requiring attention to the actual circumstances. Those later decisions, and Hibbs, describe the current rule; they do not supply notice missing at the time of conduct. The preexisting prohibition itself resolves the legal immunity issue on Back's supported account. Saucier v. Katz leaves the established defense intact without imposing an additional reasonableness defense or a mandatory merits-first sequence.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Virginia is applied to individual employment action grounded in unsupported maternal-role assumptions; its requirement of a genuine important justification substantially related to a sex classification retains force.
- Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins supplies the relevance of sex-stereotyping evidence without converting this constitutional action into a Title VII claim.
- Nevada Department of Human Resources v. Hibbs confirms the constitutional significance of state employment stereotypes; its later date does not establish earlier notice.
- St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks and Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products preserve plaintiff persuasion, employer production and permissible whole-record inferences. Neither makes an actual opposite-sex comparator indispensable.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton continue to govern personal immunity. Hope v. Pelzer and Brosseau v. Haugen explain contextual fair warning and its obvious applications without replacing that defense.
- Saucier v. Katz retains its existing treatment of sequencing and the independent immunity inquiry.

## Law After Decision

Intentional public-employment sex discrimination may be proved through decisionmakers' sex-role statements and other evidence even when the workplace supplies no similarly situated man. Courts must assess the whole record, preserve the employee's burden and examine each official's causal role. On the supported explicit maternal-stereotyping account presented here, conduct-date law defeats personal immunity at summary judgment.
