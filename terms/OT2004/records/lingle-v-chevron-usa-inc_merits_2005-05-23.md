## Controlling holdings

### Regulatory efficacy does not independently establish a taking

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure substantially to advance a legitimate governmental objective is not, by itself, a taking requiring compensation under the Fifth Amendment. A claimant must establish the relevant property burden or demanded dedication under the applicable taking rule; an economic-efficacy trial cannot substitute for that showing, whether the challenged regulation is general or an individual permit denial.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion supplies nine direct joins; the same coalition joins Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** The compensation command addresses the nature and burden of governmental interference with property. An ineffective regulation may impose little loss, while an effective one may take property and require payment. Efficacy cannot distinguish those cases. Loretto identifies a compelled permanent occupation; Lucas addresses complete loss of beneficial use; Penn Central and Tahoe-Sierra examine the continuing property interest and the actual regulatory burden. Nollan and Dolan address the distinct demand to surrender identified property as a condition of permission. None makes ordinary economic disagreement the constitutional event. Chevron's strongest objection is reliance on the real purpose-relationship holding in Del Monte Dunes. Restricting that holding to individual denials would retain the same source error: ineffective means do not become a taking merely because they concern one parcel. Independently unlawful or arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable constitutional protections, including due process, with their own elements and remedies. Compensation is not a license to undertake action forbidden by those independent protections.

### The earlier efficacy holding is overruled at its precise scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Agins and Del Monte Dunes are overruled insofar as they recognize lack of substantial advancement as an independent taking predicate; Del Monte Dunes's allocation of that displaced substantive issue likewise ceases to govern. Its final judgment and award, independent complete-deprivation ground, municipal compensation rules, finality rulings, and constitutional jury entitlement and task allocation for surviving legal claims remain in force.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion expressly changes the earlier controlling rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Del Monte Dunes sustained an undifferentiated award on sufficient evidence supporting both submitted alternatives. Its purpose-relationship ground was an actual holding. The Court therefore confronts and withdraws that ground instead of dismissing it as dictum. Stare decisis carries particular weight where property and transactions are arranged under judicial rules, but it does not justify retaining a liability predicate unrelated to the burden that the compensation command identifies. Preserving final judgments and independent valid grounds respects those interests without perpetuating the mismatch in pending and future cases. Rejecting the substantive efficacy issue also removes the occasion for assigning that issue to a jury; it does not abolish a jury right for an otherwise valid legal damages claim. The replacement rule governs this case and other cases still open on direct review where the issue is properly presented. The completed Del Monte Dunes compensation judgment remains final.

### The surviving property rules govern distinct interferences

**Holding and operative rule:** Compelled permanent physical occupation follows Loretto; total elimination of beneficial use follows Lucas, subject to substantiated background property or nuisance limits already inherent in ownership; other regulatory burdens follow Penn Central's inquiry into economic impact, interference with distinct investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action. For an individualized development-permit condition demanding identified property interests, the government must establish Nollan's essential nexus and demonstrate Dolan's rough proportionality between the dedication's nature and extent and the development's effects, allowing reasonable estimates without exact mathematical equivalence or unique attribution to the project.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion preserves these rules as the operative alternatives to the rejected predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The court must classify the actual interference before choosing its test. Tahoe-Sierra preserves examination of a continuing property interest rather than manufacture of total loss by temporal segmentation. Lucas requires the State to substantiate an antecedent ownership limitation rather than rely on a new legislative harm label. Dolan requires a connection between the nature and extent of demanded property and the development's effects, a distinct burden that ordinary scrutiny of regulatory effectiveness cannot replace. These rules retain their own triggers, proof and consequences.

### Chevron's selected theory cannot sustain the judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment resting solely on failure of substantial advancement must be reversed, and relief based on that predicate must be removed. Chevron's surviving efficacy theory therefore fails; independently preserved claims require their own legal and procedural basis.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion supplies the judgment and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The economic testimony addressed whether Hawaii's cap would achieve its objectives. That inquiry establishes neither the nature nor the magnitude of a taking. Chevron's reviewed theory does not invoke a physical occupation, total deprivation or demanded conveyance. The lower court must reject the chosen predicate and identify any independently preserved matter actually remaining. A distinct due-process or compensation claim is not presented for decision here, and a remand cannot create one by relabeling the failed theory.

## Public Projection

## Event

Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc., No. 04-163. Argued February 22, 2005; decided May 23, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed relief against Hawaii's limit on oil-company rents charged to dealer-operated service stations. Chevron's surviving facial theory alleged that the cap failed substantially to advance the State's objective. The lower courts evaluated competing economic predictions about the cap's effects. The question is whether that efficacy inquiry independently identifies a taking and supports the judgment.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the judgment resting on the substantial-advancement taking theory and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower court must remove the declaration or injunction resting solely on the rejected taking theory and enter judgment consistent with its rejection. Any independently preserved matter must satisfy its own substantive and procedural predicates.

## Holdings

### Regulatory efficacy does not independently establish a taking

**Holding and operative rule:** Failure substantially to advance a legitimate governmental objective is not, by itself, a taking requiring compensation under the Fifth Amendment. A claimant must establish the relevant property burden or demanded dedication under the applicable taking rule; an economic-efficacy trial cannot substitute for that showing, whether the challenged regulation is general or an individual permit denial.

**Authority:** Part I of the unanimous opinion supplies nine direct joins; the same coalition joins Parts II and III.

**Controlling explanation:** The compensation command addresses the nature and burden of governmental interference with property. An ineffective regulation may impose little loss, while an effective one may take property and require payment. Efficacy cannot distinguish those cases. Loretto identifies a compelled permanent occupation; Lucas addresses complete loss of beneficial use; Penn Central and Tahoe-Sierra examine the continuing property interest and the actual regulatory burden. Nollan and Dolan address the distinct demand to surrender identified property as a condition of permission. None makes ordinary economic disagreement the constitutional event. Chevron's strongest objection is reliance on the real purpose-relationship holding in Del Monte Dunes. Restricting that holding to individual denials would retain the same source error: ineffective means do not become a taking merely because they concern one parcel. Independently unlawful or arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable constitutional protections, including due process, with their own elements and remedies. Compensation is not a license to undertake action forbidden by those independent protections.

### The earlier efficacy holding is overruled at its precise scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Agins and Del Monte Dunes are overruled insofar as they recognize lack of substantial advancement as an independent taking predicate; Del Monte Dunes's allocation of that displaced substantive issue likewise ceases to govern. Its final judgment and award, independent complete-deprivation ground, municipal compensation rules, finality rulings, and constitutional jury entitlement and task allocation for surviving legal claims remain in force.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion expressly changes the earlier controlling rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Del Monte Dunes sustained an undifferentiated award on sufficient evidence supporting both submitted alternatives. Its purpose-relationship ground was an actual holding. The Court therefore confronts and withdraws that ground instead of dismissing it as dictum. Stare decisis carries particular weight where property and transactions are arranged under judicial rules, but it does not justify retaining a liability predicate unrelated to the burden that the compensation command identifies. Preserving final judgments and independent valid grounds respects those interests without perpetuating the mismatch in pending and future cases. Rejecting the substantive efficacy issue also removes the occasion for assigning that issue to a jury; it does not abolish a jury right for an otherwise valid legal damages claim. The replacement rule governs this case and other cases still open on direct review where the issue is properly presented. The completed Del Monte Dunes compensation judgment remains final.

### The surviving property rules govern distinct interferences

**Holding and operative rule:** Compelled permanent physical occupation follows Loretto; total elimination of beneficial use follows Lucas, subject to substantiated background property or nuisance limits already inherent in ownership; other regulatory burdens follow Penn Central's inquiry into economic impact, interference with distinct investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action. For an individualized development-permit condition demanding identified property interests, the government must establish Nollan's essential nexus and demonstrate Dolan's rough proportionality between the dedication's nature and extent and the development's effects, allowing reasonable estimates without exact mathematical equivalence or unique attribution to the project.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion preserves these rules as the operative alternatives to the rejected predicate.

**Controlling explanation:** The court must classify the actual interference before choosing its test. Tahoe-Sierra preserves examination of a continuing property interest rather than manufacture of total loss by temporal segmentation. Lucas requires the State to substantiate an antecedent ownership limitation rather than rely on a new legislative harm label. Dolan requires a connection between the nature and extent of demanded property and the development's effects, a distinct burden that ordinary scrutiny of regulatory effectiveness cannot replace. These rules retain their own triggers, proof and consequences.

### Chevron's selected theory cannot sustain the judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** A judgment resting solely on failure of substantial advancement must be reversed, and relief based on that predicate must be removed. Chevron's surviving efficacy theory therefore fails; independently preserved claims require their own legal and procedural basis.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion supplies the judgment and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The economic testimony addressed whether Hawaii's cap would achieve its objectives. That inquiry establishes neither the nature nor the magnitude of a taking. Chevron's reviewed theory does not invoke a physical occupation, total deprivation or demanded conveyance. The lower court must reject the chosen predicate and identify any independently preserved matter actually remaining. A distinct due-process or compensation claim is not presented for decision here, and a remand cannot create one by relabeling the failed theory.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agins v. City of Tiburon is overruled only insofar as lack of substantial advancement supplies an independent taking ground.
- Del Monte Dunes at Monterey, Ltd. v. City of Monterey is overruled at its accepted purpose-relationship alternative and the assignment of that displaced issue. Its final award, independent complete-deprivation holding, municipal compensation route, damages and finality rules, and jury entitlement and allocation for surviving claims remain controlling.
- Loretto v. Teleprompter Manhattan CATV Corp. retains the categorical compelled-permanent-occupation rule.
- Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council retains the total-beneficial-use rule and substantiated background-title limitation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City retains the contextual property-burden inquiry.
- Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency retains its continuing-property treatment and rejection of automatic temporal segmentation.
- Nollan v. California Coastal Commission and Dolan v. City of Tigard retain their distinct nexus and rough-proportionality requirements for demanded property dedications.

## Law After Decision

Regulatory-taking liability can no longer rest solely on a mismatch between a regulation and its asserted objective. That change reaches general rules and particular denials, while the recognized occupation, deprivation, regulatory-burden and dedication rules remain available on their own predicates. Independently arbitrary regulation remains subject to applicable due-process law. The new rule applies in this case and properly presented cases still open on direct review; final compensation judgments retain their finality.
