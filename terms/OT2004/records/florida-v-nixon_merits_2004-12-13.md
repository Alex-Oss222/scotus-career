## Controlling holdings

**Controlling rule.** When capital defense counsel adequately discusses a concession strategy with the defendant, the defendant neither agrees nor objects, and counsel continues meaningful adversarial representation in an actual trial, the absence of express consent alone establishes neither deficient performance nor presumed prejudice. The defendant must prove objectively deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different result under *Strickland v. Washington*, which supplies the ordinary performance-and-prejudice inquiry.

**Authority.** Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all other participating Justices, controls the rule, its silent-client limitation, and the remand.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a disclosed trial strategy must be assessed by what counsel actually did and the trial protections that remained. *Bell v. Cone*, which distinguishes particular choices within substantive advocacy from abandonment of the adversarial role, applies to Corin's continued evidentiary and instruction advocacy and substantial penalty presentation. *United States v. Cronic*, which permits presumed prejudice for recognized complete-denial or complete-failure circumstances, does not turn this concession alone into complete abandonment.

Nixon's argument treats the concession as a guilty plea requiring a personal waiver. *Boykin v. Alabama*, which protects the rights surrendered by a guilty plea, addresses a different legal act. Nixon entered no plea; the prosecution had to prove guilt and the jury had to return its verdict after trial. *Brookhart v. Janis*, which disallows an abbreviated proceeding imposed contrary to the accused's expressed trial choice, likewise differs from this record of consultation and silence. The Court does not resolve a concession over an expressed contrary trial objective, because that question is outside this record and the question decided.

Ordinary review examines the actual investigation, consultation and integrated guilt-and-penalty strategy under trial-era professional standards. Strong prosecution evidence can make a concession intelligible, but neither that evidence nor a strategy label excuses uninformed surrender. *Williams v. Taylor*, which rejects an additional fundamental-unfairness hurdle for lawful-outcome prejudice, leaves the defendant with the ordinary reasonable-probability burden. A court may resolve a properly supported dispositive performance or prejudice prong. Nixon's preserved ordinary allegations, including the challenge concerning unexamined evidentiary inconsistencies, remain for that assessment; the categorical consent rule cannot support the ordered new trial.

## Precedent treatment

- *Strickland v. Washington*, 466 U.S. 668: apply separate reasonable-performance and reasonable-probability-prejudice requirements to the disclosed capital concession strategy; consultation, investigation and professional judgment retain their independent importance.
- *United States v. Cronic*, 466 U.S. 648: preserve recognized presumed-prejudice categories while rejecting the claim that this concession alone constitutes complete adversarial failure.
- *Bell v. Cone*, 535 U.S. 685: apply the distinction between particular challenged choices and substantive abandonment. Its separate federal-habeas review conditions do not govern this direct review of a state judgment.
- *Williams v. Taylor*, 529 U.S. 362: apply ordinary lawful-outcome prejudice without an additional fundamental-unfairness requirement.
- *Boykin v. Alabama*, 395 U.S. 238: preserve personal waiver requirements for an actual guilty plea; a concession in a continuing trial does not itself enter that plea.
- *Brookhart v. Janis*, 384 U.S. 1: distinguish a materially abbreviated proceeding imposed against the accused's expressed trial choice from the disclosed strategy and unresponsive client presented here.

## Resulting law

The ordinary ineffective-assistance framework governs disclosed capital concession strategies when the defendant remains unresponsive and counsel continues meaningful adversarial representation. Absence of affirmative consent supplies no automatic deficiency or prejudice rule. Courts assess the real preparation and advocacy, using professional standards applicable when counsel acted, and order the corrective trial or sentencing proceeding that a proved constitutional defect and the applicable postconviction remedy require.
