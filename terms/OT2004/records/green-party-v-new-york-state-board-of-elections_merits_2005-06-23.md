## Controlling holdings

### I. The substantial enrollment burden lacks the required regulatory fit

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State maintains official affiliation information for political organization as well as primary administration, erasing and refusing the affiliations of organizations with demonstrated statewide ballot support imposes a substantial associational burden. Under the calibrated Anderson–Burdick inquiry, an important state objective must have a demonstrated substantial relationship to that exclusion; the preliminary findings establish a substantial likelihood that New York's categorical exclusion fails that requirement.

**Authority:** Part I receives six direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Its substantial-burden classification and application control without combining them with the Chief Justice's severe-burden rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Burdick requires examination of the actual burden within the electoral system. Cartwright gives independent work to a substantial burden: serious injury need not make political success impossible, but it requires a substantial connection between the restriction and an important objective. The organizations retain nomination, campaigning and other political activity. Their exclusion from a useful continuing official channel nevertheless causes concrete injury to identification, communication and organization.

Norman requires support rules to fit the electoral function at issue, and Timmons requires a real relationship between an associational restriction and the asserted stability interest. A threshold governing primary and statutory party status does not by itself justify destroying or refusing affiliation information. Accurate instructions can distinguish recorded association from primary eligibility.

The State and dissent regard enrollment as a limited, publicly financed primary service whose incidental organizing value need not be extended. That description overlooks how the existing system operates. The State supplies continuing nonprimary advantages to established parties while removing them from organizations with demonstrated statewide support. The challenged erasure contributes insufficiently to administering primaries or avoiding confusion. The same actual-burden-and-fit inquiry resolves the overlapping First and Fourteenth Amendment claims; unequal treatment does not automatically establish severe burden.

### II. Ongoing associational loss warrants the existing interim protection

**Holding and operative rule:** The continuing loss of identification and communication with willing affiliates establishes irreparable associational injury, and the preliminary findings satisfy the substantial-likelihood requirement applied to this affirmative injunction. Effective interim protection must preserve existing affiliations and permit new affiliations for the qualifying organizations while the merits proceed.

**Authority:** Part II has seven direct joins: the six Justices joining Part I and Stone-Zsela. Agreement concerns the need and scope of interim protection, with the Chief Justice relying on his separately stated merits ground.

**Controlling explanation:** A static copy of former lists cannot identify new voters or preserve a continuing channel of political organization. The preliminary record establishes both losses. Preserving only old data would therefore leave part of the continuing injury intact. The findings meet the substantial-likelihood standard the lower court used for affirmative relief. This interlocutory determination preserves effective association during litigation; final merits proceedings remain necessary under the same governing constitutional standard.

### III. Implementation must cure the common exclusion for the qualified category

**Holding and operative rule:** Interim relief retains the Other write-in mechanism with accurate primary-eligibility instructions, continued maintenance and updating for organizations satisfying the established support criterion, best efforts to notify already disenrolled Liberal and Right to Life voters of reenrollment, and equal-form access to Green Party enrollment data. The decree must cure the policy for organizations and voters subject to it who satisfy the support condition, unless the State shows that narrower relief fully cures the same defect; an equally effective administrative arrangement remains permissible under supervision.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices directly join Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** Clingman requires a remedy for the unlawful common policy rather than a limitation justified solely by naming particular plaintiffs. The district court must apply the objective qualification requirement and determine an administrable scope. Continuing data maintenance applies to organizations with sufficient support to place a statewide candidate on the ballot at the most recent gubernatorial election. The form may permit a voter to name any organization without requiring continuing maintenance for every name supplied.

The separate notice and data commands perform distinct work. Best-efforts notice gives already disenrolled Liberal and Right to Life voters an opportunity to reenroll using the new form. The Green Party data order bars publication of its voters as unenrolled and requires the same enrollment data in the same form supplied to statutory parties. Equivalent implementation may replace particular mechanics if it preserves those functions. Existing protection continues while implementation and scope are settled. Separate primary eligibility and full-party requirements remain operative.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice regards the demonstrated loss of affiliation records, organizing opportunities and equal access as severe. Burdick's severe-burden rule therefore requires a narrowly drawn restriction serving a compelling interest. The organizations' continuing ability to campaign does not adequately measure the injury to this established channel of political identity and organization. Erasure does not administer the independent primary threshold, and clear instructions address confusion without destroying the association. He would sustain the injunction under that stricter standard.

He joins the Court's interim-protection, functional-implementation and common-policy remedy holdings. To make that relief effective, he would require concrete form, record and publication compliance dates tied to the next registration cycle, immediate preservation of existing data, and reports showing correction. Any alternative administration must provide equivalent protection, and any narrower relief must fully cure the common injury for the objectively qualified category.

**Justice Scalia, dissenting, joined by Justice Thomas.** The State may define official enrollment as part of an administrable primary system. The dissent accepts that political affiliation is protected and that the lists have real organizing value. It nonetheless regards the added benefits of a publicly financed system as an ordinary burden rather than the substantial restriction the Court finds. The organizations remain able to nominate, campaign and associate, and the State may link its enrollment service to the parties whose primaries it administers.

Burdick permits reasonable regulatory administration and does not require the State to await actual confusion or disruption. The dissent acknowledges that these organizations have demonstrated statewide support and that the challenged erasure removes useful information. Those facts do not, in its view, require continuing state recognition and data services beyond the defined primary structure. It would reverse the interlocutory judgment and dissolve the injunction.

## Public Projection

## Event

Green Party of New York State v. New York State Board of Elections; review of 389 F.3d 411 (Second Circuit).

Merits decision, June 23, 2005. The Second Circuit's November 17, 2004 judgment affirmed preliminary injunctions issued in May and September 2003. The question is whether New York may erase affiliations and exclude future enrollment for organizations that lack statutory party status but demonstrated sufficient support to place statewide candidates on the 2002 ballot.

New York conditions full party status on 50,000 gubernatorial votes. The Green, Liberal and Right to Life organizations lost that status; the Libertarian and Marijuana Reform organizations had not held it. All five nevertheless qualified statewide candidates. The official lists serve organization, contact, mobilization and fundraising as well as primary administration. The plaintiffs challenge the resulting enrollment exclusion and seek continued interim protection. The State seeks dissolution of that protection.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The preliminary protection is affirmed, and the case is remanded for continued proceedings and determination of effective remedial scope.

## Holdings

### I. The substantial enrollment burden lacks the required regulatory fit

**Holding and operative rule:** When a State maintains official affiliation information for political organization as well as primary administration, erasing and refusing the affiliations of organizations with demonstrated statewide ballot support imposes a substantial associational burden. Under the calibrated Anderson–Burdick inquiry, an important state objective must have a demonstrated substantial relationship to that exclusion; the preliminary findings establish a substantial likelihood that New York's categorical exclusion fails that requirement.

**Authority:** Part I receives six direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Its substantial-burden classification and application control without combining them with the Chief Justice's severe-burden rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Burdick requires examination of the actual burden within the electoral system. Cartwright gives independent work to a substantial burden: serious injury need not make political success impossible, but it requires a substantial connection between the restriction and an important objective. The organizations retain nomination, campaigning and other political activity. Their exclusion from a useful continuing official channel nevertheless causes concrete injury to identification, communication and organization.

Norman requires support rules to fit the electoral function at issue, and Timmons requires a real relationship between an associational restriction and the asserted stability interest. A threshold governing primary and statutory party status does not by itself justify destroying or refusing affiliation information. Accurate instructions can distinguish recorded association from primary eligibility.

The State and dissent regard enrollment as a limited, publicly financed primary service whose incidental organizing value need not be extended. That description overlooks how the existing system operates. The State supplies continuing nonprimary advantages to established parties while removing them from organizations with demonstrated statewide support. The challenged erasure contributes insufficiently to administering primaries or avoiding confusion. The same actual-burden-and-fit inquiry resolves the overlapping First and Fourteenth Amendment claims; unequal treatment does not automatically establish severe burden.

### II. Ongoing associational loss warrants the existing interim protection

**Holding and operative rule:** The continuing loss of identification and communication with willing affiliates establishes irreparable associational injury, and the preliminary findings satisfy the substantial-likelihood requirement applied to this affirmative injunction. Effective interim protection must preserve existing affiliations and permit new affiliations for the qualifying organizations while the merits proceed.

**Authority:** Part II has seven direct joins: the six Justices joining Part I and Stone-Zsela. Agreement concerns the need and scope of interim protection, with the Chief Justice relying on his separately stated merits ground.

**Controlling explanation:** A static copy of former lists cannot identify new voters or preserve a continuing channel of political organization. The preliminary record establishes both losses. Preserving only old data would therefore leave part of the continuing injury intact. The findings meet the substantial-likelihood standard the lower court used for affirmative relief. This interlocutory determination preserves effective association during litigation; final merits proceedings remain necessary under the same governing constitutional standard.

### III. Implementation must cure the common exclusion for the qualified category

**Holding and operative rule:** Interim relief retains the Other write-in mechanism with accurate primary-eligibility instructions, continued maintenance and updating for organizations satisfying the established support criterion, best efforts to notify already disenrolled Liberal and Right to Life voters of reenrollment, and equal-form access to Green Party enrollment data. The decree must cure the policy for organizations and voters subject to it who satisfy the support condition, unless the State shows that narrower relief fully cures the same defect; an equally effective administrative arrangement remains permissible under supervision.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices directly join Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** Clingman requires a remedy for the unlawful common policy rather than a limitation justified solely by naming particular plaintiffs. The district court must apply the objective qualification requirement and determine an administrable scope. Continuing data maintenance applies to organizations with sufficient support to place a statewide candidate on the ballot at the most recent gubernatorial election. The form may permit a voter to name any organization without requiring continuing maintenance for every name supplied.

The separate notice and data commands perform distinct work. Best-efforts notice gives already disenrolled Liberal and Right to Life voters an opportunity to reenroll using the new form. The Green Party data order bars publication of its voters as unenrolled and requires the same enrollment data in the same form supplied to statutory parties. Equivalent implementation may replace particular mechanics if it preserves those functions. Existing protection continues while implementation and scope are settled. Separate primary eligibility and full-party requirements remain operative.

## Precedent Treatment

- Burdick v. Takushi supplies the actual-burden inquiry within the complete electoral system.
- Cartwright v. Barnes supplies the substantial-burden requirement of an important objective and a demonstrated substantial relationship to the restriction.
- Norman v. Reed is applied to the connection between a support requirement and the particular electoral function.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party is applied to require an actual connection between a restriction and claimed electoral stability.
- Clingman v. Beaver supplies the common-policy remedy rule and the State's opportunity to show a fully effective narrower cure.
- Belitskus v. Pizzingrilli reinforces examination of actual access burdens; its inability-to-pay application remains distinct.

## Law After Decision

Qualifying political organizations receive protection against the selective loss of ongoing official affiliation information under the substantial-burden standard. States retain objective support criteria and primary-eligibility rules, but must justify the different enrollment restriction by its actual function. The injunction continues while the district court resolves final merits and applies the governing qualification and common-policy remedy requirements.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice regards the demonstrated loss of affiliation records, organizing opportunities and equal access as severe. Burdick's severe-burden rule therefore requires a narrowly drawn restriction serving a compelling interest. The organizations' continuing ability to campaign does not adequately measure the injury to this established channel of political identity and organization. Erasure does not administer the independent primary threshold, and clear instructions address confusion without destroying the association. He would sustain the injunction under that stricter standard.

He joins the Court's interim-protection, functional-implementation and common-policy remedy holdings. To make that relief effective, he would require concrete form, record and publication compliance dates tied to the next registration cycle, immediate preservation of existing data, and reports showing correction. Any alternative administration must provide equivalent protection, and any narrower relief must fully cure the common injury for the objectively qualified category.

**Justice Scalia, dissenting, joined by Justice Thomas.** The State may define official enrollment as part of an administrable primary system. The dissent accepts that political affiliation is protected and that the lists have real organizing value. It nonetheless regards the added benefits of a publicly financed system as an ordinary burden rather than the substantial restriction the Court finds. The organizations remain able to nominate, campaign and associate, and the State may link its enrollment service to the parties whose primaries it administers.

Burdick permits reasonable regulatory administration and does not require the State to await actual confusion or disruption. The dissent acknowledges that these organizations have demonstrated statewide support and that the challenged erasure removes useful information. Those facts do not, in its view, require continuing state recognition and data services beyond the defined primary structure. It would reverse the interlocutory judgment and dissolve the injunction.

The case returns for continued merits proceedings, actual qualification determinations where needed, and a decree implementing the common-policy standard. Current record preservation, enrollment, notice and data-access protection remains in effect during that work.
