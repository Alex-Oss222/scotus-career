#### Components and controlling-rule compatibility

**B1 — requirements and actual duty.** All eight accept that a common-law damages duty can be a requirement under section 136v(b), but displacement requires a duty for labeling or packaging that is different from or additional to the applicable federal obligation. A verdict's possible incentive to change a label does not itself supply that duty. The state duty's actual operation controls, not its caption. Section 136v(a)'s sale/use authority remains subject to its prohibition on authorizing federally prohibited sale/use; it does not erase subsection (b).

**B2a — design, manufacture and testing.** All eight remove the categorical inducement-based barriers to genuine independent design, manufacture and testing duties. A product need not be dangerous in every possible use to support a design claim. Genuine proof and the actual Texas duty remain necessary: an obligation whose substance requires different warnings receives the label-duty comparison. A failure to prove testing, design or manufacturing error is a merits problem, not proof that every such theory is necessarily a warning claim. No newly pleaded failure-to-warn count is invented.

**B2a application — oral representations.** All eight's frozen distinction between genuine nonlabel conduct and label duties also governs actual oral sales statements. Section 136(p)(1) defines a label as written, printed or graphic matter on or attached to the pesticide or device or its containers or wrappers. Section 136(p)(2) defines labeling as all labels and other written, printed or graphic matter accompanying the pesticide or device at any time, or matter referenced on the label or in accompanying literature; the reference branch excepts current official publications of EPA, the Departments of Agriculture, Interior and Health and Human Services, state experiment stations and agricultural colleges, and similar federal or state institutions or agencies legally authorized to conduct pesticide research. An actual duty governing a sales agent's oral representation is therefore outside subsection (b)'s labeling category, even when the words repeat a label statement. That is an application of the already frozen actual-duty and nonlabel grounds, supported by the existing lower-opinion facts and statutory definition, not a new factual premise or new general cause of action. The lower court must identify which theories actually rest on oral statements, label content or a mixture; no claim is declared entirely oral merely because agents made some oral representations. Scalia and Thomas's warranty objection concerns liability imposed on label content and remains fully preserved.

**B2b — voluntary warranty and corresponding DTPA ground.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer hold an enforceable voluntary express promise outside section 136v(b)'s labeling-requirement category, even when the promise appears on the label, because the manufacturer supplies the substantive undertaking. The DTPA survives on that same ground only to the extent it supplies a remedy for that surviving warranty. Scalia and Thomas require remand for actual equivalence insofar as either theory attaches liability to label statements: the voluntary-promise description does not answer their objection that state law may make the manufacturer liable for content that satisfies FIFRA. They accept survival of a genuinely equal duty and reject the inducement test; they do not hold every warranty preempted.

**B3 — fraud, nonwarranty DTPA and the disclaimer.** All eight support remand to compare the actual state labeling duty with section 136(q)(1)(A)'s false-or-misleading-label requirement and any relevant conduct-date regulation. Directions and warnings under subsections (F) and (G) remain distinct federal provisions, not interchangeable exceptions. Equivalent duties can use different words, and additional recovery elements can narrow liability without demanding different conduct. The counterclaim's express disclaimer remains part of the pleadings and receives its proper procedural effect; remand neither deletes it nor grants a right to amend. Scalia and Thomas's own historical opinion recognized that fact and still required substantive comparison. Its strongest objection—that liability cannot rest on federally compliant label content—is met by requiring genuine equivalence, not by foreclosing the comparison the lower judgment omitted.

**B4 — registration and other conflict.** All eight retain the binding Geier/Dowhal distinction between express displacement and independently established actual conflict. Registration is not conclusive immunity: section 136a(f)(2) denies a defense to an offense and supplies prima facie evidence of registration compliance when cancellation proceedings are absent. EPA's efficacy-data waiver does not eliminate federal misbranding duties. A party invoking an independent conflict must identify an operative federal command or supported regulatory method and the incompatible state obligation. The supplied record establishes no affirmative requirement protecting the alleged false efficacy message simply because EPA registered the product. This is the application of current law and a preserved remand limit, not a new holding that all independently pleaded conflicts have been adjudicated. General uniformity aspirations and changed agency litigation advocacy cannot replace the required federal source.

## Event

Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC, No. 03-388; 544 U.S. 431. Argued January 10, 2005; decided April 27, 2005 (2005-04-27). On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, 332 F.3d 323. The question is whether the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act (FIFRA) preempts the farmers' state-law claims that Dow's Strongarm herbicide damaged their peanut crops.

Twenty-nine Texas farmers alleged damage from Strongarm used in alkaline soils. Dow brought a federal declaratory action, and the farmers counterclaimed. The lower courts treated liability likely to induce a label change as sufficient for preemption. The claims at issue concern design, manufacture, testing, representations, express warranties and the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA). The farmers do not press an independent failure-to-warn claim in this Court.

## Holdings

Parts I, II, IV and V of the Court's opinion are unanimous. Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer and controls its warranty and corresponding DTPA holdings.

### 1. Actual duties determine whether FIFRA displaces state law

**Operative rule.** A state statutory or common-law damages duty is preempted under 7 U.S.C. §136v(b) when its actual substantive command concerns pesticide labeling or packaging and is different from or additional to the applicable federal requirement. A manufacturer's possible response to a verdict by changing its label does not itself make the underlying duty a labeling requirement. Part I supplies the controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation.** Cipollone directs attention to the particular statutory clause and the duty on which liability rests. Medtronic establishes that common-law duties can be requirements while a state remedy enforcing equal conduct obligations is not an additional substantive command. FIFRA's text gives both propositions effect. Section 136v(a) permits state regulation of pesticide sale or use only insofar as it does not permit sale or use federal law prohibits; subsection (b) separately governs labeling and packaging. The lower court's inducement test omits those distinct statutory conditions. A manufacturer might choose to change a label after a verdict concerning defective production, but that choice does not establish what state law commanded. Conversely, describing a demand for different warnings as negligence cannot remove it from the clause. The actual duty, rather than a claim's title or an optional business response, supplies the comparison.

### 2. Independent product duties and actual oral sales duties fall outside labeling preemption

**Operative rule.** Genuine state duties governing product design, manufacture, testing or an agent's actual oral representations are outside §136v(b)'s labeling category. Courts must identify the substantive duty for each theory, including theories combining oral statements and written labeling; a demand whose actual content requires different labeling remains subject to the statutory comparison. Part II supplies the controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation.** Design, manufacture and testing can require conduct apart from a label change. A product need not be dangerous in every possible use before its design may be defective, and a failure of proof on a particular defect theory does not transform every such theory into a warning claim. Cipollone's duty-specific method requires examination of the actual state-law obligation.

Sections 136(p)(1) and (2) define a label through written, printed or graphic matter on or attached to the pesticide, device, container or wrapper. Labeling includes labels and other such matter accompanying the product at any time, or referenced on the label or in accompanying literature. The reference branch excepts current official publications of EPA, the Departments of Agriculture, Interior and Health and Human Services, state experiment stations and agricultural colleges, and similar federal or state institutions or agencies legally authorized to conduct pesticide research. An agent's oral statement is outside these written-matter categories even when its words repeat a label. The lower court must identify whether each actual theory rests on oral conduct, label content or both. That classification preserves the statutory distinction while preventing a pleading caption from concealing a demand for different warnings.

### 3. Voluntary express promises retain their state-law enforcement

**Operative rule.** An enforceable express warranty voluntarily undertaken by a manufacturer falls outside §136v(b)'s prohibition of state-imposed labeling requirements, even when the promise appears on the label. A DTPA claim receives the same protection to the extent it supplies a remedy for that surviving warranty. Part III's seven-Justice majority controls both propositions.

**Controlling explanation.** Cipollone distinguishes enforcement of a manufacturer's own undertaking from a state rule dictating label content. The manufacturer chooses the promised performance; state law holds it to that undertaking. The possibility that liability will lead the manufacturer to revise future promises does not change the source of the existing substantive obligation. The opposing view treats liability for a federally compliant label statement as sufficient to require an equivalence comparison. But federal compliance and a voluntary promise answer different questions: compliance establishes satisfaction of the federal command, while warranty liability enforces performance the manufacturer chose to promise. The same reasoning governs a DTPA remedy derived from the surviving warranty. Other DTPA duties receive the separate actual-duty analysis appropriate to their content.

### 4. Equal labeling duties survive, subject to actual comparison and proper pleading

**Operative rule.** State labeling duties survive §136v(b) when they require conduct substantively equivalent to the applicable federal obligations, even if state law uses different words or adds recovery elements that narrow liability. For duties within this comparison, courts must compare the actual state and federal commands and frame jury instructions to prevent recovery for labeling conduct federal law permits. Part III separately governs voluntarily undertaken warranty promises. Part IV supplies the controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation.** Medtronic distinguishes the substantive conduct required from the additional state remedy. Section 136(q)(1)(A)'s prohibition of false or misleading labeling supplies the relevant federal comparator for the fraud and deception theories; the provisions concerning adequate directions in subsection (F) and necessary warnings in subsection (G) remain distinct. Lawfully adopted EPA regulations can specify these obligations within statutory and administrative limits. A general federal command can preempt a genuinely divergent duty; equivalence does not require identical phrasing or a product-specific rule.

The lower courts must compare the fraud and nonwarranty DTPA duties with the applicable federal requirements governing the 2000 conduct. Additional state elements such as reliance may narrow recovery while leaving the required label conduct unchanged. The first amended counterclaim expressly disclaimed a FIFRA violation, App. 192. That disclaimer receives its proper effect under ordinary pleading rules; the remand neither removes it nor grants an entitlement to amend. Its presence does not justify preserving a judgment that substituted inducement for the required substantive comparison. The surviving causes remain state-law causes governed by their own elements.

### 5. Registration retains its statutory effect, and independent conflict requires an operative federal source

**Operative rule.** FIFRA registration is prima facie evidence of compliance with registration provisions when cancellation proceedings are absent, but §136a(f)(2) makes registration no defense to an offense under the Act. Independently of §136v(b), a state duty is displaced when the party asserting preemption identifies an operative federal command or protected regulatory method and establishes an actual incompatible state obligation. Part V supplies the controlling authority.

**Controlling explanation.** Geier preserves an independent conflict inquiry where an actual federal method requires protection; Myrick requires an operative federal source rather than an assumed policy. Dowhal applies that distinction to an established agency warning determination. Here registration alone does not establish such incompatibility. Section 136a(c)(5) permits EPA to waive efficacy data and then register a pesticide without determining that its composition warrants efficacy claims. That waiver does not eliminate the separate misbranding duty. An abstract desire for nationwide uniformity or a changed agency litigation position likewise cannot supply the missing command.

Dow must identify the actual federal source and incompatible state duty for any preserved independent-conflict contention. The record establishes no affirmative federal requirement protecting the alleged false efficacy message merely because the product was registered. Claim classification and the remaining equivalence and conflict applications proceed under these rules, while the abandoned contract challenge and the unappealed limitation-of-remedies ruling retain their effect.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.:** Reaffirmed and applied for clause-specific examination of the actual duty; its distinction between voluntary promises and imposed labeling commands governs the express-warranty issue.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Reaffirmed and applied for common-law inclusion and the protection of genuinely equal substantive duties despite an additional state remedy; the same distinction governs FIFRA's comparison clause.
- **Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.:** Reaffirmed as preserving independently demonstrated conflict with an operative federal command or protected regulatory method after express-clause analysis.
- **Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick:** Applied to require the actual operative federal source for the asserted conflict.
- **Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare:** Its concrete agency-warning conflict is distinguished from registration, efficacy-data waiver and general uniformity advocacy here; its operative-source requirement remains controlling.

## Law After Decision

For pesticide liability claims, courts identify the actual state duty before asking whether it concerns labeling or packaging and differs from federal law. Genuine nonlabel safety and oral-sales duties remain outside the express clause. Enforceable voluntary express warranties, including label promises, retain state-law enforcement; a DTPA remedy shares that protection insofar as it enforces the surviving warranty. Label-based fraud and other DTPA theories remain available when their substantive duties are genuinely parallel, subject to their ordinary elements and proper pleading.

This framework applies across the covered class of pesticide claims. Registration retains its statutory evidentiary effect, and a preserved independently demonstrated conflict with operative federal law can still require displacement. The Texas duties' classification, precise equivalence and pleaded application are work for remand under these governing rules.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The separate opinion accepts the actual-duty rule, the exclusion of genuine nonlabel conduct, the protection of equal label obligations and the remand for fraud and DTPA comparison. Its disagreement concerns label-based express warranties and DTPA theories derived from them. A promise's voluntary origin does not answer whether state law imposes liability for label content that satisfies the federal obligation. Cipollone's recognition that common-law duties can impose requirements and Medtronic's distinction between equal and divergent conduct therefore call for the same federal comparison on those theories. A warranty-based duty may survive if it is genuinely parallel; the two Justices would require that determination instead of categorically excluding label promises from §136v(b). They recognize the pleading disclaimer and would give it its proper procedural effect while conducting the comparison the lower court omitted.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Fifth Circuit for further proceedings consistent with the opinion. The lower courts must classify the actual duties, apply the surviving-warranty rule, compare the remaining labeling duties with the federal obligations applicable to the alleged conduct, and decide any preserved independently supported conflict. They must give the Appendix 192 disclaimer its ordinary procedural effect and apply the state causes' elements. Any jury instructions must confine recovery on the remaining labeling theories subject to federal comparison to substantively equivalent duties. Part III governs the surviving voluntary-warranty and warranty-derived DTPA theories. The abandoned breach-of-contract challenge and the unappealed limitation-of-remedies ruling on the materially different presale representation remain effective.
