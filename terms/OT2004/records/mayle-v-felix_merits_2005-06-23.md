## Controlling holdings

### Relation back requires shared operative facts, not merely the same conviction

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Rule 15(c)(2), an amended habeas ground asserted after limitations expire relates back only when it shares a common core of operative facts set forth or attempted in a timely pleading. A new legal theory, additional detail or fair elaboration of an imperfect pro se account may qualify; the same trial, conviction or requested release alone does not supply the factual connection.

**Authority:** All seven Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt the rule, its stated qualifications and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2242 permits civil-rule amendment, but Rule 15(a)'s permission to amend differs from Rule 15(c)(2)'s effect on an expired limitation. The latter provision links the amendment to conduct or events attempted in the original pleading. Habeas Rule 2(c)'s requirement of supporting facts for each ground gives that link practical content because a prosecution can contain many independent alleged wrongs.

Scarborough permits completion of an already identified timely request. Tiller permits a different legal theory concerning the same identified event. Neither makes an entire criminal judgment the pleaded transaction for every later allegation. Courts must read actual factual allegations fairly, including pro se attempts, rather than demand unchanged legal labels or professional drafting.

The contrary trial-occurrence reading emphasizes that both evidentiary challenges concern one trial and that Rule 15 deliberately accommodates limitations. But the State's possession of the prosecution file is not notice that every act in it is challenged. Treating the whole trial as the occurrence would allow almost any new ground while the first action remains pending, making the effective limitation depend on how long that action lasts. Ordinary amendment controls against prejudice and delay do not perform the separate factual-connection work enacted in Rule 15(c)(2).

Felix's allegedly coerced interrogation and the circumstances of Williams's videotaped testimony are distinct episodes. None of the original grounds attempted the interrogation facts. The amended coercion ground therefore does not relate back. The related assistance theory must be handled under the same rule within the proceedings actually before the lower court. The separately resolved confrontation merits lie outside the grant.

**Operative application:** Timely amendments remain available under the applicable rules. Later amendments sharing the pleaded factual core may relate back even when they change the legal theory. Independent commencement, pendency or equitable grounds require their own established predicates; the remand must enforce the common-core rule and adjudicate any actually preserved independent ground on its own basis.

## Precedent treatment and resulting law

- Scarborough v. Principi is applied to distinguish completing an identified timely matter from adding a different factual wrong; its fee-specific prerequisites do not become habeas rules.
- Tiller v. Atlantic Coast Line Railroad Co. supports changing a legal theory while retaining the same identified factual occurrence.
- Artuz v. Bennett, Duncan v. Walker and Carey v. Saffold retain their distinct rules governing filing and qualifying state pendency.
- Rhines v. Weber retains bounded authority to preserve an existing timely mixed petition; it does not supply relation back for a new factual ground.
- Pliler v. Ford retains its specific judicial-act, causal-loss, diligence and lawful-remedy requirements.
- Pace v. DiGuglielmo and Dodd v. United States retain their separate state-filing and new-right timing holdings.

Post-limitations habeas amendments must be compared with the actual operative events pleaded or attempted on time. Genuine factual elaboration and new legal theories concerning those events may relate back; distinct episodes do not become timely merely because they contributed to the same trial or conviction. The lower court must apply that rule to the added theories within their actual procedural scope and preserve the separate confrontation disposition.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justice Souter.** The dissent treats the trial's use of the challenged evidence as the relevant connected occurrence. Rule 15(c) deliberately permits some amendments despite expiration of limitations; its text does not itself require every habeas ground to be a separate transaction. The State received a timely challenge to the trial's validity, and both the videotape and interrogation theories concern evidence introduced there.

The dissent acknowledges the distinct interrogation facts and does not claim that Felix originally pleaded coercion. It instead reads the transaction broadly in this pending attack on one conviction. Section 2242 provides ordinary civil amendment, and Rule 15(a) allows courts to reject abuse, delay or prejudice. The timely original filing and the different regime governing later actions after final judgment provide additional limits. Those safeguards, in its view, answer the Court's finality concern without narrowing occurrence to each constitutional episode.

That reading also preserves the usefulness of counsel appointed after a prisoner's initial filing: later identification of a trial defect need not be excluded simply because an unrepresented petition used a different legal or factual route. The dissent would affirm the relation-back ruling and permit the remaining proceedings under the ordinary amendment rules.

## Holdings

### Relation back requires shared operative facts, not merely the same conviction

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Rule 15(c)(2), an amended habeas ground asserted after limitations expire relates back only when it shares a common core of operative facts set forth or attempted in a timely pleading. A new legal theory, additional detail or fair elaboration of an imperfect pro se account may qualify; the same trial, conviction or requested release alone does not supply the factual connection.

**Authority:** All seven Justices joining the Court's opinion directly adopt the rule, its stated qualifications and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2242 permits civil-rule amendment, but Rule 15(a)'s permission to amend differs from Rule 15(c)(2)'s effect on an expired limitation. The latter provision links the amendment to conduct or events attempted in the original pleading. Habeas Rule 2(c)'s requirement of supporting facts for each ground gives that link practical content because a prosecution can contain many independent alleged wrongs.

Scarborough permits completion of an already identified timely request. Tiller permits a different legal theory concerning the same identified event. Neither makes an entire criminal judgment the pleaded transaction for every later allegation. Courts must read actual factual allegations fairly, including pro se attempts, rather than demand unchanged legal labels or professional drafting.

The contrary trial-occurrence reading emphasizes that both evidentiary challenges concern one trial and that Rule 15 deliberately accommodates limitations. But the State's possession of the prosecution file is not notice that every act in it is challenged. Treating the whole trial as the occurrence would allow almost any new ground while the first action remains pending, making the effective limitation depend on how long that action lasts. Ordinary amendment controls against prejudice and delay do not perform the separate factual-connection work enacted in Rule 15(c)(2).

Felix's allegedly coerced interrogation and the circumstances of Williams's videotaped testimony are distinct episodes. None of the original grounds attempted the interrogation facts. The amended coercion ground therefore does not relate back. The related assistance theory must be handled under the same rule within the proceedings actually before the lower court. The separately resolved confrontation merits lie outside the grant.

**Operative application:** Timely amendments remain available under the applicable rules. Later amendments sharing the pleaded factual core may relate back even when they change the legal theory. Independent commencement, pendency or equitable grounds require their own established predicates; the remand must enforce the common-core rule and adjudicate any actually preserved independent ground on its own basis.

## Precedent Treatment

- Scarborough v. Principi is applied to distinguish completing an identified timely matter from adding a different factual wrong; its fee-specific prerequisites do not become habeas rules.
- Tiller v. Atlantic Coast Line Railroad Co. supports changing a legal theory while retaining the same identified factual occurrence.
- Artuz v. Bennett, Duncan v. Walker and Carey v. Saffold retain their distinct rules governing filing and qualifying state pendency.
- Rhines v. Weber retains bounded authority to preserve an existing timely mixed petition; it does not supply relation back for a new factual ground.
- Pliler v. Ford retains its specific judicial-act, causal-loss, diligence and lawful-remedy requirements.
- Pace v. DiGuglielmo and Dodd v. United States retain their separate state-filing and new-right timing holdings.

## Law After Decision

Post-limitations habeas amendments must be compared with the actual operative events pleaded or attempted on time. Genuine factual elaboration and new legal theories concerning those events may relate back; distinct episodes do not become timely merely because they contributed to the same trial or conviction. The lower court must apply that rule to the added theories within their actual procedural scope and preserve the separate confrontation disposition.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justice Souter.** The dissent treats the trial's use of the challenged evidence as the relevant connected occurrence. Rule 15(c) deliberately permits some amendments despite expiration of limitations; its text does not itself require every habeas ground to be a separate transaction. The State received a timely challenge to the trial's validity, and both the videotape and interrogation theories concern evidence introduced there.

The dissent acknowledges the distinct interrogation facts and does not claim that Felix originally pleaded coercion. It instead reads the transaction broadly in this pending attack on one conviction. Section 2242 provides ordinary civil amendment, and Rule 15(a) allows courts to reject abuse, delay or prejudice. The timely original filing and the different regime governing later actions after final judgment provide additional limits. Those safeguards, in its view, answer the Court's finality concern without narrowing occurrence to each constitutional episode.

That reading also preserves the usefulness of counsel appointed after a prisoner's initial filing: later identification of a trial defect need not be excluded simply because an unrepresented petition used a different legal or factual route. The dissent would affirm the relation-back ruling and permit the remaining proceedings under the ordinary amendment rules.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit must remove the trial-wide relation-back ground and return the case for proceedings consistent with the common-core requirement. The added coercion claim is time barred absent an independently preserved sufficient basis; the related assistance theory must be handled within its actual procedural scope. The separate confrontation merits judgment remains in force.
