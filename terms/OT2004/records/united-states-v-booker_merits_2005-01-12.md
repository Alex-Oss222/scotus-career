## Controlling holdings

### I. Facts increasing a binding punishment boundary require element protections

**Controlling proposition:** A fact legally necessary to increase a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must receive applicable grand-jury charging protection and be proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt, validly admitted as an element, or determined beyond a reasonable doubt after a lawful jury waiver. This rule governs present-offense and necessary recidivist facts, including those that increase a binding Federal Sentencing Guidelines range; judicial selection within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions remains permissible.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the established punishment-authorization inquiry. Apprendi protects facts that authorize greater punishment. Angle already applies that rule to binding federal Guidelines ceilings and floors, Harris confirms the mandatory-floor component, Gatewood rejects a categorical recidivism exception, and Blakely identifies the range authorized by a verdict or valid plea admission. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner range discretionary. Mistretta sustains the Commission's institutional arrangement, not an exemption from independent constitutional guarantees; Witte and Watts preserve ordinary assessment only within otherwise authorized punishment. The argument that Commission rules permit ordinary judicial enhancement therefore conflicts with the existing rule rather than distinguishing it. Nor does the dissent's concern about judicial administration justify withdrawing safeguards from facts that legally compel greater punishment. An unchallenged assertion in a sentencing report is not by itself a valid element admission. A jury waiver retains the proof burden and the applicable charge boundary. Booker's additional judicial findings raised a 210-to-262-month calculation to 360 months to life and produced a 360-month sentence. Those findings cannot authorize that increase. Fanfan properly refused to use the comparable unsupported findings. The stated ranges describe the sentencing records; other disputed inputs must satisfy their own applicable requirements.

### II. Constitutional applications preserve the binding statutory system

**Controlling proposition:** The Federal Sentencing Guidelines remain binding to the extent their ranges rest on constitutionally established facts; an unsupported enhancement must be withheld rather than used to replace Congress's mandatory system with an advisory one. Section 3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and the enacted review and remand provisions remain effective in their lawful applications, with judicial discretion exercised within the independently authorized range and lawful statutory departures.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for this holding and the mandate in Holding III. Ginsburg joins Part I only.

**Controlling explanation:** Alaska Airlines v. Brock asks whether the enacted remainder can function consistently with Congress's design. Severability can remove unconstitutional applications as well as invalid provisions; the necessary question is what cannot lawfully operate. Here a binding system can operate on facts established with the required protections. Section 3553(b)(1) commands the applicable kind and range of sentence unless an aggravating or mitigating circumstance of a kind or degree not adequately considered by the Commission warrants a different sentence. Adequate consideration is assessed from the Guidelines, policy statements and official commentary. Those departure conditions remain subject to the constitutional authorization rule.

The Government and the remedial dissent emphasize real-conduct sentencing, information available to judges, charging-driven disparities and unequal effects of upward and downward adjustments. Those concerns identify consequences of constitutional safeguards, not an inability of the enacted mandatory command to govern lawful applications. The Court cannot preserve every feature that conflicts with those safeguards, but it can preserve Congress's binding directions and actual appellate-review provisions. Removing both mandatory force and the review structure would substitute a materially different sentencing policy. Angle demonstrated the legal availability of protected factfinding within binding ranges while expressly leaving this system-level remedy open; today's holding answers that reservation. The presumption favoring lawful surviving applications is not overcome by the asserted administrative inconvenience.

### III. The two records require different relief under the retained framework

**Controlling proposition:** Booker's appellate sentence vacatur is affirmed, and his case is remanded for a replacement sentence within a lawfully established binding range, subject to correction of independently established calculation errors. Fanfan's refusal to enhance and resulting 78-month sentence are affirmed against the asserted entitlement to use the excluded findings; any independently preserved calculation issue remains for ordinary disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Booker received the unsupported increased punishment; Fanfan did not. Preserving the binding statute therefore requires correcting Booker's sentence while sustaining Fanfan's refusal to impose the enhancement. Blakely and Gatewood distinguish sentence relief from authority to institute new aggravated proceedings. This mandate supplies no automatic enhancement jury or new charging authority: further proceedings require an independent procedural source and compliance with charging, proof, waiver and finality constraints. Booker's convictions remain intact. Griffith governs application to nonfinal cases on direct review. For a forfeited claim, Olano separately requires error, plainness, an ordinary showing of effect on substantial rights, and a discretionary finding that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. An assumed identical sentence cannot replace those inquiries or make an unauthorized binding range lawful. Existing deadlines, retroactivity rules and collateral-review gateways govern final judgments separately.

- Apprendi: reaffirmed as the punishment-authorization principle for facts that expose a defendant to greater legally binding punishment.
- United States v. Angle: applied as the settled federal binding-ceiling and binding-floor rule; its reserved system-level remedial question is now resolved by retaining lawful mandatory operation.
- Harris v. United States: reaffirmed as requiring applicable element protections for facts raising a mandatory floor; an unchanged outer maximum does not excuse them.
- United States v. Gatewood: reaffirmed as rejecting the categorical recidivism exemption and preserving applicable charging and proof requirements and independent authority for further proceedings.
- Blakely v. Washington: applied to the range authorized by verdict or valid element admission and the distinction between sentence relief and authority for an additional enhancement proceeding.
- Mistretta v. United States: institutional validity is preserved; it supplies no exception to the separate factfinding guarantees.
- Witte v. United States and United States v. Watts: their within-range sentencing assessment remains subject to the binding-range authorization rule.
- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock: applied to preserve lawful statutory applications rather than replace the mandatory system; application severability remains available.
- Griffith v. Kentucky and United States v. Olano: ordinary direct-review application and the four distinct forfeiture requirements remain effective.

## Law After Decision

Federal sentencing continues under binding Guidelines supported by constitutionally established facts. The existing rule covers any necessary present-offense or recidivist fact increasing a binding ceiling or floor, with applicable charging, jury, admission and waiver requirements. The system-level remedy now preserves §3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and the enacted review and remand structure. Unsupported enhancements must be withheld, and judicial choice proceeds within lawful authority. The rule governs applicable nonfinal cases on direct review; final judgments remain subject to their existing collateral-review regime.

## Separate Writings

O'Connor, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, dissents from the constitutional holding. Their earlier Angle positions distinguish the statutory offense limits from rules regulating judicial selection within them. Mistretta's institutional sentencing arrangement and the established judicial sentencing role support reconsidering the extension of element protections to those internal constraints. They would permit the challenged judicial findings, reverse Booker's constitutional ruling and return Fanfan for ordinary mandatory-Guidelines sentencing under that preferred rule. They recognize that Angle governs unless reconsidered; they do not treat the federal question as previously unanswered.

Breyer's separate Part I explains his additional constitutional position. He finds no coherent logical distinction between a fact raising a mandatory minimum and one raising a maximum, yet opposes extending the element requirement to the sentencing system in either form. That disagreement concerns the constitutional allocation itself rather than an exception consistent with Angle or Harris.

In Part II, Breyer is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Given the Court's constitutional holding, they would sever §3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and necessarily incompatible cross-references, preserving guided advisory sentencing. Congress's integrated arrangement relied on judicial assessment of actual conduct and sentencing information beyond charging choices; compulsory ranges rebuilt around jury findings would create disparities and unequal upward and downward operation. They view advisory guidance as the closer surviving statutory design. Courts would remain required to consider the Guidelines and policy statements, offense and offender circumstances, lawful alternatives, seriousness, respect for law, just punishment, deterrence, public protection, effective correctional needs, avoidance of unwarranted disparities and restitution. The sentence would have to be sufficient but no greater than necessary for the applicable statutory purposes, accompanied by reasons and reasonableness review. They would affirm the setting aside of Booker's sentence and resentence him under that system, but vacate Fanfan's sentence for advisory reconsideration even though he suffered no constitutional enhancement error. Ginsburg joins the Court's constitutional rule and this remedial position; the other three retain their constitutional objections.
