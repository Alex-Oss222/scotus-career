## Durable decision kernel

The Third Circuit's judgment reversing dismissal is **affirmed, 9–0**, and the action returns for prospective-enforcement proceedings under the identified assistance rights, the dated statutory definition and the ordinary requirements for breach and lawful relief.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The named beneficiaries' claims proceed against the responsible Secretary; further proceedings determine any class and the proper scope of relief.

### I. The identified assistance and promptness commands create individual rights

**Holding and authority — Part I, joined by all nine Justices.** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and 1396a(a)(8) give eligible beneficiaries individual rights to the covered medical assistance their participating State has undertaken to provide and to that assistance with reasonable promptness. The claimant must identify the adopted benefit and satisfy its governing financial, categorical and clinical conditions. For the intermediate-care benefit here, the operative section 1396d(a) definition concerns payment of part or all of covered costs; paragraph (15) identifies the covered category in conjunction with those commands. Pennsylvania's adoption of the benefit and these plaintiffs' eligibility are accepted at this stage.

The category covers intermediate care outside an institution for mental diseases for persons determined under section 1396a(a)(31) to need it. That provision requires a written care plan before admission or authorization of benefits and regular independent professional review of continuing need. Section 1396d(d) requires a qualifying institution or distinct part with the prescribed health or rehabilitative purpose and standards, and active treatment for the payment sought. Those conditions operate at their statutory stages; an eligible waiting-list claimant need not already receive the allegedly withheld treatment before challenging delay. The public-institution expenditure-maintenance condition by its terms governs quarters before January 1, 1975.

**Controlling explanation.** Blessing asks whether the provision identifies an intended individual beneficiary, imposes a binding obligation and defines a right sufficiently definite for judicial enforcement. These provisions do so: they direct assistance to eligible people and require its reasonably prompt provision. The identified benefit, eligibility and evidence of delay make that qualitative timing requirement judicially manageable. Clinical and fiscal questions inform its application rather than erase it.

The Secretary emphasizes state-plan form and program administration. Suter's rejection of the particular reasonable-efforts and reporting claims establishes no general immunity for plan provisions. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove plan placement alone as a ground of nonenforceability, without creating a right independently or disturbing Suter's preserved section 671(a)(15) result. Wright and Wilder exemplify enforceable, defined benefit obligations rather than a general promise of program success. Gonzaga rejects the particular FERPA entitlement; its four-Justice general reformulation does not displace Blessing.

The dated payment definition fixes the obligation's scope. It reaches covered care provided in or after the third month before the application month; its distinct qualified-Medicare-beneficiary cost-sharing rule begins after the month that status is acquired. The pertinent benefit remains subject to the actual statutory categories and conditions. The command and definition together supply the personal right. The State's accepted adoption and the plaintiffs' accepted eligibility make the claimed assistance concrete without turning every statutory definition into an independent entitlement.

### II. Medicaid's hearing and funding mechanisms do not foreclose this section 1983 action

**Holding and authority — Part II, joined by all nine Justices.** The personal assistance rights recognized in Part I are enforceable through section 1983. The Secretary has shown neither an express exclusion nor a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with that route. Section 1396a(a)(3)'s individual fair hearing and section 1396c's federal funding supervision, considered together, do not establish the asserted foreclosure.

**Controlling explanation.** Blessing separates an individual right from Congress's displacement of section 1983. Once the right is identified, the defendant must establish the asserted exclusion or actual incompatibility. Section 1396a(a)(3) gives an individual whose assistance claim is denied or not acted on promptly an opportunity for a state-agency fair hearing. That personal corrective remedy deserves consideration in its own right. Section 1396c separately requires reasonable notice and an opportunity for hearing before the Secretary finds that a changed state plan no longer complies with section 1396a or that administration fails substantially to comply with its provisions. Upon that finding, the Secretary must notify the state agency and withhold further payments until satisfied that the failure will no longer occur; the Secretary may instead limit payments to unaffected categories or parts. That aggregate enforcement measure does not replace an individual's timing entitlement.

Rancho distinguishes a specific personal judicial action, its thirty-day filing period and expedited adjudication, whose restrictions a parallel section 1983 action would bypass. Here, neither the fair hearing nor funding supervision, alone or together, identifies a comparable incompatibility or another congressional remedial restriction this action would evade. Their presence and detail do not establish exclusivity. The inquiry concerns the actual combined design, not a rule that administrative remedies can never displace section 1983. Wright, Wilder, Blessing and Bruneau preserve that distinction between particular rights, funding administration and an incompatible remedy. The State's comprehensiveness argument therefore fails on this statutory structure.

### III. Prospective enforcement proceeds through findings and effective lawful relief

**Holding and authority — Part III, joined by all nine Justices.** The Third Circuit's reversal of dismissal is affirmed. The named beneficiaries may proceed for prospective enforcement against the responsible Secretary. The District Court must determine the assistance owed, unreasonable delay, causation, official responsibility and lawful means of compliance, and adjudicate any proposed representative scope under Rule 23. If a violation is proved, an injunction must identify the lawful assistance, compliance steps and reasonable deadlines supported by the findings, with reporting adequate to test performance and enforcement upon noncompliance.

**Controlling explanation.** Blessing requires the particular right, breach and remedy to remain distinct, and the prospective-officer framework permits effective enforcement of the federal obligation against the responsible official. Accepted eligibility and alleged years of waiting permit adjudication; they do not supply the missing findings about the assistance withheld, cause of delay or lawful compliance arrangement. The District Court must develop those matters and decide the request for prospective relief.

The Third Circuit expressly reserved whether this dated obligation requires financing rather than furnishing services, and the precise compliance means. That legal question remains open because the operative assistance and remedial facts are undeveloped. The payment-based definition governs its resolution; neither nominal willingness to pay nor an assumed general service entitlement resolves the actual duty. Budgetary administration cannot erase the entitlement, but relief must remain authorized by the operative statute.

The named claims proceed. Broader relief requires a properly established class and common unlawful practice. If proved, the remedy must cure that practice for those it governs unless a narrower cure is demonstrated. Findings must support the assistance obligations and reasonable timetable, so the remand produces a decision on workable compliance rather than an indefinite placeholder.

### Material precedent treatment

- **Blessing v. Freestone, April 21, 1997:** applies its provision-specific rights inquiry, separate presumption of section 1983 enforcement and requirement of actual congressional incompatibility. Its distinction between personal assistance and aggregate administration governs both the rights and remedy analysis.
- **Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418:** applies its recognition of a defined individual benefit obligation and its separate foreclosure inquiry; a program's administrative structure alone does not defeat the identified right.
- **Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498:** applies its specific Medicaid-obligation and enforcement principles, without converting every administrative requirement into a beneficiary right.
- **Suter v. Artist M., March 25, 1992:** preserves its particular reasonable-efforts, reporting and implied-action rulings. The state-plan form is not a categorical bar; sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 retain their enacted correction and express preservation of the section 671(a)(15) result.
- **Gonzaga University v. Doe, June 20, 2002:** preserves its rejection of the asserted FERPA entitlement and its fractured authority. The four-Justice general rights formulation supplies no controlling replacement for Blessing.
- **Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District, June 3, 1999:** preserves the distinction between funding administration and an actually incompatible remedy; that distinction informs the combined Medicaid-mechanism analysis.
- **City of Rancho Palos Verdes v. Abrams, March 22, 2005:** applies its separate rights and channel inquiries and distinguishes the specific personal judicial action, thirty-day filing period and expedited adjudication that supported foreclosure there. The holding depends on the concrete statutory structure rather than the mere existence of an express remedy.
- **Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123:** supplies the prospective-officer framework for enforcement of a proved ongoing federal obligation against the responsible Secretary; relief must fit that obligation and official responsibility.

### Resulting law and procedure

Eligible beneficiaries of the adopted intermediate-care benefit possess individual rights to the covered payment-based medical assistance and its provision with reasonable promptness under sections 1396a(a)(10) and (8), read with the operative section 1396d definition and conditions. Section 1983 supplies an enforcement channel because the combined Medicaid hearing and funding mechanisms do not establish congressional incompatibility. Courts must identify the benefit and eligibility, apply the definition in force when performance was required, and fit effective prospective relief to proved delay and official responsibility. The financing-versus-furnishing question remains for resolution on the developed remedial record under that dated definition.

The action returns for adjudication of the named beneficiaries' claims. The District Court must identify the operative benefits and waiting-list decisions; develop the timing, causes of delay, official responsibility and lawful compliance options; and decide the request for prospective relief. It must resolve the reserved financing-versus-furnishing issue under the dated payment definition and the developed record. Any representative relief requires the Rule 23 determinations and proof of a common unlawful practice. An injunction following proof must set measurable lawful assistance obligations, reasonable deadlines and adequate reporting, and must be enforced if the Secretary fails to perform. Its reach must cure the established violation, including a proved common practice for those governed by it unless a narrower cure is demonstrated.

#### Common legal and factual premises

Blessing, April 21, 1997, unanimously requires a particular intended beneficiary, a binding duty, and an individual entitlement sufficiently definite for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone is inadequate. Qualitative terms are not categorically unmanageable. State-plan location alone does not defeat a right; sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 also do not create one. A proven right is presumptively enforceable under section 1983, subject to express exclusion or a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial arrangement. Defendant, violation, causation and relief remain separate inquiries.

Gonzaga, June 20, 2002, supplies a six-Justice FERPA-specific no-right result. The four-Justice lead opinion has no controlling new general rights formula. Neither that opinion nor the Breyer-Souter concurrence categorically excludes funding statutes, makes an independently implied action necessary, or automatically displaces section 1983 through agency oversight. Bruneau, June 3, 1999, likewise distinguishes an established personal statutory right from its enforcement channel. No historical version of these cases replaces their supplied public law.

The present statutory commands identify medical assistance, eligible persons, and prompt performance. The definition then supplies a specified category of covered costs. Pennsylvania's adoption of the intermediate-care benefit and these plaintiffs' eligibility are accepted at this stage. The category is optional at the plan-selection level; this analysis does not require every State to adopt it or suspend its conditions. Applicable conditions include a qualifying facility other than an institution for mental diseases, the need determination connected to section 1396a(a)(31), a written plan before admission or authorization of benefits, periodic independent review, and section 1396d(d)'s facility and active-treatment requirements. Accepted eligibility is not reopened by inventing a new factual contest about those conditions.

Medical assistance at the relevant time means payment of part or all of covered costs. Recognition of a financing entitlement does not settle whether, on an actual violation record, effective prospective compliance requires an arrangement beyond merely announcing that payment is available. Conversely, the practical need for care does not itself authorize an order creating beds, choosing a facility, or directing unlimited home services. The Court can answer the pleaded enforceability questions fully and leave the distinct reserved remedy issue to the District Court.

The strongest common adverse path is to characterize the cited provisions as directions for a federally approved financing plan, with undefined timing and specialized clinical conditions that leave only a federal-state compliance obligation. A second adverse path accepts personal rights but treats the fair hearing and federal funding mechanisms together as an intentionally exclusive remedial system. Both require close statutory analysis; neither follows merely from the Spending Clause or from the absence of an additional implied action. A narrower adverse conclusion concerning an asserted right to immediate physical placement would not itself justify dismissing the actual assistance claim.

####### Issue 1: The individual entitlement in the operative Medicaid provisions

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Blessing v. Freestone requires intended beneficiaries, a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement. ####### Issue 2: Enforcement channel and workable prospective relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Blessing presumes section 1983 enforcement once a particular right is shown, unless Congress expressly excludes it or provides an incompatible comprehensive remedial scheme. * **Other Governing Law:** Section 1396c federal funding oversight; section 1396a(a)(3) state fair-hearing mechanism; section 1983. No express exclusion of the asserted federal action is identified. * **Material Other Authority:** The separate direct action in the Telecommunications Act illustrates why statutory structures must be compared in detail. This case supplies no comparable thirty-day exclusive judicial track or remedial design whose operation a section 1983 action would displace.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law rejects categorical foreclosure based on agency oversight. Recognizing an enforcement channel still does not award immediate placement or allow a federal court to administer the whole Medicaid program without a proved need.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual Medicaid mechanisms rebut the presumption and what proceedings are necessary to produce effective, legally bounded relief for proved unreasonable delay.

###### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The District Court treated the statutes as insufficiently individual after Gonzaga. The Third Circuit emphasized commands benefiting eligible persons and found no exclusive remedial scheme. The Secretary stresses spending-program structure, federal funding oversight, state hearings and limits on the meaning of medical assistance. The beneficiaries identify their conceded eligibility, the specified covered category and years of delay. Their strongest position is provision-specific assistance with reasonable promptness, not a right to judicial redesign of all disability services. * **Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can recognize the particular assistance and timing rights while distinguishing them from aggregate administration and later statutory language. ## Holdings

### I. The identified assistance and promptness commands create individual rights

**Holding and authority — Part I, joined by all nine Justices.** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and 1396a(a)(8) give eligible beneficiaries individual rights to the covered medical assistance their participating State has undertaken to provide and to that assistance with reasonable promptness. The claimant must identify the adopted benefit and satisfy its governing financial, categorical and clinical conditions. For the intermediate-care benefit here, the operative section 1396d(a) definition concerns payment of part or all of covered costs; paragraph (15) identifies the covered category in conjunction with those commands. Pennsylvania's adoption of the benefit and these plaintiffs' eligibility are accepted at this stage.

The category covers intermediate care outside an institution for mental diseases for persons determined under section 1396a(a)(31) to need it. That provision requires a written care plan before admission or authorization of benefits and regular independent professional review of continuing need. Section 1396d(d) requires a qualifying institution or distinct part with the prescribed health or rehabilitative purpose and standards, and active treatment for the payment sought. Those conditions operate at their statutory stages; an eligible waiting-list claimant need not already receive the allegedly withheld treatment before challenging delay. The public-institution expenditure-maintenance condition by its terms governs quarters before January 1, 1975.

**Controlling explanation.** Blessing asks whether the provision identifies an intended individual beneficiary, imposes a binding obligation and defines a right sufficiently definite for judicial enforcement. These provisions do so: they direct assistance to eligible people and require its reasonably prompt provision. The identified benefit, eligibility and evidence of delay make that qualitative timing requirement judicially manageable. Clinical and fiscal questions inform its application rather than erase it.

The Secretary emphasizes state-plan form and program administration. Suter's rejection of the particular reasonable-efforts and reporting claims establishes no general immunity for plan provisions. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove plan placement alone as a ground of nonenforceability, without creating a right independently or disturbing Suter's preserved section 671(a)(15) result. Wright and Wilder exemplify enforceable, defined benefit obligations rather than a general promise of program success. Gonzaga rejects the particular FERPA entitlement; its four-Justice general reformulation does not displace Blessing.

The dated payment definition fixes the obligation's scope. It reaches covered care provided in or after the third month before the application month; its distinct qualified-Medicare-beneficiary cost-sharing rule begins after the month that status is acquired. The pertinent benefit remains subject to the actual statutory categories and conditions. The command and definition together supply the personal right. The State's accepted adoption and the plaintiffs' accepted eligibility make the claimed assistance concrete without turning every statutory definition into an independent entitlement.

### II. Medicaid's hearing and funding mechanisms do not foreclose this section 1983 action

**Holding and authority — Part II, joined by all nine Justices.** The personal assistance rights recognized in Part I are enforceable through section 1983. The Secretary has shown neither an express exclusion nor a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with that route. Section 1396a(a)(3)'s individual fair hearing and section 1396c's federal funding supervision, considered together, do not establish the asserted foreclosure.

**Controlling explanation.** Blessing separates an individual right from Congress's displacement of section 1983. Once the right is identified, the defendant must establish the asserted exclusion or actual incompatibility. Section 1396a(a)(3) gives an individual whose assistance claim is denied or not acted on promptly an opportunity for a state-agency fair hearing. That personal corrective remedy deserves consideration in its own right. Section 1396c separately requires reasonable notice and an opportunity for hearing before the Secretary finds that a changed state plan no longer complies with section 1396a or that administration fails substantially to comply with its provisions. Upon that finding, the Secretary must notify the state agency and withhold further payments until satisfied that the failure will no longer occur; the Secretary may instead limit payments to unaffected categories or parts. That aggregate enforcement measure does not replace an individual's timing entitlement.

Rancho distinguishes a specific personal judicial action, its thirty-day filing period and expedited adjudication, whose restrictions a parallel section 1983 action would bypass. Here, neither the fair hearing nor funding supervision, alone or together, identifies a comparable incompatibility or another congressional remedial restriction this action would evade. Their presence and detail do not establish exclusivity. The inquiry concerns the actual combined design, not a rule that administrative remedies can never displace section 1983. Wright, Wilder, Blessing and Bruneau preserve that distinction between particular rights, funding administration and an incompatible remedy. The State's comprehensiveness argument therefore fails on this statutory structure.

### III. Prospective enforcement proceeds through findings and effective lawful relief

**Holding and authority — Part III, joined by all nine Justices.** The Third Circuit's reversal of dismissal is affirmed. The named beneficiaries may proceed for prospective enforcement against the responsible Secretary. The District Court must determine the assistance owed, unreasonable delay, causation, official responsibility and lawful means of compliance, and adjudicate any proposed representative scope under Rule 23. If a violation is proved, an injunction must identify the lawful assistance, compliance steps and reasonable deadlines supported by the findings, with reporting adequate to test performance and enforcement upon noncompliance.

**Controlling explanation.** Blessing requires the particular right, breach and remedy to remain distinct, and the prospective-officer framework permits effective enforcement of the federal obligation against the responsible official. Accepted eligibility and alleged years of waiting permit adjudication; they do not supply the missing findings about the assistance withheld, cause of delay or lawful compliance arrangement. The District Court must develop those matters and decide the request for prospective relief.

The Third Circuit expressly reserved whether this dated obligation requires financing rather than furnishing services, and the precise compliance means. That legal question remains open because the operative assistance and remedial facts are undeveloped. The payment-based definition governs its resolution; neither nominal willingness to pay nor an assumed general service entitlement resolves the actual duty. Budgetary administration cannot erase the entitlement, but relief must remain authorized by the operative statute.

The named claims proceed. Broader relief requires a properly established class and common unlawful practice. If proved, the remedy must cure that practice for those it governs unless a narrower cure is demonstrated. Findings must support the assistance obligations and reasonable timetable, so the remand produces a decision on workable compliance rather than an indefinite placeholder.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Blessing v. Freestone, April 21, 1997:** applies its provision-specific rights inquiry, separate presumption of section 1983 enforcement and requirement of actual congressional incompatibility. Its distinction between personal assistance and aggregate administration governs both the rights and remedy analysis.
- **Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418:** applies its recognition of a defined individual benefit obligation and its separate foreclosure inquiry; a program's administrative structure alone does not defeat the identified right.
- **Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498:** applies its specific Medicaid-obligation and enforcement principles, without converting every administrative requirement into a beneficiary right.
- **Suter v. Artist M., March 25, 1992:** preserves its particular reasonable-efforts, reporting and implied-action rulings. The state-plan form is not a categorical bar; sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 retain their enacted correction and express preservation of the section 671(a)(15) result.
- **Gonzaga University v. Doe, June 20, 2002:** preserves its rejection of the asserted FERPA entitlement and its fractured authority. The four-Justice general rights formulation supplies no controlling replacement for Blessing.
- **Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District, June 3, 1999:** preserves the distinction between funding administration and an actually incompatible remedy; that distinction informs the combined Medicaid-mechanism analysis.
- **City of Rancho Palos Verdes v. Abrams, March 22, 2005:** applies its separate rights and channel inquiries and distinguishes the specific personal judicial action, thirty-day filing period and expedited adjudication that supported foreclosure there. The holding depends on the concrete statutory structure rather than the mere existence of an express remedy.
- **Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123:** supplies the prospective-officer framework for enforcement of a proved ongoing federal obligation against the responsible Secretary; relief must fit that obligation and official responsibility.

## Law After Decision

Eligible beneficiaries of the adopted intermediate-care benefit possess individual rights to the covered payment-based medical assistance and its provision with reasonable promptness under sections 1396a(a)(10) and (8), read with the operative section 1396d definition and conditions. Section 1983 supplies an enforcement channel because the combined Medicaid hearing and funding mechanisms do not establish congressional incompatibility. Courts must identify the benefit and eligibility, apply the definition in force when performance was required, and fit effective prospective relief to proved delay and official responsibility. The financing-versus-furnishing question remains for resolution on the developed remedial record under that dated definition.
