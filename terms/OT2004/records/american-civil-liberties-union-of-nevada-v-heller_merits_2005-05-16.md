#### Complete statutory and record boundaries

Section 294A.320(1) makes it unlawful for a person to publish any material or information relating to an election, candidate or ballot question unless the publication contains **the name and mailing or street address of each person who paid or is responsible for paying for it**, and a statement that each such person paid or is responsible. The rule identifies payment responsibility, not automatically every member, author or distributor. The address alternative does not invariably demand a home address. Identification of a payer rather than every affiliate still creates an actual expressive and associational burden requiring its governing justification.

The separate qualifications are retained in their own terms:

- Subsection (2)(a) excludes a candidate or that candidate's political party paying or responsible for paying for a billboard, sign or other advertisement that refers only to that candidate and prominently displays the candidate's name.
- Subsection (2)(b) excludes material expressly approved and paid for by the candidate when the preparation and publication cost has been reported by the candidate as a campaign contribution under §294A.120. Candidate approval alone is insufficient.
- Subsection (2)(c) excludes a natural person acting independently and neither cooperating with nor acting at the direction of a business or social organization, nongovernmental legal entity or governmental entity. The record presents the burdens on cooperative expression; the exception does not establish organizational anonymity.

## Event

American Civil Liberties Union of Nevada v. Heller; Ninth Circuit No. 01-15462, 378 F.3d 979. Plenary merits decision, May 16, 2005 (2005-05-16), after full merits submission. The Court reviews the Ninth Circuit's August 6, 2004 judgment setting aside the district court's validation of Nevada's identification law and directing proceedings under a facial-invalidation holding. The question is whether a State may require organizations financing printed communications about candidates or ballot questions to identify sponsors and addresses on the communication while exempting independently acting individuals.

Nevada Revised Statutes §294A.320(1) requires the name and mailing or street address of each person paying or responsible for paying for material or information relating to an election, candidate or ballot question, together with a statement of that payment responsibility. The statute's qualifications are distinct:

- Subsection (2)(a) exempts a billboard, sign or other advertisement paid for, or the payment responsibility for which is borne, by a candidate or that candidate's political party when it refers only to that candidate and prominently displays the candidate's name.
- Subsection (2)(b) exempts material expressly approved and paid for by the candidate when the candidate has reported its preparation and publication cost as a campaign contribution under §294A.120.
- Subsection (2)(c) exempts a natural person acting independently, neither cooperating with nor acting at the direction of a business or social organization, nongovernmental legal entity or governmental entity.
- Subsection (3) treats identification satisfying the Communications Act of 1934 and its regulations as sufficient compliance.

Material includes printed or written matter and photographs. Publication includes printing, posting, broadcasting, mailing or otherwise disseminating to the public, and causing those acts. This dispute concerns the presented printed communications. The statute contains no general expenditure floor or pre-election window.

## Holdings

### 1. Concrete intended publications support review of the statute's actual coverage

**Rule and authority.** A speaker with concrete plans to publish material covered by this election-identification statute may seek prospective protection on a supported threat of enforcement without waiting for prosecution. The statute must be assessed at its actual information-related scope; the offered construction replacing that scope with express advocacy is unavailable. All nine Justices join Part I and its controlling explanation.

**Controlling explanation.** The supplemented allegations and affidavits identify intended anonymous publications about local ballot matters, public-comment rules and redistricting information. Together with the reported prior prosecution, they establish a concrete self-censorship setting. The claim thus concerns actual intended speech. The statute covers information relating to elections, candidates and ballot questions, with the distinct exceptions stated above. Replacing that category with express advocacy would rewrite its reach. Certifying that proposed rewriting would not supply a permissible construction or justify delaying the federal inquiry. Payment responsibility identifies the person to whom the duty attaches, and the text permits a mailing or street address; the constitutional inquiry must evaluate that actual exposure.

### 2. Organizational sponsor identification requires a material justification

**Rule and authority.** A State may require factual identification of an organizational financer of electoral communication when it establishes a material electoral-information or source-tracing connection, subject to protection for substantiated associational injury. Government must justify the actual information and exposure demanded; a claimant seeking protective relief must substantiate the asserted injury, and government retains the burden of justifying remaining exposure. Part II is supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation.** McIntyre protects ordinary independently produced individual political leaflets and reserves organizational campaign-finance settings. Stengel addresses that distinct setting by sustaining payer name-and-address identification for the presented organized paid candidate advocacy. McConnell sustains the challenged extension of sponsor identification beyond express advocacy where its material informational or source-tracing relation is established. Together, those decisions permit justified on-message attribution while requiring examination of its real scope and burden.

The Ninth Circuit gave inadequate weight to the established legitimate field when it discounted organizational payer information and treated placement on the message, as opposed to a financial report, as determinative. Factual identification can help voters understand who finances electoral expression and assist source tracing even if a violator might conceal the truth. McConnell's separate $10,000 financial-reporting threshold does not migrate into the sponsorship duty. An expenditure floor, election window or set of express-advocacy words is not universally required; each bears on the actual connection and burden.

The individual exception preserves McIntyre's protected setting rather than establishing a categorical exemption for organizational financing. At the same time, modest cooperation does not extinguish every anonymity interest. Small-group leaflets and neutral information remote from a campaign require their own fit assessment; a collective label cannot establish justification. The address alternative and each statutory exception must be considered as written. NAACP v. Alabama and Brown v. Socialist Workers supply protection for substantiated associational injury, with justification still required for the exposure that remains.

### 3. Facial invalidity and claimant protection require application of that rule

**Rule and authority.** The lower court must compare established unjustified applications with the statute's legitimate reach before determining substantial overbreadth, and must decide supported prospective protection for the named claimants under the governing material-connection and associational-injury rules. Relief must follow the violation established and lawful severability. Part III is supported by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation.** The statute reaches ordinary information, small cooperative expenditures and speech outside a campaign period. Those are real features to assess against the valid financed-electoral applications established by Stengel and McConnell. The dissenters consider the existing excess sufficient for facial invalidity. But the appellate analysis discounted legitimate sponsorship applications rather than applying their governing force to that comparison. Its ultimate facial conclusion therefore requires reconsideration under the correct rule.

The remand leaves that conclusion genuinely open. The court must proceed promptly on the existing record, seek only genuinely necessary additional material, and resolve the asserted broad-coverage defect before final relief. It must distinguish information needed to identify an actual financer from unrelated association records. Ordinary equitable standards govern any appropriate interim protection. The court must assess the existing record under the governing legal standard.

## Precedent Treatment

- **McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission:** Preserved at its ordinary individual-leaflet scope; its reserved organizational-finance setting is assessed under the later attribution decisions.
- **Kentucky Right to Life v. Stengel, Part IV:** Applied for the validity of materially justified payer name-and-address identification in the presented organized candidate-advertising setting.
- **McConnell v. Federal Election Commission, sponsor identification:** Applied for justified identification beyond express advocacy and for keeping that duty's trigger separate from financial-reporting thresholds.
- **North Carolina Right to Life, Inc. v. Bartlett:** Its distinct rules for expenditure restrictions and full committee status do not automatically define the trigger for sponsor attribution.
- **NAACP v. Alabama and Brown v. Socialist Workers:** Applied for protection upon a substantiated associational-injury showing, with government justification of remaining exposure.

## Law After Decision

The governing rule applies to organizational electoral financing as a class: factual payer identification may be required when the State establishes the requisite material electoral-information or source-tracing connection and honors supported associational protection. Courts must examine the particular information, publication class and exposure demanded. Ordinary individual-leaflet protection and materially justified organized attribution coexist. Reporting and sponsorship remain distinct duties with distinct triggers. The facial and claimant-specific consequences of Nevada's broader statute await application of this settled standard on remand.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** These Justices join Part I's standing and actual-coverage holdings. Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, accepts that Stengel and McConnell sustain a real field of paid electoral sponsor identification, including justified address information and some communications beyond express advocacy. The dissent nevertheless finds Nevada's actual coverage substantially excessive on the existing record. The law reaches neutral election information and ordinary cooperative leaflets throughout the calendar, so pooling a small expenditure exposes expression that an independently acting individual may publish anonymously. The State's offered source-tracing and information rationales do not justify that extensive reach merely because some candidate advertising may validly be identified. Correcting the appellate court's doctrinal description therefore does not require another facial merits inquiry. Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm facial invalidity and proceed to lawful severability and a conforming prospective decree, preserving valid attribution precedents and separate reporting duties.

**Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Thomas joins Part I's standing and actual-coverage holdings. Thomas relies on the historical protection of anonymous issue pamphlets developed in his McIntyre concurrence and maintained in his Stengel and McConnell positions. Ordinary issue expression retains that protection when people cooperate. Acceptance of external campaign reports and definite candidate-finance attribution does not justify compelled identification across Nevada's broad printed issue-information category. Because that protected reach is substantial and the offered express-advocacy rewriting cannot remove it, Thomas would affirm facial invalidity. His ground is distinct from adopting the complete modern overbreadth-balancing explanation; it permits genuine candidate-finance attribution and does not depend on a mandatory dollar floor.
