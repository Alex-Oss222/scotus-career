## Holdings

**Common authority:** Both holdings and the remand instructions are part of the unanimous Opinion of the Court identified in Opinion Topology. All nine Justices join them at the stated level of generality.

### I. A judgment about one servitude does not merge distinct property claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Federal common law governs the preclusive effect of the earlier federal-question judgment. A final title judgment binds the claim it adjudicated, including grounds that could have been advanced within that claim, but distinct servitudes with different acquisition and use histories do not become the same claim merely because they share owners, related conveyances or a legal premise; the party invoking preclusion must establish the required identity from the actual instruments and adjudication.

**Controlling explanation:** Nevada v. United States requires attention to the same property right before the conclusive effect of a title judgment can control a later dispute. The 96 servitudes here arose through six mineral conveyances and eleven federal land acquisitions. Nebo Oil's operative decree concerns the servitude on approximately 800 acres described in its complaint. Its broader findings do not extend that decree to the other 95 servitudes. The United States remains bound as to the adjudicated property.

Petro-Hunt's strongest argument treats the related acquisitions as one connected transaction and the earlier ruling as settling a common right to retain the minerals. That connection does not eliminate the distinct property rights or their individual creation and use histories. One dispute concerning the original servitude did not adjudicate every other servitude held through the same predecessors. Semtek requires examination of the source and actual effect of a federal judgment, while its ordinary state-law incorporation rule concerns diversity judgments and does not govern this federal-question decree. Plaut protects a completed adjudication at its actual scope; it does not enlarge the adjudicated property. The district court's blanket claim-preclusion ruling therefore fails.

### II. An assumed choice of law is not an actually litigated issue

**Holding and operative rule:** Mutual issue preclusion requires the identical issue to have been actually litigated and necessarily determined. Nebo Oil's adjudication of state-law retroactivity and constitutional objections, while assuming Louisiana law supplied the rule of decision, does not preclude the distinct federal choice-of-law issue now asserted.

**Controlling explanation:** Different claims may share an issue that has binding effect, so the distinct servitudes do not by themselves defeat Petro-Hunt's issue-preclusion theory. Sunnen and Montana require examination of the precise issue actually decided. The earlier litigation addressed whether the state statute operated retroactively and whether its application violated constitutional protections; it did not litigate the federal choice-of-law issue. A judgment's dependence on an assumed legal rule does not transform the assumption into an actually contested and resolved question.

Stauffer protects mutual issue preclusion when its predicates are established. It does not supply the missing actual litigation here. The claim-identity and issue-litigation defects independently answer the corresponding preclusion theories. Because the asserted issue never acquired preclusive effect, the granted question about a changed-law exception to an otherwise established issue estoppel does not arise on this record. The Court therefore does not reach that question or adopt the Government's Little Lake Misere exception theory. The original final decree retains its binding disposition.

## Precedent Treatment

- Nevada v. United States supplies the inquiry into the property right actually adjudicated and protects final title within that scope.
- Semtek International Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp. supplies the distinction between a judgment's source and actual external effect; its diversity-specific state-law incorporation rule remains confined to that setting.
- Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc. retains its protection of completed federal adjudications; applying preclusion to the decree's actual subject matter preserves that protection.
- Commissioner v. Sunnen and Montana v. United States supply the issue-identity and actual-adjudication requirements applied here.
- United States v. Stauffer Chemical Co. retains mutual issue preclusion where its predicates exist; the missing actual litigation distinguishes this case.

## Law After Decision

Final federal property judgments remain conclusive for the claims and issues they actually adjudicate. Related conveyances and common ownership do not merge distinct servitudes into the same claim, and a legal premise assumed in earlier litigation does not satisfy actual issue adjudication. These rules permit adjudication of the other 95 servitudes while preserving Nebo Oil's binding title determination. Their remaining prescription proceedings apply the governing ten-year nonuse rule; the broader changed-law exception remains undecided for want of an otherwise preclusive issue here.
