### Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare — February 23, 2005

#### Material premises and components

The reviewed judgment rejects the state warning claim on conflict-preemption grounds. The operative California duty is clear and reasonable communication of reproductive toxicity, not compulsory use of one safe-harbor sentence. Labeling, point-of-sale notice, and advertising are distinct compliance routes. Section 379r(d)(2) exempts a qualifying pre-September 1, 1997 initiative from that section; the separate product-liability provision in subsection (e) and identical-requirement enforcement provision in subsection (f) are not substituted for it. Nonidentity or national uniformity alone cannot defeat the preserved initiative.

The August 17, 2001 citizen-petition response is the definitive action established by the published judicial recitation. Earlier communications required continued approved warnings while review remained pending; they are not retroactively treated as definitive determinations about Dowhal's proposal. The agency rejected the prior heart-rate formulation and the Habitrol formulation, required the comparative-risk communication, and allowed another supported proposal. That route for reconsideration does not authorize present disregard of an effective prohibition. The record supplies neither a new manufacturer dataset nor a final agency response to every conceivable combined warning.

The FDA's quoted reasons address uncertainty, comparative risk, and the misleading consumer implication of the proposed communication. They do not find nicotine harmless or establish categorical safety during pregnancy. The state court's inference that every complying warning conflicts is an inference to review, not an express sentence in the agency response. The off-label-notice argument involves obstruction of the particular federal method, not automatic extension of labeling jurisdiction to every public statement.

The separable legal components are: (1) whether the initiative saving provision forecloses an independent actual-conflict inquiry; (2) whether statutory misleadingness may include a literally accurate statement whose contextual implication is misleading, and whether the definitive labeling action is a legally material federal predicate; and (3) whether this record establishes conflict across the asserted state claim's available compliance routes. The formal judgment turns on component 3. No independent First Amendment claim is presented.

#### Competing lawful paths

**Affirm the preemption judgment on the identified warning claim.** Geier Parts I–II preserve independently demonstrated actual conflict despite saving language, while its application recognizes a supported federal regulatory method. Here the agency did more than permit a product: its definitive action rejected the reproductive-harm warning in the course of regulating these products and required a different communication to prevent a misleading inference about smoking and replacement therapy. On this view, the mandatory state message defeats that specific method whether on a label or on a point-of-sale notice, and the ability to seek reconsideration with supporting data does not remove the present conflict. The strongest objection is that the state duty allows explanatory wording and channels beyond the label, so rejection of particular formulations does not prove incompatibility with every complying communication. An affirming commitment must adopt the substantive-incompatibility inference and explain its health basis; uniformity cannot supply it.

**Vacate the blanket preemption judgment and remand for the actual-duty comparison.** The operative federal action can bar the formulations it actually rejects, but the broader state duty permits a qualifying explanation addressing nicotine, product uncertainty, professional consultation, and relative risk. No such analytical possibility is treated as a proposal a party submitted or as a warning judicially approved. The point is that the defendants' summary-judgment theory must establish conflict at the scope of the obligation displaced. The strongest objection is that the FDA has already rejected the relevant substantive message and that a state court cannot evade the binding decision by adding words or changing the medium. The remanding commitments answer that objection by preserving the definitive decision and requiring genuine compatibility, rather than treating agency approval as irrelevant or authorizing the rejected warning. The permitted future proceedings are ordinary state adjudication of the actual claim and available forms of compliance, not a mandatory new agency proceeding or an evidentiary search detached from the pleaded duty.

Both paths reject the absolute-saving theory and the claim that literal accuracy alone defeats statutory misleadingness. A remand based on treating every FDA letter as legally ineffective would ignore this definitive approval-related action; no Justice adopts that categorical proposition. Neither path adjudicates a new constitutional speech claim.

#### Components and common propositions

**D1 — The initiative saving provision and actual conflict.** Section 379r(d)(2) exempts a qualifying requirement adopted through a state public initiative or referendum enacted before September 1, 1997 from that section. It therefore defeats express displacement based merely on a nonidentical requirement, but does not repeal the rest of valid federal drug law or foreclose a separately demonstrated actual conflict. Subsection (e)'s product-liability provision and subsection (f)'s identical-requirement enforcement provision are distinct. All eight Associates join this construction and Geier's requirement of an actual federal/state comparison.

**D2 — Authorized contextual misleadingness and the particular federal predicate.** Sections 321(n), 352(a), and 355 permit evaluation of what drug labeling conveys in context; literal accuracy of an isolated assertion does not conclusively establish that the communication is not misleading. The August 17, 2001 citizen-petition response is the definitive agency action established here. Its product-specific rejection, approved-label obligations, reasons concerning comparative risk and uncertainty, and allowance for a later supported proposal must receive their actual force. Earlier communications are not retrospectively converted into definitive rulings on the proposal. All eight Associates join these propositions. They do not adopt a free-standing power to suppress accurate information solely to induce preferred behavior or to override statutory conditions through public-health preferences.

**D3 — Scope of demonstrated conflict and the judgment.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Breyer conclude that the mandatory reproductive-harm message asserted in this claim restores the misleading implication the FDA specifically rejected and frustrates the supported comparative-risk method, whether placed on the label or conveyed through the asserted off-label channels. They would affirm. Stevens, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg conclude that rejection of the identified formulations does not establish that the full retained state duty cannot be met through a genuinely compatible explanation. They would vacate the claim-wide preemption judgment and remand for ordinary adjudication of that actual duty and asserted conflict. They preserve the operative federal decision and do not authorize a forbidden warning.

The difference is substantive incompatibility at the scope of the claim, not whether genuine conflict can displace a saved initiative or whether a true statement can mislead in context. The source does not establish that the FDA expressly prohibited every conceivable formulation. The affirming position adopts the lower court's inference for the present demand; the remanding position rejects its sufficiency. Neither position turns that inference into additional agency text. The affirming four support a holding confined to the identified message, products, agency decision, and present record, preserving genuinely compatible independent duties. They do not support a universal bar to every conceivable qualifying warning. Their conclusion is that the asserted state demand conflicts now, and that a merely hypothetical reformulation does not defeat judgment on that demand; they do not certify that all future formulations or independent state duties conflict.

#### Remedy, objections, and assembly limits

The affirming four would leave the California Supreme Court's preemption judgment effective for the asserted warning claim. The remanding four would return the actual duty and possible genuinely compatible compliance for ordinary adjudication, keeping the definitive federal decision effective. A possible combined warning is an analytical alternative, not a warning proved to have been proposed by a party or approved by either court. A remand is not an instruction to compel a new agency proceeding or to accept a rejected warning. A supported future agency proposal remains governed by the actual approval process and does not provide present permission to disregard existing obligations.

The affirming rationale must answer the compatible-explanation objection by explaining the present substantive conflict, not by treating national uniformity as enough. The remanding rationale must answer the specific-prohibition objection by preserving the federal decision and distinguishing its actual reach from an inferred bar to all compliance. The two paths do not share D3 merely because both accept D1–D2. No author, required separate-opinion author, or full-Court disposition is selected here.

## Holdings

### Saved initiative requirements and actual conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 379r(d)(2) exempts a state requirement adopted by a public initiative or referendum enacted before September 1, 1997 from that section's express displacement of nonidentical requirements. A saved requirement nevertheless yields when the party asserting preemption establishes incompatibility with a valid, specifically applicable federal command or supported regulatory method under other operative federal law.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices, controls this construction.

**Controlling explanation:** Geier gives independent effect to both saving language and demonstrated conflict. The words limiting this exemption to the particular section preserve Proposition 65 against the section's uniformity rule while leaving other valid federal obligations operative. Myrick requires an actual federal source; regulatory silence supplies none. Medtronic requires comparison of the duties themselves and preserves compatible obligations. Thus, nonidentity alone cannot defeat the saved initiative, while its preservation does not compel obedience to incompatible federal and state commands. The separate product-liability saving in subsection (e) and identical-requirement enforcement provision in subsection (f) perform their own statutory work; neither substitutes for the initiative exemption applied here.

### Contextually misleading labeling

**Holding and operative rule:** Under §§321(n), 352(a) and 355, the FDA may reject drug labeling that is literally accurate but materially misleading in context, considering its representations and material omissions concerning consequences under prescribed or customary use. That authority requires an authorized labeling determination supported by its actual reasons; a bare preference for how consumers should behave is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part I, joined by all nine Justices, controls this rule and the identification of the definitive federal action.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted distinction between falsity and misleadingness requires attention to the message a label conveys as a whole. Here the agency considered the implications of reproductive-risk language alongside the relative dangers of smoking, replacement therapy and failed abstinence. The August 17, 2001 citizen-petition response establishes the definitive determination, distinct from earlier interim communications requiring continued approved warnings. Its reasons concern a misleading risk comparison and the uncertainty relevant to that comparison. They supply a statutory labeling ground, rather than power to suppress accurate discussion generally. The agency's allowance for another data-supported proposal preserves the lawful reconsideration process while the current decision remains effective.

### Conflict in the warning claim presented

**Holding and operative rule:** The reproductive-harm warning demanded for the covered nicotine-replacement products conflicts with the operative FDA decision because it restores the misleading comparative-risk message that decision rejected. The claim and its dependent remedies therefore cannot be enforced while that decision governs; the party asserting displacement must establish the relevant action's coverage and conflict for a materially different warning or duty.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer, supplies a five-Justice controlling application.

**Controlling explanation:** The federal determination did more than approve a minimum label. It rejected the added message in the course of requiring pregnancy advice that addressed smoking, inability to stop without assistance and the comparative risks of replacement therapy. On the present demand, repeating the rejected implication through a label, a point-of-sale notice or the asserted advertising route would frustrate the same supported method. The dissent rightly identifies the need to examine the full state obligation, which permits different forms of clear and reasonable warning. But a hypothetical reformulation does not remove the substantive conflict established for the message this claim demands. This conclusion applies the federal determination to the actual claim; it does not attribute an express prohibition of every conceivable formulation to the agency. A genuinely compatible independent duty and a supported later labeling proposal remain subject to their own requirements.

## Precedent Treatment

- Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. is applied: saving language and actual conflict remain separate inquiries, and displacement requires the actual federal choice and incompatible state obligation.
- Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick is applied: this case contains an operative federal determination, unlike an inference based on regulatory absence.
- Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr is applied: approval alone supplies no general immunity, and genuinely compatible duties retain their force.
- FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp. supplies independent examination of statutory delegation; preemption does not enlarge the agency's authority.

## Law After Decision

Qualifying initiative requirements remain protected from §379r's express uniformity rule. Courts must separately determine actual conflict with valid federal law by identifying the action, the covered product and the state duty. The current warning claim is displaced because of the supported substantive conflict, while contextual misleadingness remains bounded by the enacted labeling authority. Later supported agency reconsideration and genuinely different duties receive the same actual-coverage inquiry.

## Separate Writings

Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, Souter and Thomas, agrees that the initiative saving provision permits independently demonstrated conflict and that a true statement can materially mislead in context. The dissent would vacate and remand because the agency's rejection of identified formulations does not establish incompatibility with the entire state obligation, which permits explanatory language and several communication methods. Myrick and Medtronic require an operative source and precise comparison, and the Geier disagreement concerns whether that comparison proves exclusivity. The definitive response establishes more than mere approval, but its force cannot be enlarged into the missing claim-wide showing. Their proposed remand would preserve the operative federal decision while testing genuinely compatible compliance, rather than order a rejected warning. The distinction is the scope of displacement on this record, not a general prohibition on regulation of misleading labeling.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for implementation of the affirmed preemption judgment at its stated scope. The state court must identify the covered products and warning demand when giving effect to that judgment and adjudicate any independently preserved compatible duty on its own basis.
