#### Governing distinctions and supported paths

**Statutory question.** Taylor, especially Part IV, makes the earlier conviction's necessary adjudicated content decisive. Generic burglary requires unlawful or unprivileged entry into, or remaining in, a building or structure with intent to commit a crime. A broader statute is not enough. Taylor's specified trial documents identify the necessary basis of the conviction; they do not license a fresh conduct trial. Its plea-bargain discussion independently warns that evidence of underlying conduct cannot enlarge what a plea established. The present extension concerns analogous plea records.

**Path A ? conviction-record limit.** Determine the necessary generic basis from the charging document together with the written plea agreement, plea colloquy, an explicit factual finding to which the defendant assented, or a comparable judicial record establishing the actual admission. A police report or complaint application, merely placed in the historical file and not made part of the accepted plea, cannot establish the building element. The objection is its unadopted status, not that every police description is untrustworthy. An expressly incorporated account must be assessed as the actual judicial admission; the present handoff establishes no such incorporation.

The strongest contrary argument is that state plea practices may leave their shared understanding in documents other than a transcript and that excluding reliable contemporaneous material makes equivalent convictions turn on recordkeeping. The answer is that Congress chose convictions, and the decisive object is what was necessarily admitted rather than what the judge now believes happened. The same difficulty affects trial evidence; Taylor accepted that cost to avoid reconstructing conduct and overriding plea bargains.

**Path B ? reliable evidence identifying the plea's agreed basis.** Taylor's named trial documents can be read as illustrations of a confined inquiry rather than an exhaustive documentary taxonomy. On that reading, reliable contemporaneous reports and complaint applications may help establish what the parties understood the plea to admit, provided the inquiry identifies the conviction's basis rather than independently retries criminal conduct. The strongest objection is that a reliable account of conduct does not establish a defendant's adoption, particularly where the broader charge made the building fact unnecessary and adoption is disputed. This path accordingly requires an actual finding about the scope of the plea; the allegations cannot establish agreement merely by being uncontradicted at a state proceeding or by appearing plausible now.

**Independent constitutional question.** Gatewood requires applicable federal charging and proper factfinding beyond a reasonable doubt, or valid admission and lawful waiver, for necessary adult-recidivist existence, identity, historical qualification and chronology that raise a binding maximum or minimum. Legal classification of established facts remains judicial. A permissible Taylor source does not itself satisfy the new federal charge or substitute a new punishment-authorizing factual determination. Booker reaffirmed that rule on January 12 and retained binding Guidelines in their lawful applications. Its holding excludes an advisory-system remand here. Taylor's older statement about alternative Guidelines consideration now operates only within the constitutionally authorized range under Angle and Booker.

**Disposition paths.** The five Path-A commitments below support reversal of the First Circuit's statutory rule and its enhancement direction, with a bounded sentencing remand. The three Path-B commitments reject that categorical statutory rule but support vacatur of the mandatory enhancement direction and remand under the independently controlling Gatewood/Booker requirements. Their recent constitutional dissents are maintained as objections to the rule's correctness; they do not assume a nonexistent recidivism exception, and this commitment does not request renewed overruling in a case whose stated question concerns conviction materials. No Justice's disagreement with a precedent mechanically decides whether that Justice must repeat an overruling demand in every subsequent application.

The thirty-to-thirty-seven-month description in the historical proceeding is not an irrevocable present range or mandate. Any new aggravated proceeding requires its own lawful authority and the applicable charge, proof, waiver, notice, sufficiency, procedure, finality and double-jeopardy protections; this remand supplies none. The firearm conviction remains intact against this sentencing challenge.

## Event

## Holdings

### I. Qualifying burglary pleas must be established through conclusive judicial plea records

**Holding and operative rule:** In determining whether a plea under a burglary statute broader than generic burglary necessarily admitted that generic offense, an ACCA court may examine the statutory elements, the charging document, written plea terms, the plea colloquy, or a comparable express judicial finding assented to by the defendant. Unadopted police reports and complaint applications cannot turn a broad plea into an admission of the qualifying elements; their reliability as accounts of conduct does not satisfy the conviction-centered inquiry.

**Authority:** Thomas's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for the rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that what a conviction necessarily established is different from what a later judge finds probably happened. Taylor v. United States ties generic burglary to a conviction establishing the required elements and limits retrospective reconstruction of old conduct. Its reasoning applies equally to convictions entered on pleas. A written agreement or recorded assent to a specific factual basis can establish what the defendant admitted; the investigative reason for bringing a broader charge cannot do so by itself.

The Government's shared-understanding approach would allow a sentencing court to infer an admitted element from a persuasive account that the plea never adopted. Even internally consistent police documents can describe facts that the prosecution did not require the defendant to concede. Differences in state recordkeeping do not justify replacing Taylor's inquiry with another factual trial of the old burglary. Express incorporation of a building-specific charge through an appropriate judicial plea record is different from an unadopted narrative merely present in the file. Here the broader complaints leave the qualifying distinction unresolved, so the police materials cannot support the First Circuit's statutory direction.

### II. The enhancement and resentencing must satisfy the existing charging and proof rules

**Holding and operative rule:** Any genuinely disputed adult-recidivist fact indispensable to a higher federal sentencing maximum or mandatory minimum must satisfy Gatewood's applicable charging and unanimous jury-proof requirements beyond a reasonable doubt, or rest on a valid admission and any necessary lawful waiver. Courts may legally classify elements already established by qualifying records, but a permissible document does not itself cure an indispensable omitted charge or authorize judicial resolution of a new disputed enhancement fact. A lawful jury waiver permits judicial determination under the same reasonable-doubt standard and charging boundary.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II's application of the governing Gatewood and Booker rules and its remand. The statutory disagreement in Part I remains distinct from agreement that those existing rules control the sentencing court.

**Controlling explanation:** Gatewood governs existence, identity, historical qualification and chronology of adult predicates when those facts authorize increased punishment. Angle and Harris preserve the same protection for mandatory floors as for ceilings. The former constitutional prior-conviction exception therefore cannot justify the First Circuit's direction. Calling disputed conduct an interpretation of a prior plea does not change its punishment-authorizing function.

The reproduced federal indictment and the defense's actual charging objection make the operative record material. The district court must examine the governing instruments, the scope and validity of admissions and waivers, and preservation. If an indispensable enhancement allegation was omitted from the federal charge, the objection was preserved and no valid charging waiver supplies authority, sentencing notice and judicial findings cannot authorize the enhancement; sentencing must begin with the independently authorized range. For a genuinely forfeited claim, Rule 52(b) requires error, plainness, the defendant's ordinary showing of outcome-affecting prejudice, and a separate determination that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction. Where settled trial law changes during direct review, plainness is assessed at review. A further enhancement proceeding requires independent lawful authority and its protections for charging, notice, proof, finality and double jeopardy. This remand does not create that authority.

Booker retains the binding Guidelines in their lawful applications. The district court must therefore determine the lawfully supported range and apply the retained sentencing and departure provisions. The earlier thirty-to-thirty-seven-month description is not an automatic resentencing instruction. The statutory evidence limit and constitutional allocation perform separate work, and both govern the proposed predicates.

## Precedent Treatment

- Taylor v. United States is extended to guilty pleas under broader burglary statutes through judicial records establishing the elements necessarily admitted; its conviction-centered method retains force.
- United States v. Gatewood is applied directly to disputed adult predicates and the operative federal charging and admission record. Legal classification of established elements remains judicial.
- United States v. Angle and Harris v. United States preserve the equivalent treatment of increased maximums and mandatory minimums.
- Almendarez-Torres v. United States supplies no surviving constitutional recidivism exception after Gatewood.
- United States v. Booker governs resentencing through the retained binding Guidelines, lawful factfinding and existing sentencing provisions.

## Law After Decision

ACCA qualification of a guilty plea under a broader burglary statute turns on conclusive judicial plea records establishing generic elements, with an express distinction between adopted facts and unadopted investigative accounts. The separate Gatewood requirements govern any indispensable disputed enhancement fact, and Booker supplies the ordinary binding sentencing framework on remand.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Kennedy and Breyer.** The partial dissent would permit a confined inquiry into the plea's actual shared factual basis using reliable contemporaneous records. In its view, Taylor's examples of trial documents do not establish an exclusive list for pleas. Matching complaints, applications and reports, together with the State's factual-basis requirements, may reliably identify the offense both sides understood was admitted. Equivalent guilty pleas should not have different federal consequences solely because one State records a colloquy differently. The inquiry would remain directed to the conviction rather than an unrestricted retrial of old conduct.

The three Justices nevertheless agree that the unconditional enhancement command must be removed under the governing Gatewood and Booker decisions. Their earlier constitutional objections remain, but those objections do not permit the lower court to impose punishment through a procedure current law forbids. They join Part II's present-law remand and oppose only the categorical statutory restriction in Part I. The Court answers their statutory position by distinguishing reliable proof of conduct from a record establishing what the plea necessarily admitted.
