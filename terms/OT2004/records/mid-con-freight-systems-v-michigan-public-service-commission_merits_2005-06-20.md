## Controlling holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 14504 preempts additional state registration obligations concerning the federal system's specified subjects—evidence of federal operating authority, insurance, a service agent and associated registration fees—when those obligations exceed the federal standards. It does not displace a genuinely separate state regulatory obligation on another subject merely because it applies to interstate carriers; Michigan's section 478.2(2) charge falls outside the covered subjects on this record.

**Authority:** The signed opinion of the Court, with the seven complete joins stated in Opinion Topology, directly controls the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Both sentences of section 14504(b) measure registration against the Secretary's standards. Subsection (a) defines those standards by their forms-and-procedures function, and subsection (c) identifies the federal-registration system. The repeated registration language concerns that defined subject. The excess-obligation prohibition therefore protects the federal system against additional covered demands without absorbing every state carrier-registration program.

The competing interstate-status reading would treat every registration requirement triggered by interstate operation as covered, while preserving general neutral registration. That distinction is narrower than a universal fee cap, but the statute identifies the transportation governed by the federal system; it does not say that an obligation on a different subject becomes covered whenever interstate operation triggers it. Dividing the work between subsections (b) and (c) cannot overcome their common reference to the defined system. The replacement system's centralization of relevant filings and fees confirms the enacted structure. Yellow Transportation enforces the benchmark within that system and preserves unrelated taxes and licensing policies.

Michigan's separate program seeks vehicle information and a fee rather than the listed federal permit, insurance or service-agent filings. The apparent $10 waiver creates an administrative connection but does not establish that payment of the $100 is necessary to complete federal registration. A fee's label, age or regulatory purpose alone would not establish independence; the actual obligation supplies the distinction here. Because general neutrality is not the adopted test, the dispute over the combined coverage of Michigan's two provisions does not require a characterization remand under this rule.

**Operative qualifications:** Covered federal-registration demands remain subject to the federal standards despite a different state label. The insurance-proof fee system retains its November 15, 1991 benchmark and $10 ceiling. The prohibition on charging for filing and maintaining evidence of federal registration is distinct. The vehicle-identification restriction governs what the Secretary's standards may require, and the separate inconsistent-fee provision concerns fees under the federal system. These commands do not create a general $10 ceiling for state carrier regulation.

## Precedent treatment and resulting law

Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan is applied for its enforcement of the delegated registration benchmark, qualifying reciprocity and express system-specific boundary. The present decision resolves which subject section 14504 governs; Yellow's separate fee-calculation holding remains in force.

The federal registration ceiling governs demands within the Single State Registration System's specified subjects. Courts must identify the actual information, payment and conditions a state program imposes. A separate program on a different subject remains outside that ceiling, while relabeling an additional covered demand cannot evade it. Michigan's challenged fee satisfies that distinction on the present record.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, dissenting.** The dissent reads subsection (b) to prohibit additional registration obligations imposed because a carrier conducts interstate transportation, while preserving general neutral registration that only incidentally covers interstate carriers. Subsection (a) defines the permitted federal standards, rather than every additional demand subsection (b) forbids. On that reading, a State cannot avoid the ceiling by asking for different information or spending the fee on safety administration. The information, decal and payment constitute registration even though they serve a regulatory purpose.

The Court's defined-subject construction, in the dissent's view, adds an exemption the excess-obligation sentence does not contain. Applicable subsection (c) commands still independently protect the specified federal demands; the general-neutral qualification does not permit excess covered fees. Yellow's enforcement of the federal benchmark leaves this scope question open.

The dissent would vacate and remand rather than invalidate the fee outright. Michigan argues that sections 478.2(1) and (2) together impose one neutral $100 requirement on Michigan-plated for-hire vehicles; the carriers dispute that account. The lower court's regulatory-purpose rationale did not resolve that characterization under the dissent's rule. The required inquiry concerns the combined scheme's operation, rather than an assumed condition linking the $100 payment to federal registration.

#### M1 — Affirmance rule, application and limits

Section 14504 preempts state registration obligations that concern the federal system's specified subjects and exceed the federal standards for those subjects. Those subjects are registration of evidence of federal operating authority, proof of insurance, the local service agent and the associated allocated fee system. The statute does not displace an independent state registration program on another subject merely because the carrier operates interstate, the State gathers vehicle information or requires a decal.

Both sentences of subsection (b) refer to registration measured against the Secretary's standards. Subsection (a) defines those standards by their forms-and-procedures function, and subsection (c) identifies the particular federal registration system. Reading the same registration terminology in that setting gives the excess-obligation sentence real work without treating it as a separate prohibition on every registration demand. The restrictions are substantive: a State cannot use another label to impose excess obligations within the covered subject.

The narrower opposing M2 argument is addressed directly. It reads the carrier's interstate description as limiting the reason for registration and then treats the standards as the ceiling on all obligations within that class. M1 instead reads that description as identifying the transportation regulated by the federal system. The statute does not state that a demand outside the defined subjects becomes covered whenever interstate operation is its trigger. M2's allocation of functions between subsections (b) and (c) does not overcome the common subject identified by the definition, standards and repeated language. Rejection of M2 does not depend on pretending that it is the broader M3 rule.

The former system and its replacement confirm the defined-subject reading: the change centralizes the relevant filings and fee collection and ties the fee to insurance proof rather than federal-permit filing. *Yellow* supplies the fee-system context and enforces the benchmark within the actual delegated system. Statutory history corroborates the reading of the enacted text; the charge's age and a general preference for state regulation do not supply an exemption by themselves. The national system's burden-reduction objective does not become a universal carrier-fee limit.

Michigan's section 478.2(2) requires information identifying the vehicle and payment for its separate program, not the listed federal permit, insurance or agent filings. The record does not establish that the carrier must pay the $100 to complete federal registration. The apparent waiver of Michigan's otherwise available $10 fee creates a subsidiary administrative connection but does not convert the different obligation into a federal-registration charge. This is not a stipulated finding that every aspect of the systems is unconnected; it is the application supported by the actual requirements and the absence of demonstrated conditioning. An unsupported legal linkage cannot be supplied by the waiver alone.

The State's disputed claim that its two $100 provisions form a neutral general scheme need not be resolved under M1. Neutrality is not the adopted test for subsection (b)'s reach, and no finding of equal actual coverage or treatment is made. The lower court's categorical reasoning that regulatory purpose necessarily makes a fee nonregistration is not adopted: registration may serve regulatory purposes. The judgment is sustained because the actual obligation concerns a different subject.

**Remedy:** Affirm the judgment rejecting this federal statutory claim. Section 478.2(2)'s charge remains enforceable against this challenge. #### M2 — Vacatur-and-remand rule and substantive separate position

O'Connor and Kennedy directly join the following position. Subsection (b) reaches registration obligations imposed on a motor carrier because it provides interstate transportation in the State. The first sentence recognizes compliance with the federal standards; the second prohibits additional registration obligations in that interstate-specific category. A general neutral registration rule incidentally affecting interstate carriers is not brought within subsection (b) by their mere inclusion, but applicable subsection (c) restrictions still govern the listed federal-system demands.

On this reading, subsection (a) defines what the federal standards require, not every additional obligation that subsection (b) forbids a State to impose. The first subsection (b) sentence identifies the class of interstate-registration requirements and the allowed subset; the second makes the standards a ceiling. A State cannot escape that ceiling by seeking different information or using the proceeds for safety regulation. Michigan's information, fee and decal amount to registration in the ordinary sense even if they serve a regulatory purpose. The lower court's regulatory-purpose explanation therefore cannot decide the federal question.

M1's strongest objection is that the statute describes a carrier and its transportation without expressly saying that registration must be imposed because of that status. M2 answers that the description naturally identifies the category of registration the provision governs, while the generally phrased excess-obligation prohibition cannot be confined to the already listed federal proofs without adding another subject-matter exemption. The general-neutral qualification avoids turning the Act into universal immunity from ordinary tax and licensing registration. Separate subsection (c) commands protect the listed federal registration process, so neutrality under subsection (b) does not authorize excessive covered fees or multiple demands for the federal proofs.

The older regulatory system cannot manufacture an ambiguity that defeats the replacement's operative wording. The lack of confirmatory legislative statements does not add a subject-matter limitation absent from the enacted command. *Yellow*'s enforcement of the federal benchmark is compatible with this reading and its preservation of unrelated regulation does not decide relatedness. These are independent statutory grounds for withholding assent to M1, not a demand to treat every carrier charge as part of the $10 fee system.

The proper outcome under M2 is not immediate invalidation of the $100 charge. Section 478.2(2) expressly refers to wholly interstate vehicles, but Michigan contends that its operation with section 478.2(1) creates a common neutral scheme for Michigan-plated for-hire vehicles. The carriers dispute that characterization. Neither equal dollar amounts nor subsection (2)'s wording alone resolves the legally relevant combined operation. **Remedy:** Vacate and remand to determine whether the combined provisions actually impose an additional registration requirement because of interstate-carrier status, keeping subsection (c)'s applicable independent commands distinct. ## Public Projection

## Event

Mid-Con Freight Systems, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission, No. 03-1234.

Argued April 26, 2005; decided June 20, 2005. On review of the Michigan Court of Appeals, which sustained the annual $100 charge under Michigan Compiled Laws §478.2(2) for Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely interstate. The question is whether the federal Single State Registration System preempts that separate charge under 49 U.S.C. §14504.

The State requires vehicle-identifying information and a decal for the separate program and appears to waive its otherwise available $10 federal-system fee for covered carriers choosing Michigan as their registration State. The carriers seek invalidation and relief from collection. Michigan contends that its interstate and intrastate $100 provisions together form a general neutral program; the carriers dispute that characterization. The distinct local-haul fee and constitutional challenge are presented in American Trucking Associations.

## Holdings

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 14504 preempts additional state registration obligations concerning the federal system's specified subjects—evidence of federal operating authority, insurance, a service agent and associated registration fees—when those obligations exceed the federal standards. It does not displace a genuinely separate state regulatory obligation on another subject merely because it applies to interstate carriers; Michigan's section 478.2(2) charge falls outside the covered subjects on this record.

**Authority:** The signed opinion of the Court, with the seven complete joins stated in Opinion Topology, directly controls the rule, application and remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** Both sentences of section 14504(b) measure registration against the Secretary's standards. Subsection (a) defines those standards by their forms-and-procedures function, and subsection (c) identifies the federal-registration system. The repeated registration language concerns that defined subject. The excess-obligation prohibition therefore protects the federal system against additional covered demands without absorbing every state carrier-registration program.

The competing interstate-status reading would treat every registration requirement triggered by interstate operation as covered, while preserving general neutral registration. That distinction is narrower than a universal fee cap, but the statute identifies the transportation governed by the federal system; it does not say that an obligation on a different subject becomes covered whenever interstate operation triggers it. Dividing the work between subsections (b) and (c) cannot overcome their common reference to the defined system. The replacement system's centralization of relevant filings and fees confirms the enacted structure. Yellow Transportation enforces the benchmark within that system and preserves unrelated taxes and licensing policies.

Michigan's separate program seeks vehicle information and a fee rather than the listed federal permit, insurance or service-agent filings. The apparent $10 waiver creates an administrative connection but does not establish that payment of the $100 is necessary to complete federal registration. A fee's label, age or regulatory purpose alone would not establish independence; the actual obligation supplies the distinction here. Because general neutrality is not the adopted test, the dispute over the combined coverage of Michigan's two provisions does not require a characterization remand under this rule.

**Operative qualifications:** Covered federal-registration demands remain subject to the federal standards despite a different state label. The insurance-proof fee system retains its November 15, 1991 benchmark and $10 ceiling. The prohibition on charging for filing and maintaining evidence of federal registration is distinct. The vehicle-identification restriction governs what the Secretary's standards may require, and the separate inconsistent-fee provision concerns fees under the federal system. These commands do not create a general $10 ceiling for state carrier regulation.

## Precedent Treatment

Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan is applied for its enforcement of the delegated registration benchmark, qualifying reciprocity and express system-specific boundary. The present decision resolves which subject section 14504 governs; Yellow's separate fee-calculation holding remains in force.

## Law After Decision

The federal registration ceiling governs demands within the Single State Registration System's specified subjects. Courts must identify the actual information, payment and conditions a state program imposes. A separate program on a different subject remains outside that ceiling, while relabeling an additional covered demand cannot evade it. Michigan's challenged fee satisfies that distinction on the present record.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, dissenting.** The dissent reads subsection (b) to prohibit additional registration obligations imposed because a carrier conducts interstate transportation, while preserving general neutral registration that only incidentally covers interstate carriers. Subsection (a) defines the permitted federal standards, rather than every additional demand subsection (b) forbids. On that reading, a State cannot avoid the ceiling by asking for different information or spending the fee on safety administration. The information, decal and payment constitute registration even though they serve a regulatory purpose.

The Court's defined-subject construction, in the dissent's view, adds an exemption the excess-obligation sentence does not contain. Applicable subsection (c) commands still independently protect the specified federal demands; the general-neutral qualification does not permit excess covered fees. Yellow's enforcement of the federal benchmark leaves this scope question open.

The dissent would vacate and remand rather than invalidate the fee outright. Michigan argues that sections 478.2(1) and (2) together impose one neutral $100 requirement on Michigan-plated for-hire vehicles; the carriers dispute that account. The lower court's regulatory-purpose rationale did not resolve that characterization under the dissent's rule. The required inquiry concerns the combined scheme's operation, rather than an assumed condition linking the $100 payment to federal registration.

## Procedure After Action

The affirmed judgment concludes this section 14504 challenge. Michigan may continue collecting the reviewed separate fee, subject to the federal requirements governing any actual covered registration demands.
