## Holdings

**Common authority:** The seven Justices joining Souter's opinion directly support every holding below.

**Mitigation relevance.** Supported evidence of reduced capital culpability must receive constitutional consideration even when the defendant has not shown a severe permanent impairment or a causal connection to the crime. Tennard v. Dretke rejects both screening requirements; Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas establish that culpability mitigation may matter independently of deliberateness or future danger. Smith's intellectual-functioning and educational evidence falls within that principle. Relevance gives the juror authority to consider the evidence, without prescribing favorable weight or requiring a categorical disability diagnosis.

**A coherent verdict must give that consideration effect.** When supported mitigation may reduce culpability independently of the factual special issues, the jury must have an authorized, coherent means of giving that judgment effect in a life verdict. A negative special-issue answer cannot serve that purpose if the final instructions simultaneously require it to certify factual reasonable doubt that the juror may not hold.

Penry v. Johnson supplies that distinction between considering evidence and lawfully acting on it. Under Boyde v. California, the Court examines the complete charge and trial context for a reasonable likelihood of restricted mitigating use. Smith's instruction retains the conflict: a juror may accept the facts supporting deliberateness and future danger yet find a death sentence unwarranted on mitigation. A stronger direction to consider mitigation does not reconcile the incompatible certification. The dissent's specific-exception reading cannot eliminate what the final answer still requires the juror to certify. The constitutional requirement protects the independent culpability judgment recognized in Graham and Johnson; the structured factual questions do not exhaust it.

**Error and actual effect remain distinct.** For a preserved original-charge restriction of this kind, the State must establish beyond reasonable doubt on the complete penalty record that the defect did not contribute to the death sentence. If it cannot, constitutionally adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence must follow under the conditional remedy stated in the judgment.

Penry supplies this effect rule; the present case reaches this Court directly from the state judgment. Factual special-issue answers cannot themselves establish the result of an unconflicted mitigation decision. The complete penalty record must be considered. That remaining factual adjudication applies settled law.

## Precedent Treatment

Tennard v. Dretke is reaffirmed and applied to reject the heightened relevance screen.

Penry v. Johnson is reaffirmed and applied to the contradictory verdict mechanism and the separate preserved-error effect inquiry.

Graham v. Collins and Johnson v. Texas are applied for mitigation's independent culpability significance.

Boyde v. California supplies the complete-charge reasonable-likelihood standard used in assessing the instruction.

## Law After Decision

Capital courts must allow supported culpability mitigation both to be considered and to be given lawful effect beyond the factual special issues. A direction to consider it is inadequate when the verdict mechanism retains the identified contradiction. Sentence correction follows the separate whole-record effect inquiry under the State's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden for the preserved restriction.

## Separate Writings

Scalia, joined by Thomas, would affirm. The dissent maintains that the constitutional requirement of mitigation beyond structured capital special issues should be reconsidered, and does not give that broader requirement controlling stare-decisis weight. It also offers an independent reading under the continuing rule: the specific supplemental permission to answer negatively for mitigation qualifies the general factual-answer instructions, allowing the charge to operate coherently as a whole. On that reading there is no reasonable likelihood that jurors lacked authority to give mitigation effect. The dissent therefore opposes the merits correction and effect remand.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Texas Court of Criminal Appeals for the remaining procedural and effect adjudication and, if required, implementation of the conditional sentencing correction. The two constitutional errors are settled for those proceedings.
