## Holdings

All nine Justices join each holding and its controlling explanation.

### 1. The emergency process was actually completed

**Operative rule.** Section 1997e(a) requires completion of the administrative process actually available for the prisoner's grievance before suit. A substantive final-level decision on an emergency submission completes that route when the governing procedure prescribes no further review for that disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** Booth v. Churner requires exhaustion even when the process cannot grant the particular form of relief sought; Porter v. Nussle applies the requirement throughout prison life. Here the Commissioner addressed Russell's emergency grievance on its substance before suit. The policy's permission to resubmit a matter through ordinary channels if it is deemed nonemergent does not create a second mandatory sequence after the final substantive response actually given. Officials had the prescribed opportunity to correct the conditions. The statute enforces that opportunity and its real steps, rather than a later invented requirement.

### 2. A properly exhausted common prospective claim can proceed through a certified class

**Operative rule.** A named prisoner who actually completes the available process for a common condition may represent a properly certified class seeking the same indivisible prospective correction without requiring each unnamed member to duplicate the grievance. The court must establish prefiling completion and identity among the grievance, class condition and requested correction; a different condition or individual claim retains its own exhaustion requirement.

**Controlling explanation.** Booth and Porter supply mandatory exhaustion; Rule 23 supplies representative procedure after that prerequisite is met. Russell completed the process for the systemic conditions for which the class seeks the same correction. Repetition by each absent member would add no distinct administrative claim to the already exhausted common request. This rule rests on an exhausted representative and an identical prospective claim, not administrative futility or substantial compliance. It therefore preserves the statutory requirement while allowing the representative remedy the civil rules provide.

### 3. Officials must reasonably address known interacting dangers to an identifiable human need

**Operative rule.** Under the governing Eighth Amendment standard, prisoners must prove by the ordinary civil preponderance standard a substantial risk of serious harm and officials' actual awareness followed by unreasonable disregard. Conditions may be considered together when their interaction deprives an identifiable need such as sanitation, health or safety; reasonable protective responses remain a defense even when harm occurs.

**Controlling explanation.** Wilson v. Seiter permits analysis of mutually reinforcing conditions tied to an identified need. Helling v. McKinney permits prevention of serious future harm without awaiting disease. Farmer v. Brennan requires actual knowledge, which persistent obvious risks and repeated notice may support by inference but do not conclusively establish from official position alone. The credited evidence establishes awareness and inadequate responses to fecal contamination, sewage backflow, dangerous heat aggravated by illness and medication, insects and lost sleep, insufficient light, and serious psychiatric-care failures. Repair efforts that left the known serious dangers unreasonably unaddressed do not establish Farmer's reasonable-response defense. These particular findings sustain the constitutional judgment.

### 4. Each prospective command must correct the proved violation within the statutory limits

**Operative rule.** Section 3626 permits only relief necessary to correct a proved federal violation, narrowly drawn and the least intrusive necessary means, with substantial weight given to public safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting departure from state or local law additionally requires that federal law demand that departure, that it be necessary, and that other relief cannot correct the violation.

**Controlling explanation.** Lewis v. Casey makes the proved injury determine remedial scope. Benjamin v. Fraser supplies the current requirements for supported care and lawful continuing implementation; its pretrial liability standard does not replace Farmer for convicted prisoners. Johnson v. California likewise preserves the separate statutory remedial conditions. The particular cleaning, heat, screening, sewage, lighting and care measures answer the credited Unit 32-C dangers. Their measurements are supported means of correction on this record, not universal numerical constitutional standards. The general unpreserved demand to restart the decree for want of ritual findings does not defeat that support. Paragraph 9's unrestricted code incorporation nevertheless exceeds the required connection because it can reach practices never found to violate federal law. Actual vacatur and an operative limitation cure that defect while retaining necessary care. Officials proposing a narrower means must establish that it fully corrects the violation. The district court must preserve the findings required for continuing implementation and give security evidence its statutory weight.
