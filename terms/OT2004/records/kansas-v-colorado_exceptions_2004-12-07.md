## Law of the decision

**Common authority.** Breyer's opinion of the Court, adopted in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine direct votes for every holding, its explanation and the corresponding disposition.

#### The adopted 1969 method governs both commencement and eligible principal

**Holding and operative rule.** The June 11, 2001 judgment requires additional lost-investment interest only on qualifying losses incurred from 1969 onward, never before the underlying loss; it does not make pre-1969 principal interest-bearing merely because it remained unpaid after that date. The Master must replace the incompatible 1985 calculation with supported year-specific principal, proper inflation and additional-interest components, preserving separately recoverable earlier principal and inflation while excluding duplicate inflation and speculative returns.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that implementation must follow the judgment actually entered. Kansas v. Colorado (2001) adopted the Master's 1969 commencement and supported compensation method. That method limited the additional-interest base to losses incurred from 1969 onward. A calculation using 1985 therefore omits an eligible cohort; a calculation adding all older unpaid principal would enlarge the adopted remedy in the other direction. Both depart from the mandate.

The absence of a shared majority rationale for selecting the exact cutoff does not remove the binding component judgment. The earlier decision separately resolved additional compensation, nonduplicative loss measurement and Kansas's ownership of the recovery. City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co. explains compensation for lost use, but its maritime rationale cannot reopen the compact-specific accrual limit. Kansas must establish eligible losses and supported rates under the adopted method. The final computation is the Master's next task under the resolved principal-base rule.

#### A reliable longer-period model may measure compliance without changing Compact rights

**Holding and operative rule.** The rolling ten-year H-I calculation is permissible here as a supported method of measuring compliance: the first ten years determine the year-eleven obligation, and each succeeding calculation advances the window one year. The method cannot authorize accumulated credits forbidden by the Compact or extinguish an independently demonstrated material depletion of protected usable quantity or availability; Kansas retains its burden to establish a separately asserted compensable injury.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a shorter interval is not necessarily a more accurate measure. The Master found annual diversion errors as large as 22 percent in either direction, substantial annual flow discrepancies and a much closer fit over 540 months. Those findings support the longer window. The replacement program supplies water or retires pre-1949 rights, with monthly reporting and credit administration; the window operates within that program.

Kansas v. Colorado (1995) protects usable quantity and timing without prescribing one permanent accounting formula. Articles IV-D and V-E(5) continue to govern material depletion and forbidden credit accumulation. Treating the model as evidence of compliance respects those provisions; treating an aggregate balance as permission to erase an independently proved usable-water injury would not. Kansas's categorical annual-period objection therefore fails. Its corresponding exception to the 1997–1999 finding also fails because the Master found compliance using Kansas's own figures. That disposition concerns the measurement-period objection; distinct calibration, acreage, credit and monitoring disputes retain their separate grounds.

#### Initial state credit determinations require effective independent federal review

**Holding and operative rule.** Initial Colorado water-court replacement-credit determinations are permissible under this decree only with this Court's independent review of every issue essential to Compact compliance at the state line. The decree must preserve a concrete and timely route to challenge demonstrated noncompliance.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that coordinated initial administration does not transfer ultimate authority over the interstate obligation. West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims prevents a State from conclusively judging its own Compact duties, and Oklahoma v. New Mexico preserves this Court's responsibility for essential interstate questions. The Fourth Report expressly reserves that review. Initial state determinations can reduce inconsistent credit treatment affecting Colorado's senior users and Kansas while leaving Kansas free to invoke federal review. Acceptance rests on that reservation, not state finality.

#### The existing reference meets the present need for technical administration

**Holding and operative rule.** The current record does not require appointment of a River Master; the existing Special Master and retained original jurisdiction continue to administer this proceeding. Technical complexity alone does not compel a new appointment when the available reference and Compact mechanisms can provide effective resolution and timely relief.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the disputed model and credit issues may require judgments about the States' legal entitlements rather than only mechanical arithmetic. Texas v. New Mexico illustrates the availability of technical assistance without making a River Master necessary in every water dispute. The existing reference and consensual Compact mechanisms remain available here. The requested new appointment is therefore declined on present need, while supervision remains effective.

#### Technical questions may await the information necessary to decide them

**Holding and operative rule.** The identified technical issues may be deferred through the existing reference where additional information will materially improve their determination, with either State entitled to return a concrete dispute when the needed evidence becomes available. Questions immaterial to the accepted 1997–1999 conclusion need not be resolved to implement that finding; a surviving material application remains subject to the retained proceeding.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that reliable adjudication requires the information on which these calibration and future-compliance disputes turn. The Master's proposed deferrals provide that opportunity, and Kansas's own figures already support the reviewed period finding against the categorical annual-accounting objection. Deferral must operate together with effective review and correction of demonstrated noncompliance. The conforming decree must preserve the return to the Master and this Court's ultimate jurisdiction.

## Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

## Holdings

**Common authority.** Breyer's opinion of the Court, adopted in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine direct votes for every holding, its explanation and the corresponding disposition.

#### The adopted 1969 method governs both commencement and eligible principal

**Holding and operative rule.** The June 11, 2001 judgment requires additional lost-investment interest only on qualifying losses incurred from 1969 onward, never before the underlying loss; it does not make pre-1969 principal interest-bearing merely because it remained unpaid after that date. The Master must replace the incompatible 1985 calculation with supported year-specific principal, proper inflation and additional-interest components, preserving separately recoverable earlier principal and inflation while excluding duplicate inflation and speculative returns.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that implementation must follow the judgment actually entered. Kansas v. Colorado (2001) adopted the Master's 1969 commencement and supported compensation method. That method limited the additional-interest base to losses incurred from 1969 onward. A calculation using 1985 therefore omits an eligible cohort; a calculation adding all older unpaid principal would enlarge the adopted remedy in the other direction. Both depart from the mandate.

The absence of a shared majority rationale for selecting the exact cutoff does not remove the binding component judgment. The earlier decision separately resolved additional compensation, nonduplicative loss measurement and Kansas's ownership of the recovery. City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co. explains compensation for lost use, but its maritime rationale cannot reopen the compact-specific accrual limit. Kansas must establish eligible losses and supported rates under the adopted method. The final computation is the Master's next task under the resolved principal-base rule.

#### A reliable longer-period model may measure compliance without changing Compact rights

**Holding and operative rule.** The rolling ten-year H-I calculation is permissible here as a supported method of measuring compliance: the first ten years determine the year-eleven obligation, and each succeeding calculation advances the window one year. The method cannot authorize accumulated credits forbidden by the Compact or extinguish an independently demonstrated material depletion of protected usable quantity or availability; Kansas retains its burden to establish a separately asserted compensable injury.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a shorter interval is not necessarily a more accurate measure. The Master found annual diversion errors as large as 22 percent in either direction, substantial annual flow discrepancies and a much closer fit over 540 months. Those findings support the longer window. The replacement program supplies water or retires pre-1949 rights, with monthly reporting and credit administration; the window operates within that program.

Kansas v. Colorado (1995) protects usable quantity and timing without prescribing one permanent accounting formula. Articles IV-D and V-E(5) continue to govern material depletion and forbidden credit accumulation. Treating the model as evidence of compliance respects those provisions; treating an aggregate balance as permission to erase an independently proved usable-water injury would not. Kansas's categorical annual-period objection therefore fails. Its corresponding exception to the 1997–1999 finding also fails because the Master found compliance using Kansas's own figures. That disposition concerns the measurement-period objection; distinct calibration, acreage, credit and monitoring disputes retain their separate grounds.

#### Initial state credit determinations require effective independent federal review

**Holding and operative rule.** Initial Colorado water-court replacement-credit determinations are permissible under this decree only with this Court's independent review of every issue essential to Compact compliance at the state line. The decree must preserve a concrete and timely route to challenge demonstrated noncompliance.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that coordinated initial administration does not transfer ultimate authority over the interstate obligation. West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims prevents a State from conclusively judging its own Compact duties, and Oklahoma v. New Mexico preserves this Court's responsibility for essential interstate questions. The Fourth Report expressly reserves that review. Initial state determinations can reduce inconsistent credit treatment affecting Colorado's senior users and Kansas while leaving Kansas free to invoke federal review. Acceptance rests on that reservation, not state finality.

#### The existing reference meets the present need for technical administration

**Holding and operative rule.** The current record does not require appointment of a River Master; the existing Special Master and retained original jurisdiction continue to administer this proceeding. Technical complexity alone does not compel a new appointment when the available reference and Compact mechanisms can provide effective resolution and timely relief.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the disputed model and credit issues may require judgments about the States' legal entitlements rather than only mechanical arithmetic. Texas v. New Mexico illustrates the availability of technical assistance without making a River Master necessary in every water dispute. The existing reference and consensual Compact mechanisms remain available here. The requested new appointment is therefore declined on present need, while supervision remains effective.

#### Technical questions may await the information necessary to decide them

**Holding and operative rule.** The identified technical issues may be deferred through the existing reference where additional information will materially improve their determination, with either State entitled to return a concrete dispute when the needed evidence becomes available. Questions immaterial to the accepted 1997–1999 conclusion need not be resolved to implement that finding; a surviving material application remains subject to the retained proceeding.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that reliable adjudication requires the information on which these calibration and future-compliance disputes turn. The Master's proposed deferrals provide that opportunity, and Kansas's own figures already support the reviewed period finding against the categorical annual-accounting objection. Deferral must operate together with effective review and correction of demonstrated noncompliance. The conforming decree must preserve the return to the Master and this Court's ultimate jurisdiction.

## Precedent Treatment

- Kansas v. Colorado, May 15, 1995: applies the settled pumping liability, usable-quantity and availability protection, claimant's proof responsibility and prohibition on unbargained credits; its measurement rulings remain tied to their actual records.
- Kansas v. Colorado, June 11, 2001: enforces the binding 1969 component judgment and adopted compensation method; clarifies that the additional-interest principal base consists of losses incurred from 1969 onward while preserving separate principal, inflation and sovereign ownership.
- City of Milwaukee v. Cement Division, National Gypsum Co.: preserves its compensatory lost-use analogy while distinguishing its setting from this Compact's settled commencement and principal-base limits.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims: applies the prohibition against a State conclusively determining its own interstate obligations; independent review is an operative condition of the accepted state credit process.
- Oklahoma v. New Mexico: applies this Court's responsibility to decide matters essential to interstate compliance despite useful initial state proceedings.
- Texas v. New Mexico: applies its distinction between enforcement of actual Compact rights, permissible remedial administration and technical assistance; the present need does not require a new River Master.

## Law After Decision

The additional-interest award must use the 1969 loss cohort and the adopted nonduplicative compensation method. The rolling ten-year model is an evidentiary implementation of this Compact, bounded by protected usable availability and the prohibition on unauthorized credits. Initial Colorado credit proceedings operate subject to effective independent review. The present 1997–1999 period objection is resolved; surviving material technical disputes can return through the existing reference. Kansas continues to own and control any recovery.

The original action remains open.
