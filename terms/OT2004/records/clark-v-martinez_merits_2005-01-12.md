## Controlling holdings

### 1. The common removal-detention provision has one temporal meaning

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1231(a)(6) permits detention of every category it covers, including inadmissible persons who have never been admitted, only for the period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. When removal lacks a significant likelihood within the reasonably foreseeable future and no independent lawful custody ground applies, the person is entitled to enforceable release under lawful supervision; release does not grant admission.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct votes for this holding and Holding 2.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the same statutory words authorize the same kind of detention for each covered category. Zadvydas v. Davis construed those words to link custody to removal and expressly reserved their application to previously unadmitted persons. Today's holding resolves that reservation. Different constitutional positions at entry cannot turn one undifferentiated detention grant into two different statutory powers. Constitutional avoidance selects a permissible meaning of a statute; it does not allow its meaning to vary with the constitutional claim of each person detained. The contrary reading mistakes the reason for choosing the statutory construction for a restriction on what the chosen words mean. Mezei concerned a different statutory and procedural setting and does not construe this provision. Parole regulates release without supplying perpetual detention authority. The accepted findings in both cases establish that removal to Cuba is not reasonably foreseeable, and the Government has supplied no sufficient rebuttal. Martinez's release protection therefore stands, and Benitez is entitled to equivalent enforceable protection despite his temporary parole.

### 2. The existing presumption and burdens govern the previously reserved category

**Controlling proposition:** Six months, counted within the removal process rather than added after the ordinary ninety-day removal period, is a presumptively reasonable detention interval. After it, the detainee must give good reason to believe that removal lacks a significant likelihood within the reasonably foreseeable future, and the Government must sufficiently rebut that showing; the interval alone does not require release, and supported evidence that a longer period is reasonably necessary, including relevant evidence about an inadmissible class, may justify continued detention.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Zadvydas's workable removal-prospect inquiry to the statutory category now decided. As detention continues, the reasonably foreseeable horizon shortens. Actual removal efforts, foreign responses, negotiations, documents, receiving arrangements and material detainee conduct bear on that inquiry; permanent impossibility need not be proved. Neither dangerousness alone nor a foreign government's refusal substitutes for the statutory removal purpose. Section 1231(a)(1)(C) retains its separate extension for failure or refusal to make a timely good-faith application for necessary travel documents, or conspiracy or action preventing removal; foreign refusal alone establishes none of those grounds. Section 1231(a)(3) permits periodic identification, necessary medical or psychiatric examination at government expense, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. Renewed custody requires changed removal facts or a separately applicable lawful source and its required process. Section 1226a supplies a distinct statutory source with its own required predicates and process, not a dangerousness exception to the removal-purpose limit. Temporary parole cannot displace effective judicial protection from custody forbidden by the removal statute. Both records therefore require prompt supervised release protection under the existing, rebuttable rule.

### Complete rule, contrary path and relief

The seven apply the same operative words in §1231(a)(6) to every category they cover. Once the Court construes that detention authorization to last only as long as reasonably necessary to accomplish removal, a different constitutional concern for a different category cannot give those words a second meaning. Constitutional avoidance chooses between plausible statutory meanings; it does not establish a variable license to detain until an individual constitutional boundary is approached. The prior express reservation means that this extension requires a present holding, not that the initial construction already adjudicated these petitioners. This statutory conclusion leaves the constitutional status distinction intact where it actually matters.

The six-month presumption operates within the removal process, not as an extra period after the ordinary ninety days. After six months, a detainee's good reason to believe there is no significant likelihood of removal within the reasonably foreseeable future requires sufficient government rebuttal. The foreseeable horizon shortens as detention continues. Six months alone does not mandate release, and supported reasons for a genuinely longer necessary removal period can be considered. Here the accepted lower findings establish lack of foreseeable Cuban removal and the frozen record supplies no sufficient rebuttal or independent custody ground. Section 1231(a)(6) therefore does not authorize continued or resumed custody on the rejected premise. Martinez's protection stands, and Benitez receives effective protection despite temporary parole; both matters return for proceedings consistent with that result.

The contrary path argues that Zadvydas's construction followed constitutional concerns peculiar to admitted persons, and that its express reservation permits a different result for the unadmitted class. Thomas treats that as the actual scope of the earlier decision; he also considers its original implied limit incorrect. The seven answer that the prior construction selected a statutory meaning, while the new category is governed by the same undifferentiated operative words. They do not need to find identical constitutional rights or to convert constitutional avoidance into adjudication of another person's rights.

Parole under §1182(d)(5) is not admission and supplies no independent perpetual detention authority after the removal period. The paragraph (1)(C) extension remains confined to failure or refusal to make a timely good-faith application for needed documents, or conspiracy or action preventing removal; a foreign government's refusal alone proves none of those predicates. Supervision under paragraph (3) retains periodic identification, necessary government-funded medical or psychiatric examination, sworn information and reasonable written conduct restrictions. A supervision violation or renewed removal prospect requires actual lawful authority and procedure before custody resumes. Section 1226a detention requires its distinct certification, specified activity and danger predicates; none is established here. Independent criminal or lawful civil-commitment authority likewise requires its own predicates.

## Precedent Treatment

- Zadvydas v. Davis: extended to the previously reserved inadmissible category under the same statutory grant; its removal-purpose limit, rebuttable six-month presumption and supervised-release implementation retain their force.
- Shaughnessy v. United States ex rel. Mezei: distinguished because its statutory and procedural setting does not determine the meaning of §1231(a)(6); the present statutory holding does not equate constitutional entry statuses.

## Law After Decision

Section 1231(a)(6) now has the same removal-related temporal scope for all its covered categories. The qualified foreseeable-removal inquiry governs the inadmissible category as well as previously admitted persons. Effective judicial release protection, lawful supervision and independently supported renewed custody remain distinct legal operations.

## Separate Writings

Thomas dissents. He would reconsider Zadvydas because the statute authorizes detention beyond the removal period without the implied general duration limit. Alternatively, he would preserve that decision's admitted-person scope and express reservation: the constitutional concerns supporting its construction do not apply identically to persons never admitted. He rejects using the earlier avoidance construction to resolve a materially different constitutional setting. He would reverse Martinez's statutory release entitlement and affirm Benitez's denial of this general duration claim, with implementation under the governing custody procedures rather than an automatic rearrest command.

## Procedure After Action

Both matters return for implementation of enforceable supervised-release protection. The courts must give effect to the unrebutted removal-prospect findings and retain the lawful supervision authorized by the statute. A later custody action must have an actual new lawful basis and available review; repeated short parole periods cannot defeat the adjudicated duration limit.
