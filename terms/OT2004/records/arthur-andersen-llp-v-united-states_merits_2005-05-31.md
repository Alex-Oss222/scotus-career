## Decision kernel and posture

## Controlling holdings and precedent treatment

### Knowing corrupt persuasion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1512(b)(2)(A) and (B), as applicable to the 2001 conduct, requires awareness of the wrongful character of persuasion, together with the specified intent to cause withholding of evidence from an official proceeding or impairment of an object's integrity or availability for use there. Merely intending to impede government factfinding is insufficient; knowledge of the statutory citation or of the precise criminal prohibition is unnecessary.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this construction and the following proceeding and verdict holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Persuasion is often lawful. Advice to assert a valid privilege or to comply with an ordinary document policy does not become corrupt merely because it makes information unavailable. The enacted mental-state language distinguishes that conduct from consciously wrongful interference. Equally, a policy or counsel-participation label does not protect knowing obstruction. Investigative warnings, timing, internal advice and anticipated evidentiary use can establish the required state of mind under a correct charge. Here the instruction affirmatively permitted an honest belief that the conduct was lawful and reduced corrupt purpose to interference with factfinding. It therefore failed to require the wrongful awareness Congress made part of this offense. The Government's interest in preventing anticipatory destruction cannot remove that enacted limit.

### A particular contemplated official proceeding

**Holding and operative rule:** The persuader must contemplate an identifiable official proceeding in which the evidence might be material and act with the statutory withholding or impairment purpose toward that use. The proceeding need not already be pending, about to begin, certain to occur or assigned a known date; no subpoena or successful obstruction is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1512(e)(1) gives the offense anticipatory reach. It eliminates pendency, rather than the contemplated official object to which the specific intent relates. Aguilar supplies the need to respect the enacted connection to official work, but its section 1503 pending-proceeding requirement cannot be transferred to a statute expressly reaching future proceedings. Abstract awareness that records might interest government someday does not supply this connection. Conversely, the SEC contacts and legal discussions could support contemplation under correct instructions; their significance belongs to a properly instructed jury. The challenged charge removed the necessary connection by allowing the prospect of indefinite future government factfinding to suffice.

The applicable section 1512(e)(2) permits the offense even if the evidence is inadmissible or subject to a privilege claim. Section 1512(f) removes a need to prove knowledge of a proceeding's federal character; it does not eliminate contemplation and purpose concerning the proceeding itself. Section 1512(d) supplies a separate affirmative defense, proved by the defendant by a preponderance, when the conduct was solely lawful and the sole intention was to encourage, induce or cause truthful testimony. That defense does not replace the Government's obligation to prove the offense's elements. The separate contention that an informal inquiry is outside the statutory definition of an official proceeding was not preserved and is not decided.

### The errors contributed unreliably to this verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** On this charge and contested mental-state record, the Government has not established beyond reasonable doubt that the instructional errors did not contribute to the verdict. The conviction must be set aside and the matter remanded for lawful further proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** Neder requires attention to the actual verdict, rather than an appellate conclusion that a properly instructed jury could have convicted. Here the charge permitted a disputed mental state insufficient under the statute, the verdict supplies no actual equivalent findings establishing the required culpability and proceeding relationship, and the evidence leaves competing mental-state inferences. Those features together defeat reliable noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt. A finding of deliberate withholding under this charge does not itself establish conscious wrongful persuasion toward contemplated official use. The reported deadlock does not independently prove prejudice; the defect is the absence of a reliable connection between this verdict and the required offense. Another harmlessness inquiry cannot supply that missing connection on this record. This conclusion resolves these instructions without adopting a new general classification or inferential rule for other instructional errors.

- **United States v. Aguilar:** Applied for offense-specific connection and knowledge concerning official work. Its section 1503 pendency rule remains confined to that provision; section 1512's express anticipatory coverage is preserved.
- **Neder v. United States:** Applied for the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden and attention to the actual verdict. Its existing qualified method retains its stated scope; the shared conclusion here rests on absent actual equivalent findings and competing mental-state inferences.

Knowing corrupt persuasion under the charged statute requires conscious wrongdoing and the specified intent concerning a particular contemplated official proceeding. The construction protects lawful persuasion while allowing prosecution of proved anticipatory obstruction before formal process. Andersen's conviction cannot stand under instructions that omitted those requirements. The decision applies the governing version of section 1512.

## Holdings

### Knowing corrupt persuasion

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1512(b)(2)(A) and (B), as applicable to the 2001 conduct, requires awareness of the wrongful character of persuasion, together with the specified intent to cause withholding of evidence from an official proceeding or impairment of an object's integrity or availability for use there. Merely intending to impede government factfinding is insufficient; knowledge of the statutory citation or of the precise criminal prohibition is unnecessary.

**Authority:** The opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices, controls this construction and the following proceeding and verdict holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Persuasion is often lawful. Advice to assert a valid privilege or to comply with an ordinary document policy does not become corrupt merely because it makes information unavailable. The enacted mental-state language distinguishes that conduct from consciously wrongful interference. Equally, a policy or counsel-participation label does not protect knowing obstruction. Investigative warnings, timing, internal advice and anticipated evidentiary use can establish the required state of mind under a correct charge. Here the instruction affirmatively permitted an honest belief that the conduct was lawful and reduced corrupt purpose to interference with factfinding. It therefore failed to require the wrongful awareness Congress made part of this offense. The Government's interest in preventing anticipatory destruction cannot remove that enacted limit.

### A particular contemplated official proceeding

**Holding and operative rule:** The persuader must contemplate an identifiable official proceeding in which the evidence might be material and act with the statutory withholding or impairment purpose toward that use. The proceeding need not already be pending, about to begin, certain to occur or assigned a known date; no subpoena or successful obstruction is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1512(e)(1) gives the offense anticipatory reach. It eliminates pendency, rather than the contemplated official object to which the specific intent relates. Aguilar supplies the need to respect the enacted connection to official work, but its section 1503 pending-proceeding requirement cannot be transferred to a statute expressly reaching future proceedings. Abstract awareness that records might interest government someday does not supply this connection. Conversely, the SEC contacts and legal discussions could support contemplation under correct instructions; their significance belongs to a properly instructed jury. The challenged charge removed the necessary connection by allowing the prospect of indefinite future government factfinding to suffice.

The applicable section 1512(e)(2) permits the offense even if the evidence is inadmissible or subject to a privilege claim. Section 1512(f) removes a need to prove knowledge of a proceeding's federal character; it does not eliminate contemplation and purpose concerning the proceeding itself. Section 1512(d) supplies a separate affirmative defense, proved by the defendant by a preponderance, when the conduct was solely lawful and the sole intention was to encourage, induce or cause truthful testimony. That defense does not replace the Government's obligation to prove the offense's elements. The separate contention that an informal inquiry is outside the statutory definition of an official proceeding was not preserved and is not decided.

### The errors contributed unreliably to this verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** On this charge and contested mental-state record, the Government has not established beyond reasonable doubt that the instructional errors did not contribute to the verdict. The conviction must be set aside and the matter remanded for lawful further proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** Neder requires attention to the actual verdict, rather than an appellate conclusion that a properly instructed jury could have convicted. Here the charge permitted a disputed mental state insufficient under the statute, the verdict supplies no actual equivalent findings establishing the required culpability and proceeding relationship, and the evidence leaves competing mental-state inferences. Those features together defeat reliable noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt. A finding of deliberate withholding under this charge does not itself establish conscious wrongful persuasion toward contemplated official use. The reported deadlock does not independently prove prejudice; the defect is the absence of a reliable connection between this verdict and the required offense. Another harmlessness inquiry cannot supply that missing connection on this record. This conclusion resolves these instructions without adopting a new general classification or inferential rule for other instructional errors.

## Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Aguilar:** Applied for offense-specific connection and knowledge concerning official work. Its section 1503 pendency rule remains confined to that provision; section 1512's express anticipatory coverage is preserved.
- **Neder v. United States:** Applied for the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden and attention to the actual verdict. Its existing qualified method retains its stated scope; the shared conclusion here rests on absent actual equivalent findings and competing mental-state inferences.

## Law After Decision

Knowing corrupt persuasion under the charged statute requires conscious wrongdoing and the specified intent concerning a particular contemplated official proceeding. The construction protects lawful persuasion while allowing prosecution of proved anticipatory obstruction before formal process. Andersen's conviction cannot stand under instructions that omitted those requirements. The decision applies the governing version of section 1512.
