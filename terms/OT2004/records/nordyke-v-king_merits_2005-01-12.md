## Controlling holdings

### I. The personal arms right governs this county-property challenge

**Controlling proposition:** Individuals may invoke the Second Amendment, as incorporated through the Fourteenth Amendment, against a county restriction burdening protected possession or acquisition necessary to protected keeping. Public ownership does not extinguish the right; the lower court must assess the claimants' actual injuries, covered conduct, practical burdens, required justification and remaining preliminary-relief requirements under the governing law.

**Remedial rule:** If the lower court establishes an unlawful operation of the county policy and the requirements for the relief under consideration are satisfied, relief must precisely correct that adjudicated unlawful operation and protect the persons governed by that operation unless the county establishes that narrower relief fully cures the proved violation. The decree must preserve independently lawful safety conditions; the county's narrower-relief showing concerns remedial scope, while the claimants retain their existing burdens concerning standing, liability and preliminary-relief prerequisites.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the existing-law application, conditional remedial rule and bounded remand. Part II has the separate five-member framework coalition identified below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a collective-right or nonincorporation premise cannot defeat the claim. Love v. Pepersack established personal protection, application against the States and acquisition necessary to protected keeping. United States v. Hale distinguishes that coverage from the validity of particular regulations. The county's ownership informs the actual setting and justification; it does not eliminate the constitutional inquiry. The organizers must identify protected conduct and their own concrete injury. The court must examine the challenged fairgrounds applications, meaningful acquisition routes, possession rules, actual exemptions and safety evidence. The lower court stopped at an erroneous merits premise and did not perform the required burden analysis or reach the hardship balance. The record therefore supports vacatur of the arms component and prompt application of existing law. It does not establish an entitlement to the immediate preliminary injunction the organizers seek. An independently unsatisfied indispensable prerequisite may support denial with reasons; the rejected categorical rights premise may not. Anderson v. Spear requires effective, precise relief against adjudicated unlawful policy operation. ### II. Wright's coverage and practical-burden framework supplies the standard

**Controlling proposition:** The claimant must first identify protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation through text, history and precedent; the county must then justify the regulation at the level required by its actual burden. A severe core burden requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective materially less restrictive alternative; a substantial burden requires an important objective, a substantial relationship and no unnecessary or effective elimination of protected activity; an incidental, nonobstructive regulation receives reasonableness review while remaining subject to prohibitions against arbitrariness, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Ginsburg, supplies five direct rationale votes reaffirming the complete framework. Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer accept its binding application on remand without joining this renewed doctrinal endorsement.

**Controlling explanation:** Wright requires the practical operation of a rule to determine the necessary justification. Calling a restriction a safety measure or an administrative condition cannot reduce review when it materially obstructs protected conduct. Its independent categorical rule also forbids abolition of meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home; that meaningful core does not immunize every weapon, person or use. The fairgrounds activity cannot simply be equated with that home setting. Emerson shows how a supported safety record and a suitably confined restriction can satisfy the required justification; a safety assertion by itself does not do that work. The county's reliance on other channels must therefore be tested against the actual acquisition burden, while the organizers' commercial interest in a familiar venue does not itself prove a severe core deprivation. The missing practical-burden, alternative-channel and fit findings are matters for application on remand, not uncertainty about the governing standard.

### III. The categorical facial speech attack does not establish preliminary-relief entitlement

**Controlling proposition:** Ordinary firearm possession and exchange do not become protected speech in every application because a show also includes advocacy, and isolated expressive displays do not establish a basis to invalidate the entire possession ordinance. A demonstrably communicative display receives the protection appropriate to its actual context; the claimant must establish that expressive application and the requested breadth of relief, and the county must satisfy the scrutiny applicable to a proved burden, with viewpoint discrimination separately prohibited.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and supplies nine votes for the facial result and these operative distinctions.

**Controlling explanation:** Hurley protects genuine expression without converting ordinary goods into speech. Texas v. Johnson and Spence require attention to what conduct communicates in context. Some message-bearing rifles may communicate, but those examples do not establish that all possession or exchange at a gun show is expression or that the entire ordinance must fall. O'Brien permits justified incidental regulation of expressive conduct. Under City of Erie, however, that treatment is unavailable when a law's justification and operation principally target communication; general wording alone does not establish neutrality. The record does not establish the expressive breadth or actual targeting needed for the requested facial relief. The facial denial therefore stands, while a properly preserved, concrete expressive-display claim remains subject to its own record and applicable conduct or forum rules. The time, place and manner theory was not pressed on appeal and is outside the question decided; application-specific display and exemption issues require facts absent from this facial preliminary record.
