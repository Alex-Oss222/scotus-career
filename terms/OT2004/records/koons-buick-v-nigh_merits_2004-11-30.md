## Holdings

**The retained general ceiling governs clause (i).** Under the applicable version of 15 U.S.C. §1640(a)(2)(A), individual statutory damages calculated under clause (i) remain within the $100-to-$1,000 range, except where the statute expressly provides another range. Clause (iii) supplies $200 to $2,000 for an individual action involving credit not under an open-end plan and secured by real property or a dwelling. Nigh's personal-property credit transaction falls under the retained $1,000 ceiling.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls this construction and its remedy.

The statute retains a range for the subparagraph, while its new clause sets a distinct range for a defined credit category. A general rule and a specific qualification can operate together. The successive enacted versions confirm that Congress added the specified category's range while retaining the earlier language. The contrary interpretation relies on the general range's location after the lease clause and the presence of three separate alternatives. But location does not require reducing the retained subparagraph reference to the adjacent clause when both monetary ranges can be given effect according to their scopes.

Lamie v. United States Trustee requires enforcement of enacted eligibility and structure; the Court's reading inserts no omitted ceiling and deletes no specific range. Hartford Underwriters Insurance Co. v. Union Planters Bank likewise requires an enacted entitlement before a remedy. Those principles resolve the amount through statutory language rather than a preferred level of deterrence. The $24,192.80 statutory award therefore exceeds the authorized ceiling. Actual damages under subsection (a)(1), and costs and reasonable fees under subsection (a)(3), remain governed by their distinct provisions.
