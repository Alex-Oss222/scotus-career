## Controlling holdings

### I. Affirmative promotion creates responsibility for resulting infringement

**Holding and operative rule:** A distributor that distributes a device with the object of promoting copyright infringement, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps directed to that object, is responsible for resulting direct infringement when the owner proves the culpable promotion and causal connection. Substantial lawful capability defeats culpability inferred from distribution alone, but does not immunize this proved inducement.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly supports this rule, its safeguards and its application to the categorical immunity asserted here.

**Controlling explanation:** Sony protects the distribution of useful technology from liability inferred merely from its capability for misuse. Kalem recognizes responsibility for purposeful promotion of infringing use. Napster likewise distinguishes protected capability from knowing material assistance to identified infringement. Together those principles permit an inducement claim based on the distributor's own affirmative conduct rather than the mere provision of a product.

Decentralized architecture may affect the ability to assist or supervise particular transfers. It does not remove the distributor's responsibility for deliberately promoting infringement resulting from distribution with that object. The record's supported promotion, assistance and business evidence must therefore receive legal effect; lawful capability alone cannot sustain the distributors' judgment.

The distributors' strongest objection is that an intent inquiry could penalize ordinary innovation because users infringe. The rule requires affirmative culpable promotion and resulting infringement under the ordinary civil burden. Ordinary distribution, neutral technical support, product updates, lawful advertising and general awareness of misuse are insufficient by themselves. Infringement volume, advertising revenue and nonfiltering may bear on purpose with other supported affirmative evidence, but none independently supplies the required conduct and connection. The rule creates no freestanding monitoring or redesign duty. Patent inducement provisions illustrate the distinction; the copyright ground rests on the recognized purposeful-promotion principle.

### II. Earlier evidence may illuminate current distribution without enlarging judgment scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Earlier conduct may show the purpose and character of current software distribution when the record supplies an actual connection; its date alone does not exclude its evidentiary use. Review of the current-version judgment nevertheless remains distinct from adjudication of damages for excluded past versions or activities, and each party's motion and each distributor's conduct must receive separate application of the summary-judgment standard.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this application and remand instruction.

**Controlling explanation:** The distributors argue that a conduct-based theory falls outside review because the lower courts confined the judgment to current software. That argument confuses the scope of liability adjudicated with the evidence relevant to its purpose. An earlier promotional act may illuminate the object of continuing distribution without making damages for that earlier act part of this judgment. The required actual connection prevents old conduct from automatically establishing current culpability or causation.

An unpublished promotional draft can evidence purpose, but its development does not prove publication or a resulting infringing transfer. Nor does the rule require a published message reaching every user: the culpable conduct may consist of distribution with the established infringing object. Resulting infringement must still be causally connected to that conduct.

The district court's direct-infringement conclusion is already established and was uncontested on appeal. The remaining inquiry concerns each defendant's secondary responsibility and its causal and remedial scope. The owners' cross-motion must be reconsidered under the correct rule, with the inferences appropriate to that motion; vacating the distributors' categorical defense neither grants the owners' motion nor requires trial of every issue. Summary judgment remains available where the actual record warrants it.

### III. Independent secondary theories and effective relief retain their own predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** Knowing material assistance to identified infringement remains a distinct contributory theory, and vicarious liability requires both direct financial benefit and a right and practical ability to supervise. Relief must address proved infringement and culpable conduct through feasible measures that stop any established continuing inducement practice and its supported effects while preserving lawful operation; an equally effective narrower measure can satisfy that obligation.

**Authority:** The unanimous Court applies Napster's separate liability and remedial rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Napster's supervisory ground rested on actual account exclusion and index control. Those capacities cannot be inferred for these decentralized distributors from advertising receipts or theoretical power to cease distributing software. The inducement rule supplies a separate ground for responsibility, not the missing supervisory fact.

The lower court must identify the conduct and infringement attributable to each defendant before setting monetary or injunctive relief. A continuing unlawful practice requires a remedy reaching that practice and its supported effects. Defendants may establish that narrower measures fully cure the injury while preserving lawful uses. Feasibility, effective protection and lawful operation guide the remedial terms; sharing software for lawful and unlawful files alone cannot justify a blanket technology prohibition. Any actually invoked statutory service protection must be considered under its own conditions and remedial limits.

## Event

Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Studios Inc. v. Grokster, Ltd., No. 04-480. Argued March 29, 2005; decided June 27, 2005. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment for Grokster and StreamCast concerning distribution of their then-current peer-to-peer software.

The questions concern purposeful promotion of copyright infringement despite lawful software capability and decentralized operation, the distinct Sony distribution theory, and the independent predicates for secondary liability and relief. Both sides moved for summary judgment. The district court limited the reviewed judgment to the then-current versions, leaving damages arising from past versions and other past activities outside that ruling. It held that the identified users' unauthorized downloads directly infringed the owners' copyrights; that conclusion was uncontested on appeal.

The distributors offer free file-sharing software supported by advertising. The record includes lawful uses, extensive unauthorized sharing, marketing aimed at former Napster users, user assistance and business evidence. Some StreamCast promotional materials were developed as drafts, with publication unestablished. The owners seek vacatur and adjudication of liability and relief; the distributors invoke lawful capability and the limits of their decentralized systems.

## Holdings

All nine Justices join the Court's inducement and procedural holdings. The separate Sony applications have only the joins stated in Opinion Topology and supply no controlling application or new minimum-use test.

### I. Affirmative promotion creates responsibility for resulting infringement

**Holding and operative rule:** A distributor that distributes a device with the object of promoting copyright infringement, shown by clear expression or other affirmative steps directed to that object, is responsible for resulting direct infringement when the owner proves the culpable promotion and causal connection. Substantial lawful capability defeats culpability inferred from distribution alone, but does not immunize this proved inducement.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court directly supports this rule, its safeguards and its application to the categorical immunity asserted here.

**Controlling explanation:** Sony protects the distribution of useful technology from liability inferred merely from its capability for misuse. Kalem recognizes responsibility for purposeful promotion of infringing use. Napster likewise distinguishes protected capability from knowing material assistance to identified infringement. Together those principles permit an inducement claim based on the distributor's own affirmative conduct rather than the mere provision of a product.

Decentralized architecture may affect the ability to assist or supervise particular transfers. It does not remove the distributor's responsibility for deliberately promoting infringement resulting from distribution with that object. The record's supported promotion, assistance and business evidence must therefore receive legal effect; lawful capability alone cannot sustain the distributors' judgment.

The distributors' strongest objection is that an intent inquiry could penalize ordinary innovation because users infringe. The rule requires affirmative culpable promotion and resulting infringement under the ordinary civil burden. Ordinary distribution, neutral technical support, product updates, lawful advertising and general awareness of misuse are insufficient by themselves. Infringement volume, advertising revenue and nonfiltering may bear on purpose with other supported affirmative evidence, but none independently supplies the required conduct and connection. The rule creates no freestanding monitoring or redesign duty. Patent inducement provisions illustrate the distinction; the copyright ground rests on the recognized purposeful-promotion principle.

### II. Earlier evidence may illuminate current distribution without enlarging judgment scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Earlier conduct may show the purpose and character of current software distribution when the record supplies an actual connection; its date alone does not exclude its evidentiary use. Review of the current-version judgment nevertheless remains distinct from adjudication of damages for excluded past versions or activities, and each party's motion and each distributor's conduct must receive separate application of the summary-judgment standard.

**Authority:** All nine Justices adopt this application and remand instruction.

**Controlling explanation:** The distributors argue that a conduct-based theory falls outside review because the lower courts confined the judgment to current software. That argument confuses the scope of liability adjudicated with the evidence relevant to its purpose. An earlier promotional act may illuminate the object of continuing distribution without making damages for that earlier act part of this judgment. The required actual connection prevents old conduct from automatically establishing current culpability or causation.

An unpublished promotional draft can evidence purpose, but its development does not prove publication or a resulting infringing transfer. Nor does the rule require a published message reaching every user: the culpable conduct may consist of distribution with the established infringing object. Resulting infringement must still be causally connected to that conduct.

The district court's direct-infringement conclusion is already established and was uncontested on appeal. The remaining inquiry concerns each defendant's secondary responsibility and its causal and remedial scope. The owners' cross-motion must be reconsidered under the correct rule, with the inferences appropriate to that motion; vacating the distributors' categorical defense neither grants the owners' motion nor requires trial of every issue. Summary judgment remains available where the actual record warrants it.

### III. Independent secondary theories and effective relief retain their own predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** Knowing material assistance to identified infringement remains a distinct contributory theory, and vicarious liability requires both direct financial benefit and a right and practical ability to supervise. Relief must address proved infringement and culpable conduct through feasible measures that stop any established continuing inducement practice and its supported effects while preserving lawful operation; an equally effective narrower measure can satisfy that obligation.

**Authority:** The unanimous Court applies Napster's separate liability and remedial rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Napster's supervisory ground rested on actual account exclusion and index control. Those capacities cannot be inferred for these decentralized distributors from advertising receipts or theoretical power to cease distributing software. The inducement rule supplies a separate ground for responsibility, not the missing supervisory fact.

The lower court must identify the conduct and infringement attributable to each defendant before setting monetary or injunctive relief. A continuing unlawful practice requires a remedy reaching that practice and its supported effects. Defendants may establish that narrower measures fully cure the injury while preserving lawful uses. Feasibility, effective protection and lawful operation guide the remedial terms; sharing software for lawful and unlawful files alone cannot justify a blanket technology prohibition. Any actually invoked statutory service protection must be considered under its own conditions and remedial limits.

### IV. The separate Sony application has no controlling resolution

**Existing controlling rule:** Genuine current and prospective substantial lawful capability protects against liability inferred from product distribution alone. Present infringement percentages are not the exclusive measure; Napster's independent knowing-assistance route and today's inducement rule remain available on their own facts.

**Authority and unresolved scope:** The Court unanimously preserves that capability rule. Ginsburg and Kennedy would reject the distributors' favorable Sony determination because material dispute remains; Breyer, Stevens and O'Connor would sustain that determination. Neither product-specific application has five joins, and the opposing applications cannot be combined as narrower versions of one rule. The separate distribution claim therefore remains available without a new Court resolution of this contested showing or a new minimum-use threshold.

**Record limits:** The survey classified available files rather than actual downloads. Its residual ten percent was unknown, not established lawful use; copyrighted status likewise did not alone prove unauthorized use. Identified authorized and public-domain uses and their prospective development must be distinguished from an unsupported numerical conclusion. The differing legal evaluations of those qualified facts appear in the separate writings.

## Precedent Treatment

- Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, Inc. retains its protection against imputing culpability from useful product distribution alone, including genuine prospective lawful capability. Affirmative inducement falls outside that protection.
- Kalem Co. v. Harper Brothers supplies the recognized purposeful-promotion principle, applied to software distribution with an infringing object and resulting infringement.
- A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc. is extended in principle from knowing material assistance to the separately supported inducement route; its particular knowledge-and-assistance, practical-supervision, statutory-protection and feasible-relief rules remain distinct.

## Law After Decision

A distributor may be held responsible for resulting infringement when affirmative conduct establishes an object of promoting that infringement and the required causal connection. Product capability and network architecture do not immunize that conduct. Ordinary distribution and neutral support remain protected against liability on those facts alone. Independent distribution, knowing-assistance and vicarious theories retain their own predicates; the disputed product-specific Sony application has no controlling resolution. Further proceedings apply the announced rule separately to the parties' motions, defendants, relevant distribution and requested relief.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Ginsburg, concurring, joined by Justice Kennedy.** The concurrence joins the inducement opinion and would also reject the distributors' summary judgment under Sony. The relevant inquiry concerns these products' reasonable capability for commercially significant lawful use, rather than benefits attributed to peer-to-peer technology in general. Particularized evidence matters even though lawful use need not be predominant and genuine future markets count.

The declarations and anecdotal examples do not, in this view, establish that capability beyond genuine dispute. General statements about authorized distribution, public-domain works or possible future uses may describe real opportunities without proving the commercially significant prospect asserted for these products. The available-file survey does not establish actual-download proportions; its residual is unknown, and the owners continued to dispute the lawful-use showing. A legal assumption that all uncertainty favors sufficient capability would give too little force to Sony's substantive condition.

The concurrence therefore sees an additional reason to set aside the distributors' judgment and would revisit this theory on a fuller record if inducement does not dispose of the case. It would not grant the owners' motion automatically, adopt a numerical requirement, or make infringement percentages, economic dependence or nonfiltering an independent final-liability test.

**Justice Breyer, concurring, joined by Justices Stevens and O'Connor.** This concurrence joins vacatur on inducement while accepting the lower court's favorable Sony capability determination. Concrete authorized and public-domain uses, together with their plausible development, give practical force to the difference between present misuse and the product's lawful capabilities. The survey's unknown residual is not a proved lawful percentage, and the position requires no precise numerical finding from it.

Sony protects technological development when a tool can serve substantial lawful purposes even though current users predominantly infringe. Requiring detailed profitability plans for each future use, or replacing capability with an inquiry into the distributor's present dependence on infringement, would make that protection uncertain and expose useful technologies before lawful markets have developed. The government's business-centered alternative would shift this balance without a sufficiently demonstrated need or administrable advantage.

The concurrence evaluates the proposed change against Sony's protection of innovation, the strength of the demonstrated enforcement need and the comparative costs of alteration. That assessment is a reason to retain Sony, not a new three-step liability test. Inducement and direct-infringement enforcement provide protection for copyright; Napster's distinct knowing-assistance route remains available. Its protective account concerns liability inferred from distribution alone and does not immunize purposeful promotion or proved material assistance. It would preserve the Sony determination while returning the independent inducement claim for adjudication.
