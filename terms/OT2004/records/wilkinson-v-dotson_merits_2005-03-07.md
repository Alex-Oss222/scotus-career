## Holdings

### Compatible prospective procedure claims may proceed under section 1983

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner may pursue prospective relief under section 1983 when success would require only a new lawful proceeding and would neither invalidate the legal basis of custody nor necessarily require release or a shorter custodial term. The court must examine the challenged procedure, the relief requested and their actual statutory consequences; when lawful official discretion permits the same continued confinement, the possibility of a favorable later decision does not require prior invalidation before the civil claim can be heard.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other eight Justices. The rule governs both complaints and equivalent prospective procedural claims having the same legal effect.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a chance to obtain earlier release is different from a judgment establishing an entitlement to it. Cavanaugh v. Roller already permits restoration of a legally required occasion for discretionary parole consideration while a valid sentence continues to authorize custody. Heck v. Humphrey protects the continuing legal validity of a conviction or sentence, and Edwards v. Balisok requires attention to the actual consequences of the challenged disciplinary decision and relief. Muhammad v. Close rejects a shortcut based merely on the prison setting. Nelson v. Campbell likewise preserves procedural relief compatible with valid punishment while retaining independent conditions on the remedy.

Both requested parole proceedings leave the responsible officials free to deny release lawfully. Ohio's position substitutes the prisoners' hoped-for outcome for the legal effect of the requested judgments. Its analogy to resentencing overlooks the essential difference: a challenge to an invalid sentence attacks the legal authority for custody even though a later sentence could be equally long; these complaints leave a valid sentence authorizing confinement throughout. A general appeal to state-court exhaustion cannot change that classification.

Preiser v. Rodriguez continues to govern relief necessarily directed to custody. An order restoring credits that automatically shorten confinement, vacating a sentence or directing release has a different legal effect. A procedural label cannot avoid that consequence. Here each plaintiff still must prove a federal constitutional violation and satisfy independently applicable screening, administrative-exhaustion and remedial requirements. Whether Ohio's guideline changes violate the Constitution and whether the plaintiffs possess protected liberty interests lie outside the vehicle question and remain for adjudication in the restored actions.

## Precedent Treatment

- Cavanaugh v. Roller is applied to prospective restoration of discretionary parole consideration; its opportunity-only rule remains controlling.
- Heck v. Humphrey is applied according to the necessary effect on the legal basis of custody, not the prisoner's hoped-for release prospects.
- Edwards v. Balisok preserves the distinction between relief invalidating a custody-producing credit determination and genuinely compatible prospective procedure.
- Muhammad v. Close rejects confinement alone as a sufficient reason to require prior invalidation.
- Nelson v. Campbell supports compatible procedural relief with independent procedural and remedial requirements.
- Preiser v. Rodriguez retains habeas exclusivity for claims necessarily directed to release, reduced custody or the invalidity of its legal basis.

This classification settles the enforcement vehicle while leaving the plaintiff to establish the asserted federal right and violation.
