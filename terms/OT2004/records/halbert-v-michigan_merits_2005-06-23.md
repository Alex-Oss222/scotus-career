## Controlling holdings

The seven Justices joining the Court's opinion directly support all three holdings at the stated level of generality.

### I. Counsel must assist the initial appellate merits examination

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment requires appointed advocacy for an indigent plea-convicted defendant seeking initial appellate examination of alleged error in the Michigan Court of Appeals. A State may use a genuinely equivalent advocate-assisted screening mechanism and valid frivolousness procedures, but must supply the legal-assistance function before deciding the initial application.

**Controlling explanation:** Douglas protects fair access to initial merits review. Ross permits later discretionary selection after the defendant has received counseled appellate consideration and the materials that consideration produces. Halbert has received no such first presentation. Michigan's intermediate court examines the merits of the claimed error; the leave label cannot replace attention to that function.

Smith v. Robbins requires an appellate advocate's actual legal-screening contribution while allowing equivalent formats. A plea narrows the available claims but leaves potentially difficult questions concerning sentence, plea and representation. Post-grant counsel comes after the initial screening, and an application form cannot identify and present legal issues as an advocate can. Resource conservation and the narrower range of surviving issues therefore do not justify the missing first-review function.

Michigan's statute provides meaningful but bounded exceptions. Appointment is mandatory for an indigent plea defendant when the prosecutor seeks leave, the sentence exceeds the upper limit of the minimum applicable guideline range, an appellate court grants leave, or the defendant challenges a conditional plea under Rule 6.301(C)(2) or its successor. Appointment is discretionary when all three scoring conditions hold: an alleged offense-variable or prior-record-variable scoring error, objection or other preservation, and an upward departure above the upper limit of the minimum range the defendant contends should have applied. Those routes do not supply the assistance denied here. The constitutional protection follows the initial merits function rather than those exceptions alone.

### II. The whole reported plea record does not establish surrender of this distinct assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty or no-contest plea does not itself waive counsel for the first appellate review that remains available. The State must establish a knowing, intelligent and voluntary surrender of that distinct assistance from the actual record; Halbert's reported advice, confirmations and general plea finding do not establish that surrender.

**Controlling explanation:** Godinez distinguishes capacity from actual waiver. Tovar requires real understanding without a prescribed judicial formula. Stumpf permits the whole record, including counsel's actual explanation and the defendant's confirmation, to establish informed assent. These rules require weighing the affirmative evidence rather than invalidating a waiver because particular words are absent.

Halbert confirmed that he understood loss of an appeal as of right and the described mandatory and discretionary appointment categories. The court found his plea understandingly made, voluntary and accurate. Counsel gave the reported affirmative responses concerning other promises or considerations and compliance with the no-contest rule. Those facts support Michigan's contextual argument, but they do not identify an explanation and intentional surrender of the distinct assistance needed to prepare the remaining leave application. Stumpf's counsel representation specifically concerned the essential elements whose understanding was disputed; the general confirmations here concern different subjects. The content of any additional promise is unspecified.

The statute commands advice about the ordinary appointment consequence, a determination of understanding and provision of a nontechnical application form. That command is not evidence that every unreported act occurred or a substitute for constitutional waiver. Halbert's later requests and reported learning limitations bear on the inquiry but neither make a valid waiver revocable nor alone prove incapacity. On the combined record, the State has not established the particular informed surrender it invokes.

### III. Restoration supplies the missing first-review function

**Holding and operative rule:** When required first-tier appellate advocacy was wholly absent and was not validly waived, the defendant must receive renewed adequate consideration with appointed assistance without first proving that an omitted argument probably would prevail. Halbert must receive counsel, relevant-record access and a meaningful renewed application period, subject to ordinary preservation and constitutionally adequate frivolousness screening.

**Controlling explanation:** Robbins restores a missing process whose value lies in finding and presenting potentially arguable issues. Requiring an uncounseled defendant to demonstrate a winning omitted claim would demand the very professional work withheld. The state court must therefore permit an assisted application before treating the first review as complete. Valid screening may conclude that no arguable issue exists; it must include the required advocate function.

## Precedent treatment and resulting law

- Douglas v. California is applied to initial appellate error correction and extended expressly to Michigan's first-tier leave process.
- Ross v. Moffitt is distinguished by the earlier counseled presentation and later selective function on which its ruling rests.
- Smith v. Robbins supplies the actual advocate-assisted screening requirement, equivalent-format permission and restoration remedy for a wholly absent function.
- Godinez v. Moran preserves the independent inquiry into actual knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver.
- Iowa v. Tovar permits flexible proof of understanding without mandatory ritual warnings.
- Bradshaw v. Stumpf is applied as a whole-record rule; its specific counsel explanation is distinguished from general plea-compliance confirmations.
- Kowalski v. Tesmer retains its third-party relationship holding; Halbert presents his own denial.

Indigent plea defendants seeking initial merits review in the Michigan Court of Appeals are entitled to appointed advocacy before the leave determination, subject to valid waiver and adequate frivolousness procedures. The State must establish an actual informed surrender rather than infer it automatically from the plea. A wholly missing, unwaived function requires restored assisted review under Robbins.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

**Justice Thomas, dissenting, joined by Justice Scalia.** The dissent would sustain the judgment on two independently sufficient grounds. First, Douglas governs an appeal as of right, while Ross permits a State to limit counsel at a genuinely selective stage. Michigan affords a leave application after a plea has narrowed the possible issues and supplies appointment in defined circumstances, including after leave is granted. The absence of an earlier counseled appellate presentation is significant, but the dissent does not regard it as sufficient to extend Douglas to this different procedure. Robbins governs the required assistance once that entitlement exists; it does not itself settle this classification.

Second, assuming an entitlement, the dissent finds an actual waiver from the reported record. Halbert repeatedly confirmed the changed appellate rights and finite appointment categories. Counsel's compliance response and the trial court's understanding-and-voluntariness finding reinforce the inference that he understood and accepted the ordinary absence of appointed assistance. The statutory duty supplies the legal context, rather than proof of unreported advice.

The dissent recognizes that surrender of an appeal as of right differs from surrender of help preparing an available application. It nevertheless reads the cumulative advice and confirmations as sufficiently conveying that residual consequence. Under Tovar and Stumpf, demonstrated understanding may be inferred without an express residual exclusion or a prescribed waiver formula. The subsequent requests do not negate the knowing choice the dissent finds. It would affirm on either ground.

#### Statutory architecture and supported record

Michigan section 770.3a generally denies appointed appellate counsel after guilty, guilty-but-mentally-ill, and no-contest pleas, subject to two distinct kinds of exception. Subsection (2) requires appointment for an indigent plea defendant if the prosecutor seeks leave to appeal; the sentence exceeds the upper limit of the minimum range under the applicable sentencing guidelines; the Court of Appeals or Supreme Court grants the defendant's leave application; or the defendant seeks leave from a conditional plea under Michigan Court Rule 6.301(C)(2) or its successor. Each is an independent mandatory trigger.

Subsection (3) permits appointment only when all three conditions hold: the defendant seeks leave concerning an allegedly improper offense-variable or prior-record-variable score; the defendant objected or otherwise preserved the issue; and the imposed sentence departs upward from the upper limit of the minimum range the defendant alleges should have been scored. Eligibility leaves appointment discretionary. This alleged-correct-range comparison differs from subsection (2)(b)'s applicable-guidelines-range comparison.

Subsection (4) directs the court, before accepting the plea and while determining its understanding and voluntariness, to advise that acceptance ordinarily waives publicly appointed assistance for a leave application and other postconviction remedies, subject to the section's exceptions, and to determine understanding of that waiver. Upon sentencing it requires a nontechnical, easily understood application form. These statutory commands are distinct from proof that the actual advice or form was supplied and from the federal constitutional validity of a waiver. No statutory-exception entitlement is adjudicated without its matching facts. The complete enactment is copied in the final annex.

#### Reconciled controlling-proposition candidates and remedy

**H1 - Initial appellate assistance.** The Fourteenth Amendment requires appointed advocacy for an indigent plea-convicted defendant seeking this initial appellate examination of alleged error in the Michigan Court of Appeals. An equivalent advocate-assisted screening mechanism may satisfy the function, and counsel need not advance frivolous arguments. The distinction from Ross rests on both the initial error-correcting function and the absence of prior counseled appellate presentation, rather than the word first alone.

Douglas supplies fair access to initial merits review. Robbins establishes that the advocate's actual legal-screening function, rather than a prescribed filing format, must be supplied. Michigan's mandatory exceptions and conjunctive discretionary scoring route provide real protection in their defined circumstances, but do not supply that assistance to this denied applicant. Post-grant counsel arrives after the initial screening decision. A form cannot itself perform legal identification and presentation. The State's resource interests and the narrower issues surviving a plea do not replace the function at this first merits stage.

**H2 - Actual waiver.** Entry of a plea does not automatically relinquish the distinct assistance required to seek first-tier appellate review. An actual knowing, intelligent and voluntary surrender may be shown from the whole record without prescribed words. The combined reported advice, affirmative responses, general counsel-compliance response and general plea-validity finding do not sufficiently establish intentional informed surrender of this appellate assistance.

Godinez separates capacity from actual waiver. Tovar rejects mandatory ritual warnings while requiring real understanding of the relevant assistance. Stumpf permits an actual specific explanation by counsel, confirmed by the defendant, to establish informed assent; its record contained that offense-element explanation. Here the court and defendant expressly discussed loss of appeal as of right and the affirmative appointment categories, while the counsel responses and finding do not identify an explanation or surrender of the distinct residual assistance. The six Associates give the affirmative evidence weight but reject the proposed inference as insufficient. This is an evidentiary conclusion on the whole reported record, not a rule demanding the precise words absent from it or assuming every statutory duty went unperformed.

**H3 - Restoration.** Vacate the judgment of the Michigan Court of Appeals denying Halbert's uncounseled leave application, and remand for constitutionally adequate first-tier consideration with appointed assistance. Robbins's missing-function rule does not require Halbert first to prove that an omitted argument probably would prevail. Ordinary preservation and adequate frivolousness screening govern that consideration. His conviction remains in force during the restored review.

#### Reconciled opposing grounds

**D2 - Actual waiver, assuming entitlement.** Independently, Scalia and Thomas would hold that Halbert knowingly and voluntarily accepted the ordinary absence of appointed counsel when entering his plea. ## Holdings

The seven Justices joining the Court's opinion directly support all three holdings at the stated level of generality.

### I. Counsel must assist the initial appellate merits examination

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourteenth Amendment requires appointed advocacy for an indigent plea-convicted defendant seeking initial appellate examination of alleged error in the Michigan Court of Appeals. A State may use a genuinely equivalent advocate-assisted screening mechanism and valid frivolousness procedures, but must supply the legal-assistance function before deciding the initial application.

**Controlling explanation:** Douglas protects fair access to initial merits review. Ross permits later discretionary selection after the defendant has received counseled appellate consideration and the materials that consideration produces. Halbert has received no such first presentation. Michigan's intermediate court examines the merits of the claimed error; the leave label cannot replace attention to that function.

Smith v. Robbins requires an appellate advocate's actual legal-screening contribution while allowing equivalent formats. A plea narrows the available claims but leaves potentially difficult questions concerning sentence, plea and representation. Post-grant counsel comes after the initial screening, and an application form cannot identify and present legal issues as an advocate can. Resource conservation and the narrower range of surviving issues therefore do not justify the missing first-review function.

Michigan's statute provides meaningful but bounded exceptions. Appointment is mandatory for an indigent plea defendant when the prosecutor seeks leave, the sentence exceeds the upper limit of the minimum applicable guideline range, an appellate court grants leave, or the defendant challenges a conditional plea under Rule 6.301(C)(2) or its successor. Appointment is discretionary when all three scoring conditions hold: an alleged offense-variable or prior-record-variable scoring error, objection or other preservation, and an upward departure above the upper limit of the minimum range the defendant contends should have applied. Those routes do not supply the assistance denied here. The constitutional protection follows the initial merits function rather than those exceptions alone.

### II. The whole reported plea record does not establish surrender of this distinct assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A guilty or no-contest plea does not itself waive counsel for the first appellate review that remains available. The State must establish a knowing, intelligent and voluntary surrender of that distinct assistance from the actual record; Halbert's reported advice, confirmations and general plea finding do not establish that surrender.

**Controlling explanation:** Godinez distinguishes capacity from actual waiver. Tovar requires real understanding without a prescribed judicial formula. Stumpf permits the whole record, including counsel's actual explanation and the defendant's confirmation, to establish informed assent. These rules require weighing the affirmative evidence rather than invalidating a waiver because particular words are absent.

Halbert confirmed that he understood loss of an appeal as of right and the described mandatory and discretionary appointment categories. The court found his plea understandingly made, voluntary and accurate. Counsel gave the reported affirmative responses concerning other promises or considerations and compliance with the no-contest rule. Those facts support Michigan's contextual argument, but they do not identify an explanation and intentional surrender of the distinct assistance needed to prepare the remaining leave application. Stumpf's counsel representation specifically concerned the essential elements whose understanding was disputed; the general confirmations here concern different subjects. The content of any additional promise is unspecified.

The statute commands advice about the ordinary appointment consequence, a determination of understanding and provision of a nontechnical application form. That command is not evidence that every unreported act occurred or a substitute for constitutional waiver. Halbert's later requests and reported learning limitations bear on the inquiry but neither make a valid waiver revocable nor alone prove incapacity. On the combined record, the State has not established the particular informed surrender it invokes.

### III. Restoration supplies the missing first-review function

**Holding and operative rule:** When required first-tier appellate advocacy was wholly absent and was not validly waived, the defendant must receive renewed adequate consideration with appointed assistance without first proving that an omitted argument probably would prevail. Halbert must receive counsel, relevant-record access and a meaningful renewed application period, subject to ordinary preservation and constitutionally adequate frivolousness screening.

**Controlling explanation:** Robbins restores a missing process whose value lies in finding and presenting potentially arguable issues. Requiring an uncounseled defendant to demonstrate a winning omitted claim would demand the very professional work withheld. The state court must therefore permit an assisted application before treating the first review as complete. Valid screening may conclude that no arguable issue exists; it must include the required advocate function.

## Precedent Treatment

- Douglas v. California is applied to initial appellate error correction and extended expressly to Michigan's first-tier leave process.
- Ross v. Moffitt is distinguished by the earlier counseled presentation and later selective function on which its ruling rests.
- Smith v. Robbins supplies the actual advocate-assisted screening requirement, equivalent-format permission and restoration remedy for a wholly absent function.
- Godinez v. Moran preserves the independent inquiry into actual knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver.
- Iowa v. Tovar permits flexible proof of understanding without mandatory ritual warnings.
- Bradshaw v. Stumpf is applied as a whole-record rule; its specific counsel explanation is distinguished from general plea-compliance confirmations.
- Kowalski v. Tesmer retains its third-party relationship holding; Halbert presents his own denial.

## Law After Decision

Indigent plea defendants seeking initial merits review in the Michigan Court of Appeals are entitled to appointed advocacy before the leave determination, subject to valid waiver and adequate frivolousness procedures. The State must establish an actual informed surrender rather than infer it automatically from the plea. A wholly missing, unwaived function requires restored assisted review under Robbins.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, dissenting, joined by Justice Scalia.** The dissent would sustain the judgment on two independently sufficient grounds. First, Douglas governs an appeal as of right, while Ross permits a State to limit counsel at a genuinely selective stage. Michigan affords a leave application after a plea has narrowed the possible issues and supplies appointment in defined circumstances, including after leave is granted. The absence of an earlier counseled appellate presentation is significant, but the dissent does not regard it as sufficient to extend Douglas to this different procedure. Robbins governs the required assistance once that entitlement exists; it does not itself settle this classification.

Second, assuming an entitlement, the dissent finds an actual waiver from the reported record. Halbert repeatedly confirmed the changed appellate rights and finite appointment categories. Counsel's compliance response and the trial court's understanding-and-voluntariness finding reinforce the inference that he understood and accepted the ordinary absence of appointed assistance. The statutory duty supplies the legal context, rather than proof of unreported advice.

The dissent recognizes that surrender of an appeal as of right differs from surrender of help preparing an available application. It nevertheless reads the cumulative advice and confirmations as sufficiently conveying that residual consequence. Under Tovar and Stumpf, demonstrated understanding may be inferred without an express residual exclusion or a prescribed waiver formula. The subsequent requests do not negate the knowing choice the dissent finds. It would affirm on either ground.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to Michigan for appointment, relevant-record access and a meaningful renewed filing opportunity before first-tier merits consideration. The renewed process must contain the required legal screening function, with ordinary preservation and valid frivolousness rules.
