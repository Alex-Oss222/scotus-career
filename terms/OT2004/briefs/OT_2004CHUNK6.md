## 48. Smith v. City of Jackson

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Smith v. City of Jackson
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1160; judgment below, 351 F.3d 183.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-30 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Age Discrimination in Employment Act permits a disparate-impact challenge to an employment practice and, if so, whether the challenged municipal pay plan satisfies that rule.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for the City on the disparate-impact claim, reasoning that the ADEA does not authorize that theory. The intentional-discrimination claim had a separate procedural course and is not silently disposed of here.
* **Relief Requested:** The older officers seek recognition of disparate-impact liability and restoration of their challenge to unequal percentage raises. The City seeks affirmance, including on the alternative ground that seniority and market competitiveness are reasonable factors other than age.
* **Material Record:** Jackson revised police and dispatcher pay to improve compensation relative to neighboring departments. Employees with fewer than five years of service received larger percentage increases; most employees over forty had longer service. The challenged differentiation used rank and tenure, rather than an express age cutoff. Statistical correlation between tenure and age does not by itself establish intentional age discrimination. The record does not establish that every older officer had the same tenure or that every younger officer received the larger increase.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The claim is against a municipality, so state sovereign immunity does not supply a threshold disposition. The legal availability of the impact theory is preserved. Whether the City can prevail on an alternative summary-judgment ground must be tested against the actual evidence and adequate opportunity to address that ground.
* **Relevant Dates:** Pay revisions occurred in 1998 and 1999; the appellate decision was November 13, 2003. Review is fixed to March 30, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment permits no adverse resolution of genuine factual disputes; statutory meaning is reviewed independently. ADEA coverage and the proof of a particular violation remain distinct.

##### Issue 1: Statutory disparate impact and reasonable nonage factors

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins requires actual age motivation for disparate treatment and expressly reserves disparate impact. General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline addresses the Act’s protection of older workers against disadvantage favoring younger workers, not this impact question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing age-discrimination rule preserves the difference between age and service, while permitting proof that service was deliberately used as an age proxy. Its reservation of impact liability remains operative; neither an inference of intent nor a categorical bar to impact claims follows.
* **Other Governing Law:** ADEA §§4(a)(2) and 4(f)(1), 29 U.S.C. §§623(a)(2), (f)(1), address deprivation of employment opportunities because of age and differentiation based on reasonable factors other than age. The 1991 amendments changing Title VII impact law did not amend the ADEA; their policy cannot be transposed as enacted ADEA language.
* **Material Other Authority:** Griggs v. Duke Power Co. and Wards Cove Packing Co. v. Atonio supply the surviving background for analogous statutory language, subject to the ADEA’s separate defense. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)’s interpretation is relevant for its statutory reasoning; no unexamined deference rule supplies the answer.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Entering law resolves intentional treatment and directional age protection but leaves this statutory cause-of-action question open. An employer’s reasonable reliance on nonage factors is not proof that all impact claims are unavailable.
* **Open Point:** Whether §623(a)(2) reaches disparate effects, the specificity required in identifying a challenged practice, and the operation of the reasonable-factors defense.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fifth Circuit relied on the distinction between discrimination because of age and a neutral policy’s correlated effects. The officers invoke the effects-oriented language of §623(a)(2), its similarity to Title VII, and the express reasonable-factors defense, which has work to do even absent intent. Their proposed rule permits a claimant to isolate a practice and prove an age-linked adverse effect. The City argues that the Act addresses intentional age decisions; alternatively, it says competitive starting salaries and tenure-based adjustments are reasonable. The defense does not require an employer to choose the least disparate of every workable compensation system.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject impact liability and affirm this branch, recognize it but affirm on an undisputed reasonable-factors ground, or recognize it and remand if practice identification, causation or reasonableness presents a genuine issue. A remand restores litigation, not a backpay award. Any subsequent monetary remedy requires a proven violation, actual loss and the ADEA’s independent remedial predicates.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A text-based impact rule can cover neutral practices that deprive older employees of employment opportunities, while requiring identification and causal proof and preserving the statutory reasonable-factors defense. A categorical exclusion would foreclose this class of claim. Recognition of liability need not import Title VII’s amended business-necessity test.

* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No impact-based backpay, compensation or injunction is awarded. The judgment concerns the impact claim under review; separately surviving intentional-treatment litigation keeps its own posture.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: ADEA impact liability and this pay plan

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The employees challenge their own compensation and the statutory question is preserved. The alternative defense is a legal basis for judgment only insofar as the record leaves no material factual dispute.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether neutral employment practices producing age-related disadvantage can violate the ADEA, and whether this plan does so.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the question Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins expressly left open, applying the enacted reasonable-factors limitation without changing intentional-treatment law.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule must govern the statutory claim category and its defense. A broader rule importing Title VII’s amended necessity requirement would rewrite the ADEA. A narrower rule confined to police pay would leave the same statutory question unresolved for other employers.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The ADEA permits a claim that an identified neutral employment practice causes a disadvantage to older employees, subject to the employer’s defense that the differentiation rests on reasonable factors other than age. The employees must prove the practice, protected-age disadvantage and causal connection by the ordinary civil standard; the employer bears the statutory defense. Reasonableness concerns the actual factor and its relation to the employment objective, without a universal least-restrictive-alternative requirement. Here rank, tenure and the adjustment of entry salaries to the surrounding market establish that defense on the reviewed record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers effects-based claims under §623(a)(2), not a free-standing entitlement to equal percentage raises or compensation based on age alone. Deliberate use of tenure as an age proxy remains subject to intentional-treatment law.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Ordinary job criteria remain challengeable on identified impact and causation, but reasonable nonage factors need not be indispensable or the least disparate conceivable choice.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins separates years of service from chronological age and preserves liability when one masks the other. That distinction supports treating this compensation plan’s tenure component as nonage while leaving the separate effects inquiry available. The reasonable-factors clause limits the effects-oriented prohibition without making it redundant. The record identifies a concrete compensation objective and a factor reasonably fitted to it; no finding of animus is necessary to decide this branch.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins: reaffirm its treatment rule and answer its impact reservation. General Dynamics Land Systems, Inc. v. Cline: preserve directional protection. No overruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The separately proceeding intentional-treatment claim lies outside the granted disparate-impact question.

## 49. Rhines v. Weber

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Rhines v. Weber
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-9046; judgment below, 346 F.3d 799.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-30 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a federal district court may stay a timely habeas petition containing exhausted and unexhausted claims while the prisoner seeks state-court exhaustion, and what limits govern that discretion.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit vacated the district court’s stay and remanded to consider deletion of unexhausted claims under its restrictive view of stay authority.
* **Relief Requested:** Rhines seeks reinstatement of a stay preserving federal review while state courts consider unexhausted claims. The warden seeks enforcement of total exhaustion and limitations without an open-ended federal hold.
* **Material Record:** Rhines filed his federal capital habeas petition with more than eleven months remaining under the limitations calculation. Before the district court identified eight unexhausted claims, that period expired. Dismissal could therefore prevent a later federal return. The court ordered him to begin state proceedings within sixty days and return within sixty days after their completion. The stay did not adjudicate any unexhausted constitutional claim.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** There is a live custody and federal-review dispute. Exhaustion concerns whether state courts received a fair opportunity to adjudicate each claim, not whether a stay itself grants habeas relief. Particular claims’ merits and actual reasons for nonexhaustion remain separate from the existence of discretion.
* **Relevant Dates:** The event date above controls date eligibility; later proceedings and authorities are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Pace v. DiGuglielmo concerns a state filing rejected as untimely, rather than retention of a pending federal case. Mayle v. Felix concerns amendment and relation back, not a stay’s existence.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** The limitations and exhaustion provisions of the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA) operate together. Review of the existence of legal authority is independent; exercise of lawful stay discretion is reviewed for abuse.

##### Issue 1: Retention of a mixed petition pending exhaustion

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Duncan v. Walker excludes federal habeas pendency from §2244(d)(2) statutory tolling. Pliler v. Ford requires neutral notice when a court presents an unrepresented prisoner with a qualifying dismissal election and permits narrowly proved equitable protection against court-caused filing loss. Both leave general mixed-petition stay authority unresolved.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules separate commencement, proper state filing, pendency, court-created impediments and lawful remedies. Pliler v. Ford does not guarantee a stay or make a later case an amendment of a closed action.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§2244(d), 2254(b)-(c) and 2254(b)(2), together with federal courts’ ordinary case-management authority. Rose v. Lundy supplies total-exhaustion background; §2254(b)(2) permits merits denial of an unexhausted claim but does not compel that disposition.
* **Material Other Authority:** The district court’s actual timing conditions matter to whether a stay preserves exhaustion without allowing indefinite federal delay. The general stay question remains open.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Federal pendency cannot restart or statutorily pause the limitations clock; a timely retained action is nevertheless different from an untimely new action. The warning and equitable rules address different mechanisms of filing loss.
* **Open Point:** Whether and under what cause, potential-merit and diligence conditions a court may retain the timely mixed action.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Eighth Circuit treated stays as unavailable absent exceptional circumstances. Rhines argues that destroying a timely filed action during exhaustion frustrates the compatibility Congress assumed between exhaustion and review; he asks for bounded case-management discretion. The warden argues that routine stays weaken finality, reward premature filing and invite delay. A lawful stay cannot excuse permanent nonexhaustion or adjudicate a claim state courts have never considered. The particular merits and reasons for omission need evaluation rather than assumption.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A categorical no-stay rule would require deletion or dismissal, with Pliler v. Ford’s notice only where its unrepresented-prisoner and court-directed-election predicates are met, and any separately established equitable protection. Recognition of bounded authority permits remand to examine cause, potential merit and delay. Unexhausted plainly meritless claims may be denied under the statute; a prisoner may instead delete them to proceed on the exhausted claims. None of those procedural paths awards release.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A bounded stay rule can apply to timely mixed petitions without creating statutory tolling for federal pendency or a right to revive closed cases. A strict exceptional-circumstances rule may preserve some discretion but risks leaving its operative standard undefined. Automatic stays whenever limitations expired would disregard the exhaustion obligation and intentional delay.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit’s categorical restriction and remand for review of the actual stay under a bounded discretion standard.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Retain the federal proceeding during prompt consideration of the stay’s predicates. The district court must identify the reason for nonexhaustion, the claims’ potential merit and any deliberate delay, and set reasonable exhaustion-and-return conditions if the stay remains warranted.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief preserves Rhines’s pending application only. It creates no writ, damages, release or automatic reopening for other prisoners.
* **Practical Relief:** The district court must decide whether the existing sixty-day conditions and actual state-court progress satisfy the rule. If a stay is refused, it must permit a meaningful election to delete the unexhausted claims, with the neutral warning required by Pliler v. Ford only if its unrepresented-prisoner and qualifying court-directed-election predicates are met. Rhines had appointed counsel for the amended federal petition, so that notice holding does not automatically govern this represented election. Any dismissal must identify its actual effect on timely federal review.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A bounded stay preserving exhaustion and review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Rhines filed timely, and dismissal after judicial processing could eliminate review. The question concerns disposition of that pending action, not creation of a new application after expiration.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the district court can retain jurisdiction while Rhines promptly exhausts the identified claims.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve reserved stay authority as a new procedural holding, leaving Duncan v. Walker’s statutory interpretation and Pliler v. Ford’s separate protections intact.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule addresses recurring mixed-petition management and supplies a workable standard. A broader automatic stay would excuse strategic nonexhaustion. A narrower remedy for Rhines alone would leave identical filing traps to arbitrary discretion.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A district court may stay a timely mixed habeas petition when the prisoner shows a reasonable justification for nonexhaustion, the unexhausted claims are not plainly meritless, and the record does not show abusive or intentionally dilatory litigation. The prisoner bears the factual showing by the ordinary civil standard. The court must impose reasonable deadlines for seeking state relief and returning, accounting for delay outside the prisoner’s control. Where those predicates are met and dismissal would defeat timely review, refusal of a stay requires a reason compatible with the statute rather than a categorical prohibition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs retained timely mixed applications. It does not toll every federal filing, disregard state procedural requirements, guarantee relief on any claim or reopen a terminated case.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts retain only timely applications under this stay rule and require prompt exhaustion and return. Reopening a closed action requires an independently valid procedural ground.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Duncan v. Walker holds that Congress excluded federal pendency from statutory tolling; retaining an existing action does not contradict that holding. Pliler v. Ford recognizes that courts must not turn a procedural election into avoidable loss of timely review, but expressly reserves stays. A bounded retention rule gives state courts their first opportunity while preserving the federal filing Congress allowed. Rhines’s actual reasons and progress must be found rather than supplied by appellate assumption.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Duncan v. Walker: reaffirm the tolling rule and answer its stay reservation. Pliler v. Ford: preserve its distinct notice, causation, diligence and lawful-remedy rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate constitutional merits and any future amendment are outside this procedural question.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded, 9-0 in the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote the Court’s opinion; Souter concurred in part and in the judgment, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens also concurred, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer.
* **Historical Holding:** District courts have limited stay-and-abeyance discretion upon good cause, potentially meritorious claims and absence of intentionally dilatory tactics, with reasonable time limits.
* **Historical Authority:** 544 U.S. 269.

## 50. Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Industries Corp.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Industries Corp.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1696; judgment below, 364 F.3d 102.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-30 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether entry of judgment in a parallel state action deprives a federal district court of jurisdiction over an independently filed action concerning the same dispute.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit ordered dismissal for lack of jurisdiction after a Delaware judgment intervened during the federal proceeding.
* **Relief Requested:** Exxon Mobil seeks restoration of federal jurisdiction and adjudication of any remaining issues under the proper preclusion and procedural rules. SABIC seeks dismissal, invoking the state judgment and finality of the parallel litigation.
* **Material Record:** SABIC and Exxon-related entities disputed royalties arising from petrochemical joint ventures. SABIC sued in Delaware state court; Exxon Mobil commenced federal litigation before the state trial judgment, asserting independent claims concerning the same transactions. Exxon prevailed in the state trial. The federal appeal involved SABIC’s asserted immunity, but the court of appeals disposed of the federal action on a different jurisdictional theory. A later state judgment can alter what claims remain without retroactively changing the nature of the original federal complaint.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The continuing effect of the federal dismissal prevents treating the dispute as automatically moot merely because Exxon prevailed in state court. The Court must distinguish a request to reverse an earlier state judgment from an original claim based on underlying conduct. Any residual sovereign-immunity or preclusion issue keeps its own requirements.
* **Relevant Dates:** The federal action began before the state judgment. The Third Circuit acted March 24, 2004; the event date is March 30, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Petro-Hunt, L.L.C. v. United States concerns preclusion among distinct mineral-servitude disputes. It supplies no effective same-term decision unless one has actually been entered. Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Allapattah Services, Inc. involves supplemental jurisdiction, not review of state judgments.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 28 U.S.C. §1257 allocates Supreme Court review of qualifying state judgments. Original district-court jurisdiction, §1738 preclusion and abstention are separate legal questions.

##### Issue 1: Original jurisdiction and the state-judgment boundary

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia (1998) confines the Rooker–Feldman bar to claims asserting the legal error of particular state injunctions and seeking damages for their direct effects; a connection to state litigation does not bar an independently actionable injury. Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co. separately distinguishes jurisdiction from abstention and protects independent legal damages claims against an inapplicable equitable-abstention rationale.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing federal-forum standards require the precise statutory or abstention channel and preserve legal claims unless that channel lawfully permits dismissal. A jurisdictional label cannot substitute for the elements of preclusion.
* **Other Governing Law:** 28 U.S.C. §§1257, 1332 and 1738. Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman supply surviving background restricting district-court appellate review of state judicial decisions; Colorado River Water Conservation District v. United States concerns the distinct exceptional circumstances for parallel-litigation abstention.
* **Material Other Authority:** The actual complaints, commencement sequence and requested federal relief determine whether the federal court is asked to review a state judgment. Similar issues and an adverse legal consequence from state litigation do not alone establish appellate review.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Jordahl already distinguishes appellate reassessment from independently actionable injury, including when damages are sought. It does not decide the effect of a later state judgment on an earlier-filed original federal action. Ordinary preclusion governs a qualifying judgment’s binding effect in an otherwise competent federal court.
* **Open Point:** Whether that jurisdictional boundary also eliminates an already pending original action when a parallel state proceeding reaches judgment first.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Third Circuit treated federal adjudication after the state judgment as impermissible review of that judgment. Exxon Mobil argues that its claims predated the judgment and sought relief for SABIC’s conduct, so ordinary preclusion rather than loss of subject-matter jurisdiction is the correct channel. SABIC emphasizes federalism, duplicative litigation and the state proceeding’s final resolution, and alternatively argues that no practical controversy remains. These arguments can support claim-specific defenses without establishing that an originally proper federal case becomes an appeal.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse the jurisdictional dismissal and remand for any live immunity, preclusion or mootness issues. If the pleadings actually seek reversal of a completed prior state judgment, the appellate-review bar would require dismissal of that claim. A properly established preclusion defense can end an independent federal claim on its own terms; escape from the jurisdictional bar does not guarantee trial or recovery.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The appropriate distinction can cover state-court losers suing over injuries caused by judgments already rendered, while preserving independent actions based on underlying conduct. Parallel litigation does not itself establish jurisdictional error, and an expansive “intertwined” formulation cannot displace statutory preclusion.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The federal suit preceded the state judgment and asserts injury from the underlying royalty conduct. Exxon’s state-court success does not turn that complaint into an appellate challenge.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a later state judgment automatically terminates original federal jurisdiction.

The prohibition on district-court appellate review applies when a state-court loser brings a federal action seeking relief from injury caused by an already rendered state judicial judgment and invites its rejection. An independent claim remains governed by jurisdictional statutes, preclusion and any properly applicable abstention doctrine, each under its own requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This governs civil original actions, including overlapping state and federal litigation. It does not create a district-court appeal, excuse an absent jurisdictional basis or displace congressionally authorized habeas review.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A complaint nominally styled as damages still fails if its injury and requested relief actually require appellate rejection of an earlier state judgment. A contrary judgment on an independently arising claim instead invokes preclusion.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Jordahl barred damages counts whose asserted wrong was the legal error of state injunctions, while preserving claims based on independently actionable injury. Exxon’s royalty claims concern underlying conduct and preceded the state judgment. Section 1257 does not convert that original action into an appeal when parallel litigation ends; this resolves the timing question Jordahl did not decide. Quackenbush likewise requires a lawful source before surrendering federal adjudication. SABIC’s finality concerns belong in preclusion or mootness analysis, not automatic jurisdictional extinction.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Jordahl v. Democratic Party of Virginia: reaffirm its claim-specific appellate boundary and resolve the previously unanswered pending-action timing question. Rooker v. Fidelity Trust Co. and District of Columbia Court of Appeals v. Feldman: preserve their appellate-review boundary. Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co.: reaffirm source-specific analysis. ## 51. California Public Employees’ Retirement System v. WorldCom, Inc.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** California Public Employees’ Retirement System v. WorldCom, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** 368 F.3d 86 (2d Cir. 2004); lower-court citation, not a Supreme Court docket.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-30 (scheduled merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether 28 U.S.C. §1452(a) permits removal of individual Securities Act of 1933 actions related to a bankruptcy proceeding despite §22(a) of that Act expressly prohibiting removal from state court?
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Second Circuit, on a certified interlocutory appeal, upheld federal retention of the individual Securities Act actions removed as related to WorldCom’s bankruptcy.
* **Relief Requested:** The bondholders seek reversal and remand of their actions to the state courts in which they filed. Defendants seek a federal forum for claims asserted to affect the bankruptcy estate.
* **Material Record:** Pension funds purchased WorldCom bonds and sued under the Securities Act in individual state-court actions. ##### Issue 1: Discovery of a vacatur and diligence

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Custis v. United States and Daniels v. United States direct most attacks on counseled state convictions to their proper review channel. They preserve a possible authorized federal application after successful state invalidation without promising reopening or reduction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing predicate-review rule separates original-conviction review, the complete-denial-of-counsel exception, statutory counting and later invalidation. It supplies no special commencement date and no guarantee that a later application satisfies limitations.

* **Material Other Authority:** The statute distinguishes a change in applicable legal precedent from an event altering an operative predicate judgment. The state court’s vacatur is an adjudicative event, not merely a prisoner’s later discovery of an old legal argument.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Entering law prevents a general collateral attack at federal sentencing, but preserves only procedurally authorized relief after state success. It does not answer whether diligence in discovery includes a judicially imposed duty to obtain the state order earlier.
* **Open Point:** Whether vacatur is a supporting fact, and whether the statute measures discovery of that event or also diligence in producing it.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The appellate court treated vacatur as a legal proposition rather than a fact. Johnson argues that the event removed a necessary predicate and could not be discovered before it existed; his federal filing followed it promptly. The United States argues that the relevant facts were those underlying the state challenge, already known much earlier, or that diligence must require prompt pursuit of vacatur lest prisoners manufacture new commencement dates indefinitely. The distinction affects finality, but the statute’s specific language remains the source. Neither pro se status alone nor a promise of automatic resentencing is a necessary premise of Johnson’s textual position.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can treat the old underlying facts as controlling and affirm, treat the vacatur as a fact but require diligent procurement and assess this record, or treat the newly existing order as the supporting event while requiring diligence in learning of it and timely federal filing. A timely motion returns for its substantive prerequisites; it does not itself erase the federal judgment.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Any vacatur rule should identify whether it governs acquisition of an order, discovery of an existing order or both. It should preserve the distinction between an intervening legal precedent and an event changing a particular conviction’s status, and leave sentence effect to adjudication.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Federal Power Act §313(b) makes supported agency fact findings conclusive; legal authority remains for independent judicial determination and arbitrary-or-capricious review examines the agency’s actual explanation.

##### Issue 1: Functional comparability under lawful rate authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** New York v. FERC recognizes FERC’s authority over covered interstate transmission and preserves statutory limits and state functions. California ex rel. Lockyer v. FERC requires review of the agency’s actual reasons and administrative record rather than substitution of judicial policy preferences.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing administrative principles preserve independent review of statutory power, distinguish it from technical application and reject post hoc counsel rationales. No governing standard commands foreign systems to duplicate every domestic tariff mechanism.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Power Act §§205, 206 and 313(b), 16 U.S.C. §§824d, 824e and 825l(b); FERC’s Order No. 888 and the actual foreign-affiliate market-based-rate policy. * **Existing Rule’s Reach:** FERC may select supported means for addressing market power under its delegated rate authority. Its discretion does not excuse a demonstrated discriminatory barrier or permit jurisdiction over foreign facilities without a source.

Consumers argues that inability to reserve transmission at a known price denies the reciprocal access domestic affiliates must supply. It treats financial products and spot transactions as materially less secure than firm point-to-point service. FERC responds that the whole arrangement provides usable, nondiscriminatory access, with hedges addressing congestion exposure and actual participation supporting functionality. The court below accepted that explanation under deferential factual and technical review. The intervenors defend the foreign design’s commercial operation. Comparability does not require identical organization, but an assertion of competition cannot replace an answer to an evidenced access disadvantage.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance preserves the authorization without adjudicating future discriminatory conduct. Vacatur and remand would be appropriate if the explanation ignores a material difference or relies on unsupported equivalence. A judicial order requiring Canada to adopt the domestic tariff would exceed this proceeding; the available consequence is lawful treatment of the affiliate’s United States authorization.

## 55. Dura Pharmaceuticals, Inc. v. Broudo

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dura Pharmaceuticals, Inc. v. Broudo
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-932; judgment below, 339 F.3d 933.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-19 (historical decision-calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether alleging and proving an inflated purchase price, without identifying a resulting economic loss caused by the fraud, satisfies the loss-causation requirement of a private securities action.
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit reinstated the challenged complaint branch on the theory that fraud-induced purchase-price inflation itself supplied the necessary loss.
* **Relief Requested:** Dura seeks reversal of the inflation-only rule and dismissal of the insufficient claim. The purchasers seek continued litigation, arguing that they overpaid because of the alleged false statements.
* **Material Record:** The complaint alleged false statements concerning drug profitability and prospects for Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval of a spray device. A price decline followed an earnings announcement; the later device announcement produced a different market response. The reviewed branch concerns the device representation and an allegation of loss framed principally as payment of an inflated purchase price. Those different announcements cannot be collapsed into a finding that the claimed device fraud caused the earlier earnings-related decline.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Purchaser status and the existence of a pleaded private action do not dispense with loss and causal injury. The distinct profitability and scienter issues are not silently resolved by this grant. The question is pleading sufficiency under the then-operative rules, not proof of an event-study model at trial.
* **Relevant Dates:** The alleged purchases occurred in 1997 and 1998. The event is April 19, 2005; later pleading decisions and statutory amendments are excluded.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Rule 12 review accepts properly pleaded allegations but applies the governing substantive elements. Rule 8, Rule 9 and the Private Securities Litigation Reform Act of 1995 (PSLRA) must each be assigned to the matters their text governs.

##### Issue 1: Loss causation and fair notice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. retains private liability for qualifying culpable substantial assistance in the governing law, but requires underlying fraud, reliance, causation and recoverable loss. Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A. rejects converting an evidentiary proof framework into a heightened pleading checklist.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing private-securities rule preserves causation and actual recoverable loss under the ordinary civil burden. The fair-notice pleading rule requires a short intelligible account of the claim, without importing trial proof or a new judge-made heightened standard.
* **Other Governing Law:** Exchange Act §10(b), Rule 10b-5 and 15 U.S.C. §78u-4(b)(4), which assigns plaintiffs the burden to prove that the challenged act caused the loss. The PSLRA’s enacted requirements are admitted law; a historical premise that private aiding liability was abolished cannot override Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A.
* **Material Other Authority:** Basic Inc. v. Levinson’s surviving reliance framework concerns transaction causation, not an automatic measure of loss. A security’s later value can change for reasons unrelated to the alleged falsehood.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Existing law requires causal loss; it does not establish that every overpayment allegation is a realized compensable loss or prescribe one exclusive method of proof. Expanded actor coverage does not erase those elements.
* **Open Point:** Whether inflation alone suffices and what fair notice of the causal-loss theory requires in this complaint.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The purchasers argue that fraud caused them to surrender more money than the security was worth and that their pleaded inflation identifies both injury and cause. Dura distinguishes inducing a purchase from causing a loss: a holder may sell before correction or lose value because of unrelated events. The Ninth Circuit accepted the former approach. The United States supported requiring actual loss caused by the misrepresentation rather than inflation alone. The dispute does not require assuming that every fraud must be exposed in one formal corrective disclosure or that accounting for competing causes must be proved in the complaint.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can require identification of economic loss and its causal connection, followed by consideration of amendment under ordinary rules. Affirming inflation alone would permit litigation without a distinct causal-loss allegation. A narrowed affirmance is possible only if the actual complaint already gives fair notice of a supported loss theory beyond the rule the appellate court used.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A substantive distinction between purchase inducement and loss can govern public-market damages actions while allowing different causal mechanisms. A rigid requirement of one corrective announcement, a completed sale or a specified econometric method would require additional justification beyond this record.

## 56. Monsanto Co. v. McFarling

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Monsanto Co. v. McFarling
* **Citation or Docket:** 363 F.3d 1336 (Fed. Cir. 2004); lower-court citation, not a Supreme Court docket.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment described below after merits submission.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-04-19 (scheduled merits event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether a patentee may condition a farmer’s purchase of patented seed technology on an agreement prohibiting the saving and replanting of harvested seed without committing patent misuse? Whether a seed-licensing restriction falling within the patent grant is necessarily immune from a Sherman Act tying challenge?
* **Question Granted:** The question presented above.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit affirmed contract liability and rejection of misuse and antitrust defenses, but vacated the liquidated-damages award and remanded for actual damages.
* **Relief Requested:** McFarling seeks relief from the liability judgment and dismissal of his antitrust claim. Monsanto seeks enforcement of the license restrictions and preservation of its liability victory. The already ordered damages remand is a separate component.
* **Material Record:** McFarling purchased Roundup Ready soybean seed subject to a technology agreement and saved harvested seed for replanting. The asserted patent claims covered the genetic technology and qualifying seed across generations. His proposed alternative was payment of the technology fee while reusing harvested seed. The appellate court treated the restriction as within the patent grant and used that conclusion to reject antitrust liability, expressly declining to resolve the lower court’s separate-product analysis. The patent’s validity was not properly presented as a defense in the reviewed Rule 54(b) judgment.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The Rule 54(b) judgment and federal appellate review supply a vehicle even though other infringement and damages matters remained. Review concerns the 2004 liability rulings; later damages proceedings are outside this judgment. The assumed patent coverage and unpreserved validity issue must not become a new validity adjudication.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific recusal or nonparticipation identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective for this analysis.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None material to the disposition on the stated baseline.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment requires the absence of a genuine material dispute under the correct substantive rule. Patent misuse, Sherman Act liability, contract enforceability and damages remain separate inquiries.

##### Issue 1: Utility-patent protection and saved seed

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc. permits concurrent utility-patent and plant-variety protection and holds that certificate-specific exceptions do not create an immunity from a valid utility patent. It does not validate every sales restriction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing plant-protection rule preserves each statute’s distinct requirements and transition. Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer’s certificate-based saved-seed analysis cannot be imported into a utility patent.
* **Other Governing Law:** 35 U.S.C. §§101, 154 and 271; the Plant Variety Protection Act and its saved-seed provisions; the technology agreement and applicable contract law.
* **Material Other Authority:** The scope of the patent claims, the licensed act and production of additional patented seeds matter. Owning the purchased seed does not by itself decide authority to reproduce patented subject matter.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statutory coexistence question is settled; this restriction’s scope and independent defenses remain subject to their own sources.
* **Open Point:** Whether the presented no-replanting condition impermissibly extends the patent rather than enforcing protection of additional patented seed.

##### Issue 2: Patent scope and an independent antitrust claim

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** CSU, L.L.C. v. Xerox Corporation rejects conclusive patent- or copyright-scope immunity in monopolization cases; its application to this §1 tying defense requires an express extension. Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services, Inc. requires actual separate demand, market conditions and the ordinary antitrust elements rather than conclusive assumptions from product integration.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rule preserves statutory exclusion as a substantial justification without making it conclusive. Intellectual property does not itself prove market power, distinct products, coercion or competitive injury.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sherman Act §1 and applicable tying requirements, with ordinary civil burdens. Patent misuse and an antitrust counterclaim have different elements and consequences.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Federal Circuit treats patent scope as a conclusive defense. CSU rejects that premise for §2 monopolization; whether the same reasoning governs this §1 tie is presented here.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Entering law rejects categorical immunity under §2; applying that rationale to this §1 claim does not establish that McFarling actually proves a tie or merits a compulsory license.
* **Open Point:** Whether CSU’s rationale also forecloses a conclusive patent-scope defense to this tying claim and, if so, whether the preserved record satisfies its independent elements.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Monsanto argues that saving and replanting makes additional patented seed and that the agreement enforces, rather than enlarges, its statutory right. McFarling invokes the saved-seed tradition and contends that conditioning access to the trait on annual purchases forces acquisition of separate germplasm. The appellate court rejected misuse and then treated the same patent-scope conclusion as dispositive of antitrust. Monsanto alternatively argues that McFarling seeks only a compelled reproduction license rather than two separately demanded products. The absence of a saved-seed exception does not itself answer that independent claim.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can sustain the utility-patent restriction against the categorical saved-seed defense while vacating the antitrust judgment for application of its separate elements. If the actual record establishes no triable tie independent of immunity, renewed summary judgment remains possible. Neither rejection of immunity nor a surviving antitrust claim automatically establishes misuse or an entitlement to replant. The unchallenged damages-remand component should not be converted into revival of the vacated multiplier.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The patent holding can concern additional seeds embodying the assumed valid claims. The antitrust holding can reject categorical immunity while preserving the full ordinary proof burden and substantial weight of lawful patent exclusion. A universal license to reproduce or a universal duty to supply rivals would exceed both questions.
