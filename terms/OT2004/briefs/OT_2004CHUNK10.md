## 91. Mid-Con Freight Systems, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Mid-Con Freight Systems, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-1234; 545 U.S. 440
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (historical calendar date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether the federal Single State Registration System preempts Michigan's separate annual $100 fee on Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely in interstate commerce.
* **Question Granted:** Whether the federal Single State Registration System preempts Michigan's separate annual $100 fee on Michigan-plated trucks operating entirely in interstate commerce.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Michigan Court of Appeals sustained the fee as a charge for administration of the Motor Carrier Act and state safety regulation, outside the federal registration requirements addressed by 49 U.S.C. §14504.
* **Relief Requested:** The carriers seek invalidation of §478.2(2) under the federal registration ceiling and appropriate relief from its collection. Michigan seeks affirmance of its separate regulatory fee.
* **Material Record:** Federal registration requires proof of operating authority, insurance and an agent for service, with a base State collecting participating States' permitted fees. Michigan separately charges $100 annually for each Michigan-plated truck operating entirely interstate. The State requires vehicle-identifying information and a decal for this separate program. It appears to waive its $10 federal-system fee for covered carriers choosing Michigan as their base State. The challenged charge predates Michigan's participation in the earlier federal-permit registration system. The record does not establish that paying it is necessary to complete the federally prescribed registration.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The issue is the scope of express statutory preemption, not whether all fees paid by interstate carriers are constitutional. The identity of the regulated information and legal obligation matters more than Michigan's label or the appearance of a decal. A real linkage that conditions federal registration on paying the additional fee would require separate analysis.
* **Relevant Dates:** Congress replaced the earlier registration system in 1991, with the replacement operating from 1994. Yellow Transportation became effective November 5, 2002. The state judgment under review issued in 2003.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Michigan Public Service Commission concerns the distinct §478.2(1) fee for local hauls and a Commerce Clause challenge. This docket concerns §478.2(2), Michigan-plated wholly interstate trucks and federal registration preemption.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None effective in the current baseline; apply any relevant intervening decision before adjudication.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No same-term decision in American Trucking Associations is effective in the current baseline. Its different fee and constitutional ground cannot be used as an existing resolution here.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Preemption follows Congress's enacted scope. A state regulatory label cannot defeat a federal ceiling that actually applies, but a federal system addressing defined registration subjects does not automatically displace all state carrier regulation.

##### Issue 1: The subject matter of the federal registration ceiling

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Yellow Transportation, Inc. v. Michigan upholds the delegated rule preserving qualifying reciprocity in the federal registration benchmark, subject to the $10 ceiling. It expressly does not freeze unrelated taxes and licensing policies. United States v. Mead Corp. preserves the need for actual delegated force-of-law authority when agency constructions are invoked.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The governing rules preserve the benchmark and reciprocity limits within the specified registration system, not a universal fee cap for interstate motor carriers. Statutory scope must be established before a ceiling is applied.
* **Other Governing Law:** 49 U.S.C. §14504(b) and (c), as operative at this event, regulate state registration of federal motor-carrier authority and the associated proof, insurance, service-agent and fee requirements. Michigan Compiled Laws §478.2(2) supplies the separate $100 fee. No later replacement registration statute governs by anticipation.
* **Material Other Authority:** None material.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A State may not add a prohibited charge to covered federal-permit registration. Whether this separate Michigan requirement concerns the same subject is the disputed application.
* **Open Point:** Whether the phrase State registration requirement reaches every state carrier registration or only the defined federal-permit registration system, and whether Michigan's actual program falls within that system.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The subject matter of the federal registration ceiling

* **Reasoning Below and Alternative Grounds:** The Michigan appellate court treated the fee as supporting separate safety administration rather than registration of federal authority and therefore found no preemption.
* **Petitioner’s or Appellant’s Position:** The carriers argue that registration of Michigan-plated interstate trucks, associated information and decals, and the connection to the $10 waiver make the $100 charge an additional registration burden Congress prohibited.
* **Respondent’s or Appellee’s Position:** Michigan argues that its charge does not demand proof of a federal permit, insurance or a service agent, can operate independently of federal-system compliance, and supports a separate regulatory program predating that system.
* **Necessary Concessions and Limiting Principles:** Neither an old enactment date nor a regulatory label is conclusive if the actual charge duplicates or conditions federally covered registration. Conversely, a State's decision to waive a small covered fee does not by itself convert every separate charge into that fee.
* **Solicitor General or Material Amicus Position:** No distinct material proposal is verified in the sources identified here.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

* **Threshold Paths:** The issue is the scope of express statutory preemption, not whether all fees paid by interstate carriers are constitutional. The identity of the regulated information and legal obligation matters more than Michigan's label or the appearance of a decal. A real linkage that conditions federal registration on paying the additional fee would require separate analysis.

##### Issue 1: The subject matter of the federal registration ceiling

* **Merits Paths and Judgment Consequences:** Affirm if §14504 is confined to the defined registration subject and the record establishes a separate program; reverse if the provision reaches the additional requirement or if the fee is actually a condition of covered registration; remand only for a material unresolved operational linkage. A preemption ruling must specify the displaced obligation and any lawful remaining fee.
* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A subject-specific construction can enforce the federal ceiling while leaving unrelated state safety and licensing powers subject to their own constitutional and statutory limits.

* **Record, Source, Posture, and Legal Uncertainty:** The disposition depends on the program as demonstrated in the record. If a preserved state rule actually makes federal registration contingent on the extra charge, that rule must be examined rather than ignored. The brief supplies no unsupported finding about precise regulatory costs or refund totals.

## 92. Eulitt v. Maine Department of Education

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Eulitt v. Maine Department of Education
* **Citation or Docket:** 386 F.3d 344 (1st Cir. 2004)
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** Merits review of the judgment identified below.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-06-20 (scheduled merits date).
* **Question Presented:** Whether Maine violates equal protection by excluding sectarian secondary schools from a program that permits publicly funded tuition at approved nonsectarian private schools when local public schools do not meet a student’s educational needs? Whether parents may assert a religious school’s separate equal-protection rights without showing any hindrance to the school’s bringing its own suit?
* **Question Granted:** Whether Maine violates equal protection by excluding sectarian secondary schools from a program that permits publicly funded tuition at approved nonsectarian private schools when local public schools do not meet a student’s educational needs? Whether parents may assert a religious school’s separate equal-protection rights without showing any hindrance to the school’s bringing its own suit?
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit affirmed summary judgment for Maine on the parents' equal-protection claims, using reasoning different from the district court's reliance on the earlier lower-court Strout decision. It separately addressed the attempted assertion of St. Dominic's rights.
* **Relief Requested:** The parents seek removal of the sectarian-school funding exclusion and public payment for their daughters' schooling at St. Dominic's. Maine seeks affirmance against those claims.
* **Material Record:** Minot operates elementary grades and contracts for at least ninety percent of its high-school students to attend Poland Regional High School. Up to ten percent may attend other approved nonsectarian schools when they demonstrate educational needs the public school cannot meet. The parents pay for St. Dominic's, a Catholic school, and identify Catholic instruction and viewpoint as the unmet need. They did not formally apply because the statutory sectarian exclusion would foreclose approval. The request is for the school's integrated ordinary tuition, not an identified separately controlled secular service. The parents also attempt to assert a distinct equal-protection claim on the school's behalf.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The categorical exclusion can make a formal application futile, but the parents must still show their own concrete injury and the relevance of the ordinary program's eligibility conditions. Their own claim differs from third-party assertion of the school's claim. No obstacle preventing the school from suing on its own behalf is established merely by the parent-school relationship.
* **Relevant Dates:** The First Circuit decision issued October 22, 2004. The June 20, 2005 event is scheduled merits date. Strout v. Albanese became effective February 28, 2000; Zelman and Freedom From Religion Foundation v. McCallum also precede this event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Strout concerns the same kind of ordinary St. Dominic's tuition purchase, but its granted claims were Establishment and Free Exercise, not a final disposition of these different parents' equal-protection claim. Kowalski v. Tesmer concerns attorneys' hypothetical future clients, not these parents' own live claims; no same-term Kowalski decision is effective in the current baseline.

* **Open Point:** Whether the parents' equality theory requires the very integrated devotional purchase the governing cases permit Maine to refuse, or identifies a distinct exclusion from eligible secular work.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Delegated interpretation and the force of an earlier judicial construction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the precedent issue because it is the Ninth Circuit's stated ground and the petitioners directly seek authority to displace that construction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether silence about unambiguity makes a completed judicial construction automatically defeasible by an agency.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose a limiting clarification of the unresolved precedent relationship: actual judicial construction remains binding unless lawfully reconsidered, while a judicially recognized delegated policy range remains open to agency choice. This is Stone's proposed holding, not an existing universal rule attributed to Neal.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the case squarely requires a rule about judicial interpretation and later agency action. A broader abolition of Chevron across all statutes would exceed what this dispute requires; a narrower demand for a particular judicial adjective would let form replace the substance of what the prior court decided.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A later agency interpretation does not displace an earlier court's authoritative construction of statutory meaning merely because the court did not use the word unambiguous; agency choice remains available only within a range the statute actually delegates and the judicial decision leaves open. Portland must be assessed by the legal question it resolved, and any change to its construction must occur through lawful judicial reconsideration rather than automatic agency override.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The relationship between this prior statutory decision and the later formal agency interpretation, preserving actual delegated policy authority and ordinary judicial reconsideration.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Identify what the prior judgment actually resolved about statutory meaning and delegation. An adjudicated legal meaning binds within ordinary precedent rules; a choice expressly or substantively left to the agency remains available under Chevron and Mead.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mead makes authority a prerequisite rather than a reward for expertise. Walton and Yellow Transportation show why a recognized delegated choice can remain flexible. Neal shows statutory precedent can control a later administrative measure, but its express limit prevents pretending it already settled every agency case. The proposed distinction protects both adjudication and Congress's real assignments without making the omission of a formula dispositive.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. Mead Corp., Barnhart v. Walton and Yellow Transportation are reaffirmed within actual delegations. Neal v. United States is extended only through the stated proposed distinction, not recast as a universal prior holding. Chevron's general framework is not overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A general challenge to Chevron across unrelated statutory delegations lies outside the presented relationship between this earlier judicial construction and the FCC's later interpretation.

##### Issue 2: The offered transmission component under the statutory definitions

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory classification independently because it determines whether the challenged order can survive even apart from Portland's precedential status.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether integration with information capabilities removes the transmission service actually offered for a fee.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Independently construe the statutory boundary and reject the FCC's categorical exclusion on the described offering, even if controlling deference would otherwise be available.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the definition must apply to the actual bundled offering and resist evasion through packaging. A broader rule treating every use of telecommunications as a public transmission offering would overreach; a narrower rule requiring a separately priced transmission product would add words Congress did not enact.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A provider offers telecommunications service when it sells end users the defined transmission of their chosen information between their chosen points, even when that service is bundled with information-processing capabilities and not separately priced. The described cable broadband offering includes that transmission service, so integration alone cannot support the FCC's categorical exclusion from Title II.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The transmission component actually offered to cable broadband subscribers, not every information service that merely uses another carrier's transmission as an input.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Apply the definitions to the service actually offered. A facilities-based seller's public transmission offering differs from an information provider's mere use of a carrier's transmission as an input.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The definition focuses on transmission, user choice and an offering for a fee, not separate billing. The customer pays for the ability to send and receive selected information through the cable network; additional processing capabilities do not erase that offered function. Gulf Power expressly reserved this classification and cannot supply a contrary answer. Regulatory consequences can be addressed through Congress's forbearance mechanism, not by rewriting coverage to avoid them.
* **Precedent Treatment:** National Cable & Telecommunications Association v. Gulf Power Co. is applied at its express reservation. AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board and United States v. Mead Corp. preserve statutory limits on implementation. No previously settled Supreme Court classification is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

##### Issue 3: Reasoned treatment of cable and DSL and the agency remand

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Preserve the independent APA obligation but do not issue an unnecessary final arbitrariness holding after rejecting the classification's legal premise.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What the FCC may decide on remand within the corrected statutory boundary.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing reasoned-decisionmaking requirements and reserve the factual implementation choices Congress actually assigned.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow: the legal error requires a new agency implementation within the statute. A broader judicial regulatory plan would usurp assigned choices; a narrower remand leaving the rejected categorical exemption operative would fail to implement the statutory holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FCC must implement the statutory classification through its lawful regulatory and forbearance powers, explaining material technological and market distinctions on the administrative record; policy reasons cannot preserve the invalid categorical exclusion. The independent cable-versus-DSL arbitrariness question need not be finally decided on an agency framework that must be reconsidered.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Implementation of the challenged order after correction of its statutory premise, not every broadband regulation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Smiley permits reasoned administrative flexibility within an actual assignment. It does not permit classification by preferred policy outcome. The Commission should make the technical and market findings relevant to forbearance and particular duties itself, while the court ensures that the statutory category and enforceable review remain intact.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Smiley v. Citibank is applied within its existing authority and explanation limits; no new general administrative-law test is created.
