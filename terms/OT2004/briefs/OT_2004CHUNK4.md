## 30. Johnson v. California, No. 03-636

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Johnson v. California, No. 03-636
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-636; 543 U.S. 499 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of summary judgment in a §1983 action challenging a prison reception-center housing policy.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-02-23; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether California’s express racial classification in reception-center double-cell assignments is governed by the constitutional standard for governmental racial classifications or by general deference to prison regulations.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Ninth Circuit affirmed judgment for prison officials under Turner v. Safley’s deferential penological standard; the district court had relied on qualified immunity.
* **Relief Requested:** Johnson seeks reversal, adjudication of the policy under the governing equality standard, damages from former directors for their own conduct and prospective relief against the responsible official. California seeks affirmance based on security and individual immunity.
* **Material Record:** California generally assigns reception-center cellmates by race for up to sixty days on entry and transfer while assessing permanent placement. Johnson experienced the practice on initial imprisonment and subsequent transfers. Officials cite racial gangs and violence; other prison areas are integrated. The policy’s near-categorical operation is acknowledged, but whether particular periods and assignments require separation is not established merely by those general descriptions. The record distinguishes prospective policy correction from personal damages for earlier conduct.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The policy personally classified Johnson. The case concerns reception housing, not an uninjured observer’s objection. Current standing and official substitution must support prospective relief; damages require personal responsibility and conduct-date immunity analysis. The other Johnson v. California matter on the list concerns jury selection and is distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** Complaint filed February 24, 1995; appellate judgment reported in 2003; argument November 2, 2004; event February 23, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Johnson v. California, No. 04-6964, concerns peremptory strikes. Neither shared caption nor race-related subject merges the records or remedies.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Brosseau v. Haugen could affect the individual-immunity framework if an effective decision changes it before this event. No such decision is identified; existing Harlow and Anderson law remains operative, and the race-classification merits are separate.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Summary judgment cannot resolve material security facts against the nonmovant. Under the Saucier v. Katz, no compulsory merits-first sequence is imposed; immunity remains independent of prospective relief. Any prison injunction must satisfy 18 U.S.C. §3626.

##### Issue 1: The governing racial-classification standard

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña establishes one common substantial-justification standard for federal, state and local racial classifications, displacing categorical strict-scrutiny formulation under governing law. Brown v. City of Oneonta confirms that deliberate racial selection requires that justification even without hostility. General prison deference does not itself decide whether that specific equality standard is displaced.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Government must establish an important lawful objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship, and justification for coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives that comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes and allocation for their own sake are impermissible.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; 42 U.S.C. §1983; 18 U.S.C. §3626 for prospective prison relief.
* **Material Other Authority:** Turner v. Safley supplies its surviving prison-regulation framework; Lee v. Washington rejects racial segregation as an ordinary incident of incarceration while recognizing genuine security circumstances. Their scopes must be reconciled with the equality rule, not with historical Adarand language.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The common rule governs deliberate classifications across public functions. Custodial danger affects the evidence, alternatives and practical fit; it is not an exemption from equality.
* **Open Point:** How the common standard applies to blanket reception separation and what present evidence supports each part of the policy, including repeated transfers.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Johnson argues that the State classifies him by race and must justify that treatment rather than invoke ordinary prison discretion. California argues that reception officials lack information, gangs are organized racially and short-term separation prevents violence. The court below accepted general penological reasonableness. The strongest legitimate limit on Johnson’s claim is that actual threats and feasibility can justify fitted precautions; the strongest limit on California’s claim is that security expertise does not validate racial assumptions without an operational fit. The United States, as amicus, urges strict scrutiny and a finding that the broad reception policy fails even that fact-sensitive inquiry, emphasizing known nonviolent transferees, the absence of specific reception-cell violence and the State’s contrary use of integration elsewhere. It also argues that the policy fails Turner. Those operational objections bear on the common substantial-justification inquiry, but the brief’s historical strict-scrutiny premise is advocacy rather than entering law.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Vacatur permits application of the correct equality standard with distinct adjudication of damages and prospective relief. A fully supported justification could sustain particular assignments or durations. Failure of justification could support policy-wide correction at the proved scope, while conduct-date immunity could still defeat individual damages. Dismissal of a prospective branch requires an actual standing or proper-defendant defect, not merely the availability of damages.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The common-standard path establishes no categorical immunity for prisons and no categorical invalidity of every emergency precaution. A general-deference path would require identifying and defending a limitation of the existing racial rule. Relief must distinguish a proved common policy violation from individual historical responsibility.
* **Material Uncertainty:** No final finding establishes that all applications are necessary or that all former officials lacked fair warning. Historical nonparticipation does not determine the bench.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The governing racial-classification standard

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Johnson was repeatedly subjected to the challenged classification. The preserved standard-of-review issue can be resolved without deciding every personal-immunity or security fact.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether reception-center racial assignment escapes the common governmental racial-classification standard.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña to the prison setting; do not replace it with either historical strict scrutiny or general rational deference.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate, reaching the explicit housing policy and comparable custodial classifications. A universal ban on every emergency separation would exceed the security record. A rule confined to Johnson’s particular transfer would leave the recurring policy’s legal standard unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An express racial prison-housing classification must satisfy the common substantial-justification standard, and custody does not relieve the State of its burden to justify the classification’s actual coverage, duration, burdens and alternatives. Preventing substantiated violence is an important lawful objective, but generalized racial assumptions or administrative convenience cannot establish that a blanket entry-and-transfer policy substantially serves it. The judgment under a materially more deferential standard must be vacated.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Explicit racial cell assignments for new arrivals and transferees. Ordinary classification by individualized threats, gang activity and other lawfully relevant information remains available under its proper source.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts evaluate supported emergencies and genuinely unavailable information within the same standard. They do not presume that a sixty-day period or a transfer automatically requires racial separation.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña requires evidence-supported fit and actual-burden justification for every government’s racial treatment. Brown v. City of Oneonta prevents a benign explanation from avoiding that trigger. Security expertise can support factual judgments, but cannot shift the burden to a prisoner to disprove all conceivable dangers. Existing information about transferees and the integrated operation of other areas are relevant comparisons, not conclusive proof that every temporary precaution is unnecessary.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Extend the application of Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña to express prison housing classifications; preserve Turner v. Safley for matters its general framework governs.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual damages immunity and disputed security facts require adjudication on the proper record. Until those issues are decided, courts apply the conduct-date immunity rule and the common equality standard independently.

## 31. Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Dowhal v. SmithKline Beecham Consumer Healthcare
* **Citation or Docket:** 32 Cal. 4th 910; California Supreme Court No. S109306.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the state supreme court’s federal-preemption ruling.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-02-23; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether federal drug law preempts California’s Proposition 65 warning for nicotine-replacement products when the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically prohibited that warning, notwithstanding Congress’s preservation of preexisting state warning requirements? Whether the FDA may prohibit an otherwise truthful state-required warning because it concludes that the warning would mislead consumers about comparative health risks?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The California Supreme Court held that the savings clause defeats the express uniformity preemption provision but does not displace an independently demonstrated actual conflict. It found the requested warning incompatible with the FDA’s specific labeling decisions and reversed the intermediate appellate judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Dowhal seeks enforcement of the state warning requirement and associated statutory relief. The manufacturers and retailers seek judgment rejecting that claim on federal-preemption grounds.
* **Material Record:** The products deliver nicotine to assist smoking cessation. California listed nicotine as a reproductive toxicant. FDA-approved labeling advises pregnant users to consult a health professional, explains smoking’s danger and addresses the comparative risks and uncertainty of replacement therapy. FDA repeatedly considered and rejected the additional Proposition 65 warning, concerned that it could discourage cessation or imply a false risk comparison. This is a dispute over the legal effect of those specific actions, not an independent judicial finding that the products are safe during pregnancy.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The judgment turns on federal preemption of an identified state duty. The claimant’s state statutory enforcement standing and remaining remedies must not be confused with a federal right to enforce the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FDCA). No general damages cause under federal labeling law is asserted.
* **Relevant Dates:** California Supreme Court decision April 15, 2004; assigned event February 23, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Bates v. Dow Agrosciences LLC is scheduled later and concerns pesticide-labeling preemption under a different statute; it supplies no entering rule here.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Identify the exact state duty and legally operative federal action before finding conflict. Review the scope of agency authority independently and assess authorized technical judgments on their actual administrative record.

##### Issue 1: Saved state requirements and actual conflict

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Geier v. American Honda Motor Co. preserves claims under savings language while permitting independently demonstrated conflict with an operative federal command or supported regulatory objective. Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick requires an operative federal basis, not regulatory absence. Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr preserves compatible state duties; approval alone supplies no general immunity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the express clause and savings clause together; then identify the particular federal command, its authority and the precise incompatible state duty. General regulatory objectives or approval alone do not establish conflict.
* **Other Governing Law:** 21 U.S.C. §§352(a), 355 and 379r, especially §379r(d)(2); California Health and Safety Code §25249.6. The 1997 provision preserves qualifying preexisting initiative requirements from that section’s express preemption, not expressly from all valid federal law.
* **Material Other Authority:** The contemporaneous FDA approval conditions and specific warning determinations supply the possible conflict. Later federal labeling amendments are not backdated.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law permits actual-conflict analysis despite savings language while demanding an operative federal source. It does not itself decide the FDA’s authority or the reach of the particular prohibition.
* **Open Point:** Whether the retained California duty requires the warning the FDA validly forbade and whether a genuinely compatible form of compliance remains.

##### Issue 2: FDA authority over literally true but misleading labeling

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The governing decisions preserve judicial responsibility for statutory delegation and require agency action to rest on its authorized domain and actual record. Federal preemption cannot enlarge agency power; the statutory misbranding rule must authorize the prohibition before supremacy attaches.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish literal accuracy from the message conveyed in context. Identify statutory labeling authority, an authoritative action, its evidentiary basis and its treatment of material contrary information. Do not supply a litigation rationale for the agency.
* **Other Governing Law:** 21 U.S.C. §§321(n), 352(a) and 355 authorize evaluation of misleading labeling in context and approval of new-drug labeling.
* **Material Other Authority:** The agency’s comparative-risk explanation is a technical judgment relevant to misleadingness. It is not a power to suppress any truthful statement simply because officials prefer another public-health message.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The statutory term misleading can reach context and omission, not only statements false in isolation. The authority remains product- and labeling-specific.
* **Open Point:** Whether the challenged comparative-risk determination rests on the actual statutory criteria and administrative basis.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Dowhal relies on Congress’s deliberate preservation of Proposition 65 and the warning’s factual accuracy. The companies rely on FDA’s specific rejection and the impossibility of obeying both commands. The State’s broad preservation argument must account for the clause’s reference to this section; the companies must account for the distinction between approval and prohibition and for compatible compliance alternatives. FDA’s concern was a misleading comparison with smoking, not an assertion that nicotine lacks reproductive risk. No unverified oral concession or broader FDA policy is imported.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A valid and actual conflict supports judgment for defendants on the identified warning claim. If FDA lacked authority, issued only a nonbinding preference or left a compatible compliance route, the preemption judgment would require reversal or a targeted remand. A broader express-preemption holding would disregard the initiative savings provision.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The case can establish that saved state requirements remain subject to demonstrated conflict with valid particular federal commands, and that misleadingness is contextual. It cannot resolve all state product duties or immunize all FDA-approved products.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The legal force and scope of each FDA communication must be traced to the contemporaneous approval framework. The historical state opinion supplies that account; no new safety conclusion or later labeling rule is assumed.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The actual warning demand and identified federal actions permit a focused supremacy determination.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §379r(d)(2) protects this warning from independently established conflict preemption.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current actual-conflict doctrine.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow, because the dispute concerns a specifically rejected warning. Broad immunity for all nicotine-product claims would lack support. Restricting the decision to the particular manufacturer’s name would obscure the operative warning conflict.

The demand for the specifically prohibited warning cannot be enforced while that operative federal decision remains valid.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The particular warning and covered products for which the actual federal command establishes conflict; compatible warnings and independent lawful duties are not extinguished by approval alone.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts identify the actual federal labeling command and assess any legally available alternative means of complying with the state duty. A different warning or communication is preempted only if the relevant federal action reaches and conflicts with it.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.

* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.; preserve Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick and Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr’s operative-source and compatible-duty limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different warnings, products and nonlabel communications require their own federal coverage and conflict showing.

##### Issue 2: FDA authority over literally true but misleading labeling

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The source and validity of the asserted federal command are necessary antecedents to preemption.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the FDA’s labeling authority can reach a truthful statement that materially misleads in context.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the enacted misbranding provisions and independent review of authority.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate within drug labeling, because the statutory distinction between truth and misleading context recurs. A general government power to censor truth would exceed the source. A literal-falsity-only rule would omit the enacted misleadingness criterion.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The FDA may reject a proposed drug label that is literally accurate but materially misleading in its actual context when the misbranding statute covers the communication and the agency supports that conclusion through its authorized decision process. It may not substitute a bare preference for consumer behavior for that statutory finding. The comparative-risk explanation supports the particular labeling determination reviewed here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Authorized drug-labeling judgments, not independent restrictions on all public discussion of product risks.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A different claim must establish what the communication conveys and why the FDA’s governing statute and actual action reach it; no general public-health exception to speech rights is announced.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted reference to misleading labeling would be deprived of work if literal accuracy were conclusive. The concern here is the warning’s implication about relative harms and cessation, not denial that nicotine has risks. The conflict analysis therefore depends on a bounded labeling determination, not deference on the existence of federal power.
* **Precedent Treatment:** No precedent is overruled; the actual-conflict rule is applied only after identifying valid agency authority.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An independent First Amendment challenge to a different speech restriction lies outside the question presented.

## 32. Roper v. Simmons

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Roper v. Simmons
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-633; 543 U.S. 551 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of a state postconviction judgment replacing a death sentence with life imprisonment without release eligibility.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-03-01; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit executing an offender who was younger than eighteen when the offense was committed, and whether Stanford v. Kentucky should retain authority at that scope.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Missouri Supreme Court concluded that developments since Stanford v. Kentucky established a constitutional bar to executing offenders under eighteen at the crime and substituted life without release eligibility.
* **Relief Requested:** Missouri seeks reinstatement of death eligibility and reversal of the state postconviction judgment. Simmons seeks preservation of the noncapital judgment.
* **Material Record:** Simmons was seventeen when he planned and participated in Shirley Crook’s murder and eighteen when tried. The record contains serious evidence of planning and cruelty as well as age-related mitigation. The question is categorical eligibility, not innocence or whether this offense was aggravated. State legislation, actual sentencing practice and executions provide the domestic evidence concerning juvenile punishment; the legal inference from that evidence is contested.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The state judgment rests on federal constitutional law and is reviewable under §1257. This is state postconviction review, not a federal §2254 petition; the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA)’s gateway is not imported. A lower court’s duty to follow controlling precedent and this Court’s power to reconsider it are distinct.
* **Relevant Dates:** Offense committed when Simmons was seventeen; state postconviction judgment in 2003; argument October 13, 2004; event March 1, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the question presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Categorical proportionality requires examination of the protected class, objective domestic evidence and culpability in relation to legitimate punishment purposes. The judgment may preserve conviction while changing only the permissible sentence.

##### Issue 1: Capital punishment for conduct committed before eighteen

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Atkins v. Virginia bars execution of persons with established intellectual disability and uses objective domestic evidence, culpability and legitimate punishment purposes. It does not decide youth. Stanford v. Kentucky remains the prior age-specific rule unless this Court changes it. Thompson v. Oklahoma retains its narrower juvenile scope.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the precise class, evaluate enactments and actual practice, and determine whether the punishment is disproportionate in relation to culpability and legitimate penal purposes. Individual mitigation is distinct from a categorical restriction when a class cannot reliably be selected for death.
* **Other Governing Law:** Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments; the applicable Missouri murder and sentencing provisions. International instruments may inform comparison but cannot independently change the United States constitutional rule.
* **Material Other Authority:** Trop v. Dulles supplies evolving standards; Coker v. Georgia and Enmund v. Florida illustrate categorical proportionality. The source of the asserted right remains the Constitution, not foreign majorities.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Atkins v. Virginia establishes that individualized sentencing does not foreclose a supported categorical limitation. It leaves juvenile eligibility unresolved. Stanford v. Kentucky is not silently displaced by analogy.
* **Open Point:** Whether the domestic development and class-based culpability evidence justify replacing Stanford v. Kentucky with an under-eighteen rule.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Missouri argues that the relevant states retaining capital punishment have not produced a sufficiently clear consensus, that legislatures and sentencing juries can evaluate youth and that the crime demonstrates the need for individualized judgment. Simmons argues that enactments, actual rarity and the common developmental characteristics of youth make death categorically disproportionate. The state court relied on developments after Stanford v. Kentucky. A valid comparison must distinguish abolition of death generally, age-specific prohibitions and actual practice rather than manipulate the denominator. Neither international agreement alone nor the brutality of one crime answers the constitutional category.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance can rest on an expressly announced substantive age rule preserving the noncapital judgment. Retaining Stanford v. Kentucky would require reversal of the federal constitutional basis, with state sentencing consequences determined on remand. A narrower individualized-mitigation ruling would not answer the categorical question unless supported by a preserved independent defect; none is manufactured.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The presented record supports a binary age-at-offense capital-eligibility rule or retention of individualized eligibility for sixteen- and seventeen-year-old offenders. It does not support adjudication of life-without-parole, age twenty-one or the overall death penalty.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The state-law consequences of reversal should not be presumed to permit execution without further lawful process. Exact contemporary counts must be tied to offense and decision dates rather than current law.

Other courts applying the rule must verify offense-date age and enter the noncapital correction authorized by their governing law, without disturbing valid convictions.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Capital punishment for conduct committed before eighteen

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The final state judgment directly presents the continued federal validity of juvenile death eligibility; the Court may reconsider its own precedent.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether persons under eighteen when they offended may be executed.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose changing the age-specific law by overruling Stanford v. Kentucky to the extent it permits execution for offenses committed before eighteen.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad within juvenile capital eligibility, because the identified defect is categorical rather than unique to Simmons. A prohibition of all capital punishment is outside the question. Case-by-case maturity screening would leave the identified unreliability of separating juveniles into a death-eligible subclass unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit the death penalty for an offense committed before the offender’s eighteenth birthday. Age at the offense controls; later adulthood, the crime’s severity and a judicial assessment of exceptional maturity do not create an exception. An existing death sentence within that class must be replaced through lawful noncapital sentencing procedures, while conviction and independent punishment remain subject to their ordinary rules.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Every offender whose crime occurred before eighteen, including cases on collateral review through an available lawful channel. No ruling is made on noncapital juvenile sentences or on adults with asserted developmental immaturity.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Age is measured at the offense, so later adulthood does not restore death eligibility. A genuinely disputed birth date requires reliable adjudication before execution can be authorized; the rule does not require reassessment of an individual juvenile’s maturity.
* **Transition:** The proposed rule removes a class from death eligibility and is substantive. It applies to Simmons’s state collateral proceeding and to nonfinal and final death judgments reached through lawful review mechanisms; it does not create new jurisdiction or erase independent procedural requirements.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Atkins v. Virginia recognizes that diminished culpability and unreliable death selection can require a categorical limit despite the availability of mitigation. Youth’s developmental differences concern responsibility, susceptibility and capacity for change, and the domestic legislative and practice evidence supports treating eighteen as the administrable boundary. The especially grave facts here test rather than defeat a categorical rule. The developments in domestic law and practice since Stanford, together with the unreliability of selecting exceptionally mature juveniles, undermine its reliance on individualized mitigation. Replacing that rule protects an identifiable class without unsettling convictions or any legitimate entitlement to continue an unconstitutional punishment. It objects religiously to contraceptive coverage. The exemption requires a purpose of inculcating religious values, primarily employing and serving adherents, and specified religious tax status. Catholic Charities concedes it fails the statutory definition. Eliminating all prescription coverage is alleged to conflict with its separate obligation to provide adequate employee benefits; that contention establishes the asserted burden, not an exemption by itself.

##### Issue 2: Free exercise and neutral employment-benefit regulation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah requires strict scrutiny for objective religious targeting or substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm. Employment Division v. * **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the burden, the actual risk the law regulates, any comparable secular exclusions, discretionary exemption mechanism and evidence of targeting. A religious accommodation does not automatically make every remaining secular rule nonneutral.

* **Open Point:** If jurisdiction is established, whether the limited exemption or other exclusions defeat general applicability and whether any required scrutiny is satisfied.

##### Issue 3: Religious-employer definition and establishment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Agostini v. Felton retains Lemon only as a unified inquiry into the actual governmental act and religious authority or function. Purpose, effect and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not independent or fallback prongs. A specific denominational-classification rule or established permissible accommodation controls at its actual scope. Commack Self-Service Kosher Meats v. Weiss forbids governmental theological choice while allowing objective secular administration.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine whether the criteria classify denominations or instead identify a legally relevant institutional function, whether administering them requires theological judgments and what concrete burden the exemption or its expansion transfers to employees.
* **Other Governing Law:** Establishment Clause; the four statutory religious-employer criteria; the integrated insurance-coverage requirement.
* **Material Other Authority:** Corporation of Presiding Bishop v. Amos concerns religious employment accommodation, while Larson v. Valente addresses denominational preference. Their different predicates must be examined, not merged.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An accommodation may have lawful limits, but the government cannot prefer denominations or adjudicate the truth of religious obligations. Neither proposition alone resolves these four criteria.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statutory line is a permissible institutional accommodation or impermissibly favors inward-facing religious organizations, and what severable relief would follow.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Catholic Charities argues that its religious obligation to serve outsiders should not disqualify it from an accommodation, that the State pressures institutional conduct and that the criteria favor some religious forms over others. Regulators argue that the law protects employees against unequal health benefits, applies to secular employment activity, and confines an accommodation to predominantly internal religious organizations. The California court accepted the latter account. If strict scrutiny applies, employee access, coverage alternatives and transferred costs require evidence; if the ordinary neutral-law rule applies, sincerity alone does not create an exemption. These merits positions remain neutral and conditional on jurisdiction.
