## Current Law

- **October 22 — [American Jobs Creation Act, section 703](../records/american-jobs-creation-act-section-703_source_2004-10-22.md).** Enacted noncase law adds then-section 62(a)(19) treatment for otherwise allowable attorney-fee and court-cost deductions on the covered discrimination, employment, civil-rights, retaliation, specified false-claim and Medicare secondary-payer actions defined in section 703. The deduction cannot exceed the amount included in gross income for that taxable year from the relevant judgment or settlement. Both the payment of fees or costs and the judgment or settlement must occur after October 22, 2004. The exact enacted coverage, exceptions and dual temporal trigger are preserved in the source Record. Banks and Banaitis concern earlier transactions and retain their applicable tax-year law.

- **November 9 — [Leocal v. Ashcroft](../records/leocal-v-ashcroft_merits_2004-11-09.md#public-projection).** O'Connor's unanimous opinion excludes an offense permitting accidental or negligent injury without employment of force against another from both parts of 18 U.S.C. §16. Elements and the applicable conviction record govern; injury cannot supply force use, and risk of injury cannot substitute for the specified risk of force use during the felony. A court may decide a disputed criminal predicate before applying the review restriction. The DUI aggravated-felony ground must be removed and continuing immigration consequences corrected through the reviewing court and agency; the convictions remain valid. A recklessness-specific offense is outside the presented question. Bailey and Taylor are applied; Calcano-Martinez is distinguished on its conceded predicates.

- **November 15 — [Smith v. Texas](../records/smith-v-texas_merits_2004-11-15.md#public-projection).** Souter's seven-Justice opinion applies Tennard's rejection of severe-permanent-impairment and crime-nexus relevance screens and Penry's requirement of a lawful, coherent life-verdict mechanism for independent culpability mitigation. A negative answer still certifying factual doubt the juror may not hold does not supply that mechanism. Relevance prescribes no favorable weight or disability diagnosis. The state judgment is reversed on both merits grounds. If the restriction was preserved and no remaining procedural issue precludes relief, the State must prove beyond reasonable doubt on the full penalty record that it did not contribute to death; failure requires adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence within a reasonable correction period. Guilt remains intact. Scalia and Thomas dissent; seven directly support every holding.

- **November 15 — [United States v. $100,348.00 in U.S. Currency](../records/united-states-v-100348-us-currency_merits_2004-11-15.md#public-projection).** Thomas's Parts I–II are unanimous: a lawful possessor may protect an established property interest or enforceable obligations throughout the property they cover, despite another person's title; this first-party protection does not revive the owner's defaulted claim. Partly punitive reporting forfeiture receives gross-disproportionality review, with the claimant's burden extended from Wagoner County, independent constitutional review and clear-error review of supported facts. Six Justices sustain $10,000 and reject $100,348; $90,348 must be returned to the legally entitled claimant. The whole-fund interest includes the established $100,000 bailment, without a personal-title finding for the remaining $348. The lower hypothetical fine level requires all four source/knowledge/recklessness/purpose conditions; the constitutional result holds under either $5,000 or $30,000 benchmark without inventing missing findings. The pre-CAFRA regime, separate civil forfeitability and independent fee/default rulings remain intact. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy dissent only on amount.

- **2004-12-13 — [Educational Credit Management Corp. v. Polleys](../records/educational-credit-management-v-polleys_merits_2004-12-13.md#public-projection).** Tenth Circuit affirmed, 9–0; the adjudicated student-loan debt remains discharged. A debtor establishes undue hardship under the operative 11 U.S.C. §523(a)(8) by proving, more likely than not, that repayment would prevent a minimal standard of living for the debtor and dependents, that specific circumstances make that inability likely to persist for a significant portion of the repayment period, and that the claimed hardship accords with reasonable good-faith efforts to earn income, keep expenses reasonable and meet the obligation when able. Courts may not demand certainty of hopelessness, permanent disability or a payment the debtor lacked resources to make; mere inconvenience, deliberately contrived inability and unsupported pessimism do not establish the exception. The affirmed discharge continues to bar collection of the covered obligation under the applicable discharge injunction. ECMC must treat that debt as discharged; ordinary bankruptcy enforcement addresses violation of the injunction.

- **December 13 — [Florida v. The automatic new-trial judgment is reversed for the preserved ordinary claims, including necessary preparation or consultation questions. A concession over an expressed contrary objective lies outside this record.

- **December 13 — [Kowalski v. - **2005-01-11 — [Timken Co. v. United States](../records/timken-v-united-states_merits_2005-01-11.md#public-projection).** Federal Circuit affirmed on the granted zeroing issue, 9–0; the reviewed determination remains effective. Section 3512(c) does not bar a challenge brought under the domestic antidumping statute merely because the challenger invokes international obligations as an aid to interpreting that statute. Koyo's statutory challenge is therefore properly considered on its merits. Under the operative 19 U.S.C. §§1675(a), 1677(35) and 1677b(a), Commerce may, in an authorized administrative review, treat transactions whose export price or constructed export price exceeds normal value as zero when aggregating positive dumping margins, provided it observes the prescribed comparison adjustments and gives a reasoned explanation. Zeroing is permitted rather than compelled; a challenger must establish a statutory prohibition or a defect under the governing review standard, and neither the fair-comparison argument nor the cited international report establishes the requested offset requirement here. Commerce and the trade court apply the operative determination without an offset compelled by this challenge. Ordinary administration implements the affirmed judgment.

- **2005-01-11 — [Whitfield v. United States and Hall v. United States](../records/whitfield-v-united-states_merits_2005-01-11.md#public-projection).** Both Eleventh Circuit judgments affirmed on the overt-act instruction question, 9–0 in each docket. Section 1956(h) independently defines conspiracy to commit an offense specified in §1956 or §1957 and requires neither allegation nor proof of an additional overt act. The Government must prove the actual unlawful agreement, the defendant's knowing participation and the required unlawful object beyond a reasonable doubt; association alone is insufficient, and the requested overt-act instruction was properly refused in both cases. Both reviewed judgments remain in force against this instruction challenge.

- **2005-01-12 — [Clark v. Martinez](../records/clark-v-martinez_merits_2005-01-12.md#public-projection).** Martinez affirmed; Benitez reversed; both remanded for enforceable supervised-release protection, each 8–1. Section 1231(a)(6) permits detention of every category it covers, including inadmissible persons who have never been admitted, only for the period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. When removal lacks a significant likelihood within the reasonably foreseeable future and no independent lawful custody ground applies, the person is entitled to enforceable release under lawful supervision; release does not grant admission. Six months, counted within the removal process rather than added after the ordinary ninety-day removal period, is a presumptively reasonable detention interval. After it, the detainee must give good reason to believe that removal lacks a significant likelihood within the reasonably foreseeable future, and the Government must sufficiently rebut that showing; the interval alone does not require release, and supported evidence that a longer period is reasonably necessary, including relevant evidence about an inadmissible class, may justify continued detention. Both matters return for implementation of enforceable supervised-release protection. The courts must give effect to the unrebutted removal-prospect findings and retain the lawful supervision authorized by the statute. A later custody action must have an actual new lawful basis and available review; repeated short parole periods cannot defeat the adjudicated duration limit.

- **2005-01-12 — [Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement](../records/jama-v-immigration-and-customs-enforcement_merits_2005-01-12.md#public-projection).** Eighth Circuit affirmed on the claimed advance-acceptance requirement, 5–4. Section 1231(b)(2)(E)(iv) permits selection of the country of a person's birth without an additional advance-acceptance condition when the preceding statutory steps and that clause's own predicates are satisfied. Clause (vii)'s express acceptance requirement governs its residual destination, not clause (iv); the agency remains bound by independently applicable removal protections and custody limits. The Eighth Circuit judgment remains effective, and the acceptance-based bar is removed. Implementation must satisfy the actual destination sequence and any operative protection or stay. Independently presented protection and detention claims proceed through their applicable channels and on their own records.

- **2005-01-12 — [Nordyke v. King](../records/nordyke-v-king_merits_2005-01-12.md#public-projection).** Affirmed as to the facial First Amendment denial; vacated as to the Second Amendment denial and remanded; both components unanimous. Individuals may invoke the Second Amendment, as incorporated through the Fourteenth Amendment, against a county restriction burdening protected possession or acquisition necessary to protected keeping. Public ownership does not extinguish the right; the lower court must assess the claimants' actual injuries, covered conduct, practical burdens, required justification and remaining preliminary-relief requirements under the governing law. The claimant must first identify protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation through text, history and precedent; the county must then justify the regulation at the level required by its actual burden. A severe core burden requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective materially less restrictive alternative; a substantial burden requires an important objective, a substantial relationship and no unnecessary or effective elimination of protected activity; an incidental, nonobstructive regulation receives reasonableness review while remaining subject to prohibitions against arbitrariness, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right. Ordinary firearm possession and exchange do not become protected speech in every application because a show also includes advocacy, and isolated expressive displays do not establish a basis to invalidate the entire possession ordinance. A demonstrably communicative display receives the protection appropriate to its actual context; the claimant must establish that expressive application and the requested breadth of relief, and the county must satisfy the scrutiny applicable to a proved burden, with viewpoint discrimination separately prohibited. The case returns for prompt consideration of the actual arms burden, the county's required justification and all remaining preliminary-relief requirements, including the hardship balance the lower court did not decide. The facial speech denial stands. Any properly preserved concrete expressive-display claim requires its own factual inquiry and the applicable expression standard.

- **2005-01-12 — [United States v. Booker](../records/united-states-v-booker_merits_2005-01-12.md#public-projection).** Constitutional rule reaffirmed 6–3; binding Guidelines retained 5–4. Booker sentence vacatur affirmed and remanded, with all nine accepting vacatur once the constitutional premise governs and five supporting the mandatory-framework directions; Fanfan sentence affirmed on the presented challenge, 5–4. A fact legally necessary to increase a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must receive applicable grand-jury charging protection and be proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt, validly admitted as an element, or determined beyond a reasonable doubt after a lawful jury waiver. This rule governs present-offense and necessary recidivist facts, including those that increase a binding Federal Sentencing Guidelines range; judicial selection within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions remains permissible. The Federal Sentencing Guidelines remain binding to the extent their ranges rest on constitutionally established facts; an unsupported enhancement must be withheld rather than used to replace Congress's mandatory system with an advisory one. Section 3553(b)(1), §3742(e) and the enacted review and remand provisions remain effective in their lawful applications, with judicial discretion exercised within the independently authorized range and lawful statutory departures. Booker's appellate sentence vacatur is affirmed, and his case is remanded for a replacement sentence within a lawfully established binding range, subject to correction of independently established calculation errors. Fanfan's refusal to enhance and resulting 78-month sentence are affirmed against the asserted entitlement to use the excluded findings; any independently preserved calculation issue remains for ordinary disposition. Booker returns for lawful resentencing under the retained binding framework. Fanfan's 78-month sentence stands against the enhancement challenge; any independently preserved calculation question proceeds ordinarily. The Government must identify an independent lawful procedural source before seeking additional enhancement factfinding. Ordinary appellate review continues under the surviving enacted provisions.

- **2005-01-24 — [Bell v. Cone, No. 04-394](../records/bell-v-cone_merits_2005-01-24.md#public-projection).** Vacate the Sixth Circuit's aggravator-based sentence-relief judgment and remand, 7–2; actual judicial narrowing conclusively settled by eight Associates; six support the complete confined mandate. The Sixth Circuit's aggravator-based sentence-relief judgment is vacated, and the case is remanded to dispose of Cone's existing, distinct jury-guidance and weighing claim under its actual state adjudication, the applicable federal-law cutoff, §2254 and any preserved valid default.
