## Current Law

The opening trackers remain the baseline; the following completed Records add controlling law at their effective dates. Their Public Projections supply the complete operative rules, authority, explanations and remedies. These summaries preserve the material distinctions needed for continuation; the underlying Records control any conflict.

- **November 9 — [Leocal v. Ashcroft](../records/leocal-v-ashcroft_merits_2004-11-09.md#public-projection).** O'Connor's unanimous opinion excludes an offense permitting accidental or negligent injury without employment of force against another from both parts of 18 U.S.C. §16. Elements and the applicable conviction record govern; injury cannot supply force use, and risk of injury cannot substitute for the specified risk of force use during the felony. A court may decide a disputed criminal predicate before applying the review restriction. The DUI aggravated-felony ground must be removed and continuing immigration consequences corrected through the reviewing court and agency; the convictions remain valid. A recklessness-specific offense is outside the presented question. Bailey and Taylor are applied; Calcano-Martinez is distinguished on its conceded predicates.
- **November 15 — [Smith v. Texas](../records/smith-v-texas_merits_2004-11-15.md#public-projection).** Souter's seven-Justice opinion applies Tennard's rejection of severe-permanent-impairment and crime-nexus relevance screens and Penry's requirement of a lawful, coherent life-verdict mechanism for independent culpability mitigation. A negative answer still certifying factual doubt the juror may not hold does not supply that mechanism. Relevance prescribes no favorable weight or disability diagnosis. The state judgment is reversed on both merits grounds. If the restriction was preserved and no remaining procedural issue precludes relief, the State must prove beyond reasonable doubt on the full penalty record that it did not contribute to death; failure requires adequate capital resentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence within a reasonable correction period. Guilt remains intact. Scalia and Thomas dissent; seven directly support every holding.
- **November 15 — [United States v. $100,348.00 in U.S. Currency](../records/united-states-v-100348-us-currency_merits_2004-11-15.md#public-projection).** Thomas's Parts I–II are unanimous: a lawful possessor may protect an established property interest or enforceable obligations throughout the property they cover, despite another person's title; this first-party protection does not revive the owner's defaulted claim. Partly punitive reporting forfeiture receives gross-disproportionality review, with the claimant's burden extended from Wagoner County, independent constitutional review and clear-error review of supported facts. Six Justices sustain $10,000 and reject $100,348; $90,348 must be returned to the legally entitled claimant. The whole-fund interest includes the established $100,000 bailment, without a personal-title finding for the remaining $348. The lower hypothetical fine level requires all four source/knowledge/recklessness/purpose conditions; the constitutional result holds under either $5,000 or $30,000 benchmark without inventing missing findings. The pre-CAFRA regime, separate civil forfeitability and independent fee/default rulings remain intact. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy dissent only on amount.
- **November 30 — [Anderson v. Spear](../records/anderson-v-spear_merits_2004-11-30.md#public-projection).** Kennedy's Part I has seven direct joins applying Burson's strict scrutiny to ordinary public streets and sidewalks; the unsupported 500-foot electioneering reach, including nonintimidating write-in instructions, fails. The exit-polling exclusion and confined indoor ownership exception do not restore the public-forum speech. All nine invalidate the $50,000 personal-loan ceiling and complete post-election contribution ban: initial personal funding and later donor repayment are distinct, and actual contributions remain subject to justified, closely fitted regulation. All nine support vacatur of the universal express-advocacy-only construction; eight join that ground, while Stone-Zsela joins that component's judgment alone. The decree must correct the policies' unlawful operation unless the State shows narrower relief fully cures the violation, preserve independently lawful rules and determine lawful severability. Scalia and Thomas have separate speech-method grounds.
- **November 30 — [Koons Buick Pontiac GMC, Inc. v. Nigh](../records/koons-buick-v-nigh_merits_2004-11-30.md#public-projection).** Ginsburg's eight-Justice opinion holds that the operative §1640(a)(2)(A) retains the $100-to-$1,000 individual-action range for clause (i), with the specific $200-to-$2,000 range for credit not under an open-end plan and secured by real property or a dwelling. The successive enacted structure gives effect to both ranges. The $24,192.80 statutory award is reversed for the $1,000 ceiling; independent actual damages, liability and fee entitlement retain their own bases. Thomas joins this actual text-and-structure explanation in full; Scalia dissents on amended grammar. Lamie and Hartford Underwriters are applied.
- **December 6 — [San Diego v. Roe](../records/san-diego-v-roe_merits_2004-12-06.md#public-projection).** Stevens's eight-Justice opinion restores dismissal of the reviewed employment-speech theory. The particular police-associated commercial performance fails Connick's content/form/context public-concern inquiry; public availability, compensation and lawful sexual content do not themselves establish that threshold. NTEU's protection of unrelated citizen expression and broad-prospective-ban rule remain intact; Pickering balancing is not triggered on this expression. The Court makes no operational-disruption finding as a premise. Stone-Zsela dissents on the protected-expression threshold and would require the ordinary individual employment inquiry.
- **December 6 — [Theofel v. Farey-Jones](../records/theofel-v-farey-jones_merits_2004-12-06.md#public-projection).** Stone-Zsela's unanimous opinion recognizes genuine provider backup storage under §2510(17)(B) after delivery or recipient access, requiring proof of the actual backup function by a preponderance. A sole archive without that function is insufficient. Under §2701(c)(1), physical disclosure is not conclusive authorization when consciously procured by materially deceptive invocation of patently invalid compulsory process going to the essential nature of access; negligence, routine irregularity and collateral deception do not meet that rule. Actual unauthorized access, defendant-specific mental state, remaining §2707 predicates and supported good-faith or authorization defenses remain for adjudication. Both categorical dismissal grounds are rejected; distinct statutory and state-law branches remain at their existing scope. Konop's interception and actual-user rules retain their separate operation.
- **December 7 — [Kansas v. Colorado](../records/kansas-v-colorado_exceptions_2004-12-07.md#public-projection).** Breyer's unanimous opinion resolves six exceptions and retains the original action. The Master must use the binding 1969 method for additional interest on qualifying losses incurred from 1969 onward, never before loss, preserving separately recoverable earlier principal and inflation and excluding duplicated inflation or speculative returns. The rolling ten-year H-I window is accepted as evidence under the supported error findings; it cannot authorize forbidden accumulated credits or extinguish independently proved usable-water depletion. Initial Colorado credit decisions require independent federal review of every issue essential to state-line compliance. The annual-period objection to 1997–1999 compliance is rejected on Kansas's own figures; distinct material technical objections remain returnable. The current River Master request is denied, and justified technical deferrals remain subject to a concrete, timely return through the existing reference. The Master must prepare a corrected calculation and conforming decree. Kansas owns any recovery.
- **December 8 — [KP Permanent Make-Up v. Lasting Impression](../records/kp-permanent-make-up-v-lasting-impression_merits_2004-12-08.md#public-projection).** Scalia's unanimous opinion keeps likely confusion with the infringement claimant, including an incontestable registration, and places the ordinary civil burden of §1115(b)(4)'s conditions on the defense claimant: use otherwise than as a mark, fairly and in good faith, only to describe its goods or services or geographic origin. Some likely confusion can coexist with fair use; relevant confusion evidence may illuminate the actual defense conditions without shifting infringement proof. The no-confusion burden is vacated; ordinary summary-judgment proceedings determine actual presentation and protect a complete fair use established without genuine dispute. Park 'N Fly, Qualitex and TrafFix retain their actual boundaries.
- **December 13 — [Brosseau v. Haugen](../records/brosseau-v-haugen_merits_2004-12-13.md#public-projection).** Kennedy's seven-Justice opinion grants personal qualified immunity and ends the reviewed individual federal damages claim. The plaintiff-favorable account includes a stationary Jeep, clear escape route and no identified immediate person-or-weapon threat, together with determined resistance, imminent departure and nearby occupied cars. Pre-February 21, 1999 law did not make this anticipatory-force boundary sufficiently clear; obvious applications may give notice without a factual twin, and there is no categorical vehicle/flight exception. Harlow and Anderson remain operative. Saucier supplies no mandatory merits-first rule; later Hope does not supply earlier notice. Force merits lie outside the limited grant, and separate municipal/state branches retain their status. Stevens and Stone-Zsela dissent separately.
- **December 13 — [Devenpeck v. Alford](../records/devenpeck-v-alford_merits_2004-12-13.md#public-projection).** O'Connor's substantive rule and remand grounds have all nine joins: actual contemporaneously known facts may establish probable cause for a different, unrelated offense; the announced label does not confine objective authority. The alternative must satisfy the actual offense elements and justify the restraint imposed. Whren and Pringle apply; Atwater and Ricci preserve independent requirements for added fine-only custody. Eight Associates reverse; Stone-Zsela would vacate the challenged ruling and joins the complete substantive rule and remand, withholding only formal reversal. Actual impersonation and obstruction applications, any properly presented custody issue and personal immunity remain for adjudication. Conduct-date immunity uses November 22, 1997; same-day Brosseau supplies no entering law.
- **December 13 — [Florida v. Nixon](../records/florida-v-nixon_merits_2004-12-13.md#public-projection).** Breyer's unanimous opinion rejects automatic deficiency and presumed prejudice from the absence of express consent where counsel adequately discusses a capital concession, the defendant neither agrees nor objects, and meaningful adversarial representation and an actual jury trial continue. Strickland requires actual reasonable-performance and reasonable-probability-prejudice adjudication under trial-era standards; Bell distinguishes specific choices from complete abandonment, and Williams supplies ordinary lawful-outcome prejudice. The automatic new-trial judgment is reversed for the preserved ordinary claims, including necessary preparation or consultation questions. A concession over an expressed contrary objective lies outside this record.
- **December 13 — [Kowalski v. Tesmer](../records/kowalski-v-tesmer_merits_2004-12-13.md#public-projection).** Stone-Zsela's six-Justice opinion rejects the attorneys' third-party claim for want of a concrete existing or specifically prevented client relationship. Their pleaded reduction in regular paid rotation appointments supports a personal economic theory, which the majority assumes without deciding Article III; it does not itself supply entitlement to assert unidentified clients' rights. Practical indigent-litigation barriers do not alone cure the relationship defect. The counsel merits remain open. The attorney claim must be dismissed and dependent relief removed, after identifying any independently surviving basis before altering other relief; the individuals' separate abstention disposition remains intact. Ginsburg dissents with Stevens and Souter on the contextual prospective relationship and particular access obstacle.

Roe must establish protection and causation; the City may prove actual grounds and a lawful same-decision defense. The broad prospective-ban burden does not automatically govern the individual discharge.
- **Brosseau, December 13:** Stevens applies the existing defense and regards Garner as sufficiently clear on the assumed nonthreatening flight, leaving proper factual adjudication rather than ending the claim. Stone-Zsela separately proposes replacing broad legal-notice immunity with the official's preponderance proof of identified binding precedent or enactment directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance and reasonable applicability. Legal silence, departmental practice and lack of a factual twin would not suffice; a supervisor's instruction would qualify only by accurately conveying such authority actually relied on. That proposal receives no Associate join and is not current law. It retains the complete force merits and the existing cause of action, with absolute, sovereign and independent institutional immunities outside its scope.
- **Kowalski, December 13:** Ginsburg's dissent, joined by Stevens and Souter, treats the denied professional relationship, regular appointment practice, pleaded economic loss and the obstacle of asserting counsel rights without counsel as sufficiently direct for the third-party route. It would return only genuinely unresolved preserved matters, without inventing missing injury allegations or deciding the appointment merits.

### Standing practice

**Referral of applications, effective October 7, 1991; signed explanations and certification, effective October 2, 1995.** By the Court's internal practice adopted at the opening of October Term 1991, an application for a stay, injunction, or other interim relief in a matter on the Term's inventory is referred by the Circuit Justice to the full Court and decided by the participating Justices under the participating-majority rule. The order issues in the Court's name and recites the presenting Circuit Justice ("presented to Justice ___ and by him or her referred to the Court"). From October 2, 1995, referral does not prevent the Circuit Justice, or the substitute the term-effective Rules designate, from taking a necessary temporary action in his or her own name while the Court considers the application. Each action is explained in short signed writings, including the Circuit Justice's own explanation at final disposition, whether prevailing or dissenting, and a statement by each participating Justice in each Court action deciding relief, and is certified for issuance by the Chief Justice or the Chief's substitute, as Engine §10 provides. A Justice may participate in a referred application from the date of the judicial oath. An application not on the Term's inventory remains subject to ordinary Circuit Justice practice. Source: the OT1991 opening practice carried in the opening Standing State and the OT1993 Term Continuity Note. Source of the October 2, 1995 revision: user instruction.
