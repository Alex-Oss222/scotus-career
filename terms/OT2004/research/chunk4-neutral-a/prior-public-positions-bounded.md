### Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc., Nos. 95-754 and 95-886

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Breyer delivered the Opinion of the Court, with a majority for each part as shown below.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and II | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Affirms design restoration and reverses the categorical parallel-duty barrier | Statutory comparison, design clearance, genuinely parallel duties and remedies; unanimous |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Reverses manufacturing/warning barriers | Complete manufacturing/warning application; six Justices |
| Opinion of the Court, Part IV | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas | Inclusion rule does not itself preempt a surviving claim | Common-law duties can be requirements; five Justices |

| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | O'Connor | Scalia, Thomas | Supports design and equal-duty results; would preserve preemption of genuinely divergent manufacturing/warning duties | Joins Parts I, II and IV |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | | Joins the judgment | Joins Parts I through IV, but not Part V |

Every controlling part has majority support. The concurrences of Stevens and O'Connor have the joins shown; Stone-Zsela writes alone.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They join the design, parallel-duty and manufacturing/warning holdings and the formal FDA interpretation. They would leave open whether some other common-law duty may be expressly preempted by section 360k. Rejecting Medtronic's sweeping displacement argument does not require categorical immunity for tort law, but neither does it require affirmative inclusion beyond the duties actually resolved. Cipollone construed different statutory language and does not compel that additional step here. Their reserved position is not the Court's rule because five Justices directly adopt Part IV. Their agreement with the present application does not depend on a prediction that future common-law preemption must be rare.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I, II and IV. Substantial-equivalence clearance supplies no substantive design approval; genuinely equal state duties are not different merely because damages are available. But actual federal manufacturing and warning commands apply to this device even though they cover other devices. In their view, the statute requires applicability, difference or addition and subject connection, not an extra specificity prerequisite. The FDA cannot impose that extra condition against text they find clear. Section 808.1's recognition of court decisions and divergent substantive applications also defeats a categorical exemption based on general state-law form. They would affirm preemption of genuinely divergent manufacturing/warning duties, reverse the parallel-duty bar and remand to identify the actual obligations. They make no finding of compliance, breach or Florida entitlement and do not demand dismissal of an entire count that may contain a viable equal-duty theory.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins Parts I through IV and the judgment. Congress's express comparison controls: a damages duty can be a requirement, but clearance and the agency's presence do not supply a missing substantive federal comparator. Equal conduct does not become different merely because damages are available. His conclusion rests on that statutory ground, so he does not join Part V's independent reliance on agency interpretation. He neither rejects that ground as unlawful nor adopts a general tort exemption. The unresolved content of a particular pleaded duty can be identified through ordinary proceedings without requiring a new preliminary hearing in every products case; breach, causation and relief remain unproved.

### Geier v. American Honda Motor Co., No. 98-1811

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer throughout; Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in Parts I–II | Affirms on the actual-conflict ground in Part III | Part I: statutory construction and background; Part II: independent actual-conflict inquiry; Part III: the particular no-airbag conflict and preserved boundaries |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Would reverse the preemption dismissal and remand for ordinary tort adjudication | Disagrees with Part III's inference of protected exclusivity, while joining Parts I–II |

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The dissenters join Parts I–II, including the rejection of express displacement and the availability of independently established actual conflict. They would reverse and remand for ordinary adjudication of the actual tort claim because the statutory and regulatory scheme does not sufficiently establish protection of these compliance choices against the common-law liability Congress expressly saved.

Cipollone requires the actual statutory language and predicate duty; Myrick requires an affirmative federal premise; Medtronic and the particular Shanklin arrangement require the substantive counterpart claimed to displace the state obligation. The options and phase-in genuinely advance innovation and public acceptance, but permission to satisfy national requirements in several ways does not itself establish immunity. The absence of an airbag-omission command or fixed mix is not an exclusive physical-impossibility test; it bears on whether the asserted protected choice has been shown here. The agency's safety purpose, encouragement of airbags and own reliance on potential liability for deficient systems reinforce the statutory reservation.

The dissent confronts the Court's concern that widespread airbag duties would influence manufacturers and hasten the transition. That practical pressure does not, in its view, demonstrate legally protected exclusivity under this saving provision. It does not deny that damages can impose duties or that an options rule could produce a demonstrated conflict in another application. It would remove this defense without giving it a new evidentiary trial, awarding damages, finding a defect or adding a different vehicle-specific theory. Its preserved-liability conclusion is not the Court's holding.

### Saucier v. Katz, No. 99-1977

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signed opinion announcing the judgment | Souter | No other Justice joins the reasoning | Vacate and remand for the existing distinct immunity inquiry | Souter's explanation of the existing defense, proper assumed facts and open application remand; his sequencing position is not a majority holding |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Joins the exact vacatur and remand instruction only | Separately proposes replacing insufficiently-clearly-established-law immunity with authoritative-reliance protection |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Ginsburg | Stevens and Breyer | All three join the exact vacatur and remand instruction | Preserve their force-specific objection to a second overlapping reasonableness screen; do not join Souter's contrary rationale or Stone-Zsela's general replacement |
| Dissent | Kennedy | O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas | Would reverse and direct personal damages immunity | Retain the distinct legal-error defense and find the particular force boundary insufficiently clear in September 1994 on the supported adverse facts |

The five votes for the mandate are not five joins to Souter's reasons. No opinion supplies a majority rationale for a new general immunity or sequencing rule.

#### Separate Writings

**Souter, announcing the judgment.** Souter considers the merits and immunity inquiries legally distinct: an officer may perceive the facts correctly yet reasonably misunderstand the particular constitutional limit. Harlow and Anderson preserve that legal-error protection; Graham's allowance for reasonable factual perceptions does not itself decide it. General prohibition of excessive force is insufficient by itself, but a materially identical precedent is not indispensable. Hunter and Elder require the proper adverse factual assumptions and the full relevant conduct-date law. Souter would have ordinary lower-court immunity adjudication first determine whether those assumed facts establish a constitutional violation and, if so, whether the particular right was sufficiently established, so that the constitutional rule can develop. He recognizes the limits of the granted review here and assumes a possible violation; he does not require decision of a constitutional issue outside review or an advisory ruling. That sequencing view is his own, not a rule adopted by the judgment majority. Unlike the dissenters, he leaves the first distinct application to the lower court and directs neither immunity nor its denial. Unlike the other concurring Justices, he retains the distinct legal-error rationale in this force setting.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice accepts the exact existing-law remand while proposing a different personal defense. He would displace Harlow and Anderson only insofar as they protect an official merely because the violated right was insufficiently clearly established, preserving their compatible particularized analysis and lawful summary-judgment requirements. First, the complete constitutional rule would account for urgency, reasonably perceived danger, reasonable factual mistakes and operational discretion. If a violation were established, the official would bear the burden of identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance on it and a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately conveying that authority could implement reliance. Legal silence, lack of a materially identical case, department custom, an unsupported supervisor's assurance and general job authority would not suffice.

In his view, legitimate protection against unexpected legal change concerns affirmative authoritative permission. The existing defense's predictability and litigation-control benefits do not justify withholding an otherwise available remedy from the first claimant establishing a particular application of settled protection. Graham already accounts for the officer's practical circumstances; those same factual allowances should not excuse wrongful force again. On Katz's supported account, an unnecessary shove of an already controlled, unresisting demonstrator violates the force standard without requiring physical injury, while necessary proportionate security removal remains lawful. Actual necessity, resistance, perceptions and participation remain disputed. The record identifies no binding precedent or enactment directly authorizing the alleged unnecessary shove; general security authority is insufficient.

His proposed defense would govern this pending case, with a fair opportunity for bounded submissions on genuinely new reliance elements. An expectation of immunity from unclear law would not preserve the displaced defense; final judgments and ordinary preservation and preclusion rules would remain. The proposal concerns this personal legal-uncertainty defense and materially equivalent claims, not function-specific absolute immunity, sovereign immunity, proper defendants, causation, injury or the availability of a cause of action. Failure of the proposed defense would establish neither a constitutional violation, damages, institutional liability nor a new cause of action; each independent requirement remains. These are separate views: the Court's mandate imposes no reliance burden, transition rule or additional submissions requirement.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens and Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** These Justices join the exact vacatur and open application remand while maintaining that Graham already tests the practical choices and reasonable mistakes available to an officer using force. They reject a second overlapping reasonableness shelter for force found objectively unreasonable under that complete standard. That objection is confined to this excessive-force setting; they retain ordinary Harlow protection elsewhere and do not endorse the Chief Justice's general actual-reliance replacement.

The supported unnecessary shared shove of a controlled, unresisting person cannot be dismissed by treating every degree of force as justified by the initial need for security removal. Johnson and Sterling prevent reappraisal of the participation evidence on this appeal. Plakas permits early merits judgment on a properly supported no-violation account, but its immediate armed attack without a contrary material account is absent here. The continuous movement into the van is relevant context, not a factual answer to the alleged unnecessary force. Their separate analysis does not alter their vote for the mandate: neither immunity nor denial, automatic trial, liability or damages is directed.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** These Justices would reverse and direct personal damages immunity while assuming a possible constitutional violation. Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal error independently of Graham's merits inquiry. Accepting the plaintiff-favorable two-officer account, they regard the carrying and van entry as a continuous urgent removal from a protected event; the particular boundary of this brief nondeadly force was not sufficiently apparent in September 1994. They recognize the strongest contrary point: a controlled, unresisting person's forceful van entry may lack additional demonstrated need. They nevertheless treat this as uncertainty over the degree of force during removal, not a legally unmistakable separate assault after the seizure ended.

Their position does not depend on deciding that Parker acted alone, finding resistance, declaring the force lawful or making injury a prerequisite. Hunter and Sterling preserve the adverse assumed facts; their disagreement concerns the legal conclusion drawn from them. They support an ordinary merits-first approach for the reasons of legal development Souter describes, subject to the actual scope of review. Their agreement with him on that position is not a join in the judgment-announcing opinion and supplies no controlling sequence rule. They reject an open application remand because they would decide the legal defense now.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice joins the retained-association standard and the independent Eighth Amendment application. He accepts reversal of the insufficient facial showing on the association components while emphasizing the need to examine real family contact. The two-strike minimum creates a serious fit concern because the substance violation need not involve visitation, and eligibility to request restoration is no guaranteed end to separation. Giving institutional predictions greater weight for this facial disposition still leaves concrete indefinite suspension, arbitrary restoration, and an absence of realistic alternatives subject to actual review. For an independent conditions claim, he would require prisoners to prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, an objectively serious deprivation or substantial risk of serious harm, together with the responsible officials’ actual awareness and unreasonable disregard. Any resulting decree must satisfy the PLRA.

**Scalia, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Thomas.** They accept the full Turner applications as sufficient to reject these facial claims and the bounded Farmer application as in Delaney. Their Gerber distinction between a compatible liberty and a claimed entitlement to institutional assistance leads them to withhold the broader retained-visitation recognition in Part I. The accepted applications do not require that recognition. They preserve review of rights actually retained and do not replace binding conditions law with categorical immunity for a disciplinary label.

### Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, No. 01-344

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, and THOMAS, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion. BREYER, J., filed an opinion dissenting in part, in which STEVENS and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | The five Justices joining the Court's opinion, named above | Affirms | Complete reasonable-fit holding, statutory qualifications and remedy; Thomas accepts the sufficient application without endorsing Central Hudson as the complete constitutional account |
| Concurrence | Thomas | — | Affirms and joins the Court's opinion in full | Stronger objection to suppression of truthful information; no categorical invalidation of direct drug controls |
| Dissent in part | Breyer | Stevens, Ginsburg | Would reverse the consumer-directed constitutional invalidation and permit appropriate further consideration of the physician-directed issue | Consumer promotion only; no adjudication sustaining physician-directed restrictions and no independent severability determination |

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the full fit holding because these restrictions fail even the lesser Central Hudson test. He separately regards suppression of accurate product information to prevent its effect on willing listeners as the mechanism his 44 Liquormart position rejects. Drug safety is legitimate, but that objective does not itself justify withholding accurate information; direct drug controls remain distinct. His concurrence is not judgment-only agreement and does not supply the Court with a broader categorical rule.

**Breyer, dissenting in part, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** The dissent would reverse the constitutional invalidation insofar as it rests on promotion to consumers. The statutory balance preserves medically needed preparations while preventing cumulative risks from a growing market of products that have not undergone approval. Product promotion can generate demand apart from a clinician's prior individualized assessment. Production caps, wholesale restrictions and controls on equipment or advance preparation may either exclude needed treatment or fail to address that demand. General service advertising and continuing physician involvement preserve meaningful channels. Under ordinary reasonable-fit review, the dissent therefore finds the consumer restriction proportionate despite its burden on truthful information. It reserves the distinct question of physician-directed promotion because that issue requires separate consideration; it neither sustains every communication restriction nor announces a benefit-condition exception to the First Amendment.

### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, No. 93-1841

#### Opinion Topology

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Concurrence in part and concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | No joiners | Joins the Court's standing part and concurs in vacatur and remand | Strict scrutiny and bounded remand; own benign-program and paternalism objection |

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor concurs in part and concurs in the judgment, joined by Justice Kennedy throughout and by Justices Scalia and Thomas in the uniform-scrutiny and bounded-remand portions. She joins the Court's standing holding but rejects the common standard in favor of a compelling interest and narrow tailoring for every racial classification, federal, state or local. She supports overruling Metro's lower federal tier and concurs in vacatur and remand for actual application of strict scrutiny. Congress's institutional competence may inform evidence and fit, but does not replace individual judicial protection. Strict scrutiny need not invalidate every racial remedy. O'Connor and Kennedy support her particular stare-decisis explanation; Scalia and Thomas do not join it, and it is not the Court's ground.

Justice Scalia concurs in part and concurs in the judgment. He joins the Court's standing holding and supports uniform strict scrutiny and bounded remand, but rejects compensation or entitlement based merely on shared racial ancestry and does not join an affirmative endorsement of group catchup remedies. Justice Thomas concurs in part and concurs in the judgment, joins the Court's standing holding, and supports strict scrutiny and bounded remand while rejecting a benign or paternalistic exception and racial entitlement by group ancestry. His broader equivalence and paternalism reasoning is his own; neither separate position makes the unknown certification route invalid or issues an injunction.

Justice Souter concurs, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer, and joins the Court's opinion in full. Constitutional stare decisis requires reasons for departing from Metro, attention to Fullilove's actual supported result, and preservation of Congress's differentiated remedial role. The Court supplies those reasons: a common obligation still considers supported correction, legislative authority, relevant evidence and real burdens, without assigning a lower tier merely because a program is federal and described as benign. Fullilove's recognition of supported congressional action remains. The remand identifies concrete certification and economic-disadvantage issues, preserves concessions and findings in their actual scope, and does not invent a lack of evidence or an unconditional waiver. Those grounds support the common rule and remand while preserving Souter's concern for precedent and the actual challenge.

Justice Ginsburg concurs, joined by Justice Breyer, and joins the Court's opinion in full. Continuing exclusion and barriers to meaningful access may warrant supported repair; they cannot be equated without examination to racial subordination. Congress's remedial competence and relevant national evidence remain important under the common standard, while evidence, operation and outsiders' actual burdens receive meaningful judicial review. Genuine-disadvantage safeguards, preserved concessions and the bounded inquiry on remand prevent either remedial purpose or a racial category from becoming an automatic entitlement. These concurrences add no unrestricted quota entitlement or universal benign-program exemption.

### FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp., No. 98-1152

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Those five Justices adopt both holdings and all their qualifications. Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The dissent would reverse the categorical no-authority judgment and remand, distinguishing statutory coverage from lawful exercise without exempting the agency from otherwise applicable commands.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would reverse the categorical authority judgment and remand. The structure-and-function definition reaches nicotine deliberately supplied for pharmacological effects, and the delivery-component definition can reach the apparatus used to supply it. Therapeutic advertising is not the only evidence of intended use. The earlier regulatory architecture at least permits that construction within the delegation recognized by Chevron, Sweet Home and Haggar.

They accept that restricted-device authority cannot erase misbranding, applicable approval or cessation duties. Their sufficient disagreement is that an obligation to remove a covered product, if established under the statute's own conditions, limits the FDA's permissible methods rather than proving that the product is outside the protective statute. They do not make section 360h(e)(1) elective merely because other remedies exist, and do not adopt a universal exception for withdrawal or substitute risks. The precise provision must permit consideration of those consequences.

The tobacco-specific statutes address their actual labeling, advertising, reporting and access subjects; they do not establish the categorical FDCA exclusion necessary to sustain the judgment. MCI's insistence on mandatory limits does not erase different, broad coverage language. Section 422 reserves the authority question and requires the earlier regime, supplying neither new power nor a new exemption. No new mission, classification shortcut or later review flexibility is used.

Their remand would address the actual combination-product method, particular restrictions and unresolved constitutional objections under ordinary procedures. It would not unconditionally reinstate the full rule, guarantee a lawful continuing tobacco market, order a ban or recall, or supply new agency findings. The statutory constraints remain enforceable at their own scope.

### Grutter v. Bollinger, No. 02-241

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. KENNEDY, J., filed a dissenting opinion. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined as to Part I.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining the policy | Parts I–IV in full |
| Dissent | Kennedy | Kennedy alone | Reverse and remand for policy and surviving individual relief | Actual-operation objection under the governing standard; statutory invalidity at that scope |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia as to Part I | Reverse and remand | Part I: broader nonremedial-preference objection and actual-mechanism objection; Part II: Thomas's distinct selective-prestige objection |

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting.** Kennedy accepts the existing availability of a supported educational-diversity objective but finds inadequate proof that the actual racial weight and numerical management preserve genuinely individual competition throughout selection. Hopwood's individual-comparison requirement and Patrolmen's evidence-supported fit rule demand more than academic assurance or flexible language. Statistics alone need not establish a quota, yet the whole operational record in his assessment fails the common justification standard. He would reverse on that narrower actual-operation ground and sustain the intentional-treatment statutory challenges at the same scope, without deciding a categorical prohibition of all supported educational consideration. On remand, policy relief and individual claims would remain distinct; Hopwood and Lesage would place the preponderance same-decision burden on the school, with claimants establishing actual loss under an available cause against a proper defendant.

**Thomas, dissenting, with Scalia joining Part I.** Part I maintains the broader personal-equality objection to nonremedial ancestry preferences, together with the conclusion that this mechanism fails genuine competitive treatment. Their earlier Adarand and Hopwood separate positions supply that objection to the governing permission; they do not treat the separate position as existing Court law. They also read §1981's same-right guarantee to prohibit the purposeful preference and would sustain the Title VI challenge. Their proposed reversal would require fitted policy relief and separate adjudication of individual causation and remedies, rather than an automatic admission or monetary award.

In Part II, Thomas separately argues that preserving selective prestige does not create a constitutionally sufficient need for unequal treatment. An institution's chosen level of selectivity cannot itself justify an ancestry preference. His supported violation-linked remedial-duty position in Hopwood remains distinct from this nonremedial policy. Both dissenters oppose the present approval; their writing supplies no assent to the Court's continuing-permission rule.
