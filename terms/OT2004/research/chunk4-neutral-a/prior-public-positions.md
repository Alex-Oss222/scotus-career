### Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr; Lohr v. Medtronic, Inc., Nos. 95-754 and 95-886

Decision, June 26, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 23, 1996; decided June 26, 1996. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, 56 F.3d 1335, reviewing dismissal of a Florida action removed on diversity grounds.

The question is whether the Medical Device Amendments displace the Lohrs' negligent-design, negligent-manufacture, failure-to-warn and strict-liability claims concerning a pacemaker lead cleared through substantial equivalence. The Court separately considers what counts as a state requirement, whether a relevant federal requirement applies, whether the state obligation differs from it or adds to it, and whether a genuinely equal duty becomes different because state law supplies damages.

The Model 4011 lead received substantial-equivalence clearance in 1982, without premarket approval. The FDA's November 30 letter permitted marketing subject to general controls and did not endorse safety. Lora Lohr received the pacemaker in 1987; failure in December 1990 required emergency surgery. Her physician identified a likely lead defect. The alleged design, manufacture and warning failures, and their causal consequences, have not been adjudicated.

The Eleventh Circuit restored negligent design and strict liability based on dangerous design, while sustaining preemption of negligent manufacture, failure to warn and strict-liability contentions based on manufacture or labeling. Medtronic challenges the restoration in No. 95-754; the Lohrs challenge the adverse preemption rulings in No. 95-886. Michael Lohr's consortium claim follows the underlying claims. The separate warranty dismissal was not appealed.

#### Holdings

##### I. Substantial-equivalence clearance does not supply the asserted design approval

**Operative rule:** Section 360k(a) requires an applicable federal requirement and a state requirement respecting a human-use device that differs from or adds to it and concerns safety, effectiveness or another matter included in the federal requirement, subject to subsection (b). This lead's substantial-equivalence clearance does not establish substantive federal approval of its design and therefore does not preempt the restored negligent-design or design-based strict-liability claims on that asserted ground.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by all nine Justices named in the topology.

**Controlling explanation:** Permission to market is not the substantive safety determination Medtronic's design argument requires. Section 510(k), codified at section 360(k), requires advance notification and information about classification and compliance with applicable standards or approval requirements. It does not itself declare the notified design safe. Section 807.97 expressly separates a substantial-equivalence determination from official approval. The 1982 letter likewise did not endorse safety.

The separate premarket-approval system examines safety and effectiveness under its own statutory predicates. Class III status does not establish that this device underwent that process. Nor does grandfathering supply a federal judgment selecting its design. Part 820's requirement that manufacture conform to an original design or authorized changes ensures manufacturing conformance; it does not itself make that design the FDA's substantive choice.

Easterwood requires the actual federal predicate, not regulatory overlap. Freightliner likewise rejects inventing a protected federal choice where the asserted operative standard is absent. Their distinct statutory language remains intact. Here, the presence of real federal controls does not fill the missing design comparator.

The Court therefore affirms the design restoration. It decides no defect, breach or causal issue, no claim involving an actual premarket approval, and no hypothetical device-specific standard. No independently established actual conflict arises merely from this clearance. The surviving claims remain subject to their state-law elements.

##### II. An equal conduct duty is not made additional merely by a damages remedy

**Operative rule:** Section 360k(a) does not expressly preempt an independently available state duty that genuinely requires the same conduct as the applicable federal duty merely because state law adds damages. Requiring negligence or an unreasonable hazard in addition to violation of that same conduct obligation can narrow recovery without making the substantive duty different; a claim requiring different warnings or production conduct is not parallel merely because its pleading invokes federal law.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all nine Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress required a difference or addition in the obligation that the State imposes. A remedy for violating the same obligation does not supply that difference. The lower court therefore erred in treating the prospect of damages as sufficient to bar an equal-duty theory. Additional state-law conditions for recovery, such as negligence or an unreasonable hazard, do not broaden the conduct requirement when a violation of the same federal obligation remains necessary.

Section 808.1(d)(2) expressly preserves equal or substantially identical requirements. The comparison concerns how the duty actually operates, not identical words alone. A theory permitting liability despite satisfaction of the identified federal conduct obligation, or demanding additional warning content or changed production conduct, cannot establish equality by calling itself parallel. Cipollone's claim-specific approach similarly requires attention to the duty supporting liability rather than the pleading label.

The Eleventh Circuit reached this argument despite its first appearance in reply, so no forfeiture bars review. It assumed Florida recognition and adequate pleading without deciding them. Those questions, actual violation, causal connection and available relief remain for ordinary proceedings.

This holding creates no private action directly enforcing the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. Section 337's enforcement allocation and any independently presented question under it remain distinct from the express-preemption issue decided here. Freightliner also preserves inquiry into an independently established actual conflict; no such conflict is proved merely by regulatory overlap or an additional remedy. The categorical parallel-duty barrier is reversed.

##### III. The asserted manufacturing and warning rules do not displace these claims

**Operative rule:** The general manufacturing and prescription-information requirements invoked here do not establish a federal counterpart that makes the presented ordinary manufacturing and warning duties different or additional within section 360k(a). The challenged preemption barriers are therefore reversed for negligent manufacture, failure to warn and corresponding strict-liability theories, without exempting all tort law or all claims involving generally applicable regulations.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Part 820 and section 801.109 impose enforceable obligations. Describing them as general does not make them advice or establish that no federal law governs this lead. But legal force and common subject matter do not by themselves identify the substantive federal choice that these state duties would alter.

The manufacturing provisions govern quality assurance, component acceptance, production controls, inspection, records, labeling and related operations. They leave substantial choices to manufacturers; maintaining a device master record and conforming production to the original design do not establish substantive FDA approval of that design. The warning rules require practitioner information with their own qualifications. Submission of proposed labels under section 807.87 does not establish substantive approval of their precise contents.

The contrary argument would treat these applicable commands as necessarily sufficient to displace any further state assessment of manufacture or warning. It omits the required comparison of actual obligations. Neither the negligence label nor the strict-liability label resolves that comparison. Freightliner supplies the need to identify the operative federal choice; Travelers reinforces examination of the particular statutory connection rather than unlimited effects from shared subject matter.

The result is confined to the asserted federal predicates and the duties presented. A generally framed rule can contain a precise applicable command; the Court imposes no universal condition that a regulation name one manufacturer or model. An actually ambiguous pleaded duty may require identification in ordinary proceedings, but every products case need not begin with a new evidentiary hearing. Regulatory compliance, liability and a different approval pathway remain undecided.

##### IV. A common-law damages duty can be a preemptible requirement

**Operative rule:** A state obligation enforced through common-law damages can constitute a requirement under section 360k(a); common law has no categorical exemption from the express clause. Inclusion alone does not establish preemption: the applicable federal obligation, actual difference or addition, subject connection and statutory exceptions must still be satisfied.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer. These are five direct votes on this proposition despite their different positions on Part III.

**Controlling explanation:** A damages judgment can require a manufacturer to change its conduct as effectively as an enacted command. The statutory word requirement does not cease to apply because a court enforces the obligation. Cipollone's covered common-law warning duties illustrate that distinction, while its different statutory clauses and separate surviving categories caution against treating inclusion as universal displacement.

Section 808.1(b) also recognizes requirements having legal force through court decisions. That provision is consistent with the enacted term. It does not eliminate the rest of the statutory comparison or turn every state tort rule into a forbidden device requirement.

Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg would leave the general question open because these claims survive on narrower grounds. The Court decides the limited inclusion question but no hypothetical future application. Part IV does not itself preempt a surviving Lohr claim, establish a new balancing test, or make the dissenters' manufacturing/warning application the Court's rule. The direct majority for inclusion and the direct majority rejecting preemption on this record answer different questions.

##### V. The FDA's formal interpretation independently supports the bounded manufacturing and warning result

**Operative rule:** Within the statutory comparison, the FDA reasonably interprets section 360k through section 808.1(d) to require specific counterpart regulations or other specific applicable requirements before divergent state requirements are displaced. Giving that formal construction substantial weight independently supports survival of these manufacturing and warning claims, while preserving its general-applicability, equal-duty and divergent substantive-application qualifications.

**Authority:** Part V, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. This independent ground has five direct supporters; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Controlling explanation:** The FDA administers the device scheme, exercises the express exemption responsibility in section 360k(b), and has section 371(a)'s authority to issue regulations for efficient enforcement. Those responsibilities support consideration of its formal interpretation of the relation between federal and state requirements. MCI first requires adherence to statutory boundaries; Sweet Home and Smiley then give force to reasonable interpretation within actual delegated responsibility. No agency receives authority to contradict Congress.

Section 808.1 reads the federal and state requirements together. Its specificity provision identifies a meaningful counterpart; its examples preserve general requirements reaching products beyond devices and genuinely equal obligations. These terms reasonably support the conclusion that the asserted generic controls do not displace the ordinary duties here. They are read with subsection (d)(6)(ii), which permits preemption when a generally worded adulteration or misbranding prohibition actually imposes a divergent substantive requirement on a specific device. Labels alone therefore protect neither side.

The objection that this construction adds an impermissible condition gives insufficient weight to the ambiguity in the required federal/state comparison and the agency's responsible administration of that boundary. But the construction is not a universal individual-model requirement. Nor does it establish automatic deference to every litigating position or agency pronouncement about preemption. Smiley assumed without deciding that courts always independently determine statutory preemption; its substantive banking interpretation supplies no free-standing FDA power to preempt. The Court decides this reasonable formal construction and application, not every possible device regulation or state duty.

##### Operative statutory and regulatory qualifications

These qualifications govern the foregoing holdings and remand; the descriptions of regulatory text do not enlarge a Justice's joins to Parts IV or V.

- **Separate state-requirement exemption:** Section 360k(b) permits the Secretary, on a State or political subdivision's application, after notice and opportunity for an oral hearing, to exempt a requirement by regulation on prescribed conditions if it is more stringent than the otherwise applicable federal requirement, or if compelling local conditions require it and compliance would not violate an applicable federal requirement. The no-federal-violation condition belongs to the second alternative. An exemption does not remove federal obligations. No pertinent exemption is established; an equal, nonpreempted duty does not first require one.
- **Section 808.1's complete scope:** Subsection (b) includes force-of-law court decisions. Subsection (d)(1) preserves general requirements whose purpose reaches other products as well as devices, including general electrical codes and the Uniform Commercial Code's fitness warranty, and unfair-trade rules not limited to devices. Subsection (d)(2) preserves equal or substantially identical requirements. Subsection (d)(6)(ii) preserves general adulteration/misbranding prohibitions but displaces an actual divergent substantive device requirement; interpretation and enforcement, rather than literal wording alone, control that comparison.
- **Other section 808.1 qualifications:** Professional-practice permits, licensing, registration, certification and related approval remain subject to conflicting federal restrictions under sections 520(e) or (g), which prevail. State procurement specifications and Medicaid or similar payment criteria are preserved. General enforcement, inspections, registration and licensing remain subject to conflicting federal records, reporting and good-manufacturing-practice rules under sections 519 and 520(f), which prevail. Administrative delegations and related matters, measures solely raising revenue or charging fees, and the identified atomic-energy and radiation measures remain outside preemption on their stated terms; the last category lasts until applicable specific FDA requirements intervene. Subsection (e) assigns the FDA a determination role through prescribed procedures subject to judicial review, without establishing an exhaustion bar here. Subsections (c) and (f) preserve applicable federal duties whether or not a state requirement is exempt or preempted. No unestablished factual exception is applied.
- **Original manufacturing regime:** Part 820 applies to finished human-use devices and defines a finished device to include an accessory suitable for use whether packaged or labeled. It is not generally directed at manufacturers of components or parts; blood and blood components have Part 606's separate treatment. The everyday description of this lead as a component does not settle that classification. Additional critical-device duties retain their own applicability: the definition addresses an implant or life-supporting device whose failure when properly used can reasonably be expected to cause significant injury, with identification by the Commissioner after advisory consultation. Class III status alone is not a substitute finding. Where simultaneous compliance with applicable regulations is impossible, the specific device regulation supersedes the general regulation. No exemption, variance or violation is found here.
- **Real manufacturing obligations:** The original provisions require appropriate quality assurance, trained personnel, suitable premises and equipment, controlled component acceptance and handling, written manufacturing specifications and processing controls, inspection and failure investigation, labeling integrity, protective packaging, distribution controls and records. Section 820.100 requires manufacturing conformance to the original design or approved changes, with controlled and documented specification and process changes; it does not itself approve the design's safety. Critical-device additions, including component sampling, operation verification, labeling, distribution and history records, depend on their actual applicability. The Court decides no breach of any particular provision.
- **Distinct manufacturing exemption or variance:** Section 360j(f)(2) permits a subject person to petition in the prescribed form with required information. An exemption requires a determination that compliance is unnecessary to assure safety, effectiveness and statutory compliance. A variance requires a determination that the proposed replacement methods, facilities and controls for manufacture, packing, storage and installation are sufficient to assure safety, effectiveness and compliance with the Act; an approval order must impose conditions necessary to assure those ends. Optional advisory referral requires recommendations within sixty days; the Secretary must approve or deny within sixty days after submission or, if referred, after expiration of the committee's sixty-day period, whichever is later. An informal hearing is available after the order. These provisions differ from a State's section 360k(b) exemption, and no granted exception is inferred.
- **Prescription information:** Section 801.109 conditionally exempts a device unsafe without licensed-practitioner supervision from consumer adequate-directions requirements. The device must be in the possession of a person, or that person's agents or employees, regularly and lawfully engaged in its manufacture, transportation, storage or wholesale or retail distribution, or in the possession of a practitioner licensed by law to use or order it; it must be sold only to or on the prescription or other order of such a practitioner for professional use. Subsection (b)'s prescription caution and method-of-use label requirements exclude surgical instruments. Subsection (c) requires dispensing-package information about indications, effects, routes, methods, frequency and duration, relevant hazards, contraindications, side effects and precautions for intended and advertised uses. That information may be omitted only when commonly known to licensed practitioners; on written request stating reasonable grounds, the Commissioner will offer an opinion on proposed omission. No omission qualification is found here.
- **Separate labeling exceptions:** Section 801.109(d) independently requires adequate use information on manufacturer, packer or distributor labeling that furnishes or purports to furnish use information, whether or not within the dispensing package. Its exception covers a reminder piece calling attention only to the device name without indications or other use information. Subsection (e)'s issuance-or-revision-date duty excludes labels and cartons. These exceptions do not merge with subsection (c)'s permissive omission or subsection (b)'s surgical-instrument exception. Section 807.87(e)'s submission of proposed labels, labeling and advertisements is not substantive approval of their contents.
- **Clearance, classification and approval:** Section 360(k)'s report ordinarily precedes intended interstate commercial introduction by at least ninety days. Classification, substantial equivalence and premarket approval remain distinct. Section 360e(a) requires approval for a class III device subject to a subsection (b) regulation or classified under section 360c(f), unless exempt under section 360j(g). Section 360e(b) supplies the rulemaking route for pre-1976 devices and their substantially equivalent types; no such approval is found for this lead. PMA's distinct evaluation and application requirements do not become clearance requirements by inference. The Court decides neither a different approval pathway nor a particular investigational exemption.
- **Temporal limits:** The 1994 Code and April 1, 1996 CFR are text sources, not findings that every amendment applied to the 1982 clearance, 1987 implantation or 1990 injury. The 1990 codification requires the same intended use and a Secretary's order finding either the same technological characteristics or, for different characteristics, submitted information—including clinical data if the Secretary deems it necessary—demonstrating that the device is as safe and effective as a legally marketed device and raises no different questions of safety and efficacy than the predicate. Different technological characteristics means a significant change in materials, design, energy source or other device features. A predicate removed from the market at the Secretary's initiative or judicially determined adulterated or misbranded cannot establish substantial equivalence. That later text is not backdated; the lower court assumed that it described earlier practice. Later submission amendments and design-validation language create no earlier approval or breach. The original Part 820 regime governs the comparison, and no later quality-system revision enters the decision. An actual conduct-date duty remains to be identified for liability.
- **Savings and federal enforcement:** Section 360h(d) preserves liability despite compliance with an order under that section; it is not an unrestricted saving clause for every device action. When damages for economic loss enforce such liability, the value of a remedy supplied under the order must be taken into account. No such order or offset is adjudicated. Section 337 reserves direct enforcement to the United States except for its State food-enforcement provision. A State may bring proceedings in its own name and jurisdiction to civilly enforce, or restrain violations of, sections 341 and 343(b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g), (h), (i), (k), (q) and (r), only for food located in the State. Proceedings may not begin until thirty days after notice to the Secretary, or until ninety days after notice if the Secretary begins an informal or formal enforcement action concerning the food within the first thirty days. Proceedings are barred while the Secretary diligently prosecutes a court proceeding concerning the food or after settlement of that proceeding or the related informal or formal enforcement action; the State may intervene as of right in the described court proceeding. This exception creates no private device action. No unpresented standalone enforcement defense or later implied-preemption rule is decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., June 24, 1992:** Its actual covered common-law warning proposition supports identifying the duty imposed by liability. Its distinct statutory clauses and category-specific surviving grounds remain intact; no whole amended-Act opinion or blanket tort exemption is imported. Part IV applies the inclusion principle to section 360k through its own text.
- **CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood, April 21, 1993:** Supplies the requirement of the actual applicable federal predicate and subject comparison. Its railroad-specific substantial-subsumption language, warning predicates, local-hazard qualification and speed holding are not substituted for the Medical Device Amendments.
- **Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick, April 18, 1995:** Applies the distinction between an operative federal requirement and regulatory absence or overlap, and preserves independently established actual-conflict analysis. Its reserved common-law question under another statute and its motor-vehicle saving clause do not decide this clause.
- **New York State Conference of Blue Cross & Blue Shield Plans v. Travelers Insurance Co., April 26, 1995:** Supports examining the connection required by the particular statute instead of unlimited displacement from indirect effects. Its ERISA holding becomes no general exemption for state health or tort law.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T, June 17, 1994:** Requires independent adherence to statutory limits before agency deference. The FDA may not dispense with Congress's comparison or create unlimited preemption authority.
- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837; Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Preserve reasonable agency interpretation within enacted ambiguity and actual delegated responsibility. Part V applies that bounded principle to the formal FDA construction without adopting every agency view or enlarging the statutory exemption power.
- **Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., June 3, 1996:** Preserves statutory construction before reasonable delegated interpretation, and distinguishes substantive agency responsibility from free-standing power to preempt. Its specific banking permission and qualified use of interpretation for antecedent transactions do not supply substantive approval or retroactive duties for this device.

#### Law After Decision

Section 360k(a) can reach common-law damages duties, but it does not categorically displace them. Courts must identify the actual state obligation, an applicable federal requirement, the difference or addition and the required subject connection, preserving statutory exceptions. A separate remedy and additional elements that narrow recovery do not themselves make an otherwise equal conduct duty different. State-law recognition and all liability elements remain necessary.

The design claims here survive because substantial-equivalence clearance did not establish the asserted substantive design approval. The manufacturing and warning claims survive the asserted generic federal predicates on the six-Justice comparison in Part III and the independent five-Justice formal-regulation ground in Part V. General regulatory form alone neither guarantees survival nor establishes displacement. The decision creates no private FDCA action, no universal model-specificity test, no all-tort exemption and no rule for an actual PMA approval or another future device pathway. Independently established actual conflict remains distinct.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They join the design, parallel-duty and manufacturing/warning holdings and the formal FDA interpretation. They would leave open whether some other common-law duty may be expressly preempted by section 360k. Rejecting Medtronic's sweeping displacement argument does not require categorical immunity for tort law, but neither does it require affirmative inclusion beyond the duties actually resolved. Cipollone construed different statutory language and does not compel that additional step here. Their reserved position is not the Court's rule because five Justices directly adopt Part IV. Their agreement with the present application does not depend on a prediction that future common-law preemption must be rare.

**Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I, II and IV. Substantial-equivalence clearance supplies no substantive design approval; genuinely equal state duties are not different merely because damages are available. But actual federal manufacturing and warning commands apply to this device even though they cover other devices. In their view, the statute requires applicability, difference or addition and subject connection, not an extra specificity prerequisite. The FDA cannot impose that extra condition against text they find clear. Section 808.1's recognition of court decisions and divergent substantive applications also defeats a categorical exemption based on general state-law form. They would affirm preemption of genuinely divergent manufacturing/warning duties, reverse the parallel-duty bar and remand to identify the actual obligations. They make no finding of compliance, breach or Florida entitlement and do not demand dismissal of an entire count that may contain a viable equal-duty theory.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins Parts I through IV and the judgment. Congress's express comparison controls: a damages duty can be a requirement, but clearance and the agency's presence do not supply a missing substantive federal comparator. Equal conduct does not become different merely because damages are available. His conclusion rests on that statutory ground, so he does not join Part V's independent reliance on agency interpretation. He neither rejects that ground as unlawful nor adopts a general tort exemption. The unresolved content of a particular pleaded duty can be identified through ordinary proceedings without requiring a new preliminary hearing in every products case; breach, causation and relief remain unproved.

Other properly presented issues remain for ordinary adjudication within the Court's holdings. No special mandate or compliance date is set.

### Geier v. American Honda Motor Co., No. 98-1811

Merits decision, May 22, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 7, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, which affirmed dismissal of the District of Columbia tort action. The questions concern the Safety Act's express and common-law saving provisions and actual conflict between the asserted no-airbag duty and FMVSS 208's operative restraint transition.

In 1992 Geier was injured when her 1987 Honda Accord struck a tree. The car had manual seat belts and no airbag or other passive restraint. The presented theory alleges that the car should have had a driver's airbag despite federal compliance. No trial finding establishes defect, causation, injury prevention or damages.

**Statutory setting.** Former 15 U.S.C. §1392(d), when a federal motor-vehicle standard is in effect, bars a nonidentical state standard addressing the same aspect of performance. It permits enforcement of identical standards and higher performance requirements for vehicles or equipment procured for a federal, state or local government's own use. Former §1397(k) separately provides that compliance with a federal safety standard does not exempt a person from common-law liability. Neither permission is an exception silently transferred to the other provision; no procurement dispute is decided.

**Restraint options.** S4.1.2.1 provides automatic frontal/angular protection at front outboard positions, conforming Type 1 or Type 2 belts at center-front and rear positions, and either automatic lateral/rollover protection or the prescribed front-outboard belt arrangement with the additional testing. S4.1.2.2 permits the narrower head-on automatic system, with the prescribed belts, perpendicular-impact testing both with automatic protection and the Type 1 belt, and front-outboard warning requirements. S4.1.2.3 permits the lap-and-shoulder-belt/warning arrangement: except for convertibles and open-body vehicles, front outboard positions require either a conforming Type 2 assembly with a nondetachable shoulder belt or the Type 1 alternative meeting the prescribed perpendicular-impact requirement. Center-front and other positions retain their separate belt/warning requirements. Convertibles and open-body vehicles have distinct all-position Type 1/Type 2 belt and front-position warning requirements. The automatic amendment covers passenger-car front outboard positions; other seats and vehicle classes retain their applicable requirements. None of these alternatives is an airbag mandate.

**Phase-in and credits.** The successive September-to-August production periods beginning September 1, 1986 require automatic protection for 10%, 25% and 40%, respectively, subject to the rule's conditions. The original 1984 rule uses the preceding three-year production averages: September 1983–August 1986, September 1984–August 1987 and September 1985–August 1988. The manufacture-date 1986 provisions permit those averages or the manufacturer's annual production for the respective phase-in year. Nonbelt automatic driver protection with automatic right-front protection receives 1.5 credit; the right-front technology need not match the driver's. The 1986 provision separately allows one credit for qualifying nonbelt driver protection with a manual Type 2 right-front belt.

Qualifying production from September 1985 through August 1986 may count toward the first year. The second-year carry covers qualifying production through August 1987 not already counted toward the first; the third covers qualifying production through August 1988 not counted toward either earlier year. Cars produced by more than one manufacturer are attributed only once: imports to the importer and domestic cars to a participating marketer, subject to a qualifying written contract assigning the car to one manufacturer and Part 585 reporting. The 1986 due-care provision requires the manufacturer to establish that, despite due care, it had no reason to know of nonconformity; it addresses regulatory noncompliance, not general tort immunity. The 1984 preamble had contemplated later action on that subject. No Honda production, credit, attribution or diligence finding is made.

**Full implementation and belt-law condition.** The September 1, 1989 all-new-passenger-car automatic-protection requirement is subject to S4.1.5. No later than April 1, 1989, the Secretary must determine that qualifying enacted belt-use laws cover at least two-thirds of the combined population of the 50 States and District of Columbia, using the most recent Census estimates. Cars manufactured on or after that determination may use any of the three options. The condition is neither automatic nor a count of two-thirds of the States.

Qualifying laws require proper belt use by front-seat occupants while the car moves forward, medical-only waivers, at least a $25 penalty including allowable costs for each violator, permission to mitigate an injured violator's damages, a compliance-encouragement program, and effectiveness by September 1, 1989. Existing state permission to mitigate can suffice; reduction of damages is not mandatory. Preamble discussion of evaluation reports and waivers for substantially complying pre-August 1, 1984 laws does not replace that operative list. No finding establishes that the determination occurred.

The agency sought safety, development of alternative technologies, consumer acceptance, innovation and a gradual transition attentive to cost and production problems. It encouraged nonbelt systems and recognized airbag benefits, without prescribing a numerical technology mix or a minimum number of airbag-free cars. Its own explanation also relied on potential liability for deficient systems, competition and product pride. The legal effect of those purposes, read together, is the dispute before the Court.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

The D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining dismissal remains in place at the scope of the general no-airbag theory. The Court orders no factual remand, invalidation of FMVSS 208 or new agency proceeding. It decides no actual negligence, defect, causation, regulatory violation or damages and creates no alternative pleading.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer throughout; Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg in Parts I–II | Affirms on the actual-conflict ground in Part III | Part I: statutory construction and background; Part II: independent actual-conflict inquiry; Part III: the particular no-airbag conflict and preserved boundaries |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg | Would reverse the preemption dismissal and remand for ordinary tort adjudication | Disagrees with Part III's inference of protected exclusivity, while joining Parts I–II |

#### Holdings

##### I. The express clause does not independently extinguish this saved common-law claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Former §1392(d), read with §1397(k), does not expressly displace this common-law no-airbag claim merely because the car complied with FMVSS 208. The common-law saving rule defeats categorical compliance immunity without declaring that damages duties can never be state safety standards.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court gives effect to both provisions. Section 1392(d) addresses nonidentical standards governing the same aspect of performance and preserves identical-standard enforcement and higher government-procurement requirements. Section 1397(k) expressly refuses to make compliance an exemption from common-law liability. Reading the first as categorically eliminating this action would deprive the second of its operative protection. Cipollone requires attention to the actual enacted clause and the duty necessary for liability; Medtronic likewise distinguishes the existence of a damages duty from whether the particular federal provision displaces it. Neither supplies a universal exemption for tort law. The Court decides the interaction of this Act's provisions, leaving their separately stated qualifications intact. The failure of this express defense does not itself determine the independent conflict defense.

##### II. Preserved common-law claims remain subject to an independently established actual conflict

**Holding and operative rule:** The presence of the express clause and compliance saving provision does not foreclose an independently demonstrated conflict between a state common-law duty and an operative federal command or supported regulatory objective. Federal compliance, overlapping regulation, agency preference or regulatory silence alone does not establish that conflict.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** Myrick rejects the inference that an express clause necessarily exhausts every conflict inquiry, while requiring a demonstrated federal premise. Its invalidated standard and regulatory absence did not establish a policy against the omitted equipment. Gade likewise supplies no automatic displacement from overlap and no Court-wide physical-impossibility-only rule. The saving clause preserves liability notwithstanding compliance; it does not require enforcement of a duty that actually defeats governing federal law. Equally, describing a manufacturer's choice as federally permitted cannot establish immunity from the liability Congress saved. The inquiry must identify the valid federal method, the actual duty alleged and their concrete incompatibility. The possibility of installing an airbag while satisfying the regulation does not alone defeat every conflict argument, but a policy preference for uniformity does not alone sustain one. The present operative program therefore requires its own examination.

##### III. The general no-airbag duty defeats this deliberately chosen transition

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented general duty to install a driver's airbag is preempted because it would displace FMVSS 208's deliberately retained restraint alternatives and phased transition, which were operative means of promoting safety through development and public acceptance. This conflict sustains dismissal only of that general theory; compliance does not immunize every federally permitted design, and nonconflicting federal-minimum, noncompliance, defective-installation, performance, risk-specific and genuinely model-specific duties remain available when otherwise supported.

**Authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins. The four partial joiners dissent from this holding and the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court finds the affirmative regulatory choice absent in Myrick. The alternatives, staged percentages and differentiated incentives are means of developing restraint systems and gaining acceptance during a gradual transition. A categorical duty demanding airbags in the covered vehicles would remove the relevant nonairbag choices and accelerate the transition the agency deliberately structured. The conflict does not depend on a fixed numerical mix, a minimum number of cars without airbags, or a command that this Accord lack one.

The saving text and the agency's own recognition of deficient-system liability are substantial objections. They preserve real legal work: federal compliance alone is no defense to an independently deficient system or another duty compatible with the program. But such liability differs from the general technology mandate presented here. The safety objective, airbag benefits and encouragement of nonbelt protection do not erase the contemporaneously chosen method of obtaining those benefits through alternatives and transition. The Court relies on that method, not on every policy compromise or a later litigation assertion.

Shanklin illustrates the need to identify the operative federal selection rather than presume that every minimum standard is a ceiling; its different crossing regime supplies no automatic answer. Mining and Public Lands Council preserve actual statutory authority and temporal limits, which remain necessary here. Later safety evidence cannot retroactively alter the regulation governing this vehicle. The retained claim categories do not establish a defect, a preserved alternative pleading or recovery. Their actual duties, proof and relation to the federal program require their own analysis. On the general no-airbag theory before the Court, the demonstrated conflict warrants affirmance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general motor-vehicle field immunity, fixed technology allocation, binding deference to litigation advocacy, universal all-tort exemption or impossibility-only test is adopted. No Honda-specific production, credit, attribution, due-care or testing fact, and no completed belt-law trigger, is found. The Court neither invalidates the standard nor decides whether an independently presented different duty would conflict.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick:** Applies its independent actual-conflict inquiry and distinguishes its regulatory absence from this affirmative, operative transition. Its rejection of compliance immunity and unsupported federal purposes remains intact.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc.:** Applies clause- and duty-specific construction. Its distinct cigarette provisions supply neither a universal tort exemption nor a rule excluding every independent conflict inquiry.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Preserves the rejection of automatic displacement from regulatory overlap. No separate physical-impossibility formulation becomes the Court's exclusive rule.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Applies the need to compare actual substantive duties and keep interpretive weight within statutory authority. Damages duties can be requirements without every such duty being preempted.
- **Norfolk Southern Railway Co. v. Shanklin:** Preserves its specific device-selection holding and its distinction from a universal minimum-standard ceiling; this case rests on its own regulatory method.
- **United States v. Locke / Intertanko v. Locke:** Preserves its subject-specific federal allocation and full enacted saving language. Its national vessel field does not establish a motor-vehicle field immunity.
- **National Mining Association v. Department of the Interior; Public Lands Council v. Babbitt:** Preserve actual delegated authority, distinct statutory conditions and temporal limits. Neither authorizes later advocacy to manufacture an earlier regulatory objective or to disregard common-law saving language.

#### Law After Decision

Effective May 22, 2000, the general no-airbag theory presented here cannot impose a duty that defeats the demonstrated restraint-options transition. The Safety Act continues to preserve common-law liability despite compliance, subject to actual conflict established at the level of the duty and operative federal scheme. Federal-minimum, noncompliance, defective-installation, performance, risk-specific and genuinely model-specific duties retain their own potential operation when they do not obstruct that scheme and their ordinary predicates are met. No different claim is adjudicated, no blanket federal design immunity results, and no precedent is overruled.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The dissenters join Parts I–II, including the rejection of express displacement and the availability of independently established actual conflict. They would reverse and remand for ordinary adjudication of the actual tort claim because the statutory and regulatory scheme does not sufficiently establish protection of these compliance choices against the common-law liability Congress expressly saved.

Cipollone requires the actual statutory language and predicate duty; Myrick requires an affirmative federal premise; Medtronic and the particular Shanklin arrangement require the substantive counterpart claimed to displace the state obligation. The options and phase-in genuinely advance innovation and public acceptance, but permission to satisfy national requirements in several ways does not itself establish immunity. The absence of an airbag-omission command or fixed mix is not an exclusive physical-impossibility test; it bears on whether the asserted protected choice has been shown here. The agency's safety purpose, encouragement of airbags and own reliance on potential liability for deficient systems reinforce the statutory reservation.

The dissent confronts the Court's concern that widespread airbag duties would influence manufacturers and hasten the transition. That practical pressure does not, in its view, demonstrate legally protected exclusivity under this saving provision. It does not deny that damages can impose duties or that an options rule could produce a demonstrated conflict in another application. It would remove this defense without giving it a new evidentiary trial, awarding damages, finding a defect or adding a different vehicle-specific theory. Its preserved-liability conclusion is not the Court's holding.

### Brown v. City of Oneonta

Merits decision, November 13, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Second Circuit's amended judgment, 221 F.3d 329. The question is whether deliberate investigative selection by race escapes racial-classification review when the racial feature comes from a victim's description and additional hostility is not shown, and what constitutional standard governs that selection.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Reversed in part and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The reversal reaches the rejection of the equal-protection claim insofar as it rests on a categorical victim-description exemption. The affected claim is restored for application of the governing racial-classification standard to the actual selection, scope and burdens. Independently resolved claims, existing Fourth Amendment dispositions and the earlier qualified-immunity judgment protecting university officials for release of the list remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Deliberate racial selection requires justification.** When officials intentionally select people for investigation by race, they use a racial classification even if a victim supplied that feature and no hostility is shown. The government must establish an important lawful investigative objective and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between that objective and the actual selection, its coverage and burdens, including a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives offering comparable investigative value with materially less racial disadvantage.

**Authority:** O'Connor's opinion for all nine Justices controls the classification rule, its standard and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the source of identifying information can justify an official racial choice; it does not erase that choice. Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña supplies the common justification standard; Engineering Contractors and City of Dallas require examination of the evidence connecting the objective to the actual measure and burden. Solving the reported violent attack is an important lawful objective. A concrete description linking race to age, sex, an injured hand, location and timing may support appropriately limited measures. The Court therefore rejects the contention that classification review requires police to ignore useful descriptions. It also rejects a categorical permission to investigate a racial community without examining the description's other features and the practical scope of the operation. The wider sweep and alleged encounter with a woman require that examination. Testimony, investigative records and reasonable inferences can establish fit; no commissioned study or universal least-restrictive-means requirement follows. Consensual questioning and a Fourth Amendment seizure are distinct, but consent alone does not remove an intentionally racial burden from equal-protection review. Membership in a described group does not establish an individual encounter, injury or defendant's responsibility.

**Precedent treatment:** Peña's common standard is extended to purposeful investigative selection; its evidentiary and remedial limits remain.

**Remand rule.** The lower courts must examine the actual selection criteria, scope and burdens under that standard, and address personal responsibility, coercion where relevant, immunity and relief through their distinct governing rules. Immunity remains measured against the law at the September 1992 conduct; this decision does not retrospectively supply clearly established notice.

**Authority and explanation:** The same unanimous opinion controls. Lesage separates unequal-process injury from consequential loss and appropriate relief. The categorical exemption prevented the necessary examination; removing it restores the affected claim without reopening independently sustained dispositions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña (June 12, 1995): extended to deliberate investigative selection; the common substantial-justification standard governs rather than strict scrutiny.
- Engineering Contractors (1998) and City of Dallas (1999): applied for evidence-supported fit, actual burdens and concrete practicable alternatives.
- Eisenberg (May 30, 2000): its acknowledgment of the binding common standard is preserved; its distinct educational component is not extended to criminal investigation.
- Lesage (November 29, 1999): applied to keep the classification inquiry separate from individual causation, immunity and consequential relief.

#### Law After Decision

Purposeful investigative selection by race is subject to the common substantial-justification standard. Victim-supplied identifying information may justify fitted measures, but its source and the absence of hostility create no categorical exemption. The rule governs the classification and its justification; individual liability and remedies remain subject to their existing requirements.

#### Source Notes

U.S. Const., Amdt. 14; Brown v. City of Oneonta, 221 F.3d 329 (2d Cir. 2000).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The affected equal-protection claim returns for proceedings under the stated rule. Unchallenged and independently sustained dispositions retain their force.

**End of entry: Brown v. City of Oneonta, Merits decision, November 13, 2001.**

### Saucier v. Katz, No. 99-1977

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 20, 2001. On certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed denial of qualified immunity on the surviving federal-officer excessive-force damages claim. The questions concern the relationship between Fourth Amendment force reasonableness and qualified immunity, and whether the officer is entitled to immunity on the facts properly assumed in this interlocutory appeal.

At the September 24, 1994 Presidio ceremony attended by the Vice President, military police officers Saucier and Parker removed Katz after he displayed a protest banner. Katz's supported account describes an unresisting demonstrator carried or pulled toward a van and pushed inside with enough force that he narrowly avoided striking his head. He avoided physical injury. His leg brace was visible, but Saucier's actual awareness is disputed. The district judge reviewed the news video and identified disputes about threat, resistance, force and the officers' participation. The contention that Parker alone supplied the shove is not an established finding. #### Separate Writings

**Souter, announcing the judgment.** Souter considers the merits and immunity inquiries legally distinct: an officer may perceive the facts correctly yet reasonably misunderstand the particular constitutional limit. Harlow and Anderson preserve that legal-error protection; Graham's allowance for reasonable factual perceptions does not itself decide it. General prohibition of excessive force is insufficient by itself, but a materially identical precedent is not indispensable. Hunter and Elder require the proper adverse factual assumptions and the full relevant conduct-date law. Souter would have ordinary lower-court immunity adjudication first determine whether those assumed facts establish a constitutional violation and, if so, whether the particular right was sufficiently established, so that the constitutional rule can develop. He recognizes the limits of the granted review here and assumes a possible violation; he does not require decision of a constitutional issue outside review or an advisory ruling. That sequencing view is his own, not a rule adopted by the judgment majority. Unlike the dissenters, he leaves the first distinct application to the lower court and directs neither immunity nor its denial. Unlike the other concurring Justices, he retains the distinct legal-error rationale in this force setting.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice accepts the exact existing-law remand while proposing a different personal defense. He would displace Harlow and Anderson only insofar as they protect an official merely because the violated right was insufficiently clearly established, preserving their compatible particularized analysis and lawful summary-judgment requirements. First, the complete constitutional rule would account for urgency, reasonably perceived danger, reasonable factual mistakes and operational discretion. If a violation were established, the official would bear the burden of identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance on it and a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately conveying that authority could implement reliance. Legal silence, lack of a materially identical case, department custom, an unsupported supervisor's assurance and general job authority would not suffice.

In his view, legitimate protection against unexpected legal change concerns affirmative authoritative permission. The existing defense's predictability and litigation-control benefits do not justify withholding an otherwise available remedy from the first claimant establishing a particular application of settled protection. Graham already accounts for the officer's practical circumstances; those same factual allowances should not excuse wrongful force again. On Katz's supported account, an unnecessary shove of an already controlled, unresisting demonstrator violates the force standard without requiring physical injury, while necessary proportionate security removal remains lawful. Actual necessity, resistance, perceptions and participation remain disputed. The record identifies no binding precedent or enactment directly authorizing the alleged unnecessary shove; general security authority is insufficient.

His proposed defense would govern this pending case, with a fair opportunity for bounded submissions on genuinely new reliance elements. An expectation of immunity from unclear law would not preserve the displaced defense; final judgments and ordinary preservation and preclusion rules would remain. The proposal concerns this personal legal-uncertainty defense and materially equivalent claims, not function-specific absolute immunity, sovereign immunity, proper defendants, causation, injury or the availability of a cause of action. Failure of the proposed defense would establish neither a constitutional violation, damages, institutional liability nor a new cause of action; each independent requirement remains. These are separate views: the Court's mandate imposes no reliance burden, transition rule or additional submissions requirement.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens and Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** These Justices join the exact vacatur and open application remand while maintaining that Graham already tests the practical choices and reasonable mistakes available to an officer using force. They reject a second overlapping reasonableness shelter for force found objectively unreasonable under that complete standard. That objection is confined to this excessive-force setting; they retain ordinary Harlow protection elsewhere and do not endorse the Chief Justice's general actual-reliance replacement.

The supported unnecessary shared shove of a controlled, unresisting person cannot be dismissed by treating every degree of force as justified by the initial need for security removal. Johnson and Sterling prevent reappraisal of the participation evidence on this appeal. Plakas permits early merits judgment on a properly supported no-violation account, but its immediate armed attack without a contrary material account is absent here. The continuous movement into the van is relevant context, not a factual answer to the alleged unnecessary force. Their separate analysis does not alter their vote for the mandate: neither immunity nor denial, automatic trial, liability or damages is directed.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** These Justices would reverse and direct personal damages immunity while assuming a possible constitutional violation. Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal error independently of Graham's merits inquiry. Accepting the plaintiff-favorable two-officer account, they regard the carrying and van entry as a continuous urgent removal from a protected event; the particular boundary of this brief nondeadly force was not sufficiently apparent in September 1994. They recognize the strongest contrary point: a controlled, unresisting person's forceful van entry may lack additional demonstrated need. They nevertheless treat this as uncertainty over the degree of force during removal, not a legally unmistakable separate assault after the seizure ended.

Their position does not depend on deciding that Parker acted alone, finding resistance, declaring the force lawful or making injury a prerequisite. Hunter and Sterling preserve the adverse assumed facts; their disagreement concerns the legal conclusion drawn from them. They support an ordinary merits-first approach for the reasons of legal development Souter describes, subject to the actual scope of review. Their agreement with him on that position is not a join in the judgment-announcing opinion and supplies no controlling sequence rule. They reject an open application remand because they would decide the legal defense now.

### Overton v. Bazzetta, No. 02-94

Merits decision, June 16, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 26, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, which affirmed facial invalidation of Michigan's noncontact-visitation restrictions. The questions concern surviving association under the First and Fourteenth Amendments, the restrictions' relationship to legitimate penological interests, and the Eighth Amendment.

The approved visitor list allows unlimited immediate family and ten other designated visitors, subject to the governing restrictions; qualified clergy and attorneys on official business need not be listed. #### Holdings

##### I. Compatible family association and the governing standard

**Holding and operative rule:** Incarceration does not extinguish protected family association that is compatible with confinement. Restrictions on the retained interest are governed by Turner's reasonable relationship to legitimate penological objectives, considering the rational connection, actual alternative means, institutional impact of accommodation, and ready alternatives; the standard does not demand the least restrictive means.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court, directly supported by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas join the subsequent applications as sufficient alternatives rather than this recognition of the retained interest.

**Controlling explanation:** Pell recognizes that custody permits substantial communication restrictions while leaving compatible constitutional protection. Turner makes that protection responsive to actual institutional conditions. Shaw applies the ordinary inquiry without a heightened tier, and Gerber requires an operational examination of a claimed compatible interest rather than categorical dismissal. Prisoners must establish the burden and the absence of the required reasonable relationship. Officials must identify legitimate, neutral objectives and supported connections, receiving the latitude inherent in prison administration. Actual letters, telephone calls, supervision, capacity, and security therefore matter; none is conclusive merely because it appears in a regulation. This protection does not entail unrestricted visits or every preferred visitor. It provides the standard for reviewing the restrictions actually challenged.

##### II. The three challenged regulatory designs

**Holding and operative rule — child visitors:** The facial showing is insufficient to invalidate the limitation of under-eighteen visitors to the inmate's children, stepchildren, grandchildren, and siblings, the exclusion of a child after parental rights terminate, or the requirement of accompaniment by an adult immediate family member of the child or inmate or the child's legal guardian. These provisions bear a reasonable relationship to child protection and orderly supervision under Turner.

**Authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on these applications.

**Controlling explanation:** The limits reduce the number and variety of children prison personnel must supervise and protect children visiting a custodial environment. The escort rule permits responsible family members on either side of the relationship and legal guardians; it does not confine accompaniment to one particular adult. The terminated-rights restriction respects the legal severance of that parental relationship. Noncontact design reduces some physical-transfer risks, but does not eliminate child welfare and supervision. Remaining communication and the institutional effects of broader accommodation support the restrictions. The record supplies no ready alternative that achieves those objectives at only negligible institutional cost. Turner consequently does not justify facial invalidation.

**Holding and operative rule — former prisoners:** Requiring a former prisoner both to be immediate family and to obtain the warden's prior approval survives this facial Turner challenge because it reasonably addresses security and renewed criminal contacts while preserving an approved family exception.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Part-II coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The State may take account of the risks associated with a former prisoner's return without treating every relative as automatically eligible or categorically excluded. The family exception and prior-approval requirement work together. Noncontact visits still entail supervision, contacts within the institution, and resource demands. Turner permits these reasonable administrative predictions, and the challengers have not demonstrated an equally effective ready alternative imposing only negligible costs. Ordinary communication alternatives remain relevant without being ideal substitutes for a visit.

**Holding and operative rule — repeated substance abuse:** The record does not establish facial invalidity of suspending ordinary visits after repeated substance-abuse violations, preserving attorney and clergy visits, and allowing an application for discretionary restoration after two years. Deterrence, remaining communication, and institutional costs establish a sufficient Turner relationship on this facial showing; two years marks eligibility to apply, not guaranteed restoration or a constitutional safe harbor for every later duration.

**Authority:** The same eight-Justice Part-II coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Drug and alcohol discipline serves a legitimate institutional objective, and loss of ordinary visitation can deter repeated violations even when the violation did not occur during a visit. The noncontact setting therefore does not defeat the disciplinary connection. Attorney and clergy access remains, and letters and calls supply additional means of communication whose actual utility matters. A less severe sanction is not a ready alternative merely because it is less severe; the challenger must show the relevant negligible-cost alternative under Turner. The facial record does not establish that restoration discretion necessarily produces permanent or arbitrary exclusion. Concrete indefinite suspensions, irrational denials, or an actual absence of realistic contact require the governing test on their own facts.

##### III. The separate Eighth Amendment claim

**Holding and operative rule:** Loss of ordinary visitation does not by itself establish objectively serious deprivation or a substantial risk of serious harm. The challenged regulatory design and this facial record fail that threshold under Wilson, Farmer, Helling, and Delaney, so the independent facial Eighth Amendment judgment cannot stand.

**Authority:** Part III of the Opinion of the Court, directly joined by all nine Justices as a bounded application of the governing conditions rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Wilson separates objective seriousness from culpability. Farmer requires actual appreciation and unreasonable disregard of a substantial danger; Helling permits prevention of an intolerable serious future risk, and Delaney applies that framework to an established serious deprivation rather than a disciplinary label. Ordinary visits, their duration, and the actual available contact must be assessed together. The design shown here does not establish the grave deprivation required for this facial claim. A proved severe or dangerous later application remains subject to the conditions rules. Actual knowledge may be shown circumstantially, but obviousness does not compel the inference; an objectively reasonable response defeats deliberate indifference. Preventive relief requires a current serious danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse likely to continue. An association injury cannot substitute for these distinct elements.

- Lewis v. Casey retains the actual-injury boundary and statutory conditions for any future prison decree.

The challenged designs survive facial review under Turner, while compatible family association remains protected through its operational standard. The two-year restoration point permits an application; the lawfulness of concrete later duration, denial, and actual communication conditions is judged on those facts. The independent Eighth Amendment elements remain distinct. ### Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, No. 01-344

Merits decision, April 29, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 26, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing its February 6, 2001 judgment, 238 F.3d 1090, holding the challenged compounding speech conditions unconstitutional and section 353a nonseverable. The question is whether conditioning the federal compounded-drug exemptions on the prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion restrictions violates the First Amendment. The separate nonseverability ruling was not brought within review.

Licensed pharmacies prepared promotional materials for distribution by mail and at medical conferences. The challenge concerns truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding. Section 503A(a) permits the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation that a compounded product is necessary for that patient. The ordinary route requires compounding by a licensed pharmacist in a State-licensed pharmacy or Federal facility, or by a licensed physician, on the identified patient's prescription from a licensed physician or another practitioner authorized by State law to prescribe. The separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship between the compounder and either that identified patient or the physician or other practitioner who will write the prescription. Section 503A(c) prohibits particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion while permitting promotion of compounding services; the necessity notation does not exempt a message from subsection (c).

Section 503A supplies only exemptions from section 501(a)(2)(B)'s manufacturing-practice requirement, section 502(f)(1)'s adequate-directions requirement and section 505's new-drug-approval requirement. Bulk drug substances must comply with an applicable United States Pharmacopoeia or National Formulary monograph and the United States Pharmacopoeia compounding chapter; if no monograph exists, they must be components of approved drugs or, if neither condition is met, appear on the Secretary's regulatory list. They must be manufactured by a registered establishment, including a foreign establishment registered under section 510(i), and accompanied by valid certificates of analysis. Nonbulk ingredients must comply with an applicable monograph, if one exists, and the compounding chapter.

The compounder may not use a drug product on the Secretary's published list of products withdrawn or removed because the product or a component was found unsafe or ineffective. Regular compounding, or compounding in inordinate amounts as defined by the Secretary, of essentially commercially available copies is barred; a change for an identified patient producing a significant difference determined by the prescribing practitioner is excluded from that copy category. The product must not be one identified by regulation as presenting demonstrable compounding difficulties reasonably demonstrating an adverse effect on safety or effectiveness.

The interstate alternatives are compounding in a State that has entered a memorandum with the Secretary addressing inordinate interstate distribution and providing for appropriate State-agency investigation of complaints about compounded products distributed outside that State, or, absent that memorandum, out-of-State distribution no greater than five percent of the pharmacy's or physician's total prescription orders dispensed or distributed. The statute contains no enacted twenty-percent memorandum ceiling. PET compounded drugs and radiopharmaceuticals are excluded. Mixing, reconstitution and other acts performed according to the manufacturer's approved labeling and other directions consistent with that labeling lie outside the statutory compounding definition. These distinct provisions are not interchangeable exceptions to the challenged promotion rule.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The challenged conditions burden truthful promotion more broadly than their justification supports

**Holding and operative rule.** The prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion conditions of section 503A violate the First Amendment as applied to truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding because the Government has not established a reasonable fit between their informational burden and its asserted distinction between individualized compounding and manufacturing subject to approval. The holding applies the ordinary commercial-speech framework; it neither requires the least restrictive imaginable measure nor prevents restrictions on misleading claims, unlawful transactions, actual manufacturing conduct or narrower commercial-speech measures supported by an adequate justification.

**Authority.** The six Justices joining Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, named above, adopt the complete fit holding, statutory explanation and affirmance. Thomas accepts this sufficient application without endorsing the framework as the full measure of constitutional protection. This is a directly shared rationale, not an aggregation of distinct advancement findings.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that calling drug-specific silence a condition of an exemption does not answer the First Amendment question. Central Hudson and Fox require a proportionate relationship between a lawful commercial-speech burden and the substantial interest it serves. The Court assumes the Government's strongest combined account of drug safety, approval integrity, individualized access and material advancement. The defect is fit.

Product information can enable clinicians and patients to locate an individualized preparation without establishing manufacture for a broad market. Section 503A(a) allows the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation of necessity; its separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship with either that identified patient or the practitioner who will prescribe it. Section 503A(c), however, separately bars promotion of a particular drug, drug class or drug type while allowing service promotion. The necessity notation is not an exception to that separate speech prohibition.

Production, distribution, patient-prescription and commercial-copy controls bear directly on the conduct Congress seeks to distinguish. The Court recognizes the contrary concern: quantity limits can exclude medically needed preparations and may not address every demand-driven use of unapproved drugs. That does not explain why useful truthful product information must carry this broad burden. The Government has not adequately justified this proxy against the available conduct distinctions. The Court selects no production cap and finds no guarantee that every proposed alternative is equally effective. As 44 Liquormart and Lorillard require, the actual speech burden remains a distinct inquiry even when the regulatory objective and some preventive effect are accepted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island: applied for independent examination of the burden on truthful information; its broader separate theories are not converted into a controlling categorical ban.
- Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly: applied for separate advancement and fit inquiries and attention to the actual lawful-speech burden; this decision rests on fit and assumes the Government's advancement account.
- Edenfield v. Fane: applied for justification responsive to the actual professional setting; the physician audience is part of the covered speech burden.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States: applied for practical examination of the fit between a selective speech restriction and the regulatory objective; no conclusion that all compounded products are safe follows.
- Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado: its benefit-condition principle remains applicable at its actual scope; selecting speech as the condition does not eliminate ordinary constitutional scrutiny.

The reviewed compounding speech conditions fail the existing reasonable-fit requirement. The Court establishes no independent failure of material advancement, categorical commercial-speech immunity or replacement compounding program. The Ninth Circuit's unreviewed nonseverability determination remains part of the affirmed judgment. Substantive drug-safety and manufacturing authority retains its independent scope.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the full fit holding because these restrictions fail even the lesser Central Hudson test. He separately regards suppression of accurate product information to prevent its effect on willing listeners as the mechanism his 44 Liquormart position rejects. Drug safety is legitimate, but that objective does not itself justify withholding accurate information; direct drug controls remain distinct. His concurrence is not judgment-only agreement and does not supply the Court with a broader categorical rule.

**Breyer, dissenting in part, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** The dissent would reverse the constitutional invalidation insofar as it rests on promotion to consumers. The statutory balance preserves medically needed preparations while preventing cumulative risks from a growing market of products that have not undergone approval. Product promotion can generate demand apart from a clinician's prior individualized assessment. Production caps, wholesale restrictions and controls on equipment or advance preparation may either exclude needed treatment or fail to address that demand. General service advertising and continuing physician involvement preserve meaningful channels. Under ordinary reasonable-fit review, the dissent therefore finds the consumer restriction proportionate despite its burden on truthful information. It reserves the distinct question of physician-directed promotion because that issue requires separate consideration; it neither sustains every communication restriction nor announces a benefit-condition exception to the First Amendment.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing

**Controlling proposition:** A bidder able and ready to compete suffers a personal injury when a governmental racial preference denies equal competitive opportunity; it need not prove that it would have received the contract. Prospective relief also requires a real, sufficiently imminent renewed exposure to that preference, which Adarand's repeated bidding and continuing opportunity to encounter the challenged incentive establish here.

**Authority:** Standing portion of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt the holding and its reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that unequal opportunity to compete is an injury even when the award remains uncertain. Northeastern Florida identifies the governmental barrier, rather than proof of the award that would follow its removal, as the relevant competitive injury. Adarand submitted the low bid, but the prime contractor selected a certified disadvantaged business in response to the financial incentive. The contractor could decline that incentive; its optional character does not remove its effect on competitive opportunity. Lujan requires the connection to be concrete and the requested relief appropriate to a continuing or threatened injury. Adarand's recurring bids and the likelihood of encountering the challenged clause again satisfy that prospective requirement on this record. The Court does not infer a guaranteed future award, an entitlement to damages, or a waiver of sovereign immunity. Nor does accepting this competitive injury establish that the challenged preference is unconstitutional; justification remains a separate question.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville (June 14, 1993): applies its equal-opportunity injury rule; no guaranteed contract award is required.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife (June 12, 1992): retains concrete injury, causal connection and stage-appropriate proof; prospective relief requires sufficiently imminent renewed exposure.

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Controlling proposition:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a common equality obligation requires substantial justification for racial treatment by federal, state and local government. The incentive burdens an opportunity to compete even though the prime may decline it; that burden requires justification, not automatic invalidation. Corrective purpose matters because overcoming supported exclusion differs from maintaining subordination, but purpose alone cannot establish validity. Evidence must connect credit, bonding, subcontracting relationships or performance opportunities to the relevant market, institution, program or lawful legislative category. General small-business difficulties do not justify every racial category; neither may courts assume that differences in experience are unaffected by documented exclusion.

Croson supplies the demand for relevant evidence; Wygant supplies attention to actual burdens; Paradise supplies flexibility and fit; Fullilove recognizes supported congressional remedial action without furnishing a universal rationale from its fractured opinions. These principles require examination of purpose, operation and burden rather than a classification's benign label. Metro's lower federal tier and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation obscure that inquiry and are displaced to the extent specified below. A uniform obligation does not require ignoring relevant differences between subordination and supported correction.

The objection that common review either erases Congress's role or weakens individual protection fails. Congress may justify a national remedial category through authority and evidence unavailable to a municipality's independently designed measure. An agency need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for each project, but must remain within its delegation and justify materially different or unsupported implementation. Courts retain responsibility for constitutional sufficiency. Conversely, the affected competitor's actual burden must be justified even when correction is important. Supported barriers may warrant action before discrimination against every beneficiary has been adjudicated. This decision supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures, no constitutional duty to adopt preferences, and no power over every private inequality. It leaves the arrangement's validity for application of the standard on remand.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.

**Controlling proposition:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

### FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp., No. 98-1152

Merits decision, March 21, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 1, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 153 F.3d 155, decided August 14, 1998.

The question is whether the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act authorizes the FDA's asserted drug-and-device regulation of cigarettes and smokeless tobacco as customarily marketed. The agency classified nicotine as a drug and its delivery components as devices, treated the products as combinations, and issued its August 28, 1996 youth-sale, distribution and advertising rule under device authorities. It relied on pharmacological effects, consumer use, manufacturer knowledge and deliberate nicotine-delivery design. The regulated parties did not dispute the agency's factual findings for this appeal; they disputed their statutory consequences.

The FDA acknowledged serious health risks but maintained that abrupt removal could cause greater harm to addicted adults through withdrawal and unsafe substitutes, while youth restrictions could prevent addiction. The courts below disagreed about whether that approach could fit the statute. No new epidemiological finding is made here.

The District Court sustained general authority and device treatment but rejected advertising authority, staying most restrictions while leaving age verification and the under-eighteen sales prohibition in place. Certified interlocutory and injunction appeals brought the case to the Fourth Circuit. That court invalidated the entire August 28 rule for lack of statutory authority and vacated the District Court's separate advertising construction without approving or disapproving it.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5-4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would reverse the categorical no-authority judgment and remand for review of the actual regulatory methods and remaining objections.

The Court leaves the rule invalid. Any further proceedings are confined to administrative or severability matters genuinely necessary to implement that judgment; no replacement regulation is ordered. The judgment creates no separate advertising-authority holding, decides no First Amendment claim, and orders no tobacco ban, recall, damages or retrospective liability. Authority over products marketed with therapeutic claims remains outside this decision.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Those five Justices adopt both holdings and all their qualifications. Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The dissent would reverse the categorical no-authority judgment and remand, distinguishing statutory coverage from lawful exercise without exempting the agency from otherwise applicable commands.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The 1997 Act preserves the authority question and specifies the earlier tobacco regime

**Holding and operative rule:** Public Law 105-115, section 422, leaves the question of tobacco authority unchanged and requires any such authority to be exercised under the FDCA as it stood on November 20, 1997. The Act's new mission and regulatory procedures therefore supply neither a new tobacco delegation nor a means to avoid a restriction of that earlier regime.

**Controlling explanation:** The two commands perform different work. Reserving whether authority exists favors neither party's construction; specifying the earlier Act identifies the rules under which any authority operates. Section 501's general effective date, ninety days after November 21, 1997, is subject to other express provisions and excludes sections 111, 121, 125 and 307. That general provision does not override section 422.

Section 406's new mission is not part of the earlier tobacco regime. Nor may the later classification-default, de novo, postmarket, least-burdensome or added clinical-evidence procedures be used here. The earlier provisions retain their own qualified scientific-evidence and reclassification routes; excluding later additions does not erase that existing flexibility or establish a new numerical minimum of clinical studies. The authority question must be decided under the actual statutory system, not by importing only the later provisions favorable to one side.

##### The asserted customary-tobacco regime exceeds the delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Read with its operative safety and marketing requirements and Congress's distinct tobacco enactments, the FDCA does not authorize the asserted drug/device regime for tobacco as customarily marketed. The FDA cannot obtain that power by construing broad coverage terms to include a continuing-market accommodation that the connected statutory requirements do not permit; the challenged rule is invalid on that ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court assumes for this inquiry the force of the agency's intended-use evidence. Section 321's structure-and-function category is not confined to therapeutic advertising, and nicotine's chemical action does not itself answer the classification of a delivery component. But those definitions operate within the statute's duties, not apart from them.

Section 353(g)'s assignment of primary responsibility by a combination's mode of action does not exempt it from otherwise applicable drug obligations. Misbranding, applicable new-drug approval, device safety and conditional cessation duties remain independent of section 360j(e)'s restriction power. Graduated controls permit real choices; they do not confer a general power to dispense with those duties. The agency's accepted danger findings and its proposal to retain customary tobacco through restrictions expose the conflict. A preference for the least harmful available policy supplies no universal exception to the enacted commands.

Congress's cigarette-labeling, advertising, smokeless-tobacco, reporting and youth-access enactments reinforce this reading at their actual scopes. They are not individually express exemptions from all health law. Their combined treatment of customary tobacco, read with the FDCA's operating requirements, does not support the asserted regime. Failed bills, agency disclaimers and congressional silence alone would not decide the question.

The dissent would separate coverage from correction of an unlawful method. Here the claimed customary-tobacco authority itself presupposes an accommodation the connected scheme does not supply. MCI requires an operative statutory obligation to retain its essential force, while Haggar and Chevron require a genuine delegation before deference. Sweet Home and Iowa Utilities Board likewise distinguish broad delegated authority from independent limits. This conclusion turns on the particular enacted scheme, not economic magnitude or a universal major-questions test. Good Samaritan permits reasoned interpretive change within the statute; a changed view alone is not the defect.

##### Operative qualifications and questions left open

The following distinctions limit the statutory reasoning; they are not new enforcement findings:

- **Directions and warnings:** Section 352(f)(1) requires an exemption by regulation when adequate directions are unnecessary for public-health protection. That directions exemption does not waive section 352(f)(2)'s separate necessary warnings concerning pathological conditions, childhood use, unsafe dosage, method or duration. Section 352(j) concerns danger under labeled dosage, manner, frequency or duration; existing tobacco warnings do not automatically establish FDCA compliance.
- **Classification and approval:** Device classes depend on their statutory control and information criteria, including the qualified low-risk route; risk alone does not compel an immediate judicial ban. The earlier scientific-evidence and reclassification procedures remain. No particular classification, new-drug application, grandfather status or approval is adjudicated. Section 321(p)'s pre-June 25, 1938 exception requires the drug's prior-Act status and unchanged labeling representations; the Court does not find those conditions satisfied or unsatisfied here.
- **Restricted devices:** Section 360j(e) permits practitioner-authorization or other sale, distribution or use conditions upon the required determination that harmful potential or necessary collateral measures prevent reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness otherwise. Training or facility limits require their own safe-use determination; lack of specified board eligibility or certification alone cannot exclude a user. Required label statements remain. None of those permissions is a general exemption from another command.
- **Ban proceedings:** Section 360f permits rulemaking on findings of substantial deception or unreasonable and substantial risk. When the Secretary has determined that labeling can correct the problem and provided written notice specifying the change and time allowed, failure to make that change is separately required. Immediate effect additionally requires unreasonable, direct and substantial danger and advance manufacturer notice, followed by prompt notice, an informal-hearing opportunity and expeditious affirmance, modification or revocation.
- **Cessation and recall:** Section 360h(e)(1) requires immediate cessation of distribution and notification and cessation-of-use instructions to health professionals and user facilities after a reasonable-probability finding that a human-use device would cause serious adverse health consequences or death. An informal-hearing opportunity must be provided within ten days of the order, and inadequate grounds require vacatur. Recall requires a further determination, timetable and progress reports; it cannot reach devices held by individuals, and the greater-risk exception concerns recall from user facilities. Those recall limits do not make the initial cessation command elective. Individual risk-notice duties remain. The Court makes none of the required enforcement determinations itself.
- **Other enactments and State law:** The cigarette statutes' labeling and advertising limits and the smokeless-tobacco statute's distinct package/advertisement rules retain their own scopes, including the latter's outdoor-billboard exception. Section 360k requires a relevant federal device requirement and a different or additional State duty in the specified subject relationship. Its exemption procedure requires an application, regulation, notice and oral-hearing opportunity; greater stringency is one route, compelling local conditions without violating federal requirements another. Medtronic therefore prevents treating coverage alone as universal displacement of State youth-access laws. No particular preemption or exemption is decided.

The Court decides no advertising merits, constitutional objection, therapeutic-claim product, biological-license question, particular new-drug refusal ground, international coordination requirement or later classification procedure. It does not pronounce every risky device unmarketable, require therapeutic claims for every drug, create a general prohibition on agency change, or require Congress to use particular words in every delegation. Congress may confer tobacco authority expressly.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applies statutory construction before deference; agency reasonableness cannot supply a delegation absent from the enacted scheme.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.:** Preserves the distinction between genuine delegated ambiguity, regulatory validity and application; formal rulemaking alone does not establish authority.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.:** Applies its requirement that a bounded agency power preserve an operative statutory obligation. It creates no universal magnitude-based exception to delegation.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon:** Preserves broad statutory coverage within actual delegated language and concrete limits; it does not authorize disregard of a connected command.
- **AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board:** Preserves separate analysis of general implementing power, particular statutory constraints and unresolved applications.
- **Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala:** Preserves considered interpretive change within the statute; the FDA's former position is not an automatic legal bar.
- **National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.:** Preserves recognition of the agency's actual discernible ground without supplying missing findings or a judicial replacement policy.
- **Adams Fruit Co. v. Barrett:** Preserves the requirement of an actual congressional delegation; an agency cannot create its own authority.
- **United States v. Bacto-Unidisk:** Its broad protective reading of the FDCA remains, subject to the entire enacted scheme rather than isolated definitions.
- **United States v. Rutherford:** Preserves statutory safety and effectiveness requirements against a judicially created exemption; it is not a present tobacco ban or an adjudication of an application.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Preserves the actual federal/state counterpart and subject conditions for device preemption; federal coverage does not automatically eliminate every State rule.
- **United States v. Locke:** Its particular national-vessel and local-waterway allocation remains distinct; overlap alone supplies no general preemption rule here.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 21, 2000, the FDA lacks the asserted FDCA authority for the August 28, 1996 drug/device regime governing tobacco as customarily marketed. The rule remains invalid. This is a tobacco-specific construction of the connected statutory scheme, not a universal major-questions doctrine, categorical bar on agency reinterpretation, or automatic preemption rule. Section 422 preserves the authority question and the earlier statutory version for exercise of any tobacco power. Therapeutic-claim products, particular advertising and constitutional questions, and independently authorized regulatory subjects remain outside the holding; no new ban or recall follows.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would reverse the categorical authority judgment and remand. The structure-and-function definition reaches nicotine deliberately supplied for pharmacological effects, and the delivery-component definition can reach the apparatus used to supply it. Therapeutic advertising is not the only evidence of intended use. The earlier regulatory architecture at least permits that construction within the delegation recognized by Chevron, Sweet Home and Haggar.

They accept that restricted-device authority cannot erase misbranding, applicable approval or cessation duties. Their sufficient disagreement is that an obligation to remove a covered product, if established under the statute's own conditions, limits the FDA's permissible methods rather than proving that the product is outside the protective statute. They do not make section 360h(e)(1) elective merely because other remedies exist, and do not adopt a universal exception for withdrawal or substitute risks. The precise provision must permit consideration of those consequences.

The tobacco-specific statutes address their actual labeling, advertising, reporting and access subjects; they do not establish the categorical FDCA exclusion necessary to sustain the judgment. MCI's insistence on mandatory limits does not erase different, broad coverage language. Section 422 reserves the authority question and requires the earlier regime, supplying neither new power nor a new exemption. No new mission, classification shortcut or later review flexibility is used.

Their remand would address the actual combination-product method, particular restrictions and unresolved constitutional objections under ordinary procedures. It would not unconditionally reinstate the full rule, guarantee a lawful continuing tobacco market, order a ban or recall, or supply new agency findings. The statutory constraints remain enforceable at their own scope.

### Grutter v. Bollinger, No. 02-241

Merits decision, June 23, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 1, 2003. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit. The Sixth Circuit reversed the District Court's invalidation of the Law School's admissions policy and sustained its consideration of race. The Court decides whether that policy violates equal protection, Title VI or 42 U.S.C. §1981 on the developed record of individual review, educational effects, actual outcomes, enrollment monitoring and alternatives.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The requested invalidation and individual relief resting on the resolved policy-violation theories are denied. The District Court's injunction, vacated by the Sixth Circuit, is not reinstated.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. KENNEDY, J., filed a dissenting opinion. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined as to Part I.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirm the Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining the policy | Parts I–IV in full |
| Dissent | Kennedy | Kennedy alone | Reverse and remand for policy and surviving individual relief | Actual-operation objection under the governing standard; statutory invalidity at that scope |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia as to Part I | Reverse and remand | Part I: broader nonremedial-preference objection and actual-mechanism objection; Part II: Thomas's distinct selective-prestige objection |

#### Holdings

##### I. The actual Law School program satisfies the common substantial-justification standard

**Holding and operative rule:** A public university may consider race in admissions when it establishes an important lawful educational objective, a substantial evidence-supported relationship between its actual racial treatment and that objective, justified coverage and competitive burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives comparably advancing the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. The Law School satisfies that standard on this record through its supported educational benefits, actual individual comparisons, meaningful nonracial considerations and genuine competition; academic expertise is evidence, not an exemption from the University's burden.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt the complete rule and application. Their six direct joins establish controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand requires proof of the actual objective, relationship and burdens. Hopwood already recognizes supported educational diversity as important and requires genuine individual competition. Bakke supplies the competitive-consideration boundary. The evidence supports educational interaction, reduced isolation and correction of stereotyped expectations. The policy examines academic and other individual contributions throughout the applicant pool. Actual variation and meaningful nonracial considerations support the conclusion that race informs a competitive assessment rather than fixes an entitlement to a place.

The opposing evidence deserves weight: substantial admissions differentials and attention to enrollment can reveal a racial allocation concealed by flexible language. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas find that objection decisive on their respective grounds. The Court assesses the full record, rather than treating comparisons limited to measured credentials as a complete description of applicants. Supported individual comparisons and varied outcomes answer the objection here. Neither monitoring enrollment nor the phrase critical mass by itself establishes genuine competition or a forbidden fixed share. Race does not determine an applicant's viewpoint, and the permission does not protect a hidden racial allocation.

The express racial classification triggers the inquiry without proof of exclusive hostility. Montiel preserves independent constitutional injuries and distinct statutory elements; its apportionment setting supplies no admissions safe harbor. Patrolmen's likewise requires the government's evidence-supported justification despite an operational benefit. The Court applies that same common rule independently while respecting supported trial findings. It approves the actual program because the evidence sustains its operation, rather than because individualized language or an institution's good faith shifts the burden to the challenger.

##### II. Alternatives and continued operation require continuing factual justification

**Holding and operative rule:** The University must answer concrete practicable alternatives that comparably advance its supported educational objectives with materially less racial disadvantage; it need not exhaust every conceivable method or abandon legitimate educational selection for a materially different objective. Permission to continue racial consideration lasts only while the actual educational relationship, competitive burdens and response to effective alternatives remain justified.

**Authority:** Part II; the same six-Justice coalition adopts this application and continuing condition.

**Controlling explanation:** Adarand supplies the alternatives requirement, and Hopwood identifies the supported educational objective against which feasibility and comparable effect must be judged. Outreach, socioeconomic consideration, different credential weighting, percentage approaches and lottery proposals must be assessed in that setting. The Law School's national pool and legitimate individual-selection aims matter to comparability; an undergraduate percentage plan is not automatically an equivalent substitute. The actual evidence supports the conclusion that the proposed alternatives would not comparably achieve the demonstrated educational interaction while preserving legitimate selection practices. Prestige for its own sake would not justify racial treatment. The duty is to respond to supported practical alternatives, not to perform a ritual exhaustion of every imagined plan. Continuing justification supplies an endpoint: when changed conditions, effective alternatives or actual burdens defeat that showing, the permission ends.

##### III. Title VI permits the justified competitive consideration established here

**Holding and operative rule:** Title VI §601's equality guarantee permits the genuinely competitive educational consideration established by this supported substantive justification. The presented intentional-treatment claim therefore fails, while §601's intent requirement and the statute's own coverage, proper-defendant and remedy predicates retain their distinct operation.

**Authority:** Part III; the same six Justices separately adopt this statutory construction and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The policy intentionally considers race. Hopwood prevents treating an educational purpose or good faith as absence of statutory intent. The independent statutory answer concerns whether that purposeful treatment denies the equality §601 protects. Bakke's competitive-consideration proposition, preserved and developed in Hopwood, permits genuinely competitive consideration within a substantively justified educational process rather than requiring a categorical prohibition of every racial consideration. The Law School's program satisfies that guarantee within the scope of the claim before the Court. The conclusion does not arise merely by renaming the constitutional result; it rests on the statute's equality protection and the program's actual competitive operation. Sandoval preserves §601's intentional-treatment requirement.

##### IV. Section 1981 protects equal educational-contract opportunity under the justified-competition construction

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1981 permits a substantively justified educational consideration of race that preserves genuine competition and neither imposes racial ineligibility nor assigns a separate racial entitlement to the educational contract. The actual Law School program satisfies that same-right guarantee, so the presented §1981 policy challenge fails independently of the constitutional and Title VI dispositions.

**Authority:** Part IV; the same six Justices adopt this independent statutory construction and application.

**Controlling explanation:** Runyon establishes educational-contract coverage; McDonald protects applicants of every race; General Building Contractors supplies the relationship between purposeful discrimination and the constitutional equality guarantee. That relationship alone does not make every constitutionally permitted classification lawful under every statute. The Court construes §1981's same-right protection here as equal competitive access to educational contracting, informed by the preserved Bakke–Hopwood competition principle. The evidence establishes genuine competition within a substantively justified process, rather than racial exclusion or a separate contractual entitlement.

The contrary textual argument treats every purposeful racial preference as necessarily denying the same contract right. Scalia and Thomas accept that broader view; Kennedy finds the actual operation inadequate even under the justified-competition construction. The Court rejects the categorical reading in this setting because the supported competitive process supplies the equality the statute protects. Academic freedom does not excuse a statutory exclusion: Runyon's rejection of an associational defense to exclusion remains intact. Section 1983 supplies an authorized enforcement route, not a different admissions standard; source-specific defendants, causes and relief remain distinct. The requested relief resting on these resolved policy-violation theories is therefore denied.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, June 12, 1995: applies the unchanged common substantial-justification standard and government-borne evidentiary burden.
- Hopwood v. Texas, November 25, 1996: applies the already established educational-diversity permission, genuine-competition requirement, intentional-treatment rule and distinction between process injury and lost admission.
- Regents of the University of California v. Bakke, 438 U.S. 265: applies the preserved competitive-consideration boundary.
- City of New York v. Patrolmen's Benevolent Association, April 23, 2003: applies actual objective, relationship, burdens and alternatives despite a useful operational purpose.
- Montiel v. Davis, January 13, 2003: preserves distinct constitutional and statutory inquiries without replacing the admissions-classification standard.
- Runyon v. McCrary, 427 U.S. 160: applies educational-contract coverage and preserves rejection of an associational defense to racial exclusion.
- McDonald v. Santa Fe Trail Transportation Co., 427 U.S. 273: applies §1981's protection of applicants of every race.
- General Building Contractors Association v. Pennsylvania, 458 U.S. 375: applies the intentional-discrimination relationship without making all constitutional permissions statutory defenses.
- Alexander v. Sandoval, April 24, 2001: preserves §601's intentional-treatment protection.
- Texas v. Lesage, November 29, 1999: preserves the distinction between competitive-process injury and consequential admission relief.

#### Law After Decision

The existing common standard permits the Law School's supported program of genuine competitive consideration. Actual educational function, burdens and practical alternatives govern both present permission and its continuation; individualized language or academic expertise supplies no exemption. Title VI and §1981 independently permit the justified competitive treatment established here at the stated statutory scope. The factual basis must continue to support operation, and changed circumstances are assessed under the same rule.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting.** Kennedy accepts the existing availability of a supported educational-diversity objective but finds inadequate proof that the actual racial weight and numerical management preserve genuinely individual competition throughout selection. Hopwood's individual-comparison requirement and Patrolmen's evidence-supported fit rule demand more than academic assurance or flexible language. Statistics alone need not establish a quota, yet the whole operational record in his assessment fails the common justification standard. He would reverse on that narrower actual-operation ground and sustain the intentional-treatment statutory challenges at the same scope, without deciding a categorical prohibition of all supported educational consideration. On remand, policy relief and individual claims would remain distinct; Hopwood and Lesage would place the preponderance same-decision burden on the school, with claimants establishing actual loss under an available cause against a proper defendant.

**Thomas, dissenting, with Scalia joining Part I.** Part I maintains the broader personal-equality objection to nonremedial ancestry preferences, together with the conclusion that this mechanism fails genuine competitive treatment. Their earlier Adarand and Hopwood separate positions supply that objection to the governing permission; they do not treat the separate position as existing Court law. They also read §1981's same-right guarantee to prohibit the purposeful preference and would sustain the Title VI challenge. Their proposed reversal would require fitted policy relief and separate adjudication of individual causation and remedies, rather than an automatic admission or monetary award.

In Part II, Thomas separately argues that preserving selective prestige does not create a constitutionally sufficient need for unequal treatment. An institution's chosen level of selectivity cannot itself justify an ancestry preference. His supported violation-linked remedial-duty position in Hopwood remains distinct from this nonremedial policy. Both dissenters oppose the present approval; their writing supplies no assent to the Court's continuing-permission rule.
