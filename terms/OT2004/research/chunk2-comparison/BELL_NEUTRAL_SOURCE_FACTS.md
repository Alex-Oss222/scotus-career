# Bell v. Cone — supplemental neutral source facts

Scope: objective lower-record facts and a date-eligible statutory alternative for the January 24, 2005 aggravator-return matter. These facts supplement the existing neutral packet. They do not decide the legal sufficiency of narrowing, federal presentation, actual merits adjudication, procedural default, or constitutional correction.

## 1. The parties' state appellate submissions addressed narrowing authorities

The reported record identifies Cone's state appellate brief, page 20, as relying on State v. Pritchett, 621 S. W. 2d 127 (Tenn. 1981), in arguing that the State had not established the victims' suffering. The State's responsive brief, page 34, relied on Pritchett and State v. Melson, 638 S. W. 2d 342 (Tenn. 1982). These are state decisions that articulated or applied the narrowed aggravator. This establishes the cited submissions' attention to those state authorities and victim suffering. It does not by itself establish that a separate federal vagueness claim was expressly presented or adjudicated.

Source locator: the quotations and document references at 543 U.S. 457 n.7; underlying documents identified there as Brief for Appellant and Brief for Appellee in No. 02C019403CR00052, pages 20 and 34. The complete official reporter text is saved in `research/chunk2-comparison/bell_official.txt`; the corresponding primary text is in `research/chunk2-comparison/bell_full.txt`. The original appellate briefs were not separately recovered for this supplement. The reported recitations support only the facts stated above under Engine section 14.

## 2. A separate aggravator was rejected and received an express harmlessness ruling

The Tennessee Supreme Court rejected the jury's risk-to-two-or-more-other-persons aggravator because the threat Cone posed to others earlier in the day was insufficiently close in time to the murders. The court characterized that particular error as harmless beyond a reasonable doubt based on the strength of the other aggravating circumstances. This was an express ruling about the rejected risk-to-others aggravator; it was not an express harmlessness ruling about a separately established defect in the heinousness aggravator. Its adequacy and relationship to any distinct weighing error require their own legal analysis.

Source locator: State v. Cone, 665 S. W. 2d 87, 95 (Tenn. 1984), as reproduced at 543 U.S. 449 n.2. Sources saved as above. The characterization is the state court's finding and legal conclusion, not a new factual or constitutional finding here.

## 3. The state collateral court and federal district court described the procedural treatment differently

Cone's second amended state postconviction petition asserted 52 constitutional grounds, including the contention that the heinousness aggravator was unconstitutionally vague under the Eighth Amendment. The December 16, 1993 state trial-court ruling applied Tennessee Code section 40-30-111, covering waived or previously determined grounds. It specifically characterized the vagueness challenge, together with other claims, as a restatement of grounds previously determined and denied on direct appeal or the first postconviction petition. The Tennessee Court of Criminal Appeals affirmed denial, 927 S. W. 2d 579, 582 (1995), and the Tennessee Supreme Court denied permission to appeal. The federal district court subsequently treated the vagueness claim as procedurally barred for failure to raise it on direct appeal.

Source locators: 543 U.S. 450–451, quoting Cone v. State, No. P-06874 (Tenn. Crim. Ct., Dec. 16, 1993), page 6, and identifying the later appellate and district dispositions. These recitations establish the procedural accounts, not a final resolution of which claim was actually decided on its merits, when that occurred, or whether an asserted default supplies a valid federal bar.

## 4. Statutory alternative available at the event date

The operative 28 U.S.C. section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of a habeas application on its merits notwithstanding a failure to exhaust available state remedies. Accordingly, unresolved exhaustion need not invariably prevent a merits denial. The statute does not establish that denial is warranted, prove that a state merits adjudication occurred, resolve every procedural-default question, or eliminate any applicable substantive or section 2254(d) requirement. The modeled paths should assess this alternative on its actual terms without presuming that it necessarily controls the disposition.

Source locator: 28 U.S.C. section 2254(b)(2), in the version governing the July 1, 1997 petition; the statutory provision is reproduced at 543 U.S. 451 n.3. No later law is imported.
