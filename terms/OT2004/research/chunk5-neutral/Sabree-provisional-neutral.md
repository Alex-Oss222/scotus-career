Four components are kept distinct:

**A. Covered individual assistance.** Whether section 1396a(a)(10), with the selected covered category and the operative payment definition in section 1396d(a), gives these eligible persons a sufficiently definite individual right. Paragraph (a)(15) of section 1396d defines the intermediate-care category; it is not a freestanding command to provide every conceivable disability service.

**B. Reasonable promptness.** Whether section 1396a(a)(8)'s timing command gives an eligible beneficiary an enforceable right concerning that assistance, as distinct from a claim to efficient administration generally. Application access is another statutory command, but no separate application-refusal claim is presented.

**C. Section 1983 foreclosure.** If those rights exist, whether section 1396c funding enforcement and section 1396a(a)(3) fair hearings establish congressional displacement of this express remedial channel. This is the component directly awaiting the March 22 law.

**D. Disposition and remedy.** Whether the appellate reversal of dismissal should stand, and which issues return for adjudication. The enforceability judgment does not itself establish an unreasonable delay, a particular placement entitlement, damages, a classwide violation, or a decree.

## Common legal and factual premises

Blessing, April 21, 1997, unanimously requires a particular intended beneficiary, a binding duty, and an individual entitlement sufficiently definite for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone is inadequate. Qualitative terms are not categorically unmanageable. State-plan location alone does not defeat a right; sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 also do not create one. A proven right is presumptively enforceable under section 1983, subject to express exclusion or a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial arrangement. Defendant, violation, causation and relief remain separate inquiries.

Gonzaga, June 20, 2002, supplies a six-Justice FERPA-specific no-right result. The four-Justice lead opinion has no controlling new general rights formula. Neither that opinion nor the Breyer-Souter concurrence categorically excludes funding statutes, makes an independently implied action necessary, or automatically displaces section 1983 through agency oversight. Bruneau, June 3, 1999, likewise distinguishes an established personal statutory right from its enforcement channel. No historical version of these cases replaces their supplied public law.

The present statutory commands identify medical assistance, eligible persons, and prompt performance. The definition then supplies a specified category of covered costs. Pennsylvania's adoption of the intermediate-care benefit and these plaintiffs' eligibility are accepted at this stage. The category is optional at the plan-selection level; this analysis does not require every State to adopt it or suspend its conditions. Applicable conditions include a qualifying facility other than an institution for mental diseases, the need determination connected to section 1396a(a)(31), a written plan before admission or authorization of benefits, periodic independent review, and section 1396d(d)'s facility and active-treatment requirements. Accepted eligibility is not reopened by inventing a new factual contest about those conditions.

Medical assistance at the relevant time means payment of part or all of covered costs. Recognition of a financing entitlement does not settle whether, on an actual violation record, effective prospective compliance requires an arrangement beyond merely announcing that payment is available. Conversely, the practical need for care does not itself authorize an order creating beds, choosing a facility, or directing unlimited home services. The Court can answer the pleaded enforceability questions fully and leave the distinct reserved remedy issue to the District Court.

The strongest common adverse path is to characterize the cited provisions as directions for a federally approved financing plan, with undefined timing and specialized clinical conditions that leave only a federal-state compliance obligation. A second adverse path accepts personal rights but treats the fair hearing and federal funding mechanisms together as an intentionally exclusive remedial system. Both require close statutory analysis; neither follows merely from the Spending Clause or from the absence of an additional implied action. A narrower adverse conclusion concerning an asserted right to immediate physical placement would not itself justify dismissing the actual assistance claim.

## Stevens

**Own grounds.** Stevens authored and all eight colleagues joined the supplied Blessing opinion, particularly its individual-right, oversight, state-plan and fitted-remedy holdings. He joined the majorities in Wright, 479 U.S. at 423–432, and Wilder, 496 U.S. at 509–523. He joined the supplied Gonzaga dissent identifying a personal statutory entitlement despite an institutional enforcement setting, while retaining its actual statutory conditions.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the covered assistance right.** The eligible person and the covered cost category are defined in the statute, and the State must make the adopted assistance available. This is closer to Wilder's actual reimbursement command than to Blessing's systemwide administrative targets. The contrary path emphasizes that the claimants seek care from institutions rather than reimbursement already accrued and that paragraph (a)(15) is only a definition. Stevens would answer that the operative right comes from paragraph (a)(10) read with the definition, not from the definition alone; the remaining payment-versus-service question limits relief rather than erasing the financing right. He would not join a universal entitlement to every optional Medicaid service or an unqualified right to institutional placement.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize the timing right.** The promptness obligation runs to eligible individuals and the assistance just identified. Wright and Wilder establish that a reasonable standard can be judicially manageable without being reduced to an automatic number. The strongest contrary argument is that only program restructuring could shorten these waits. Blessing's separation between a right and the means of remedy answers it: evidence of an administrative defect can bear on a proved breach without making administrative adequacy itself the protected right. No universal deadline or automatic violation from a waiting-list entry is adopted.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure under existing law, subject to Rancho refresh.** His Wright and Wilder joins and Blessing opinion reject the inference that funding withdrawal alone is exclusive. The fair hearing is an actual individual process and must be considered, but neither it nor the federal sanction identifies a congressional decision to substitute a specially bounded judicial action for section 1983. The contrary path would need an incompatibility shown in this particular remedial structure, not a count of available administrative steps. He would join a reasoned distinction based on that structure and withhold assent from a rule that administrative remedies can never displace section 1983.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm and return for ordinary further proceedings.** His Blessing mandate requires identification of breach, responsible official, causation, affected persons and fitted relief. The strongest argument for resolving placement now is that a purely nominal right could leave years of delay untouched. A legally sufficient remedy for a proved violation must be effective, but this enforceability appeal lacks an adjudicated basis to select one. He would reject both immediate institutional placement ordered here and a remand that treats a paper promise as necessarily sufficient compliance. No automatic classwide decree follows.

## O'Connor

**Own grounds.** O'Connor wrote the Wright dissent, 479 U.S. at 432–441, and joined the Wilder dissent, 496 U.S. at 524–529. Those writings require Congress's particular right to be identified before an administrative regulation or remedial preference supplies content. She joined the supplied unanimous Blessing, the provision-specific Suter result, and Thomas's institution-focused Gonzaga lead opinion.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the narrowly stated statutory assistance right.** Her Wright objection was that the utility claim had not been supplied by Congress and that HUD's allowances did not promise the individual amount sought. Here Congress itself directs assistance to eligible persons and defines covered costs; the accepted coverage and eligibility premises identify the claimant and benefit. Her Wilder dissent's objection to converting a state finding-and-assurance process into a substantive judicial rate guarantee is not reproduced by this direct beneficiary command. The adverse path retains force if plaintiffs insist that financing language itself grants every requested service arrangement. She would separate and leave that question, not dismiss the narrower right. Joinder requires retaining the payment definition and the adopted-benefit and eligibility limits.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize reasonable promptness in furnishing that assistance.** The qualifier concerns when a specified personal benefit must be furnished, rather than a generalized command for reasonable administrative efforts. The strongest objection is that determining reasonable delay in a scarce, clinically constrained system invites judicial policy setting. The right can be adjudicated by the circumstances of a specific delay, with those conditions examined in the merits and remedy proceedings; the presence of factual judgment does not erase a direct timing obligation. She would not join an opinion turning the word reasonable into either a fixed judicial deadline or a categorical fiscal excuse.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure under the current supplied rule, subject to Rancho refresh.** Her Blessing join adopts the distinction between federal oversight and an incompatible substitute remedy, and her Gonzaga join is a no-right analysis rather than an automatic administrative-preclusion holding. The strongest State argument is the combination of beneficiary hearings with federal enforcement, not the latter alone. She would require an actual explanation of why a section 1983 claim would defeat Congress's remedial design. On the supplied Medicaid provisions that conflict is not established. Her join would stop short of holding every federal-state program's hearing machinery nonexclusive regardless of structure.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm the enforceability judgment with explicit remand limits.** Restoring the suit does not adjudicate an actual payment shortfall, medical need beyond the accepted stage, class certification terms, or a mandate to build facilities. Her Wright and Wilder objections counsel particular care that the court enforces the congressional right actually found. Against the claimants' request for an immediately effective service order, she would require a proved violation and a lawful compliance choice first; against wholesale dismissal, she would preserve the individual financing and timing claims. Proper prospective officer relief remains available when its predicates are established; retrospective treasury relief is not awarded.

## Scalia

**Own grounds.** Scalia joined O'Connor's Wright dissent and the Wilder dissent, and wrote the supplied Blessing concurrence joined by Kennedy while joining the Court in full. That concurrence reserves the original-meaning funding-contract issue; it does not create a present categorical bar. He joined Thomas's supplied Gonzaga lead opinion and the supplied Suter provision-specific disposition.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the actual statutory assistance right under governing precedent.** Congress's commands identify the individual eligible beneficiary and the benefit through the payment and intermediate-care definitions. Unlike the asserted regulatory utility entitlement challenged in Wright or the disputed substantive rate construction in Wilder, the claimed narrow obligation is not generated by an administrative policy preference. The strongest contrary route is his reserved argument that a beneficiary of a federal-state funding agreement lacks a right secured by law within the original scope of section 1983. His Blessing full join and explicit reservation do not decide that foundational question adversely to claimants here. He would preserve it rather than convert it into a new ground of dismissal without the required justification for displacing governing law. Joinder excludes a categorical constitutional endorsement of every spending-program beneficiary suit.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize the textual promptness right.** The enacted timing term modifies furnishing assistance to eligible individuals. The adverse analogy to Suter's reasonable-efforts obligation fails at the identified entitlement: the requested object here is a defined covered benefit, not a program's unbounded efforts to achieve a social result. The strongest manageability objection concerns the many possible reasons for delay. Those matter to breach and remedy; they do not make a personal statutory timing command disappear. He would join a right stated at that level and refuse a judicially invented across-the-board waiting period or an unconditional services entitlement.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure on the presently supplied statutory structure, pending Rancho.** Blessing's full join supplies the governing oversight distinction, and the Wilder dissent accepted the separate section 1983 remedial inquiry once a congressional right exists. A congressionally limited individual judicial route could support a different result; that is why Rancho's actual law must be applied rather than assumed. Medicaid's funding sanction and fair hearing presently show no comparable conflict. The State's strongest response is that combining remedies should count even if neither alone is exclusive. He would assess the combination, but require textual and structural evidence of displacement. No free-standing presumption that all administrative mechanisms are cumulative is adopted.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm without selecting the ultimate remedy.** The District Court must enforce the dated congressional obligation, not later amendments or a freestanding equitable ideal. His own Wright/Wilder joins support the strongest objection to converting a payment definition into compulsory state provision of any desired service. That objection warrants preserving the reserved issue, not extinguishing the pleaded financing right. No damages award, facility order, class decree or new original-meaning holding accompanies the affirmance. Any reservation in a separate writing must leave his actual joins and the mandate intact.

## Kennedy

**Own grounds.** Kennedy joined the Wilder dissent, the supplied unanimous Blessing and Scalia's separate reservation, the supplied Suter result, and Thomas's supplied Gonzaga lead. He did not sit in Wright and receives no attributed join in that case.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the narrow assistance right.** The Wilder dissent he joined insists on locating Congress's right rather than treating judicial desirability as proof of it. The personal eligibility and covered-assistance commands here provide that location. The contrary institutional reading draws support from federal-state plan administration and specialized conditions, but cannot rest on the plan heading alone after Blessing and sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10. His Gonzaga position concerned a policy-or-practice funding condition; it does not foreclose a statute that commands a defined benefit for eligible persons. Joinder requires an adopted covered benefit and actual statutory eligibility, not a general right to disability services.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize the beneficiary-specific timing right.** This right concerns performance of the obligation identified in A. The serious opposing path is to see reasonable promptness as a broad program standard like Suter's disputed reasonable efforts. The text's direct connection to furnishing assistance to each eligible person, with an identified benefit, supplies the missing boundary. He would leave individual clinical circumstances and compliance options open and refuse a universal timetable or a holding that every prolonged wait has already been adjudicated unlawful.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure under current supplied law, pending exact Rancho.** His Blessing join accepts presumptive section 1983 enforcement after a personal right is found and rejects foreclosure through general oversight alone. His Wilder dissent does not demand an additional private-remedy-intent showing. The fair-hearing provision makes the State's structural argument stronger than one based only on funding withdrawal, but no express exclusive court route or incompatible limit has yet been identified. The alternative would prevail if a concrete statutory design made section 1983 an evasion of Congress's chosen channel. He would not announce a rule resolving that hypothetical by label.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm for further adjudication.** His reservation concerning federal-state contract beneficiaries remains a reserved question, not an extra procedural hurdle for these litigants. The proper limit is to the right and remedy actually before the Court. He would reject a facility-management decree without findings, and would also reject dismissal merely because the most expansive proposed injunction is unavailable. A future order must be prospective, directed to a responsible official, effective for the proved violation, and confined to the actual plaintiffs or certified class. No retrospective treasury payment is decided.

## Souter

**Own grounds.** Souter joined the supplied Suter no-right result for the particular reasonable-efforts provision, the unanimous Blessing, and Breyer's supplied Gonzaga concurrence. The latter evaluates practical uncertainty and institutional structure without adopting a new requirement of independently intended private remedies. He has no Wright or Wilder join; those are governing authorities, not his personal writings.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the covered assistance right.** The practical inquiry from his Gonzaga concurrence asks whether the statute identifies an administrable personal entitlement, rather than whether it contains any provision helping individuals. Accepted eligibility, an adopted benefit and a cost-payment definition give the present claim a definite object. The strongest contrary path combines technical eligibility and treatment requirements with the state-plan setting to argue that individual entitlement remains uncertain. Those conditions define the entitlement; unlike FERPA's broadly uncertain record coverage as analyzed in his concurrence, they do not leave the asserted benefit unspecified. He would not join a claim to every therapeutic service outside those boundaries.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize the reasonable-promptness right.** His Blessing join rejects treating all qualitative terms as unmanageable, while his Suter join demands more than an open-ended program-efforts obligation. A timing duty tied to a defined covered benefit meets that distinction. The State's best objection is that financial and clinical constraints could yield many reasonable outcomes. That is a reason to adjudicate the actual delay with its circumstances, not to hold no judicially cognizable timing obligation can exist. He would decline either an unconditional waiting-list rule or an indefinite administrative-discretion exception.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure under current law, Rancho pending.** His Gonzaga concurrence treats administrative consistency as relevant to construing the particular right, not as automatic extinction after the right is established. His Blessing join supplies the independent incompatibility test. The strongest adverse path is that state fair hearings and federal supervision work together to settle precisely these assistance claims. A hearing opportunity can address a claim without being Congress's exclusive replacement for section 1983; a contrary result needs the actual conflict that is currently missing. He would reserve what a materially different express judicial scheme would do until applying Rancho's text and holding.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm with remedy left for proof.** The accepted eligibility and years of waiting establish the context of the enforceability dispute, not every factual predicate of a judicial order. His Blessing join supports a concrete benefit claim surviving a broad prayer for relief without licensing general supervision. Against the concern that deferring remedy leaves the claim empty, he would require the District Court ultimately to identify a legally effective compliance path if a violation is proved. The present judgment does not choose payment, placement, provider arrangements, a class definition or a timetable.

## Thomas

**Own grounds.** Thomas authored the supplied Gonzaga lead opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; authored the supplied Bruneau opinion recognizing the distinction between a statutory right and incompatible displacement; and joined the unanimous Blessing and the supplied Suter result. He did not participate in Wright or Wilder and is not assigned their dissenting positions by ideological inference.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the particular assistance right.** His Gonzaga analysis rejects inferring a personal entitlement from institution-directed funding policy merely because individuals benefit. Here the command expressly reaches eligible individuals and incorporates a defined covered benefit. His Blessing join also prevents substituting the state-plan heading for the provision-specific inquiry. The strongest contrary reading emphasizes that the State's plan is the grammatical subject and the program is financed through grants. The beneficiary and performance clauses nevertheless specify who receives what; the general subject does not erase the command's direct personal object. His join would be confined to assistance as the dated statute defines it and would reject a right resting only on the definition or the general desirability of care.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize reasonable promptness in that assistance.** The timing requirement is enacted in the same individual command rather than inferred from efficient administration. The strongest contrary route is that no numerical deadline or service-delivery machinery supplies a manageable rule. Blessing does not demand a numerical term, and the benefit identified in A makes delay judicially assessable without asserting a programwide adequacy right. Thomas would withhold assent from regulatory or equitable expansion of this congressional right and from any predetermined finding that the allegations prove breach.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure under the existing supplied law, subject to Rancho.** His Bruneau and Blessing positions require actual displacement after a statutory right exists; his Gonzaga lead expressly does not establish automatic administrative foreclosure. The State can properly rely on its fair-hearing mechanism as part of the statutory structure, but the supplied provisions do not establish an express exclusion or an incompatible tailored federal judicial remedy. The strongest contrary argument is that section 1983 would undermine the intended primacy of agency enforcement. Primacy is not itself exclusivity. His join requires that the opinion identify the statutory absence of conflict, not infer an additional action from a remedial policy preference or assert that every funding program is privately enforceable.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm the reversal of dismissal, with the operative definition controlling further proceedings.** This follows his insistence on the congressional entitlement and his Blessing join's separate remedial predicates. The strongest claimant argument for immediate placement is that assistance without actual access to care is ineffective; it does not answer the reserved legal and factual questions about what this payment-based statute requires on this record. He would leave those questions to a developed case rather than issue an advisory negative ruling against every effective arrangement. No later services-or-payment wording, assumed state default, facility command, classwide breach or damages determination enters his join.

## Ginsburg

**Own grounds.** Ginsburg joined the supplied unanimous Blessing, the supplied Gonzaga dissent's personal-right analysis with its statutory qualifications, and the statutory section 1983 portion of Bruneau. She has no attributed Wright or Wilder vote; those decisions supply governing examples and the doctrinal rule she joined in Blessing.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the beneficiary's covered assistance right.** Her Gonzaga dissent join treats concrete persons, subject matter and conditions as a personal right even though a funding statute administers it institutionally. This statute is more direct: an eligible individual must receive an adopted covered benefit, with the definition specifying the payment obligation's object. The strongest State argument is that plaintiffs seek institutional services beyond a financing right. The proper answer is to identify the actual statutory assistance right and preserve the separate remedy question. Her join would retain all applicable coverage, eligibility and facility qualifications, not treat beneficiary status alone as sufficient.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize the timing right.** An eligible person's benefit need not wait indefinitely merely because federal oversight operates in aggregate. Blessing distinguishes aggregate compliance from personal entitlement, and her Gonzaga position permits manageable individualized enforcement with real statutory qualifications. The strongest contrary argument is that evaluating delay requires a court to supervise allocation choices. Those choices may matter in determining the violation and relief, but reasonable promptness has the specified individual's assistance as its object. She would not join a categorical finding that every wait is unlawful or that any budget limitation necessarily defeats a claim.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure under existing law, subject to Rancho.** Her Gonzaga dissent join separately rejects making central correction and funding sanctions an incompatible substitute without more; Blessing supplies the controlling rule. The fair hearing must be analyzed on its actual terms, rather than ignored as merely administrative. The State's best case is that it affords the particular beneficiary a route to correction. That availability does not establish that Congress withdrew section 1983 or designed incompatible judicial limits. She would join a provision-specific conclusion, not a broad claim that an administrative remedy can never be exclusive.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm and preserve effective prospective adjudication.** Her Blessing join bars automatic structural relief while preserving concrete claims embedded in a broader prayer. The strongest concern is that repeated procedural remands could leave a recognized right practically unenforced. The proper mandate directs further proceedings on the actual violation and a lawful, effective remedy if proved; it cannot decide the precise remedy without the necessary findings. Class scope, need and payment details, causation and compliance options remain open. Neither immediate institutional placement nor a ruling that nominal payment availability always suffices follows.

## Breyer

**Own grounds.** Breyer authored the supplied Gonzaga concurrence, joined by Souter, joined the unanimous Blessing, and joined Bruneau's statutory enforcement holding. His Gonzaga position refuses to turn uncertainty about one statute into a new general formula equating rights with a separately intended private remedy. Wright and Wilder are governing examples, not decisions in which he voted.

**A — provisional commitment: recognize the covered assistance right.** His Gonzaga concurrence's practical concern was identifying the content and limits of the asserted privacy obligation within a broad institutional scheme. Here the adopted benefit, accepted eligibility and dated cost-payment definition identify a much more concrete entitlement. The adverse path points to medical and institutional complexity. Complexity of applying specific conditions is not equivalent to absence of an entitlement; the conditions provide boundaries for later findings. He would refuse a generalized right to satisfactory disability care or an unsupported promise that the government itself must furnish every service.

**B — provisional commitment: recognize reasonable promptness as a manageable individual standard.** His Blessing join allows qualitative language when its object is sufficiently defined. The strongest objection is the difficulty of separating unreasonable administrative delay from clinical and capacity limits. That separation belongs in adjudicating breach and constructing relief; it does not justify making the timing command categorically unenforceable. He would insist that the opinion leave those fact-sensitive questions open and not announce either a rigid national waiting period or a blanket administrative-capacity defense.

**C — provisional commitment: no foreclosure under current law, subject to the completed Rancho decision.** His Gonzaga concurrence distinguishes administrative consistency in interpreting a right from extinction of an established right. His Blessing and Bruneau joins require incompatibility in the enacted remedial system. The State's strongest argument is that beneficiary hearings and funding oversight offer a coherent correction process that judicial intervention might disrupt. The comparison must identify a concrete statutory conflict; the mere possibility of overlapping proceedings is insufficient. He would support a narrow conclusion on Medicaid's actual mechanisms and consider any relevant Rancho explanation once supplied.

**D — provisional commitment: affirm for practical, bounded further proceedings.** The financing definition constrains the right, but does not permit an appellate assumption that any nominal undertaking cures a proved violation. Conversely, perceived remedial usefulness cannot enlarge the statute. His Blessing join supplies the sequence: establish the particular violation and causation, identify a responsible official and lawful prospective relief, then fit an effective order to the individuals or properly defined class. The present disposition orders no institutional placement, fixed expenditure, broad program redesign or treasury damages.
