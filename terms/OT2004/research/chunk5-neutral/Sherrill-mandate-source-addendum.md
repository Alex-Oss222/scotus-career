# Sherrill — additional judgment-scope source facts

This is a sanitized source-control addendum, not a modeled position or historical comparison. It contains no present-matter Supreme Court result or writing and no private judicial position. The source is the Second Circuit opinion reproduced in the saved petition, `research/chunk5-record/sherrill_02-petition-for-writ-of-certiorari.txt`, appendix A3–4, A44–49 and A52. The adjacent `Sherrill-mandate-source-excerpts.txt` mechanically copies the relevant lower-opinion passages. No certification of reading the entire petition or joint appendix is made.

The three final Sherrill judgments concern the Lead, Eviction and Members cases. The lower court's reasons must be distinguished from the appellate reasons and from the substantive validity of the property tax.

1. **Lead case.** The Nation obtained summary judgment on its property-tax claim and the City's counterclaims. The Second Circuit's A44 reasoning states that the parcels' nontaxability defeats the tax and eviction claims. Its A45 note 27 separately agrees that the counterclaims are barred by tribal immunity from suit absent waiver or congressional abrogation, citing Potawatomi and United States Fidelity & Guaranty. A property-tax ruling alone therefore does not dispose of that independent suit-immunity ground.
2. **Eviction case.** The City brought the eviction claim and the Nation removed it. The lower procedural recital at A4 identifies sovereign immunity as a district-court ground. The appellate A44 discussion affirms because the parcels are not taxable. Those two grounds must not be collapsed into one. A change to the tax premise does not itself validate a tax sale, transfer title, authorize eviction or determine whether an independent immunity ground remains effective.
3. **Members case.** A48 rejects the property-tax claims because the parcels are nontaxable. A48–49 separately addresses the City's unjust-enrichment damages claim concerning state sales taxes on goods sold to non-Indians. The Second Circuit treats that claim against officials sued in their official capacities as a claim against the Nation because the complaint did not allege the officials acted outside tribal authority, and affirms immunity from that damages suit. A49 note 28 expressly does not reach the district court's additional necessary-and-indispensable-party ground. That sales-tax damages component is distinct from the property-tax question.
4. **Amendment.** A46–48 identifies the proposed defenses: statute of limitations, laches, waiver, estoppel, in pari delicto and ratification. The district court denied amendment as futile and the Second Circuit affirmed. The appellate reasoning treats delay defenses as legally unavailable under its reading of Oneida II and rejects the alleged inconsistency between Indian title and open-market purchase. The proposed amendment is thus identified in the supplied record; it must not be described as an entirely unprovided proposal. The individual proposed defenses remain distinct.
5. **Other rulings.** A45–46 affirms denial of Rule 56(f) discovery for the City's failure to identify particular information, its expected material effect and why it had not been obtained, after an adequate opportunity to develop the issues. A106–108, already supplied in the financial-findings excerpt, rejects the City's preliminary acquisition injunction for lack of demonstrated irreparable harm as well as lack of merits likelihood. A52 lists these rulings together with affirmance of the three Sherrill judgments. The separate Madison County remand remains a different matter.

The clean model should confirm the component and remedy consequences under each independently supported legal path. In particular, it should separate property-tax immunity from suit immunity, retain the distinct sales-tax damages component, and assess the identified amendment rather than infer its absence. These source facts select no path or vote and require no invented procedural act.
