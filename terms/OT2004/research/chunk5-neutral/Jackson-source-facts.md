## Facts and lower judgment

The United States describes Jackson as a teacher and girls' basketball coach who complained to supervisors about unequal funding and access to facilities/equipment. It reports allegations that adverse evaluations followed and coaching duties were removed in May 2001. He remained a teacher and lost coaching supplemental pay. These are complaint allegations; they are not findings of discrimination, retaliatory motive, recipient responsibility or damages.

The district court dismissed on the ground that Title IX supplied no retaliation action. The Eleventh Circuit affirmed, construing §§1681 and 1682 and the regulatory enforcement theory, and alternatively holding that a person who opposed discrimination directed at others was outside the protected class. The brief's Statement supplies these grounds and references to the lower-court appendix. The action asserted the statute itself; the United States cites complaint paragraphs rather than claiming that a regulation alone creates the action.

## Government's attributed arguments

The United States supports reversal and further proceedings. It argues that intentional retaliation because a complaint concerns sex discrimination is itself intentional discrimination on the basis of sex under §1681. Sullivan v. Little Hunting Park, 396 U.S. 229 (1969), is its principal pre-enactment analogy: the white property owner's advocacy of another person's protected rights did not remove his own retaliatory injury from protection. It also relies on Cannon and Franklin at the historical holdings described in the brief; the receiving model must substitute the current simulated versions where applicable.

The brief distinguishes Title VII's enumerated employment prohibitions and its language referring to the particular individual's sex from Title IX's broad prohibition. It does not claim that every employee grievance becomes a Title IX action. Its argument specifically treats a recipient retaliating against all complainers without considering the subject matter as outside the sex-based causal theory it advances. That is the government's proposed construction, not a judicially adopted exception.

The government relies on 34 C.F.R. §106.71's incorporation of §100.7(e), and related agency statements, as confirmation. It expressly says a direct statutory construction can decide the case without reliance on deference or a regulation-only cause of action. The regulation protects any person subjected to intimidation, threats, coercion or discrimination for interference with a protected right or for a covered complaint, testimony, assistance or participation. Its full copied text is in the excerpt file; the receiving context should preserve the distinction between the regulation's terms and any statutory holding it adopts.

The government argues that effective enforcement depends on teachers/coaches who know the program and can advocate for students. Its discussion of an exclusive actual-notice prerequisite describes historical Gebser; that exclusive formulation is displaced by the supplied simulated Gebser and must not be imported as controlling law. Its discussion of historical Sandoval likewise cannot replace this timeline's Powell/Sandoval holdings.
