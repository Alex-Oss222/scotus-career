## Smith

Historical judgment affirmed 8–0. Rehnquist did not participate; that personal nonparticipation supplies no automatic Stone recusal. Stevens authored Parts I, II and IV for Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part III was Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia joined every part except III and agreed with III's reasoning as the basis for agency deference, not an independent construction. His separate opinion relied on §628 rulemaking authority, the 1981 notice-and-comment interpretation, its express adverse-impact/business-necessity provision, and the separation of the regulation's recognition of an impact theory from its mistaken business-necessity threshold. He did not treat those two interpretive propositions as inseparable.

O'Connor concurred in judgment, joined by Kennedy and Thomas. Her main position rejects impact liability: parallel intent language, grammar, RFOA as safe harbor, Wirtz Report/structural distinction between prohibitions and noncoercive responses, and limits on transplanting Griggs. She rejects deference because the agency did not, in her view, construe §4(a)(2), and because its business-necessity reading was wrong. Part IV separately accepts, on the assumption impact claims exist, narrow RFOA protection and Wards Cove specificity; it also states a plaintiff burden to disprove the employer's produced nonage rationale. That last burden statement is the concurrence's statement, not a holding silently attributable to the five joining Stevens Part IV.

Published facts describe the five rank ranges, steps and half-steps, regional wage survey, minimum two-percent raise mechanism, larger dollar but smaller percentage increases at higher ranks, and the two reported statistics. The reasonable-factor conclusion and the claimed failure to identify a practice are judicial conclusions; the separate neutral supplement supplies only factual mechanics. Intentional treatment returned below for further discovery and is not disposed of by impact merits. Root instructed no broader records search because the possible narrow reasonable-factor resolution does not require independent reliance on claim-identification failure.

Official 2004 ADEA §623 and official July 2004 CFR §1625.7 were fetched; operative relevant subsections read. The CFR expressly uses business necessity in (d), and its (e) burden wording concerns an individual discriminatory-treatment claim. Do not substitute modern regulatory wording or transform that textual agency position into the Court's accepted standard.

## Rhines

Historical judgment vacated and remanded 9–0. O'Connor's opinion was joined by Rehnquist, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Souter did not give an unqualified full join. Stevens concurred, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, understanding good cause as flexible enough not to trap unwary unrepresented prisoners. Souter concurred in part and in judgment, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer: he would deny a stay upon demonstrated intentional delay instead of requiring an initial good-cause inquiry, describing the reservation as practical rather than doctrinal. Ginsburg and Breyer therefore signed the Court opinion and both separate writings.

The Court's rule required good cause for nonexhaustion, claims not plainly meritless, no intentional delay/abusive tactics, and reasonable state-return limits. It said denying and dismissing despite good cause, potential merit and no intentional delay likely abuses discretion; when a stay is unsuitable, deletion should be allowed if whole-petition dismissal would unreasonably impair federal relief. The Court did not itself decide that Rhines met the new test; appellate remand was to assess whether the particular stay abused discretion. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of unexhausted claims; it is not a command to deny every such claim.

Cornell's initial text contains two chronology slips corrected in final Reports: the South Dakota citation is 2000 SD 19, and the second state petition was August 22, 2002, not 2003. The July 3, 2002 stay had sixty-day initiation and return conditions. Use final Reports pp. 272–273 for those facts; do not infer a missed deadline from the Cornell typo.

## Exxon Mobil

Ginsburg for all nine; no separate writing; reversed and remanded. The decision confines Rooker–Feldman to state losers seeking rejection of injurious state judgments entered before federal commencement. It preserves separate preclusion and abstention rules, and the statutory possibility of express federal review such as §2254. Independent claims are not jurisdictionally barred merely because a state tribunal decided an issue first, even if federal adjudication could reject the tribunal's legal conclusion. Section 1738 supplies state-law preclusion effect, not loss of subject-matter jurisdiction.

Objective sequence: Delaware suit July 2000; federal action about two weeks later; subsidiaries asserted state counterclaims in January 2002; state jury verdict March 2003 above $400 million. Federal jurisdiction was invoked under §1330 (foreign states), not merely §1332. SABIC's immunity appeal remained distinct from the sua sponte jurisdictional dismissal. Footnote 7 describes Delaware affirmance and February 22, 2005 denial of reargument; controversy remained because SABIC continued opposition and represented it would seek Supreme Court review. Footnote 9 cites same-day Rhines as one protective-action example; that citation may be used only if this term's effective Rhines record supports it. There is no automatic import of a historical same-day decision.

## Johnson

Historical affirmance 5–4. Souter for Rehnquist, O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer; Kennedy dissent joined by Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg. Everyone accepted vacatur as a fact. The split concerned diligence in procuring it. Majority starts procurement diligence at federal judgment and the one-year filing period on notice of diligent vacatur; finality and the practical construction of discovery supply its basis. Kennedy rejects the added procurement duty as absent from text, trusts state timing law, and criticizes diversion of counsel during appeal and uncertainty. His alternative statutory rule requires diligent discovery of the order and federal filing within a year of when it was or reasonably could be discovered. His finality date discussion is an objection to the majority's chosen trigger, not adoption of procurement diligence.

Majority footnote 8 has three material qualifications: nonattributable state-court delay after diligent initiation is not fatal; later discovery of underlying invalidity facts permits diligence from their discoverability; and even inadequate diligence may suffice when federal filing occurs within one year of the notice date diligent action would have produced. Those conditions must accompany any adopted majority framework. The majority's admission that pro se status alone is insufficient is not an across-the-board exclusion of all external impediments.

The challenged enhancement is career-offender guideline §4B1.1, based on two Georgia drug convictions. Judgment November 29, 1994; state collateral initiation February 6, 1998; federal filing just over three months after vacatur. The order reversed seven guilty-plea judgments, one a predicate of the enhancement. The lower court recorded absence of affirmative waiver of counsel. Footnotes 1 and 3 distinguish potential Gideon availability from a record showing the parties actually litigated that exception. Do not invent its preservation. The mailbox rule was not invoked for the April 25, 1997 extension motion; a later procedural numbering change must not replace event-date §2255 paragraph 6(4).

## Rousey

Thomas for all nine; no separate writings; reversed and remanded. IRA categorical eligibility rests on age-conditioned full access and similarity to wage-replacement plans, not an unlimited exemption of all balances. Ten-percent early-distribution tax was substantial; footnote 2 leaves lower or flat penalties undecided. Age need not be the sole permitted distribution trigger. The reasonably-necessary support ceiling remains; the opinion decides only age nexus and similar-plan category.

The insider exception is conjunctive and retains employment timing: (i) an insider established or sponsored the plan and employed the debtor when rights arose, (ii) payment is based on age or service, AND (iii) the plan fails §401(a), §403(a), §403(b) or §408 qualification. The neutral statutory supplement mechanically copies the complete provision. Do not abbreviate it into an insider-only exclusion.

The full opinion and its notes identify death, disability, substantially equal life-expectancy payments, medical expense, health-premium, education and first-home exceptions. Important statutory precision: health premiums can be withdrawn in the unemployment-benefit year OR the succeeding taxable year, although the opinion's shorthand mentions the same year only. The copied §72(t) supplies additional qualifications. The payment exception is §72(t)(2)(A)(iv); recapture is §72(t)(4), despite abbreviated/miscited references in footnote 9. It exempts modifications for death/disability. Amount and scope qualifications remain essential; a blanket “no pre-59½ access” statement is false.

Official §522 (2004) was fetched and relevant operative text read; §72(t) from the official 2001 supplement was completely read; later operative cross-reference amendments were verified in enacted Public Law 108-311 §§207(6)-(7). Failed consolidated 2004 §72 URLs are not authority. The statutory supplement separately records limits and source status.

## Dura

Breyer for all nine; no separate writing; reversed and remanded. Inflation alone is insufficient economic loss and loss causation in this public-market fraud setting. The purchase may induce a transaction without causing compensable later loss. Other economic and firm-specific causes remain possible. Court assumes for argument that no pleading demand beyond Rule 8 applies to these elements, and finds even fair notice missing. It does not adopt a new heightened pleading rule, mandate one formal corrective disclosure, impose a completed-sale requirement, or prescribe econometric proof. It expressly leaves other causation/loss questions beyond its resolution.

The complaint involved both earnings and spray-device claims. February 24, 1998 earnings announcement was followed by about $39-to-$21 decline. November 1998 device announcement caused temporary decline, nearly recovered in a week. The district court rejected profitability allegations for scienter and device allegations for loss causation; reviewed branch is device loss causation. Avoid conflating those causal histories or treating the scienter branch as adjudicated here. Swierkiewicz supports modest fair-notice demands; PSLRA burden of proving causal loss remains distinct from its particularity requirements for misstatement/scienter. Historical general private-action descriptions do not displace this simulation's Central Bank actor-coverage rule.
