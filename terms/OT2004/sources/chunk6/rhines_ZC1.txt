[March 30, 2005]
Justice Souter, with
whom Justice Ginsburg and Justice Breyer join, concurring in
part and concurring in the judgment.
I join the Court’s opinion with
one reservation, not doctrinal but practical.  Instead of
conditioning stay-and-abeyance on “good cause” for
delay, ante, at 7, I would simply hold the order
unavailable on a demonstration of “intentionally dilatory
litigation tactics,” ante, at 8.  The trickiness of
some exhaustion determinations promises to infect issues of
good cause when a court finds a failure to exhaust; pro
se petitioners (as most habeas petitioners are) do not
come well trained to address such matters.  I fear that
threshold enquiries into good cause will give the district
courts too much trouble to be worth the time; far better to
wait for the alarm to sound when there is some indication that
a petitioner is gaming the system.
