### Sandin v. Conner, No. 93-1911

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Sufficient nonexclusive rule; no ultimate application holding; bounded remand |
| Concurrence | Ginsburg | Stevens | Full common rule/remand join; would already find interest and retains direct liberty |
| Concurrence | Breyer | Souter | Full common rule/remand join; would already find interest under whole Hawaii scheme |
| Dissent | O’Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would reverse reviewed liberty predicate under replacement hardship framework |

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, concurs. They would recognize protected liberty already on the actual punitive confinement record and also preserve an independent constitutional retained-liberty ground. Severe imposed isolation and its punitive character matter without depending on how a State drafts its code; administrative similarity and later expungement do not retroactively remove the need for process. They join the Court’s sufficient state-entitlement rule because it does not make codification necessary or replace intrinsic liberty with an exclusive hardship prerequisite. Breyer, joined by Souter, also concurs. The full defined-misconduct, impartial-decision and valid-evidence scheme constrains serious punishment, and the thirty-day isolation materially restricts ordinary activity. They would find the protected interest already established, while accepting the Court’s narrower remand and leaving actual procedural adequacy open. O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. They would replace the mandatory-regulation methodology with a rule ordinarily limiting state-created interests to atypical and significant hardship relative to ordinary prison life, while preserving direct unexpected-restraint and inevitable sentence-duration interests. They find the reviewed thirty-day confinement insufficient under that rule in light of comparable administrative and protective confinement and discretionary parole. Their proposed framework receives four votes and does not control. The concurrences’ affirmative application likewise has four votes; no Marks aggregation makes it the Court’s application holding.

### Carlisle v. United States, No. 94-9247

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivers the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt every controlling proposition and explanation below. Justice Stevens dissents, joined throughout by Justice Kennedy.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justice Kennedy.** They would reverse the no-authority ruling and remand for appellate consideration of the Government's disputed sufficiency ground. Their source of power is the court's retained pre-sentence judicial authority to prevent judgment on legally insufficient evidence, informed by the acquittal duty recognized in Rule 29(a). They do not treat that subdivision alone as an unrestricted postverdict motion remedy.

They accept that Rules 29(c) and 45(b) foreclose the defendant's late-motion entitlement, but do not read those provisions as withdrawing the judge's distinct authority while the case awaits sentence. Smith's completed-appellate setting makes its finality concern materially different. Manypenny's timely-motion holding does not itself decide this further question, but recognizes judicial correction within a continuing proceeding. Williams supplies an example of bounded supervision; it is not a license to disregard a controlling rule. On their reading, no rule withdraws the particular power asserted here.

The Court's strongest objection is that this construction allows the same relief the time limit restricts and substitutes sentencing for the specified deadline. The dissent answers that the source and function differ: a lost party motion does not compel entry of a judgment the judge concludes lacks legal evidentiary support. The position is confined to that pre-sentence responsibility, not a one-day, no-prejudice or general fairness exception, and it does not reopen completed criminal judgments indefinitely.

The dissent does not decide that the evidence was insufficient or that Carlisle was factually innocent. The Government contested the sufficiency ruling, and the Sixth Circuit did not resolve it. Acquittal therefore could not finally be restored without that review. The dissent orders neither a new trial nor release and does not rely on an unrestricted collateral remedy or automatic judicial-assurance exception.

### Doe v. District of Columbia, D.C. Circuit No. 95-7077

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter | Supports both federal affirmances | Entire opinion, including the CAPTA holding and investigative-procedure holding. |
| Partial joins in the Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer | Support investigative-procedure affirmance; oppose CAPTA affirmance | Investigative-procedure holding and preservation of certification and the distinct local-remedy question only. |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Ginsburg and Breyer | Would reverse the CAPTA dismissal and remand; agrees with the remaining disposition | Limited report-triggered statutory right, §1983 channel, and the unresolved elements and defenses on remand; no personal-immunity rule is decided. |

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Stevens, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Ginsburg and Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The dissent would recognize a bounded right of the child identified by a qualifying report to prompt initiation of an appropriate investigation directed to that report's accuracy. Wright and Wilder require attention to the actual entitlement; Blessing separates that question from aggregate agency performance and from the remedy. The identified beneficiary, mandatory report trigger, and concrete required service together supply the right. A complete failure to start can be judged without fixing every investigative method or inventing a universal numerical deadline.

Suter's rejection of different reasonable-efforts and reporting claims is a serious limit, but the dissent distinguishes the concrete investigation service claimed here from the broader caseworker relief sought there. The duty does not guarantee protection from private violence, removal, every service, or a successful outcome. Neither plan placement nor the absence of a freestanding implied action defeats the enacted §1983 channel, and no comprehensive remedial scheme foreclosing that channel has been demonstrated.

The dissent would reverse only the CAPTA dismissal and remand for the conduct-time duty, reports and their timing, breach, defendant-specific responsibility or lawful municipal attribution, causation, applicable defenses, and appropriate relief. It would impose no employer liability merely because the District employed the workers and would decide no personal immunity or damages. The dissent joins the Court's investigative-procedure ruling and its preservation of certification and the distinct local-remedy question.

### Agostini v. Felton and Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV | Stone-Zsela | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in full | Vacate both judgments and remand for tailored prospective relief | Part I: modification of Lemon; Part II: onsite secular-service rule and precise overruling; Part III: present legal change under Rule 60(b)(5); Part IV: tailored removal with retained safeguards and lawful obligations |
| Dissent, Part I | Souter | Stevens and Ginsburg | Would affirm both judgments | Affirmative substantive objection to this public instructional support; Breyer does not join |
| Same dissent, Part II | Souter | Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer | Would affirm both judgments | Retention of precedent and absence of sufficient intervening erosion or factual change |
| Dissent | Ginsburg | Stevens, Souter and Breyer | Would affirm both judgments on an independent procedural ground | Rule 60 review cannot supply this means of rehearing the original constitutional ruling |

#### Separate Writings

##### Justice Souter, dissenting

Stevens and Ginsburg join the dissent in full. Breyer joins Part II only.

In Part I, Souter, Stevens and Ginsburg would retain Aguilar's and Ball's applicable substantive rules. Public employees conducting education within a religious school's ordinary instructional setting supply governmental educational support, not independently attributable private speech. Public staffing, neutral eligibility and safeguards do not alone resolve the institutional subsidy and entanglement concerns. Rosenberger's private publication and the private-forum decisions therefore do not decide this case. They reject the Court's modification of Lemon because its treatment of purpose, effects and entanglement within attribution and function understates the constitutional significance of continuing public instructional support. Their objection concerns this governmental program rather than a claim that every private religious act must become government speech. These Justices do not find proved proselytizing and do not require it for their structural objection. Breyer does not join this affirmative direct-aid rationale.

In Part II, all four dissenters would adhere to the direct precedents. The safeguards and anticipated costs did not newly arise. Kiryas Joel preserved Aguilar and Ball, Zobrest supplied no establishment merits holding, and the bounded expression decisions did not erode the public-instruction rule sufficiently to justify its abandonment. They oppose adopting the general methodological change as a basis for abandoning these precedents in this case. They would affirm denial of relief and preserve the injunction.

##### Justice Ginsburg, dissenting; joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer

The four dissenters would also affirm on an independent procedural ground. Browder limits an appeal from denial of Rule 60 relief; it does not reopen the original judgment for indefinite rehearing. The district court followed still-binding Aguilar, and Rufo requires a qualifying change rather than an appeal whose own desired outcome creates the prerequisite for reopening. This Court may examine the continued force of its precedents in a proper case, but that power does not establish this vehicle's propriety. Their position does not turn an adjudicated injunction into a negotiated consent promise, deny Rule 60 relief after an actual qualifying change, or treat costs alone as sufficient. The Court's adoption of an updated Lemon test does not, in their view, cure that procedural defect. This writing addresses the route to relief independently of the substantive merits disagreement.

### Calderon v. Thompson, No. 97-215

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part I only | Reverses recall and restores the prior mandate | Part I: review channel, first-application record and temporal law; Parts II–III: recall standard, gateway applications and remedy |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Would affirm the recall | Exceptional institutional correction; no independent innocence or ineffective-assistance holding |

#### Separate Positions

**Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** These Justices would affirm the circuit’s exceptional correction of its own first-application process. They acknowledge the January notice, March belated-call request and rejection, June mandate and later delay. Those facts defeat a theory of a clerical lapse first discovered on the eve of execution. But the supported clerk-transition failure, actual effort to obtain consideration and independent June recall initiative establish a concrete institutional basis beyond regret about a merits result. The panel’s refusal does not extinguish the full court’s exceptional authority. The first-record limitation and explanation for awaiting state proceedings suffice under restrained abuse-of-discretion review, without making the delay necessary or every judge blameless.

Hazel-Atlas supports judicial-process protection while its fraud predicate remains distinct; Ohio Power confirms that procedural finality does not abolish all exceptional correction. Carlisle forbids circumvention of an actual rule but supplies no rule eliminating this power. Lonchar protects proper process without requiring en banc consideration. On that ground the dissent would not impose Schlup or Sawyer as a prerequisite to recall. It makes no finding that Thompson satisfies either gateway, proves Strickland prejudice or is entitled to every merits proposition stated below.

Affirmance would preserve the direction to enter the partial rape-related writ unless California elected to retry within a reasonable time, the associated death-sentence vacation, further review of murder and the then-entered execution-order vacatur. It would not create a fixed election or trial deadline, unconditional release, a murder acquittal or an independent Supreme Court ineffective-assistance holding.

### Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, addressing attribution and corrective relief. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas; they would reverse and restore defense summary judgment because private operation defeats present municipal attribution on the stipulated record.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The genuine conveyance, actual private maintenance and lighting, and absence of asserted covenant enforcement establish private operation on this stipulated record. Pinette and Chabad protect that expression; Agostini and Brentwood require a governmental act, not an association drawn from visual continuity or ordinary public access. Robinson concerned an actual official emblem, which this privately operated parcel is not. The dissent regards the surrounding public park's continued operation as insufficient to turn the Fund's message into the City's. It would reverse and restore defense summary judgment without a new factual hearing or disclaimer prerequisite. Its conclusion does not protect a future sham transfer, actual governmental adoption or enforcement directing religious operation.

### United States v. Mead Corp., No. 99-1434

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and II | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supplies controlling interpretive rules | No controlling deference for this mechanism; independent judicial construction with Skidmore consideration |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacates and remands | Corrected classification inquiry without a predetermined tariff answer |
| Concurrence in Parts I and II and dissent from the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse and sustain the classification | Independent reading of bound diary; remand only for implementation |
| Dissent | Scalia | No other Justice | Would reverse and sustain the classification | Controlling reasonable-choice treatment for the official agency interpretation |

#### Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins Parts I and II but dissents from the judgment. He would independently classify the goods under the tariff's ordinary words. A diary may organize prospective appointments and daily affairs, rather than only retrospective narrative. Rings fasten pages into a bound volume despite removability; neither sewing, glue nor permanence is required. Calendar, note and appointment pages satisfy that description, and auxiliary telephone pages do not defeat it. Loose unattached sheets remain different from the assembled imported articles. Customs' dictionary and commercial reasons persuade but do not control. The narrower appellate reading adds restrictions absent from the text. With both the question and undisputed product features before the Court, he would sustain the bound-diary classification and remand only for entry-specific judgment, assessment or refund implementation, without fixing an amount.

Scalia dissents on a different ground. Congress's classification assignment, genuine tariff ambiguity and Customs' publicly confirmed official interpretation warrant controlling reasonable-choice treatment in his view. The Solicitor General's brief cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel establishes the official-position condition; the statutory scheme supplies delegation, not those signatures alone. Ring binding and dated calendar and note pages make the classification reasonable despite the residual category. He objects to the Court's less determinate delegation threshold and its substitution of open persuasive review, while declining to treat every employee opinion as authoritative. He would sustain Customs' classification and leave only ordinary assessment implementation. His controlling-deference ground does not join Stone-Zsela's independent textual construction or either Court deference proposition.

### National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co., Nos. 00-832 and 00-843

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Souter and Thomas as to Part II only | Supports both reversals | Seven join Part I, mixed-service coverage; all nine join Part II, wireless coverage |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Thomas | Souter | Concurs in wireless reversal; would vacate the mixed-service component for agency explanation | Mandatory formula classification and reasoned statutory basis |

#### Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by Souter, concurs in the wireless holding but would vacate the mixed-service portion for a reasoned agency determination. If cable-modem access is cable service, subsection (d)'s formula controls; if it is telecommunications service, subsection (e)'s formula controls. Only after resolving those possibilities can residual authority under subsection (b) support the particular regulatory choice. The service supplied by an Internet service provider and the transmission carrying customers to that provider are distinct classification questions. The Commission's treatment of one does not necessarily resolve the other. Permian Basin's demand for a reasoned rate method supports returning that choice to the agency. Probable ultimate jurisdiction and policy favoring broadband deployment cannot supply the missing statutory explanation. This position rejects categorical noncoverage but withholds approval of the existing unexplained agency determination.

### Connecticut Department of Public Safety v. Doe, No. 01-1231

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal on the procedural ground | Complete opinion; protected liberty interest assumed |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stevens | — | Supports reversal without joining the Court's opinion | Affirmative liberty-and-punishment foundation |

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** The substantial registration duties and official stigma burden liberty and, in his view, operate as punishment. For conviction-based burdens that may lawfully be imposed prospectively, a constitutionally adequate criminal trial supplies the process for establishing the conviction. Another hearing about current dangerousness is unnecessary because it would not change that predicate. Stevens therefore agrees that the procedural injunction must be reversed, but does not join an opinion that leaves the liberty-and-punishment foundation wholly assumed. His distinction between prospective punishment and retrospective punishment objections leaves an unpresented retrospective claim to its proper inquiry; it establishes neither Doe's offense date nor the validity of the entire statutory scheme.
