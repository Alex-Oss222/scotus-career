## 91. Mid-Con: statutory scope before the fee ceiling

The material question is whether Michigan's section 478.2(2) obligation falls within the federal registration subject addressed by section 14504. The official 2000 Code text is saved and copied into the slice. Subsection (b)'s general reference to a state registration requirement must be read with subsection (a)'s forms-and-procedures definition and subsection (c)'s federal registration evidence, insurance, service-agent and allocated fee system. Subsection (c)(2)(B)(iii)'s vehicle-identification prohibition is a command about the Secretary's standards. Its application to an independent state safety program is a statutory-scope question; the presence of a decal does not answer it by itself. Subsection (c)(2)(C) concerns a fee under this section, and (B)(v) separately prohibits a fee for filing and maintaining evidence of federal registration. These are distinct commands.

Yellow establishes a valid reciprocity-preserving rule inside the federal fee system; it expressly leaves unrelated taxes and licensing policies outside that holding. The record supplies the claimed independence of Michigan's fee, its older origin, vehicle information and decal, and the apparent $10 waiver. It supplies no finding that federal registration legally depends on paying the $100. Neither age nor a regulatory label proves independence; the waiver alone proves no legal conditioning.

The two supported paths are a subject-specific construction preserving a genuinely separate program, or a broader construction reaching the additional vehicle registration and fee. A narrower actual-linkage path would matter only if the supplied record supported conditioning federal compliance on the extra payment. No such condition may be inferred from the waiver. Relief must identify the covered obligation displaced, with no invented refund total or finding about exact regulatory costs. The separate dormant-Commerce-Clause issue and local-haul fee remain outside this granted question.

Published prior support: Kennedy authored Yellow's eight-Justice opinion; O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined its express-delegation and statutory-limit rationale. For each, the useful competing considerations are enforcing the real federal benchmark against evasion and preserving the express limitation to the regulated subject. Stevens's concurrence in Yellow rests on Congress's express fee-system rulemaking task rather than ambiguity in the cap itself. His competing paths therefore require particular attention to the scope of that task, not an assumed universal federal fee ceiling. These positions support analysis; they do not resolve this new scope question.

## 92. Eulitt: own equality claim and institutional third-party claim

The parents' own equal-protection claim must receive its own adjudication. Strout decided Religion Clause questions concerning integrated St. Dominic's tuition; it did not preclude these different parents' equality theory. Its actual financed-function rule nevertheless supplies the governing substantive distinction. The supplied request purchases integrated ordinary tuition and identifies Catholic instruction and viewpoint as the unmet need. No independently controlled secular-service request is supplied. The local educational-needs condition, school approval conditions and categorical sectarian exclusion perform different legal work. Futility of a formal application does not establish eligibility under every neutral criterion, and the Court should not invent a factual refusal on an untested neutral ground.

The supported merits paths are to reject the actual request under the integrated-purchase distinction while preserving equal secular eligibility; to find a separately supported status exclusion affecting eligible secular work and grant fitted relief; or expressly reconsider the existing integrated-tuition rule. The strongest challenge to the first path is that a program supporting private secular alternatives cannot use religious identity as a proxy for an actual devotional purchase. The response must be tied to the supplied integrated tuition and the function it finances, not religious governance or hypothetical fungibility. The second path needs an actual distinct secular request; none can be manufactured by the legal characterization alone.

Kowalski v. Tesmer, December 13, 2004, now governs the earlier same-term standing reference. Its majority rejects lawyers' assertion of unidentified future clients' rights for want of a concrete existing or specifically prevented relationship. Eulitt supplies an actual parent-school relationship, so Kowalski's failed relationship predicate cannot mechanically decide this case. The separate hindrance requirement remains: no actual obstacle to St. Dominic's own suit is supplied. Denying representative standing on the school's institutional claim does not eliminate the parents' own injury or equality claim.

## 93. Kelo: public use remains distinct from compensation and regulatory efficacy

Boston & Maine, March 25, 1992, directly permits compensated private reconveyance serving rationally supported passenger service and rejects naked private favoritism. Berman and Midkiff remain operative pre-divergence authority. Lingle, May 23, 2005, removes substantial advancement as an independent compensation-based taking test and expressly changes Del Monte Dunes at that point. It does not repeal the independent public-use condition on an actual condemnation. San Remo Hotel, June 20, 2005, supplies issue-specific recognition of an earlier state judgment; this direct review of the Connecticut judgment is not a later federal collateral attack on it.

The supplied comprehensive redevelopment plan supports the public-purpose path. The strongest opposing path distinguishes removal of existing harmful land conditions or a passenger-service use from taking ordinary homes for predicted private economic benefits. A meaningful distinction must confront Midkiff's broad public-purpose rationale and Boston & Maine's private-transfer holding; it cannot describe them as requiring public ownership. The property owners' concern that any more profitable successor could justify transfer supports a limiting rule, but does not itself prove a sham in this record. Pretext remains available if supported; the packet reports no finding of a sham solely favoring an identified recipient. Courts cannot guarantee future employment or revenue, predict later failure, or treat compensation as curing a constitutionally private purpose.

## 94. Gonzalez: classification and extraordinary relief must remain separate

Abdur'Rahman already rejects categorical treatment of every Rule 60 motion as successive. The supplied motion challenges the federal limitations barrier, not a merits disposition of the guilty-plea claim. That is a direct application of the process-only rule, subject to confirmation that the motion contains no additional merits demand. Any new custody claim, new factual merits supplement or renewed merits adjudication must instead receive the applicable successive treatment, with genuine components separated. The lower categorical approach cannot be sustained without confronting and changing the controlling rule.

The live second question is Rule 60(b)(6). The record supplies an abandoned certificate-review route, months between Artuz and the motion, and no separately established external impediment. Those facts permit a finality-and-diligence ground denying extraordinary relief even when the motion is properly classified. The competing path treats the initial lack of any federal merits hearing and the intervening correction of a procedural interpretation as sufficiently exceptional, or calls for a supported factual remand. The motion's procedural character does not itself supply that conclusion, and months alone must not become a categorical time bar. No external impediment, excused failure to appeal, or new evidence may be invented.

Pace's April 27, 2005 rule distinguishes a genuine application-filing deadline from a claim-relief bar and preserves reasonable diligence in the actual circumstances. It does not establish that Gonzalez's underlying state application was untimely or resolve his habeas merits. Rhines, March 30, 2005, governs retention of an existing timely mixed petition; its reasonable-explanation good-cause standard is not Rule 60(b)(6)'s extraordinary-circumstances standard, and it creates no retroactive protective-filing duty. The separate same-day Mayle and later Bell matters supply no entering law.

## 95. Allapattah and Ortega: two judgments under one enacted structure

The official section 1367 text is copied in full, including subsection (b)'s limiting phrase concerning inconsistency with section 1332 and subsection (c)'s four separate grounds. The statute predates the divergence and follows Finley; no missing alternate-timeline enactment predicate exists. The question is the statute's effect on Zahn and Clark, not whether procedural rules alone expand statutory jurisdiction. Rule 23 and Rule 20 remain distinct joinder settings. Subsection (b) lists claims by plaintiffs against defendants made parties under specified rules, and separately persons proposed as plaintiffs under Rule 19 or intervening as plaintiffs under Rule 24. It does not textually list Rule 23 claimants or additional Rule 20 plaintiffs as such.

The broad-grant path starts with an independently qualifying claim, complete diversity and same-case relatedness, then applies the enumerated exclusions. Its strongest objection is that original jurisdiction over a civil action may itself presuppose amount compliance by every separate claimant, with Zahn supplying that antecedent meaning. The narrower path must explain how subsection (a)'s additional-party language and subsection (b)'s selective exceptions perform work without silently inserting Rule 20 plaintiffs or Rule 23 into the exception list. Neither path may treat citizenship and amount as interchangeable without explaining the statutory distinction, or aggregate separate claims to create the original qualifying claim.

City of Chicago establishes that statutory power over related claims and lawful discretion to decline them remain separate. Grupo Dataflux preserves the real filing-time citizenship requirement; it does not decide the present amount question. Grable, June 13, 2005, supplies an original federal-question route for a small class of embedded federal issues, not a replacement diversity basis here. Neither section 1367(c) discretion nor litigation expense creates missing original jurisdiction. The 2005 class-action legislation supplies no unexamined substitute basis for these earlier actions.
