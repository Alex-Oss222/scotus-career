## OT_1994CHUNK8.md ? Sandin v.

### Sandin v. Conner, No. 93-1911

Merits decision, June 19, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 28, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which reversed summary judgment for prison officials after recognizing a liberty interest and an unresolved dispute about process under Wolff. The question concerns Hawaii’s disciplinary segregation rules and the predicate for protected liberty.

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

#### Judgment

Ninth Circuit affirmed in the reviewed respect and case remanded, 5–4; genuine constraint, meaningful deprivation and process must be determined; no liability finding.

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reviewed liberty/process summary judgment | Affirmed reversal of summary judgment; remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Genuine constraint, meaningful deprivation and process determinations |

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Sufficient nonexclusive rule; no ultimate application holding; bounded remand |
| Concurrence | Ginsburg | Stevens | Full common rule/remand join; would already find interest and retains direct liberty |
| Concurrence | Breyer | Souter | Full common rule/remand join; would already find interest under whole Hawaii scheme |
| Dissent | O’Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would reverse reviewed liberty predicate under replacement hardship framework |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty

**Controlling proposition:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory wording alone is insufficient: courts must examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and the meaningful restraint, without making state codification necessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that legal substance matters more than a regulation’s drafting conventions. Hewitt supplies the recognition that substantive state constraints may create protected liberty; Thompson distinguishes genuine predicates from procedures that do not require a particular substantive outcome. The Court narrows reliance on mandatory words alone without discarding real constraints simply because they operate inside prison. The complete scheme must be considered: defined misconduct, an impartial adjudicative process, valid evidence and the relationship between guilt and a significant sanction can confine official authority even when officials retain discretion to select a commensurate penalty afterward. Wolff distinguishes substantial protected liberty from routine management and supplies the separate inquiry into appropriate process. Mere mandatory administration, routine transfers or brief inconveniences do not automatically create entitlements. Nor is this an exclusive requirement of state codification for intrinsic constitutional liberty, an unexpected restraint or a sentence-duration interest. The Court adopts no new multifactor hardship test and leaves the ultimate application to the district court under the stated rule.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hewitt v. Helms: narrows mechanical reliance on mandatory wording while retaining genuine substantive-predicate liberty protection; no wholesale methodology replacement controls.
- Kentucky Department of Corrections v. Thompson: applies distinction between substantive constraints and procedural directions alone.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves significant prison liberty and context-sensitive procedural protection.
- Meachum v. Fano: distinguishes ordinary sentence-authorized management from a genuine protected constraint.
- Vitek v. Jones; Washington v. Harper: preserve distinct directly protected, qualitatively different restraints; this state-entitlement rule is nonexclusive.
- Farmer v. Brennan; Helling v. McKinney: distinguish Eighth Amendment conditions liability from this procedural-liberty inquiry.

##### The reviewed summary judgment and remand

**Controlling proposition:** The summary judgment rejecting the reviewed liberty-and-process claim cannot stand on a mechanical reading of Hawaii’s regulatory words or dismissal of genuine punitive constraints. The district court must determine whether the rules genuinely constrain authority, whether the deprivation is meaningful rather than routine, and what process is due; the Court makes no liability or ultimate protected-interest finding.

**Authority:** Breyer’s Opinion of the Court and remedial disposition; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg join the bounded rule-and-remand ground. Four concurring Justices separately would find the interest already established.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the dispute requires application of the substantive rule to the complete disciplinary scheme and the actual deprivation. Hawaii’s direction to find guilt on substantial evidence is not rewritten as an express acquittal command; it must be read with the defined offenses, impartial committee, evidence rules and authority for serious sanctions. The record concerns thirty days of punitive isolation and material restriction of ordinary out-of-cell activity. Similarity to separately authorized administrative or protective confinement does not by itself eliminate the legal constraint on punishment, while punitive labeling alone does not create protected liberty. Later expungement of the serious charge does not retroactively supply adequate process. Discretionary parole consequences are not an inevitable sentence extension. The district court must make the necessary determinations and assess process if protected liberty is implicated. Wolff permits refusal of witnesses for irrelevance, lack of necessity or hazards to safety or correctional goals. No automatic witness right, useless-witness finding, damages, immunity or unrelated amended-complaint disposition follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Wolff: applies witness qualifications and the separate process inquiry once protected liberty is implicated.
- Hewitt; Thompson: require whole-scheme substantive analysis rather than a one-way mandatory-word inference.
- Meachum: preserves ordinary authorized custody without making every punitive restraint legally unconstrained.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Hewitt is narrowed, not wholesale replaced. The proposed atypical-and-significant-hardship framework has four dissenting votes; the concurrences’ positive application has four votes. Neither controls through Marks.

The five-Justice sufficient state-entitlement rule and bounded remand govern. The Court does not hold that protected liberty is absent or already established on this record. Other direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration protections are not displaced; unrelated claims and defenses remain. No new multifactor prison test is adopted.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Ginsburg, joined by Stevens, concurs. They would recognize protected liberty already on the actual punitive confinement record and also preserve an independent constitutional retained-liberty ground. Severe imposed isolation and its punitive character matter without depending on how a State drafts its code; administrative similarity and later expungement do not retroactively remove the need for process. They join the Court’s sufficient state-entitlement rule because it does not make codification necessary or replace intrinsic liberty with an exclusive hardship prerequisite. Breyer, joined by Souter, also concurs. The full defined-misconduct, impartial-decision and valid-evidence scheme constrains serious punishment, and the thirty-day isolation materially restricts ordinary activity. They would find the protected interest already established, while accepting the Court’s narrower remand and leaving actual procedural adequacy open. O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissents. They would replace the mandatory-regulation methodology with a rule ordinarily limiting state-created interests to atypical and significant hardship relative to ordinary prison life, while preserving direct unexpected-restraint and inevitable sentence-duration interests. They find the reviewed thirty-day confinement insufficient under that rule in light of comparable administrative and protective confinement and discretionary parole. Their proposed framework receives four votes and does not control. The concurrences’ affirmative application likewise has four votes; no Marks aggregation makes it the Court’s application holding.

#### Source Notes

Ninth Circuit, 15 F.3d 1463; Hawaii disciplinary rules §§17-201-7, 12, 13, 16–20; merits filings and record passages in No. 93-1911, including the segregation comparison. The defined-offense, impartial-decision and evidence provisions are distinct from the rule directing a guilt finding on substantial evidence; that wording supplies no automatic acquittal command.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit is affirmed in the reviewed respect and the case remanded. The district court shall determine whether Hawaii rules genuinely constrain officials’ authority to impose punitive segregation, whether the deprivation is meaningful rather than routine, and what process is due. The Court makes no liability or final interest finding; no unrelated amended-complaint claim is swept into the disposition. Properly framed ensuing proceedings remain available.

**End of entry: Sandin v. Conner, merits decision, June 19, 1995.**

## OT_1995CHUNK3.md ? Neal v.

### Neal v. United States, No. 94-9088

Merits decision, January 22, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

516 U.S. 284. Argued December 4, 1995. On certiorari to the Seventh Circuit, 46 F.3d 1405. Neal seeks use of the Sentencing Commission's amended weight per dose of LSD when calculating the statutory mandatory minimum. The question is whether that Guidelines amendment displaces Chapman's construction of 21 U.S.C. §841.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The statutory-minimum judgment remains in force; no new sentence or calculation is ordered.

#### Opinions and Holdings

Kennedy writes for a unanimous Court. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer join the entire opinion. This unanimous opinion controls the rule and explanation below.

##### The statute and Guidelines retain their separate LSD measures

**Operative rule:** For LSD absorbed into the blotter-paper carrier at issue, §841(b)(1)'s statutory mixture-or-substance weight includes the actual carrier weight under Chapman. Amendment 488's 0.4-milligram-per-dose calculation governs the Guidelines Drug Quantity Table within its lawful scope and does not replace that statutory measure; when the statutory minimum exceeds the maximum of the applicable Guidelines range, §5G1.1(b) makes the statutory minimum the Guidelines sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** Chapman v. United States construed the statute to count the LSD and its carrier together. The Commission's later amendment selects a constructive weight to reduce disparities among carrier media. Its commentary expressly preserves statutory mandatory minima. Courts must give both rules their proper operation rather than turn the Guidelines change into a statutory amendment.

Stinson v. United States makes valid interpretive commentary authoritative within the Guidelines system, while excluding commentary that conflicts with a federal statute. It does not empower the Commission to overrule a judicial construction of §841. Patterson v. McLean Credit Union gives particular force to statutory stare decisis because Congress can amend the enacted rule. The amendment's policy criticism of carrier disparities is not itself an adequate ground to overrule Chapman here. The Court does not hold that statutory precedent can never be reconsidered, or that every later agency interpretation is foreclosed by every earlier judicial statement.

The amendment's qualifications remain within their own field. For unmarked blotter paper, a quarter-inch by quarter-inch section is presumed one dose. For liquid LSD not yet placed on a carrier, pure-drug weight may inadequately reflect seriousness and may warrant an upward departure. Neither provision creates an exception to §841's minimum or requires a departure in this case.

The Court leaves lawful Commission policymaking intact. It decides no unpresented sentencing exemption, Guidelines temporal question, or distinct constitutional challenge involving an unusually heavy carrier with very little LSD.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453:** Followed for actual LSD/carrier weight under §841; the Commission's constructive Guidelines measure does not displace it.
- **Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36:** Applied to preserve valid commentary within the Guidelines system and its limitation against conflict with a federal statute.
- **Patterson v. McLean Credit Union, 491 U.S. 164:** Applied for the special force of statutory stare decisis while Congress retains authority to amend the rule.

#### Law After Decision

Statutory and Guidelines quantities may differ. Chapman controls the statutory mandatory minimum; Amendment 488 controls its delegated Guidelines calculation subject to that minimum. Neither source is silently rewritten to match the other.

#### Source Notes

The [Seventh Circuit opinion, 46 F.3d 1405](https://openjurist.org/46/f3d/1405/united-states-v-neal) and the [official reported record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-516/pdf/USREPORTS-516-284.pdf) supply the reviewed statutory sentencing question. The [Commission's original Amendment 488](https://www.ussc.gov/guidelines/amendment/488) supplies the calculation, qualifications, and express preservation of mandatory minima. No new carrier weight, dose count, statutory tier, or replacement sentence is found.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Seventh Circuit's judgment stands. The Court orders no resentencing, alternative calculation, or inquiry into an unspecified exception.

**End of entry: Neal v. United States, Merits decision, January 22, 1996.**

## OT_1995CHUNK6.md ? Carlisle v.

### Carlisle v. United States, No. 94-9247

Merits decision, April 29, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 16, 1996, in October Term 1995. The Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the Sixth Circuit's judgment, 48 F.3d 190, reversing a postverdict judgment of acquittal for lack of authority under Rule 29.

The jury returned a marijuana-conspiracy guilty verdict and was discharged July 13, 1993. Then-operative Rule 45(a) excluded intervening weekends from the seven-day period, making July 22 the deadline. Counsel filed July 23; the Government timely objected. No trial motion, reserved decision or extension fixed within the seven days is established.

The judge initially denied acquittal on sufficiency grounds, reconsidered at the October 14 sentencing hearing and entered acquittal October 28. Acknowledging the late filing, he relied on lack of Government prejudice and prevention of injustice. His two assessments drew different inferences from the same trial conduct; neither establishes factual innocence. The Sixth Circuit decided only authority, leaving evidentiary sufficiency disputed, and directed reinstatement of the verdict and sentencing.

That mandate was implemented before this review. Sentence was imposed June 27, 1995. The July 7 amended judgment vacated the earlier judgment under Rule 35(c), readjudged guilt and retained June 27 as the sentence-imposition date. The present issue is authority to grant the late acquittal, not a new guilt or sentencing determination. All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Justices Stevens and Kennedy.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Scalia delivers the Opinion of the Court, Parts I–III, joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven Justices adopt every controlling proposition and explanation below. Justice Stevens dissents, joined throughout by Justice Kennedy.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Rules 29 and 45 do not authorize this new, untimely postverdict acquittal

**Controlling proposition:** With the Government's timely objection preserved, the district court lacked authority to grant Carlisle's new postverdict motion outside Rule 29(c)'s period. Rule 45(b) excludes ordinary excusable-neglect enlargement beyond Rule 29's own conditions. This is a limitation on the requested motion remedy, not a loss of federal criminal subject-matter jurisdiction.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by the seven Justices identified in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** The applicable Rule 29(c) permits a motion within seven days after jury discharge or within further time fixed during that period. It authorizes setting aside a guilty verdict on such a motion and requires no earlier trial motion. Rule 29(a) requires acquittal, on a defendant's or the court's motion after the evidence closes, if the evidence cannot sustain conviction. Rule 29(b) permits reserving a timely trial motion and deciding it later on the evidence when reserved. These provisions supply different authorized paths; none was followed here.

Rule 45(b)'s general enlargement power expressly excepts action under Rules 29, 33, 34 and 35 except to the extent and under the conditions those Rules state. A one-day delay, attorney error or absence of Government prejudice therefore cannot itself supply power to extend this period.

The strongest contrary reading relies on Rule 29(a)'s lack of an express terminal phrase and the judge's responsibility not to enter judgment on insufficient evidence. But reading that provision to authorize the identical new acquittal without the postverdict conditions would make those conditions ineffective. The Court relies on the integrated text, not a caption alone. Sentencing supplies no substitute deadline in the Rule.

The decision leaves open a court-initiated action within the permitted period, reconsideration of a timely motion, valid reservation and Government forfeiture of its objection. Neither an earlier denial nor the judge's later changed evidentiary appraisal created a timely motion or reservation here. Enforcing this vehicle's conditions does not decide that the trial evidence was sufficient.

##### II. General rules and inherent authority cannot supply the same act that the specific Rules foreclose

**Controlling proposition:** Rule 2, Rule 57 and inherent supervisory authority do not authorize this otherwise prohibited postverdict acquittal. The asserted separate pre-sentence power cannot circumvent Rules 29(c) and 45(b) by changing the label attached to the same relief. The holding does not abolish inherent judicial authority generally.

**Authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 2 directs just determination, simplicity, fairness and elimination of unjustifiable expense and delay. It guides interpretation; it does not displace a specific restriction. Former Rule 57 allowed consistent practice where the Rules made no provision. This case presents a governing provision, not a procedural gap.

The December 1, 1995 amendment retains consistency with federal law and national and local rules. Its protection against nonwillful violations of local form requirements does not excuse a national Rule 29 deadline. Its actual-notice protection concerns requirements outside federal law, federal rules and local rules. The amendment governs pending proceedings only insofar as just and practicable; the Court neither applies it automatically to the 1993 conduct nor derives an enlargement power from it.

The dissent's distinct retained-power argument is substantial: a trial judge traditionally protects against legally insufficient convictions while the case remains before the court. But the asserted authority here would deliver precisely the relief the specific postverdict mechanism restricts. Bank of Nova Scotia requires supervision to remain consistent with governing rules. Williams's bounded supervision of a materially distorted grand-jury presentation survives; its prejudice-based, rule-consistent remedy does not authorize this conflicting exercise.

Fallen likewise does not supply an attorney-error exception. There the ill, effectively unrepresented prisoner did all reasonably possible to transmit a timely notice. That treatment of timely efforts differs from excusing counsel's admitted late motion. Existing inherent powers are not denied, and hypothetical Article III limits on withdrawing them are not presented. The Court decides the claimed power in this rule structure, not every judicial correction before judgment.

##### III. The alternative writ and due-process theories do not require this acquittal or a second trial

**Controlling proposition:** Neither the All Writs Act nor due process supplies authority for the untimely acquittal on these facts. The Government may obtain restoration of the existing verdict without a second factfinding trial. The ruling preserves, but does not adjudicate or guarantee, independently available review of properly presented claims.

**Authority:** Part III of the same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1651 permits necessary or appropriate writs in aid of jurisdiction consistent with the usages and principles of law. Morgan's extraordinary coram nobis remedy is not an alternative deadline for counsel's late motion in an ongoing criminal case. No independent exceptional predicate is established here.

Nor is this a case of a predeadline judicial assurance inducing lateness. The judge's later reconsideration supplies no such assurance. The Court need not decide every judicial-misinformation case to reject ordinary counsel error as that different circumstance; Harris Truck Lines and Thompson do not establish the missing predicate.

Jackson protects against conviction without legally sufficient evidence, but does not itself require this additional late district-court vehicle. Carlisle had the opportunities provided by the Rules; a missed motion is not automatically informed waiver of every other claim. Remaining appellate and collateral channels depend on their own authority, preservation, proof and procedural conditions. Plain-error review is not declared equivalent to ordinary sufficiency review, and no ineffective-assistance remedy is promised. AEDPA's new conditions and unresolved application questions must be respected, rather than describing collateral relief as unlimited.

Finally, Wilson permits the Government's appeal because reversal restores the jury's existing verdict without subjecting Carlisle to another trial. That consequence does not decide sufficiency, which the Sixth Circuit left unresolved. Reinstatement and sentencing have already occurred, so affirmance orders neither repetition nor release. The dissent's requested sufficiency-review remand follows from its different authority construction, not from a sufficiency determination made by this Court.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

*Smith* supports rejecting an unrestricted judicial substitute for a restricted motion, although its postappeal setting differs. *Pioneer* allows excusable-neglect flexibility only where authorized and preserves specific enlargement restrictions. *Manypenny* concerns reconsideration of a timely motion and is not decided to govern this new late motion.

*Bank of Nova Scotia* requires supervision to remain consistent with governing rules. *Williams* preserves bounded supervision of a materially distorted grand-jury presentation through a prejudice-based, rule-consistent remedy. *Fallen* protects timely efforts by an effectively unrepresented prisoner, without creating an ordinary attorney-error exception. *Lonchar* and *Cooper* require meaningful performance of legally required process, but establish no additional Rule 29 route or still-owed proceeding requiring this motion.

*Morgan* preserves extraordinary coram nobis relief without supplying a substitute deadline here. *Jackson* preserves protection against conviction on legally insufficient evidence; *Wilson* permits restoration of an existing verdict without a second trial. *Olano* preserves the distinction between forfeiture and waiver and conditions on correction; it neither enlarges district-court motion authority nor supplies a new collateral holding. *Pioneer*, *Olano* and *Libretti* preserve the separate inquiries into authorized correction, procedural default, actual waiver and lawful punishment. *Markman's* reserved civil motion and *Holly Farms'* distinct statutory exception do not change this criminal deadline. No precedent is overruled.

The decision bars this new late acquittal without determining evidentiary sufficiency or withdrawing independently authorized review. Any separately available federal collateral proceeding must account for AEDPA, enacted April 24. Section 2255 now imposes a one-year period measured from the latest specified event: finality; removal of an unlawful governmental filing impediment; a newly recognized Supreme Court right made retroactive on collateral review; or discovery of supporting facts through due diligence. A second or successive motion requires appellate certification for qualifying newly discovered evidence sufficient, if proved and considered with the whole record, to establish clearly and convincingly that no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt, or for a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by the Supreme Court. The amended appellate-certificate provisions require the specified substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identification of qualifying issues.

These statutory conditions do not determine whether a particular later Carlisle proceeding satisfies or is governed by them. Chapter 154's pending-case clause does not resolve every other transition question. The June 1995 dismissal of an early §2255 motion as premature establishes no subsequent-motion classification, deadline, custody predicate or entitlement here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justice Kennedy.** They would reverse the no-authority ruling and remand for appellate consideration of the Government's disputed sufficiency ground. Their source of power is the court's retained pre-sentence judicial authority to prevent judgment on legally insufficient evidence, informed by the acquittal duty recognized in Rule 29(a). They do not treat that subdivision alone as an unrestricted postverdict motion remedy.

They accept that Rules 29(c) and 45(b) foreclose the defendant's late-motion entitlement, but do not read those provisions as withdrawing the judge's distinct authority while the case awaits sentence. Smith's completed-appellate setting makes its finality concern materially different. Manypenny's timely-motion holding does not itself decide this further question, but recognizes judicial correction within a continuing proceeding. Williams supplies an example of bounded supervision; it is not a license to disregard a controlling rule. On their reading, no rule withdraws the particular power asserted here.

The Court's strongest objection is that this construction allows the same relief the time limit restricts and substitutes sentencing for the specified deadline. The dissent answers that the source and function differ: a lost party motion does not compel entry of a judgment the judge concludes lacks legal evidentiary support. The position is confined to that pre-sentence responsibility, not a one-day, no-prejudice or general fairness exception, and it does not reopen completed criminal judgments indefinitely.

The dissent does not decide that the evidence was insufficient or that Carlisle was factually innocent. The Government contested the sufficiency ruling, and the Sixth Circuit did not resolve it. Acquittal therefore could not finally be restored without that review. The dissent orders neither a new trial nor release and does not rely on an unrestricted collateral remedy or automatic judicial-assurance exception.

#### Source Notes

The [filed record and briefs in No. 94-9247](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385013_0626) supply Joint Appendix 6–14 for filing and objection; 15–23 and 24–45 for the district court's two appraisals; 46–49 for the Sixth Circuit's authority-only decision; and 50–51 for the intervening sentencing judgment. The Government's brief 2–3 and appendix 2a–5a reproduce the pertinent trial-era rules; brief 9–10 n.4 describes sentencing and the premature §2255 motion; 38–39 n.24 identifies uncertainty about ordinary and plain-error sufficiency review. Those accounts do not establish present custody or completion of sentence.

[Rule 57's official amendment and transition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CDOC-104hdoc65/pdf/CDOC-104hdoc65.pdf) distinguish the 1993 rule from the qualified December 1, 1995 change. [AEDPA Title I, especially §§102 and 105, 110 Stat. 1217–1226](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-104publ132/pdf/PLAW-104publ132.pdf), supplies the enacted collateral conditions; their particular application remains undecided here. The [official case report, 517 U.S. 416](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-517/pdf/USREPORTS-517-416.pdf), corroborates the event and reported record. No unsupported claim of factual innocence, new sufficiency finding or present collateral proceeding is adopted.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed on the authority question. The late acquittal remains unauthorized; the reinstated conviction and already-entered sentencing judgment remain undisturbed on the issue reviewed. No duplicate sentencing, new trial, release, custody-status determination or collateral action is ordered. No special mandate date or further Supreme Court action is prescribed. Independently authorized and properly presented sufficiency, sentencing or collateral claims retain their lawful procedural posture and remain subject to their own conditions; none is adjudicated or automatically reopened here.

**End of entry: Carlisle v. United States, No. 94-9247, Merits decision, April 29, 1996.**

## OT_1996CHUNK10.md ? Doe v. District

### Doe v. District of Columbia, D.C. Circuit No. 95-7077

Merits decision, June 2, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Merits hearing June 2, 1997. On granted review of the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254; the decision under review is 93 F.3d 861 (D.C. Cir. 1996).

The District Court dismissed federal claims arising from alleged failures to investigate reports that a young child was being neglected before she suffered severe scalding injuries. The Court of Appeals resolved the federal claims and certified unsettled D.C. notice questions to the District of Columbia Court of Appeals. Review of dismissal accepts well-pleaded allegations; it establishes no preinjury notice, breach, causal responsibility, or damages.

The questions are whether CAPTA's report-triggered prompt-investigation command creates an individual right enforceable through §1983; whether the asserted mandatory funding obligation supplies such a right rather than only a public administrative duty; and whether mandatory investigative procedures alone create the claimed protected liberty interest when the injury preceded government custody.

All nine Justices participated at argument or submission and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or reservation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CAPTA report-triggered investigation claim under §1983 | Affirm dismissal, 5–4 | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer | No federal statutory damages or institutional relief follows from this claim. |
| Due process based solely on mandatory investigative procedures | Affirm dismissal, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer | — | Independently created substantive interests are not categorically excluded; the distinct local-remedy theory remains reserved. |

The Court does not answer the certified D.C.-law notice questions or decide whether the asserted local tort remedy was validly lost through §12-309. It awards no damages and orders no service plan, staffing level, removal, or institutional decree.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter | Supports both federal affirmances | Entire opinion, including the CAPTA holding and investigative-procedure holding. |
| Partial joins in the Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer | Support investigative-procedure affirmance; oppose CAPTA affirmance | Investigative-procedure holding and preservation of certification and the distinct local-remedy question only. |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Ginsburg and Breyer | Would reverse the CAPTA dismissal and remand; agrees with the remaining disposition | Limited report-triggered statutory right, §1983 channel, and the unresolved elements and defenses on remand; no personal-immunity rule is decided. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### CAPTA's report-triggered investigation command

**Holding and operative rule:** The conduct-time 42 U.S.C. §5106a(b)(2) obliges a participating jurisdiction to provide for prompt initiation of an investigation after receipt of a report of known or suspected child abuse or neglect, but does not confer the asserted individual federal right to that investigative response enforceable under §1983. The holding concerns that command, not every CAPTA provision or every condition attached to federal funding.

**Authority:** The statutory portion of the Opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, controls by five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Blessing's distinction between a binding public duty and an individual entitlement. Congress's direction is mandatory: the recipient must provide for an investigation to begin promptly after a report. But intended benefit, a concrete trigger, and an administrable task do not alone establish that Congress gave each reported child a private federal entitlement. In its statutory setting this provision directs the recipient's investigative system. Suter's separate reporting holding supplies the closest analogy: even a child-specific reporting obligation may govern public administration without creating the asserted individual right.

Doe's strongest argument is that complete failure to begin a particular investigation can be assessed without prescribing professional methods or a universal deadline. That shows why the command is more concrete than a demand for general agency adequacy. It does not overcome the distinction between the recipient's obligation and an individual entitlement. Wright and Wilder remain applicable where the actual provision secures such an entitlement; Suter does not create a categorical state-plan bar.

The Court does not rest on absence of a mandatory duty, statutory location alone, lack of an implied action, or automatic foreclosure by funding supervision. Because the asserted right is absent, no separate foreclosure holding is necessary. The clause requiring immediate protective steps follows a finding of abuse or neglect and extends to other endangered children under the same care; it has a different trigger, and no preinjury finding is established here. Later plan requirements do not govern the 1989 conduct. Breach, causation, personal responsibility, municipal attribution, immunity, and damages are not adjudicated.

##### Investigative procedures and substantive liberty

**Holding and operative rule:** Mandatory directions governing an investigation do not, by themselves, create a substantive liberty or property entitlement protected by procedural due process. A claimant must identify a substantive interest and its deprivation, rather than only an official failure to perform an investigative step; the theory presented here does not do so.

**Authority:** The investigative-procedure portion of the Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices and controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Sandin requires attention to the substantive constraint a legal scheme actually creates, rather than a count of mandatory procedural words. The D.C. provisions invoked here require investigation, permit provision or securing of services, and condition emergency removal on findings that available services are insufficient and time to petition is lacking. The pleaded investigative omission does not establish the necessary preinjury finding commanding that protective disposition.

DeShaney supplies the rule against a general constitutional duty to prevent private violence outside custody. It does not mean that no noncustodial person can possess an independently created statutory entitlement. Pinder separates an omission from an official's own affirmative deprivation; the alleged failure to investigate does not establish affirmative obstruction of private protection or government placement in danger. Custody acquired after the injury cannot establish a preinjury custodial duty.

The comprehensive local scheme and the claimed special duty may matter to local tort liability. They do not convert every required investigation into a substantive federal constitutional benefit. The Court therefore affirms only rejection of this investigative-procedure theory. It does not decide the different constitutional objection to loss of the asserted local tort remedy through §12-309 in light of infancy, incapacity, and District custody. The pending certified notice questions must not be answered by assuming that remedy exists or is adequate.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Blessing v. Freestone:** Applied to require an individual entitlement in addition to a binding administrative obligation; its separate §1983 foreclosure inquiry remains intact.

#### Holdings

All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding and operative rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Precedent treatment:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding and operative rule:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Precedent treatment:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

##### III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding and operative rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

##### IV. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reinstate that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Precedent treatment:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

#### Chronology and Posture

| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part I only | Reverses recall and restores the prior mandate | Part I: review channel, first-application record and temporal law; Parts II–III: recall standard, gateway applications and remedy |

#### Holdings

##### The recall judgment is reviewable through the ordinary channel

**Controlling proposition:** Felker’s §2244(b)(3)(E) restriction on review of permission to file a successive district-court application does not bar ordinary review of this separate mandate-recall judgment confined to the first application’s claims and evidence. Under Lindh and Carriger, the identified amended §2254 merits and replacement factual-presumption provisions do not govern this capital application filed in January 1990, outside established chapter 154 qualification; other statutory provisions retain their own temporal and procedural predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor’s opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the legal channel must match the action reviewed. Felker concerns a distinct authorization decision; the rejection of Thompson’s separate July motion does not settle the court-initiated recall. Its limitation to the first application excludes Leitch’s later parole account. Calling action court-initiated would not permit a disguised successive application to evade its governing statute, but that basis is not established here.

Lindh’s provision-specific temporal construction, as Carriger applies it to already pending capital applications, preserves the former merits and factual-finding rules here. No California chapter 154 qualification is established. This conclusion does not exempt every later filing from AEDPA or decide a hearing provision’s independent temporal reach.

##### Completed habeas merits cannot be reopened on ordinary reconsideration alone

**Controlling proposition:** When recall reopens the completed merits of a state prisoner’s habeas proceeding, rather than correcting a genuinely different defect in judicial process, exceptional recall requires the applicable miscarriage-of-justice showing: Schlup’s crime-innocence gateway for an attack on a conviction, or Sawyer’s distinct gateway for an eligibility-only attack. A clerical correction, fraud, a mandate that does not express the court’s judgment or another genuinely different process defect remains distinct; capital punishment or disagreement with the prior merits result alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, including the author. These five directly adopt this rule, its application and the remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the circuit’s action reopened completed merits and abused its exceptional recall discretion. Hazel-Atlas recognizes protection of the judicial process on its demonstrated fraud predicate, while Ohio Power illustrates exceptional authority over the Court’s own procedure. Neither makes every declined rehearing perpetually reopenable. Carlisle forbids using inherent power to bypass actual governing constraints; Lonchar protects required process without creating a right to en banc review.

The January notice and actual March request and rejection allowed institutional attention before the June mandate. The problem was not first discovered immediately before execution. The separate June initiative confirms that the full court acted independently of the prisoner’s later motion, but it neither suspended nor recalled the mandate. The ensuing delay, while the State proceeded in reliance on finality, is not justified merely by invoking the stated wait for state proceedings.

Most decisively, the August action restored rape-related relief and reopened murder review. It changed the completed substantive adjudication. No finding of dishonest purpose, universal judicial awareness or unlawful March rejection is necessary. The Court therefore extends the appropriate innocence gateway to this category of merits recall, giving effect to finality and comity while retaining genuine process correction. It does not hold that Schlup or Sawyer already governed every recall or that every court-initiated recall is a successive district-court application.

##### The augmented first-application record meets neither distinct gateway

**Controlling proposition:** A conviction attack under Schlup requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record showing that, more likely than not, no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict beyond a reasonable doubt. An eligibility-only attack under Sawyer requires clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the necessary rape special circumstance and other state-law eligibility predicates; Thompson’s first-application record satisfies neither showing.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Parts II–III coalition directly adopts both component-specific applications. No fractured-decision synthesis is involved.

It further weakens testimony already substantially impeached at trial; it is not disregarded merely because it impeaches. But the added information does not require every reasonable juror to reject the accounts in light of the remaining evidence.

Root disputes the handcuff inference, bruising chronology and sparse sperm. His opinions provide no alternative cause for the crushing injury, select neither douching nor low sperm count, and do not establish consent. Semen supports intercourse rather than force by itself. Restraint evidence, altered clothing, gagging, remaining confession accounts and Thompson’s inconsistent explanations nevertheless permit a reasonable adverse conclusion on the augmented record. Conflicting accounts of which wrist was injured support no inference. The excluded later Leitch account does not enter this assessment.

A prior finding cannot conclusively resolve evidence it never assessed.

Evidence may be new relative to trial without postdating the mandate; no AEDPA due-diligence condition is imported into Schlup. ### Freedom From Religion Foundation, Inc. v. City of Marshfield

Merits decision, February 27, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on the merits. On review of the Seventh Circuit's judgment, 203 F.3d 487, vacating summary judgment for the City and Fund and remanding for corrective proceedings. The question is whether a genuine sale of a religious statue and its parcel ends municipal sponsorship when the parcel remains dedicated to public use and integrated into the surrounding public park. The City and Fund defend their transfer; the challengers seek effective separation of the private display from the City's presentation. The parties stipulated undisputed material facts and the appropriateness of summary judgment. The Fund maintains its parcel and statue and pays its lighting charge; the City separated that charge from park street lighting. The sale complied with state requirements and conveyed the parcel at fair market value, although the City sought no competing bids. Plaintiffs asserted no attempted enforcement of the public-park covenant.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, addressing attribution and corrective relief. Justice Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas; they would reverse and restore defense summary judgment because private operation defeats present municipal attribution on the stipulated record.

#### Holdings

##### I. A genuine transfer must end the City's continuing religious function

**Holding and operative rule:** A bona fide sale ends public ownership but does not cure an establishment violation if the municipality continues acts that present the religious message as its own. The City's continued operation of its surrounding park as the undifferentiated setting for its historical religious centerpiece, together with the retained public-park dedication and ineffective separation, preserves that municipal presentation here despite genuine private ownership, maintenance and lighting.

**Authority:** The six-Justice opinion of the Court identified above directly adopts both this application and the remedy in Holding II.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini requires identification of the governmental act and its function. It retains Lemon through a unified inquiry: governing specific prohibitions and distinct doctrines apply first, established permissible relationships retain their scope, and residual questions turn on actual attribution and function. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence within that inquiry; independent former prongs do not supply a different test.

The act here is the City's continuing presentation of its own park as an integrated setting for this religious centerpiece. The parties' stipulation establishes that the Fund, not the City, maintains and lights its parcel. The fair-price, lawful sale is real; the public-use covenant has not been shown to have been enforced. Those facts materially favor private attribution. They do not resolve the separate significance of the City's continued surrounding presentation, retained park dedication and lack of intelligible differentiation. No single feature, including the covenant, establishes the violation by itself.

Pinette and Chabad protect genuinely private expression and reject attribution from religious content or location alone. This centered municipal setting has not been established as their generally available forum. Robinson distinguishes the City's own institutional presentation from private expression; Brentwood likewise demands actual governmental responsibility rather than a label. The Court identifies continuing City conduct, not an observer's offense, dislike or possible mistake. The dissent gives the private-operation facts dispositive force; that overlooks the public function that the City retains around the transferred parcel. The genuine transfer therefore does not moot the challenge to that remaining function.

##### II. Corrective relief must end municipal sponsorship while preserving private expression

**Holding and operative rule:** The remand must secure effective cessation of the identified municipal religious presentation through operational and intelligible separation, using clarification or boundary measures on land the City controls or another lawful means. Relief must preserve the genuine conveyance and the Fund's private expression and property, and cannot require destruction, relocation or alteration of private speech merely to prevent possible misunderstanding.

**Authority:** The same six Justices directly join the Court's remedial holding and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini ties the cure to the governmental function that violates the Constitution; Pinette protects the private religious expression that remains outside that function. The existing disclaimer is on Fund property and the record establishes no completed separation. The lower court may require accurate boundaries and clear attribution on municipal land, but cannot assume power to install a replacement sign on the Fund's parcel or compel the Fund to change its message. A disclaimer succeeds only when the City's actual presentation supports it.

The final design, placement and lawful treatment of the retained-use arrangement require site and property details for implementation. Those are remedial tasks under the announced rule, not a reopening of the stipulated private maintenance and lighting. No particular fence, cancellation of the covenant, physical removal, relocation or destruction is necessary as a categorical condition. An equally effective lawful separation may suffice. The Court affirms corrective proceedings under that standard rather than endorsing the lower court's independent endorsement prong or a universal two-condition rule for all divestitures.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton: applies the unified governmental-act and function inquiry to an unresolved monument divestiture; its distinct doctrines and established permissible relationships retain their actual scope.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman: remains governing as modified by Agostini; its former independent prongs are not revived.
- Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette: preserves genuine private religious access and limits the cure; neither categorical forum immunity nor attribution from proximity follows.
- Chabad-Lubavitch of Georgia v. Miller: preserves contextual private-expression protection; interior or adjacent public location alone is insufficient.
- Robinson v. City of Edmond: applies the distinction between official institutional presentation and private expression without converting its seal holding into a categorical monument rule.
- Brentwood Academy: reinforces examination of actual governmental responsibility; the athletic-association application supplies no automatic rule for adjoining private land.

#### Law After Decision

Effective February 27, 2001, the unified Establishment Clause inquiry has a controlling divestiture application: formal and operational private ownership cannot alone cure continuing municipal presentation of a religious message, while proximity, religious content or possible confusion alone cannot establish that presentation. Relief must end the proved governmental function through lawful, effective separation and preserve private expression. No general sale, wall, disclaimer or removal prerequisite applies to every monument.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** The genuine conveyance, actual private maintenance and lighting, and absence of asserted covenant enforcement establish private operation on this stipulated record. Pinette and Chabad protect that expression; Agostini and Brentwood require a governmental act, not an association drawn from visual continuity or ordinary public access. Robinson concerned an actual official emblem, which this privately operated parcel is not. The dissent regards the surrounding public park's continued operation as insufficient to turn the Fund's message into the City's. It would reverse and restore defense summary judgment without a new factual hearing or disclaimer prerequisite. Its conclusion does not protect a future sham transfer, actual governmental adoption or enforcement directing religious operation.

#### Source Notes

The [Seventh Circuit opinion](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/203/203.F3d.487.99-1639.html), 203 F.3d 487, supplies the sale, covenant, stipulated operations, surrounding setting and judgment below. Its No. 99-1639 is the lower-court docket. The existing disclaimer stands on Fund land; the opinion's remedial discussion does not establish municipal authority to install a new sign there. The decree addresses City-controlled presentation and leaves lawful site implementation to the lower court.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Seventh Circuit's vacatur of defense summary judgment and corrective remand stand. The lower court must require effective cessation of the City's continuing religious presentation through lawful separation while preserving the genuine conveyance and the Fund's private speech and property. The stipulated private upkeep facts remain fixed; the lower court determines the site and property details needed for implementation. No particular fence, private-land sign, covenant cancellation, removal, relocation or destruction is ordered.

## OT_2000CHUNK7.md ? United States v. Mead

### United States v. Mead Corp., No. 99-1434

Merits decision, June 18, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 8, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit. The questions concern the judicial weight of Customs tariff-classification ruling letters and whether the bound-diary provision includes these ring-bound day planners. The Federal Circuit reversed judgment for the United States, rejected both controlling and persuasive weight and held the planners outside the challenged dutiable classification. The January 1993 headquarters letter changed earlier duty-free treatment and offered dictionary and commercial reasons.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela and Scalia.

The remand requires independent tariff construction with persuasive consideration of Customs' explanation. It directs no particular classification or monetary amount. The two dissenters would instead reverse and sustain the classification, on distinct grounds.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and II | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supplies controlling interpretive rules | No controlling deference for this mechanism; independent judicial construction with Skidmore consideration |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacates and remands | Corrected classification inquiry without a predetermined tariff answer |
| Concurrence in Parts I and II and dissent from the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse and sustain the classification | Independent reading of bound diary; remand only for implementation |
| Dissent | Scalia | No other Justice | Would reverse and sustain the classification | Controlling reasonable-choice treatment for the official agency interpretation |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding and operative rule:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Authority for Holdings I and II:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion have the eight direct supporters identified in the opinion table, including its author. Scalia does not join either proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the power to administer and classify does not make every administrative interpretation controlling law for a reviewing court. Haggar requires actual delegated authority and statutory fit before acceptance of a reasonable choice within genuine ambiguity. Its formally promulgated Customs regulation does not establish the same authority for the different mechanism here. Christensen distinguishes controlling authority from an advisory interpretation's useful reasoning without deciding that all informal actions have the same status.

Customs letters guide particular transactions, may be modified, issue from numerous offices and do not bind unrelated importers as a generally applicable regulation would. Their place in the protest and judicial-review scheme confirms that administrative operation is distinct from delegated interpretive lawmaking. Formal rulemaking or adjudication can be important evidence of delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite. Independent judicial factfinding likewise does not itself defeat lawful deference; Haggar remains intact.

The Solicitor General's brief, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, represents this as Customs' official position. The Court accepts that representation. It identifies whose interpretation is defended, but does not establish that Congress gave this ruling mechanism controlling legal effect. Headquarters' developed reasons and the responsible institution's endorsement strengthen the claim to consideration, not the statutory delegation. The 1993 action remains governed by its own legal regime; later procedures for changing rulings do not supply retrospective authority. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations are outside this grant.

##### II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding and operative rule:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an agency can offer useful reasons without possessing authority to bind the court's answer. Skidmore provides that separate inquiry, and Christensen preserves it when controlling statutory deference is unavailable. Customs addressed dictionary meaning, commercial usage and whether a ring mechanism binds pages. Those reasons bear on the actual tariff language and cannot be discarded by attaching a nonregulatory label to the letter.

Mead correctly points to the change from earlier duty-free treatment. That change may weaken a consistency claim; it does not eliminate every reason the agency offers. Expertise, national responsibility and official endorsement likewise do not automatically make the position persuasive. The court evaluates the explanation on its merits within the enacted tariff boundaries. Neither the fact that Customs changed course nor the fact that it administers a complex scheme decides the tariff question in advance. The distinction preserves meaningful independent judicial interpretation rather than converting persuasion into a lesser form of mandatory deference.

##### III. The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with the persuasive consideration required by Holding II. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Authority:** Part III has the seven direct supporters identified in the opinion table, including Kennedy as author. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appellate judgment cannot stand on an inquiry that wholly excluded the permissible persuasive contribution of Customs' interpretation. The goods are described as ring-bound planners containing calendar and memorandum pages; no missing product fact supplies the reason for return. Nor is there a general entitlement to have a lower court decide every interpretive question first. The complete tariff question is presented, but neither proposed definitive construction commands a majority: independent construction sustaining the category and controlling acceptance of Customs' reasonable construction rest on distinct noncontrolling grounds. The seven-Justice disposition instead requires the corrected judicial inquiry without predetermining its answer.

The arguments remain concrete. Mead contends that removable pages and prospective scheduling are outside bound diaries. Customs answers that rings bind pages and that diaries include dated prospective entries. The reviewing court must decide the statutory meaning, consider the actual agency reasons and implement the resulting classification through ordinary tariff procedures. The disputed tariff meaning is genuinely undecided here for want of a majority construction; assessment calculations following the eventual lawful classification are ordinary implementation, not a second unresolved deference rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

#### Law After Decision

Materially equivalent Customs ruling letters do not command controlling Chevron treatment through this mechanism. Courts must independently interpret the tariff while giving the agency's actual reasons the persuasive consideration they earn. Other genuine delegations, including valid regulations governed by Haggar, retain their own authority. Official endorsement does not collapse the delegation and persuasion questions. Neither ultimate classification of these planners commands a majority; the governing judicial inquiry is settled, and the tariff answer returns for decision under it.

#### Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins Parts I and II but dissents from the judgment. He would independently classify the goods under the tariff's ordinary words. A diary may organize prospective appointments and daily affairs, rather than only retrospective narrative. Rings fasten pages into a bound volume despite removability; neither sewing, glue nor permanence is required. Calendar, note and appointment pages satisfy that description, and auxiliary telephone pages do not defeat it. Loose unattached sheets remain different from the assembled imported articles. Customs' dictionary and commercial reasons persuade but do not control. The narrower appellate reading adds restrictions absent from the text. With both the question and undisputed product features before the Court, he would sustain the bound-diary classification and remand only for entry-specific judgment, assessment or refund implementation, without fixing an amount.

Scalia dissents on a different ground. Congress's classification assignment, genuine tariff ambiguity and Customs' publicly confirmed official interpretation warrant controlling reasonable-choice treatment in his view. The Solicitor General's brief cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel establishes the official-position condition; the statutory scheme supplies delegation, not those signatures alone. Ring binding and dated calendar and note pages make the classification reasonable despite the residual category. He objects to the Court's less determinate delegation threshold and its substitution of open persuasive review, while declining to treat every employee opinion as authoritative. He would sustain Customs' classification and leave only ordinary assessment implementation. His controlling-deference ground does not join Stone-Zsela's independent textual construction or either Court deference proposition.

The agency mechanism, merchandise and public representation of Customs' official position are reported in [United States v. The Federal Circuit must reconsider the tariff classification through independent statutory construction that evaluates the actual persuasive force of Customs' explanation. ### A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc.

Merits decision, April 23, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On full merits submission. Review of the Ninth Circuit judgment, 239 F.3d 1004, entered February 12, 2001, concerning the preliminary injunction entered July 26, 2000 and explained and slightly modified August 10, 2000. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. The questions concern the supported direct-infringement and secondary-liability predicates, sampling and space-shifting defenses, the Audio Home Recording Act and §512, the remaining owner-conduct and procedural objections, and the scope of preliminary relief. Napster's searchable directory facilitates music-file exchange; notices and account/index controls are established, but its servers do not possess users' MP3 contents. The review concerns the original restraint and its required correction, not a later replacement order.

#### Judgment

**The Ninth Circuit's mixed preliminary disposition is affirmed, and the case is remanded, 9–0.**

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supported preliminary infringement and secondary-liability predicates; asserted defenses and objections | Sustained, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Findings remain preliminary; the $5 million security is sustained. |
| Scope of the original preliminary injunction | Requirement to replace the overbroad restraint affirmed; remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | The original July 26, 2000 restraint remains stayed until modification conforming to this judgment. |

The owners must supply usable identification of works and infringing files. The replacement order must fit the service's actual capabilities and applicable statutory limits. Further proceedings retain the distinction between preliminary findings and final liability.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The preliminary fair-use defenses fail under the integrated factors

**Holding and operative rule:** On this supported preliminary record, unrestricted exchange of complete protected creative recordings with unrelated users is unlikely to be fair use under §107. Nontransformative whole-work copying and supported substitution for existing and developing markets outweigh the claimed sampling or relocation purposes; artist-authorized and otherwise lawful files remain lawful.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight other Justices. All nine directly support each of Parts I–VII, including the independent contributory and vicarious grounds and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Campbell requires purpose, nature, amount and market effect together, without a dispositive commerciality presumption. The findings include displaced college sales and injury to a developing licensed-download market; this is not merely loss of fees for every unlicensed use. The contrary survey was weighed, not excluded. A plan to buy music later does not erase the present redistribution. Sony's private home time shifting does not establish immunity for public exchanges, and the claimed relocation extends beyond one's private personal devices. The result holds even assuming the owners bear the preliminary burden of likely defeating fair use; the allocation of that burden across all preliminary cases is unnecessary to resolve. The prima facie direct-infringement showing was unchallenged on this appeal; findings remain preliminary.

##### II. Substantial lawful capability does not immunize specific ongoing assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A service can incur contributory liability by knowingly providing material assistance to identified infringement, notwithstanding substantial noninfringing uses of its technology. Sony bars imputing the needed knowledge from capability alone; it does not immunize continuing materially assisting operation after specific knowledge.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The notices identified more than 12,000 infringing files, continued availability followed asserted account terminations, and the search and index functions materially assisted access. Those circumstances support likely knowledge and contribution. General knowledge that copyrighted music circulates does not establish unauthorized copying; lawful capability remains material and present infringement percentages are not an exclusive test. The rule requires no server access to MP3 contents the system does not possess.

##### III. Financial benefit and actual supervision independently support preliminary vicarious liability

**Holding and operative rule:** Vicarious liability separately requires a direct financial benefit from infringement and the right and practical ability to supervise it. Attraction of infringing recordings to the user base can satisfy the benefit element without direct transaction fees, while account exclusion and index control support supervision over the service's actual assistance; both elements are supported here at the preliminary stage.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** This is an independently sufficient preliminary ground, not a substitute for contributory knowledge. The architecture both supports limited control and restricts the remedy. A theoretical ability to close the entire service does not create a general duty to inspect inaccessible user files or guarantee prevention of unknown copying.

##### IV. The Audio Home Recording Act is confined to its defined coverage

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1008 does not immunize this general-purpose computer service's open redistribution merely because users characterize copying as noncommercial. Its consumer-recording protection depends on the defined devices, media and recording acts.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Under §1001(5), a digital musical recording is a material object containing, in digital form, only fixed sounds and related, relatively minor material, statements or instructions, from which the sounds and material can be perceived, reproduced or communicated directly or with a device. Entirely spoken-word recordings, including speech accompanied by merely incidental sounds, and objects containing computer programs are excluded; instructions constituting the fixed sounds and incidental material or bringing about their perception, reproduction or communication remain permitted. A digital audio recording device must be of a type commonly distributed to individuals for their use, whether separate or part of another machine, have a digital recording function designed or marketed primarily to make private digital audio copied recordings, and be capable of doing so. Professional model products and dictation, answering or other audio equipment designed and marketed primarily to fix nonmusical sounds are excluded. The service cannot detach the exemption from those conditions. This disposes of the asserted exemption without classifying every future dedicated player.

##### V. Section 512 independently limits liability and relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Secondary-liability status does not itself defeat a §512 protection. The lower court must determine each invoked service category and condition independently and apply the corresponding injunction limits before imposing relief beyond them; failure under one category does not decide another.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts Part V and the complete operative conditions below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the infringement theories and Congress's service protections perform separate work. Supported likely liability does not answer whether a particular transmission or location-tool function satisfies its statutory conditions. Nor does failure under one category establish another's failure. The contrary demand for immediate unrestricted policing would make the statute's separate notice, knowledge, control and injunction limits disappear before the necessary findings. The preliminary record permits continued consideration of fitted relief, while the court must establish the applicable classification and predicates before imposing relief exceeding a statutory protection. This is affirmative remand work under the governing rules, not a decision that every safe harbor is unavailable.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no content modification. Any intermediate copy must not be maintained in a manner ordinarily accessible to anyone other than anticipated recipients, and must not remain ordinarily accessible to those recipients longer than reasonably necessary for transmission, routing or connection. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption and reasonable implementation of a policy terminating repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances, with subscribers and account holders informed of that policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized to act for the owner of an allegedly infringed exclusive right. Under §512(c)(3)(B), incorporated by (d)(3), a notice that fails substantial compliance may not be considered in determining statutory actual knowledge or awareness of facts making infringement apparent. When a notice delivered to the designated agent substantially supplies the work, material or link location, and contact information but fails other elements, that exclusion applies only if the provider promptly attempts to contact the sender or takes other reasonable steps to assist receipt of a notice substantially satisfying all required elements. The preliminary notices support the underlying knowledge issue, but final satisfaction of each formal notice condition, service category and policy requirement remains for determination on remand.

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect. These limitations must govern if their predicates are satisfied; an unresolved classification is not permission to evade them through a generalized policing command.

##### VI. The remaining defenses and preliminary objections do not defeat fitted relief

**Holding and operative rule:** On this preliminary record, the owners' exploration of MP3 businesses does not establish intentional abandonment or permission for this service, and enforcing reproduction and distribution rights does not establish misuse extending copyright to unrelated property. No demonstrated abuse of discretion requires more discovery or an evidentiary hearing, the $5 million security is supported against the competing evidence, and no statutory basis or special circumstance requires a compulsory-royalty substitute.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The defense elements and record-specific review standard govern separately. Express objection to the service defeats the asserted inference of permission; toleration or business exploration alone is neither abandonment nor a license. Security need not equal the claimed enterprise value merely because that value is asserted. Copyright's remedies cannot be replaced with a new license solely for technological novelty. First Amendment interests protect lawful files and independent expression through the remedy's scope, without immunizing the identified infringement. Later materially supported defenses and security evidence remain matters for their own proceedings.

##### VII. The replacement injunction must connect usable notice to feasible service action

**Holding and operative rule:** Owners must identify protected works and sufficiently locate infringing files for practical response, and Napster must act within its actual index and account capabilities, including reasonable action against identified repeat infringers. The decree must preserve lawful files and authorized exchange, satisfy the remaining equitable predicates and any applicable §512 limits, and cannot make Napster guarantee prevention of every unidentified infringement.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The original order shifted the full identification/prevention burden onto the service and exceeded the supported controls. Specific knowledge and practical contribution justify effective relief at their proved scope; they do not justify impossible filtering or a blanket technology prohibition. Remand is for a conforming preliminary decree under these rules. The original July 26, 2000 order remains stayed until conformity; neither a later replacement order nor final liability is assumed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.:** The integrated four-factor inquiry governs; commerciality alone creates no dispositive presumption, and concrete substitution is distinct from mere loss of a fee for unlicensed use. The preliminary outcome does not require resolving the general burden allocation.
- **Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417:** Substantial noninfringing capability prevents imputed contributory liability from capability alone. Its private time-shifting application does not immunize public redistribution or specific ongoing assistance after knowledge.
- **New York Times Co. v. Tasini:** Its separation of actual statutory rights, use permissions and individualized remedy remains; it supplies neither a general digital-use exemption nor a compulsory license.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.:** Its demand for a remedy fitted to the proved conduct informs preservation of independent lawful expression; infringement does not justify restraints on unrelated lawful files.

#### Law After Decision

Specific knowing material assistance and separately established financial benefit with practical supervision can support preliminary secondary liability for an ongoing service despite substantial lawful capability. Fair use remains contextual; open whole-recording redistribution on this record receives no categorical sampling, relocation or device exemption. Congress's §512 categories and relief limits remain independently enforceable. Effective injunctive relief must connect usable identification to feasible service action while preserving lawful exchange.

#### Source Notes

[A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc., 239 F.3d 1004](https://openjurist.org/239/f3d/1004), including notes 3 and 7 and Parts VIII–X; 17 U.S.C. §§106–107, 502, 512, 1001, 1008; Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(c). The prima facie direct-infringement showing was not contested in this appeal; the competing survey was weighed rather than excluded. The known service architecture supplies index and account controls, not access to user-file contents.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower court must frame a replacement preliminary injunction under the stated notice, actual-capability, lawful-use, equity and §512 requirements. The original restraint remains stayed until conformity. Further proceedings retain the ordinary distinction between preliminary findings and final liability; this decision does not presume a replacement order already issued.

**End of entry: A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc., merits decision, April 23, 2002.**

## OT_2001CHUNK2.md ? National Cable

### National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co., Nos. 00-832 and 00-843

Merits decision, January 16, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 2, 2001. Review of the Eleventh Circuit's judgment setting aside the FCC's authority over mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications pole attachments. The questions are whether section 224's category-specific rate formulas restrict its general coverage, and whether qualifying wireless equipment falls outside the statute.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed cable-Internet attachments | Categorical jurisdictional exclusion reversed, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Souter, Thomas | Remanded; Souter and Thomas would vacate for agency classification and explanation |
| Wireless telecommunications attachments | Categorical exclusion reversed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Remanded under the wireless-coverage holding |

Further proceedings must recognize coverage while preserving every applicable statutory formula and any properly remaining classification or particular-rate objection. The judgment does not approve a particular attachment charge.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Souter and Thomas as to Part II only | Supports both reversals | Seven join Part I, mixed-service coverage; all nine join Part II, wireless coverage |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Thomas | Souter | Concurs in wireless reversal; would vacate the mixed-service component for agency explanation | Mandatory formula classification and reasoned statutory basis |

#### Holdings

##### I. Adding Internet service does not remove a qualifying cable-system attachment from the statute

**Holding and operative rule:** An attachment by a cable television system that satisfies 47 U.S.C. §224(a)(4) remains within the FCC's authority under §224(b) when it carries Internet service together with cable television. Sections 224(d) and (e) prescribe mandatory rates within their respective terms; they do not exhaust the broader attachment definition or permit the agency to evade an applicable formula.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion supplies seven votes for the full holding, with the joins stated above.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress defined the regulated attachment before prescribing methods for setting its rate. An attachment by a cable system does not cease to meet that definition when the system adds a service. The limitation to attachments used solely for cable service controls the cable formula's application; it does not rewrite the general definition. Section 224(b)'s direction to ensure just and reasonable rates, terms and conditions and its rulemaking grant therefore continue to govern covered attachments. Iowa Utilities Board supplies the distinction between an actual congressional assignment and the limits governing its exercise. The utilities' method objection deserves examination when a particular rate method is challenged, but does not establish a categorical absence of authority. Nor must the Commission resolve Internet classification to answer this coverage question: either possible classification leaves an attachment by a cable system within the general definition. Mandatory formulas remain mandatory when their conditions are met. Mead requires an actual delegation before an agency interpretation receives controlling force; the enacted text itself resolves this question, so no new deference rule is needed. The Eleventh Circuit's categorical exclusion cannot stand. Classification of Internet service and validity of a particular rate are outside the questions decided.

##### II. Qualifying wireless equipment is a covered attachment

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 224 covers an attachment by a provider offering telecommunications to the public for a fee, without excluding the provider's equipment because it serves wireless transmission. Both the attachment and its owner must satisfy the statutory definitions; the utility's wire-use condition does not impose a separate wire-only restriction on the attached provider's service.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of Kennedy's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The telecommunications-service definition addresses service offered for a fee regardless of the facilities used. The reference to wire communications in the utility definition identifies the covered owner and structure; it does not alter the separately defined service or exclude physical wireless equipment attached to that structure. Reading the definitions together covers both wireline attachments used by wireless providers and qualifying wireless equipment. The contrary construction relocates an owner-related condition into a service limitation Congress did not enact. Iowa Utilities Board's distinction between statutory eligibility and the duties governing eligible facilities supports preserving those separate functions. The statute's exclusions for railroads, cooperatively organized persons and federal or state ownership remain, as do the incumbent-local-exchange-carrier limitation in §224(a)(5) and §224(c)'s allocation to qualifying state regulation. The holding reaches qualifying poles, ducts, conduits and rights-of-way; it does not extend authority to every independent wireless tower or property outside those definitions. The wireless categorical exclusion is reversed, with lawful rates, terms and conditions governed by the applicable statutory provisions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board (January 25, 1999): applies its separation of congressionally conferred FCC authority from the independent limits on particular implementing rules; its local-competition holdings remain intact.
- United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001): preserves its actual-delegation requirement; the Court resolves section 224's coverage from its text without adding a deference holding.
- Permian Basin Area Rate Cases, 390 U.S. 747: the separate writing invokes reasoned ratemaking to require an agency explanation; that objection does not become a controlling ground for additional remand.

#### Law After Decision

Section 224's general coverage encompasses qualifying mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications attachments. A gap or dispute about an applicable rate formula does not itself create a jurisdictional exclusion. The statutory formulas, ownership and provider limits, and qualifying state-regulation allocation retain their force. These are new coverage holdings; no Internet-service classification or particular rate is approved.

#### Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by Souter, concurs in the wireless holding but would vacate the mixed-service portion for a reasoned agency determination. If cable-modem access is cable service, subsection (d)'s formula controls; if it is telecommunications service, subsection (e)'s formula controls. Only after resolving those possibilities can residual authority under subsection (b) support the particular regulatory choice. The service supplied by an Internet service provider and the transmission carrying customers to that provider are distinct classification questions. The Commission's treatment of one does not necessarily resolve the other. Permian Basin's demand for a reasoned rate method supports returning that choice to the agency. Probable ultimate jurisdiction and policy favoring broadband deployment cannot supply the missing statutory explanation. This position rejects categorical noncoverage but withholds approval of the existing unexplained agency determination.

#### Source Notes

47 U.S.C. §§153, 224(a)-(e); Gulf Power Co. v. FCC, 208 F.3d 1263, rehearing 226 F.3d 1220; Implementation of Section 703(e) of the Telecommunications Act of 1996, 13 FCC Rcd. 6777. The controversy concerns categorical attachment coverage; a particular attachment price is not adjudicated.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Eleventh Circuit for proceedings consistent with both coverage holdings, preserving properly remaining classification and rate-method objections under the applicable statutory commands.

**End of entry: National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co., merits decision, January 16, 2002.**

## OT_2002CHUNK5.md ? Connecticut Department

### Connecticut Department of Public Safety v. Doe, No. 01-1231

Merits decision, March 5, 2003

#### Chronology and Posture

538 U.S. 1. Argued November 13, 2002. On review of the Second Circuit's judgment, 271 F.3d 38, affirming a procedural-due-process injunction. The question is whether current-dangerousness process is required before the conviction-based public registration challenged by Doe.

All nine Justices participated at submission or argument and at decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Second Circuit's procedural-due-process judgment and the injunction requiring a current-dangerousness hearing for Doe's conviction-based inclusion are reversed. On remand the lower court must dissolve that hearing prerequisite and determine from the actual pleadings whether any independently pleaded and preserved claim remains. The expressly disavowed substantive-due-process challenge is not created on remand.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reversal on the procedural ground | Complete opinion; protected liberty interest assumed |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Stevens | — | Supports reversal without joining the Court's opinion | Affirmative liberty-and-punishment foundation |

#### Holdings

##### The requested dangerousness hearing concerns no legally material fact for Doe's inclusion

**Holding and operative rule:** Procedural due process does not require a hearing on current dangerousness for a registrant in Doe's position when an undisputed qualifying conviction authorizes the challenged inclusion and the State has made no operative individual risk finding. Even assuming a protected liberty interest, a claimant must identify a disputed fact material to the deprivation the governing law authorizes; procedure does not supply a substantive dangerousness exception to the applicable conviction-based rule.

**Authority:** The eight-Justice majority identified above directly supports Stone-Zsela's complete procedural rule and reversal. Stevens concurs in the judgment on his separate affirmative liberty-and-punishment ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Doe's proposed proof cannot alter the fact authorizing his inclusion. He does not dispute the qualifying conviction, and the criminal proceeding supplied an opportunity to contest it. The website does not claim that the State has individually determined every listed person currently dangerous. The substantial consequences of disclosure explain the claimed interest but do not make a legally unused fact material.

W.P. required an opportunity to contest risk classification and the notification plan because those determinations had substantive legal force in that regime. Its State risk-proof requirement cannot be transplanted here without changing the substantive basis for inclusion. Paul P. separately protects specified compiled nonpublic residential information through a sufficient relationship among information, audience and protective purpose. That safeguards claim retains its own inquiry; this procedural holding does not adjudicate it.

The same procedural defect remains whether liberty is assumed or, as Stevens concludes, affirmatively established through the burdens and punitive character of registration: the requested hearing addresses dangerousness rather than the operative conviction. Doe expressly disavowed substantive due process. That unpresented challenge cannot be supplied through a procedural demand. Adequate process for a genuinely disputed conviction or other actual inclusion criterion remains required under the governing law.

#### Precedent Treatment

- W.P. v. Verniero: distinguished because risk grading and the notification plan were legally operative conditions; its advance-process and proof holdings retain that scope.
- Paul P. v. Verniero: its independent protection of compiled nonpublic current residential information and fitted disclosure safeguards remains operative, with a separate claim and record required.
- Paul v. Davis: its reputation distinction remains effective; the Court assumes the asserted protected interest.

#### Law After Decision

For a registrant in Doe's position, procedural due process requires an opportunity to contest material authorizing facts, not current dangerousness when that fact cannot change the applicable conviction-based inclusion. W.P.'s protections for legally operative risk determinations and Paul P.'s independent residential-disclosure safeguards retain their separate force. The substantive-due-process claim was expressly disavowed and remains outside this decision.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** The substantial registration duties and official stigma burden liberty and, in his view, operate as punishment. For conviction-based burdens that may lawfully be imposed prospectively, a constitutionally adequate criminal trial supplies the process for establishing the conviction. Another hearing about current dangerousness is unnecessary because it would not change that predicate. Stevens therefore agrees that the procedural injunction must be reversed, but does not join an opinion that leaves the liberty-and-punishment foundation wholly assumed. His distinction between prospective punishment and retrospective punishment objections leaves an unpresented retrospective claim to its proper inquiry; it establishes neither Doe's offense date nor the validity of the entire statutory scheme.

#### Source Notes

Connecticut Department of Public Safety v. Doe, 271 F.3d 38 (2d Cir. 2001); No. 01-1231; Connecticut General Statutes §§54-250 through 54-261. Doe's qualifying conviction is undisputed. The website does not represent an individualized finding that every registrant is currently dangerous; substantive due process was expressly disavowed.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The lower court must remove the dangerousness-hearing prerequisite and implement the reversal, addressing only independent claims that the actual pleadings and preservation permit. Connecticut may implement the applicable conviction-based rule subject to independently governing law.

**End of entry: Connecticut Department of Public Safety v. Doe, Merits decision, March 5, 2003.**
