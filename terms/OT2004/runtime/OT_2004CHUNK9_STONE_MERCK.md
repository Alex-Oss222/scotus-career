## 81. Merck KGaA v. Integra Lifesciences I, Ltd.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the current request.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22; current review request, with revisions authorized in advance.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004.
* **Conditions:** Subject to intervening controlling decisions and the stated record and jurisdictional limits.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording changes consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning and scope; no alternative disposition authorized.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Federal Circuit's safe-harbor disposition and remand for application of the correct statutory construction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require review of the challenged research uses under the reasonable regulatory relationship standard, with any retrial or judgment adjustment determined by the preserved record. Eliminate infringement damages attributable to exempt uses if the defense is established; retain liability only for independently actionable uses.
* **Remedial Reach:** The parties and patented uses litigated in this action. No general license, industrywide injunction or extinguishment of Integra's patents follows.
* **Practical Relief:** The Federal Circuit must evaluate the actual evidentiary challenge under the corrected rule and direct the district court to adjust liability and damages accordingly. The remand cannot preserve an award resting on a legal exclusion of unsuccessful or unsubmitted experiments alone.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reasonable relation to the development and submission of regulatory information

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory construction and remand factual application where the evidence has not been evaluated under that construction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statute protects reasonably directed preclinical experiments even when their results are not submitted to FDA.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a text-based construction of the unresolved exemption that evaluates the reasonable relationship when the research is conducted.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate: the rule must cover the real sequence of regulatory development, including failed candidates. One step broader would exempt research based on a merely conceivable pharmaceutical use; one step narrower would make protection depend on successful outcomes the investigator cannot know in advance.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 271(e)(1) protects use of a patented compound when there is a reasonable basis to believe the candidate could be developed for an FDA-regulated drug and the experiment is directed to producing information of a kind relevant to that regulatory process. Neither failure of the candidate nor omission of the result from an eventual submission defeats the defense by itself; the party invoking the exemption must establish the required relationship for the challenged uses.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Preclinical studies linked to candidate development and regulatory information, including relevant efficacy, mechanism and safety work. Basic inquiry without an identified reasonable drug-development connection is not automatically exempt.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Assess the reasonable regulatory relationship when the use occurs. A failed candidate or unsubmitted result is eligible on the same terms as successful research; eventual success cannot supply a missing contemporaneous relationship.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute refers to development as well as submission. That distinction has work to do: development necessarily tests possibilities that may be rejected. Eli Lilly recognizes the statute's regulatory function, but patent rights remain enforceable where the challenged use lacks the required connection. The appellate construction risks collapsing development into the eventual contents of an application and must therefore be corrected before the evidence is judged.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Eli Lilly & Co. v. Medtronic is applied to the regulatory character of the exemption. No controlling decision is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A categorical rule for distinct research-tool patents lies outside the compound-research question presented. The experiment-specific application is ordinary factfinding on remand, not a reserved legal question.
