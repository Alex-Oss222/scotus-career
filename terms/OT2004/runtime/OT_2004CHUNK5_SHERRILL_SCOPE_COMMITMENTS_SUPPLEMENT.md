All eight Associates retain their property-tax affirmance and their rejection of a complete equitable bar on this supplied record. They also sustain the denial of the actual proposed amendment, Rule 56(f) discovery and preliminary acquisition injunction on the particular grounds below. The correction changes the component explanation and the boundaries of affirmance: independent suit-immunity judgments are preserved at their actual scope, not newly decided through the property-tax rule.

No established completed federal disposition or other congressional authorization makes these parcels taxable. The City's proposed complete equitable bar is independently considered and rejected for the current ownership, new assessments, requested prospective remedy and supported prejudice record. The unchanged original commitments supply the treaty, statutory, continuity and independent equity analysis; the six-defense examination below does not replace that analysis with a presumption that equitable defenses are unavailable.

## The actual proposed defenses

The lower court's categorical treatment of delay defenses is not adopted at that breadth. Oneida II did not adjudicate every possible equitable limit on a later remedy. The defense-specific conclusions below nevertheless permit affirming the amendment denial without remanding to add defenses that cannot defeat this claim on the supplied theories. The strongest additional formulations in the middle column are analytical alternatives under Engine section 14 where the source does not reproduce a full pleaded formulation; they are not representations that counsel filed those exact arguments.

These defenses remain distinct. The amendment ruling is sustained because the presented and strongest supported legal theories do not defeat the current claim, not because the defense labels are forbidden, because the City failed to make a nonexistent filing, or because the source was missing. The ordinary standard favoring amendment does not require adding a futile defense. Conversely, a different legally sufficient defense would require its own analysis; this ruling does not authorize courts to reject amendments merely because the claimant has invoked federal tribal rights.

## Independent discovery and preliminary-injunction grounds

The Rule 56(f) denial is sustained on the supplied showing: the City listed desired subjects but did not identify particular facts or documents, explain the expected material effect or adequately explain unsuccessful prior efforts, despite an opportunity to develop the issues and substantial submissions. The court considered the disputed historical and legal materials rather than treating them as conceded. No new adjudication establishes every historical proposition. The original legal analysis does not depend on an additional unidentified fact, and a request to investigate whether a contrary source might exist does not itself establish that the court abused its discretion. The Associates do not adopt every formulation of the circuit's discovery test as a new Supreme Court rule.

The preliminary acquisition injunction remains denied for the independent failure to demonstrate irreparable harm, as well as the City's failure on the selected property-tax theory. The preliminary findings about surpluses, services and exaggerated collapse have that limited procedural role. They do not permanently adjudicate municipal finances or exclude later relief on a materially different showing. Denial of this preliminary restraint does not declare that every future purchase is exempt or that all regulatory consequences of future purchases are lawful.

## Consequences of the strongest alternative paths

These conditional consequences are adversarial checks, not additional votes or proposed Court holdings. They demonstrate why a different property-tax answer would require a component-specific mandate.

1. **A legally established federal termination or taxable disposal.** Such a path would remove the adopted basis for the Nation's property-tax declaration and injunction, and for the appellate property-tax reasoning concerning eviction and the Members claims. It would not itself abrogate suit immunity, validate an earlier sale or order eviction. A mandate confined to the tax question could reverse the tax ruling while expressly leaving the independently supported counterclaim, eviction and official-capacity judgments undisturbed; any later effort to alter those judgments would require a properly presented basis. The separate Members sales-tax damages dismissal would remain outside review. No such federal predicate is established on the supplied record.

2. **A newly adopted complete equitable nonrevival bar.** This would require an express rule justifying the denial of present property-tax protection despite the retained instruments, including an explanation of why prospective relief is unavailable. It could defeat the Nation's current declaration and injunction without necessarily extinguishing the reservation or deciding every sovereign power. It would remove the lower court's categorical futility rationale for the corresponding laches defense; amendment would have to be allowed or treated as unnecessary only to the extent the new merits ruling itself resolves that defense. The other five defenses would not become valid by association. Independent suit-immunity judgments and the distinct sales-tax damages dismissal would survive the tax change; no enforcement permission follows automatically.

3. **Only a narrower equitable adjustment of relief.** If a Justice found a specific implementation prejudice warranting a narrower injunction, the tax rule and invalid-assessment consequences would remain unless that Justice also adopted a separate rule changing them. A remedial limitation could not silently make the assessments lawful or remove the independent immunity dispositions. The decree would have to provide the lawful effective relief justified by the right and the actual problem. No such unresolved implementation predicate is established here.

4. **A genuinely material discovery deficiency or an amendment that could succeed.** Such a finding could support a targeted procedural remand, but the missing modern research files, known defense names or desire to investigate general historical subjects would not establish it. The supplied lower-opinion record supports neither additional fact. Even on a City-favorable merits path, the independent failure to show irreparable harm would prevent automatic issuance of the acquisition injunction.

These distinctions preserve both the complete-resolution requirement and the authorized question's limits. They do not manufacture a litigation waiver, a party default or a new review gate. The additional source changes the accuracy of the reasons and mandate, while the eight individual property-tax and affected procedural commitments remain as stated above.
