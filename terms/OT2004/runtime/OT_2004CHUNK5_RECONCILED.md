## Governing law and the lower opinion

Actual Blessing, unanimously joined by the current Associates who sat, requires a provision-specific inquiry into an intended individual beneficiary, a mandatory obligation and a sufficiently definite right. A qualitative standard is not automatically unenforceable, and a broad prayer for relief does not erase a particular valid claim. Aggregate substantial compliance, staffing or computer-system goals do not themselves become individual rights. Once a right is established, section 1983 supplies the channel unless the defendant demonstrates an express exclusion or a comprehensive incompatible scheme. Oversight alone is insufficient.

Actual Suter denies the particular section 671(a)(15) reasonable-efforts and related reporting claims; it does not establish that every state-plan provision lacks personal force. Its treatment of Wright and Wilder, and its reservation of distinct provisions, prevent a categorical plan-heading rule. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove plan location alone as a reason to deny enforceability, create no independent right, and preserve Suter's result for section 671(a)(15). Actual Blessing already accounts for the governing legislative correction. No historical-predicate reconstruction is required to apply it here.

Actual Gonzaga recognizes the particular FERPA right. Stevens wrote for Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, restating Blessing's factors and the two inquiries; Breyer and Souter added their practical statutory analysis in concurrence; Thomas, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy dissented on the institution-policy/practice reading. The historical lower opinion's use of a blanket post-Gonzaga standard therefore cannot be treated as controlling current law. The actual positions still supply meaningful individual evidence: all require identification of the claimed statutory entitlement, and none turns remedy desirability alone into a right.

Rancho, completed March 22, unanimously separates existence of a right from availability of a parallel section 1983 remedy. Its foreclosure result rests on Congress's specific personal judicial action with a thirty-day filing period and expedited hearing and decision, which the additional action would bypass. It adopts no presumption from any express remedy and expressly distinguishes Bruneau's funding administration. Medicaid's individual hearing must be considered seriously alongside agency funding enforcement. Neither alone nor together establishes the concrete incompatibility shown in Rancho, or another identified congressional remedial restriction this action would evade. This is a comparison of these actual schemes, not a rule that administrative remedies can never foreclose or that only a judicial filing deadline can establish incompatibility.

Dotson's March 7 distinction between the legal effect of requested relief and a guaranteed outcome supports care about the present enforceability stage, but creates no Medicaid right or foreclosure rule. The other March 7 and March 22 public decisions were read and checked; their employment-discrimination, decisional-record, sentencing, voting, habeas and search holdings do not alter these Medicaid elements. Historical Sandoval's categorical rule does not replace the actual bounded Title VI action, and an implied Medicaid action need not be invented because section 1983 is the asserted statutory channel.

## Statutory qualifications retained

The operative section 1396d(a) definition concerns payment of part or all of the costs of covered care and services. A later services-or-payment formulation is not used. Section 1396a(a)(10)'s actual mandatory and optional categories, exclusions, financial eligibility requirements and incorporated conditions remain applicable. Pennsylvania has adopted the pertinent intermediate-care benefit and the plaintiffs' eligibility is accepted at this stage; this case does not require every State to adopt every optional benefit. The copied dated definition retains its actual coverage timing, including the third month before the application month and the distinct Medicare cost-sharing timing; neither is converted into a newly contested fact here.

Paragraph (15) identifies intermediate-care services other than in an institution for mental diseases, for persons determined under section 1396a(a)(31) to need them. That provision requires a written care plan before admission or authorization of benefits and regular independent professional review, including evaluation of continuing need. Section 1396d(d) requires the qualifying institution or distinct part, its primary health or rehabilitative purpose, the prescribed standards and active treatment for the payment sought. Its public-institution expenditure-maintenance condition expressly concerns quarters before January 1, 1975; it is not a new current eligibility obstacle.

These conditions operate at their actual statutory stages. They do not require an accepted eligible waiting-list claimant already to receive the treatment allegedly withheld before challenging denial or delay. Nor does an undeveloped detail establish that a claimant failed a care-plan or need-determination requirement. The complete script-copied statutory text controls all actual qualifications; the recognition of an individual right does not erase them.

Section 1396c affords notice and an opportunity for hearing to the state agency when the plan fails to comply or administration substantially fails to comply. The funding consequence continues until the Secretary is satisfied that the failure will no longer occur, with statutory discretion to limit withholding to affected categories or parts. That aggregate standard does not replace the individual promptness duty. Section 1396a(a)(3) separately affords a fair hearing to an individual whose assistance claim is denied or not acted on promptly. It is a personal remedy, not merely oversight, but its presence with section 1396c does not itself demonstrate exclusivity. Section 1983 supplies the express enforcement channel; its judicial-officer injunction qualification is not implicated by this action against the responsible administering official.
