# OT_2004CHUNK3

## 25. GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** GDF Realty Investments, Ltd. v. Norton
* **Citation or Docket:** 326 F.3d 622; rehearing denied, 362 F.3d 286. No Supreme Court docket identified.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the Fifth Circuit judgment sustaining the challenged application of the Endangered Species Act.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-24; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether Congress may prohibit takes of wholly intrastate, noncommercial endangered cave species caused by commercial land development because regulating those takes is an essential part of the Endangered Species Act’s broader regulatory scheme?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment for federal officials on an as-applied Commerce Clause challenge. It treated the relevant activity as species taking, rejected development alone as the answer, and sustained inclusion within the broader endangered-species scheme.
* **Relief Requested:** The landowners seek reversal and declaratory and injunctive protection against applying the taking prohibition to the cave species. Federal officials seek affirmance; neither side obtains a development permit merely by prevailing on constitutional power.
* **Material Record:** The properties near Austin contain habitat for six endangered subterranean invertebrate species found only in two Texas counties. Proposed commercial development would affect that habitat. The constitutional challenge concerns application of the taking prohibition, not a completed finding of unlawful take, an adjudicated compensation claim or entitlement to an incidental-take permit. The species’ lack of an independent commercial market is material to the landowners’ theory but does not establish the absence of every statutory-regime connection.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The owners allege a concrete restriction on proposed use of their land. Their challenge is to the prohibition’s application, not a generalized objection to conservation. Review proceeds on the identified appellate judgment after rehearing.
* **Relevant Dates:** Panel decision March 26, 2003; rehearing denied February 27, 2004. Scheduled review date: January 24, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton is already binding law concerning another wholly intrastate listed species. Gonzales v. Raich is scheduled later and supplies no entering rule for this event.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Gonzales v. Raich is prospective only. Its later placement cannot alter the law entering this January case.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** A federal appellate merits judgment is reviewable under 28 U.S.C. §1254. Constitutional authority is reviewed independently; summary judgment supplies no license to find disputed ecological facts.

##### Issue 1: Necessary inclusion within interstate wildlife regulation

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt and Rancho Viejo, LLC v. Norton sustain inclusion of unauthorized listed-wildlife takes as an integral, reasonably adapted means of preserving the populations and enforceability of the Act’s independently valid regulation of interstate exploitation, movement and commercial disposition. United States v. Lopez requires an eligible connection before aggregation. The project’s commercial character does not replace the enacted taking class.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enacted class, the valid interstate regulation and the genuine functional relationship between them. The court independently determines those boundaries and respects reasonable congressional judgments within them. No species-specific trading, crossing or independent substantial-effects showing is required. A merely nominal relationship, an invalid underlying interstate regime or conduct outside the covered class remains challengeable.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I, §8, Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses; 16 U.S.C. §§1538(a)(1)(B), 1532(19) and 1539. No intervening statutory amendment alters that rule.
* **Material Other Authority:** Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon separately governs actual habitat-caused injury, proximate causation and statutory liability. Those elements are not presumed from constitutional power.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering rule already reaches a listed single-state species within the same operative system. Commercial development, biodiversity value and speculative future uses cannot independently substitute for the required constitutional mechanism.
* **Open Point:** Whether these plaintiffs establish a material distinction from the already sustained statutory class or show a nominal or pretextual relationship; the abstract constitutional question is otherwise settled.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The owners argue that noncommercial local takes cannot be aggregated into commerce merely because other species are traded; they seek a rule requiring an independently economic class or a demonstrated species-specific interstate effect. The Government answers that removing the taking protection would impair an integrated interstate wildlife system and that Congress need not exempt isolated species. The Fifth Circuit’s treatment of the enacted class matters independently of its historical substantial-effects vocabulary. A generalized appeal to biodiversity cannot carry the Government’s burden under entering law, while a categorical noneconomic exclusion cannot carry the owners’ argument.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can affirm by applying the existing necessary-inclusion rule, leaving enforcement and permits to their ordinary statutory standards. A concrete showing that the challenged application falls outside that rule could require a limited remand or application-specific protection. A judgment accepting the owners’ categorical theory would require an express change to binding authority, rather than silent use of historical doctrine. Any jurisdictional defect would require disposition before merits.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** Affirmance can rest on the established listed-wildlife class and its functional relation to interstate regulation, governing analogous intrastate species without finding a take. A genuinely supported distinction could yield application-specific relief. Invalidating every intrastate species application would require overruling the existing class rule.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The full permit and development history is not needed for the preserved power issue. Neither the proposed event date nor the historical opinion establishes a present permit entitlement or actual prohibited injury.

[Fifth Circuit opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/326/622/526143/); [Endangered Species Act taking prohibition](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/16/1538); [statutory permits](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/16/1539).

## 26. Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Taft
* **Citation or Docket:** 353 F.3d 436 (Sixth Circuit); no Supreme Court docket identified.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of a judgment reversing declaratory and injunctive relief against Ohio’s revised abortion-method statute.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-01-24; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether Ohio’s prohibition on the defined partial-birth abortion procedure imposes an undue burden by reaching dilation-and-evacuation abortions despite its express exemption for that method? Whether the Act’s maternal-health exception is constitutionally adequate when a prohibited procedure may be medically safer than the available alternatives?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The district court invalidated the law. The Sixth Circuit reversed, treating the express D&E exclusion and the significant-health-risk exception as sufficient. A dissent disputed the exception’s practical treatment of comparative physical risks and grave mental-health conditions.
* **Relief Requested:** The providers seek reinstatement of effective declaratory and injunctive protection. Ohio seeks enforcement of the revised statute, with its D&E exclusion and statutory defenses.
* **Material Record:** Ohio revised its earlier statute after litigation. Ohio Rev. Code §2919.151 defines the prohibited conduct and expressly excludes D&E. Its exception turns on reasonable medical judgment and a medically diagnosed condition complicating pregnancy that threatens substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The medical record addresses comparative risks of intact and other methods. The scope of the exclusion, the availability of clinically suitable alternatives and the practical operation of the health defense require separate assessment. Expert evidence is not a finding that one method is always superior.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Providers challenge threatened enforcement and the effect on patients they treat. The new enactment differs from the statute addressed in Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich; that prior decision is governing law, not a disposition of this revised text. The case is a merits challenge, with ordinary preservation and severability limits.
* **Relevant Dates:** Panel decision December 17, 2003; rehearing denied April 1, 2004. The assigned event is January 24, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Stenberg v. Carhart concerned a different definition and a life-only defense. Tucson Woman’s Clinic v. Eden is scheduled later and concerns clinic regulations, inspections and privacy, not this method prohibition.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review the statute’s authoritative construction and constitutional standard independently; preserve supported clinical findings and ordinary review of disputed facts. Enjoin proved unconstitutional applications and dependent sanctions under Ohio severability law.

##### Issue 1: Scope of the method prohibition and complete access inquiry

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies the complete six-step rule. Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich rejects categorical invalidity merely because medical and legal labels overlap, while requiring examination of actual acts, fault and usable alternatives. Stenberg v. Carhart applies the same complete framework to a supported ordinary-D&E obstacle.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the enacted duty, exceptions and protected baseline; assess actual function and purpose; establish the affected population and attributable interference; independently invalidate baseline violations, obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles; require compelling justification and narrow tailoring for remaining severe interference, and substantiated justification, fit and answers to concrete less burdensome alternatives for remaining meaningful interference; protect clinical judgment and truthful communication; tailor relief and address dependent sanctions.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment; Ohio Rev. Code §2919.151, including its D&E exclusion. The revised text governs; the earlier judgment addressed a different enactment.
* **Material Other Authority:** The revised statutory definition controls coverage. A prosecutor’s assurance cannot replace an authoritative limiting construction or establish that all patients retain usable care.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An obstacle is sufficient but not necessary for invalidity. The State’s burdens arise after the challengers establish the duty, causation and triggering interference. Neither universal physician preference nor a numerical facial formula controls.
* **Open Point:** Whether the revised exclusion actually preserves ordinary D&E and whether the remaining prohibition creates severe or meaningful interference inadequately justified on this record.

##### Issue 2: Necessary physical and mental health care

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey protects timely necessary life-and-health care. Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich includes grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. Stenberg v. Carhart independently rejects compelled substitution when it creates supported serious health risk and the prohibited method is medically necessary for safe and suitable care.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A usable defense must address the medically supported risk imposed by the prohibition itself, not solely an independently diseased pregnancy. Competent disagreement does not erase supported serious-risk applications; unsupported preference does not establish necessity. No patient must await catastrophe.
* **Other Governing Law:** Ohio Rev. Code §2919.151(A), (B) and (C); Fourteenth Amendment. Criminal liability, licensing consequences and civil enforcement must not reintroduce an invalid exclusion.
* **Material Other Authority:** The revised health language and credited medical evidence are decisive. The historical dissent identifies disputes requiring examination, not judicially established findings under governing law.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law protects serious medically established physical and mental danger and necessary comparative risk reduction. It does not constitutionalize every marginal safety advantage.
* **Open Point:** Whether Ohio’s exception can authoritatively cover all required necessary-care applications, and which applications and sanctions require protection if it cannot.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Ohio relies on the express D&E exclusion, its reasonable-medical-judgment language and authority to distinguish significant danger from negligible comparative advantage. The providers answer that the defense can exclude serious risks caused by forced substitution and grave mental-health cases, and that formal availability of D&E does not establish suitability for all affected patients. The Sixth Circuit majority treated the enacted safeguards as adequate; the dissent disputed both the health construction and the treatment of the medical record. Neither a professional association’s preference nor legislative disapproval alone resolves the case. No separate transcript-verified concession is attributed.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A sufficiently protective authoritative construction, coupled with supported findings that remaining burdens satisfy the complete standard, could sustain the law. An established obstacle or necessary-care exclusion requires declaratory and injunctive relief at its proved scope. Incomplete findings or application of an obstacle-only rule support vacatur and a focused remand, with prompt adjudication of interim protection. Severability may preserve independent lawful applications, but may not permit dependent criminal or licensing sanctions to evade protection.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** One path preserves a precisely defined method restriction with genuinely usable necessary-care safeguards; another bars the demonstrated ordinary-D&E or serious-risk applications. Neither automatically resolves every abortion method, every patient preference or every other provision of Ohio law.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The record does not authorize new clinical findings. The exact interaction of the revised definition, affirmative defenses and medically supported subgroups must be addressed on remand.

#### E. SOURCES

[Sixth Circuit opinion and dissent](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/353/436/577145/), including the enacted text and reproduced district-court findings. The relevant historical enactment is quoted there; later versions of Ohio law are not substituted.

## 27. Smith v. Massachusetts

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Smith v. Massachusetts
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-8661; 543 U.S. 462 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Direct review of a state criminal judgment affirming conviction after reconsideration of an acquittal.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-02-22; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Double Jeopardy Clause permits a trial judge to reconsider an unqualified midtrial acquittal on one count after the defense has proceeded on the remaining charges.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Massachusetts Appeals Court affirmed. It treated reconsideration within the same jury trial as permissible and found no state procedural prohibition.
* **Relief Requested:** Smith seeks removal of the firearm conviction and its punishment. Massachusetts seeks affirmance without a new prosecution.
* **Material Record:** At the close of the prosecution’s case the judge granted a required finding of not guilty on firearm possession because the prosecution had not proved the statutory barrel-length element. Trial continued on other counts. Before closing arguments the prosecutor supplied authority that testimony identifying a pistol or revolver could suffice. The judge reversed the acquittal and submitted the restored count to the jury, which convicted. The distinction is between an actual completed insufficiency determination and an expressly provisional comment, not whether the original legal ruling was correct.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal double-jeopardy objection concerns renewed factfinding on a count already removed for evidentiary insufficiency. Final state review permits §1257 jurisdiction. Neither acquittal nomenclature nor its legal error controls the federal characterization.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument December 1, 2004; assigned merits event February 22, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the question presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Direct review of the constitutional issue, without the deference required by the Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996 (AEDPA). State law identifies the procedural setting; federal law determines the effect of a completed acquittal.

##### Issue 1: Finality of the completed insufficiency determination

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Price v. Vincent holds that a completed substantive determination of insufficiency is an acquittal regardless of label or legal error; neither signature nor announcement to the jury is an independent prerequisite. The whole trial episode distinguishes completion from a tentative remark.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the actual ruling and whether it completed the court’s insufficiency determination. If it did, further factfinding on guilt is barred. A provisional statement must be genuinely provisional in its context, not recast after the defense proceeds.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments; Massachusetts Rule of Criminal Procedure 25(a) and the contemporaneous reconsideration rules.
* **Material Other Authority:** Smalis v. Pennsylvania and United States v. Martin Linen Supply Co. supply the surviving acquittal and renewed-factfinding principles. An erroneous sufficiency ruling retains its acquittal character.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The binding rule already rejects formal-label and signature requirements. It does not make every preliminary evidentiary observation final or decide every expressly established reconsideration procedure.
* **Open Point:** Whether this facially unconditional Rule 25 ruling was completed and whether any preexisting express reconsideration procedure made it provisional.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Smith argues that a merits acquittal ends exposure on that count irrespective of error, and that continuing the defense after the ruling makes retroactive reopening especially objectionable. Massachusetts argues that the same jury remained empaneled, no second trial occurred and ordinary reconsideration could correct a legal mistake. The court below adopted the within-one-trial distinction. The material limit to Smith’s position is that genuinely tentative discussion is not an acquittal; the State must identify an actual basis for provisional status, not simply the judge’s later change of mind. No independent amicus rule is necessary to the issue.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A completed acquittal requires reversal of the restored-count conviction and forecloses retrial on that count. A genuinely provisional ruling could permit continued adjudication, leaving the conviction subject to other preserved objections. The unaffected convictions and lawful aggregate sentencing consequences require separate state-law treatment.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A completion-based rule reaches erroneous midtrial acquittals without freezing every interlocutory observation. A rule allowing reconsideration under an expressly applicable preexisting procedure would require facts and law establishing that distinct posture.
* **Material Uncertainty:** No contemporaneous express procedure making this Rule 25 acquittal provisional is identified. The record supplies no basis for disturbing the merits of the independent counts.

#### E. SOURCES

[Judicial opinion, trial account and Massachusetts rules](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/543/462/); [official OT2004 argument calendar](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcript/2004).

## 28. Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co.

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Stewart v. Dutra Construction Co.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 03-814; 543 U.S. 481 (historical reporter citation).
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of summary judgments concerning Jones Act and LHWCA remedies.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-02-22; historical decision-calendar date
* **Question Presented:** Whether a dredge that is practically capable of transporting equipment and workers over water is a vessel under the Longshore and Harbor Workers’ Compensation Act, although transportation is not its primary purpose and it was stationary during the injury.
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The First Circuit treated the Super Scoop as outside the Jones Act vessel requirement under its primary-purpose and actual-navigation approach. In the alternative LHWCA action, it accepted vessel status as conceded but found the alleged negligence to be employer negligence rather than vessel-owner negligence.
* **Relief Requested:** Stewart seeks reversal and a meaningful opportunity to pursue the applicable maritime negligence remedy. Dutra seeks to preserve summary judgment.
* **Material Record:** The Super Scoop is a large floating dredge used on Boston Harbor construction. It moves long distances by tug and short distances through anchors and cables, transporting its equipment and workers while operating. Stewart maintained its mechanical systems. He was injured while working on an adjacent scow when the dredge and scow moved relative to one another. Temporary idleness for repair was not permanent withdrawal from transport. Vessel status, seaman status, negligent conduct and the owner-employer distinction are separate elements.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case presents a legal classification on summary judgment. Dutra’s litigation concession concerning §905(b) does not determine the statutory definition across the integrated scheme. The actual source of negligence remains relevant to the proper remedy.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument November 1, 2004; event February 22, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None material to the question presented.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review statutory construction and summary judgment independently. LHWCA compensation and Jones Act seaman remedies use distinct coverage predicates; double recovery is not authorized.

##### Issue 1: Practical water-transport capacity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni preserves the independent Jones Act status inquiry despite occupational labeling or voluntary LHWCA benefits. Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis requires a substantial employment connection, in duration and nature, to a vessel in navigation and contribution to its function. Harbor Tug & Barge Co. v. Papai supplies the identifiable-fleet boundary.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish the object’s vessel status from the worker’s substantial connection and the defendant’s alleged capacity. Temporary repair or a momentary stationary condition does not itself remove a craft from navigation; permanent withdrawal and major reconstruction may do so on a sufficient record.
* **Other Governing Law:** 1 U.S.C. §3; the then-current Jones Act, 46 U.S.C. App. §688; 33 U.S.C. §§902(3)(G) and 905(b). The Dictionary Act defines vessel through use or capability as a means of transportation on water.
* **Material Other Authority:** The enacted default definition supplies the issue not fully answered by the worker-connection precedents. Neither a judicial primary-business requirement nor self-propulsion appears in that definition.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Entering law establishes worker status and temporary-repair principles. It does not yet resolve the proposed primary-purpose restriction for this dredge.
* **Open Point:** Whether practical transportation capability, rather than primary purpose or movement at the instant of injury, supplies the statutory vessel boundary.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Stewart relies on the statutory capability language and the dredge’s actual transport of crew and equipment. Dutra emphasizes its construction function, limited propulsion and stationary condition, while defending the independent employer-capacity ground. The First Circuit’s primary-purpose approach narrows both coverage and available remedies; Stewart’s strongest limiting principle is practical maritime transport, not flotation alone. The alternative negligence ground must be addressed without confusing vessel classification with liability. No transcript-derived concession is asserted here; the reported §905(b) concession is a procedural record fact.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting the primary-purpose test permits the worker’s status and proper negligence theory to be considered on remand. Sustaining that exclusion would leave the Jones Act claim barred but would not itself resolve the distinct LHWCA owner-capacity issue. A narrow disposition could correct the statutory vessel premise without deciding compensation, causation or damages.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** The practical-capability construction reaches dredges and similar working craft capable of waterborne transport. It does not extend vessel status to every floating platform or dispense with a substantial employment connection.
* **Material Uncertainty:** No finding of ultimate negligence or compensatory amount follows from the vessel definition. The lower court must identify the actual claims and remaining issues preserved after both summary-judgment stages.

#### E. SOURCES

[Judicial opinion and procedural record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/543/481/); [Dictionary Act vessel definition](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/1/3); [LHWCA definitions](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/33/902).

## 29. Karaha Bodas Co., L.L.C. v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara

### SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Karaha Bodas Co., L.L.C. v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara
* **Citation or Docket:** 364 F.3d 274 (Fifth Circuit); no Supreme Court docket identified.
* **October Term:** 2004
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the separate judgment recognizing and enforcing a Swiss arbitral award.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2005-02-22; scheduled merits date
* **Question Presented:** Whether Article V(1)(e) of the New York Convention permits a United States court to refuse enforcement of an award rendered in Switzerland because an Indonesian court annulled it, where the contracts selected Indonesian substantive law but Swiss procedural law governed the arbitration?
* **Question Granted:** The stated merits questions delimit review.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fifth Circuit affirmed enforcement. It distinguished the country whose arbitration law governed from the country whose substantive contract law applied and rejected the Indonesian annulment as a qualifying Article V(1)(e) defense.
* **Relief Requested:** Pertamina seeks refusal or vacatur of United States recognition based on the Indonesian annulment and its preserved Convention objections. Karaha Bodas seeks enforcement of the existing award.
* **Material Record:** The parties’ geothermal contracts selected arbitration in Geneva and Indonesian substantive law. The arbitral award ordered substantial payment to Karaha Bodas. Pertamina sought relief in Switzerland and later obtained an Indonesian annulment after the United States enforcement judgment. The issue concerns the legal authority of that annulment, not a de novo determination of contractual damages. The chronology of enforcement and foreign proceedings does not itself establish bad faith or a substantive defense.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** This is a distinct recognition appeal. The Court already decided the foreign anti-suit injunction dispute on March 9, 2004, reviewing 335 F.3d 357, and expressly left recognition, primary annulment jurisdiction and the effect of an actual annulment open. That decision neither bars this reserved appeal nor permits relitigation of the vacated injunction.
* **Relevant Dates:** Earlier anti-suit decision March 9, 2004; recognition opinion below March 23, 2004; scheduled review February 22, 2005.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The earlier Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara concerns coercive restraint of foreign proceedings. This matter concerns recognition after those proceedings; it is not a second brief of the old injunction.
* **Participation Matter:** None identified.

#### B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Law effective through June 30, 2004; no later effective decision is identified.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None identified as effective before this event.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None identified.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 9 U.S.C. §§203 and 207 provide the Convention enforcement channel. Article V defenses are bounded defenses to recognition, not general appellate review of the award. Any foreign-sovereign immunity or execution question remains distinct from award recognition.

##### Issue 1: Country of governing arbitral law under Article V(1)(e)

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Karaha Bodas Co., LLC v. Perusahaan Pertambangan Minyak Dan Gas Bumi Negara requires a concrete threat inadequately addressed by ordinary Convention tools before foreign anti-suit relief; ordinary competing proceedings and expense do not suffice. It expressly reserves this recognition issue and preserves Article VI’s permissive adjournment and security mechanisms.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Keep supervisory annulment authority distinct from secondary recognition jurisdiction. Apply the Convention defense actually established by the resisting party. The earlier anti-suit ruling does not determine which country qualifies as the primary annulment authority.
* **Other Governing Law:** New York Convention Articles V(1)(e) and VI; 9 U.S.C. §§201, 203 and 207; the parties’ actual choice-of-law and arbitration clauses.
* **Material Other Authority:** Article V(1)(e) refers to an award set aside by a competent authority of the country in which, or under the law of which, it was made. The dispute concerns whether the latter phrase identifies arbitral procedural law or also the chosen substantive contract law.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The earlier decision is controlling on anti-suit power and the availability of ordinary recognition tools. It deliberately does not allocate primary annulment authority.
* **Open Point:** Whether choosing Indonesian substantive law makes Indonesia a competent primary annulment forum despite the Swiss seat and procedural regime.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Pertamina argues that the Indonesian substantive-law clause brings the award within Article V(1)(e), and that respecting the annulment gives proper effect to the chosen law and international comity. Karaha Bodas answers that the selected Swiss arbitral procedure and seat identify the competent annulment forum; another country’s substantive law does not transfer supervisory jurisdiction. The Fifth Circuit adopted the latter construction and separately addressed other Convention defenses. Indonesia supported Pertamina’s position below, but no broader government or oral-argument concession is presumed. Ordinary merits disagreement is not a Convention defense.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting the Indonesian annulment defense supports affirmance of recognition, subject to any independently preserved defense. Accepting it would require deciding whether Article V authorizes or requires nonrecognition and the resulting disposition. A record defect concerning the selected arbitral regime could justify a limited remand. None of those paths reinstates the vacated anti-suit injunction or establishes execution rights against every asset.

* **Plausible Holding Scope:** A procedural-law construction gives a general rule for split substantive-law and seat clauses. It can leave the treatment of valid primary-country annulments open. A substantive-law construction would materially broaden the eligible annulment authorities and require express treatment of potentially conflicting primary decisions.
* **Material Uncertainty:** The question centers on Article V(1)(e); no new contract clause, foreign-law ruling or sovereign-immunity waiver is invented. The prior appeal must not be mislabeled as a prior recognition holding.

#### E. SOURCES

[Fifth Circuit recognition opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/364/274/532990/); [Convention implementation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/chapter-2); [UNCITRAL Convention text](https://uncitral.un.org/en/texts/arbitration/conventions/foreign_arbitral_awards).
