## Common disposition and rule

**Disposition coalition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the Federal Circuit's rejection of the statutory defense and remand for evaluation of the preserved verdict challenge under the correct construction of §271(e)(1). No Associate would direct judgment that all challenged experiments are exempt on the supplied trial record.

Section 271(e)(1) reaches preclinical uses reasonably related, when undertaken, to developing and submitting information under the covered federal drug-regulatory law. A qualifying use need not involve a generic drug, an already selected final candidate, a clinical trial, a successful experiment, or information ultimately submitted. The connection must be objectively reasonable and concrete: when the researcher has a reasonable basis to believe that a candidate may produce a relevant physiological effect and uses it in experiments capable of generating information of a kind pertinent to FDA review, abandonment or non-submission does not retrospectively eliminate the exemption. General scientific inquiry with only a speculative prospect that something learned might someday assist drug development does not qualify on that prospect alone. These are applications of the enacted reasonable-relationship requirement, not an additional mandatory sequence of research phases.

The exemption applies to the specified acts solely for qualifying uses. A commercial research objective does not itself defeat a qualifying regulatory use; conversely, the existence of a regulatory project does not immunize a portion of a challenged use lacking the required connection. Safety, efficacy, pharmacology and mechanism work can supply the requisite regulatory information; labels such as discovery, screening or preclinical work do not decide the question. All eight would accept that scope as necessary to answer the categorical restrictions in the appellate reasoning. The biotechnology exception for the specified animal-drug and veterinary products remains operative but is not implicated by these human-drug candidates.

The decisive statutory explanation is that development of regulatory information necessarily precedes submission and can fail to yield an eventual application. Requiring success or actual submission would replace reasonable relation with hindsight. FDA's use of preclinical information defeats the categorical premise that the agency has no pertinent interest before clinical testing. Integra's strongest answer is that early commercial drug discovery consumes the patent owner's principal market and that nearly any research can be described as helping a future application. That concern requires a genuine, use-specific regulatory connection and an evidentiary burden; it does not authorize deleting development or creating categorical restrictions absent from the text.

## Trial posture, remedy and final join test

The trial instruction, copied exactly into the fresh neutral handoff, required Merck to prove an objectively reasonable prospect that the accused activities would contribute relatively directly to kinds of information likely relevant to FDA approval. The jury rejected that defense and the district court denied judgment as a matter of law. The appellate court's restrictive premises can affect its review of that ruling even though the jury instruction was not a categorical clinical-only charge. All eight would require the Federal Circuit to reconsider the preserved challenge under the correct construction and the admissible trial evidence, including whether an independent basis sustains the verdict. They do not now decide which experiments satisfy the standard, the adequacy of any differently preserved instructional objection, or whether the record legally compels a particular result. That remand is the supported appellate work remaining; it is not a request that the operator locate a new historical document before deciding the statutory issue.

NCI's later IND was excluded from the trial evidence. Its illustrative use in an amicus brief cannot repair the trial proof or supply judgment as a matter of law. The dates and reported inclusion of certain research can explain the regulatory question but cannot establish what the jury was entitled to find. The district court's September 7, 2004 award of $6.375 million followed the earlier damages remand. The mandate vacates the reviewed safe-harbor liability determination and requires the lower courts to address the consequent effect on that dependent award through the proper proceedings; it neither resurrects the original $15 million award nor decides a separately unpresented damages appeal.

All eight can join the foregoing statutory construction, rejection of the categorical exclusions, and confined remand. All eight reserve the distinct research-tool question because the supplied candidate-compound dispute does not establish that category's proper resolution. That reservation is not adoption of the United States' proposed categorical tool exclusion. All eight leave the distinct common-law experimental-use ruling outside this statutory grant. These bounded issues do not fracture the common rule or require an extra writing merely to recite its limits. No proposed dissent or concurrence remains necessary after the opinion adopts those limits; an assembly draft exceeding them requires an actual renewed join test.
