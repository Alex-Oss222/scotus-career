## Illinois v. Caballes

### Reconciled result and complete sufficient ground

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer retain vacatur and remand. Souter and Ginsburg retain affirmance of suppression on their distinct stated grounds. The six support the complete narrow rule and application: an exterior examination by a properly trained narcotics dog, actually confined to contraband detection, during an independently lawful traffic detention and without unlawful added restraint does not require separate drug suspicion. The accepted lawful duration and individual reliability finding are retained. A traffic violation supplies detention authority; it does not independently authorize a trunk search or an additional constitutional intrusion. The later physical search has its separate probable-cause predicate.

The six apply Place and Jacobsen to the examination's informational scope. They do not convert the mere discovery of drugs into proof of a lawful method or assume that every dog is infallible. Their answer to the dissent is that a possibility of evidentiary error and a later search are analytically distinct from disclosure of lawful private information by this limited examination. The source supports the packet's accepted findings without requiring importation of its attributed respondent concession or empirical error-rate claims.

Souter retains the view that using the dog to investigate concealed effects is a search: the claimed special exemption cannot follow merely from investigative aim or an alert's sufficient reliability for probable cause. The examination is the initial investigative step toward opening a private enclosure and requires an independent lawful basis beyond this speeding detention. Ginsburg retains the additional view that the compulsory drug investigation materially expands the limited traffic seizure even if it adds no time. She may agree with the limited rejection of a universal contraband-only exemption without thereby joining every part of Souter's separate search rationale. The exact cross-joins of any separately drafted opinions remain for assembly compatibility; historical cross-joinders are not automatically imported.

The corrected federal rule removes the Illinois judgment's categorical independent-suspicion premise for the six. Remand preserves genuinely independent existing grounds without reopening the accepted duration premise, reinstating the conviction by fiat or creating a new reliance defense. The two dissenters would leave suppression effective on the asserted additional constitutional intrusion.

## Commissioner v. Banks and Commissioner v. Banaitis

### Reconciled result and complete sufficient ground

All eight retain reversal and remand in each docket. When an otherwise taxable recovery derives from a client's claim that the client continues to own and control, the contingent-fee portion paid to counsel remains part of the client's gross income under section 61. Counsel's services, risk, tactical independence and direct receipt do not by themselves transfer the claim. The client retains the dispositive control, including whether to settle. The same reasoning covers Oregon's superior payment lien on this record: enforceable security for compensation does not turn the attorney from agent into owner of the income-producing claim.

Earl and Horst support attribution to the owner of the source despite anticipatory diversion. The strongest competing grounds—uncertainty of recovery and the attorney's own value-producing effort—explain the compensation arrangement but do not eliminate the client's retained ownership and benefit. A genuine transfer that changes that relationship would present a distinct question. No actual tax-avoidance motive is required by the source-attribution rule; no finding of such a motive is made.

Inclusion and deduction remain distinct. The actual old-year deduction and AMT provisions govern the separate calculations below. Section 703 of the October 22, 2004 Act does not apply because its temporal conditions require qualifying payments and the judgment or settlement after enactment. The handoff relies on the admitted enactment's actual prospective terms, not on an inference that Congress retroactively changed these returns. The Banks settlement supplies no separately awarded statutory fee, and the fee-shifting-policy objection therefore does not alter the contractual-fee disposition. Genuine statutory awards and supplementary theories not developed below remain outside this determination. No exact liability is fixed.
