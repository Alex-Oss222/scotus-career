## 65. Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A. Inc.

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Conditions:** The record and preservation limits stated below.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition and substantive rules.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment invalidating the rent cap under the substantially-advances takings theory and remand for proceedings consistent with rejection of that theory.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the takings-based bar to enforcement. Do not award compensation, decide an unpreserved due-process claim, or order a new economic-efficacy trial. Any genuinely preserved independent claim must satisfy its own substantive and procedural predicates.
* **Remedial Reach:** The judgment concerns Chevron’s challenge and the injunction or declaration grounded on the rejected theory. No independent unlawful policy is proved under a surviving constitutional rule. Del Monte Dunes’s completed compensation judgment remains final.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must vacate relief resting solely on the efficacy test and identify any independently preserved matter actually remaining. Hawaii may enforce the cap subject to other valid orders or claims. The mandate does not declare that every application is immune from a properly established taking claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Remove efficacy as a freestanding takings predicate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Chevron selected and obtained judgment on the efficacy theory, so its constitutional status must be resolved. The Court has no factual basis or preserved instruction to decide a different property-loss or due-process claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether lack of substantial advancement alone constitutes a taking and supports this judgment.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Change entering law at the purpose-relationship boundary: overrule the freestanding Agins formulation and Del Monte Dunes’s acceptance of that alternative as a takings basis. Preserve its completed judgment and independent holdings.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. The error is in the relation between the constitutional source and the proposed liability predicate, so the replacement rule applies beyond rent caps. One step broader would disturb independent compensation, exaction and due-process protections; one step narrower would preserve the same source error merely because a regulator acts on one parcel.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Failure to substantially advance a legitimate governmental objective is not, by itself, a taking of property requiring compensation under the Fifth Amendment. A claimant must establish a taking under the applicable property-burden or exaction rule; the court must not substitute an economic-efficacy trial for that showing. Chevron’s judgment rests solely on the rejected predicate and therefore cannot stand. Governmental invalidity under an independently preserved constitutional source remains a separate question with its own elements and remedy.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The replacement governs regulatory-taking claims based solely on a means-end mismatch, including general regulation and particular permit denials. Loretto’s per se rule for compelled permanent physical occupation, Lucas’s total-deprivation rule, Penn Central’s multifactor inquiry and validly presented dedication claims retain their separate rules.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Courts classify the actual interference before selecting a takings test. Permanent physical occupation follows Loretto; total beneficial-use deprivation follows Lucas; other regulatory burdens follow Penn Central; demanded dedications retain Nollan and Dolan’s distinct limits.
* **Transition:** Apply the replacement to this case and other cases still open on direct review in which the issue is properly presented. Do not reopen final compensation judgments merely because one supporting theory has been displaced; no special collateral-reopening rule is adopted.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lucas and Tahoe-Sierra measure the property burden, while Dolan addresses the distinctive demand that an owner convey property as a permit condition. They are bounded rules in different fields, not three competing definitions of the same efficacy inquiry, and should not be collapsed. An ineffective regulation might impose little property loss; a well-designed one might require compensation. The efficacy test therefore cannot identify the constitutional event. A permanent physical occupation triggers compensation because it appropriates the owner’s control, regardless of regulatory success; this cap imposes no such occupation. Property and transactional reliance support preserving final judgments, not continued compensation for a means-end mismatch unrelated to appropriation. Del Monte Dunes did more than repeat dictum: it accepted sufficient evidence on both submitted alternatives. Stone must withdraw its purpose-relationship alternative as an independent future taking basis, while preserving its economic-deprivation application, jury entitlement in a valid legal damages action, finality rulings and completed award.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Agins v. City of Tiburon and Del Monte Dunes at Monterey v. City of Monterey are overruled only insofar as they recognize the purpose-relationship formulation as an independent takings predicate; Del Monte Dunes’s completed judgment and independent jury, compensation and economic-deprivation holdings remain. Lucas, Dolan, Tahoe-Sierra and Penn Central retain their distinct rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No independent due-process claim or distinct compensation theory is presented for decision on this record.

## 66. Johanns v. Livestock Marketing Association

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Conditions:** The record and preservation limits stated below.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit’s compelled-subsidy judgment in both dockets and remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the facial prohibition on collecting assessments insofar as it rests on a right not to finance this governmental message. Preserve separately pleaded, unresolved statutory and constitutional claims and any independently supported relief. No automatic refund, new levy or adjudication of every producer communication follows.
* **Remedial Reach:** The rejected facial funding theory supplies no basis for a programwide collection injunction. The ruling governs the reviewed assessment challenge and leaves any distinct proved attribution injury to its own properly supported remedy.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify which restraints rested solely on the rejected funding theory and dissolve those restraints, while resolving actual remaining claims under their own law. The Secretary’s continued authority depends on the enacted program and the actual governmental control supporting this judgment, not a new license for autonomous private advertising.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The Government must actually control its own message

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The operational defense is preserved and the record identifies actual approval and participation. No late-argument barrier comparable to United Foods applies. The facial claim cannot be sustained on an attribution feature the statute does not require.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether compulsory financing of the controlled beef campaign violates the asserted freedom against subsidizing private expression.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Resolve the government-speech question reserved in United Foods, applying actual function and control rather than formal board identity.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule governs a program whose message the Government sets and supervises, including targeted assessments. One step broader would immunize private propaganda through a nominal approval power; one step narrower would require government to abandon ordinary policy communication whenever a particular taxpayer or regulated producer objects.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Compulsory financing of a message actually selected and controlled by the Government does not, without compelled personal expression or another independent constitutional defect, violate the First Amendment freedom against subsidizing private speech. Government must establish its operational authorship or control rather than rely on appointment power or a label alone. The enacted direction, supervision in development and final control over the communications establish that relationship here. Funding through an industry assessment rather than general revenue does not change the speaker.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the challenged governmental advertising, not independent private messages, involuntary personal endorsement, or every claim against the Act. It does not require agreement with the Government or suppress producers’ contrary advertising.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An agency’s compliance review of private speech is insufficient; actual message selection and control are required. Until this Court decides the preserved individual-attribution claim, courts apply Barnette and Wooley’s protection against compelled personal expression to established attribution and injury.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Glickman supplies one permissible private collective relationship, but that relationship need not be manufactured when the speaker is actually government. United Foods prohibits an advertising program from using its own private speech as the justification for compulsory association; it explicitly left the operational governmental defense undecided. Keller requires examination of the particular expressive function, which prevents the board’s statutory status from doing all the work. Here the Secretary’s real message control supplies the missing distinction. These are different funded relationships rather than competing rules that should be collapsed into one generic subsidy test.
* **Precedent Treatment:** United States v. United Foods is distinguished and its express reservation resolved. Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott and Keller v. State Bar of California retain their private-function boundaries.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual individual endorsement, statutory misuse of funds and other certified-out claims require facts or legal questions not resolved here. Existing ordinary rules govern those claims on remand; the present ruling supplies no immunity from them.

## 67. Clingman v. Beaver

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Conditions:** The record and preservation limits stated below.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition and substantive rules.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment against the challenged exclusion, while confining the mandate to voluntary party invitations and explaining that strict scrutiny need not be automatic.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Prevent Oklahoma from excluding otherwise qualified voters from an inviting party’s primary solely because they retain another party registration. Preserve neutral rules limiting a voter to one primary and ordinary verification. Clarify any broader appellate injunction rather than disabling the entire primary system.
* **Remedial Reach:** The categorical exclusion is the challenged policy, so relief covers qualified inviting parties and their otherwise eligible invited voters, not only the named Libertarians. Oklahoma bears the burden of showing that narrower party-specific relief fully cures the demonstrated policy defect.
* **Practical Relief:** Election officials must accept the party’s timely invitation and provide an administrable single-primary choice for eligible voters without demanding an otherwise unnecessary change in continuing affiliation. Existing deadlines and verification rules remain unless separately unlawful; implementation must not recreate the forbidden exclusion under a different label. No past election result changes.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Consent and the primary association

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** A qualified party actually invited these voters and the State actually excluded them. This concrete reciprocal choice is enough to assess the challenged condition without deciding other Oklahoma laws.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a voter must surrender existing party registration to accept another party’s voluntary primary invitation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the contextual burden-and-fit rule and extend the voluntary-association reasoning of Timmons to this primary invitation; distinguish Jones’s compelled outsider selection.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule protects a party’s actual invitation while preserving election administration and other parties’ autonomy. One step broader would grant outsiders access against a party’s will; one step narrower would leave the same reciprocal association subject to exclusion merely by naming a different existing registration.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State may not exclude an otherwise qualified voter from a political party’s primary solely because the voter retains another party registration when the inviting party consents and the State has not shown a sufficient regulatory need for that exclusion. The court evaluates actual burden and the State’s precise justification under Anderson and Burdick; this substantial associational burden fails that inquiry on the presented record without a new scrutiny tier. The State may require a single primary choice, verify eligibility and enforce independently lawful rules that actually address fraud or administrative needs.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers voluntary invitations and qualified participants, not compelled crossover, duplicate voting or unlimited party authority to override constitutional voter protections.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** An objecting party retains Jones’s protection. Distinct supported threats receive contextual review; continued affiliation by an invited voter is not itself proof of raiding.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Timmons recognizes that meaningful nomination association is not replaced by permission to speak outside the official election. The same point applies when the party chooses who may participate in its nomination: outreach alone is not the legally effective invitation it seeks. Jones protects choice against compulsory outsiders, so it supports examining consent rather than treating every outsider as constitutionally unwelcome. The asserted stability interest cannot mean preserving rigid alliances for their own sake. Single-primary verification addresses the concrete duplicate-participation concern without forcing enduring disaffiliation. Anderson, Burdick and Timmons can be restated as one contextual burden, precise-interest and fit inquiry; Jones supplies a distinct severe-burden application when association is compelled.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party is extended to the reciprocal invitation setting. California Democratic Party v. Jones is distinguished and reaffirmed. Tashjian’s reserved cross-party question is resolved at this consent-based scope.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other Oklahoma ballot-access and affiliation statutes were not raised or decided below.

## 68. Deck v. Missouri

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Missouri judgment and remand for a new penalty proceeding unless the State establishes that the unconstitutional visible restraint was harmless beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the reviewed sentencing affirmance; require proper direct-review harmlessness analysis and, absent that showing, a new sentencing proceeding. Preserve the unchallenged convictions. Any future visible restraints require a contemporaneous case-specific justification.
* **Remedial Reach:** Relief covers Deck’s challenged sentence. No institutional policy claim or broader class remedy is proved. The governing rule informs future proceedings but does not reopen final sentences automatically.
* **Practical Relief:** The reviewing court must place the harmlessness burden on Missouri and assess the actual penalty record, not require Deck to reconstruct an unshackled trial. If that burden cannot be met, the State must conduct a lawful new sentencing or impose the lawful alternative under state procedure. At retrial the judge must decide actual security needs before exposing the jury to restraints and reconsider changed circumstances.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Punishment remains undecided despite a valid conviction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Deck objected before and during jury selection and identified the visible effect. The court’s categorical reliance on convicted status supplies no individualized security determination. The penalty-only posture permits relief without disturbing guilt.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether visible shackling without specific necessity is permissible at capital sentencing and who bears the remedial burden.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the existing protection against unjustified prejudicial courtroom measures to capital punishment selection and expressly apply Chapman to the resulting preserved constitutional error.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The rule addresses the recurring capital penalty-stage practice while retaining actual security authority. One step broader would decide all restraints or make every error structural; one step narrower would protect a fair guilt decision while permitting an unjustified official signal at the equally consequential punishment decision.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A court may not require a defendant to appear visibly shackled before a capital sentencing jury without a supported, defendant-specific finding of an essential security or comparable courtroom need. The State bears that justification burden, with reasonable less prejudicial alternatives considered under ordinary procedures; no ritual hearing or fixed security script is required. Once unjustified visible restraint is established, the defendant need not prove its actual effect on each juror. On direct review the State must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the sentence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers deliberately imposed visible restraints during the jury’s capital sentencing decision and permits measures actually justified by particular circumstances. It leaves concealed measures and materially different proceedings to their own facts and governing law.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Conviction may inform a defendant-specific danger assessment but cannot replace it. Necessary security measures remain lawful; unjustified visible restraints during capital jury sentencing receive Chapman review on direct appeal.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Riggins recognizes that a compelled courtroom condition may influence the defense in ways the transcript cannot reconstruct; its particular remedy does not already decide this case’s harmlessness rule. Allen preserves necessary control, so the correct rule requires justification rather than forbids restraints absolutely. Holbrook distinguishes measures that carry an inherently prejudicial signal from ordinary security arrangements. Conviction eliminates the guilt presumption but not the requirement that punishment be selected from lawful evidence and argument. Applying Chapman places the burden on the party responsible for the constitutional error instead of demanding impossible proof from Deck.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Riggins v. Nevada is extended by analogy without enlarging its controlling holding. Illinois v. Allen and Holbrook v. Flynn are applied to capital sentencing. Chapman v. California is expressly applied to the preserved error; no structural-error rule is adopted.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Concealed restraints, brief accidental sightings and noncapital proceedings are outside the record. Collateral retroactivity is not presented in this direct review.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the writ as improvidently granted on the changed vehicle, without deciding the treaty or presidential-power merits.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Issue no federal writ, new sentence, automatic certificate or execution restraint. Do not characterize dismissal as approval of the Fifth Circuit’s categorical treaty reasoning.
* **Remedial Reach:** No constitutional or treaty violation with a proved domestic remedy is adjudicated in this disposition. It terminates this grant only and leaves the existing judgments and separate pending proceeding in their actual procedural positions.
* **Practical Relief:** The parties continue the pending Texas proceeding under applicable law. Any later request for Supreme Court review must identify the actual judgment and satisfy its own timing and jurisdictional requirements. Any necessary execution protection must be requested and decided on its actual legal and factual predicates; dismissal neither commands execution nor certifies that interim protection is unnecessary.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Resolve the vehicle without converting reservations into merits law

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The reviewed act is a certificate denial on an older claim; the newly invoked presidential source has not been adjudicated below and is now before the Texas court. The Court can exercise review discretion without assuming how that court will decide or pronouncing the case moot.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether to resolve the granted binding-effect and comity questions through this materially changed federal habeas vehicle.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing source-specific jurisdiction and certificate rules and dismiss this grant on its changed posture. Adopt no new treaty enforceability, executive-power or default rule.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record. The unadjudicated state route and certificate posture together justify the disposition. One step broader would convert review discretion into a categorical international-judgment exclusion; one step narrower would omit the actual vehicle reason and risk implying substantive approval of the lower judgment.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A grant directed to an older federal certificate denial may be dismissed as improvidently granted when a new, unadjudicated domestic implementation ground is being presented in a state proceeding capable of supplying the requested review and the federal gateways remain independently unresolved. This is a discretionary vehicle disposition, not a finding that state relief is certain or that the international judgment lacks domestic force. The applicable certificate requirements, exhaustion rules and lawful interim remedies retain their existing burdens.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The disposition reaches this grant and supplies no substantive bar to Medellín’s pending state application, a proper later petition, or another litigant’s timely treaty claim.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Until this Court decides Avena’s domestic binding effect or the memorandum’s authority, courts apply §§2253–2254 to federal habeas requests and the governing treaty text through the proper domestic remedial channel. Breard’s interim orders do not establish a categorical merits bar.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Slack permits certiorari review of a certificate denial but distinguishes review from the certificate and underlying relief. Miller-El prevents a merits-proof demand at that stage, yet retains statutory eligibility. Reed separates federal-law character from collateral entitlement; it does not decide every treaty claim. Breard’s interim orders reserved the questions now pressed and therefore cannot dispose of them. The new state application raises a different source of implementation not reached below. Resolving that source in the first instance here would conflate the old federal gateway, international obligation and new executive act. Discretionary dismissal keeps those questions attached to an actual adjudicated record rather than issuing hypothetical instructions about a hoped-for result.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Slack v. McDaniel, Miller-El v. Cockrell, Reed v. Farley and the two Breard proceedings retain their exact scopes. No precedent is overruled.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The memorandum’s effect was not adjudicated below, and the treaty merits are not reached in this dismissal on the changed vehicle.

## 70. Lofton v. Secretary of the Department of Children & Family Services

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Conditions:** The record and preservation limits stated below.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition and substantive rules.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the categorical exclusion under equal protection and remand for declaratory and injunctive relief against §63.042(3). That ground supplies the requested opportunity for adoption consideration; do not reach the alternative substantive-due-process theory.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require Florida to accept and evaluate applications without the categorical orientation bar, applying otherwise lawful child-specific eligibility and best-interest criteria. No applicant receives an adoption decree from this Court. Existing placements and guardianship rights are not terminated or automatically enlarged.
* **Remedial Reach:** The proved defect is the statewide categorical exclusion, so relief reaches all applicants governed by it. Florida must show that a narrower order would fully cure that policy defect; an exception limited to the named adults would not. The judgment does not guarantee any individual placement.
* **Practical Relief:** The Department must remove the disqualifying criterion from its operative instructions and application decisions, notify responsible personnel, and provide prompt lawful reconsideration for applications rejected on that ground within ordinary applicable procedures. The district court must set and enforce a compliance schedule. Decisions remain child-specific, but an agency cannot reintroduce the invalid categorical assumption as an unexamined fitness rule.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The State must justify the actual exclusion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory bar directly prevents consideration, and its orientation basis is evident even under the stated conduct construction. The developed justifications can be assessed without finding any particular adult entitled to adopt.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the categorical adoption exclusion satisfies equal protection despite lawful child-welfare regulation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Shahar and Equality Foundation’s existing intermediate scrutiny; do not announce a new suspect classification or rely solely on historical rational-basis analysis.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. The invalid mechanism is statewide categorical ineligibility, so the answer must cover that mechanism. One step broader would replace neutral child-welfare determinations with an adoption entitlement; one step narrower would preserve the identical unsupported exclusion for other applicants.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State may not categorically exclude gay and lesbian adults from adoption without demonstrating a substantial relationship between that classification and a genuine important child-welfare objective. Florida bears the justificatory burden under intermediate scrutiny; ordinary civil evidence and supported professional judgment can satisfy it, but stereotype and merely conceivable future households cannot. The categorical exclusion fails on this record, which does not substantiate why orientation establishes unfitness while comparable heterosexual single applicants remain eligible and gay caregivers are entrusted with children.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding removes orientation-based ineligibility, including a deliberate conduct proxy. It preserves actual parenting-capacity, safety, stability and child-specific welfare criteria applied without the unsupported categorical assumption.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A denied adoption may remain lawful for supported child-specific reasons. Equal eligibility means a genuine evaluation, not a presumption of approval or a requirement that every proposed family arrangement receive identical treatment without regard to relevant facts.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Shahar makes government prove a real substantial fit rather than rely on post hoc possible benefits. Equality Foundation demonstrates that this is not merely an employment rule. Child welfare is important, but the label does not establish the exclusion’s relationship to it. The asserted possibility that a heterosexual single applicant will later marry does not explain why every gay applicant, including a long-standing caregiver, is unfit for consideration. Foster and adoption differences are real, yet permanence alone cannot transform an unsupported orientation inference into evidence. Lawrence’s reserved civil-status question does not suspend this independently established equality protection.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Shahar v. Bowers and Equality Foundation of Greater Cincinnati v. City of Cincinnati are applied. Romer v. Evans is preserved as an independent floor. Lawrence v. Texas is distinguished at its civil-status reservation, not enlarged into automatic adoption eligibility by itself.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The alternative family-integrity theory is unnecessary because equality affords the complete requested eligibility relief; no independent custody, removal or guardianship judgment requires decision. It is neither accepted nor rejected.

## 71. Arthur Andersen LLP v. United States

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user's express preapproval.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** Current revision request.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 30, 2004, evaluated as of the scheduled event.
* **Conditions:** The record and preservation limits stated below.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the conviction because the instructions allowed guilt without the required wrongful awareness and contemplated-proceeding connection.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the conviction and remand for lawful further proceedings under correct instructions. Do not direct acquittal or mandatory retrial; preserve any independent sufficiency or other issue whose resolution governs what proceedings remain available.
* **Remedial Reach:** The challenged conviction and its dependent sentence. This decision does not adjudicate separate actors’ convictions, uncharged conduct or liability under later statutes.
* **Practical Relief:** Andersen receives relief from a conviction obtained through an insufficient legal standard. Prosecutors retain the ability to charge and prove corrupt anticipatory destruction within the enacted offense, subject to ordinary procedural limits.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Awareness and a contemplated official proceeding

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both elements because each independently affects the legality of the charge supporting the conviction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can mere persuasion to impair possible government access constitute knowing corrupt persuasion without wrongful awareness or a contemplated proceeding?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** No. Require conscious wrongdoing in the persuasion and intent directed to evidence use in a particular contemplated official proceeding. The proceeding need not be pending or imminent, and its exact date or docket need not be known.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Stone’s offense-specific mens rea method requires a reusable construction of the charged persuasion statute while respecting its express anticipatory reach. One step broader would create an overarching knowledge-of-illegality rule for all obstruction crimes. One step narrower would leave the recurring distinction between ordinary document management and knowing corrupt persuasion undefined.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 1512(b)(2) requires knowing corrupt persuasion, meaning awareness of the wrongful character of the persuasion, with the specified intent concerning evidence in an official proceeding the persuader contemplates. The instruction cannot replace those requirements with any purpose to impede government factfinding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves both granted instructional objections without creating a requirement to know the criminal statute or wait for formal compulsory process.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A policy label supplies no immunity. Investigative warnings, internal advice, timing and anticipated evidentiary use may establish wrongful awareness and a contemplated proceeding; abstract awareness of possible future government interest is insufficient.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ordinary persuasion can protect legitimate confidences or implement valid retention rules. The words knowingly and corruptly must distinguish those acts from wrongful interference. At the same time, the actual SEC contacts and internal legal discussions could support culpability under the right charge. The jury, not this Court, must make that determination. Congress’s removal of a pendency requirement supports preventive reach without erasing a concrete official object.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apply United States v. Aguilar’s insistence on the enacted obstruction object by analogy, expressly distinguishing its pending-proceeding requirement. Overrule no controlling precedent.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Outside the charged offense and question presented: construction of later §1519 and §1512(c). The holding creates neither a blanket pre-service privilege nor counsel-participation immunity.

##### Issue 2: Actual-verdict error and remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach prejudice on the challenged charge; do not turn legal error into an unsupported sufficiency judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can the conviction survive instructions that expressly allowed lawful-belief conduct and omitted the necessary proceeding connection?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** The Government has not established noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt on this charge and contested mental-state record.
* **Reach Calibration:** Confined to this record. Stone applies the existing actual-verdict method to these instructions and contested mental-state evidence. One step broader would declare every error concerning an obstruction element automatically structural. One step narrower would postpone relief despite the Government’s failure to establish reliable noncontribution on the charge actually given.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The stated instructions permitted conviction through legally insufficient culpability findings and supply no reliable basis to conclude beyond reasonable doubt that those errors did not contribute to this verdict. The conviction must be set aside.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The record-specific prejudice conclusion supports reversal now rather than a speculative harmlessness presumption.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Further proceedings apply the correctly construed elements and ordinary sufficiency and double-jeopardy limits; reversal for trial error alone does not command acquittal or retrial.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The central dispute was what the firm’s policy directions and timing meant. Evidence supporting the prosecution cannot show what the jury would have found after being told that wrongful awareness and a contemplated proceeding were necessary when its actual instruction denied those limits. Lengthy deliberations do not themselves prove prejudice; the controlling failure is the unreliable connection between the actual legal findings and the required offense.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Follow Neder v. United States’s controlling burden and verdict-sensitive limits, including its distinction between powerful evidence and reliable noncontribution.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.
