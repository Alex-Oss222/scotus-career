## 17. United States v. Booker, consolidated with United States v. Fanfan

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2005-01-12; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Booker: affirm the setting aside of the enhanced sentence and remand for lawful resentencing. Fanfan: affirm the refusal to impose the unsupported enhancement and the resulting sentence against that challenge, leaving only any independently preserved calculation issue for ordinary disposition.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Retain the binding Guidelines. The sentencing court must calculate the range using only facts established with the applicable constitutional protections, then exercise the discretion lawfully available within it. No freestanding order for a new enhancement jury follows; further proceedings require independent legal authority and compliance with charging, waiver, proof and finality constraints.
* **Remedial Reach:** The two defendants' judgments and the governing rule for nonfinal federal cases to which it applies. Convictions are not vacated merely because a sentencing enhancement fails. No class resentencing order or automatic reopening of final judgments is entered.
* **Practical Relief:** Booker's sentencing court must enter a replacement sentence authorized by the lawful range, correcting any independently established calculation defect. Fanfan's 78-month sentence remains effective against the asserted entitlement to use the excluded findings. The Government may seek additional proceedings only if an actual procedural source permits them; the mandate does not supply that source. Ordinary appellate review continues under surviving enacted provisions.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The constitutional boundary already established

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The two records concretely distinguish an imposed judicial enhancement from refusal to impose one. Existing law answers the asserted federal-Guidelines distinction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the additional findings can raise the binding range without the applicable safeguards.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law, restating Apprendi, Angle, Harris, Gatewood and Blakely as the single authorization inquiry they already support.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad. The legal effect of a fact, rather than its label or rulemaker, defines the constitutional boundary. One step broader would regulate factfinding that does not change authorized punishment. One step narrower would recreate the statutory-label exceptions already rejected.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A fact that increases a binding sentencing ceiling or floor must be charged when the applicable grand-jury guarantee requires it and established to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt, validly admitted as an element, or determined beyond a reasonable doubt after a lawful jury waiver. The Government bears those charging and proof obligations; judicial discretion remains within the range already authorized. Booker's additional findings cannot support his increased range, and Fanfan's court properly declined the comparable enhancement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Present-offense and recidivist facts that increase binding federal punishment boundaries, subject to the precise existing admission and waiver rules. Ordinary selection within a lawfully fixed range is excluded.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A sentencing report’s unchallenged assertion is not by itself a valid element-level admission.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Angle already rejects an outer-statutory-maximum escape from the jury guarantee for binding Guidelines. Harris establishes the same principle for a mandatory floor. Gatewood rejects a categorical recidivism carveout, and Blakely confirms that a plea authorizes only the range supported by the admitted elements. These are more than three bounded rules governing the same authorization question; Stone would state them as one inquiry without overruling any. Mistretta sustains the Commission's institution but supplies no exception to an independent constitutional guarantee.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Apprendi, Angle, Harris, Gatewood and Blakely: reaffirm and restate their common authorization inquiry. Mistretta v. United States: preserve institutional validity while distinguishing the sentencing-fact question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The validity of any particular unexamined admission, indictment or recidivist input needs the actual record.

##### Issue 2: Constitutional operation without an advisory rewrite

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The severability question is presented and requires a full answer because the Government seeks a different statutory system in both dockets.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Court should remove binding force or retain the enacted system subject to the constitutional safeguards.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the reserved remedial question by preserving the binding structure and rejecting the requested judicial advisory replacement.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad within the federal sentencing statute. The remedy must govern ordinary future application and both present judgments. One step broader would redesign substantive sentencing policy or authorize new prosecutions. One step narrower would leave courts unable to know whether the enacted ranges remain mandatory.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Federal Sentencing Guidelines remain binding to the extent a sentence is supported by constitutionally established facts; the constitutional defect does not authorize replacing Congress's mandatory system with an advisory one. Courts must withhold an unsupported enhancement and impose a lawful sentence under the remaining enacted framework. Severability removes only what cannot constitutionally operate, and existing law supplies no automatic authority to convene a new enhancement proceeding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The Federal Sentencing Reform Act and challenged applications, with ordinary review of lawful ranges retained. The rule does not dictate what replacement system Congress may enact.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** Further enhancement factfinding requires an independent procedural source and the governing charging, proof and waiver protections.
* **Transition:** Ordinary direct-review rules govern nonfinal cases; forfeited claims retain all Olano requirements. No categorical collateral reopening is proposed. Existing statutory deadlines, retroactivity rules and habeas gateways govern final cases separately.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Angle already demonstrated an operative binding framework with protected factfinding. The constitutional minimum constrains enforcement without requiring a new statutory policy. Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock directs attention to the functioning enacted remainder; it does not allow a court to substitute a more discretionary arrangement because the Government predicts Congress would prefer it. Blakely and Gatewood require an actual source for additional proceedings after vacatur. The two defendants consequently receive different dispositions because only Booker suffered the identified enhancement.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Angle: reaffirm binding operation and decide its reserved system-level remedy. Blakely and Gatewood: reaffirm the limit on automatic additional enhancement proceedings. Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock: apply severability without judicial replacement of the statutory design.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent record-specific calculation and preservation issues, if actually raised, require their existing records.

## 18. Jama v. Immigration and Customs Enforcement

* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the claimed categorical advance-acceptance requirement.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject this statutory habeas ground. The agency may proceed under the birthplace clause only within the actual selection sequence and all independently applicable restrictions. The judgment supplies neither a new detention order nor a finding that every possible removal protection fails.
* **Remedial Reach:** Jama's legal challenge to the identified destination selection. No class relief or universal removal command is involved.
* **Practical Relief:** The acceptance-based bar is removed. Before carrying out removal, officials remain responsible for satisfying the existing order and any operative stay or protection; the judgment requires no fictitious acceptance document. Any continued detention remains subject to its own statutory source and foreseeable-removal limits.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The condition attached to the actual clause

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The preserved legal question can be resolved from the operative statute without making findings about unpresented protection claims.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the birthplace clause demands receiving-government acceptance.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new statutory construction through ordinary text and structure.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. The connected-country clause needs a rule applicable beyond this one destination. One step broader would decide independent protection or detention powers. One step narrower would leave the same textual question unanswered whenever another country lacks an accepting government.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1231(b)(2)(E)(iv) permits selection of the country of birth without an additional advance-acceptance condition, provided the preceding statutory steps and the clause's own predicates are satisfied. The Government must identify the actual statutory route; it may not use the clause to evade a separate bar to removal or an applicable custody limit. The express acceptance condition in clause (vii) does not extend backward to clause (iv).
* **Reach Within the Question:** The connected-country selection question presented by clause (iv). Other express acceptance requirements retain their full operation.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress separated designated, nationality, connected-country and residual destinations and expressly attached acceptance to the residual clause. Treating the last clause as an unstated limit on all preceding clauses would erase that chosen difference. St. Cyr preserves legal review without resolving the substance. Zadvydas v. Davis prevents the distinct inference that a legally selectable destination supplies indefinite custody when actual removal remains unforeseeable.
* **Precedent Treatment:** INS v. St. Cyr and Zadvydas v. Davis: preserve their separate review and detention rules; neither is expanded into a destination-acceptance requirement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unpresented withholding, torture-protection and actual removal-feasibility claims require their own records.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in Martinez; reverse in Benitez; remand both for implementation of enforceable release protection under the removal-related statutory limit.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain or order release under lawful supervision because the established lack of foreseeable removal is unrebutted. Temporary parole does not substitute for the legal protection adjudicated. Any future detention must rest on changed removal facts or a separately applicable lawful source and its required process.
* **Remedial Reach:** The two detainees and their challenged custody. The statutory rule applies to the provision's categories, but no class injunction or immigration admission is granted.
* **Practical Relief:** The district courts must ensure prompt release if either man is held solely on the rejected indefinite-detention theory and specify lawful supervision. A future custody action requires an actual new lawful basis, with available review. Officials may not indefinitely recycle short parole periods to avoid the adjudicated temporal limit.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: One removal-related grant of detention authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory question remains live and the findings of no foreseeable removal permit application without additional speculation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the reserved inadmissible category is governed by the same statutory purpose and duration limit.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend Zadvydas through a new statutory holding, respecting its express earlier reservation.
* **Reach Calibration:** Broad within §1231(a)(6). The common grant of removal-related authority requires a coherent scope across its covered categories. One step broader would decide constitutional admission rights or invalidate separate detention enactments. One step narrower would let the identical statutory authority change into indefinite preventive detention without a textual basis.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1231(a)(6) authorizes detention of its covered inadmissible as well as admitted noncitizens only for the period reasonably necessary to accomplish removal. After the six-month presumptive period, the detainee must give good reason to believe that removal lacks a significant likelihood in the reasonably foreseeable future, and the Government must sufficiently rebut that showing. The unrebutted findings here require release under lawful supervision; neither release nor this statutory construction grants admission.
* **Reach Within the Question:** All categories governed by the common §1231(a)(6) detention grant, with the existing obstruction rules and independent detention statutes retained on their actual predicates.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A claim of dangerousness alone supplies no detention authority beyond the invoked statute’s predicates and procedures.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Zadvydas v. Davis tied this statutory custody to removal and expressly left this category open. Resolving that reservation requires examining the common language and function, not pretending an earlier holding already covered the men. Different constitutional positions at entry do not create an unstated second statutory purpose. Parole regulates release but supplies no independent indefinite-custody grant. The separate 2001 enactment illustrates why a distinct preventive power must be identified and proved rather than inferred from criminal history alone.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Zadvydas v. Davis: extend its statutory construction to the previously reserved inadmissible category, retaining its presumption and burdens. Mezei: distinguish its statutory and procedural setting without resolving every entry-detention constitutional question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A properly invoked separate national-security detention statute and a materially changed removal prospect require facts absent here.

## 20. Nordyke v. King

### SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved for this revision under the user’s express authorization.
* **Approval Date or Turn:** 2026-11-22, current revision request.
* **Discretion Envelope:** Nonmaterial wording consistent with the stated disposition, reasoning, scope and remedy.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** June 30, 2004, assessed for 2005-01-12; subject to intervening controlling decisions.
* **Conditions:** Subject to the stated record limits and any intervening controlling decision.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the preliminary-relief denial to the extent necessary for application of the established individual-right and incorporation rules; reject a categorical facial First Amendment invalidation on this record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for prompt determination of the actual acquisition and possession burden, the county's justification and the remaining preliminary-relief requirements. Preserve any properly presented concrete expressive-display claim under its own test. The mandate does not itself require unrestricted possession on all county property.
* **Remedial Reach:** The plaintiffs' challenge to the ordinance. If the plaintiffs establish an unlawful policy and obtain relief, the restraint must reach that policy and those it governs unless the county shows a narrower order completely cures the demonstrated violation. No such merits finding is assumed at this preliminary stage.
* **Practical Relief:** The lower court must identify the challenged fairgrounds applications, available meaningful acquisition routes, actual exemptions and safety evidence, then promptly decide preliminary protection under the corrected law. Any injunction must specify the prohibited enforcement and preserved lawful safety conditions. Failure to establish an indispensable prerequisite permits denial with reasons; a no-individual-right premise does not.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Apply the existing individual guarantee

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenged premise directly denies rights already recognized by this Court; the record nevertheless does not establish the burden and justification necessary for final relief.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether individuals may invoke the Amendment and how the fairgrounds restriction is assessed.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current law, not create a first individual-right or incorporation holding.
* **Reach Calibration:** Intermediate. Love and Wright already supply the rule, and the remand needs concrete application instructions. One step broader would decide all public-property possession without a developed record. One step narrower would correct standing terminology while leaving the wrong substantive test in place.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Individuals may invoke the incorporated Second Amendment against a county restriction that burdens protected possession or necessary acquisition, and public ownership does not eliminate the right. The claimant must identify protected conduct and the actual burden; the county must then satisfy the justification required by Wright for that burden. This record cannot be rejected on collective-right or nonincorporation grounds, but it does not establish an unconditional entitlement to operate a gun show with unrestricted possession.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The presented fairgrounds and acquisition restriction, including its actual exemptions. Defensive home possession and public-property conditions remain distinct applications of the established framework.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** None.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Love v. Pepersack already resolves individual protection, incorporation and acquisition incident to keeping arms. Wright supplies the calibrated burden framework and forbids reducing it to a weapons list or automatic safety exception. Emerson shows that supported safety circumstances can justify a substantial restriction, not that invoking safety ends review. Here the lower court's erroneous premise prevented the necessary burden analysis; correcting it requires actual application rather than an invented constitutional violation.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Love v. Pepersack and United States v. Wright: reaffirm and apply. United States v. Emerson: preserve its evidence-based justification and factual limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The precise burden, alternative channels and fit require findings absent from this preliminary record.

##### Issue 2: Actual expression and appropriate breadth

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial speech theory can be assessed at its asserted breadth while any properly preserved concrete display claim remains tied to its own facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether every firearm possession at a show is protected speech requiring facial invalidation.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing expressive-conduct principles without converting the separate arms right into a speech presumption.
* **Reach Calibration:** Narrow. The present record supports rejection of the categorical facial theory, not final adjudication of every communicative display. One step broader would insulate viewpoint discrimination or special expressive applications. One step narrower would leave the proposed equation of every possession with speech unresolved.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Firearm possession does not become speech in every application merely because the possessor supports firearm rights, but a demonstrably communicative display receives the First Amendment protection appropriate to its actual context. The claimant must establish that expressive application and the basis for the requested breadth of relief; the county must satisfy the applicable scrutiny for any proved burden. This record does not support invalidating the entire possession ordinance on a categorical facial speech theory.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Ordinary possession, commercial offers and actual political or educational display are assessed according to what the rule regulates and what the claimant communicates.
* **Next-Case Guidance:** A concrete communicative display is assessed under the applicable expressive-conduct and forum rules, with viewpoint discrimination separately prohibited.
* **Transition:** Not applicable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Texas v. Johnson protects conduct that communicates in context without making every object constitutionally expressive. O'Brien addresses a genuinely incidental burden, while expression-targeted or viewpoint-based enforcement requires its own stricter rule. The presence of advocacy at a show therefore warrants attention to concrete displays and exemptions, but does not establish that every possession application is invalid.
* **Precedent Treatment:** Texas v. Johnson and United States v. O'Brien: apply their distinct expression and incidental-regulation inquiries.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A specific expressive-display application and any claimed discriminatory exemption require the actual preserved record.
