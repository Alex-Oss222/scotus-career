## G1 — Genuine process correction and independent relief requirements

All eight Associates directly join the controlling Abdur'Rahman distinction and its application. A motion confined to an asserted federal procedural defect preventing adjudication of a claim already pleaded is not a second or successive habeas application merely because reopening might eventually permit custody relief. Gonzalez's motion challenges the federal limitations barrier, not a federal merits rejection of his plea claim. It contains no new custody claim, new factual merits supplement or demand for renewed constitutional-merits adjudication. Section 2244(b) therefore does not require advance authorization for this motion.

A label alone does not establish that classification. An actual merits demand remains subject to the applicable successive restrictions, and a mixed filing requires separation of genuine process correction from new or renewed merits demands. The distinction rests on the substance of the requested correction and the integrity of the federal proceeding, not on whether the pleading happens to use the word claim. These propositions are applied from existing law, rather than announced as though Abdur'Rahman had never been decided.

Classification supplies no reopening, certificate or custody relief. Rule 60(b)(6) retains its extraordinary-circumstances and reasonable-time requirements. It cannot be used to evade the distinct requirements of the Rule's other grounds. The section 2254 state-custody proceeding is the case presented; no separate section 2255 rule is decided. Nor does this decision determine that a new full habeas petition after a limitations dismissal would be nonsuccessive. Applicable certificate conditions remain independent, including the required constitutional and procedural reasonable-debate showings where they apply.

Florida's argument that any eventual opportunity for custody relief makes the motion successive conflicts with the operative process distinction. Calderon's completed-merits recall rule does not place an innocence gateway on this genuine nonmerits request. Artuz and Pace identify filing conditions and claim-relief bars; they do not establish the first petition's actual timeliness here.

## G2 — Reconciled extraordinary-relief paths

**G2-A: affirm denial on the complete Rule 60 showing.** Assume, for this sufficient-ground analysis, that Gonzalez made the alleged timely rehearing submission, received the described returned-paper information and consequently misunderstood the remaining ordinary-review opportunity. Do not find that he made no attempt, that the clerk acted correctly, or that the State's account is true. Even with that account considered, the later clarification of a procedural statute, the absence of a prior merits hearing, the asserted review episode and the demonstrated subsequent pursuit do not together establish extraordinary circumstances warranting reopening this final judgment.

A later statutory interpretation does not automatically reopen judgments already final, even when it corrects a nonmerits barrier. The first-merits opportunity matters, but it is not itself the exceptional circumstance that classification deliberately leaves to Rule 60. The alleged April episode bears on the lost appellate opportunity. It does not itself establish a causal explanation or reasonably sustained pursuit for the separate eight-to-nine-month period after Artuz. The unrepresented and custodial circumstances remain relevant, but the showing identifies no further circumstance connecting the earlier misunderstanding to the later course of pursuit sufficiently to make this combination extraordinary. The ruling concerns the complete showing, not a freestanding numerical deadline.

This ground neither declares every legal change irrelevant nor holds that eight or nine months is invariably unreasonable. The assumed returned-paper episode is not dismissed as harmless or fictional; its possible significance is considered and found insufficient in this combination. A materially different showing of court-caused loss, diligence and an available lawful remedy could present a different case. The present disposition need not resolve credibility because the sufficient ground assumes the asserted account. For the same limited purpose, the Court may assume that the original limitations interpretation was mistaken; it does not decide the underlying tolling issue.

**G2-B: vacate and remand for ordinary Rule 60 discretion.** Stevens and Souter consider the original denial of all merits review, the intervening procedural clarification, the actual earlier pursuit and the disputed returned-paper episode sufficiently material to require the proper discretionary assessment below. The circuit's categorical successive rule did not perform that inquiry. They would return the motion for examination of the whole course of pursuit, including resolution of the particular disputed episode if necessary and separate assessment of the period after Artuz.

They reject the conclusion that the later interval and existing showing supply a sufficient appellate ground to affirm without the ordinary assessment. An incarcerated unrepresented applicant's timing cannot be evaluated by a categorical count of months; the possible merits of the underlying claim, actual review efforts, finality interests and alleged official information belong in the discretionary inquiry. These considerations establish neither diligence nor extraordinary circumstances as fact. Their remedy grants no automatic reopening, no certificate and no writ, and prescribes no unsupported mandatory hearing procedure.

The strongest objection to G2-A is that the incorrect threshold approach prevented an individualized assessment of a concrete allegation of obstructed review. G2-A answers by assuming that allegation and resolving the complete exceptional-relief showing without deciding disputed facts. The strongest objection to G2-B is that such an assumption can make factual remand unnecessary. Stevens and Souter conclude that the actual exercise of discretion remains necessary here; the six other Associates conclude that this complete showing can be rejected on the stated ground. Neither group converts Rule 60 into ordinary review of a completed judgment.

## Historical-premise audit and correction of provisional departures

The clean chronology refresh was required because the original neutral account overstated abandonment. It supplied better access to an existing fact dispute; it did not change that dispute from history. The complete historical dissent explicitly described the attempted rehearing, the alleged official information and the State's wrong-case-number response. Ginsburg and Breyer nevertheless joined affirmance. Their refreshed remand votes therefore cannot be justified solely by saying that a concrete dispute has now been identified.

Their prior simulated Abdur'Rahman positions were separately tested as a possible changed legal premise. That decision is real controlling law here, unlike the historical disposition of that earlier matter. Its exact rule, however, separates process classification from reasonable timing, extraordinary circumstances, omitted review and the effect of the asserted legal development. It grants no reopening and establishes no universal requirement to remand each dispute for credibility findings. Its own remand retained an unresolved question about Tennessee's opt-out rule and a distinct corrective appellate channel. Gonzalez's correct process classification is common ground, the present judicial channel is available, and the separate Rule 60 showing can be assessed on an assumption favorable to the movant. The earlier result does not require a different result here.

This inquiry identifies a changed legal setting but no material premise that defensibly explains either Justice's proposed judgment departure. Their earlier acceptance of a Rule 60 remand is not a general preference replacing the case-specific comparison. Accordingly the final commitments restore their affirmance while preserving the corrected chronology and the full independent-relief inquiry. The historical position is strong evidence because the material application premises remain intact, not a command to reproduce an omitted-fact description.

The six affirmance supporters do not adopt the historical shorthand that Gonzalez simply abandoned all review. They adopt the explicitly assumed-account sufficient ground in G2-A. The refined account is essential to accurate application but establishes no material departure in judgment or governing rule. Stevens and Souter retain their remand position with its substantive grounds. No new historical-departure label is warranted for a difference between an incomplete neutral summary and the complete historical record.

## Writings, remedy and resulting boundaries

The common explanation must keep classification and relief separate, acknowledge the alleged rehearing effort, state the assumption used to avoid an adverse factual finding, and explain the complete exceptional-relief conclusion without a numerical delay bar. It must answer the remand objection rather than imply that no dispute exists. Abdur'Rahman supplies existing law; Artuz supplies the procedural interpretation; Pace preserves actual filing and diligence distinctions; Rhines concerns pending petitions; Calderon remains a different completed-merits category.

The Stevens/Souter separate position must explain why initial nonmerits exclusion, the corrective legal development and the disputed pursuit require ordinary discretionary assessment. Their position does not guarantee reopening, assume the allegations true, or declare the first petition timely. It must preserve the State's competing account and separate the post-Artuz interval from the attempted rehearing.

Breyer's substantive reservation must be preserved in the common formulation or a concurrence: actual federal-process defects remain distinct from substantive attacks on the state conviction, and use of the term claim cannot replace that distinction. If the common opinion states G1 exactly as frozen, he can join it completely; a concurrence may clarify the limiting point without withholding the join. The historical existence of a separate writing does not itself allocate authorship or require redundant repetition.

Affirmance leaves the denial of this Rule 60 motion in force. It removes the circuit's categorical successive rationale as a permissible rule but orders no reopening or merits proceeding. Under the two-Justice alternative, the lower court would adjudicate Rule 60 before any possible original-timeliness or habeas-merits question. Neither position changes applicable certificate conditions or grants custody relief. Pliler's proof-dependent protection for court-caused loss remains available on its actual causation, diligence and lawful-remedy predicates; this judgment supplies no general rejection of that protection and no finding that the allegations establish its predicates.
